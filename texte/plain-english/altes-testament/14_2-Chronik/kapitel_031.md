# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 31
### The people give with open hands

---

## The altars of the idols are removed (Verse 1)

[1] When all this was over,
all the Israelites who were there went out to the cities of Judah.
They smashed the stone pillars.
They cut down the Asherah poles.
They tore down the high places and the altars.
They did this in all Judah and Benjamin, and also in Ephraim and Manasseh.
They did not stop until they had destroyed them all.
Then all the people of Israel went back to their own cities.
Each of them went home to his own land.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the Passover, the people themselves clean up. It is not ordered from above, but comes from their own heart. And it also happens in the north, in Ephraim and Manasseh.

---

## Order for the priests and Levites (Verses 2–4)

[2] Hezekiah divided the priests and the Levites into their groups.
Each man had his own task,
both the priests and the Levites.
They took care of the burnt offerings and the peace offerings.
They served, gave thanks, and praised God at the gates of the LORD’s camp.
[3] The king also gave a share of his own property for the burnt offerings.
It was for the burnt offerings every morning and every evening.
It was also for the burnt offerings on the Sabbaths, at the new moons, and at the set feasts,
as it is written in the LORD’s law.
[4] He also commanded the people who lived in Jerusalem
to give the priests and the Levites their share.
Then they could give all their time to the LORD’s law.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the temple is called “the LORD’s camp.” This recalls the time in the wilderness, when the tabernacle stood in the middle of the camp.
> Hezekiah leads by good example: he himself gives from his property for the offerings.
> The priests and Levites should not have to worry about earning a living. They should have time to learn and to teach God’s law.

---

## The great heaps (Verses 5–10)

[5] As soon as the order went out,
the people of Israel gave a great deal.
They gave the first fruits of grain, new wine, olive oil, and honey,
and of everything that grew in the fields.
They brought a tithe of everything, and it was a lot.
A tithe is a tenth.
[6] The people of Israel and Judah who lived in the cities of Judah
also brought a tithe of their cattle and sheep.
They brought a tithe of the holy gifts that had been set apart for the LORD their God.
They piled everything up in heaps.
[7] They began to pile up the heaps in the third month.
They finished in the seventh month.
[8] Hezekiah and the leaders came and saw the heaps.
Then they praised the LORD and blessed his people Israel.
[9] Hezekiah asked the priests and the Levites about the heaps.
[10] Azariah, the chief priest from the family of Zadok, answered him,
“Since the people began to bring their offerings into the LORD’s house,
we have had enough to eat.
And there is still plenty left over.
The LORD has blessed his people.
This great supply is what is left over.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “First fruits” are the first fruits of the harvest. The “tithe” is a tenth of the harvest. People gave both to God, as thanks for the harvest (compare Numbers 18; Deuteronomy 14:22–29).
> From the third month (grain harvest) to the seventh month (fruit and grape harvest), the heaps grow. There is more than enough.
> Remarkable in verse 8: Hezekiah praises not only God, but also the people. He sees how generous the people are.
> Verse 10 shows a principle: whoever gives generously does not become poorer. God blesses. (Compare Malachi 3:10.)

## Faithful managers (Verses 11–19)

[11] Then Hezekiah ordered them to prepare storerooms in the LORD’s house.
So they prepared them.
[12] They faithfully brought in the offerings, the tithes, and the holy gifts.
Conaniah the Levite was in charge of them.
His brother Shimei was second in charge.
[13] Under Conaniah and his brother Shimei there were supervisors:
Jehiel, Azaziah, Nahath, Asahel, Jerimoth, Jozabad, Eliel, Ismachiah, Mahath, and Benaiah.
King Hezekiah and Azariah, the leader of God’s house, had appointed them.
[14] Kore, the son of Imnah, was a Levite.
He was the gatekeeper at the East Gate.
He was in charge of the gifts that people gave to God freely.
He handed out the offerings for the LORD and the most holy gifts.
[15] Eden, Miniamin, Jeshua, Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah helped him.
They worked in the towns of the priests, in their position of trust.
They gave shares to their relatives by their groups,
to the great as well as to the small.
[16] They also gave to the males listed in the family records,
who were three years old or older.
They gave to everyone who came into the LORD’s house
to do their daily work,
the service of their group.
[17] They gave to the priests listed in the family records by their families.
And they gave to the Levites who were 20 years old or older,
according to their duties and their groups.
[18] They gave to everyone listed in the family records:
to all their little children, their wives, their sons, and their daughters,
in the whole community.
In their position of trust, they kept themselves holy.
[19] There were also the priests, the sons of Aaron,
who lived in the pasture lands around their towns.
In every town, men were named
to give shares to all the males among the priests
and to everyone listed in the family records of the Levites.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many goods are involved. That is why honest and faithful managers are needed. Chronicles names them all. The words “faithfully” and “position of trust” appear several times.
> The gifts were shared out fairly: “to the great as well as to the small.” Even the priests who lived far away in the country received their share. And the families, wives and children were provided for too.
> “Three years old or older”: even the little boys of the priestly families received a share. Some experts think that it originally said “30 years,” as in 1 Chronicles 23:3.
> These verses are hard to understand in Hebrew. But the meaning is clear: no one was forgotten.

## Hezekiah did everything with all his heart (Verses 20–21)

[20] Hezekiah did this everywhere in Judah.
He did what was good, right, and faithful before the LORD his God.
[21] He started many works in the service of God’s house,
for the law and for the commandments.
In everything, he wanted to seek his God.
He did it with all his heart, and he succeeded.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the most beautiful praise that Chronicles can give a king: he sought God “with all his heart.” And he succeeded.
> Compare Amaziah, who did what was right, “but not with a perfect heart” (2 Chronicles 25:2).
