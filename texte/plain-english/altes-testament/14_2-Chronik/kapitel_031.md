# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 31
### The people give with open hands

---

## The altars of the idols are removed (Verse 1)

[1] When all this was finished,
all Israel who were there went out to the cities of Judah.
They broke the stone pillars in pieces,
cut down the Asherah poles,
and broke down the high places and the altars
out of all Judah and Benjamin, and also in Ephraim and Manasseh,
until they had destroyed them all.
Then all the children of Israel returned to their own cities,
every man to his own property.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the Passover, the people themselves clean up. It is not ordered from above, but comes from their own heart. And it also happens in the north, in Ephraim and Manasseh.

---

## Order for the priests and Levites (Verses 2–4)

[2] Hezekiah appointed the divisions of the priests and the Levites by their divisions,
every man according to his service,
both the priests and the Levites,
for burnt offerings and for peace offerings,
to serve, to give thanks, and to praise in the gates of the LORD’s camp.
[3] He also gave the king’s share from his own property for the burnt offerings:
for the morning and evening burnt offerings,
and the burnt offerings for the Sabbaths, for the new moons, and for the set feasts,
as it is written in the LORD’s law.
[4] He also commanded the people who lived in Jerusalem
to give the share of the priests and the Levites,
so that they could give themselves to the LORD’s law.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the temple is called “the LORD’s camp”. This recalls the time in the wilderness, when the tabernacle stood in the middle of the camp.
> Hezekiah leads by good example: he himself gives from his property for the offerings.
> The priests and Levites should not have to worry about earning a living. They should have time to learn and to teach God’s law.

---

## The great heaps (Verses 5–10)

[5] As soon as the command went out,
the children of Israel gave very much of the first fruits
of grain, new wine, oil, honey, and of all the harvest of the field.
And they brought in a rich tithe of everything.
[6] The children of Israel and Judah who lived in the cities of Judah
also brought in the tithe of cattle and sheep,
and the tithe of the holy things that were dedicated to the LORD their God,
and they laid them in heaps.
[7] In the third month they began to lay the heaps,
and they finished them in the seventh month.
[8] When Hezekiah and the princes came and saw the heaps,
they blessed the LORD and his people Israel.
[9] Then Hezekiah asked the priests and the Levites about the heaps.
[10] Azariah the chief priest, of the house of Zadok, answered him,
“Since people began to bring the offerings into the LORD’s house,
we have eaten and had enough,
and we have plenty left over.
For the LORD has blessed his people.
And what is left over is this great store.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “First fruits” are the first fruits of the harvest. The “tithe” is a tenth of the harvest. People gave both to God, as thanks for the harvest (compare Numbers 18; Deuteronomy 14:22–29).
> From the third month (grain harvest) to the seventh month (fruit and grape harvest) the heaps grow. There is more than enough.
> Remarkable in verse 8: Hezekiah praises not only God, but also the people. He sees how generous the people are.
> Verse 10 shows a principle: whoever gives generously does not become poorer. God blesses. (Compare Malachi 3:10.)

## Faithful managers (Verses 11–19)

[11] Then Hezekiah commanded them to prepare storerooms in the LORD’s house,
and they prepared them.
[12] They brought in the offerings, the tithes, and the holy things faithfully.
Conaniah the Levite was in charge of them,
and Shimei his brother was second.
[13] Jehiel, Azaziah, Nahath, Asahel, Jerimoth, Jozabad, Eliel, Ismachiah, Mahath, and Benaiah
were overseers under Conaniah and Shimei his brother.
King Hezekiah and Azariah, the ruler of God’s house, had appointed them.
[14] Kore the son of Imnah the Levite, the gatekeeper at the east gate,
was over the freewill offerings to God,
to hand out the LORD’s offerings and the most holy things.
[15] Under him were Eden, Miniamin, Jeshua, Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah,
in the cities of the priests, in their position of trust.
They were to give to their brothers by divisions,
to the great as well as to the small.
[16] They gave besides to those males who were listed in the family lists,
from three years old and upward,
to everyone who came into the LORD’s house,
as the duty of every day required,
for their service in their duties by their divisions.
[17] They gave to the priests who were listed in the family lists by their families,
and to the Levites from 20 years old and upward,
in their duties by their divisions.
[18] They gave to all who were listed in the family lists:
all their little ones, their wives, their sons, and their daughters,
throughout the whole community.
For in their position of trust they kept themselves holy.
[19] Also for the sons of Aaron, the priests,
who lived in the fields of the pasture lands of their cities,
in every city there were men who were named
to give shares to all the males among the priests
and to all who were listed in the family lists among the Levites.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many goods are involved. That is why honest and faithful managers are needed. Chronicles names them all. The words “faithfully” and “position of trust” appear several times.
> The gifts were shared out fairly: “to the great as well as to the small”. Even the priests who lived far away in the country received their share. And the families, wives and children were provided for too.
> “From three years old”: even the little boys of the priestly families received a share. Some experts think that it originally said “thirty years”, as in 1 Chronicles 23:3.
> These verses are hard to understand in Hebrew. But the meaning is clear: no one was forgotten.

## Hezekiah did everything with all his heart (Verses 20–21)

[20] Hezekiah did this throughout all Judah.
He did what was good, right, and faithful before the LORD his God.
[21] In every work that he began
in the service of God’s house, in the law, and in the commandments,
to seek his God,
he did it with all his heart, and he succeeded.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the most beautiful praise that Chronicles can give a king: he sought God “with all his heart”. And he succeeded.
> Compare Amaziah, who did what was right, “but not with a perfect heart” (Chapter 25:2).
