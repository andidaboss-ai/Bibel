# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 2
### Solomon and King Huram of Tyre

---

## Solomon plans the building (Verses 1–2)

[1] Solomon decided to build a house for the LORD’s name,
and a house for his kingdom.
[2] Solomon counted out 70,000 men to carry loads,
80,000 men who cut stone in the mountains,
and 3,600 to oversee them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon builds two houses: the temple for God and a palace for himself.
> The numbers of workers are large. Verse 17 says who these people were: foreigners who lived in the land. For them it was hard forced labor.
> In some Bibles verse 1 here is chapter 1, verse 18. And all the following verses of this chapter have a number one less there: verse 2 here is verse 1 there, and so on up to verse 18 here, which is verse 17 there.

---

## Solomon’s letter to Huram (Verses 3–10)

[3] Solomon sent word to Huram the king of Tyre:
“You helped David my father.
You sent him cedars, so that he could build himself a house to live in.
Do the same for me.
[4] Look, I am about to build a house for the name of the LORD my God,
to dedicate it to him,
to burn sweet-smelling incense before him,
for the show bread that is always there,
and for the burnt offerings morning and evening,
on the Sabbaths, on the new moons, and on the set feasts of the LORD our God.
This is a rule forever for Israel.
[5] “The house that I am building will be great,
for our God is greater than all gods.
[6] But who is able to build him a house?
Heaven and the heaven of heavens can’t contain him.
Who am I, then, that I should build him a house?
Only to burn incense before him.
[7] “Now send me a man who is skilled
in working with gold, silver, bronze, and iron,
and with purple, crimson, and blue,
and who knows how to engrave,
to be with the skilled men who are with me in Judah and in Jerusalem,
whom David my father provided.
[8] “Also send me cedar trees, cypress trees, and algum trees from Lebanon.
For I know that your servants know how to cut timber in Lebanon.
Look, my servants will be with your servants,
[9] to prepare timber in great amounts for me.
For the house that I am about to build will be great and wonderful.
[10] Look, I will give to your servants, the men who cut the timber,
about 120,000 bushels of beaten wheat,
about 120,000 gallons of barley,
about 120,000 gallons of wine,
and about 120,000 gallons of oil.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Huram is the same as Hiram in the books of Kings. He was king of the Phoenicians in Tyre.
> Solomon explains to a foreign king what the temple is for: for prayer, incense, offerings and feasts.
> Verse 6 is remarkable: Solomon knows that God is much too great for a house. “Heaven and the heaven of heavens can’t contain him.” The temple is not God’s home in a narrow sense, but a place where people can meet God and honor him.
> Purple and crimson were expensive colors for cloth. The Phoenicians were famous for their purple.
> “Algum” was a precious wood, maybe red sandalwood.
> The Bible says “20,000 cors” of wheat and “20,000 baths” of barley, wine and oil. A cor is about 6 bushels, a bath about 6 gallons. 1 Kings 5:11 gives other amounts.

## Huram’s answer (Verses 11–16)

[11] Then Huram the king of Tyre answered in a letter, which he sent to Solomon:
“Because the LORD loves his people, he has made you king over them.”
[12] Huram went on:
“Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
who made heaven and earth,
who has given David the king a wise son,
full of good sense and understanding,
who will build a house for the LORD and a house for his kingdom.
[13] Now I have sent a skilled man, full of understanding,
Huram-abi,
[14] the son of a woman of the daughters of Dan.
His father was a man of Tyre.
He is skilled in working with gold, silver, bronze, iron, stone, and timber,
with purple, blue, fine linen, and crimson.
He can also engrave any kind of engraving
and work out any design that is given to him,
together with your skilled men
and the skilled men of my lord David your father.
[15] “Now let my lord send to his servants the wheat, the barley, the oil, and the wine
that he has spoken of.
[16] And we will cut wood from Lebanon, as much as you need.
We will bring it to you on rafts by sea to Joppa.
Then you shall carry it up to Jerusalem.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A pagan king praises the God of Israel as the Creator of heaven and earth. Chronicles shows: other peoples also see God’s greatness.
> “Because the LORD loves his people”: a good king is a gift of God for the people.
> Huram-abi is a man of mixed origin: his mother was an Israelite from the tribe of Dan, his father a Phoenician. And he becomes the most important artist in building the temple. 1 Kings 7:14 says that his mother was a widow from the tribe of Naphtali.
> Joppa is today Jaffa, a part of Tel Aviv. From there the wood was brought about 40 miles uphill to Jerusalem.

## The foreigners as workers (Verses 17–18)

[17] Solomon counted all the foreigners who lived in the land of Israel,
following the census with which David his father had counted them.
They found 153,600.
[18] He set 70,000 of them to carry loads,
80,000 to cut stone in the mountains,
and 3,600 as overseers to give the people their work.

> **What does this mean?**
> The foreigners, that is, people from the earlier peoples of Canaan, had to do forced labor for the building. For them this was a hard fate. The Bible tells it honestly.
> God’s law actually demanded that foreigners be treated well. About the foreigner it says: “You shall love him as yourself” (Leviticus 19:34). The splendid temple was also built on the hard work of these people. That is part of the truth.
