# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 19
### Jehoshaphat appoints judges

---

## Jehu the seer rebukes Jehoshaphat (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, came back safely to his palace in Jerusalem.
[2] Jehu, the son of Hanani the seer, went out to meet him.
He said to King Jehoshaphat,
“Should you help evil people?
Should you love those who hate the LORD?
Because of this, the LORD is angry with you.
[3] But there are also good things in you.
You have removed the Asheroth from the land.
And you have set your heart to seek God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat has just escaped death (Chapter 18). Now the prophet Jehu tells him clearly: your alliance with Ahab was wrong.
> Jehu was the son of Hanani, who had rebuked Asa (Chapter 16:7). Father and son were both brave prophets.
> Verse 3 is beautiful: God does not only see the mistake. He also sees the good. Jehoshaphat has a heart that seeks God.
> “Asheroth” is the plural of Asherah. It means the Asherah poles.

---

## Jehoshaphat leads the people back (Verses 4–7)

[4] Jehoshaphat lived in Jerusalem.
And he went out again among the people,
from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim.
He brought them back to the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
[5] He appointed judges in the land,
in all the fortified cities of Judah, in every city.
[6] He said to the judges,
“Think carefully about what you do.
You are not judging for people, but for the LORD.
He is with you when you give a judgment.
[7] So now, have respect and fear for the LORD.
Be careful in what you do.
The LORD our God is never unfair.
With him there is no favoring of anyone, and no taking of bribes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat accepts the rebuke. He himself travels through the whole land, from the south (Beersheba) to the north (Ephraim), and calls the people back to God.
> Then he makes sure there are fair courts. His words to the judges are still important today:
> – A judge does not judge only before people, but before God.
> – No “favoring of anyone”: rich and poor, powerful and weak must be treated the same.
> – No bribes.
> Jehoshaphat’s name means “The LORD judges”. He puts into practice what his name says.

---

## A highest court in Jerusalem (Verses 8–11)

[8] In Jerusalem, too, Jehoshaphat appointed some Levites, priests,
and heads of the families of Israel.
They were to judge for the LORD and settle quarrels.
They returned to Jerusalem.
[9] He gave them this order:
“Do this with respect and fear for the LORD.
Be faithful, and serve with your whole heart.
[10] Your brothers who live in their cities will bring cases to you.
It may be a case of blood and blood.
Or it may be a question about the law, a commandment, rules, or laws.
In every case you must warn them,
so that they do not become guilty before the LORD.
Otherwise the LORD’s anger will come on you and on your brothers.
Do this, and you will not be guilty.
[11] Look, Amariah, the chief priest, will be over you in all matters of the LORD.
Zebadiah, the son of Ishmael, the leader of the tribe of Judah,
will be over you in all the king’s matters.
The Levites will serve you as officials.
Be brave and do your work.
May the LORD be with the good!”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Jerusalem there is now a highest court for difficult cases (compare Deuteronomy 17:8–13).
> Verse 10: “Blood and blood” means a case where someone was killed: was it on purpose or an accident?
> It is remarkable that there are two areas. The chief priest is responsible for religious questions, a prince for matters of the state. This is an early form of the separation of powers.
> “May the LORD be with the good!”: God is on the side of those who judge fairly.
