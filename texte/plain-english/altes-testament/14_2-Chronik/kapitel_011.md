# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 11
### Rehoboam, king of Judah

---

## No war between brothers (Verses 1–4)

[1] Rehoboam came to Jerusalem.
He gathered the house of Judah and Benjamin,
180,000 chosen men who were warriors,
to fight against Israel,
to bring the kingdom back to Rehoboam.
[2] But the LORD’s word came to Shemaiah the man of God:
[3] “Speak to Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah,
and to all Israel in Judah and Benjamin.
Say,
[4] ‘The LORD says,
“You shall not go up and fight against your brothers!
Every man, return to his house,
for this thing comes from me.”’”
So they listened to the LORD’s words,
and they turned back from going against Jeroboam.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam wants to bring back unity by force. But God says through the prophet Shemaiah: do not fight against your brothers!
> Rehoboam listens to God. A civil war is prevented. Even though the kingdom is divided, the people remain “brothers”.

---

## Fortresses in Judah (Verses 5–12)

[5] Rehoboam lived in Jerusalem
and built cities for defense in Judah.
[6] He built Bethlehem, Etam, Tekoa,
[7] Beth Zur, Soco, Adullam,
[8] Gath, Mareshah, Ziph,
[9] Adoraim, Lachish, Azekah,
[10] Zorah, Aijalon, and Hebron.
These are fortified cities in Judah and in Benjamin.
[11] He made the strongholds strong
and put captains in them, with stores of food, oil, and wine.
[12] He put shields and spears in every city
and made them very strong.
Judah and Benjamin belonged to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam builds a belt of fortresses around Judah, above all in the south and west, against Egypt and the Philistines.
> This list is only in Chronicles. Many of these places, for example Lachish and Azekah, have been dug up by archaeologists.

---

## Priests and Levites come to Judah (Verses 13–17)

[13] The priests and the Levites who were in all Israel
came to him from all their territory and stood with him.
[14] For the Levites left their pasture lands and their property
and came to Judah and Jerusalem.
For Jeroboam and his sons had thrown them out,
so that they could not serve as priests to the LORD.
[15] Jeroboam himself appointed priests for the high places,
for the male goat idols and calf idols that he had made.
[16] After them, from all the tribes of Israel,
those who set their hearts to seek the LORD, the God of Israel,
came to Jerusalem to sacrifice to the LORD, the God of their fathers.
[17] So they made the kingdom of Judah strong
and made Rehoboam the son of Solomon strong for three years.
For they walked for three years in the way of David and Solomon.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the north, Jeroboam set up golden calves and appointed his own priests (1 Kings 12:28–31). The true priests and Levites lost their work.
> That is why they moved to Judah, and with them many people from the north, “those who set their hearts to seek the LORD”. They gave up their home in order to stay faithful to God.
> Chronicles shows: in the north, too, there were people who sought God. The true Israel are those who seek God, no matter from which tribe.
> “Male goat idols”: literally “goats”, probably demons or idols in the shape of goats (compare Leviticus 17:7).
> “For three years”: this good time did not last long (Chapter 12:1).

## Rehoboam’s family (Verses 18–23)

[18] Rehoboam took a wife for himself:
Mahalath, the daughter of Jerimoth the son of David,
and of Abihail the daughter of Eliab the son of Jesse.
[19] She gave birth to sons for him: Jeush, Shemariah, and Zaham.
[20] After her he took Maacah the granddaughter of Absalom.
She gave birth to Abijah, Attai, Ziza, and Shelomith for him.
[21] Rehoboam loved Maacah the granddaughter of Absalom more than all his wives and his concubines.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
For he took 18 wives and 60 concubines,
and he became the father of 28 sons and 60 daughters.
[22] Rehoboam made Abijah the son of Maacah the chief,
the prince among his brothers,
for he planned to make him king.
[23] He acted wisely.
He spread some of his sons throughout all the lands of Judah and Benjamin,
to every fortified city.
He gave them plenty of food,
and he looked for many wives for them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam married relatives from David’s family. Mahalath and Maacah were both descendants of David or Jesse.
> Like his father Solomon, Rehoboam had many wives. The law warned against this (Deuteronomy 17:17).
> Here Maacah is called the “granddaughter” of Absalom. The Hebrew says “daughter”, which can also mean “granddaughter”. In Chapter 13:2 she is called “Micaiah the daughter of Uriel”. Maybe Uriel was her father and Absalom her grandfather.
> Rehoboam placed his sons as commanders in the fortresses. That way they had tasks and did not fight over the throne. That was wise.
