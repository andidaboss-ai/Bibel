# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 11
### Rehoboam, king of Judah

---

## No war between brothers (Verses 1–4)

[1] Rehoboam came to Jerusalem.
He called together the people of Judah and Benjamin,
180,000 of the best soldiers.
They were to fight against Israel
and win the kingdom back for Rehoboam.
[2] But the LORD spoke to Shemaiah, the man of God.
He said,
[3] “Speak to Rehoboam, the son of Solomon, the king of Judah.
And speak to all the Israelites in Judah and Benjamin.
Tell them,
[4] ‘This is what the LORD says:
“Do not go up and fight against your brothers!
Every one of you, go back home.
I have made this happen.”’”
So they listened to the LORD’s words.
They turned back and did not go against Jeroboam.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam wants to bring back unity by force. But God says through the prophet Shemaiah: do not fight against your brothers!
> Rehoboam listens to God. A civil war is prevented. Even though the kingdom is divided, the people remain “brothers”.

---

## Fortresses in Judah (Verses 5–12)

[5] Rehoboam lived in Jerusalem.
He built up cities in Judah to protect the land.
[6] He built up Bethlehem, Etam, Tekoa,
[7] Beth Zur, Soco, Adullam,
[8] Gath, Mareshah, Ziph,
[9] Adoraim, Lachish, Azekah,
[10] Zorah, Aijalon, and Hebron.
These were strong cities in Judah and in Benjamin.
[11] He made the fortresses strong.
He put commanders in them.
He stored food, olive oil, and wine there.
[12] He put shields and spears in every city.
He made the cities very strong.
So Judah and Benjamin belonged to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam builds a belt of fortresses around Judah, above all in the south and west, against Egypt and the Philistines.
> This list is only in Chronicles. Many of these places, for example Lachish and Azekah, have been dug up by archaeologists.

---

## Priests and Levites come to Judah (Verses 13–17)

[13] The priests and the Levites from all over Israel
came from all their lands and stood on his side.
[14] The Levites left their pastures and their property.
They came to Judah and Jerusalem.
Jeroboam and his sons had thrown them out.
They did not let them serve the LORD as priests.
[15] Jeroboam chose his own priests for the high places.
They served the goat idols and the calf idols that he had made.
[16] After the Levites, people came from all the tribes of Israel.
These were the people who had set their hearts to seek the LORD, the God of Israel.
They came to Jerusalem to sacrifice to the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
[17] So they made the kingdom of Judah strong.
For three years they supported Rehoboam, the son of Solomon.
For three years they lived the way David and Solomon had lived.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the north, Jeroboam set up golden calves and appointed his own priests (1 Kings 12:28–31). The true priests and Levites lost their work.
> That is why they moved to Judah, and with them many people from the north, the people “who had set their hearts to seek the LORD”. They gave up their home in order to stay faithful to God.
> Chronicles shows: in the north, too, there were people who sought God. The true Israel are those who seek God, no matter from which tribe.
> “Goat idols”: literally “goats”, probably demons or idols in the shape of goats (compare Leviticus 17:7).
> “For three years”: this good time did not last long (Chapter 12:1).

## Rehoboam’s family (Verses 18–23)

[18] Rehoboam married Mahalath.
She was the daughter of Jerimoth, the son of David.
Her mother was Abihail, the daughter of Eliab, the son of Jesse.
[19] Mahalath had sons with him: Jeush, Shemariah, and Zaham.
[20] After her, he married Maacah, the granddaughter of Absalom.
She had these children with him: Abijah, Attai, Ziza, and Shelomith.
[21] Rehoboam loved Maacah, the granddaughter of Absalom, more than all his other wives and concubines.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
He had 18 wives and 60 concubines.
He had 28 sons and 60 daughters.
[22] Rehoboam made Abijah, the son of Maacah, the leader among his brothers.
He wanted to make him king.
[23] Rehoboam acted wisely.
He sent some of his sons to all the areas of Judah and Benjamin,
to every fortified city.
He gave them plenty of food.
And he found many wives for them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rehoboam married relatives from David’s family. Mahalath and Maacah were both descendants of David or Jesse.
> Like his father Solomon, Rehoboam had many wives. The law warned against this (Deuteronomy 17:17).
> Here Maacah is called the “granddaughter” of Absalom. The Hebrew says “daughter”, which can also mean “granddaughter”. In Chapter 13:2 she is called “Micaiah the daughter of Uriel”. Maybe Uriel was her father and Absalom her grandfather.
> Rehoboam placed his sons as commanders in the fortresses. That way they had tasks and did not fight over the throne. That was wise.
