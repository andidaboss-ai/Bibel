# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 10
### The kingdom is divided

---

## The people ask for relief (Verses 1–5)

[1] Rehoboam went to Shechem,
for all Israel had come to Shechem to make him king.
[2] Jeroboam the son of Nebat heard about it.
He was in Egypt, where he had fled from King Solomon.
So Jeroboam returned out of Egypt.
[3] They sent for him and called him.
And Jeroboam and all Israel came and spoke to Rehoboam:
[4] “Your father made our yoke hard to bear.
Now make the hard service of your father lighter,
and the heavy yoke that he put on us,
and we will serve you.”
[5] He said to them,
“Come back to me after three days.”
So the people left.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is almost the same in 1 Kings 12.
> Shechem was in the north. The northern tribes did not want to simply accept Rehoboam as king. They wanted to negotiate.
> A “yoke” is a wooden beam that is used to hitch animals to a plow. It is a picture of burden and oppression. The people suffered under Solomon’s high taxes and the forced labor.
> Chronicles does not tell who Jeroboam was. It expects the readers to know 1 Kings 11: a prophet had announced to Jeroboam that he would become king over the ten northern tribes.

---

## The advice of the old men and the advice of the young men (Verses 6–15)

[6] King Rehoboam asked the old men for advice.
They had served Solomon his father while he was still alive.
He said,
“What advice do you give me? How should I answer these people?”
[7] They said to him,
“If you are kind to these people, please them, and speak good words to them,
then they will be your servants forever.”
[8] But he rejected the advice that the old men had given him.
He asked for advice from the young men who had grown up with him
and who served him.
[9] He said to them,
“What advice do you give?
How should we answer these people who have said to me,
‘Make the yoke that your father put on us lighter’?”
[10] The young men who had grown up with him said to him,
“The people said to you,
‘Your father made our yoke heavy, but make it lighter for us.’
Tell them this:
‘My little finger is thicker than my father’s waist.
[11] My father put a heavy yoke on you,
but I will add to your yoke.
My father punished you with whips,
but I will punish you with scorpions.’”
[12] So Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day,
as the king had said: “Come to me again on the third day.”
[13] The king answered them harshly.
King Rehoboam rejected the advice of the old men,
[14] and spoke to them as the young men had advised:
“My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add to it.
My father punished you with whips,
but I will punish you with scorpions.”
[15] So the king didn’t listen to the people.
For God brought this about,
so that the LORD might make his word come true,
which he spoke through Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam the son of Nebat.

> **What does this mean?**
> The old men give wise advice: be kind to the people, then they will serve you. 1 Kings 12:7 says it even more clearly: “If you will be a servant to this people today …”
> The young men grew up in the palace. They do not know the hard life of the people. They advise him to be harsh and to boast.
> “Scorpions” were maybe whips with spikes.
> Verse 15: God brought this about. That does not mean that Rehoboam was not guilty. He made his choice himself. But God also works through the mistakes of people.

---

## The northern tribes break away (Verses 16–19)

[16] All Israel saw that the king didn’t listen to them.
So the people answered the king,
“What share do we have in David?
We have no inheritance in the son of Jesse!
Every man to your tents, Israel!
Now take care of your own house, David.”
So all Israel went away to their tents.
[17] But Rehoboam reigned over the children of Israel
who lived in the cities of Judah.
[18] Then King Rehoboam sent Hadoram, who was over the men who had to do forced labor.
And the children of Israel stoned him to death with stones.
King Rehoboam quickly got up into his chariot
to flee to Jerusalem.
[19] So Israel rebelled against David’s house to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> From now on there are two kingdoms: in the north Israel with ten tribes, in the south Judah with Jerusalem. That happened around 930 before Christ.
> Of all people, Rehoboam sends the chief of the forced labor to the angry people. That was very unwise. In 1 Kings 12:18 Hadoram is called “Adoram”.
> After this, Chronicles tells almost only about Judah. The northern kingdom only appears when it has to do with Judah. But for Chronicles the north also remains part of God’s people: “Israel”.
