# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 16
### Asa’s last years

---

## Asa buys help from the Syrians (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the thirty-sixth year of Asa’s reign,
Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah.
He built up Ramah,
so that he would not let anyone go out or come in to Asa king of Judah.
[2] Then Asa brought out silver and gold
from the treasures of the LORD’s house and of the king’s house.
He sent it to Ben Hadad king of Syria, who lived in Damascus, with this message:
[3] “Let there be a treaty between me and you,
as there was between my father and your father.
Look, I have sent you silver and gold.
Go, break your treaty with Baasha king of Israel,
so that he will go away from me.”
[4] Ben Hadad listened to King Asa.
He sent the captains of his armies against the cities of Israel.
They struck Ijon, Dan, Abel Maim,
and all the storage cities of Naphtali.
[5] When Baasha heard of it,
he stopped building Ramah
and let his work stop.
[6] Then King Asa took all Judah,
and they carried away the stones and timber of Ramah,
with which Baasha had been building.
And with them he built Geba and Mizpah.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 1 Kings 15:16–22.
> Ramah was only about 5 miles north of Jerusalem. If Baasha fortified Ramah, Jerusalem was cut off from the north.
> In Hebrew, Syria is called “Aram”. Damascus was its capital.
> The number “thirty-sixth year” is difficult. According to 1 Kings 16:8, Baasha was already dead by then. Many experts think that the years here are counted from the division of the kingdom, not the years of Asa. Then it would be about Asa’s sixteenth year.
> In 1 Kings 15:20 Abel Maim is called “Abel Beth Maacah”.
> Politically, Asa’s plan was a success. But for it he even gave away the temple treasure, and he relied on a foreign king, not on God.

## Hanani the seer rebukes Asa (Verses 7–10)

[7] At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah and said to him,
“You have relied on the king of Syria,
and you have not relied on the LORD your God.
That is why the army of the king of Syria has escaped out of your hand.
[8] Weren’t the Ethiopians and the Lubim a huge army,
with chariots and very many horsemen?
Yet because you relied on the LORD,
he gave them into your hand.
[9] For the LORD’s eyes run back and forth throughout the whole earth,
to show himself strong for those whose heart is perfect toward him.
You have acted foolishly in this.
For from now on you will have wars.”
[10] Then Asa was angry with the seer
and put him in prison,
for he was furious with him because of this.
At the same time Asa oppressed some of the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hanani reminds Asa of Chapter 14: back then the army was much larger, and Asa trusted God. Now the danger was smaller, but Asa no longer asked God.
> Verse 9 is a wonderful sentence: God looks over the whole earth. He looks for people who give him their whole heart, so that he can help them. God is not looking for mistakes, but for chances to help.
> Asa’s reaction is sad. Instead of turning back, he becomes angry and locks up the prophet. And he oppresses people. The king who was once so faithful has become hard in his old age.
> “Ethiopians”: the Hebrew says “Cushites” (see Chapter 14:9).

## Asa’s illness and death (Verses 11–14)

[11] Look, the acts of Asa, from first to last,
are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.
[12] In the thirty-ninth year of his reign,
Asa became sick in his feet.
His disease was very severe.
Yet in his disease he didn’t seek the LORD,
but only the physicians.
[13] Asa died and joined his fathers.
He died in the forty-first year of his reign.
[14] They buried him in his own tomb,
which he had dug out for himself in David’s city.
They laid him on a bed that was filled with sweet smells
and with many kinds of spices, prepared by the art of the perfume makers.
And they made a very great fire in his honor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 does not mean that it is wrong to go to the doctor! The Bible respects doctors (for example Sirach 38; Luke was a doctor, Colossians 4:14). The accusation is: Asa sought “only” the physicians and no longer God at all. He had pushed God completely out of his life.
> Today Jews and Christians say: go to the doctor, and pray as well. Both belong together.
> The “very great fire” was not a burning of the dead body. It was a fire of honor, in which spices were burned in honor of the king (compare Jeremiah 34:5).
> In spite of his late mistakes, Asa is honored. All in all he was a good king (Chapter 15:17).
