# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 16
### Asa’s last years

---

## Asa buys help from the Syrians (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the thirty-sixth year that Asa was king,
Baasha, the king of Israel, attacked Judah.
He built up the town of Ramah into a fortress.
He wanted to stop anyone from going out to Asa, the king of Judah, or coming in to him.
[2] Then Asa took silver and gold
from the treasures of the LORD’s house and of the king’s palace.
He sent it to Ben Hadad, the king of Syria, who lived in Damascus.
He sent this message:
[3] “Let us make a treaty, you and I,
as there was between my father and your father.
Look, I am sending you silver and gold.
Go and break your treaty with Baasha, the king of Israel.
Then he will leave me alone.”
[4] Ben Hadad agreed with King Asa.
He sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel.
They attacked Ijon, Dan, Abel Maim,
and all the storage cities of Naphtali.
[5] When Baasha heard about it,
he stopped building up Ramah.
He gave up his work.
[6] Then King Asa brought all the people of Judah.
They carried away the stones and the wood
that Baasha had used to build Ramah.
With them Asa built up Geba and Mizpah.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 1 Kings 15:16–22.
> Ramah was only about 5 miles north of Jerusalem. If Baasha fortified Ramah, Jerusalem was cut off from the north.
> In Hebrew, Syria is called “Aram”. Damascus was its capital.
> The number “thirty-sixth year” is difficult. According to 1 Kings 16:8, Baasha was already dead by then. Many experts think that the years here are counted from the division of the kingdom, not the years of Asa. Then it would be about Asa’s sixteenth year.
> In 1 Kings 15:20 Abel Maim is called “Abel Beth Maacah”.
> Politically, Asa’s plan was a success. But for it he even gave away the temple treasure, and he relied on a foreign king, not on God.

## Hanani the seer rebukes Asa (Verses 7–10)

[7] At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa, the king of Judah.
He said to him,
“You trusted in the king of Syria.
You did not trust in the LORD your God.
That is why the army of the king of Syria has escaped from you.
[8] The Ethiopians and the Lubim had a huge army,
with chariots and very many horsemen.
But you trusted in the LORD,
so he handed them over to you.
[9] The LORD’s eyes look everywhere over the whole earth.
He wants to show his strength to help those whose hearts are completely faithful to him.
You have acted foolishly in this.
From now on you will have wars.”
[10] Then Asa was angry with the seer.
He was so furious about this that he put him in prison.
At the same time Asa treated some of the people cruelly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hanani reminds Asa of Chapter 14: back then the army was much larger, and Asa trusted God. Now the danger was smaller, but Asa no longer asked God.
> Verse 9 is a wonderful sentence: God looks over the whole earth. He looks for people whose hearts are completely faithful to him, so that he can help them. God is not looking for mistakes, but for chances to help.
> Asa’s reaction is sad. Instead of turning back, he becomes angry and locks up the prophet. And he oppresses people. The king who was once so faithful has become hard in his old age.
> “Ethiopians”: the Hebrew says “Cushites” (see 2 Chronicles 14:9).

---

## Asa’s illness and death (Verses 11–14)

[11] Everything Asa did, from first to last,
is written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.
[12] In the thirty-ninth year that Asa was king,
he got a disease in his feet.
His disease was very bad.
But even in his illness he did not seek the LORD.
He only went to the doctors.
[13] Then Asa died and joined his ancestors.
He died in the forty-first year that he was king.
[14] They buried him in the tomb that he had cut out for himself in the City of David.
They laid him on a bed full of sweet perfumes
and many kinds of spices.
Perfume makers had prepared them with great skill.
And they made a huge fire in his honor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 does not mean that it is wrong to go to the doctor! The Bible respects doctors (for example Sirach 38; Luke was a doctor, Colossians 4:14). The accusation is: Asa “only” went to the doctors and no longer sought God at all. He had pushed God completely out of his life.
> Today Jews and Christians say: go to the doctor, and pray as well. Both belong together.
> The “huge fire” was not a burning of the dead body. It was a fire of honor, in which spices were burned in honor of the king (compare Jeremiah 34:5).
> In spite of his late mistakes, Asa is honored. All in all he was a good king (2 Chronicles 15:17).
