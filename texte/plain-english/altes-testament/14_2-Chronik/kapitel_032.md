# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 32
### Sennacherib threatens Jerusalem

---

## Hezekiah gets ready (Verses 1–8)

[1] After these things and this faithfulness,
Sennacherib king of Assyria came and entered Judah.
He set up camp against the fortified cities
and planned to take them for himself.
[2] Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come,
and that he was planning to fight against Jerusalem.
[3] Then he talked with his princes and his mighty men
about stopping up the waters of the springs that were outside the city.
And they helped him.
[4] So many people gathered together.
They stopped up all the springs
and the brook that flowed through the middle of the land.
They said,
“Why should the kings of Assyria come and find plenty of water?”
[5] He took courage.
He built up all the wall that was broken down
and raised it up to the towers,
with the other wall outside.
He made the Millo in David’s city strong,
and he made very many weapons and shields.
[6] He set captains of war over the people.
He gathered them to him in the open square at the city gate
and spoke encouraging words to them:
[7] “Be strong and brave.
Don’t be afraid or lose heart because of the king of Assyria,
or because of all the great crowd that is with him.
For there is a greater one with us than with him.
[8] With him is an arm of flesh,
but with us is the LORD our God, to help us and to fight our battles.”
The people relied on the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is told in more detail in 2 Kings 18–19 and Isaiah 36–37. Sennacherib marched against Judah in the year 701 before Christ. His own inscriptions report this. There he boasts that he shut Hezekiah up in Jerusalem “like a bird in a cage”. But he does not report a conquest of Jerusalem.
> Verse 1 begins in a surprising way: “After these things and this faithfulness” comes the attack. Faithfulness to God does not protect us from all difficulties. But God is there in the difficulties.
> Hezekiah acts wisely: he makes sure that the enemies find no water, and he builds up the walls. Faith and wise action belong together.
> The “Millo” was a fortification in the City of David, maybe a terrace built up with earth.
> Verses 7–8 are a great sentence of faith: “There is a greater one with us than with him.” An “arm of flesh” is human power, which does not last. Compare Jeremiah 17:5–7 and 1 John 4:4.

## Sennacherib’s mockery (Verses 9–19)

[9] After this, Sennacherib king of Assyria sent his servants to Jerusalem.
At that time he was attacking Lachish,
and all his forces were with him.
He sent them to Hezekiah king of Judah
and to all Judah who were in Jerusalem, with this message:
[10] “Sennacherib king of Assyria says:
In whom do you trust,
that you stay in Jerusalem while it is surrounded?
[11] Isn’t Hezekiah talking you into letting yourselves die of hunger and thirst,
when he says,
‘The LORD our God will save us out of the hand of the king of Assyria’?
[12] Hasn’t this same Hezekiah taken away his high places and his altars?
Hasn’t he commanded Judah and Jerusalem,
‘You shall worship before one altar,
and you shall burn incense on it’?
[13] Don’t you know what I and my fathers have done
to all the peoples of the lands?
Were the gods of the nations of those lands in any way able
to save their land out of my hand?
[14] Who among all the gods of those nations that my fathers completely destroyed
was able to save his people out of my hand?
How then should your God be able to save you out of my hand?
[15] So now don’t let Hezekiah fool you or talk you into this.
Don’t believe him.
For no god of any nation or kingdom was able to save his people
out of my hand and out of the hand of my fathers.
How much less will your God save you out of my hand!”
[16] His servants spoke even more against the LORD God
and against his servant Hezekiah.
[17] He also wrote letters to insult the LORD, the God of Israel,
and to speak against him:
“The gods of the nations of the lands have not saved their people out of my hand.
In the same way the God of Hezekiah will not save his people out of my hand.”
[18] They called out with a loud voice in the Jews’ language
to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall,
to frighten them and to trouble them,
so that they might take the city.
[19] They spoke about the God of Jerusalem
as about the gods of the peoples of the earth,
which are the work of human hands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Lachish was the second most important city of Judah. Sennacherib had the conquest of Lachish shown on large stone pictures in his palace in Nineveh. Today these pictures can be seen in the British Museum in London.
> Sennacherib’s servants try to make the people unsure:
> – They twist Hezekiah’s reform: he supposedly destroyed God’s altars. But he had removed the altars of idols and gathered the worship in Jerusalem.
> – They say: no god has been able to help against us so far. Why should your God be able to?
> – They speak “in the Jews’ language”, that is, in Hebrew, so that everyone on the wall understands it and becomes afraid.
> Verse 19 shows their greatest mistake: they think the living God is an idol made by people.

## God saves Jerusalem (Verses 20–23)

[20] Because of this, King Hezekiah and Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz,
prayed and cried out to heaven.
[21] The LORD sent an angel,
who cut off all the mighty men of courage, the leaders, and the captains
in the camp of the king of Assyria.
So he returned with a face full of shame to his own land.
When he had come into the house of his god,
those who had come out of his own body killed him there with the sword.
[22] So the LORD saved Hezekiah and the people of Jerusalem
from the hand of Sennacherib the king of Assyria
and from the hand of all others,
and he guided them on every side.
[23] Many brought gifts to the LORD in Jerusalem,
and precious things to Hezekiah king of Judah.
So from then on he was honored in the sight of all nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah and Isaiah pray. And God steps in. Chronicles tells this very briefly. 2 Kings 19:35 says that in one night 185,000 men died in the camp of the Assyrians.
> “Those who had come out of his own body” means: his own sons. Sennacherib was murdered by sons of his about 20 years later, in 681 before Christ. Babylonian and Assyrian sources report this too. Chronicles sums it up without saying how much time passed in between.
> The irony: Sennacherib mocked that no god could save his followers. And then he is killed in the temple of his own god. His god could not protect him.
> “He guided them”: some manuscripts have “he gave them rest”.

## Hezekiah’s illness and pride (Verses 24–26)

[24] In those days Hezekiah became sick and was about to die.
He prayed to the LORD,
and the LORD spoke to him and gave him a sign.
[25] But Hezekiah didn’t give back thanks for the good that was done for him,
because his heart was lifted up in pride.
So anger came on him, and on Judah and Jerusalem.
[26] But Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart,
he and the people of Jerusalem.
So the LORD’s anger didn’t come on them in the days of Hezekiah.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is told in more detail in 2 Kings 20 and Isaiah 38. God gave Hezekiah 15 more years of life. The sign was that the shadow on the sundial went back.
> Even a king as good as Hezekiah becomes proud. No one is safe from this.
> But Hezekiah humbles himself. And God spares him. This is the great theme of Chronicles: whoever humbles himself finds grace (Chapter 7:14).

## Hezekiah’s riches and works (Verses 27–31)

[27] Hezekiah had very great riches and honor.
He made himself treasuries for silver, for gold, for precious stones,
for spices, for shields, and for all kinds of valuable objects,
[28] and also storehouses for the harvest of grain, new wine, and oil,
and stalls for all kinds of animals,
and pens for the flocks.
[29] He also built cities for himself,
and he had very many flocks and herds.
For God had given him very great possessions.
[30] This same Hezekiah also stopped up the upper spring of the waters of Gihon
and brought the water straight down on the west side of David’s city.
Hezekiah succeeded in all his works.
[31] But then the ambassadors of the princes of Babylon came.
They had been sent to him to ask about the wonder that had happened in the land.
Then God left him alone to test him,
so that he might know all that was in his heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 30 describes a great technical achievement: Hezekiah had a tunnel dug through the rock. He led the water of the Gihon spring from outside into the city. So Jerusalem had water during a siege. This “Hezekiah’s Tunnel” is more than 1,600 feet long, and people can still walk through it today. In 1880 an old inscription was found in it that describes how the two groups of workers met in the middle.
> Verse 31 refers to 2 Kings 20:12–19: Hezekiah proudly showed the ambassadors from Babylon all his treasures. Then the prophet Isaiah said that everything would one day be carried to Babylon.
> “God left him alone to test him”: God let him decide alone for a time. In this way it was to become visible what was in his heart. Sometimes it is only in a test that it shows how things really stand with us.
> “The wonder”: probably Hezekiah’s healing and the sign with the shadow.

## The death of Hezekiah (Verses 32–33)

[32] The rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his good deeds,
look, they are written in the vision of Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz,
in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.
[33] Hezekiah died and joined his ancestors,
and they buried him on the way up to the tombs of the sons of David.
All Judah and the people of Jerusalem honored him at his death.
And Manasseh his son reigned in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah was buried with great honor, quite differently from his father Ahaz.
> The “vision of Isaiah” is probably the book of the prophet Isaiah, which begins with these words (Isaiah 1:1).
