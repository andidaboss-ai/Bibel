# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 20
### The battle belongs to God

---

## A huge army is coming (Verses 1–4)

[1] After this, the Moabites and the Ammonites came to fight against Jehoshaphat.
Some of the Meunites came with them.
[2] Some people came and told Jehoshaphat,
“A huge army is coming against you from beyond the sea, from Syria.
Look, they are already in Hazazon Tamar.”
Hazazon Tamar is En Gedi.
[3] Jehoshaphat was afraid.
He decided to seek the LORD.
He announced a time of fasting for all of Judah.
Fasting means: not eating for a time, in order to pray.
[4] The people of Judah came together to ask the LORD for help.
They came from all the cities of Judah to seek the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moab and Ammon were east of the Dead Sea. The Meunites were probably a people from the area of Edom (Seir).
> “From beyond the sea” means the Dead Sea. “From Syria”: the Hebrew says “Aram”. Many experts think that “Edom” was meant at first. The two words look very similar in Hebrew. And later in the chapter the text speaks of Mount Seir, that is, Edom.
> En Gedi is an oasis on the west shore of the Dead Sea, only about 25 miles from Jerusalem. The enemy was already very close.
> Jehoshaphat is afraid. That is human. But he does not let fear freeze him. He turns to God.

## Jehoshaphat’s prayer (Verses 5–13)

[5] Jehoshaphat stood up in front of the people of Judah and Jerusalem.
They were gathered in the LORD’s house, in front of the new courtyard.
[6] He said,
“LORD, God of our ancestors,
you are God in heaven!
You rule over all the kingdoms of the nations!
Power and strength are in your hand.
No one can stand against you.
[7] Our God, you drove out the people who lived in this land before your people Israel.
You gave this land forever to the descendants of your friend Abraham.
[8] They have lived in it.
They have built a holy place in it for your name.
They said,
[9] ‘Disaster may come on us:
war, punishment, a deadly disease, or hunger.
Then we will stand in front of this house and before you,
because your name is in this house.
We will cry out to you in our trouble.
And you will hear us and save us.’
[10] Now look at the people of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir.
When Israel came out of the land of Egypt, you did not let Israel invade their lands.
So Israel turned away from them and did not destroy them.
[11] And look how they repay us now!
They are coming to drive us out of your land,
the land you gave us as our own.
[12] Our God, won’t you judge them?
We have no strength against this huge army that is attacking us.
We don’t know what to do,
but our eyes are on you.”
[13] All the men of Judah stood before the LORD,
with their little ones, their wives, and their children.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat’s prayer is an example of how to pray in times of need:
> – He remembers who God is: the ruler over all peoples.
> – He remembers what God has done before.
> – He reminds God of his promise when the temple was built (Chapters 6 and 7).
> – He says honestly: “We don’t know what to do.”
> – And then: “But our eyes are on you.”
> This last sentence has helped many people in situations with no way out.
> Here Abraham is called God’s “friend”. Isaiah 41:8 and James 2:23 call him that too.
> Verse 10 recalls Deuteronomy 2: God had forbidden Israel to attack Edom, Moab and Ammon.
> Verse 13 is moving: whole families stand before God, even the little children.

## God’s answer through Jahaziel (Verses 14–19)

[14] Then the LORD’s Spirit came on Jahaziel in the middle of the assembly.
Jahaziel was a Levite from the family of Asaph.
He was the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah.
[15] Jahaziel said,
“Listen, all you people of Judah and Jerusalem, and you, King Jehoshaphat!
This is what the LORD says to you:
‘Don’t be afraid. Don’t lose courage because of this huge army.
The battle is not yours, but God’s.
[16] Tomorrow, go down against them.
Look, they will come up the pass of Ziz.
You will find them at the end of the valley,
in front of the wilderness of Jeruel.
[17] You will not need to fight in this battle.
Take your positions and stand still.
Then you will see how the LORD saves you, Judah and Jerusalem.
Don’t be afraid. Don’t lose courage.
Go out against them tomorrow.
The LORD is with you.’”
[18] Jehoshaphat bowed down with his face to the ground.
And all the people of Judah and Jerusalem fell down before the LORD
and worshiped him.
[19] Then some of the Levites stood up, from the families of Kohath and of Korah.
They praised the LORD, the God of Israel,
with very loud voices.

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers through a Levite, a singer from the family of Asaph. He was not a well-known prophet. God can speak through anyone.
> “The battle is not yours, but God’s” is one of the best-known sentences of Chronicles. It recalls Moses at the Red Sea: “Stand still and see how the LORD will save you today” (Exodus 14:13).
> Even before anything has happened, they worship and praise God. They trust his word.
> The Korahites were singers at the temple. Several psalms come from them (for example Psalms 42–49).

## The army goes out singing (Verses 20–26)

[20] Early the next morning they got up
and went out into the wilderness of Tekoa.
As they were leaving, Jehoshaphat stood up and said,
“Listen to me, people of Judah and Jerusalem!
Believe in the LORD your God, and you will stand firm!
Believe his prophets, and you will succeed.”
[21] Jehoshaphat talked it over with the people.
Then he chose singers to sing to the LORD.
They were to praise him in holy clothes as they marched in front of the army.
They sang, “Give thanks to the LORD, for his loving kindness endures forever.”
[22] As soon as they began to sing and praise,
the LORD sent an ambush against the people of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir,
who had come to attack Judah.
And they were defeated.
[23] The people of Ammon and Moab attacked the people of Mount Seir.
They wanted to kill them and wipe them out completely.
When they had finished with the people of Seir,
they began to destroy each other.
[24] The men of Judah came to the place where they could look out over the wilderness.
They looked toward the huge army.
But they saw only dead bodies lying on the ground.
No one had escaped.
[25] Jehoshaphat and his people came to take the goods of the dead.
They found a great amount of riches,
and dead bodies with precious jewels on them.
They took these things off the dead for themselves, more than they could carry.
There was so much that it took them three days to collect it all.
[26] On the fourth day they gathered in the Valley of Beracah.
There they praised the LORD.
That is why this place is still called the “Valley of Beracah” today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20 sounds like Isaiah 7:9: “If you will not believe, surely you will not be established.” In Hebrew this is a play on words: “believe” and “stand firm” come from the same word (aman; the word “Amen” also comes from it).
> This is the most unusual battle order in the Bible: in front go not the soldiers, but the singers. They sing: “Give thanks to the LORD, for his loving kindness endures forever” (like Psalm 136).
> Judah does not fight at all. The enemies quarrel with each other and destroy each other. The text does not say exactly who laid the “ambush”. What matters is: God brought it about.
> “Beracah” means “blessing” or “praise”. The WEB, our English source text, says that they “blessed” the LORD there. The valley has this name because they praised God there. To this day there is a place with a similar name south of Bethlehem.
> Here, too: the Bible does not rejoice over the dead, but over the rescue. And the rescue did not come through weapons, but through trust and praise.

## Joy in Jerusalem (Verses 27–30)

[27] Then all the men of Judah and Jerusalem went back to Jerusalem with joy.
Jehoshaphat led them.
The LORD had made them glad, because he had saved them from their enemies.
[28] They came into Jerusalem and went to the LORD’s house.
They played stringed instruments, harps, and trumpets.
[29] All the kingdoms of the other lands heard how the LORD had fought against Israel’s enemies.
Then they were terrified of God.
[30] So Jehoshaphat’s kingdom had peace.
His God gave him rest on every side.

> **What does this mean?**
> It begins with prayer and fasting. It ends with music and joy in the temple. This is what it looks like when a people trusts God completely.

## Summary about Jehoshaphat (Verses 31–37)

[31] So Jehoshaphat ruled over Judah.
He was 35 years old when he became king.
He ruled in Jerusalem for 25 years.
His mother was Azubah, the daughter of Shilhi.
[32] He lived the way his father Asa had lived.
He did not turn away from it.
He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
[33] But the high places were not removed.
The people had still not set their hearts on the God of their ancestors.
[34] Everything else Jehoshaphat did, from first to last, is written down.
It is in the records of Jehu, the son of Hanani.
These records are part of the book of the kings of Israel.
[35] Later Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, made an alliance with Ahaziah, the king of Israel.
Ahaziah did very evil things.
[36] Jehoshaphat worked together with him to build ships to sail to Tarshish.
They built the ships in Ezion Geber.
[37] Then Eliezer, the son of Dodavahu, from Mareshah, spoke as a prophet against Jehoshaphat.
He said,
“You have joined with Ahaziah.
So the LORD will destroy what you have made.”
And the ships were wrecked.
They were never able to sail to Tarshish.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 33 seems to contradict Chapter 17:6, where Jehoshaphat removed the high places. Maybe he removed them, but the people built them up again. Verse 33 also says: the heart of the people was not yet completely with God. A king can remove altars, but he cannot change hearts.
> Jehoshaphat repeats his old mistake: again he makes an alliance with the godless royal house of Ahab. Ahaziah was Ahab’s son.
> “Ships to sail to Tarshish” were large ships for the open sea, for trade. Ezion Geber was a port on the Red Sea, near today’s Eilat.
> 1 Kings 22:48–49 tells the story a little differently: there Ahaziah wants to take part, and Jehoshaphat says no after the ships were wrecked. Maybe Jehoshaphat learned from the disaster.
