# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 24
### Joash – a good beginning and a terrible end

---

## Joash and Jehoiada (Verses 1–3)

[1] Joash was seven years old when he became king.
He ruled for 40 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Zibiah.
She came from Beersheba.
[2] Joash did what was right in the LORD’s eyes
all the days of Jehoiada the priest.
[3] Jehoiada chose two wives for him.
Joash had sons and daughters.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joash ruled from about 835 to 796 before Christ.
> Verse 2 is important: “all the days of Jehoiada the priest.” As long as his teacher and protector lived, Joash was good. This is a warning: faith must not depend only on another person. Everyone needs a relationship with God of their own.
> It was important that Jehoiada chose wives for Joash: David’s family, which had almost been wiped out, was to grow again.

---

## The repair of the temple (Verses 4–14)

[4] Some time later, Joash decided to repair the LORD’s house.
[5] He called the priests and the Levites together.
He said to them,
“Go out to the cities of Judah.
Collect money from all Israel every year.
Use it to repair the house of your God.
Do this quickly.”
But the Levites did not do it right away.
[6] So the king called for Jehoiada, the chief priest.
He said to him,
“Why haven’t you made the Levites collect the tax from Judah and Jerusalem?
Moses, the servant of the LORD, and the assembly of Israel set this tax
for the Tent of the Testimony.”
[7] Athaliah was a wicked woman.
Her sons had broken into God’s house.
They had even given all the holy things of the LORD’s house to the Baals.
[8] So the king gave an order.
They made a chest
and put it outside, at the gate of the LORD’s house.
[9] Then they announced everywhere in Judah and Jerusalem:
“Bring the tax for the LORD!
Moses, the servant of God, set this tax for Israel in the wilderness.”
[10] All the leaders and all the people rejoiced.
They brought their money and threw it into the chest
until it was full.
[11] The Levites brought the chest to the king’s officials.
Whenever they saw that there was a lot of money in it,
the king’s secretary and an official of the chief priest came.
They emptied the chest
and carried it back to its place.
They did this day after day.
So they collected a lot of money.
[12] The king and Jehoiada gave the money to the men who did the work on the LORD’s house.
They hired stonemasons and carpenters to repair the LORD’s house.
They also hired workers in iron and bronze to repair the LORD’s house.
[13] So the workers worked,
and the repairs made good progress under them.
They built God’s house again the way it was planned.
And they made it strong.
[14] When they were finished,
they brought the rest of the money to the king and Jehoiada.
With it, they made objects for the LORD’s house:
objects for the service and for the offerings,
spoons, and objects of gold and silver.
They offered burnt offerings in the LORD’s house all the time,
as long as Jehoiada lived.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 12.
> The temple had fallen into ruin under Athaliah. Her “sons” were probably her followers.
> The “Tent of the Testimony” is the tabernacle, the tent in the wilderness. The tax Moses set is the tax for the holy tent from Exodus 30:12–16.
> The offering chest was a good idea: everyone could give for themselves. The money was counted openly, by an official of the king and one of the priest together. So no one could steal any of it.
> And the people gave gladly: they “rejoiced.”

## The death of Jehoiada (Verses 15–16)

[15] Jehoiada grew old.
He had lived a long and full life, and he died.
He was 130 years old when he died.
[16] They buried him in David’s city with the kings.
They did this because he had done good things in Israel,
for God and for his house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada is the only priest who was buried with the kings. That is a great honor. He had saved David’s family.

---

## Joash turns away (Verses 17–19)

[17] After Jehoiada died,
the leaders of Judah came and bowed down to the king.
And the king listened to them.
[18] They left the house of the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
They served the Asherah poles and the idols.
Because of this guilt, God’s anger came on Judah and Jerusalem.
[19] But the LORD sent prophets to them to bring them back to him.
The prophets warned them,
but they would not listen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada has hardly died when everything changes. The leaders flatter the king, and he listens to them.
> Even now, God does not give up. He sends prophets, one after the other. God is patient.

---

## The murder of Zechariah (Verses 20–22)

[20] Then the Spirit of God came on Zechariah, the son of Jehoiada the priest.
He stood up in front of the people and said to them,
“God says this:
‘Why do you disobey the LORD’s commandments?
That is why things do not go well for you.
You have left the LORD.
So he has also left you.’”
[21] But they made plans against him.
The king gave the order,
and they threw stones at him until he died.
They did this in the courtyard of the LORD’s house.
[22] King Joash forgot the kindness
that Jehoiada, Zechariah’s father, had shown him.
Instead, he killed Jehoiada’s son.
As Zechariah was dying, he said,
“May the LORD look at it, and repay it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a shocking story. Jehoiada had saved Joash’s life. And Joash has Jehoiada’s son killed, right in the temple, because he tells the truth.
> Jesus mentions this murder in Matthew 23:35 and Luke 11:51. In the Hebrew Bible, Chronicles is the last book. That is why Abel is the first and Zechariah the last murder of a righteous person that the Hebrew Bible tells about. Jesus means by this: all the martyrs from the beginning to the end of the Scriptures.
> Zechariah’s last words: “May the LORD look at it, and repay it.” He leaves the punishment to God. Jesus did even more on the cross: he prayed for his murderers (Luke 23:34). Stephen did so too (Acts 7:60).

## The end of Joash (Verses 23–27)

[23] At the end of the year,
the army of the Syrians marched against Joash.
They came to Judah and Jerusalem.
They killed all the leaders of the people.
They sent everything they had captured to the king of Damascus.
[24] The army of the Syrians came with only a small group of men.
But the LORD let them defeat a very large army,
because Judah had left the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
So the Syrians carried out God’s judgment on Joash.
[25] When the Syrians left, Joash was badly wounded.
Then his own servants made plans against him.
They did it because of the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest.
They killed him in his bed, and he died.
They buried him in David’s city,
but not in the tombs of the kings.
[26] These are the men who made plans against him:
Zabad, the son of Shimeath, a woman from Ammon,
and Jehozabad, the son of Shimrith, a woman from Moab.
[27] There is more to tell about his sons,
about the many heavy burdens laid on him,
and about the rebuilding of God’s house.
It is written in the commentary of the book of the kings.
His son Amaziah became king after him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chronicles shows the connection: the leaders who had led Joash into worshiping idols are killed by the Syrians. And Joash himself is murdered by his servants, because of the murder of Zechariah.
> Earlier, Judah had been saved from a huge army (Chapters 14 and 20). Now a large army loses against a few men. It does not depend on the number, but on whether you trust God.
> Verse 25 says “sons” of Jehoiada in the plural. Maybe more sons were killed, or it is a general way of speaking.
> Jehoiada the priest was buried with the kings. Joash the king was not. What counts is not the title, but how you have lived.
> “The many heavy burdens laid on him”: this may mean the payments he had to make to Syria. But the Hebrew word for “burden” can also mean a heavy message from a prophet. Then the words of the prophets against him would be meant (verse 19).
