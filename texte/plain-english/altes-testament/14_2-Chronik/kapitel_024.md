# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 24
### Joash – a good beginning and a terrible end

---

## Joash and Jehoiada (Verses 1–3)

[1] Joash was seven years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for 40 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Zibiah, from Beersheba.
[2] Joash did what was right in the LORD’s eyes
all the days of Jehoiada the priest.
[3] Jehoiada chose two wives for him,
and he became the father of sons and daughters.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joash reigned from about 835 to 796 before Christ.
> Verse 2 is important: “all the days of Jehoiada the priest”. As long as his teacher and protector lived, Joash was good. This is a warning: faith must not depend only on another person. Everyone needs a relationship with God of their own.
> It was important that Jehoiada chose wives for Joash: David’s family, which had almost been wiped out, was to grow again.

---

## The repair of the temple (Verses 4–14)

[4] After this, Joash planned to restore the LORD’s house.
[5] He gathered the priests and the Levites and said to them,
“Go out to the cities of Judah,
and gather money from all Israel year by year
to repair the house of your God.
See that you do this quickly.”
But the Levites didn’t do it right away.
[6] The king called for Jehoiada the chief priest and said to him,
“Why haven’t you made the Levites bring in the tax from Judah and from Jerusalem,
the tax of Moses the servant of the LORD and of the assembly of Israel,
for the Tent of the Testimony?”
[7] For the sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken into God’s house.
And they had also given all the holy things of the LORD’s house to the Baals.
[8] So the king gave the command,
and they made a chest
and set it outside at the gate of the LORD’s house.
[9] They announced throughout Judah and Jerusalem
that the people should bring for the LORD the tax
that Moses the servant of God had laid on Israel in the wilderness.
[10] All the princes and all the people rejoiced.
They brought money and threw it into the chest,
until they had filled it.
[11] The Levites brought the chest to the king’s officers.
Whenever they saw that there was much money in it,
the king’s scribe and the chief priest’s officer came.
They emptied the chest, took it, and carried it back to its place.
They did this day by day,
and they gathered a great amount of money.
[12] The king and Jehoiada gave it to those who did the work on the LORD’s house.
They hired stonemasons and carpenters to restore the LORD’s house,
and also workers in iron and bronze to repair the LORD’s house.
[13] So the workers worked,
and the work of repairing went forward in their hands.
They set up God’s house again as it was designed,
and they made it strong.
[14] When they had finished,
they brought the rest of the money to the king and Jehoiada.
From it they made objects for the LORD’s house,
objects for the service and for the offerings,
spoons, and objects of gold and silver.
They offered burnt offerings in the LORD’s house all the time,
all the days of Jehoiada.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 12.
> The temple had fallen into ruin under Athaliah. Her “sons” were probably her followers.
> The “Tent of the Testimony” is the tabernacle, the tent in the wilderness. The “tax of Moses” is the tax for the holy tent from Exodus 30:12–16.
> The offering chest was a good idea: everyone could give for themselves. The money was counted openly, by an officer of the king and one of the priest together. So no one could steal any of it.
> And the people gave gladly: they “rejoiced”.

---

## The death of Jehoiada (Verses 15–16)

[15] But Jehoiada grew old and full of days, and he died.
He was 130 years old when he died.
[16] They buried him in David’s city among the kings,
because he had done good in Israel,
and toward God and his house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada is the only priest who was buried among the kings. That is a great honor. He had saved David’s family.

---

## Joash turns away (Verses 17–19)

[17] After the death of Jehoiada,
the princes of Judah came and bowed down to the king.
Then the king listened to them.
[18] They abandoned the house of the LORD, the God of their fathers,
and served the Asherah poles and the idols.
So anger came on Judah and Jerusalem because of this guilt of theirs.
[19] Yet he sent prophets to them to bring them back to the LORD.
They warned them,
but they would not listen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hardly is Jehoiada dead when everything changes. The princes flatter the king, and he listens to them.
> Even now God does not give up. He sends prophets, one after the other. God is patient.

---

## The murder of Zechariah (Verses 20–22)

[20] The Spirit of God came on Zechariah the son of Jehoiada the priest.
He stood up above the people and said to them,
“God says,
‘Why do you disobey the LORD’s commandments,
so that you cannot succeed?
Because you have abandoned the LORD,
he has also abandoned you.’”
[21] They plotted against him.
At the king’s command they stoned him with stones
in the court of the LORD’s house.
[22] So King Joash didn’t remember the kindness
that Jehoiada, Zechariah’s father, had shown him,
but he killed his son.
When Zechariah was dying, he said,
“May the LORD look at it, and repay it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a shocking story. Jehoiada had saved Joash’s life. And Joash has Jehoiada’s son killed, right in the temple, because he tells the truth.
> Jesus mentions this murder in Matthew 23:35 and Luke 11:51. In the Hebrew Bible, Chronicles is the last book. That is why Abel is the first and Zechariah the last murder of a righteous person that the Hebrew Bible tells about. Jesus means by this: all the martyrs from the beginning to the end of the Scriptures.
> Zechariah’s last words: “May the LORD look at it, and repay it.” He leaves the punishment to God. Jesus did even more on the cross: he prayed for his murderers (Luke 23:34). Stephen did so too (Acts 7:60).

---

## The end of Joash (Verses 23–27)

[23] At the end of the year,
the army of the Syrians came up against him.
They came to Judah and Jerusalem
and destroyed all the princes of the people from among the people.
They sent all their plunder to the king of Damascus.
[24] The army of the Syrians came with only a small group of men.
But the LORD gave a very great army into their hand,
because they had abandoned the LORD, the God of their fathers.
So they carried out judgment on Joash.
[25] When they had left him, they left him badly wounded.
Then his own servants plotted against him
because of the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest.
They killed him on his bed, and he died.
They buried him in David’s city,
but they didn’t bury him in the tombs of the kings.
[26] These are the ones who plotted against him:
Zabad the son of Shimeath the Ammonitess,
and Jehozabad the son of Shimrith the Moabitess.
[27] About his sons, the many heavy burdens laid on him,
and the rebuilding of God’s house:
look, they are written in the commentary of the book of the kings.
Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chronicles shows the connection: the princes who had led Joash into worshiping idols are killed by the Syrians. And Joash himself is murdered by his servants, because of the murder of Zechariah.
> Earlier, Judah had been saved from a huge army (Chapters 14 and 20). Now a large army loses against a few men. It does not depend on the number, but on whether you trust God.
> Verse 25 says “sons” of Jehoiada in the plural. Maybe more sons were killed, or it is a general way of speaking.
> Jehoiada the priest was buried among the kings. Joash the king was not. What counts is not the title, but how you have lived.
> “The many heavy burdens laid on him”: this may mean the payments he had to make to Syria. But the Hebrew word for “burden” can also mean a “burden” in the sense of a heavy message from a prophet. Then the words of the prophets against him would be meant (verse 19).
