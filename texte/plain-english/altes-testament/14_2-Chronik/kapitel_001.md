# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 1
### Solomon asks for wisdom

---

## Before we begin: What is the second book of Chronicles?

The second book of Chronicles continues the first book of Chronicles. In the beginning, both were one single book.
It tells about King Solomon and about the building of the temple in Jerusalem (Chapters 1–9). After that it tells about the kings of Judah, until Jerusalem is destroyed by the Babylonians (Chapters 10–36).
Unlike the books of Kings, Chronicles tells almost only about Judah, the southern kingdom with Jerusalem and the temple. The northern kingdom of Israel appears only at the edge.
The most important theme is the temple and worship. For every king, Chronicles asks: Did he seek God? Did he respect the temple and worship? When a king seeks God, things go well for him and for the people. When he leaves God, disaster comes.
A favorite word of Chronicles is “seek”: to seek God, to ask him, to turn to him. And Chronicles shows: even someone who has sinned badly can turn back, and God hears him.
The book ends with a view full of hope: the Persian king Cyrus allows the Jews to return to Jerusalem and to build the temple again.
We already know many stories from the books of Kings. Where Chronicles tells them differently, the boxes point this out.

## Solomon at Gibeon (Verses 1–6)

[1] Solomon the son of David was made strong in his kingdom.
The LORD his God was with him
and made him very great.
[2] Solomon spoke to all Israel,
to the captains of thousands and of hundreds,
to the judges, and to every prince in all Israel, the heads of the families.
[3] Then Solomon and all the assembly with him went to the high place at Gibeon.
For God’s Tent of Meeting was there,
which the LORD’s servant Moses had made in the wilderness.
[4] But David had brought God’s ark up from Kiriath Jearim
to the place that David had prepared for it.
For he had set up a tent for it in Jerusalem.
[5] The bronze altar that Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, had made
was also there before the LORD’s tabernacle.
And Solomon and the assembly were seeking counsel there.
[6] Solomon went up there to the bronze altar before the LORD, which was at the Tent of Meeting,
and offered 1,000 burnt offerings on it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon begins his rule with worship. He does not go alone, but with the whole people.
> Gibeon was about 6 miles northwest of Jerusalem. The old tabernacle from the time in the wilderness still stood there. The ark was already in Jerusalem (1 Chronicles 16:39).
> Bezalel was the craftsman who had made the altar in the wilderness (Exodus 31:1–5). He came from Judah (1 Chronicles 2:20).
> “Were seeking counsel there” can also mean “were seeking the LORD there”. The word “seek” is especially important for Chronicles.

## God appears to Solomon (Verses 7–13)

[7] That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him,
“Ask for what you want me to give you.”
[8] Solomon said to God,
“You have shown great loving kindness to David my father,
and you have made me king in his place.
[9] Now, LORD God,
let your promise to David my father come true.
For you have made me king over a people
as many as the dust of the earth.
[10] Now give me wisdom and knowledge,
so that I may go out and come in before this people.
For who can judge this great people of yours?”
[11] God said to Solomon,
“This was in your heart.
You have not asked for riches, wealth, or honor,
or for the life of those who hate you,
and you have not asked for a long life either.
But you have asked for wisdom and knowledge for yourself,
so that you may judge my people, over whom I have made you king.
[12] Because of this, wisdom and knowledge are given to you.
And I will give you riches, wealth, and honor,
such as none of the kings before you have had,
and none after you will have.”
[13] So Solomon came from the high place at Gibeon,
from before the Tent of Meeting, to Jerusalem.
And he reigned over Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> God asks: “Ask for what you want me to give you.” Solomon could have wished for anything. But he asks for wisdom, so that he can care well for his people. He does not think of himself, but of the people who are entrusted to him.
> “Go out and come in” means: to guide and lead the people well.
> God is glad about this request and gives him even more. Jesus said something similar: “But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well” (Matthew 6:33).
> The story is told in more detail in 1 Kings 3. There the famous judgment of Solomon about the two women and the child follows.

## Solomon’s riches (Verses 14–17)

[14] Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen.
He had 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen.
He placed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.
[15] The king made silver and gold as common as stones in Jerusalem.
And he made cedars as common as the sycamore trees in the lowland.
[16] The horses that Solomon had were brought out of Egypt and from Kue.
The king’s merchants bought them from Kue.
[17] They imported a chariot from Egypt for 600 pieces of silver, and a horse for 150,
and then they exported them.
They also exported them to the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Syria.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s promise comes true: Solomon becomes rich and powerful.
> “Kue” is probably Cilicia, a region in the south of today’s Turkey. People bred horses there.
> “Pieces of silver” are probably shekels. A shekel is about 0.4 ounces of silver.
> In Hebrew, “Syria” is called “Aram”.
> Similar verses are in 1 Kings 10:26–29.
> In some Bibles this chapter has one more verse, verse 18. It is chapter 2, verse 1 here. That is why the verse numbering in the next chapter is shifted.
