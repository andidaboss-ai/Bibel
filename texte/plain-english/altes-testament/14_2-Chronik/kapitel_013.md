# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 13
### Abijah against Jeroboam

---

## Two armies face each other (Verses 1–3)

[1] In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam,
Abijah began to reign over Judah.
[2] He reigned for three years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah.
There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.
[3] Abijah went into battle with an army of brave men of war,
400,000 chosen men.
And Jeroboam lined up for battle against him with 800,000 chosen men,
who were mighty men of courage.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abijah reigned from about 913 to 910 before Christ. In 1 Kings 15 he is called “Abijam”, and there he is judged briefly and critically: “He walked in all the sins of his father.” Chronicles, on the other hand, tells a story in which Abijah for once trusts completely in God.
> Here his mother is called Micaiah the daughter of Uriel. In Chapter 11:20 she is called Maacah. Probably it is the same woman with two forms of the name.
> The numbers are huge. Many experts think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” can also mean “troop” or “unit” here. What matters is the message: Judah was only half as strong as Israel.

---

## Abijah’s speech on the mountain (Verses 4–12)

[4] Abijah stood up on Mount Zemaraim, in the hill country of Ephraim, and said,
“Listen to me, Jeroboam and all Israel!
[5] Shouldn’t you know that the LORD, the God of Israel,
gave the kingdom over Israel to David forever,
to him and to his sons, by a covenant of salt?
[6] Yet Jeroboam the son of Nebat, the servant of Solomon the son of David,
rose up and rebelled against his lord.
[7] Worthless men gathered to him,
wicked fellows who made themselves strong against Rehoboam the son of Solomon,
when Rehoboam was young and tender-hearted
and could not stand against them.
[8] “Now you plan to stand against the kingdom of the LORD
in the hand of the sons of David.
You are a great crowd,
and the golden calves that Jeroboam made for you as gods are with you.
[9] Haven’t you driven out the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites?
Haven’t you made priests for yourselves, as the peoples of other lands do?
Whoever comes to dedicate himself with a young bull and seven rams
may become a priest of those who are no gods.
[10] “But as for us, the LORD is our God,
and we have not abandoned him.
We have priests serving the LORD, the sons of Aaron,
and the Levites in their work.
[11] Every morning and every evening they burn burnt offerings and sweet incense to the LORD.
They also set out the show bread on the pure table,
and they take care of the gold lampstand with its lamps, to burn every evening.
For we keep the instructions of the LORD our God,
but you have abandoned him.
[12] Look, God is with us at our head,
and his priests with the trumpets to sound the alarm against you.
Children of Israel, don’t fight against the LORD, the God of your fathers,
for you will not succeed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before the fighting, Abijah gives a speech. It is a kind of sermon of Chronicles.
> A “covenant of salt” is a covenant that lasts forever. Salt keeps things from going bad (compare Numbers 18:19).
> Abijah says: the true kingdom is really the kingdom of God. And the true worship takes place in Jerusalem, with the right priests, the daily offerings, the show bread and the lampstand.
> In the north, on the other hand, anyone could become a priest who paid with enough animals. And Abijah calls the golden calves “those who are no gods”.
> Important: Abijah calls the people in the north “children of Israel”. He asks them: do not fight against God! They are still brothers.
> Abijah speaks harshly about Rehoboam as “young and tender-hearted”. But Rehoboam was 41 years old when he became king (Chapter 12:13). What is probably meant is: without experience and weak.

## Judah cries out to God (Verses 13–20)

[13] But Jeroboam sent men around to lay an ambush behind them.
So the army was in front of Judah, and the ambush was behind them.
[14] Judah looked back.
Look, the battle was in front of them and behind them!
Then they cried out to the LORD,
and the priests blew the trumpets.
[15] Then the men of Judah gave a shout.
As the men of Judah shouted,
God struck Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah.
[16] The children of Israel fled before Judah,
and God gave them into their hand.
[17] Abijah and his people killed many of them.
500,000 chosen men of Israel fell dead.
[18] So the children of Israel were brought low at that time.
And the children of Judah won,
because they relied on the LORD, the God of their fathers.
[19] Abijah chased Jeroboam and took cities from him:
Bethel with its villages,
Jeshanah with its villages,
and Ephron with its villages.
[20] Jeroboam didn’t get his strength back in the days of Abijah.
The LORD struck him, and he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah is surrounded. In military terms the situation is hopeless. But they cry out to God. The deciding sentence is in verse 18: “because they relied on the LORD, the God of their fathers”.
> Chronicles wants to give courage with this: whoever trusts in God is not lost, even if he is weaker.
> The number of the dead is shockingly high. Here, too: the numbers are probably not meant exactly. Still, it remains sad: brothers fight against brothers. Here the Bible does not celebrate war, but trust in God.
> Bethel was one of the two places where Jeroboam had set up a golden calf (1 Kings 12:29).
> Jeroboam did not die right away. He lived a few years longer than Abijah (1 Kings 14:20; 15:9). Chronicles only sums up his end here.

## Abijah’s family and end (Verses 21–22)

[21] But Abijah grew mighty.
He took 14 wives for himself,
and he became the father of 22 sons and 16 daughters.
[22] The rest of the acts of Abijah, his ways, and his sayings
are written in the commentary of the prophet Iddo.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “commentary of the prophet Iddo” is a book that no longer exists. Chronicles often names books of prophets as sources.
> In the English source text the chapter ends here. In some Bibles the next verse (Abijah’s death) is still verse 23 of this chapter.
