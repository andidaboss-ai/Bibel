# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 13
### Abijah against Jeroboam

---

## Two armies face each other (Verses 1–3)

[1] In the eighteenth year that Jeroboam was king,
Abijah became king over Judah.
[2] He ruled in Jerusalem for three years.
His mother was Micaiah, the daughter of Uriel from Gibeah.
There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.
[3] Abijah went into battle with an army of brave soldiers,
400,000 of the best men.
Jeroboam lined up his army for battle against him.
He had 800,000 of the best men, strong and brave soldiers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abijah reigned from about 913 to 910 BC. In 1 Kings 15 he is called “Abijam”, and there he is judged briefly and critically: “He committed all the same sins that his father had committed before him.” Chronicles, on the other hand, tells a story in which Abijah for once trusts completely in God.
> Here his mother is called Micaiah the daughter of Uriel. In 2 Chronicles 11:20 she is called Maacah. Probably it is the same woman with two forms of the name.
> The numbers are huge. Many experts think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” can also mean “troop” or “unit” here. What matters is the message: Judah was only half as strong as Israel.

---

## Abijah’s speech on the mountain (Verses 4–12)

[4] Abijah stood on Mount Zemaraim, in the hill country of Ephraim.
He called out, “Listen to me, Jeroboam and all Israel!
[5] Don’t you know this?
The LORD, the God of Israel, gave David the rule over Israel forever.
He gave it to David and to his sons by a covenant of salt.
A covenant of salt is a covenant that lasts forever.
[6] But Jeroboam, the son of Nebat, rose up against his master.
He was only a servant of Solomon, the son of David.
[7] Worthless men gathered around him.
These evil men became strong against Rehoboam, the son of Solomon.
At that time Rehoboam was young and timid.
He could not stand up against them.
[8] “And now you plan to stand up against the LORD’s kingdom,
the kingdom that the sons of David rule.
You are a very large army.
And you have the golden calves with you, which Jeroboam made to be your gods.
[9] You drove out the LORD’s priests, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites.
You made your own priests, like the peoples of other lands.
Anyone who comes to dedicate himself with a young bull and seven rams
can become a priest of gods that are no gods.
[10] “But for us, the LORD is our God.
We have not abandoned him.
Our priests who serve the LORD are the sons of Aaron.
And the Levites help them in their work.
[11] Every morning and every evening they burn burnt offerings and sweet-smelling incense to the LORD.
They set out the show bread in rows on the pure table.
They take care of the gold lampstand and its lamps, so that it burns every evening.
We keep the commands of the LORD our God.
But you have abandoned him.
[12] Look, God himself is with us as our leader.
His priests are here with their trumpets, ready to sound the battle call against you.
People of Israel, don’t fight against the LORD, the God of your ancestors.
You will not win!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before the fighting, Abijah gives a speech. It is a kind of sermon of Chronicles.
> A “covenant of salt” is a covenant that lasts forever. Salt keeps food from going bad (compare Numbers 18:19).
> Abijah says: the true kingdom is really the kingdom of God. And the true worship takes place in Jerusalem, with the right priests, the daily offerings, the show bread and the lampstand.
> In the north, on the other hand, anyone could become a priest who paid with enough animals. And Abijah calls the golden calves “gods that are no gods”.
> Important: Abijah calls the people in the north “people of Israel”. He asks them: do not fight against God! They are still brothers.
> Abijah speaks harshly about Rehoboam as “young and timid”. But Rehoboam was 41 years old when he became king (2 Chronicles 12:13). What is probably meant is: without experience and weak.

## Judah cries out to God (Verses 13–20)

[13] But Jeroboam had sent soldiers around to attack Judah from behind.
So Jeroboam’s army was in front of Judah, and the hidden soldiers were behind them.
[14] The men of Judah turned around.
They saw that the enemy was attacking them from the front and from behind!
Then they cried out to the LORD.
And the priests blew the trumpets.
[15] Then the men of Judah gave a battle shout.
When the men of Judah shouted,
God struck Jeroboam and all Israel down in front of Abijah and Judah.
[16] The Israelites ran away from Judah.
And God handed them over to Judah.
[17] Abijah and his soldiers killed a great number of them.
500,000 of Israel’s best men were killed.
[18] So at that time the Israelites were defeated.
And the men of Judah won the battle,
because they trusted in the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
[19] Abijah chased Jeroboam.
He captured these cities from him:
Bethel and its villages,
Jeshanah and its villages,
and Ephron and its villages.
[20] As long as Abijah lived, Jeroboam never became strong again.
The LORD struck him down, and he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah is surrounded. In military terms the situation is hopeless. But they cry out to God. The deciding sentence is in verse 18: “because they trusted in the LORD, the God of their ancestors”.
> Chronicles wants to give courage with this: whoever trusts in God is not lost, even if he is weaker.
> The number of the dead is shockingly high. Here, too: the numbers are probably not meant exactly. Still, it remains sad: brothers fight against brothers. Here the Bible does not celebrate war, but trust in God.
> Bethel was one of the two places where Jeroboam had set up a golden calf (1 Kings 12:29).
> Jeroboam did not die right away. He lived a few years longer than Abijah (1 Kings 14:20; 15:9). Chronicles only sums up his end here.

## Abijah’s family and end (Verses 21–22)

[21] But Abijah became powerful.
He married 14 wives.
He had 22 sons and 16 daughters.
[22] Everything else about Abijah is recorded in the writings of the prophet Iddo:
what he did, how he lived, and what he said.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “writings of the prophet Iddo” are a book that no longer exists. Chronicles often names books of prophets as sources.
> In the WEB, our English source text, the chapter ends here. In some Bibles the next verse (Abijah’s death) is still verse 23 of this chapter.
