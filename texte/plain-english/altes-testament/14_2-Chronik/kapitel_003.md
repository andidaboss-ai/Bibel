# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 3
### The building of the temple begins

---

## The place and the measurements (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then Solomon began to build the LORD’s house in Jerusalem
on Mount Moriah,
where the LORD had appeared to David his father,
in the place that David had chosen,
on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.
[2] He began to build on the second day of the second month,
in the fourth year of his reign.
[3] These are the foundations that Solomon laid for building God’s house:
The length, by the old measure, was about 90 feet,
and the width about 30 feet.
[4] The porch in front was as long as the house was wide, about 30 feet,
and its height was about 180 feet.
He covered it inside with pure gold.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple is built exactly where the angel stopped and David built the altar (1 Chronicles 21).
> Mount Moriah is the place where Abraham was to sacrifice his son Isaac and where God stopped him (Genesis 22:2). Only here in 2 Chronicles 3:1 does the Bible call the Temple Mount by this name. In this way two great stories of the Bible are connected: Abraham and Isaac, David and the angel. Both are stories in which God keeps life safe.
> That was around the year 966 before Christ.
> The Bible says “60 cubits long and 20 cubits wide”. A cubit is about 18 inches. “By the old measure” means: by the old, longer cubit.
> The height of the porch, “120” cubits (about 180 feet), is very high, much higher than the temple itself (30 cubits, 1 Kings 6:2). Some old manuscripts have “20 cubits” here. Maybe the larger number is a copying mistake.

---

## Gold and precious stones (Verses 5–9)

[5] He covered the larger room with cypress wood
and covered it with fine gold.
He decorated it with palm trees and chains.
[6] He decorated the house with precious stones, for beauty.
The gold was gold from Parvaim.
[7] He also covered the house, the beams, the thresholds, its walls, and its doors with gold,
and he carved cherubim on the walls.
[8] He made the most holy place.
Its length, the same as the width of the house, was about 30 feet,
and its width was about 30 feet.
He covered it with fine gold, about 45,000 pounds.
[9] The weight of the nails was about 1¼ pounds of gold.
He also covered the upper rooms with gold.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple was splendid, full of gold and decoration. For God, the most beautiful and the best should be given.
> Palm trees and cherubim remind us of the Garden of Eden. There cherubim guarded the entrance to the garden (Genesis 3:24). The temple was like a picture of paradise, the place where God and people are together.
> Nobody knows where “Parvaim” was. It was known for its gold.
> The most holy place was a cube: 20 cubits long, wide and high (compare 1 Kings 6:20). A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The Bible says “600 talents” of gold (a talent is about 75 pounds) and “50 shekels” for the nails (a shekel is about 0.4 ounces).

---

## The cherubim in the most holy place (Verses 10–14)

[10] In the most holy place he made two carved cherubim,
and they covered them with gold.
[11] The wings of the cherubim were about 30 feet long in all:
One wing of the one cherub was about 7½ feet long and reached to the wall of the house.
The other wing was about 7½ feet long and reached to the wing of the other cherub.
[12] One wing of the other cherub was about 7½ feet long and reached to the wall of the house.
The other wing was about 7½ feet long and touched the wing of the first cherub.
[13] The wings of these cherubim spread out about 30 feet.
They stood on their feet,
and their faces were turned toward the house.
[14] He made the curtain of blue, purple, crimson, and fine linen,
and decorated it with cherubim.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two large cherubim stood in the most holy place. Their wings filled the whole room from wall to wall. Later the ark of the covenant stood under their wings.
> Cherubim are heavenly beings who guard God’s throne. People imagined that God sits on his throne above them, invisible.
> The Bible says “20 cubits” and “5 cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The curtain separated the most holy place from the rest of the temple. Only the high priest was allowed to go in, once a year. The New Testament tells that when Jesus died, the curtain in the temple was torn (Mark 15:38). Christians see in this a sign that the way to God is now open for everyone.

---

## The two pillars (Verses 15–17)

[15] In front of the house he made two pillars, about 52½ feet high.
The capital on the top of each of them was about 7½ feet high.
A capital is the top part of a pillar.
[16] He made chains in the inner sanctuary
and put them on the tops of the pillars.
And he made 100 pomegranates and put them on the chains.
[17] He set up the pillars in front of the temple,
one on the right and the other on the left.
He named the one on the right Jachin,
and the one on the left Boaz.

> **What does this mean?**
> The two large bronze pillars stood free in front of the entrance.
> “Jachin” means “He (God) sets up” or “He makes firm”. “Boaz” means “In him is strength”. Together they say: God gives support and strength.
> The Bible says “35 cubits” and “5 cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches. In 1 Kings 7:15 the pillars are only 18 cubits high. Maybe both pillars are counted together here.
> “In the inner sanctuary”: the Hebrew has a word that can also be read as “like a necklace”. What is meant is probably a chain network as decoration.
