# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 15
### The covenant to seek God

---

## The message of the prophet Azariah (Verses 1–7)

[1] The Spirit of God came on Azariah the son of Oded.
[2] He went out to meet Asa and said to him,
“Listen to me, Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin!
The LORD is with you while you are with him.
If you seek him, he will be found by you.
But if you abandon him, he will abandon you.
[3] For a long time Israel was without the true God,
without a priest who taught,
and without law.
[4] But in their distress they turned to the LORD, the God of Israel,
and sought him,
and he was found by them.
[5] In those times there was no peace for anyone who went out or came in.
Great troubles were on all who lived in the lands.
[6] They were broken in pieces,
nation against nation, and city against city.
For God troubled them with every kind of hardship.
[7] But you, be strong!
Don’t let your hands grow weak,
for your work will be rewarded.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Asa has just won a great victory. Now a prophet comes and says: do not forget to whom you owe the victory!
> Verse 2 is a key sentence of the whole of Chronicles: “If you seek him, he will be found by you.” God does not hide from those who honestly seek him. Later the prophet Jeremiah says something similar (29:13–14), and Jesus: “Seek, and you will find” (Matthew 7:7).
> Verses 3–6 probably recall the time of the judges. Back then there was chaos and violence. But whenever the people cried out to God in their need, he helped.
> Verse 7 is an encouragement: do not give up! Your effort is not in vain.

---

## Asa renews the faith (Verses 8–11)

[8] Asa heard these words and the prophecy of Oded the prophet.
Then he took courage.
He put away the disgusting idols out of all the land of Judah and Benjamin,
and out of the cities that he had taken from the hill country of Ephraim.
And he renewed the LORD’s altar that was in front of the LORD’s porch.
[9] He gathered all Judah and Benjamin,
and those from Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon who lived with them.
For many came to him out of Israel,
when they saw that the LORD his God was with him.
[10] So they gathered in Jerusalem in the third month,
in the fifteenth year of Asa’s reign.
[11] On that day they sacrificed to the LORD
from the plunder that they had brought:
700 head of cattle and 7,000 sheep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 says “the prophecy of Oded the prophet”. But before, the prophet was called Azariah the son of Oded. Probably the words “Azariah the son of” were lost in copying. Some old translations have “Azariah the son of Oded” here.
> The “disgusting idols” are images of idols that God detests.
> Again people from the north come to Judah, because they see: God is with Asa. Faith that is really lived draws others.
> The third month is the time of the feast of weeks (Shavuot, later Pentecost).

---

## The covenant (Verses 12–15)

[12] They made a covenant to seek the LORD, the God of their fathers,
with all their heart and with all their soul.
[13] Whoever would not seek the LORD, the God of Israel,
was to be put to death,
whether small or great,
whether man or woman.
[14] They swore an oath to the LORD with a loud voice,
with shouting, with trumpets, and with horns.
[15] All Judah rejoiced at the oath,
for they had sworn with all their heart
and sought him with their whole desire.
And he was found by them.
Then the LORD gave them rest all around.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people solemnly promise to seek God with their whole heart. It is a feast of joy.
> Verse 13 is hard: whoever does not seek God is to be killed. This is based on the old law against worshiping idols (Deuteronomy 13 and 17:2–7). At that time worshiping idols counted as betrayal of the whole people.
> Today Jews and Christians say clearly: faith must never be forced. Violence in the name of God is wrong. Jesus did not force anyone to follow him. Faith can only grow in freedom. You can also see this in verse 15: what mattered was that they sought “with all their heart” and “with their whole desire”. That cannot be forced.

---

## Asa and his mother (Verses 16–19)

[16] King Asa also removed Maacah, his mother, from being queen mother,
because she had made a disgusting image for an Asherah.
Asa cut down her image, ground it into dust,
and burned it at the brook Kidron.
[17] But the high places were not taken away out of Israel.
Still, Asa’s heart was perfect all his days.
[18] He brought into God’s house the things that his father had dedicated
and that he himself had dedicated:
silver, gold, and objects.
[19] There was no more war until the thirty-fifth year of Asa’s reign.

> **What does this mean?**
> Maacah was really Asa’s grandmother (the mother of Abijah, Chapter 11:20). In Hebrew, “mother” can also mean “grandmother”. The “queen mother” had great influence at the court.
> Asa acts even against his own family when it is about God. That was brave.
> Verse 17 seems to contradict Chapter 14:5, where Asa removed the high places. One explanation: Asa removed the high places for foreign gods. The high places where people sacrificed to the LORD remained. Or: “Israel” here means the conquered areas in the north.
> “Asa’s heart was perfect”: the English source text says “perfect”. It does not mean that Asa was without mistakes, but that he was faithful to God with an undivided heart. Chapter 16 shows that Asa did make mistakes later.
