# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 15
### The covenant to seek God

---

## The message of the prophet Azariah (Verses 1–7)

[1] The Spirit of God came on Azariah, the son of Oded.
[2] He went out to meet Asa.
He said to him,
“Listen to me, Asa, and all of Judah and Benjamin!
The LORD is with you as long as you are with him.
If you seek him, he will let you find him.
But if you abandon him, he will abandon you.
[3] For a long time Israel was without the true God.
They had no priest to teach them,
and they had no law.
[4] But when they were in trouble, they turned to the LORD, the God of Israel.
They sought him,
and he let them find him.
[5] In those times no one was safe when they went out or came in.
All the people who lived in those lands had great troubles.
[6] Nations crushed each other.
One nation fought against another, and one city against another.
God brought every kind of trouble on them.
[7] But you must be strong!
Don’t give up.
Your work will be rewarded.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Asa has just won a great victory. Now a prophet comes and says: do not forget to whom you owe the victory!
> Verse 2 is a key sentence of the whole of Chronicles: “If you seek him, he will let you find him.” God does not hide from those who honestly seek him. Later the prophet Jeremiah says something similar (Jeremiah 29:13–14), and Jesus: “Seek, and you will find” (Matthew 7:7).
> Verses 3–6 probably recall the time of the judges. Back then there was chaos and violence. But whenever the people cried out to God in their need, he helped.
> Verse 7 is an encouragement: do not give up! Your effort is not in vain.

---

## Asa renews the faith (Verses 8–11)

[8] Asa heard these words and the prophecy of Oded the prophet.
Then he took courage.
He removed the disgusting idols from the whole land of Judah and Benjamin.
He also removed them from the cities he had captured in the hill country of Ephraim.
And he repaired the LORD’s altar in front of the porch of the LORD’s house.
[9] Asa called together all the people of Judah and Benjamin.
He also called the people from Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon who lived among them.
Many people from Israel had come over to him.
They had seen that the LORD his God was with him.
[10] So they gathered in Jerusalem in the third month
of the fifteenth year that Asa was king.
[11] On that day they sacrificed to the LORD
some of the animals they had taken in the war:
700 cattle and 7,000 sheep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 says “the prophecy of Oded the prophet”. But before, the prophet was called Azariah the son of Oded. Probably the words “Azariah the son of” were lost in copying. Some old translations have “Azariah the son of Oded” here.
> The “disgusting idols” are images of idols that God detests.
> Again people from the north come to Judah, because they see: God is with Asa. Faith that is really lived draws others.
> The third month is the time of the Feast of Weeks (Shavuot, later Pentecost).

---

## The covenant (Verses 12–15)

[12] They made a covenant to seek the LORD, the God of their ancestors,
with all their heart and with all their soul.
[13] Anyone who would not seek the LORD, the God of Israel,
was to be put to death.
This was true for young and old,
for men and women.
[14] They made a promise to the LORD with a loud voice.
They shouted and blew trumpets and horns.
[15] All the people of Judah were glad about the promise.
They had made it with all their heart.
They had sought the LORD with their whole desire.
And he let them find him.
So the LORD gave them peace on every side.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people solemnly promise to seek God with their whole heart. It is a feast of joy.
> Verse 13 is hard: whoever does not seek God is to be killed. This is based on the old law against worshiping idols (Deuteronomy 13 and 17:2–7). At that time worshiping idols counted as betrayal of the whole people.
> Today Jews and Christians say clearly: faith must never be forced. Violence in the name of God is wrong. Jesus did not force anyone to follow him. Faith can only grow in freedom. You can also see this in verse 15: what mattered was that they sought “with all their heart” and “with their whole desire”. That cannot be forced.

---

## Asa and his mother (Verses 16–19)

[16] King Asa also removed his mother Maacah from her position as queen mother.
She had made a disgusting image for the goddess Asherah.
Asa cut down her image.
He crushed it into dust
and burned it at the brook Kidron.
[17] But the high places were not removed from Israel.
Still, Asa’s heart was completely faithful as long as he lived.
[18] He brought into God’s house the things that he and his father had given to God:
silver, gold, and other objects.
[19] There was no more war until the thirty-fifth year that Asa was king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Maacah was really Asa’s grandmother (the mother of Abijah, 2 Chronicles 11:20). In Hebrew, “mother” can also mean “grandmother”. The “queen mother” had great influence at the court.
> Asa acts even against his own family when it is about God. That was brave.
> Verse 17 seems to contradict 2 Chronicles 14:5, where Asa removed the high places. One explanation: Asa removed the high places for foreign gods. The high places where people sacrificed to the LORD remained. Or: “Israel” here means the conquered areas in the north.
> “Asa’s heart was completely faithful”: the WEB, our English source text, says “perfect”. This does not mean that Asa was without mistakes, but that he was faithful to God with an undivided heart. Chapter 16 shows that Asa did make mistakes later.
