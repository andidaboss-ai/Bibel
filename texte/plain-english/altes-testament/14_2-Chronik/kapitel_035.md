# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 35
### Josiah’s Passover and his death

---

## Getting ready for the Passover (Verses 1–6)

[1] Josiah kept a Passover to the LORD in Jerusalem.
They killed the Passover lambs on the fourteenth day of the first month.
[2] He set the priests in their duties
and encouraged them in the service of the LORD’s house.
[3] He said to the Levites who taught all Israel,
who were holy to the LORD,
“Put the holy ark in the house that Solomon the son of David king of Israel built.
It will no longer be a burden on your shoulders.
Now serve the LORD your God and his people Israel.
[4] Get yourselves ready by your families and by your divisions,
as David king of Israel wrote down,
and as Solomon his son wrote down.
[5] Stand in the holy place by the divisions of the families of your brothers, the people,
For each group of families of the people,
let there be a group from a family of the Levites.
[6] Kill the Passover lamb, make yourselves holy,
and prepare it for your brothers,
to do as the LORD commanded through Moses.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is told more briefly in 2 Kings 23:21–23. The Passover took place in Josiah’s eighteenth year, in the same year in which the book of the law was found (verse 19).
> Verse 3 is a puzzle. The ark had belonged in the temple since Solomon. Maybe it had been removed under Manasseh or Amon and was now brought back. Or Josiah means: you no longer have to carry the ark as in the wilderness. So now you have time for another service: to serve the people.
> Here the Levites are called teachers, those “who taught all Israel.” That was an important task.
> The Bible does not tell what later happened to the ark of the covenant. This is the last time it is mentioned in the history books.

---

## Generous gifts (Verses 7–9)

[7] Josiah gave to the people animals from the flock,
lambs and young goats, all of them for the Passover offerings,
for all who were there, 30,000 in number,
and 3,000 bulls.
These were from the king’s own property.
[8] His princes gave a freewill offering to the people, to the priests, and to the Levites.
Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, the rulers of God’s house,
gave to the priests for the Passover offerings 2,600 small livestock and 300 head of cattle.
[9] Conaniah also, and Shemaiah and Nethanel his brothers,
and Hashabiah, Jeiel, and Jozabad, the chiefs of the Levites,
gave to the Levites for the Passover offerings 5,000 small livestock and 500 head of cattle.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king gives from his own property, so that the poor can celebrate the feast too. The princes and the chiefs follow his example.
> “Small livestock” are sheep and goats.

---

## The feast is celebrated (Verses 10–19)

[10] So the service was prepared.
The priests stood in their place,
and the Levites by their divisions, as the king had commanded.
[11] They killed the Passover lambs.
The priests sprinkled the blood that they received from their hands,
and the Levites skinned the animals.
[12] They set apart the burnt offerings,
to give them to the divisions of the families of the people,
to offer them to the LORD, as it is written in the book of Moses.
They did the same with the cattle.
[13] They roasted the Passover lambs with fire, as the rule says.
They boiled the holy offerings in pots, in kettles, and in pans,
and quickly carried them to all the people.
[14] After that they prepared food for themselves and for the priests.
For the priests, the sons of Aaron, were busy offering the burnt offerings and the fat until night.
So the Levites prepared food for themselves and for the priests, the sons of Aaron.
[15] The singers, the sons of Asaph, were in their place,
as David, Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun the king’s seer had commanded.
And the gatekeepers were at every gate.
They didn’t need to leave their service,
because their brothers the Levites prepared food for them.
[16] So all the service of the LORD was prepared on the same day,
to keep the Passover and to offer burnt offerings on the LORD’s altar,
as King Josiah had commanded.
[17] The people of Israel who were there kept the Passover at that time,
and the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days.
[18] No Passover like it had been kept in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet.
And none of the kings of Israel had kept such a Passover as Josiah kept,
with the priests, the Levites, and all Judah and Israel who were there,
and the people of Jerusalem.
[19] This Passover was kept in the eighteenth year of Josiah’s reign.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything is well organized. Everyone has his task. The Levites make sure that everyone gets their food: the people, the priests, the singers and the gatekeepers. No one is forgotten.
> The Passover lamb was roasted, as it says in Exodus 12:8–9. The other offerings were boiled.
> Verse 18: no such Passover “since the days of Samuel.” Hezekiah’s Passover (Chapter 30) was great too, but it took place in the second month, and many were not clean. Josiah’s Passover was exactly according to the law.

## Josiah’s death at Megiddo (Verses 20–27)

[20] After all this, when Josiah had set the temple in order,
Neco king of Egypt went up to fight at Carchemish by the Euphrates.
And Josiah went out against him.
[21] But Neco sent messengers to him and said,
“What have I to do with you, king of Judah?
I am not coming against you today,
but against the house with which I am at war.
God has commanded me to hurry.
Be careful! It is God who is with me.
Don’t let him destroy you.”
[22] But Josiah would not turn his face away from him.
He disguised himself so that he could fight with him.
He didn’t listen to the words of Neco, which came from the mouth of God.
And he came to fight in the valley of Megiddo.
[23] The archers shot at King Josiah.
And the king said to his servants,
“Take me away, for I am badly wounded!”
[24] So his servants took him out of the chariot
and put him in the second chariot that he had,
and they brought him to Jerusalem.
He died and was buried in the tombs of his ancestors.
All Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah.
[25] Jeremiah sang a song of mourning for Josiah.
And all the singing men and singing women have spoken of Josiah in their songs of mourning to this day.
They made it a custom in Israel.
They are written in the Lamentations.
[26] The rest of the acts of Josiah,
and his good deeds, as it is written in the LORD’s law,
[27] and his acts, from first to last,
are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the year 609 BC, Pharaoh Neco II marched north to help the Assyrians against the Babylonians. Josiah wanted to stop him.
> The astonishing thing: this time God spoke through a foreign king. Neco warned Josiah. But Josiah did not listen. He probably did not think it possible that God would speak through an Egyptian.
> Like Ahab (2 Chronicles 18:29), Josiah disguises himself. And like Ahab, he is struck by archers. Even such a good king can make a deadly mistake if he does not listen.
> The mourning for Josiah was huge. He was the last good king of Judah. The prophet Zechariah, much later, still mentions the great mourning “in the valley of Megiddo” (Zechariah 12:11).
> The “Lamentations” in verse 25 are not the same as the biblical book of Lamentations, which mourns the destruction of Jerusalem. This collection no longer exists.
> The word “Armageddon” (mountain of Megiddo) in Revelation 16:16 comes from the name Megiddo.
