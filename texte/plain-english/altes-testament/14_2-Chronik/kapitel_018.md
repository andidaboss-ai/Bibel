# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 18
### The prophet Micaiah and the death of Ahab

---

## Jehoshaphat makes an alliance with Ahab (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor.
And he made an alliance with Ahab.
[2] Some years later he went down to Ahab in Samaria.
Ahab killed a great many sheep and cattle for him
and for the people who were with him.
He talked Jehoshaphat into going with him to attack Ramoth Gilead.
[3] Ahab, the king of Israel, asked Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah,
“Will you go with me to Ramoth Gilead?”
Jehoshaphat answered,
“I am as you are, and my people are as your people.
We will be with you in the war.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is almost the same in 1 Kings 22.
> “He made an alliance”: what is meant is that Jehoshaphat’s son Jehoram married Athaliah, the daughter of Ahab (2 Chronicles 21:6). This marriage later brought great disaster on Judah (Chapters 22–23).
> Ahab was the king of the northern kingdom who, together with his wife Jezebel, promoted the worship of Baal.
> Jehoshaphat already had riches and honor. Why did he need the alliance with Ahab? Chronicles hints: he did not need it at all.

## 400 prophets say yes (Verses 4–11)

[4] Jehoshaphat also said to the king of Israel,
“But first, please ask what the LORD says.”
[5] So the king of Israel called the prophets together, 400 men.
He asked them,
“Should we go to war against Ramoth Gilead, or should I not?”
They said,
“Go! God will hand it over to the king.”
[6] But Jehoshaphat asked,
“Isn’t there another prophet of the LORD here?
Then we can ask him too.”
[7] The king of Israel answered Jehoshaphat,
“There is still one man who can ask the LORD for us.
But I hate him.
He never says anything good about me, only bad things.
He is Micaiah, the son of Imla.”
Jehoshaphat said,
“The king should not say that.”
[8] So the king of Israel called an officer.
He said, “Bring Micaiah, the son of Imla, quickly.”
[9] The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, were sitting on their thrones.
They were wearing their royal robes.
They were sitting at an open place at the entrance of the gate of Samaria.
And all the prophets were prophesying in front of them.
[10] Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, had made horns of iron for himself.
He said,
“This is what the LORD says:
‘With these you will gore the Syrians until they are destroyed.’”
[11] All the prophets were saying the same thing:
“Attack Ramoth Gilead, and you will win!
The LORD will hand it over to the king.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat is careful. He wants to know God’s will. But the 400 prophets only say what Ahab wants to hear.
> Ahab hates Micaiah because Micaiah tells the truth. This is an old temptation: people want only those around them who agree with them.
> Ramoth Gilead was an important border city east of the Jordan. The Syrians (Aram) had occupied it.
> This Micaiah is not the prophet Micah, after whom a book of the Bible is named.

---

## Micaiah tells the truth (Verses 12–27)

[12] The messenger who went to get Micaiah said to him,
“Listen, all the prophets agree. They all say good things to the king.
So please, say what they say.
Say something good.”
[13] Micaiah answered,
“As surely as the LORD lives,
I will say what my God says.”
[14] Micaiah came to the king.
The king asked him,
“Micaiah, should we go to war against Ramoth Gilead, or should I not?”
Micaiah answered,
“Go and win!
They will be handed over to you.”
[15] But the king said to him,
“How many times must I make you swear to tell me only the truth in the LORD’s name?”
[16] Then Micaiah said,
“I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains,
like sheep without a shepherd.
And the LORD said,
‘These people have no master.
Let each of them go home in peace.’”
[17] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“Didn’t I tell you? He never says anything good about me, only bad things.”
[18] Micaiah said,
“Then listen to what the LORD says!
I saw the LORD sitting on his throne.
All the armies of heaven were standing at his right and at his left.
[19] The LORD asked,
‘Who will trick Ahab, the king of Israel,
so that he will attack Ramoth Gilead and die there?’
One said this, and another said that.
[20] Then a spirit came forward and stood before the LORD.
It said, ‘I will trick him.’
“The LORD asked, ‘How?’
[21] “The spirit answered,
‘I will go and be a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets.’
“The LORD said,
‘You will trick him, and you will succeed.
Go and do it.’
[22] “So now you see:
the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouths of these prophets of yours.
The LORD has announced disaster for you.”
[23] Then Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, came up to Micaiah.
He slapped him in the face and said,
“How did the LORD’s Spirit leave me to speak to you?”
[24] Micaiah answered,
“You will find out on the day
when you run into an inner room to hide.”
[25] Then the king of Israel said,
“Take Micaiah and send him back to Amon, the governor of the city,
and to Joash, the king’s son.
[26] Tell them,
‘This is what the king says:
“Put this man in prison.
Give him nothing but the bread and water of suffering
until I come back safely.”’”
[27] Micaiah said,
“If you ever come back safely,
then the LORD has not spoken through me.”
And he said,
“Listen, you people, all of you!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Micaiah is under great pressure: 400 prophets against one. But he stays firm: “I will say what my God says.”
> First Micaiah mockingly repeats the words of the others. Ahab notices right away that he does not mean it. Then Micaiah tells the truth: Israel will be like sheep without a shepherd. That means: the king, the shepherd of the people, will die.
> The vision of the heavenly council around God’s throne is hard to understand. Does God really let someone lie? You have to look closely: Micaiah tells Ahab the whole plan! God warns Ahab openly. Ahab has the choice. He wanted to hear the lie, and God leaves him with the lie he has chosen.
> “The bread and water of suffering” means: only very little bread and water, just enough to survive.
> “Listen, you people, all of you!” is the same sentence as the first sentence in the book of the prophet Micah (Micah 1:2).

## The death of Ahab (Verses 28–34)

[28] So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, went to attack Ramoth Gilead.
[29] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“I will disguise myself and go into the battle.
But you wear your royal robes.”
So the king of Israel disguised himself,
and they went into the battle.
[30] The king of Syria had given an order to the commanders of his chariots.
He said, “Don’t fight with anyone, small or great.
Fight only with the king of Israel.”
[31] The commanders of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat.
They said, “That is the king of Israel!”
So they turned to attack him.
But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him.
And God moved them to go away from him.
[32] The commanders of the chariots saw that he was not the king of Israel.
So they stopped chasing him.
[33] But a soldier shot an arrow at random.
It hit the king of Israel between the pieces of his armor.
The king said to his chariot driver,
“Turn around and take me out of the battle.
I am badly wounded.”
[34] The battle grew worse all that day.
The king of Israel held himself up in his chariot, facing the Syrians, until evening.
And at about sunset he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab tries to escape the prophecy. He disguises himself and lets Jehoshaphat fight as the only king in royal clothes. That was dangerous and unfair to Jehoshaphat.
> But you cannot trick God. An arrow shot “at random” hits Ahab exactly at the weak spot of the armor.
> Chronicles adds something that is not in 1 Kings 22: “The LORD helped him. And God moved them to go away from him” (2 Chronicles 18:31). Jehoshaphat cries out to God, and God saves him, even though he had gotten involved with Ahab. God’s grace is greater than our mistakes.
> Ahab reigned from about 874 to 853 BC.
