# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 18
### The prophet Micaiah and the death of Ahab

---

## Jehoshaphat makes an alliance with Ahab (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor.
And he made an alliance with Ahab.
[2] After some years he went down to Ahab in Samaria.
Ahab killed very many sheep and cattle for him
and for the people who were with him.
And he urged him to go up with him to Ramoth Gilead.
[3] Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah,
“Will you go with me to Ramoth Gilead?”
He answered him,
“I am as you are, and my people are as your people.
We will be with you in the war.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is almost the same in 1 Kings 22.
> “He made an alliance”: what is meant is that Jehoshaphat’s son Jehoram married Athaliah, the daughter of Ahab (Chapter 21:6). This marriage later brought great disaster on Judah (Chapters 22–23).
> Ahab was the king of the northern kingdom who, together with his wife Jezebel, promoted the worship of Baal.
> Jehoshaphat already had riches and honor. Why did he need the alliance with Ahab? Chronicles hints: he did not need it at all.

---

## 400 prophets say yes (Verses 4–11)

[4] Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel,
“Please first ask for the LORD’s word.”
[5] Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets, 400 men, and said to them,
“Shall we go to battle against Ramoth Gilead, or shall I hold back?”
They said,
“Go up, for God will give it into the hand of the king.”
[6] But Jehoshaphat said,
“Isn’t there another prophet of the LORD here, so that we may ask him?”
[7] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“There is still one man through whom we may ask the LORD.
But I hate him,
for he never prophesies good about me, but always evil.
He is Micaiah the son of Imla.”
Jehoshaphat said,
“Don’t let the king say so.”
[8] Then the king of Israel called an officer and said,
“Get Micaiah the son of Imla quickly.”
[9] The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah each sat on his throne,
dressed in their robes.
They were sitting in an open place at the entrance of the gate of Samaria,
and all the prophets were prophesying before them.
[10] Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah made himself horns of iron and said,
“The LORD says,
‘With these you shall push the Syrians until they are destroyed.’”
[11] All the prophets prophesied the same:
“Go up to Ramoth Gilead and succeed,
for the LORD will give it into the hand of the king.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat is careful. He wants to know God’s will. But the 400 prophets only say what Ahab wants to hear.
> Ahab hates Micaiah because Micaiah tells the truth. This is an old temptation: people want only those around them who agree with them.
> Ramoth Gilead was an important border city east of the Jordan. The Syrians (Aram) had occupied it.
> This Micaiah is not the prophet Micah, after whom a book of the Bible is named.

---

## Micaiah tells the truth (Verses 12–27)

[12] The messenger who went to call Micaiah said to him,
“Look, the prophets all say with one mouth that good will come to the king.
So please, let your word be like one of theirs,
and speak good.”
[13] Micaiah said,
“As surely as the LORD lives,
I will say what my God says.”
[14] When he had come to the king, the king said to him,
“Micaiah, shall we go to battle against Ramoth Gilead, or shall I hold back?”
He said,
“Go up and succeed.
They will be given into your hand.”
[15] The king said to him,
“How many times must I make you swear
that you tell me nothing but the truth in the LORD’s name?”
[16] He said,
“I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains,
like sheep that have no shepherd.
The LORD said,
‘These have no master.
Let each of them return to his house in peace.’”
[17] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“Didn’t I tell you that he would not prophesy good about me, but evil?”
[18] Micaiah said,
“So hear the LORD’s word:
I saw the LORD sitting on his throne,
and all the army of heaven standing on his right hand and on his left.
[19] The LORD said,
‘Who will lure Ahab king of Israel,
so that he goes up and falls at Ramoth Gilead?’
One said one thing, and another said another.
[20] Then a spirit came out, stood before the LORD, and said,
‘I will lure him.’
“The LORD said to him, ‘How?’
[21] “He said,
‘I will go and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’
“The LORD said,
‘You will lure him, and you will succeed.
Go and do so.’
[22] “So now, look, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets of yours.
And the LORD has spoken evil about you.”
[23] Then Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah came near,
struck Micaiah on the cheek, and said,
“Which way did the LORD’s Spirit go from me to speak to you?”
[24] Micaiah said,
“Look, you will see on that day,
when you go into an inner room to hide yourself.”
[25] The king of Israel said,
“Take Micaiah and bring him back to Amon the governor of the city,
and to Joash the king’s son.
[26] Say,
‘The king says,
“Put this fellow in prison,
and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction,
until I return in peace.”’”
[27] Micaiah said,
“If you return in peace at all,
the LORD has not spoken through me.”
He said,
“Listen, you people, all of you!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Micaiah is under great pressure: 400 prophets against one. But he stays firm: “I will say what my God says.”
> First Micaiah mockingly repeats the words of the others. Ahab notices right away that he does not mean it. Then Micaiah tells the truth: Israel will be like sheep without a shepherd. That means: the king, the shepherd of the people, will die.
> The vision of the heavenly council around God’s throne is hard to understand. Does God really let someone lie? You have to look closely: Micaiah tells Ahab the whole plan! God warns Ahab openly. Ahab has the choice. He wanted to hear the lie, and God leaves him with the lie he has chosen.
> “Bread of affliction and water of affliction” means: only very little bread and water, just enough to survive.
> “Listen, you people, all of you!” is the same sentence as the first sentence in the book of the prophet Micah (Micah 1:2).

## The death of Ahab (Verses 28–34)

[28] So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah went up to Ramoth Gilead.
[29] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“I will disguise myself and go into the battle,
but you put on your robes.”
So the king of Israel disguised himself,
and they went into the battle.
[30] The king of Syria had commanded the captains of his chariots,
“Don’t fight with small or great,
but only with the king of Israel.”
[31] The captains of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat.
They said, “It is the king of Israel!”
So they turned around to fight against him.
But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him.
And God moved them to go away from him.
[32] The captains of the chariots saw that it was not the king of Israel.
Then they turned back from chasing him.
[33] A certain man shot his bow at random
and struck the king of Israel between the joints of the armor.
So he said to the driver of the chariot,
“Turn around and carry me out of the battle,
for I am badly wounded.”
[34] The battle grew fierce that day.
But the king of Israel held himself up in his chariot facing the Syrians until the evening.
And at about sunset he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab tries to escape the prophecy. He disguises himself and lets Jehoshaphat fight as the only king in royal clothes. That was dangerous and unfair to Jehoshaphat.
> But you cannot trick God. An arrow shot “at random” strikes Ahab exactly at the weak spot of the armor.
> Chronicles adds something that is not in 1 Kings 22: “The LORD helped him. And God moved them to go away from him” (2 Chronicles 18:31). Jehoshaphat cries out to God, and God saves him, even though he had gotten involved with Ahab. God’s grace is greater than our mistakes.
> Ahab reigned from about 874 to 853 before Christ.
