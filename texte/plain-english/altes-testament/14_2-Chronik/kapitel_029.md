# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 29
### Hezekiah opens the temple again

---

## Hezekiah’s call to the priests and Levites (Verses 1–11)

[1] Hezekiah became king when he was 25 years old.
He ruled for 29 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Abijah.
She was the daughter of Zechariah.
[2] Hezekiah did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his ancestor David had done.
[3] In the first month of his first year as king,
he opened the doors of the LORD’s house and repaired them.
[4] He brought in the priests and the Levites.
He gathered them in the open square on the east side.
[5] He said to them,
“Listen to me, you Levites!
Make yourselves holy now.
And make the house of the LORD, the God of your ancestors, holy.
Carry all the filth out of the holy place.
[6] Our ancestors were unfaithful.
They did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD our God.
They left him.
They turned their faces away from the place where the LORD lives.
They turned their backs on him.
[7] They even locked the doors of the porch
and put out the lamps.
They stopped burning incense in the holy place for the God of Israel.
They stopped offering burnt offerings there.
[8] That is why the LORD became angry with Judah and Jerusalem.
He has let them be tossed back and forth.
People are shocked when they see them, and they hiss at them.
You can see it with your own eyes.
[9] Our fathers have been killed with the sword.
Our sons, our daughters, and our wives have been taken captive because of this.
[10] Now I want to make a covenant with the LORD, the God of Israel.
Then his fierce anger will turn away from us.
[11] My sons, do not be careless now.
The LORD has chosen you to stand before him and to serve him.
You are to be his servants and burn incense.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah ruled from about 715 to 686 BC. He is one of the best kings of Judah. In three chapters (29–31), Chronicles tells how he renewed the worship.
> He does not wait a single day: right in the first month, he opens the temple that his father had closed.
> Hezekiah says honestly: our ancestors were unfaithful. He does not make anything look better than it was. And he explains the distress of the people by their turning away from God.
> A “covenant” is a solemn promise. Hezekiah wants to begin completely new with God.
> Lovingly, he calls the Levites “my sons.”

## The Levites clean the temple (Verses 12–19)

[12] Then these Levites got to work:
From the Kohathites: Mahath, the son of Amasai, and Joel, the son of Azariah.
From the Merarites: Kish, the son of Abdi, and Azariah, the son of Jehallelel.
From the Gershonites: Joah, the son of Zimmah, and Eden, the son of Joah.
[13] From the sons of Elizaphan: Shimri and Jeuel.
From the sons of Asaph: Zechariah and Mattaniah.
[14] From the sons of Heman: Jehuel and Shimei.
From the sons of Jeduthun: Shemaiah and Uzziel.
[15] They gathered their relatives, and they made themselves holy.
Then they went in to clean the LORD’s house.
They did what the king had commanded,
following the words of the LORD.
[16] The priests went into the inner part of the LORD’s house to clean it.
They brought out everything unclean that they found in the LORD’s temple.
They brought it into the courtyard of the LORD’s house.
From there, the Levites carried it out to the Kidron Valley.
[17] They began to make everything holy on the first day of the first month.
On the eighth day of the month, they reached the porch of the LORD’s house.
Then they made the LORD’s house holy in eight days.
They finished on the sixteenth day of the first month.
[18] Then they went in to King Hezekiah in the palace.
They said,
“We have cleaned the whole house of the LORD.
We have cleaned the altar of burnt offering with all its objects.
And we have cleaned the table for the holy bread with all its objects.
[19] King Ahaz threw away objects during his rule, when he was unfaithful.
We have prepared all these objects and made them holy.
They are now in front of the LORD’s altar.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Representatives of all the Levite families help: the three great families of Kohath, Merari and Gershon, and the singer families of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun.
> Only the priests were allowed into the inner part of the temple. They carried out the filth, and the Levites took it away. Everyone did their part.
> The “filth” was probably idols, foreign altars and dirt from the years when the temple was closed.
> The cleaning took 16 days. It was a lot of work, but it was done with devotion.

## Offerings to make peace with God for the whole people (Verses 20–24)

[20] Then King Hezekiah got up early.
He gathered the leaders of the city and went up to the LORD’s house.
[21] They brought seven bulls, seven rams, seven lambs, and seven male goats.
They were a sin offering for the kingdom, for the holy place, and for Judah.
Hezekiah commanded the priests, the sons of Aaron,
to offer them on the LORD’s altar.
[22] So they killed the bulls.
The priests caught the blood and sprinkled it on the altar.
They killed the rams and sprinkled the blood on the altar.
They killed the lambs and sprinkled the blood on the altar.
[23] Then they brought the male goats for the sin offering
to the king and the assembly.
They laid their hands on the goats.
[24] Then the priests killed the goats.
They put their blood on the altar as a sin offering.
This made atonement for all Israel.
Atonement means: the guilt is covered, and there is peace with God again.
The king had commanded that the burnt offering and the sin offering
be made for all Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before normal worship begins again, forgiveness is needed. The sin offerings are to make up for the guilt of the past years.
> In the Bible, the number seven stands for completeness.
> Laying on hands means: we put our guilt on this animal. It dies in our place (compare Leviticus 4 and 16:21).
> Important: “for all Israel.” Hezekiah also thinks of the northern kingdom. Christians see in these offerings a pointer to Jesus, who according to the New Testament died once for all for the guilt of all people (Hebrews 10:10–12).

## Music and worship (Verses 25–30)

[25] Hezekiah placed the Levites in the LORD’s house.
They had cymbals, stringed instruments, and harps.
This is what David had commanded,
and Gad, the king’s seer, and Nathan the prophet.
A seer is a prophet who sees visions from God.
The command came from the LORD through his prophets.
[26] The Levites stood ready with David’s instruments.
The priests stood ready with the trumpets.
[27] Hezekiah gave the order to offer the burnt offering on the altar.
When the burnt offering began,
the song for the LORD began too.
The trumpets and the instruments of David, king of Israel, played along.
[28] The whole assembly worshiped.
The singers sang,
and the trumpet players blew their trumpets.
All this went on until the burnt offering was finished.
[29] When the offering was finished,
the king and everyone with him knelt down and worshiped.
[30] King Hezekiah and the leaders told the Levites
to praise the LORD with the words of David and of Asaph the seer.
So they sang praises with joy.
They bowed their heads and worshiped.

> **What does this mean?**
> The music in the temple was not only David’s idea. Verse 25 says: it came from God through prophets.
> When the offering begins, the singing begins too. Forgiveness and joy belong together.
> “With the words of David and of Asaph”: these are psalms. Many psalms carry the name of David or Asaph (for example, Psalm 50 and 73–83 by Asaph).

## The people bring offerings willingly (Verses 31–36)

[31] Then Hezekiah said,
“Now you have given yourselves to the LORD.
Come and bring sacrifices and thank offerings into the LORD’s house.”
So the assembly brought sacrifices and thank offerings.
Everyone who had a willing heart brought burnt offerings.
[32] The assembly brought 70 bulls, 100 rams, and 200 lambs as burnt offerings.
All of these were burnt offerings for the LORD.
[33] The other holy offerings were 600 cattle and 3,000 sheep.
[34] But there were not enough priests
to skin all the burnt offerings.
So their relatives, the Levites, helped them until the work was done.
They helped until more priests had made themselves holy.
The Levites had been more careful than the priests to make themselves holy.
[35] There were also very many burnt offerings.
And there was the fat of the peace offerings,
and the drink offerings for each burnt offering.
So the service in the LORD’s house was set up again.
[36] Hezekiah and all the people were full of joy
about what God had prepared for the people.
Everything had happened so quickly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the people themselves come. No one is forced. Those give “who had a willing heart.”
> An honest remark in verse 34: the Levites were more eager than the priests. Chronicles is not afraid to criticize the priests too.
> Verse 36: the joy was great because everything happened so quickly. The people were ready for a new beginning. Chronicles says: God himself had prepared it.
