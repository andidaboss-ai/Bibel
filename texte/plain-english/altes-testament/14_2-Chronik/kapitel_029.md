# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 29
### Hezekiah opens the temple again

---

## Hezekiah’s call to the priests and Levites (Verses 1–11)

[1] Hezekiah began to reign when he was 25 years old,
and he reigned for 29 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Abijah, the daughter of Zechariah.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
just as David his father had done.
[3] In the first year of his reign, in the first month,
he opened the doors of the LORD’s house and repaired them.
[4] He brought in the priests and the Levites
and gathered them in the open square on the east.
[5] He said to them,
“Listen to me, you Levites!
Now make yourselves holy,
and make holy the house of the LORD, the God of your fathers.
Carry the filth out of the holy place.
[6] For our fathers were unfaithful.
They did what was evil in the sight of the LORD our God,
and they abandoned him.
They turned their faces away from the LORD’s dwelling place
and turned their backs on it.
[7] They also shut the doors of the porch
and put out the lamps.
They did not burn incense or offer burnt offerings in the holy place to the God of Israel.
[8] That is why the LORD’s anger was on Judah and Jerusalem.
He has made them something that is tossed back and forth,
something that shocks people and that they hiss at,
as you see with your own eyes.
[9] For look, our fathers have fallen by the sword,
and our sons and our daughters and our wives are in captivity because of this.
[10] Now it is in my heart to make a covenant with the LORD, the God of Israel,
so that his fierce anger may turn away from us.
[11] My sons, don’t be careless now.
For the LORD has chosen you to stand before him, to serve him,
to be his servants and to burn incense.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah reigned from about 715 to 686 before Christ. He is one of the best kings of Judah. In three chapters (29–31), Chronicles tells how he renewed the worship.
> He does not wait a single day: right in the first month he opens the temple that his father had closed.
> Hezekiah says honestly: our fathers were unfaithful. He does not make anything look better than it was. And he explains the distress of the people by their turning away from God.
> A “covenant” is a solemn promise. Hezekiah wants to begin completely new with God.
> Lovingly he calls the Levites “my sons”.

## The Levites clean the temple (Verses 12–19)

[12] Then the Levites got up:
Mahath the son of Amasai and Joel the son of Azariah, of the sons of the Kohathites;
of the sons of Merari, Kish the son of Abdi and Azariah the son of Jehallelel;
of the Gershonites, Joah the son of Zimmah and Eden the son of Joah;
[13] of the sons of Elizaphan, Shimri and Jeuel;
of the sons of Asaph, Zechariah and Mattaniah;
[14] of the sons of Heman, Jehuel and Shimei;
and of the sons of Jeduthun, Shemaiah and Uzziel.
[15] They gathered their brothers and made themselves holy.
Then they went in, as the king had commanded by the LORD’s words,
to clean the LORD’s house.
[16] The priests went into the inner part of the LORD’s house to clean it.
They brought out all the unclean things that they found in the LORD’s temple
into the court of the LORD’s house.
The Levites took it from there and carried it out to the brook Kidron.
[17] They began to make things holy on the first day of the first month.
On the eighth day of the month they came to the LORD’s porch.
They made the LORD’s house holy in eight days,
and on the sixteenth day of the first month they finished.
[18] Then they went in to King Hezekiah in the palace and said,
“We have cleaned the whole house of the LORD,
the altar of burnt offering with all its objects,
and the table of show bread with all its objects.
[19] We have also prepared and made holy all the objects
that King Ahaz threw away during his reign, when he was unfaithful.
Look, they are in front of the LORD’s altar.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Representatives of all the Levite families help: the three great families of Kohath, Merari and Gershon, and the singer families of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun.
> Only the priests were allowed into the inner part of the temple. They carried out the filth, and the Levites took it away. Everyone did their part.
> The “filth” was probably idols, foreign altars and dirt from the years when the temple was closed.
> The cleaning took 16 days. It was a lot of work, but it was done with devotion.

## Offerings to make peace with God for the whole people (Verses 20–24)

[20] Then King Hezekiah got up early.
He gathered the princes of the city and went up to the LORD’s house.
[21] They brought seven bulls, seven rams, seven lambs, and seven male goats,
as a sin offering for the kingdom, for the holy place, and for Judah.
He commanded the priests, the sons of Aaron,
to offer them on the LORD’s altar.
[22] So they killed the bulls,
and the priests received the blood and sprinkled it on the altar.
They killed the rams and sprinkled the blood on the altar.
They also killed the lambs and sprinkled the blood on the altar.
[23] They brought the male goats for the sin offering
before the king and the assembly,
and they laid their hands on them.
[24] Then the priests killed them,
and they made a sin offering with their blood on the altar,
to make atonement for all Israel.
Atonement means: the guilt is covered, and there is peace with God again.
For the king had commanded that the burnt offering and the sin offering
should be made for all Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before normal worship begins again, forgiveness is needed. The sin offerings are to make up for the guilt of the past years.
> In the Bible the number seven stands for completeness.
> Laying on hands means: we put our guilt on this animal. It dies in our place (compare Leviticus 4 and 16:21).
> Important: “for all Israel”. Hezekiah also thinks of the northern kingdom. Christians see in these offerings a pointer to Jesus, who according to the New Testament died once for all for the guilt of all people (Hebrews 10:10–12).

## Music and worship (Verses 25–30)

[25] He set the Levites in the LORD’s house
with cymbals, with stringed instruments, and with harps,
as David, Gad the king’s seer, and Nathan the prophet had commanded.
For the command came from the LORD through his prophets.
[26] The Levites stood with David’s instruments,
and the priests with the trumpets.
[27] Hezekiah commanded them to offer the burnt offering on the altar.
When the burnt offering began,
the LORD’s song also began,
together with the trumpets and the instruments of David king of Israel.
[28] All the assembly worshiped,
the singers sang,
and the trumpeters blew.
All this went on until the burnt offering was finished.
[29] When they had finished offering,
the king and all who were with him bowed down and worshiped.
[30] King Hezekiah and the princes also commanded the Levites
to sing praises to the LORD with the words of David and of Asaph the seer.
They sang praises with gladness,
and they bowed their heads and worshiped.

> **What does this mean?**
> The music in the temple was not only David’s idea. Verse 25 says: it came from God through prophets.
> When the offering begins, the singing begins too. Forgiveness and joy belong together.
> “With the words of David and of Asaph”: these are psalms. Many psalms carry the name of David or Asaph (for example Psalm 50 and 73–83 by Asaph).

## The people bring offerings willingly (Verses 31–36)

[31] Then Hezekiah said,
“Now you have dedicated yourselves to the LORD.
Come near, and bring sacrifices and thank offerings into the LORD’s house.”
So the assembly brought in sacrifices and thank offerings,
and all who had a willing heart brought burnt offerings.
[32] The number of the burnt offerings that the assembly brought
was 70 bulls, 100 rams, and 200 lambs.
All these were for a burnt offering to the LORD.
[33] The holy offerings were 600 head of cattle and 3,000 sheep.
[34] But there were too few priests,
so that they could not skin all the burnt offerings.
So their brothers the Levites helped them until the work was finished,
and until the priests had made themselves holy.
For the Levites were more honest in heart to make themselves holy than the priests.
[35] There were also very many burnt offerings,
with the fat of the peace offerings,
and with the drink offerings for every burnt offering.
So the service of the LORD’s house was set in order.
[36] Hezekiah and all the people rejoiced
because of what God had prepared for the people.
For it had happened quickly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the people themselves come. No one is forced. Those give “who had a willing heart”.
> An honest remark in verse 34: the Levites were more eager than the priests. Chronicles is not afraid to criticize the priests too.
> Verse 36: the joy was great because everything happened so quickly. The people were ready for a new beginning. Chronicles says: God himself had prepared it.
