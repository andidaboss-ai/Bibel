# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 23
### Joash becomes king

---

## The covenant with the captains (Verses 1–3)

[1] In the seventh year, Jehoiada found new courage.
He made a covenant with the captains of hundreds.
These were officers who each led 100 men.
Their names were:
Azariah, the son of Jeroham,
Ishmael, the son of Jehohanan,
Azariah, the son of Obed,
Maaseiah, the son of Adaiah,
and Elishaphat, the son of Zichri.
[2] They went all around Judah.
They gathered the Levites from all the cities of Judah.
They also gathered the heads of the families of Israel.
Then they came to Jerusalem.
[3] The whole assembly made a covenant with the king in God’s house.
Jehoiada said to them,
“Look, the king’s son must be king.
The LORD promised this about the sons of David.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 11. But Chronicles tells it with other main points: here the Levites and priests play the main role. In 2 Kings, it is more the bodyguard and the Carites (foreign soldiers).
> Jehoiada does not act out of greed for power. He relies on God’s promise: a descendant of David is to be king.

---

## The plan (Verses 4–10)

[4] This is what you must do:
Some of you priests and Levites start your duty on the Sabbath.
A third of you will guard the doors.
[5] A third of you will be at the king’s house.
And a third of you will be at the Foundation Gate.
All the people will stay in the courtyards of the LORD’s house.
[6] No one may come into the LORD’s house
except the priests and the Levites who serve there.
They may come in, because they are holy.
But all the people must obey the LORD’s instructions.
[7] The Levites must stand all around the king.
Each man must hold his weapons in his hand.
Anyone who comes into the house must be killed.
Stay with the king wherever he goes.”
[8] The Levites and all Judah did everything Jehoiada the priest commanded.
Each leader took his men.
He took the men who were starting their duty on the Sabbath.
And he took the men who were ending their duty on the Sabbath.
Jehoiada the priest did not send any group home.
[9] Jehoiada the priest gave the captains of hundreds
the spears and the small and large shields that had belonged to King David.
They were kept in God’s house.
[10] He put all the people in their places.
Each man held his weapon in his hand.
They stood from the right side of the house to the left side,
near the altar and the house, all around the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada uses the Sabbath, because that is when the groups on duty change. The old group and the new group are there at the same time. So he has twice as many men without anyone noticing.
> “The Foundation Gate”: nobody knows exactly where it was. In 2 Kings 11:6, it is called “the gate Sur.”
> Important for Chronicles: only the priests and Levites may go into the temple. Even during this uprising, the holiness of the temple is respected.
> David’s own weapons are used. That is a sign: here David’s rightful descendant comes to the throne.

---

## The crowning (Verse 11)

[11] Then they brought out the king’s son.
They put the crown on him.
They gave him the covenant.
And they made him king.
Jehoiada and his sons anointed him.
They shouted, “Long live the king!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The covenant”: the Hebrew says “the testimony.” This probably means a scroll with God’s law or with the covenant. The king is to rule according to God’s word (Deuteronomy 17:18–19).
> Joash is only seven years old (Chapter 24:1).

---

## The end of Athaliah (Verses 12–15)

[12] Athaliah heard the noise of the people.
They were running and cheering for the king.
So she went to the people in the LORD’s house.
[13] She looked and saw the king.
He was standing by his pillar at the entrance.
The captains and the trumpet players were next to the king.
All the people of the land were full of joy and blew trumpets.
The singers played their instruments
and led the songs of praise.
Then Athaliah tore her clothes and shouted,
“Treason! Treason!”
[14] Jehoiada the priest called out the captains of hundreds who led the army.
He said to them,
“Take her out between the rows of soldiers.
Kill anyone who follows her with the sword.”
The priest had said,
“Don’t kill her in the LORD’s house.”
[15] So they made way for her.
She came to the entrance of the Horse Gate at the king’s house.
There they killed her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Athaliah cries “Treason!” But she herself had murdered her grandchildren and stolen the throne.
> Again, Jehoiada makes sure that the temple is not made unholy by blood.
> The Bible tells of Athaliah’s death as the end of a violent rule. She herself had shed a lot of blood.

---

## A new covenant and a new beginning (Verses 16–21)

[16] Jehoiada made a covenant between himself, all the people, and the king.
They promised to be the LORD’s people.
[17] Then all the people went to the temple of Baal and tore it down.
They smashed his altars and his idols.
They killed Mattan, the priest of Baal, in front of the altars.
[18] Jehoiada put officers in charge of the LORD’s house.
They worked under the Levitical priests.
David had divided these priests into groups for the LORD’s house.
They were to offer the burnt offerings to the LORD,
as it is written in the Law of Moses.
They were to do it with joy and singing,
as David had ordered.
[19] Jehoiada put gatekeepers at the gates of the LORD’s house.
So no one who was unclean in any way could go in.
[20] He took the captains of hundreds, the nobles, the rulers of the people,
and all the people of the land.
Together they led the king down from the LORD’s house.
They went through the Upper Gate to the king’s house.
There they set the king on the royal throne.
[21] All the people of the land were full of joy.
And the city was calm,
because they had killed Athaliah with the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> The most important step is the covenant in verse 16: the king and the people promise to be “the LORD’s people.”
> Athaliah had brought the worship of Baal to Jerusalem. Now the temple of Baal is destroyed.
> Jehoiada puts the worship in order again, “as David had ordered,” with joy and singing.
> “The city was calm”: after years of fear, peace comes.
