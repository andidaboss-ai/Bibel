# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 23
### Joash becomes king

---

## The covenant with the captains (Verses 1–3)

[1] In the seventh year Jehoiada took courage.
He made a covenant with the captains of hundreds:
Azariah the son of Jeroham,
Ishmael the son of Jehohanan,
Azariah the son of Obed,
Maaseiah the son of Adaiah,
and Elishaphat the son of Zichri.
[2] They went around in Judah
and gathered the Levites out of all the cities of Judah,
and the heads of the families of Israel.
And they came to Jerusalem.
[3] All the assembly made a covenant with the king in God’s house.
Jehoiada said to them,
“Look, the king’s son must reign,
as the LORD has spoken about the sons of David.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 11. But Chronicles tells it with other main points: here the Levites and priests play the main role. In 2 Kings it is rather the bodyguard and the Carites (foreign soldiers).
> Jehoiada does not act out of greed for power. He appeals to God’s promise: a descendant of David is to be king.

---

## The plan (Verses 4–10)

[4] This is what you must do:
A third of you priests and Levites who come on duty on the Sabbath
shall be gatekeepers at the doorways.
[5] A third shall be at the king’s house,
and a third at the gate of the foundation.
All the people will be in the courts of the LORD’s house.
[6] But let no one come into the LORD’s house
except the priests and the Levites who serve.
They may come in, for they are holy.
But all the people shall follow the LORD’s instructions.
[7] The Levites shall surround the king,
every man with his weapons in his hand.
Whoever comes into the house must be killed.
Be with the king when he comes in and when he goes out.”
[8] So the Levites and all Judah did all that Jehoiada the priest commanded.
Each of them took his men,
those who were to come on duty on the Sabbath
and those who were to go off duty on the Sabbath.
For Jehoiada the priest didn’t send the groups home.
[9] Jehoiada the priest gave to the captains of hundreds
the spears and the small and large shields that had belonged to King David.
They were in God’s house.
[10] He placed all the people, every man with his weapon in his hand,
from the right side of the house to the left side of the house,
near the altar and the house, around the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada uses the Sabbath, because then the groups on duty change. The old and the new group are there at the same time. So he has twice as many men without anyone noticing.
> “The gate of the foundation”: nobody knows exactly where it was. In 2 Kings 11:6 it is called “the gate Sur”.
> Important for Chronicles: only the priests and Levites may go into the temple. Even during this uprising, the holiness of the temple is respected.
> David’s own weapons are used. That is a sign: here David’s rightful descendant comes to the throne.

---

## The crowning (Verse 11)

[11] Then they brought out the king’s son,
put the crown on him,
gave him the covenant,
and made him king.
Jehoiada and his sons anointed him,
and they said, “Long live the king!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The covenant”: the Hebrew says “the testimony”. This probably means a scroll with God’s law or with the covenant. The king is to rule according to God’s word (Deuteronomy 17:18–19).
> Joash is only seven years old (Chapter 24:1).

---

## The end of Athaliah (Verses 12–15)

[12] Athaliah heard the noise of the people running and praising the king.
So she came to the people in the LORD’s house.
[13] She looked,
and there the king stood by his pillar at the entrance,
with the captains and the trumpeters by the king.
All the people of the land rejoiced and blew trumpets.
The singers also played musical instruments
and led the singing of praise.
Then Athaliah tore her clothes and said,
“Treason! Treason!”
[14] Jehoiada the priest brought out the captains of hundreds who were set over the army
and said to them,
“Bring her out between the rows of soldiers.
Whoever follows her must be killed with the sword.”
For the priest said,
“Don’t kill her in the LORD’s house.”
[15] So they made way for her.
She went to the entrance of the horse gate to the king’s house,
and they killed her there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Athaliah cries “Treason!” But she herself had murdered her grandchildren and stolen the throne.
> Again Jehoiada makes sure that the temple is not made unholy by blood.
> The Bible tells Athaliah’s death as the end of a rule of violence. She herself had shed very much blood.

---

## A new covenant and a new beginning (Verses 16–21)

[16] Jehoiada made a covenant between himself, all the people, and the king,
that they would be the LORD’s people.
[17] All the people went to the house of Baal and tore it down.
They broke his altars and his images in pieces,
and they killed Mattan the priest of Baal in front of the altars.
[18] Jehoiada appointed the officers of the LORD’s house
under the hand of the Levitical priests,
whom David had divided into groups in the LORD’s house,
to offer the burnt offerings of the LORD,
as it is written in the law of Moses,
with rejoicing and with singing,
as David had ordered.
[19] He set the gatekeepers at the gates of the LORD’s house,
so that no one who was unclean in any way could enter.
[20] He took the captains of hundreds, the nobles, the governors of the people,
and all the people of the land,
and brought the king down from the LORD’s house.
They came through the upper gate to the king’s house
and set the king on the throne of the kingdom.
[21] So all the people of the land rejoiced,
and the city was quiet.
They had killed Athaliah with the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> The most important step is the covenant in verse 16: the king and the people promise to be “the LORD’s people”.
> Athaliah had brought the worship of Baal to Jerusalem. Now the temple of Baal is destroyed.
> Jehoiada puts the worship in order again, “as David had ordered”, with joy and singing.
> “The city was quiet”: after years of fear, peace comes.
