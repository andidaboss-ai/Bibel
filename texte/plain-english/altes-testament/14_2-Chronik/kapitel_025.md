# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 25
### Amaziah and the gods of Edom

---

## Amaziah becomes king (Verses 1–4)

[1] Amaziah was 25 years old when he became king.
He ruled for 29 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jehoaddan.
She came from Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
but not with a perfect heart.
[3] When he had firm control of the kingdom,
he killed the servants who had killed his father, the king.
[4] But he did not kill their children.
He did what is written in the Law, in the book of Moses.
There the LORD commanded:
“Parents must not die for their children,
and children must not die for their parents.
Each person must die for their own sin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 14.
> Amaziah ruled from about 796 to 767 BC.
> Verse 2 sums up his life: he did what was right, “but not with a perfect heart.” He was only half in it. This will show itself in the course of the chapter.
> Verse 4 quotes Deuteronomy 24:16. Back then, this was a great step forward: it was usual to kill the whole family of a traitor. God’s law says: each person is responsible only for their own guilt (compare Ezekiel 18:20).

---

## The hired soldiers from Israel (Verses 5–10)

[5] Amaziah called the people of Judah together.
He put them in order by their families.
He put them under captains of thousands and captains of hundreds.
He did this with all Judah and Benjamin.
He counted the men who were 20 years old or older.
There were 300,000 chosen men.
They were ready to go to war.
They could fight with spear and shield.
[6] He also hired 100,000 brave warriors from Israel.
He paid 100 talents of silver for them, about 7,500 pounds.
[7] But a man of God came to him and said,
“King, do not let the army of Israel go with you.
The LORD is not with Israel.
He is not with any of the people of Ephraim.
[8] If you go anyway, then fight bravely!
But God will let you fall before the enemy.
God has the power to help and the power to make someone fall.”
[9] Amaziah said to the man of God,
“But what about the 100 talents that I have given to the army of Israel?”
The man of God answered,
“The LORD can give you much more than this.”
[10] So Amaziah sent away the army that had come to him from Ephraim.
He sent them back home.
This made them very angry with Judah.
They went home full of rage.

> **What does this mean?**
> A talent is about 75 pounds. So 100 talents of silver are about 7,500 pounds of silver.
> Amaziah buys soldiers from the northern kingdom. The prophet warns: do not rely on an army that does not serve God. “Ephraim” is another name for the northern kingdom of Israel here.
> Amaziah’s question in verse 9 is very human: “But what about the 100 talents …?” The prophet’s answer is a lesson in faith: God can give you much more. Sometimes you have to accept a loss in order to do what is right.
> Amaziah obeys. That is good. But it has consequences (verse 13).

---

## The war against Edom (Verses 11–13)

[11] Amaziah found courage.
He led his army out to the Valley of Salt.
There he killed 10,000 of the people of Seir.
[12] The men of Judah also captured 10,000 people alive.
They took them to the top of the rock.
They threw them down from the top of the rock,
and all of them were dashed to pieces.
[13] Meanwhile, the soldiers Amaziah had sent home
so that they would not go with him into battle
attacked the cities of Judah, from Samaria to Beth Horon.
They killed 3,000 people there
and took a lot of plunder.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “people of Seir” are the Edomites. Mount Seir was in Edom, southeast of the Dead Sea.
> Verse 12 reports a terrible cruelty. The Bible does not praise it. It only tells what happened. Killing prisoners like this is a crime. That was seen as cruel even back then (compare Amos 1–2, where God condemns cruelties in war among many peoples).
> In Hebrew, “rock” is “Sela.” Maybe the place Sela is meant, which Amaziah conquered according to 2 Kings 14:7.
> The angry soldiers from Israel take revenge on Judah. It is hard to say how “Samaria” fits into this description of the place, because Samaria was in Israel. Maybe it means: they came from the direction of Samaria.

---

## Amaziah worships the gods of Edom (Verses 14–16)

[14] Amaziah came back from killing the Edomites.
He brought the gods of the people of Seir with him.
He set them up as his own gods.
He bowed down to them and burned incense to them.
[15] So the LORD became very angry with Amaziah.
He sent a prophet to him.
The prophet said,
“Why have you turned to the gods of this people?
They could not even save their own people from you!”
[16] While the prophet was still speaking, the king said to him,
“Have we made you one of the king’s advisers?
Stop! Do you want to be killed?”
Then the prophet stopped and said,
“I know that God has decided to destroy you.
You have done this,
and you have not listened to my advice.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is almost unbelievable: Amaziah defeats Edom with God’s help. And then he worships the gods of the defeated people! The prophet shows how foolish this is: these gods could not even save their own people.
> Earlier, Amaziah had listened to a prophet (verse 10). Now he threatens the prophet. That is the heart that is “not perfect” from verse 2.
> A play on words: the king asks mockingly, “Have we made you one of the king’s advisers?” The prophet answers: because you have not listened to my advice, God will destroy you.

---

## Amaziah challenges Israel (Verses 17–24)

[17] Then Amaziah, king of Judah, talked with his advisers.
He sent a message to Joash, king of Israel.
Joash was the son of Jehoahaz, the son of Jehu.
Amaziah said,
“Come! Let’s look one another in the face.”
[18] Joash, king of Israel, sent this answer to Amaziah, king of Judah:
“A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar in Lebanon.
It said, ‘Give your daughter to my son as his wife.’
Then a wild animal in Lebanon came by
and trampled the thistle.
[19] You tell yourself, ‘I have defeated Edom.’
And now you are proud and boast.
Stay at home!
Why are you looking for trouble?
You will fall, and Judah will fall with you.”
[20] But Amaziah would not listen.
This came from God.
God wanted to give them into the power of their enemies,
because they had turned to the gods of Edom.
[21] So Joash, king of Israel, marched out.
He and Amaziah, king of Judah, looked one another in the face at Beth Shemesh.
Beth Shemesh belongs to Judah.
[22] Israel defeated Judah.
Every man ran away to his home.
[23] Joash, king of Israel, captured Amaziah, king of Judah, at Beth Shemesh.
Amaziah was the son of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz.
Joash brought him to Jerusalem.
He tore down the wall of Jerusalem from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate.
That is a stretch of about 600 feet.
[24] He took all the gold and silver.
He took all the objects in God’s house that were in the care of Obed-Edom.
He took the treasures of the king’s house.
He also took hostages.
Then he went back to Samaria.

> **What does this mean?**
> Maybe Amaziah wanted to take revenge on Israel for the raids in verse 13.
> “Let’s look one another in the face” means: let’s fight.
> Joash answers with a fable: a small thistle wants to be equal with the great cedar, and it is trampled. He means: you think too much of yourself.
> Verse 20 explains from the point of view of Chronicles why Amaziah did not listen: his worship of idols led him into ruin.
> The Bible says “400 cubits.” A cubit is about 18 inches.
> “Obed-Edom”: a family of gatekeepers who guarded the treasures in the temple (1 Chronicles 26:15).
> Verse 23: the English source text says “the son of Jehoahaz.” 2 Kings 14:13 says “the son of Ahaziah.” Jehoahaz and Ahaziah are two names for the same king (see Chapter 21:17 and 22:1).

## The end of Amaziah (Verses 25–28)

[25] Amaziah, the son of Joash, king of Judah,
lived for 15 more years after the death of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.
[26] There is more to tell about Amaziah, from beginning to end.
It is written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.
[27] Amaziah turned away from following the LORD.
From that time on, people in Jerusalem made plans against him.
He fled to Lachish.
But they sent men after him to Lachish, and they killed him there.
[28] They carried his body back on horses.
They buried him with his ancestors in the City of Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like his father Joash, Amaziah is murdered too.
> Chronicles connects the plot with his turning away from God: from then on, everything went downhill.
> The “City of Judah” is Jerusalem, the City of David. 2 Kings 14:20 says “David’s city.”
