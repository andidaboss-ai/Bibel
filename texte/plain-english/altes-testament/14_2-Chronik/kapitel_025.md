# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 25
### Amaziah and the gods of Edom

---

## Amaziah becomes king (Verses 1–4)

[1] Amaziah was 25 years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for 29 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jehoaddan, from Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
but not with a perfect heart.
[3] When the kingdom was firmly in his hand,
he killed his servants who had killed his father the king.
[4] But he didn’t put their children to death.
He did what is written in the law, in the book of Moses,
as the LORD commanded:
“The fathers shall not die for the children,
and the children shall not die for the fathers.
But every man shall die for his own sin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 14.
> Amaziah reigned from about 796 to 767 before Christ.
> Verse 2 sums up his life: he did what was right, “but not with a perfect heart”. He was only half in it. This will show itself in the course of the chapter.
> Verse 4 quotes Deuteronomy 24:16. Back then this was a great step forward: it was usual to kill the whole family of a traitor. God’s law says: each person is responsible only for his own guilt (compare Ezekiel 18:20).

---

## The hired soldiers from Israel (Verses 5–10)

[5] Amaziah gathered Judah together.
He put them in order by their families,
under captains of thousands and captains of hundreds,
all Judah and Benjamin.
He counted them from 20 years old and upward.
He found that there were 300,000 chosen men,
able to go out to war, who could handle spear and shield.
[6] He also hired 100,000 mighty men of courage out of Israel
for 100 talents of silver, about 7,500 pounds.
[7] But a man of God came to him and said,
“King, don’t let the army of Israel go with you,
for the LORD is not with Israel, with all the children of Ephraim.
[8] But if you will go, then act and be strong for the battle!
God will throw you down before the enemy.
For God has power to help and to throw down.”
[9] Amaziah said to the man of God,
“But what shall we do about the 7,500 pounds of silver that I have given to the army of Israel?”
The man of God answered,
“The LORD is able to give you much more than this.”
[10] Then Amaziah separated the army that had come to him out of Ephraim,
so that they would go home again.
So they became very angry with Judah,
and they returned home in fierce anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says “100 talents of silver”. A talent is about 75 pounds.
> Amaziah buys soldiers from the northern kingdom. The prophet warns: do not rely on an army that does not serve God. “Ephraim” is another name for the northern kingdom of Israel here.
> Amaziah’s question in verse 9 is very human: “But what shall we do about the 7,500 pounds of silver …?” The prophet’s answer is a lesson in faith: God can give you much more. Sometimes you have to accept a loss in order to do what is right.
> Amaziah obeys. That is good. But it has consequences (verse 13).

---

## The war against Edom (Verses 11–13)

[11] Amaziah took courage.
He led his people out and went to the Valley of Salt.
There he struck 10,000 of the children of Seir.
[12] The children of Judah carried away another 10,000 alive.
They brought them to the top of the rock
and threw them down from the top of the rock,
so that they were all broken in pieces.
[13] But the men of the army whom Amaziah had sent back,
so that they would not go with him to battle,
attacked the cities of Judah, from Samaria to Beth Horon.
They struck 3,000 of them
and took much plunder.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “children of Seir” are the Edomites. Mount Seir was in Edom, southeast of the Dead Sea.
> Verse 12 reports a terrible cruelty. The Bible does not praise it. It only tells what happened. Killing prisoners like this is a crime. That was seen as cruel even back then (compare Amos 1–2, where God condemns cruelties in war among many peoples).
> In Hebrew, “rock” is “Sela”. Maybe the place Sela is meant, which Amaziah conquered according to 2 Kings 14:7.
> The angry soldiers from Israel take revenge on Judah. It is hard to say how “Samaria” fits into this place description, for Samaria was in Israel. Maybe it means: they came from the direction of Samaria.

---

## Amaziah worships the gods of Edom (Verses 14–16)

[14] Amaziah came back from killing the Edomites.
Then he brought the gods of the children of Seir
and set them up to be his gods.
He bowed down before them and burned incense to them.
[15] So the LORD’s anger burned against Amaziah.
He sent a prophet to him, who said to him,
“Why have you sought the gods of this people,
who have not saved their own people out of your hand?”
[16] As he was talking with him, the king said to him,
“Have we made you one of the king’s advisers?
Stop! Why should you be struck down?”
Then the prophet stopped and said,
“I know that God has decided to destroy you,
because you have done this
and have not listened to my advice.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is almost unbelievable: Amaziah defeats Edom with God’s help. And then he worships the gods of the defeated people! The prophet shows how foolish this is: these gods could not even save their own people.
> Earlier Amaziah had listened to a prophet (verse 10). Now he threatens the prophet. That is the heart that is “not perfect” from verse 2.
> A play on words: the king asks mockingly, “Have we made you one of the king’s advisers?” The prophet answers: because you have not listened to my advice, God will destroy you.

## Amaziah challenges Israel (Verses 17–24)

[17] Then Amaziah king of Judah talked with his advisers.
He sent to Joash king of Israel, the son of Jehoahaz, the son of Jehu, and said,
“Come! Let’s look one another in the face.”
[18] Joash king of Israel sent this answer to Amaziah king of Judah:
“The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon and said,
‘Give your daughter to my son as his wife.’
Then a wild animal that was in Lebanon passed by
and trampled down the thistle.
[19] You say to yourself that you have struck Edom,
and your heart lifts you up to boast.
Now stay at home.
Why should you go looking for trouble,
so that you fall, you and Judah with you?”
[20] But Amaziah would not listen.
For it came from God,
so that he might give them into the hand of their enemies,
because they had sought the gods of Edom.
[21] So Joash king of Israel went up.
He and Amaziah king of Judah looked one another in the face at Beth Shemesh,
which belongs to Judah.
[22] Judah was defeated by Israel,
and every man fled to his tent.
[23] Joash king of Israel captured Amaziah king of Judah,
the son of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, at Beth Shemesh.
He brought him to Jerusalem
and broke down the wall of Jerusalem from the gate of Ephraim to the corner gate,
about 600 feet.
[24] He took all the gold and silver,
and all the objects that were found in God’s house with Obed-Edom,
and the treasures of the king’s house, and the hostages,
and he returned to Samaria.

> **What does this mean?**
> Maybe Amaziah wanted to take revenge on Israel for the raids in verse 13.
> “Let’s look one another in the face” means: let’s fight.
> Joash answers with a fable: a small thistle wants to be equal with the great cedar, and it is trampled. He means: you think too much of yourself.
> Verse 20 explains from the point of view of Chronicles why Amaziah did not listen: his worship of idols led him into ruin.
> The Bible says “400 cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> “Obed-Edom”: a family of gatekeepers who guarded the treasures in the temple (1 Chronicles 26:15).
> Verse 23: the English source text says “the son of Jehoahaz”. 2 Kings 14:13 says “the son of Ahaziah”. Jehoahaz and Ahaziah are two names for the same king (see Chapter 21:17 and 22:1).

## The end of Amaziah (Verses 25–28)

[25] Amaziah the son of Joash, king of Judah,
lived for 15 years after the death of Joash the son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.
[26] The rest of the acts of Amaziah, from first to last,
look, are they not written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel?
[27] From the time that Amaziah turned away from following the LORD,
people plotted against him in Jerusalem.
He fled to Lachish,
but they sent men after him to Lachish and killed him there.
[28] They brought him on horses
and buried him with his fathers in the City of Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like his father Joash, Amaziah is murdered too.
> Chronicles connects the plot with his turning away from God: from then on everything went downhill.
> The “City of Judah” is Jerusalem, the City of David. 2 Kings 14:20 says “David’s city”.
