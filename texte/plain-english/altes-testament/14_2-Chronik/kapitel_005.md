# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 5
### The ark comes into the temple

---

## The temple is finished (Verses 1–6)

[1] So all the work that Solomon did for the LORD’s house was finished.
Then Solomon brought in the things that his father David had given to God:
the silver, the gold, and all the objects.
He put them in the treasure rooms of God’s house.
[2] Then Solomon called the elders of Israel to Jerusalem.
He also called all the heads of the tribes
and the leaders of the families of the people of Israel.
They were to bring the ark of the LORD’s covenant up from the City of David.
The City of David is Zion.
[3] So all the men of Israel came together to the king for the feast.
It was in the seventh month.
[4] All the elders of Israel came.
And the Levites picked up the ark.
[5] They brought up the ark,
the Tent of Meeting, and all the holy objects that were in the Tent.
The Levitical priests brought these up.
[6] King Solomon and the whole community of Israel that had gathered around him
stood in front of the ark.
They sacrificed sheep and cattle.
There were so many that no one could count them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The story is told almost the same way in 1 Kings 8.
> The feast in the seventh month is the Feast of Tabernacles (Booths) in the fall.
> This time the Levites carry the ark the right way, on their shoulders (compare 1 Chronicles 15). Chronicles stresses this: here it is “the Levites”, in 1 Kings 8:3 “the priests”.
> “The Levitical priests”: this probably means the priests from the tribe of Levi. Some manuscripts have “the priests and the Levites”.

---

## The ark in the most holy place (Verses 7–10)

[7] The priests brought the ark of the LORD’s covenant to its place.
They brought it into the inner room of the house, the most holy place.
They put it under the wings of the cherubim.
[8] The cherubim spread out their wings over the place of the ark.
So the cherubim covered the ark and its poles from above.
[9] The poles were very long.
The ends of the poles could be seen from the ark, in front of the inner room.
But they could not be seen from outside.
They are still there to this day.
[10] There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets.
Moses had put them there at Horeb.
That was when the LORD made a covenant with the people of Israel,
after they came out of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments lay in the ark. At the heart of the temple is God’s word.
> “To this day”: this sentence comes from the source that Chronicles used (1 Kings 8:8). At the time of Chronicles the ark had not existed for a long time. It was lost when Jerusalem was destroyed in 587 BC.

---

## God’s glory fills the house (Verses 11–14)

[11] Then the priests came out of the holy place.
All the priests who were there had made themselves holy.
They did not keep to their divisions.
[12] All the Levites who were singers were there too:
Asaph, Heman, Jeduthun, and their sons and relatives.
They were dressed in fine linen.
They had cymbals, stringed instruments, and harps.
They stood on the east side of the altar.
With them were 120 priests who blew trumpets.
[13] The trumpet players and the singers played and sang as one.
They made one sound, to praise and thank the LORD.
They raised their voices together with the trumpets, the cymbals, and the other instruments.
They praised the LORD and sang,
“For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!”
Loving kindness means: faithful love.
At that moment a cloud filled the house, the LORD’s house.
[14] Because of the cloud, the priests could not stand and do their work.
The LORD’s glory filled God’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point: God himself comes into his house. The cloud is the sign of his presence.
> Chronicles adds something that is missing in 1 Kings 8: the great music. All the priests, 120 trumpeters, all the singers sing “as one”, with “one sound”. And just at the moment when they sing “For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever”, God’s glory comes. Praising together opens the space for God’s presence.
> All the priests were there, no matter which division they belonged to. On this special day they all served together.
