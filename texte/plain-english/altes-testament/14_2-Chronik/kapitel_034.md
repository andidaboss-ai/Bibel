# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 34
### Josiah and the book of the law that was found

---

## Josiah seeks God even as a boy (Verses 1–7)

[1] Josiah was eight years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for 31 years in Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
He walked in the ways of David his father
and didn’t turn away to the right or to the left.
[3] For in the eighth year of his reign, while he was still young,
he began to seek the God of David his father.
And in the twelfth year he began to clean Judah and Jerusalem
of the high places, the Asherah poles, the carved images, and the cast metal images.
[4] They broke down the altars of the Baals in front of him.
He cut down the incense altars that were high above them.
He broke the Asherah poles, the carved images, and the cast metal images in pieces,
made dust of them,
and scattered it on the graves of those who had sacrificed to them.
[5] He burned the bones of the priests on their altars,
and he made Judah and Jerusalem clean.
[6] He did this in the cities of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon, as far as Naphtali,
all around in their ruins.
[7] He broke down the altars,
beat the Asherah poles and the carved images into powder,
and cut down all the incense altars throughout all the land of Israel.
Then he returned to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 22–23. Josiah reigned from about 640 to 609 before Christ.
> At 16 years old (in the eighth year of his reign) Josiah begins to seek God. At 20 years old (in the twelfth year) he begins to clean the land of idols. His grandfather Manasseh and his father Amon had filled the land with idols.
> He “didn’t turn away to the right or to the left”: he went straight ahead on God’s way (compare Deuteronomy 5:32).
> Josiah also cleans the area of the former northern kingdom. The Assyrian empire was becoming weaker at that time. So Josiah could act there too.
> Burning the bones of the priests of the idols on their altars made these altars unclean forever. So no one could sacrifice there anymore. A prophet had already announced this 300 years before (1 Kings 13:2).

## The repair of the temple (Verses 8–13)

[8] In the eighteenth year of his reign,
when he had made the land and the house clean,
he sent Shaphan the son of Azaliah, Maaseiah the governor of the city,
and Joah the son of Joahaz the recorder,
to repair the house of the LORD his God.
[9] They came to Hilkiah the high priest
and handed over the money that had been brought into God’s house.
The Levites, the keepers of the doorway, had gathered it
from Manasseh and Ephraim, from all the rest of Israel,
from all Judah and Benjamin,
and from the people of Jerusalem.
[10] They gave it into the hands of the workers who were in charge of the LORD’s house.
And the workers who worked in the LORD’s house
used it to mend and repair the house.
[11] They gave it to the carpenters and to the builders
to buy cut stone, and timber for joining beams,
and to make beams for the houses that the kings of Judah had let fall into ruin.
[12] The men did the work faithfully.
Their overseers were Jahath and Obadiah the Levites, of the sons of Merari,
and Zechariah and Meshullam, of the sons of the Kohathites, to lead the work.
And there were other Levites, who were all skilled with musical instruments.
[13] They were also over the men who carried loads,
and they directed all who did the work in every kind of service.
Of the Levites, there were scribes, officials, and gatekeepers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah is now 26 years old. After he has cleaned the land, he has the temple repaired.
> The money also comes from the north, “from all the rest of Israel”. Josiah sees the whole people as one.
> The detail in verse 12 is beautiful: the overseers were musicians. Maybe they accompanied the work with music.

## The book of the law is found (Verses 14–21)

[14] They brought out the money that had been brought into the LORD’s house.
Then Hilkiah the priest found the book of the LORD’s law given through Moses.
[15] Hilkiah said to Shaphan the scribe,
“I have found the book of the law in the LORD’s house.”
So Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan.
[16] Shaphan carried the book to the king.
He also brought word back to the king:
“Your servants are doing all that was given to them to do.
[17] They have emptied out the money that was found in the LORD’s house,
and they have given it into the hand of the overseers and into the hand of the workers.”
[18] Shaphan the scribe told the king,
“Hilkiah the priest has given me a book.”
And Shaphan read from it to the king.
[19] When the king had heard the words of the law,
he tore his clothes.
[20] The king commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam the son of Shaphan, Abdon the son of Micah,
Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah the king’s servant:
[21] “Go and ask the LORD for me,
and for those who are left in Israel and in Judah,
about the words of the book that has been found.
For the LORD’s anger that is poured out on us is great,
because our fathers have not kept the LORD’s word,
to do all that is written in this book.”

> **What does this mean?**
> During the work in the temple a book is found: the law of Moses. Under Manasseh it had probably been forgotten or hidden.
> Many experts think that it was the book of Deuteronomy, or a large part of it. For Josiah’s reforms fit very exactly with what is written there.
> Josiah tears his clothes. That is a sign of deep grief and shock. He realizes: we have not kept God’s word. He takes God’s word seriously.
> He also asks for “those who are left in Israel”. He thinks of the whole people.

## The prophetess Huldah (Verses 22–28)

[22] So Hilkiah and those whom the king had commanded went to Huldah the prophetess.
She was the wife of Shallum the son of Tokhath, the son of Hasrah,
the keeper of the wardrobe.
She lived in Jerusalem in the second quarter of the city.
They spoke to her about this.
[23] She said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘Tell the man who sent you to me,
[24] “The LORD says,
‘Look, I will bring disaster on this place and on the people who live in it,
all the curses that are written in the book
which they have read before the king of Judah.
[25] For they have abandoned me
and have burned incense to other gods,
to make me angry with all the works of their hands.
That is why my anger is poured out on this place,
and it will not be put out.’”’
[26] But to the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the LORD, say this:
‘The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
“About the words that you have heard:
[27] Your heart was tender,
and you humbled yourself before God
when you heard his words against this place and against the people who live in it.
You humbled yourself before me,
tore your clothes, and wept before me.
So I also have heard you,” says the LORD.
[28] “Look, I will gather you to your fathers,
and you will be gathered to your grave in peace.
Your eyes won’t see all the disaster that I will bring on this place
and on the people who live in it.”’”
They brought this message back to the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king sends his most important men to a woman: the prophetess Huldah. At that time she was a respected prophetess. This shows: God also speaks through women. The prophets Jeremiah and Zephaniah lived at the same time.
> Huldah says two things: the judgment on Jerusalem will come, because the people have left God for so long. But Josiah will not live to see it, because his heart has become tender and he has humbled himself.
> “Gathered to your grave in peace”: Josiah later dies in a battle (Chapter 35). What is probably meant: he dies before Jerusalem is destroyed. He does not have to see the downfall.
> “The second quarter” was probably a new part of the city in the northwest of Jerusalem.

## The covenant (Verses 29–33)

[29] Then the king sent messengers
and gathered together all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.
[30] The king went up to the LORD’s house
with all the men of Judah and the people of Jerusalem,
the priests, the Levites, and all the people, both great and small.
And he read out to them all the words of the book of the covenant
that had been found in the LORD’s house.
[31] The king stood in his place
and made a covenant before the LORD:
to follow the LORD,
and to keep his commandments, his testimonies, and his laws
with all his heart and with all his soul,
to do the words of the covenant that were written in this book.
[32] He made all who were found in Jerusalem and Benjamin stand.
The people of Jerusalem acted according to the covenant of God, the God of their fathers.
[33] Josiah took away all the disgusting idols
out of all the lands that belonged to the children of Israel.
And he made all who were found in Israel serve,
serve the LORD their God.
All his days they didn’t turn away from following the LORD, the God of their fathers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah does not keep the book of the law for himself. He reads it out to the whole people, “both great and small”. Everyone is to hear God’s word.
> Then he solemnly promises to live by God’s word, “with all his heart and with all his soul” (verse 31). This recalls the most important commandment (Deuteronomy 6:5).
> Although Huldah had said that the judgment would come, Josiah does what is right. He obeys God, not to get a reward, but because it is right.
> “He made all … stand”: that means they stood up solemnly and agreed to enter the covenant.
> “All his days”: as long as Josiah lived, the people stayed faithful to God. After his death they fell away again.
