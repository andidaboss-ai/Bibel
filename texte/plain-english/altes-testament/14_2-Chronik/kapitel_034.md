# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 34
### Josiah and the book of the law that was found

---

## Josiah seeks God even as a boy (Verses 1–7)

[1] Josiah was eight years old when he became king.
He ruled for 31 years in Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
He lived the way his ancestor David had lived.
He didn’t turn away to the right or to the left.
[3] In the eighth year of his rule, he was still young.
Then he began to seek the God of his ancestor David.
In the twelfth year, he began to make Judah and Jerusalem clean.
He removed the high places and the Asherah poles,
the carved idols and the metal idols.
[4] While he watched, the people tore down the altars of the Baals.
He cut down the incense altars that stood above them.
He smashed the Asherah poles, the carved idols, and the metal idols.
He ground them into dust.
He scattered the dust on the graves of the people who had offered sacrifices to them.
[5] He burned the bones of the priests on their own altars.
So he made Judah and Jerusalem clean.
[6] He did the same in the towns of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon,
all the way to Naphtali,
and in the ruins all around them.
[7] He tore down the altars.
He crushed the Asherah poles and the carved idols to powder.
He cut down all the incense altars in the whole land of Israel.
Then he went back to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is also told in 2 Kings 22–23. Josiah ruled from about 640 to 609 before Christ.
> At 16 years old (in the eighth year of his rule), Josiah begins to seek God. At 20 years old (in the twelfth year), he begins to clean the land of idols. His grandfather Manasseh and his father Amon had filled the land with idols.
> He “didn’t turn away to the right or to the left”: he went straight ahead on God’s way (compare Deuteronomy 5:32).
> Josiah also cleans the area of the former northern kingdom. The Assyrian empire was becoming weaker at that time. So Josiah could act there too.
> Burning the bones of the priests of the idols on their altars made these altars unclean forever. So no one could sacrifice there anymore. A prophet had already announced this 300 years before (1 Kings 13:2).

## The repair of the temple (Verses 8–13)

[8] In the eighteenth year of his rule,
Josiah had made the land and the temple clean.
Then he sent three men to repair the house of the LORD his God:
Shaphan, the son of Azaliah,
Maaseiah, the governor of the city,
and Joah, the son of Joahaz, the official who kept the records.
[9] They came to Hilkiah, the high priest.
They gave him the money that had been brought into God’s house.
The Levites who guarded the doors had collected it.
It came from the people of Manasseh and Ephraim,
from all the rest of Israel,
from all Judah and Benjamin,
and from the people of Jerusalem.
[10] They gave the money to the men who were in charge of the work on the LORD’s house.
These men paid the workers who repaired and restored the LORD’s house.
[11] They gave money to the carpenters and the builders.
With it, they bought cut stone and wood for supports and beams.
They used it for the buildings that the kings of Judah had let fall into ruin.
[12] The men did the work faithfully.
Their supervisors were Jahath and Obadiah, Levites from the family of Merari.
Zechariah and Meshullam, from the family of Kohath, led the work too.
Other Levites helped as well.
All of them were skilled with musical instruments.
[13] They were also in charge of the men who carried the loads.
They directed all the workers in every kind of job.
Some of the Levites were secretaries, officials, and gatekeepers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah is now 26 years old. After he has cleaned the land, he has the temple repaired.
> The money also comes from the north, “from all the rest of Israel.” Josiah sees the whole people as one.
> The detail in verse 12 is beautiful: the supervisors were musicians. Maybe they accompanied the work with music.

## The book of the law is found (Verses 14–21)

[14] They took out the money that had been brought into the LORD’s house.
While they did this, Hilkiah the priest found the book of the LORD’s law.
The LORD had given it through Moses.
[15] Hilkiah said to Shaphan the secretary,
“I have found the book of the law in the LORD’s house.”
And Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan.
[16] Shaphan took the book to the king.
He also gave the king a report:
“Your servants are doing everything you gave them to do.
[17] They have emptied out the money that was in the LORD’s house.
They have given it to the supervisors and to the workers.”
[18] Then Shaphan the secretary told the king,
“Hilkiah the priest has given me a book.”
And Shaphan read from it to the king.
[19] The king heard the words of the law.
Then he tore his clothes.
[20] The king gave an order to Hilkiah,
to Ahikam, the son of Shaphan,
to Abdon, the son of Micah,
to Shaphan the secretary,
and to Asaiah, the king’s servant.
He said,
[21] “Go and ask the LORD for me,
and for those who are left in Israel and in Judah.
Ask him about the words of this book that was found.
The LORD is very angry with us.
His anger has been poured out on us.
Our ancestors have not obeyed the LORD’s word.
They have not done everything that is written in this book.”

> **What does this mean?**
> During the work in the temple, a book is found: the law of Moses. Under Manasseh, it had probably been forgotten or hidden.
> Many experts think that it was the book of Deuteronomy, or a large part of it. For Josiah’s reforms fit very exactly with what is written there.
> Josiah tears his clothes. That is a sign of deep grief and shock. He realizes: we have not kept God’s word. He takes God’s word seriously.
> He also asks for “those who are left in Israel.” He thinks of the whole people.

## The prophetess Huldah (Verses 22–28)

[22] So Hilkiah and the men the king had sent went to Huldah the prophetess.
She was the wife of Shallum.
Shallum was the son of Tokhath, the son of Hasrah.
He was in charge of the royal wardrobe.
Huldah lived in Jerusalem, in the second quarter of the city.
They told her what had happened.
[23] She said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says this:
‘Tell the man who sent you to me,
[24] “The LORD says this:
‘I will bring disaster on this place and on the people who live here.
All the curses written in the book will come true,
the book that was read to the king of Judah.
[25] They have left me.
They have burned incense to other gods.
With all the idols they have made, they have made me angry.
So my anger is poured out on this place.
And it will not be put out.’”’
[26] The king of Judah sent you to ask the LORD.
Tell him this:
‘The LORD, the God of Israel, says this
about the words you have heard:
[27] “Your heart was tender.
You humbled yourself before God
when you heard what he said against this place and the people who live here.
You humbled yourself before me.
You tore your clothes and cried before me.
So I have heard you too,” says the LORD.
[28] “I will gather you to your ancestors.
You will be gathered to your grave in peace.
You will not see all the disaster that I will bring on this place
and on the people who live here.”’”
The men took this answer back to the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king sends his most important men to a woman: the prophetess Huldah. At that time, she was a respected prophetess. This shows: God also speaks through women. The prophets Jeremiah and Zephaniah lived at the same time.
> Huldah says two things: the judgment on Jerusalem will come, because the people have left God for so long. But Josiah will not live to see it, because his heart has become tender and he has humbled himself.
> “Gathered to your grave in peace”: Josiah later dies in a battle (Chapter 35). What is probably meant: he dies before Jerusalem is destroyed. He does not have to see the downfall.
> “The second quarter” was probably a new part of the city in the northwest of Jerusalem.

## The covenant (Verses 29–33)

[29] Then the king sent messengers.
He called together all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.
[30] The king went up to the LORD’s house.
All the men of Judah and the people of Jerusalem went with him.
The priests and the Levites went too,
and all the people, both great and small.
The king read aloud to them everything in the book of the covenant.
This book had been found in the LORD’s house.
[31] The king stood in his place.
He made a covenant before the LORD.
He promised to follow the LORD.
He promised to keep his commandments, his teachings, and his laws
with all his heart and with all his soul.
He promised to do what the covenant in this book said.
[32] He made everyone in Jerusalem and Benjamin stand and agree to it.
And the people of Jerusalem kept the covenant of God, the God of their ancestors.
[33] Josiah removed all the disgusting idols
from all the lands that belonged to the people of Israel.
He made everyone in Israel serve the LORD their God.
As long as Josiah lived, they did not turn away from following the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah does not keep the book of the law for himself. He reads it out to the whole people, “both great and small.” Everyone is to hear God’s word.
> Then he solemnly promises to live by God’s word, “with all his heart and with all his soul” (verse 31). This recalls the most important commandment (Deuteronomy 6:5).
> Although Huldah had said that the judgment would come, Josiah does what is right. He obeys God, not to get a reward, but because it is right.
> “He made everyone … stand”: that means they stood up solemnly and agreed to enter the covenant.
> “As long as Josiah lived”: the people stayed faithful to God for that long. After his death, they fell away again.
