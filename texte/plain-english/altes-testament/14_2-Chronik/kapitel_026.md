# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 26
### Uzziah – strong and then proud

---

## Uzziah becomes king (Verses 1–5)

[1] All the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was 16 years old,
and made him king in the place of his father Amaziah.
[2] He built Eloth and gave it back to Judah,
after the king had died and joined his fathers.
[3] Uzziah was 16 years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for 52 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jechiliah, from Jerusalem.
[4] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his father Amaziah had done.
[5] He set himself to seek God in the days of Zechariah,
who had understanding in the vision of God.
And as long as he sought the LORD,
God made things go well for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> In 2 Kings 14–15 Uzziah is usually called “Azariah”. Both names mean almost the same. Uzziah means: The LORD is my strength. Azariah means: The LORD helps.
> Uzziah reigned from about 792 to 740 before Christ, that is, for a very long time. Part of this was probably a shared reign with his father and later with his son.
> Eloth (also Elath) is the port on the Red Sea, near today’s Eilat. “The king” in verse 2 is probably his father Amaziah. What is meant: Uzziah built up Eloth after his father had died.
> Zechariah was probably a teacher or prophet who advised Uzziah. He is not the same as the prophet Zechariah, after whom a book of the Bible is named. A “vision” is a message from God. Some old manuscripts have “in the fear of God”.
> “As long as he sought the LORD”: these few words already hint that it will not stay this way.

---

## Uzziah’s successes (Verses 6–15)

[6] He went out and fought against the Philistines.
He broke down the wall of Gath, the wall of Jabneh, and the wall of Ashdod.
And he built cities in the country of Ashdod and among the Philistines.
[7] God helped him against the Philistines,
against the Arabians who lived in Gur Baal,
and against the Meunim.
[8] The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah.
His name spread as far as the entrance of Egypt,
for he became very strong.
[9] Uzziah also built towers in Jerusalem
at the corner gate, at the valley gate, and at the turning of the wall,
and he fortified them.
[10] He built towers in the wilderness
and dug out many cisterns.
For he had much livestock, both in the lowlands and in the plains.
He had farmers and vineyard keepers in the mountains and in the fruitful fields,
for he loved farming.
[11] Uzziah also had an army of fighting men,
who went out to war in troops.
They were counted by Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer,
under the hand of Hananiah, one of the king’s captains.
[12] The whole number of the heads of families,
the mighty men of courage, was 2,600.
[13] Under them was an army of 307,500,
who fought with mighty power
to help the king against the enemy.
[14] Uzziah prepared for them, for the whole army,
shields, spears, helmets, coats of armor, bows, and stones for slings.
[15] In Jerusalem he made machines, invented by skilled men,
to be on the towers and on the battlements,
to shoot arrows and great stones.
His name spread far,
because he was marvelously helped until he was strong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Under Uzziah, Judah had a time of flourishing. He was successful in war, in building and in farming.
> The small remark in verse 10 is beautiful: “He loved farming.” A king who has joy in fields and vineyards.
> The machines in verse 15 were probably protective structures on the walls, behind which archers and stone throwers could fight safely.
> The most important sentence is at the end of verse 15: “He was marvelously helped until he was strong.” Uzziah’s strength was a gift of God. But he forgot that.

## Uzziah’s pride (Verses 16–21)

[16] But when he was strong,
his heart was lifted up in pride,
so that he acted wickedly.
He was unfaithful to the LORD his God,
for he went into the LORD’s temple
to burn incense on the altar of incense.
[17] Azariah the priest went in after him,
and with him 80 priests of the LORD, who were brave men.
[18] They stood up against King Uzziah and said to him,
“It isn’t for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD,
but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are set apart to burn incense.
Go out of the holy place, for you have been unfaithful.
It will not bring you honor from the LORD God.”
[19] Then Uzziah was angry.
He had a censer in his hand to burn incense.
A censer is a pan for burning incense.
While he was angry with the priests,
leprosy broke out on his forehead in front of the priests in the LORD’s house,
beside the altar of incense.
[20] Azariah the chief priest and all the priests looked at him.
Look, he had leprosy on his forehead!
They quickly pushed him out of there.
And he himself also hurried to go out,
because the LORD had struck him.
[21] King Uzziah had leprosy until the day of his death.
He lived in a separate house, because he had leprosy,
for he was cut off from the LORD’s house.
Jotham his son was over the king’s house
and judged the people of the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Uzziah wants to be everything: king and priest. But the law was clear: only the priests from Aaron’s family were allowed to offer incense in the holy place (Numbers 18:7).
> The 80 priests are very brave. They stand in the way of a mighty king.
> In the Bible, “leprosy” is a name for different serious skin diseases, not only for what is called leprosy today. Whoever had leprosy had to live apart (Leviticus 13:46).
> The king who wanted to force his way into the temple may now never go in again. That is the bitter irony of this story.
> The message: success can make people proud. “Pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). Even great and successful people must respect limits.

## The death of Uzziah (Verses 22–23)

[22] The rest of the acts of Uzziah, from first to last,
were written by Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz.
[23] So Uzziah died and joined his fathers.
They buried him with his fathers in the burial field that belonged to the kings,
for they said, “He has leprosy.”
And Jotham his son reigned in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> The prophet Isaiah lived at this time. In the year that Uzziah died, Isaiah had his great vision in the temple. There he saw the true king: God himself on his throne (Isaiah 6:1–5).
> Uzziah was buried beside the royal tombs, but not in them, because of his disease.
> In the 20th century, an old stone plaque was found in Jerusalem with the words: “Here were brought the bones of Uzziah, king of Judah. Do not open!” It comes from a much later time, when his bones were moved to a new grave. But it shows that people remembered him for a long time.
