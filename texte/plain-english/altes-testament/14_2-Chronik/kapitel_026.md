# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 26
### Uzziah – strong and then proud

---

## Uzziah becomes king (Verses 1–5)

[1] All the people of Judah chose Uzziah.
He was 16 years old.
They made him king in place of his father Amaziah.
[2] Uzziah rebuilt Eloth and gave it back to Judah.
After that, the king died and joined his ancestors.
[3] Uzziah was 16 years old when he became king.
He ruled for 52 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jechiliah.
She came from Jerusalem.
[4] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his father Amaziah had done.
[5] He made up his mind to seek God
while Zechariah was alive.
Zechariah understood the vision of God.
As long as he sought the LORD,
God made things go well for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> In 2 Kings 14–15, Uzziah is usually called “Azariah.” Both names mean almost the same. Uzziah means: The LORD is my strength. Azariah means: The LORD helps.
> Uzziah ruled from about 792 to 740 BC, that is, for a very long time. Part of this was probably a shared rule with his father and later with his son.
> Eloth (also Elath) is the port on the Red Sea, near today’s Eilat. “The king” in verse 2 is probably his father Amaziah. The Hebrew can be understood like this: Uzziah built up Eloth after his father had died.
> Zechariah was probably a teacher or prophet who advised Uzziah. He is not the same as the prophet Zechariah, after whom a book of the Bible is named. A “vision” is a message from God. Some old manuscripts have “in the fear of God.”
> “As long as he sought the LORD”: these few words already hint that it will not stay this way.

---

## Uzziah’s successes (Verses 6–15)

[6] Uzziah went to war against the Philistines.
He tore down the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod.
He built cities near Ashdod and in other places of the Philistines.
[7] God helped him against the Philistines.
He helped him against the Arabians who lived in Gur Baal,
and against the Meunim.
[8] The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah.
Tribute is money that a defeated people must pay.
Uzziah became famous all the way to the border of Egypt,
because he became very strong.
[9] Uzziah also built towers in Jerusalem.
He built them at the Corner Gate, at the Valley Gate, and at the bend in the wall.
And he made them strong.
[10] He built towers in the wilderness
and dug many cisterns.
A cistern is a pit for collecting rainwater.
He did this because he had a lot of livestock in the lowlands and in the plains.
He had farmers and vineyard workers in the mountains and in the fertile fields,
because he loved farming.
[11] Uzziah also had an army of fighting men.
They went to war in units.
Jeiel the secretary and Maaseiah the officer counted them.
Hananiah, one of the king’s commanders, was in charge of them.
[12] There were 2,600 heads of families in all.
They were brave warriors.
[13] They led an army of 307,500 men.
These men fought with great strength
to help the king against his enemies.
[14] Uzziah gave the whole army
shields, spears, helmets, armor, bows, and stones for slings.
[15] In Jerusalem, he had machines built.
Skilled men had invented them.
They stood on the towers and on the corners of the walls.
They could shoot arrows and large stones.
Uzziah became famous far and wide,
because he was marvelously helped until he was strong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Under Uzziah, Judah had a time of flourishing. He was successful in war, in building and in farming.
> The small remark in verse 10 is beautiful: “He loved farming.” A king who has joy in fields and vineyards.
> The machines in verse 15 were probably protective structures on the walls, behind which archers and stone throwers could fight safely.
> The most important sentence is at the end of verse 15: “He was marvelously helped until he was strong.” Uzziah’s strength was a gift of God. But he forgot that.

## Uzziah’s pride (Verses 16–21)

[16] But when Uzziah was strong,
he became proud.
So he did wrong.
He was unfaithful to the LORD his God.
He went into the LORD’s temple
to burn incense on the incense altar.
[17] Azariah the priest went in after him.
With him went 80 brave priests of the LORD.
[18] They stood up to King Uzziah and said to him,
“Uzziah, it is not your job to burn incense to the LORD.
That is the job of the priests, the sons of Aaron.
They have been set apart to burn incense.
Leave the holy place!
You have been unfaithful.
The LORD God will not honor you for this.”
[19] Uzziah became angry.
He was holding a censer in his hand to burn incense.
A censer is a pan for burning incense.
While he was angry with the priests,
leprosy broke out on his forehead.
It happened in front of the priests in the LORD’s house,
beside the incense altar.
[20] Azariah the chief priest and all the other priests looked at him.
He had leprosy on his forehead!
They quickly pushed him out of there.
And he himself hurried to get out,
because the LORD had struck him.
[21] King Uzziah had leprosy until the day he died.
He lived in a separate house because of his leprosy.
He was not allowed into the LORD’s house anymore.
His son Jotham was in charge of the king’s palace.
He ruled over the people of the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Uzziah wants to be everything: king and priest. But the law was clear: only the priests from Aaron’s family were allowed to offer incense in the holy place (Numbers 18:7).
> The 80 priests are very brave. They stand in the way of a mighty king.
> In the Bible, “leprosy” is a name for different serious skin diseases, not only for what is called leprosy today. Whoever had leprosy had to live apart (Leviticus 13:46).
> The king who wanted to force his way into the temple may now never go in again. That is the bitter irony of this story.
> The message: success can make people proud. “Pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). Even great and successful people must respect limits.

---

## The death of Uzziah (Verses 22–23)

[22] There is more to tell about Uzziah, from beginning to end.
Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, wrote it down.
[23] Then Uzziah died and joined his ancestors.
They buried him with his ancestors,
in the burial field that belonged to the kings.
They said, “He has leprosy.”
His son Jotham became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> The prophet Isaiah lived at this time. In the year that Uzziah died, Isaiah had his great vision in the temple. There he saw the true king: God himself on his throne (Isaiah 6:1–5).
> Uzziah was buried beside the royal tombs, but not in them, because of his disease.
> In the 20th century, an old stone plaque was found in Jerusalem with the words: “Here were brought the bones of Uzziah, king of Judah. Do not open!” It comes from a much later time, when his bones were moved to a new grave. But it shows that people remembered him for a long time.
