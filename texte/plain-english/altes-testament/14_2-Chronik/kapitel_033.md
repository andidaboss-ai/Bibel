# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 33
### Manasseh turns back

---

## Manasseh’s evil deeds (Verses 1–10)

[1] Manasseh was 12 years old when he became king.
He ruled for 55 years in Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He copied the disgusting customs of the nations
that the LORD had driven out before the people of Israel.
[3] He rebuilt the high places that his father Hezekiah had torn down.
He set up altars for the Baals.
He made Asheroth.
Asheroth are wooden poles for the goddess Asherah.
He bowed down to all the army of the sky and served them.
[4] He built altars in the LORD’s house.
But the LORD had said about this house,
“My name will be in Jerusalem forever.”
[5] In both courtyards of the LORD’s house,
he built altars for all the army of the sky.
[6] He also made his children pass through the fire
in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom.
He practiced magic, fortune-telling, and witchcraft.
He went to people who called up the spirits of the dead,
and to people who practiced sorcery.
He did much evil in the LORD’s eyes
and made him angry.
[7] Manasseh had made a carved idol.
He put it in God’s house.
But God had said to David and to his son Solomon about this house,
“I have chosen Jerusalem out of all the tribes of Israel.
In this house and in Jerusalem
I will put my name forever.
[8] I will never again make Israel leave the land
that I gave to your ancestors.
But they must carefully do everything I have commanded them.
They must keep the whole law, all the rules and laws I gave through Moses.”
[9] But Manasseh led Judah and the people of Jerusalem astray.
They did even more evil than the nations
that the LORD had destroyed before the people of Israel.
[10] The LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people,
but they did not listen.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are almost the same in 2 Kings 21. Manasseh ruled from about 697 to 642 BC, longer than any other king of Judah.
> Manasseh undoes everything that his father Hezekiah had built up. He does everything that God had forbidden: worship of idols in the temple, worship of the stars (“the army of the sky” is the sun, the moon and the stars), child sacrifices and magic.
> “Made his children pass through the fire” very probably means that he burned them as offerings. That is a horrible crime. God does not want human sacrifices (Deuteronomy 18:10).
> He even puts an idol in the temple, in the place where God’s name is to live.
> But verse 10 shows: God still goes on speaking to him. He does not give up.

---

## Manasseh in captivity and his turning back (Verses 11–13)

[11] So the LORD sent the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria against them.
They captured Manasseh in chains.
They put him in shackles
and took him to Babylon.
[12] Manasseh was in deep trouble.
Then he begged the LORD his God for help.
He humbled himself deeply before the God of his ancestors.
[13] He prayed to God.
And God was moved by his prayer.
He listened to his plea.
He brought him back to Jerusalem, to his kingdom.
Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is only in Chronicles, not in 2 Kings.
> “In chains”: the Hebrew has a word that can also mean “hooks” or “thorns.” The Assyrians sometimes led prisoners away with hooks through the nose or lip. That was cruel and humiliating.
> It may be surprising that Manasseh was brought to Babylon, because the capital of Assyria was Nineveh. But at that time, Babylon belonged to the Assyrian empire. In Assyrian inscriptions, Manasseh is named as a vassal king who had to pay tribute.
> This is one of the most hopeful stories in the Bible: even the worst king of Judah turns back. And God forgives him. No person is too far away to come back to God.
> “Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God”: in his distress, he really came to know God.
> There is an old prayer that is called the “Prayer of Manasseh.” It is among the Apocrypha in some Bibles. But it was probably written much later.

## Manasseh builds up (Verses 14–17)

[14] After this, Manasseh built an outer wall for David’s city.
It ran west of the Gihon Spring in the valley,
as far as the entrance at the Fish Gate.
It went all around Ophel.
He made the wall very high.
He put brave commanders in all the fortified cities of Judah.
[15] He removed the foreign gods and the idol from the LORD’s house.
He also removed all the altars he had built on the hill of the LORD’s house and in Jerusalem.
He threw them all out of the city.
[16] He rebuilt the LORD’s altar.
On it, he offered peace offerings and thank offerings.
He commanded the people of Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.
[17] Still, the people kept offering sacrifices at the high places.
But now they offered them only to the LORD their God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Manasseh’s turning back is real. He cleans up what he himself has done. He offers thank offerings: he is thankful to God for saving him.
> But verse 17 shows: it is hard to put everything right again. The people go on offering on the high places, even if now to the right God. The damage that Manasseh had done was great.
> Turning back is always possible. But the results of earlier deeds do not simply disappear.

## The death of Manasseh (Verses 18–20)

[18] There is more to tell about Manasseh.
There is his prayer to his God.
There are the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.
All this is written in the records of the kings of Israel.
[19] His prayer is written down there too,
and how God listened to his plea.
All his sin and his unfaithfulness are written down.
And so are the places where he built high places
and set up Asherah poles and carved idols before he humbled himself.
All this is written in the history of Hozai.
[20] Then Manasseh died and joined his ancestors.
They buried him at his own house.
His son Amon became king after him.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hozai”: some translate “the seers” instead of a name. The book no longer exists.
> 2 Kings 21:18 says that he was buried in the garden of his house, in the garden of Uzza.

## Amon (Verses 21–25)

[21] Amon was 22 years old when he became king.
He ruled for two years in Jerusalem.
[22] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his father Manasseh had done.
Amon offered sacrifices to all the carved idols that his father Manasseh had made.
And he served them.
[23] But he did not humble himself before the LORD,
as his father Manasseh had done.
Instead, Amon became more and more guilty.
[24] His servants made plans against him.
They killed him in his own palace.
[25] But the people of the land killed all those who had made plans against King Amon.
Then the people of the land made his son Josiah king after him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amon copied the evil deeds of his father, but not his turning back. That is sad: children often learn more from the mistakes of their parents than from their turning back.
> Amon ruled from about 642 to 640 BC.
> His son Josiah will become one of the best kings of Judah.
