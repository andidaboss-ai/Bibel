# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 33
### Manasseh turns back

---

## Manasseh’s evil deeds (Verses 1–10)

[1] Manasseh was 12 years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for 55 years in Jerusalem.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight,
like the disgusting practices of the nations
whom the LORD had driven out before the children of Israel.
[3] For he built again the high places that Hezekiah his father had broken down.
He set up altars for the Baals and made Asheroth.
He worshiped all the army of the sky and served them.
[4] He built altars in the LORD’s house,
about which the LORD had said,
“My name shall be in Jerusalem forever.”
[5] He built altars for all the army of the sky
in the two courts of the LORD’s house.
[6] He also made his children pass through the fire
in the valley of the son of Hinnom.
He practiced sorcery, fortune-telling, and witchcraft,
and he dealt with those who talked with spirits of the dead and with wizards.
He did much evil in the LORD’s sight, to make him angry.
[7] He set the carved image of the idol that he had made in God’s house.
About this house God had said to David and to Solomon his son,
“In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel,
I will put my name forever.
[8] I will never again remove the foot of Israel from the land
that I have given to your fathers,
if only they will take care to do all that I have commanded them,
all the law, the laws, and the rules given through Moses.”
[9] Manasseh led Judah and the people of Jerusalem astray,
so that they did more evil than the nations
whom the LORD had destroyed before the children of Israel.
[10] The LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people,
but they didn’t listen.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are almost the same in 2 Kings 21. Manasseh reigned from about 697 to 642 before Christ, longer than any other king of Judah.
> Manasseh undoes everything that his father Hezekiah had built up. He does everything that God had forbidden: worship of idols in the temple, worship of the stars (“the army of the sky” is the sun, the moon and the stars), child sacrifices and magic.
> “Made his children pass through the fire” very probably means that he burned them as offerings. That is a horrible crime. God does not want human sacrifices (Deuteronomy 18:10).
> He even puts an idol in the temple, in the place where God’s name is to live.
> But verse 10 shows: God still goes on speaking to him. He does not give up.

---

## Manasseh in captivity and his turning back (Verses 11–13)

[11] So the LORD brought on them the captains of the army of the king of Assyria.
They took Manasseh in chains,
bound him with bronze chains,
and carried him to Babylon.
[12] When he was in distress,
he pleaded with the LORD his God
and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers.
[13] He prayed to him.
And God was moved by his prayer.
He heard his plea
and brought him back to Jerusalem, into his kingdom.
Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is only in Chronicles, not in 2 Kings.
> “In chains”: the Hebrew has a word that can also mean “hooks” or “thorns”. The Assyrians sometimes led prisoners away with hooks through the nose or lip. That was cruel and humiliating.
> It may be surprising that Manasseh was brought to Babylon, because the capital of Assyria was Nineveh. But at that time Babylon belonged to the Assyrian empire. In Assyrian inscriptions Manasseh is named as a vassal king who had to pay tribute.
> This is one of the most hopeful stories in the Bible: even the worst king of Judah turns back. And God forgives him. No person is too far away to come back to God.
> “Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God”: in his distress he really came to know God.
> There is an old prayer that is called the “Prayer of Manasseh”. It is among the Apocrypha in some Bibles. But it was probably written much later.

## Manasseh builds up (Verses 14–17)

[14] After this he built an outer wall for David’s city,
on the west side of Gihon, in the valley,
as far as the entrance at the fish gate.
He put it around Ophel
and made it very high.
And he put brave captains in all the fortified cities of Judah.
[15] He took away the foreign gods and the idol out of the LORD’s house,
and all the altars that he had built on the mountain of the LORD’s house and in Jerusalem,
and he threw them out of the city.
[16] He built up the LORD’s altar
and offered peace offerings and thank offerings on it.
And he commanded Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.
[17] But the people still sacrificed on the high places,
though only to the LORD their God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Manasseh’s turning back is real. He cleans up what he himself has done. He offers thank offerings: he is thankful to God for saving him.
> But verse 17 shows: it is hard to put everything right again. The people go on offering on the high places, even if now to the right God. The damage that Manasseh had done was great.
> Turning back is always possible. But the results of earlier deeds do not simply disappear.

## The death of Manasseh (Verses 18–20)

[18] The rest of the acts of Manasseh,
his prayer to his God,
and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel,
look, they are written among the acts of the kings of Israel.
[19] His prayer too, and how God listened to his request,
and all his sin and his unfaithfulness,
and the places where he built high places
and set up the Asherah poles and the carved images before he humbled himself:
look, they are written in the history of Hozai.
[20] So Manasseh died and joined his fathers,
and they buried him in his own house.
And Amon his son reigned in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hozai”: some translate “the seers” instead of a name. The book no longer exists.
> 2 Kings 21:18 says that he was buried in the garden of his house, in the garden of Uzza.

## Amon (Verses 21–25)

[21] Amon was 22 years old when he began to reign,
and he reigned for two years in Jerusalem.
[22] He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight,
as Manasseh his father had done.
Amon sacrificed to all the carved images that Manasseh his father had made,
and he served them.
[23] He didn’t humble himself before the LORD,
as Manasseh his father had humbled himself.
But this same Amon became more and more guilty.
[24] His servants plotted against him
and killed him in his own house.
[25] But the people of the land killed all those who had plotted against King Amon.
And the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amon copied the evil deeds of his father, but not his turning back. That is sad: children often learn more from the mistakes of their parents than from their turning back.
> Amon reigned from about 642 to 640 before Christ.
> His son Josiah will become one of the best kings of Judah.
