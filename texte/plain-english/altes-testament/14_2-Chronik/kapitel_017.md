# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 17
### Jehoshaphat, a good king

---

## Jehoshaphat seeks God (Verses 1–6)

[1] Asa’s son Jehoshaphat became king in his place.
He made his kingdom strong against Israel.
[2] He put troops in all the fortified cities of Judah.
He put soldiers in the land of Judah
and in the cities of Ephraim that his father Asa had captured.
[3] The LORD was with Jehoshaphat.
He lived the way his ancestor David had lived in his first years.
He did not seek the Baals.
[4] He sought the God of his father.
He obeyed God’s commandments.
He did not do what the people of Israel did.
[5] So the LORD made the kingdom firm under Jehoshaphat’s rule.
All the people of Judah brought gifts to Jehoshaphat.
He had great riches and honor.
[6] His heart was full of courage for the LORD’s ways.
He also removed the high places and the Asherah poles from Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat reigned from about 872 to 848 BC. Chronicles tells much more about him than the books of Kings.
> “The way his ancestor David had lived in his first years”: this means David’s good years. The English source text calls David his “father”, because in Hebrew “father” can also mean “ancestor”.
> “Baals” are the different forms of the god Baal, who was worshiped in the northern kingdom under Ahab.
> “His heart was full of courage”: literally it says “his heart was lifted up”. Usually this means “to become proud”. Here it is meant in a good way: he became full of courage for God’s ways.

---

## Teachers go through the land (Verses 7–9)

[7] In the third year that he was king,
he sent out his officials to teach in the cities of Judah.
They were Ben Hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethanel, and Micaiah.
[8] He sent Levites with them:
Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tobadonijah.
And he sent the priests Elishama and Jehoram with them.
[9] They taught the people in Judah.
They had the book of the LORD’s law with them.
They went to all the cities of Judah
and taught the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is something special: Jehoshaphat sends teachers through the whole land. Princes, Levites and priests go together. They bring the book of the law to the people.
> Jehoshaphat knew: faith needs knowledge. The people should know God’s word themselves.
> The name Jehoshaphat means “The LORD judges”. In Chapter 19 he appoints judges.

---

## Jehoshaphat becomes mighty (Verses 10–13)

[10] All the kingdoms in the lands around Judah were terrified of the LORD.
So they did not go to war against Jehoshaphat.
[11] Some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat gifts and silver as tax.
The Arabs also brought him flocks of animals:
7,700 rams and 7,700 male goats.
[12] Jehoshaphat became more and more powerful.
He built fortresses and storage cities in Judah.
[13] He carried out many projects in the cities of Judah.
And he kept soldiers in Jerusalem, strong and brave men.

> **What does this mean?**
> Because the people learned God’s word, God gave peace. The neighboring peoples had respect.
> The “Arabs” were nomad tribes in the desert south and east of Judah.

---

## Jehoshaphat’s army (Verses 14–19)

[14] Here is the list of them by their families.
From Judah, these were the commanders of thousands:
Adnah was the commander, with 300,000 strong and brave soldiers.
[15] Next to him was the commander Jehohanan, with 280,000 men.
[16] Next to him was Amasiah, the son of Zichri,
who willingly offered himself to the LORD.
He had 200,000 strong and brave soldiers.
[17] From Benjamin:
Eliada, a strong and brave soldier.
He had 200,000 men armed with bows and shields.
[18] Next to him was Jehozabad,
with 180,000 men ready for war.
[19] These men served the king.
There were also the men the king had put in the fortified cities all over Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Together these are more than a million men. That is very many for a small country like Judah. As with the other large numbers, many experts think that “thousand” here means a military unit.
> The small remark about Amasiah in verse 16 is beautiful: he “willingly offered himself to the LORD”. In the middle of a list of numbers, one person is honored for his devotion.
