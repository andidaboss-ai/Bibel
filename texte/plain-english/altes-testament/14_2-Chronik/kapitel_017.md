# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 17
### Jehoshaphat, a good king

---

## Jehoshaphat seeks God (Verses 1–6)

[1] Jehoshaphat his son reigned in his place.
He made himself strong against Israel.
[2] He placed troops in all the fortified cities of Judah.
And he set guard posts in the land of Judah
and in the cities of Ephraim that Asa his father had taken.
[3] The LORD was with Jehoshaphat,
because he walked in the first ways of his father David
and didn’t seek the Baals.
[4] He sought the God of his father
and walked in his commandments,
and not in the ways of Israel.
[5] So the LORD made the kingdom firm in his hand.
All Judah brought gifts to Jehoshaphat,
and he had great riches and honor.
[6] His heart was lifted up in the ways of the LORD.
He also took away the high places and the Asherah poles out of Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat reigned from about 872 to 848 before Christ. Chronicles tells much more about him than the books of Kings.
> “The first ways of his father David”: David was his ancestor. This means David’s good years.
> “Baals” are the different forms of the god Baal, who was worshiped in the northern kingdom under Ahab.
> “His heart was lifted up”: usually this means “to become proud”. Here it is meant in a good way: he became full of courage for God’s ways.

---

## Teachers go through the land (Verses 7–9)

[7] In the third year of his reign
he sent his princes Ben Hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethanel, and Micaiah
to teach in the cities of Judah.
[8] With them he sent Levites:
Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tobadonijah, the Levites.
And with them he sent Elishama and Jehoram, the priests.
[9] They taught in Judah,
and they had the book of the LORD’s law with them.
They went around through all the cities of Judah
and taught among the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is something special: Jehoshaphat sends teachers through the whole land. Princes, Levites and priests go together. They bring the book of the law to the people.
> Jehoshaphat knew: faith needs knowledge. The people should know God’s word themselves.
> The name Jehoshaphat means “The LORD judges”. In Chapter 19 he appoints judges.

---

## Jehoshaphat becomes mighty (Verses 10–13)

[10] The fear of the LORD fell on all the kingdoms of the lands around Judah,
so that they made no war against Jehoshaphat.
[11] Some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat presents, and silver as tribute.
The Arabians also brought him flocks:
7,700 rams and 7,700 male goats.
[12] Jehoshaphat became very great.
He built fortresses and storage cities in Judah.
[13] He had many supplies in the cities of Judah,
and men of war, mighty men of courage, in Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Because the people learned God’s word, God gave peace. The neighboring peoples had respect.
> The “Arabians” were nomad tribes in the desert south and east of Judah.

---

## Jehoshaphat’s army (Verses 14–19)

[14] This was the count of them by their families:
From Judah, the captains of thousands:
Adnah the captain, and with him 300,000 mighty men of courage;
[15] next to him Jehohanan the captain, and with him 280,000;
[16] and next to him Amasiah the son of Zichri,
who willingly offered himself to the LORD,
and with him 200,000 mighty men of courage.
[17] From Benjamin:
Eliada, a mighty man of courage,
and with him 200,000 armed with bow and shield;
[18] and next to him Jehozabad,
and with him 180,000 ready and prepared for war.
[19] These were the ones who served the king,
besides those whom the king put in the fortified cities throughout all Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Together these are more than a million men. That is very many for a small country like Judah. As with the other large numbers, many experts think that “thousand” here means a military unit.
> The small remark about Amasiah in verse 16 is beautiful: he “willingly offered himself to the LORD”. In the middle of a list of numbers, one person is honored for his devotion.
