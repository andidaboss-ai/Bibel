# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 7
### Fire from heaven and God’s answer

---

## Fire from heaven (Verses 1–3)

[1] When Solomon had finished praying,
fire came down from heaven
and burned up the burnt offering and the sacrifices.
And the LORD’s glory filled the house.
[2] The priests could not enter the LORD’s house,
because the LORD’s glory filled the LORD’s house.
[3] All the children of Israel watched when the fire came down,
and the LORD’s glory was on the house.
They bowed down with their faces to the ground on the pavement.
They worshiped and gave thanks to the LORD:
“For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!”

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers Solomon’s prayer with fire from heaven, as with Moses in the wilderness (Leviticus 9:24), with David on the threshing floor (1 Chronicles 21:26) and with Elijah on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:38). The fire shows: God accepts the offering and the house.
> The whole people falls down and sings the words of praise: “For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!” Only Chronicles tells about the fire and this scene, not 1 Kings 8.

---

## The great feast (Verses 4–10)

[4] Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices before the LORD.
[5] King Solomon offered a sacrifice of 22,000 head of cattle and 120,000 sheep.
So the king and all the people dedicated God’s house.
[6] The priests stood in their places.
The Levites also stood with the LORD’s instruments of music,
which David the king had made to give thanks to the LORD,
when David praised through their service:
“For his loving kindness endures forever.”
The priests blew trumpets before them,
and all Israel stood.
[7] Solomon also made the middle of the court in front of the LORD’s house holy.
For there he offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings,
because the bronze altar that Solomon had made was not able to hold
the burnt offering, the grain offering, and the fat.
[8] So Solomon held the feast at that time for seven days,
and all Israel with him, a very great assembly,
from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt.
[9] On the eighth day they held a solemn assembly.
For they kept the dedication of the altar for seven days,
and the feast for seven days.
[10] On the twenty-third day of the seventh month
he sent the people away to their tents,
joyful and glad of heart for the goodness
that the LORD had shown to David, to Solomon, and to Israel his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number of animals for sacrifice is huge. The meat of the peace offerings was eaten together. So it became a great feast meal for the whole people.
> The feast lasted two weeks: seven days for the dedication of the altar and seven days for the Feast of Tabernacles (Booths). Then came one more closing day.
> “The entrance of Hamath” may also be read as a place name: “Lebo-hamath”. It means the far north. The “brook of Egypt” is the southern border. People from the whole land had come.
> At the end everyone goes home “joyful and glad of heart”.

---

## God’s answer to Solomon (Verses 11–16)

[11] So Solomon finished the LORD’s house and the king’s house.
He successfully finished all that he had in his heart to make
in the LORD’s house and in his own house.
[12] Then the LORD appeared to Solomon by night and said to him,
“I have heard your prayer,
and I have chosen this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.
[13] “I may shut up the sky so that there is no rain,
or command the locust to eat up the land,
or send a deadly disease among my people.
[14] Then if my people who are called by my name
will humble themselves, pray, seek my face,
and turn from their wicked ways,
then I will hear from heaven,
I will forgive their sin,
and I will heal their land.
[15] Now my eyes will be open,
and my ears will pay attention to the prayer that is made in this place.
[16] For now I have chosen this house and made it holy,
so that my name may be there forever.
My eyes and my heart will be there always.

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers Solomon’s prayer directly: “I have heard your prayer.”
> Verse 14 is one of the best-known verses of Chronicles. It contains four steps: to humble oneself, to pray, to seek God’s face and to turn back. And three promises of God: to hear, to forgive and to heal. This verse is a key sentence of the whole of Chronicles. Again and again the book tells: when a king or the people act like this, help comes.
> “Heal their land”: God wants to make whole again not only the single people, but also the community and the land.
> These verses 13–15 are not in 1 Kings 9. Chronicles has added them.

---

## Promise and warning (Verses 17–22)

[17] “As for you:
If you will walk before me as David your father walked,
and do all that I have commanded you,
and keep my laws and my rules,
[18] then I will make the throne of your kingdom firm,
as I promised in my covenant with David your father, when I said,
‘You will never lack a man to be ruler in Israel.’
[19] But if you turn away
and leave my laws and my commandments which I have set before you,
and go and serve other gods and worship them,
[20] then I will pull them up by the roots out of my land which I have given them.
And this house, which I have made holy for my name,
I will throw out of my sight.
I will make it a proverb and a word of mockery among all peoples.
[21] This house is so high.
Everyone who passes by it will be shocked and say,
‘Why has the LORD done this to this land and to this house?’
[22] They will answer,
‘Because they abandoned the LORD, the God of their fathers,
who brought them out of the land of Egypt.
They took other gods, worshiped them, and served them.
That is why he has brought all this disaster on them.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises blessing if Solomon stays faithful. But he also warns: if the people worship other gods, even this splendid temple will be destroyed.
> That really happened later (Chapter 36). For the first readers of Chronicles this was already history. These verses explained to them: God was not too weak. The destruction was the result of unfaithfulness.
> But the way back is always open: “If my people … will humble themselves, pray … then I will hear” (verse 14).
