# 2 Chronicles – Chapter 7
### Fire from heaven and God’s answer

---

## Fire from heaven (Verses 1–3)

[1] Solomon finished praying.
Then fire came down from heaven.
It burned up the burnt offering and the sacrifices.
And the LORD’s glory filled the house.
[2] The priests could not go into the LORD’s house,
because the LORD’s glory filled the LORD’s house.
[3] All the people of Israel watched the fire come down.
They saw the LORD’s glory on the house.
They bowed down on the stone floor with their faces to the ground.
They worshiped and thanked the LORD.
They sang, “For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!”
Loving kindness means: faithful love.

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers Solomon’s prayer with fire from heaven, as with Moses in the wilderness (Leviticus 9:24), with David on the threshing floor (1 Chronicles 21:26) and with Elijah on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:38). The fire shows: God accepts the offering and the house.
> The whole people falls down and sings the words of praise: “For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!” Only Chronicles tells about the fire and this scene, not 1 Kings 8.

---

## The great feast (Verses 4–10)

[4] Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices to the LORD.
[5] King Solomon sacrificed 22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep.
In this way the king and all the people dedicated God’s house.
That means: they gave it to God.
[6] The priests stood in their places.
The Levites stood there too, with the LORD’s musical instruments.
King David had made these instruments to give thanks to the LORD.
With them David had praised God through the Levites’ service.
They sang, “For his loving kindness endures forever.”
In front of them, the priests blew trumpets.
And all Israel stood.
[7] Solomon also made the middle of the courtyard in front of the LORD’s house holy.
There he offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings.
The bronze altar that Solomon had made was too small.
It could not hold all the burnt offerings, the grain offerings, and the fat.
[8] So at that time Solomon held the feast for seven days.
All Israel was with him.
It was a very large crowd.
They came from all the land between Lebo Hamath and the Brook of Egypt.
[9] On the eighth day they held a special holy meeting.
They had celebrated the dedication of the altar for seven days,
and the feast for seven days.
[10] On the twenty-third day of the seventh month,
Solomon sent the people home to their tents.
They were joyful and glad in their hearts.
They were glad for all the good things
the LORD had done for David, for Solomon, and for his people Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number of animals for sacrifice is huge. The meat of the peace offerings was eaten together. So it became a great feast meal for the whole people.
> The feast lasted two weeks: seven days for the dedication of the altar and seven days for the Feast of Tabernacles (Booths). Then came one more closing day.
> The Hebrew words for “Lebo Hamath” can also be read as “the entrance of Hamath”. This place lies in the far north. The “brook of Egypt” is the southern border. People from the whole land had come.
> At the end everyone goes home “joyful and glad of heart”.

---

## God’s answer to Solomon (Verses 11–16)

[11] So Solomon finished the LORD’s house and the king’s palace.
He did everything he had planned to do
in the LORD’s house and in his own palace.
Everything went well.
[12] Then the LORD appeared to Solomon at night.
He said to him,
“I have heard your prayer.
I have chosen this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.
[13] “I may shut the sky, so that there is no rain.
I may command locusts to eat up the land.
I may send a deadly disease among my people.
[14] My people are called by my name.
If my people will humble themselves, pray, and seek my face,
and if they turn away from their wicked ways,
then I will hear from heaven.
I will forgive their sin,
and I will heal their land.
[15] From now on my eyes will be open.
My ears will listen to the prayers that are prayed in this place.
[16] I have chosen this house now, and I have made it holy.
My name will be there forever.
My eyes and my heart will always be there.

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers Solomon’s prayer directly: “I have heard your prayer.”
> Verse 14 is one of the best-known verses of Chronicles. It contains four steps: to humble oneself, to pray, to seek God’s face and to turn back. And three promises of God: to hear, to forgive and to heal. This verse is a key sentence of the whole of Chronicles. Again and again the book tells: when a king or the people act like this, help comes.
> “Heal their land”: God wants to make whole again not only the single people, but also the community and the land.
> Verses 13–15 are not in 1 Kings 9. Chronicles has added them.

---

## Promise and warning (Verses 17–22)

[17] “And you:
Live before me as your father David lived.
Do everything I have commanded you.
Keep my laws and my rules.
[18] Then I will make the throne of your kingdom firm.
I promised this in my covenant with your father David.
I said, ‘There will always be a man from your family to rule in Israel.’
[19] But you may turn away from me.
You may leave the laws and commandments that I have given you.
You may go and serve other gods and worship them.
[20] Then I will pull up my people by the roots from my land, the land I gave them.
And I will throw this house out of my sight,
the house I have made holy for my name.
I will make it a joke and an object of mockery among all peoples.
[21] This house is so great now.
But then everyone who passes by will be shocked.
They will say, ‘Why has the LORD done this to this land and to this house?’
[22] And people will answer,
‘Because they abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors,
who brought them out of the land of Egypt.
They turned to other gods.
They worshiped them and served them.
That is why the LORD has brought all this disaster on them.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises blessing if Solomon stays faithful. But he also warns: if the people worship other gods, even this splendid temple will be destroyed.
> That really happened later (Chapter 36). For the first readers of Chronicles this was already history. These verses explained to them: God was not too weak. The destruction was the result of unfaithfulness.
> But the way back is always open: “If my people will humble themselves, pray, and seek my face … then I will hear” (verse 14).
