# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 5
### The tribes east of the Jordan

---

## Reuben, the firstborn (Verses 1–3)

[1] The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel
(he was the firstborn,
but because he dishonored his father’s bed,
his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph the son of Israel;
so he is not listed in the family records as the firstborn.
[2] For Judah became stronger than his brothers,
and from him came the prince;
but the birthright was Joseph’s)—
[3] the sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel:
Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.

> **What does this mean?**
> Reuben was Jacob’s oldest son. But he had slept with Bilhah, a wife of his father (Genesis 35:22). That is why he lost his special right as the firstborn.
> The birthright was divided: Joseph got the double share of the land (his sons Ephraim and Manasseh became two tribes). Judah got the leadership: from him came King David.
> “The prince”: this means David. Later people also connected this word with the Messiah they were waiting for.

---

## The families of Reuben (Verses 4–10)

[4] The sons of Joel:
Shemaiah his son,
Gog his son,
Shimei his son,
[5] Micah his son,
Reaiah his son,
Baal his son,
[6] and Beerah his son,
whom Tilgath Pilneser king of Assyria carried away as a captive.
He was prince of the Reubenites.
[7] His brothers by their families,
when the family records of their generations were written down:
the chief, Jeiel, and Zechariah,
[8] and Bela the son of Azaz, the son of Shema, the son of Joel.
He lived in Aroer, even to Nebo and Baal Meon.
[9] Eastward he lived as far as the entrance of the wilderness that reaches to the river Euphrates,
because their livestock had multiplied in the land of Gilead.
[10] In the days of Saul they made war with the Hagrites,
who fell by their hand.
And they lived in their tents throughout all the land east of Gilead.

> **What does this mean?**
> We are not told how Joel is connected with Reuben.
> Tilgath Pilneser is another spelling of Tiglath Pileser. He was the Assyrian king who carried away the tribes east of the Jordan around 733 before Christ (2 Kings 15:29).
> Aroer, Nebo and Baal Meon were east of the Dead Sea. These places are also named on the Mesha Stone.
> The Hagrites were probably an Arab tribe. Their name reminds us of Hagar, the mother of Ishmael.

---

## The tribe of Gad (Verses 11–17)

[11] The sons of Gad lived beside them in the land of Bashan as far as Salecah:
[12] Joel the chief,
Shapham the second,
Janai, and Shaphat in Bashan.
[13] Their brothers of their fathers’ houses:
Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber, seven.
[14] These were the sons of Abihail,
the son of Huri, the son of Jaroah, the son of Gilead,
the son of Michael, the son of Jeshishai, the son of Jahdo, the son of Buz.
[15] Ahi the son of Abdiel, the son of Guni,
was chief of their fathers’ houses.
[16] They lived in Gilead, in Bashan and in its towns,
and in all the pasture lands of Sharon as far as their borders.
[17] All these were listed in the family records in the days of Jotham king of Judah,
and in the days of Jeroboam king of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gad lived east of the Jordan, in Gilead and Bashan. That was good pasture for herds of cattle.
> “Sharon” here is probably not the well-known coastal plain, but an area east of the Jordan.
> The family records were written down under King Jotham of Judah and King Jeroboam II of Israel, so around 750 before Christ.

---

## War against the Hagrites (Verses 18–22)

[18] The sons of Reuben, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh had brave men,
men who could carry shield and sword and shoot with the bow,
skillful in war:
44,760 men who were able to go out to war.
[19] They made war with the Hagrites,
with Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab.
[20] They were helped against them,
and the Hagrites were handed over to them,
with all who were with them.
For they cried to God in the battle,
and he answered them because they put their trust in him.
[21] They took away their livestock:
50,000 of their camels,
250,000 sheep,
2,000 donkeys,
and 100,000 people.
[22] For many fell slain, because the war was of God.
And they lived in their place until the captivity.

> **What does this mean?**
> The message of Chronicles: They won because they cried to God in their need and trusted him.
> Jetur and Naphish were sons of Ishmael (1 Chronicles 1:31). So the Hagrites were Arab tribes.
> The numbers are very large. As with other old war reports, it is unclear how exactly they are meant.
> “The war was of God” is an interpretation from the point of view of the winners. For the defeated it was great suffering, death and captivity. Today no one may call on God to justify war against other peoples.

---

## The half-tribe of Manasseh and the deportation (Verses 23–26)

[23] The children of the half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the land.
They grew in number, from Bashan to Baal Hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon.
[24] These were the heads of their fathers’ houses:
Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel,
mighty men of courage, famous men, heads of their fathers’ houses.
[25] But they were unfaithful to the God of their fathers.
They played the prostitute after the gods of the peoples of the land,
whom God had destroyed before them.
[26] So the God of Israel stirred up the spirit of Pul king of Assyria,
and the spirit of Tilgath Pilneser king of Assyria.
And he carried away the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh,
and brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and to the river of Gozan,
to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the north, as far as Mount Hermon. Hermon is the highest mountain of the region. “Senir” is another name for it.
> Chronicles explains briefly: They lost their land because they became unfaithful to God. “Playing the prostitute” is a picture for worshiping idols: being unfaithful to God like being unfaithful in marriage.
> “Pul” and “Tilgath Pilneser” are probably two names for the same king (2 Kings 15:19, 29). Chronicles names them here side by side.
> This was the first deportation of Israelites, around 733 before Christ.
> In some Bibles chapter 5 goes on to verse 41. Verses 1–15 of the next chapter are there chapter 5, verses 27–41.
