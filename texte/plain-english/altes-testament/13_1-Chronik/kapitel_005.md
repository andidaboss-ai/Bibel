# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 5
### The tribes east of the Jordan

---

## Reuben, the firstborn (Verses 1–3)

[1] These are the sons of Reuben, the firstborn son of Israel.
Reuben was the firstborn.
But he dishonored his father’s bed.
That means: he slept with one of his father’s wives.
That is why his rights as the firstborn were given to the sons of Joseph, the son of Israel.
So Reuben is not listed in the family records as the firstborn.
[2] Judah became stronger than his brothers.
The ruler came from him.
But the rights of the firstborn belonged to Joseph.
[3] The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel, were
Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.

> **What does this mean?**
> Reuben was Jacob’s oldest son. But he had slept with Bilhah, a wife of his father (Genesis 35:22). That is why he lost his special right as the firstborn.
> The birthright was divided: Joseph got the double share of the land (his sons Ephraim and Manasseh became two tribes). Judah got the leadership: from him came King David.
> “The ruler”: this means David. Later people also connected this word with the Messiah they were waiting for.

---

## The families of Reuben (Verses 4–10)

[4] The descendants of Joel were
his son Shemaiah,
his son Gog,
his son Shimei,
[5] his son Micah,
his son Reaiah,
his son Baal,
[6] and his son Beerah.
Tilgath Pilneser, the king of Assyria, took Beerah away as a prisoner.
Beerah was the leader of the Reubenites.
[7] These were his relatives by their families,
as they were written in the family records:
Jeiel, the chief, and Zechariah,
[8] and Bela, the son of Azaz, the son of Shema, the son of Joel.
Bela lived in Aroer and as far as Nebo and Baal Meon.
[9] To the east he lived as far as the edge of the wilderness that reaches to the Euphrates River.
They lived there because they had so many animals in the land of Gilead.
[10] When Saul was king, they fought a war against the Hagrites.
They defeated them.
Then they lived in their tents in the whole land east of Gilead.

> **What does this mean?**
> We are not told how Joel is connected with Reuben.
> Tilgath Pilneser is another spelling of Tiglath Pileser. He was the Assyrian king who carried away the tribes east of the Jordan around 733 BC (2 Kings 15:29).
> Aroer, Nebo and Baal Meon were east of the Dead Sea. These places are also named on the Mesha Stone.
> The Hagrites were probably an Arab tribe. Their name reminds us of Hagar, the mother of Ishmael.

## The tribe of Gad (Verses 11–17)

[11] The descendants of Gad lived next to them,
in the land of Bashan as far as Salecah.
[12] Joel was the chief.
Shapham was the second.
Then came Janai and Shaphat in Bashan.
[13] Their relatives, by their families, were
Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia, and Eber, seven in all.
[14] These were the sons of Abihail.
Abihail was the son of Huri, the son of Jaroah, the son of Gilead,
the son of Michael, the son of Jeshishai, the son of Jahdo, the son of Buz.
[15] Ahi, the son of Abdiel, the son of Guni,
was the chief of their families.
[16] They lived in Gilead, in Bashan and its towns,
and in all the pasture lands of Sharon, as far as their borders.
[17] All these were written in the family records
when Jotham was king of Judah
and Jeroboam was king of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gad lived east of the Jordan, in Gilead and Bashan. That was good pasture for herds of cattle.
> “Sharon” here is probably not the well-known coastal plain, but an area east of the Jordan.
> The family records were written down under King Jotham of Judah and King Jeroboam II of Israel, so around 750 BC.

---

## War against the Hagrites (Verses 18–22)

[18] The descendants of Reuben, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh had brave soldiers.
They could carry shield and sword.
They could shoot with the bow.
They were skilled in war.
There were 44,760 men who could go out to war.
[19] They fought a war against the Hagrites,
against Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab.
[20] They received help against these enemies.
The Hagrites were handed over to them,
together with all who were with them.
During the battle they cried out to God.
He answered them because they trusted in him.
[21] They took away the animals of the Hagrites:
50,000 camels,
250,000 sheep,
and 2,000 donkeys.
They also took away 100,000 people.
[22] Many enemies were killed, because the war came from God.
They lived in the land of the Hagrites until they were taken away as prisoners.

> **What does this mean?**
> The message of Chronicles: They won because they cried to God in their need and trusted him.
> Jetur and Naphish were sons of Ishmael (1 Chronicles 1:31). So the Hagrites were Arab tribes.
> The numbers are very large. As with other old war reports, it is unclear how exactly they are meant.
> “The war came from God” is an interpretation from the point of view of the winners. For the defeated it was great suffering, death and captivity. Today no one may call on God to justify war against other peoples.

---

## The half-tribe of Manasseh and the deportation (Verses 23–26)

[23] The people of the half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the land.
They became many, from Bashan to Baal Hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon.
[24] These were the heads of their families:
Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah, and Jahdiel.
They were brave soldiers, famous men, and heads of their families.
[25] But they were unfaithful to the God of their ancestors.
They acted like prostitutes
and ran after the gods of the peoples of the land.
God had destroyed these peoples before them.
[26] So the God of Israel moved Pul, the king of Assyria, to act.
He moved Tilgath Pilneser, the king of Assyria.
He took away the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh as prisoners.
He brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara, and to the river of Gozan.
They are still there to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the north, as far as Mount Hermon. Hermon is the highest mountain of the region. “Senir” is another name for it.
> Chronicles explains briefly: They lost their land because they became unfaithful to God. “They acted like prostitutes”: this is a picture for worshiping idols: being unfaithful to God like being unfaithful in marriage.
> “Pul” and “Tilgath Pilneser” are probably two names for the same king (2 Kings 15:19, 29). Chronicles names them here side by side.
> This was the first deportation of Israelites, around 733 BC.
> In some Bibles, Chapter 5 goes on to verse 41. There, verses 1–15 of the next chapter are 5:27–41.
