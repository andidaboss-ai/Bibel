# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 16
### David’s song of thanks

---

## The ark has arrived (Verses 1–7)

[1] They brought in God’s ark.
They put it in the middle of the tent that David had set up for it.
Then they offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before God.
[2] David finished offering the burnt offerings and the peace offerings.
Then he blessed the people in the name of the LORD.
[3] He gave something to every Israelite, man and woman.
Each one got a loaf of bread, a portion of meat, and a cake of raisins.
[4] David chose some of the Levites
to serve in front of the LORD’s ark.
They were to remember the LORD, the God of Israel,
to thank him and to praise him.
[5] Asaph was the leader.
Zechariah was second after him.
Then came Jeiel, Shemiramoth, Jehiel, Mattithiah, Eliab, Benaiah, Obed-Edom, and Jeiel.
They played lyres and harps.
Asaph played loudly on the cymbals.
[6] The priests Benaiah and Jahaziel always blew trumpets
in front of the ark of God’s covenant.
[7] On that day David for the first time gave Asaph and his relatives this task:
They were to give thanks to the LORD with this song:

> **What does this mean?**
> At last the ark is in Jerusalem. David blesses the people, and everyone gets something to eat, men and women alike. The celebration is for everyone.
> David appoints singers who sing continually before the ark and praise God. Praise and thanks should happen not only on feast days, but every day.
> “Cake of raisins”: the Hebrew word is not completely certain.

---

## The song of thanks: Tell of God’s deeds (Verses 8–22)

[8] Give thanks to the LORD!
Call on his name!
Tell the peoples what he has done!
[9] Sing to him, sing praises to him!
Tell about all his wonderful works!
[10] Be proud of his holy name!
Let the hearts of those who seek the LORD be glad.
[11] Seek the LORD and his strength!
Seek his face always!
[12] Remember the wonderful works he has done,
his wonders, and the judgments he has spoken,
[13] you descendants of Israel, his servant,
you children of Jacob, his chosen ones!
[14] He is the LORD, our God.
His judgments are in all the earth.
[15] Remember his covenant forever,
the word he commanded for a thousand generations.
[16] It is the covenant he made with Abraham,
and the promise he swore to Isaac.
[17] He confirmed it to Jacob as a law,
to Israel as a covenant that lasts forever.
[18] He said,
“I will give you the land of Canaan.
It will be your share and your inheritance.”
[19] At that time you were only a few people,
yes, very few, and you were foreigners in the land.
[20] They moved around from nation to nation,
from one kingdom to another people.
[21] He did not allow anyone to harm them.
Yes, he warned kings because of them:
[22] “Do not touch my anointed ones!
Do not harm my prophets!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This song is made of parts of three psalms. Verses 8–22 are also in Psalm 105:1–15.
> The song invites us: give thanks to God, sing, tell others about what he has done. Faith is not only something private, but should be passed on.
> “Seek his face always!”: God wants us to seek his nearness, every day.
> Then the song remembers the history: God made a covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. They were only a few, foreigners in the land, always on the move. But God protected them.
> “My anointed ones” and “my prophets” here are the ancestors Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. For example, God warned Pharaoh and King Abimelech not to harm them (Genesis 12:17; 20:3–7).
> “A thousand generations” means: forever.

---

## The song of thanks: The whole world should praise God (Verses 23–33)

[23] Sing to the LORD, all the earth!
Tell about how he saves, day after day!
[24] Tell the nations about his glory.
Tell all the peoples about his wonderful works!
[25] For the LORD is great, and he deserves great praise!
He is to be feared more than all gods.
[26] For all the gods of the peoples are idols,
but the LORD made the heavens.
[27] Honor and majesty are before him.
Strength and joy are in his place.
[28] You families of the nations, give to the LORD,
give to the LORD glory and strength!
[29] Give to the LORD the glory his name deserves!
Bring an offering, and come before him!
Worship the LORD in holy clothing!
[30] Tremble before him, all the earth!
The world also stands firm.
It cannot be moved.
[31] Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice!
Let them say among the nations,
“The LORD reigns!”
That means: The LORD is King!
[32] Let the sea roar, and everything that fills it!
Let the field and everything in it celebrate!
[33] Then the trees of the forest will sing for joy before the LORD,
for he comes to judge the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> This part is also in Psalm 96.
> Now the view goes beyond Israel: all peoples, the whole earth, should praise God. God is not only the God of one people, but the Creator of the heavens.
> All creation rejoices with them: the heavens, the earth, the sea, the fields, even the trees. What a beautiful picture!
> “He comes to judge the earth”: here this is a reason for joy. God’s judgment means: he puts everything right and brings justice for all who suffer wrong.
> “Holy clothing” can also mean “holy splendor,” that is: with awe and in festive clothes.

---

## The song of thanks: Give thanks to the LORD (Verses 34–36)

[34] Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good!
His loving kindness lasts forever.
[35] Say,
“Save us, God who saves us!
Gather us together and rescue us from the nations.
Then we will give thanks to your holy name.
Then we will be proud to praise you.”
[36] Praise the LORD, the God of Israel,
from everlasting to everlasting!
Then all the people said, “Amen,”
and they praised the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> This part is also in Psalm 106:1 and 106:47–48.
> “Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good! His loving kindness lasts forever”: this is one of the most important and most frequent words of praise in the Bible. It is still prayed today in Jewish and Christian worship.
> Verse 35 is interesting: “Gather us together and rescue us from the nations.” At the time of David the Israelites were not scattered. But for the first readers of Chronicles, after the exile, this was a very real prayer.
> “Amen” means: “So it is,” “That is certain.” All the people agree.

---

## The daily worship (Verses 37–43)

[37] So David left Asaph and his relatives there
in front of the ark of the LORD’s covenant.
They were to serve in front of the ark all the time,
as the work of each day required.
[38] He also left Obed-Edom and their 68 relatives there.
Obed-Edom, the son of Jeduthun, and Hosah were to be doorkeepers.
[39] And he left the priest Zadok and his relatives, the priests,
in front of the LORD’s tabernacle.
It stood at the place of worship on the hill at Gibeon.
[40] There they were to offer burnt offerings to the LORD on the altar of burnt offering.
They were to do this always, morning and evening.
They were to do everything that is written in the LORD’s law,
which he gave to Israel.
[41] With them were Heman and Jeduthun
and the others who had been chosen by name.
They were to give thanks to the LORD,
because his loving kindness lasts forever.
[42] Heman and Jeduthun had trumpets and cymbals for those who played loudly.
They also had instruments for the songs of God.
The sons of Jeduthun were to guard the gate.
[43] Then all the people went home.
And David went home to bless his family.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the time of David there were two places for worship: in Jerusalem the ark stood in a tent, and Asaph and his people sang there. In Gibeon the old tabernacle from the time in the wilderness still stood, with the altar of burnt offering. There Zadok and the priests offered sacrifices. Only under Solomon did everything come together in the temple.
> Jeduthun was another leader of the music. He is probably also called Ethan (1 Chronicles 15:17).
> At the end David goes home “to bless his family.” After the great celebration for the whole people, he also thinks of his own family.
