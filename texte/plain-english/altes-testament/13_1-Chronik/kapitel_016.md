# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 16
### David’s song of thanks

---

## The ark has arrived (Verses 1–7)

[1] They brought in God’s ark
and set it in the middle of the tent that David had set up for it.
And they offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before God.
[2] When David had finished offering the burnt offering and the peace offerings,
he blessed the people in the LORD’s name.
[3] He gave to everyone of Israel, both man and woman,
to everyone a loaf of bread, a portion of meat, and a cake of raisins.
[4] He appointed some of the Levites
to serve before the LORD’s ark,
and to remember, to thank, and to praise the LORD, the God of Israel:
[5] Asaph the chief,
and second to him Zechariah,
then Jeiel, Shemiramoth, Jehiel, Mattithiah, Eliab, Benaiah, Obed-Edom, and Jeiel,
with stringed instruments and with harps.
And Asaph with cymbals, sounding aloud.
[6] And Benaiah and Jahaziel the priests with trumpets,
continually before the ark of the covenant of God.
[7] Then on that day David for the first time ordered
that thanks be given to the LORD by Asaph and his brothers:

> **What does this mean?**
> At last the ark is in Jerusalem. David blesses the people, and everyone gets something to eat, men and women alike. The celebration is for everyone.
> David appoints singers who sing continually before the ark and praise God. Praise and thanks should happen not only on feast days, but every day.
> “Cake of raisins”: the Hebrew word is not completely certain.

---

## The song of thanks: Tell of God’s deeds (Verses 8–22)

[8] Oh give thanks to the LORD!
Call on his name!
Make what he has done known among the peoples.
[9] Sing to him. Sing praises to him.
Tell of all his marvelous works.
[10] Glory in his holy name.
Let the heart of those who seek the LORD rejoice.
[11] Seek the LORD and his strength.
Seek his face forever more.
[12] Remember his marvelous works that he has done,
his wonders, and the judgments of his mouth,
[13] you offspring of Israel his servant,
you children of Jacob, his chosen ones.
[14] He is the LORD our God.
His judgments are in all the earth.
[15] Remember his covenant forever,
the word which he commanded to a thousand generations,
[16] the covenant which he made with Abraham,
his oath to Isaac.
[17] He confirmed it to Jacob as a law,
and to Israel as an everlasting covenant,
[18] saying,
“I will give you the land of Canaan,
the share of your inheritance,”
[19] when you were only a few people in number,
yes, very few, and foreigners in it.
[20] They went about from nation to nation,
from one kingdom to another people.
[21] He allowed no one to do them wrong.
Yes, he corrected kings for their sakes:
[22] “Don’t touch my anointed ones!
Do my prophets no harm!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This song is made of parts of three psalms. Verses 8–22 are also in Psalm 105:1–15.
> The song invites us: give thanks to God, sing, tell others about what he has done. Faith is not only something private, but should be passed on.
> “Seek his face forever more”: God wants us to seek his nearness, every day.
> Then the song remembers the history: God made a covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. They were only a few, foreigners in the land, always on the move. But God protected them.
> “My anointed ones” and “my prophets” here are the ancestors Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. For example, God warned Pharaoh and King Abimelech not to harm them (Genesis 12:17; 20:3–7).
> “A thousand generations” means: forever.

## The song of thanks: The whole world should praise God (Verses 23–33)

[23] Sing to the LORD, all the earth!
Show his salvation from day to day.
[24] Declare his glory among the nations,
and his marvelous works among all the peoples.
[25] For great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised.
He also is to be feared above all gods.
[26] For all the gods of the peoples are idols,
but the LORD made the heavens.
[27] Honor and majesty are before him.
Strength and gladness are in his place.
[28] Give to the LORD, you families of the peoples,
give to the LORD glory and strength!
[29] Give to the LORD the glory due to his name.
Bring an offering, and come before him.
Worship the LORD in holy array.
[30] Tremble before him, all the earth.
The world also stands firm, so that it can’t be moved.
[31] Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice!
Let them say among the nations,
“The LORD reigns!”
[32] Let the sea roar, and all that fills it!
Let the field rejoice, and all that is in it!
[33] Then the trees of the forest will sing for joy before the LORD,
for he comes to judge the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> This part is also in Psalm 96.
> Now the view goes beyond Israel: all peoples, the whole earth, should praise God. God is not only the God of one people, but the Creator of the heavens.
> All creation rejoices with them: the heavens, the earth, the sea, the fields, even the trees. What a beautiful picture!
> “He comes to judge the earth”: here this is a reason for joy. God’s judgment means: he puts everything right and brings justice for all who suffer wrong.
> “Holy array” can also mean “in holy splendor”, that is: with awe and in festive clothes.

---

## The song of thanks: Give thanks to the LORD (Verses 34–36)

[34] Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness endures forever.
[35] Say,
“Save us, God of our salvation!
Gather us together and deliver us from the nations,
so that we may give thanks to your holy name
and triumph in your praise.”
[36] Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
from everlasting to everlasting.
All the people said, “Amen,”
and praised the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> This part is also in Psalm 106:1 and 106:47–48.
> “Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever”: this is one of the most important and most frequent words of praise in the Bible. It is still prayed today in Jewish and Christian worship.
> Verse 35 is interesting: “Gather us together and deliver us from the nations”. At the time of David the Israelites were not scattered. But for the first readers of Chronicles, after the exile, this was a very real prayer.
> “Amen” means: “So it is”, “That is certain”. All the people agree.

---

## The daily worship (Verses 37–43)

[37] So he left Asaph and his brothers there before the ark of the LORD’s covenant,
to serve before the ark continually, as every day’s work required;
[38] and Obed-Edom with their sixty-eight relatives;
and Obed-Edom the son of Jeduthun and Hosah to be doorkeepers.
[39] And he left Zadok the priest and his brothers the priests
before the LORD’s tabernacle in the high place that was at Gibeon,
[40] to offer burnt offerings to the LORD on the altar of burnt offering continually,
morning and evening,
according to all that is written in the LORD’s law, which he commanded to Israel.
[41] With them were Heman and Jeduthun
and the rest who were chosen, who were mentioned by name,
to give thanks to the LORD, because his loving kindness endures forever.
[42] And with them were Heman and Jeduthun with trumpets and cymbals for those who should sound aloud,
and with instruments for the songs of God.
And the sons of Jeduthun were to be at the gate.
[43] Then all the people left, each man to his house.
And David returned to bless his house.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the time of David there were two places for worship: in Jerusalem the ark stood in a tent, and Asaph and his people sang there. In Gibeon the old tabernacle from the time in the wilderness still stood, with the altar of burnt offering. There Zadok and the priests offered sacrifices. Only under Solomon did everything come together in the temple.
> Jeduthun was another leader of the music. He is probably also called Ethan (1 Chronicles 15:17).
> At the end David goes home “to bless his house”. After the great celebration for the whole people, he also thinks of his own family.
