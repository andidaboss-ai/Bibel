# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 13
### The first attempt to bring the ark

---

## David wants to bring the ark to Jerusalem (Verses 1–8)

[1] David consulted with the captains of thousands and of hundreds,
with every leader.
[2] David said to all the assembly of Israel,
“If it seems good to you, and if it is from the LORD our God,
let’s send word everywhere
to our brothers who are left in all the land of Israel,
and to the priests and Levites with them in their cities that have pasture lands,
so that they may gather to us.
[3] And let’s bring the ark of our God back to us,
for we didn’t seek it in the days of Saul.”
[4] All the assembly said that they would do so,
for the thing was right in the eyes of all the people.
[5] So David gathered all Israel together,
from the Shihor River of Egypt to the entrance of Hamath,
to bring God’s ark from Kiriath Jearim.
[6] David went up with all Israel to Baalah, that is, to Kiriath Jearim, which belonged to Judah,
to bring up from there the ark of God the LORD,
who sits above the cherubim,
the ark that is called by the Name.
[7] They carried God’s ark on a new cart
and brought it out of Abinadab’s house.
Uzza and Ahio drove the cart.
[8] David and all Israel played music before God with all their might,
with songs, with harps, with stringed instruments, with tambourines, with cymbals, and with trumpets.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chronicles tells the story of the ark differently from 2 Samuel 6: right after David has become king, he takes care of the ark. Worship is the most important thing to him.
> David asks the assembly and wants God’s will. He includes everyone, also the priests and Levites.
> The ark was the sign that God was present. It had stood forgotten in Kiriath Jearim for about 20 years (1 Samuel 7:1–2). “We didn’t seek it in the days of Saul” (1 Chronicles 13:3). That means: under Saul, God had been pushed to the edge.
> The Shihor is a river at the border of Egypt. “The entrance of Hamath” (Lebo Hamath) is far in the north. The whole land is there.
> It is a great celebration with a lot of music.

---

## Uzza dies (Verses 9–14)

[9] When they came to Chidon’s threshing floor,
Uzza put out his hand to hold the ark,
for the oxen stumbled.
[10] The LORD’s anger burned against Uzza,
and he struck him because he put his hand on the ark.
And he died there before God.
[11] David was angry,
because the LORD had broken out against Uzza.
He called that place Perez Uzza, to this day.
[12] David was afraid of God that day and said,
“How can I bring God’s ark home to me?”
[13] So David didn’t move the ark with him into David’s city,
but had it taken aside into the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite.
[14] God’s ark stayed with the family of Obed-Edom in his house three months.
And the LORD blessed Obed-Edom’s house and all that he had.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is very hard. Uzza only wants to help, and he dies. That seems unfair to us.
> Chronicles explains it in 1 Chronicles 15:13: the ark should not have been carried on a cart. It should have been carried by Levites on poles (Numbers 4:15). That is how the Philistines had done it (1 Samuel 6:7), not as God had commanded. And no one was allowed to touch the holy objects.
> The story wants to say: God’s holiness is serious. God is not an object that you can use according to your own ideas. Still, something hard and hard to understand remains. David too was angry and afraid. He was allowed to show this before God.
> “Perez Uzza” means “breaking out against Uzza”.
> Obed-Edom came from Gath. Perhaps he was a Philistine, perhaps a Levite from the Levite town of Gath Rimmon. His house is blessed: the ark brings blessing when people respect it.
> In 2 Samuel 6:6 the threshing floor is called “the threshing floor of Nacon”.
