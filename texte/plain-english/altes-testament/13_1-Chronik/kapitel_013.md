# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 13
### The first attempt to bring the ark

---

## David wants to bring the ark to Jerusalem (Verses 1–8)

[1] David talked with the commanders of thousands and of hundreds.
He talked with every leader.
[2] Then David said to the whole assembly of Israel,
“If you think it is good,
and if it comes from the LORD our God,
let us send messengers everywhere.
Let us send them to our relatives who are left in all the land of Israel.
The priests and Levites live with them in their cities with pastures.
Let us ask them all to come together to us.
[3] And let us bring the ark of our God back to us.
We did not seek it in the days of Saul.”
[4] The whole assembly agreed to do it.
All the people thought it was the right thing.
[5] So David gathered all Israel together,
from the Shihor River of Egypt to the entrance of Hamath.
They came to bring God’s ark from Kiriath Jearim.
[6] David and all Israel went up to Baalah.
Baalah is Kiriath Jearim, which belonged to Judah.
They wanted to bring up from there the ark of God, the LORD.
The LORD sits on his throne above the cherubim.
Cherubim are heavenly beings.
The ark is called by his Name.
[7] They put God’s ark on a new cart
and brought it out of Abinadab’s house.
Uzza and Ahio drove the cart.
[8] David and all Israel celebrated before God with all their strength.
They sang songs.
They played harps, lyres, tambourines, cymbals, and trumpets.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chronicles tells the story of the ark differently from 2 Samuel 6: right after David has become king, he takes care of the ark. Worship is the most important thing to him.
> David asks the assembly and wants God’s will. He includes everyone, also the priests and Levites.
> The ark was the sign that God was present. It had stood forgotten in Kiriath Jearim for about 20 years (1 Samuel 7:1–2). “We did not seek it in the days of Saul” (1 Chronicles 13:3). That means: under Saul, God had been pushed to the edge.
> The Shihor is a river at the border of Egypt. “The entrance of Hamath” (Lebo Hamath) is far in the north. The whole land is there.
> It is a great celebration with a lot of music.

---

## Uzza dies (Verses 9–14)

[9] They came to the threshing floor of Chidon.
There the oxen stumbled.
So Uzza reached out his hand to hold the ark.
[10] Then the LORD became very angry with Uzza.
He struck him down, because Uzza had put his hand on the ark.
Uzza died there before God.
[11] David was upset,
because the LORD had broken out against Uzza.
So that place is called Perez Uzza to this day.
[12] That day David was afraid of God.
He said,
“How can I ever bring God’s ark home to me?”
[13] So David did not take the ark with him into the City of David.
Instead he took it aside to the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite.
[14] God’s ark stayed with the family of Obed-Edom in his house for three months.
And the LORD blessed Obed-Edom’s family and everything he had.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is very hard. Uzza only wants to help, and he dies. That seems unfair to us.
> Chronicles explains it in 1 Chronicles 15:13: the ark should not have been carried on a cart. It should have been carried by Levites on poles (Numbers 4:15). That is how the Philistines had done it (1 Samuel 6:7), not as God had commanded. And no one was allowed to touch the holy objects.
> The story wants to say: God’s holiness is serious. God is not an object that you can use according to your own ideas. Still, something hard and hard to understand remains. David too was angry and afraid. He was allowed to show this before God.
> “Perez Uzza” means “breaking out against Uzza.”
> Obed-Edom came from Gath. Perhaps he was a Philistine, perhaps a Levite from the Levite town of Gath Rimmon. His house is blessed: the ark brings blessing when people respect it.
> In 2 Samuel 6:6 the threshing floor is called “the threshing floor of Nacon.”
