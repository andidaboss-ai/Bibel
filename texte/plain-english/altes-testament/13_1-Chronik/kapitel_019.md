# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 19
### War with the Ammonites

---

## An insult (Verses 1–5)

[1] After this, Nahash, the king of the Ammonites, died.
His son became king in his place.
[2] David said,
“I will be kind to Hanun, the son of Nahash,
because his father was kind to me.”
So David sent messengers to comfort Hanun about the death of his father.
David’s servants came to Hanun in the land of the Ammonites to comfort him.
[3] But the leaders of the Ammonites said to Hanun,
“Do you really think David wants to honor your father
by sending people to comfort you?
No! His servants have come to look around,
to destroy the land, and to spy it out.”
[4] So Hanun seized David’s servants and shaved them.
He cut off their clothes in the middle, up to their buttocks.
Then he sent them away.
[5] Some people went and told David what had happened to the men.
So David sent people to meet them,
because the men were deeply ashamed.
The king said,
“Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown back.
Then come home.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David wants to be kind and honor an old friendship. But the advisers of the new king are suspicious and turn the kindness into a suspicion.
> The messengers are badly humiliated. Shaving off the beard and cutting the clothes off up to the buttocks was a great disgrace back then.
> David shows compassion for his men. He lets them wait until they can show themselves again. He protects their dignity.
> The story is also in 2 Samuel 10.

---

## Ammon looks for allies (Verses 6–9)

[6] The Ammonites saw that David now hated them.
So Hanun and the Ammonites sent 1,000 talents of silver
to hire chariots and horsemen from Mesopotamia, from Aram-maacah, and from Zobah.
[7] They hired 32,000 chariots.
They also hired the king of Maacah and his army.
These came and set up camp near Medeba.
The Ammonites also gathered from their cities and came out to fight.
[8] David heard about it.
So he sent Joab with the whole army of mighty men.
[9] The Ammonites came out.
They lined up for battle at the gate of their city.
The kings who had come stood apart in the open field.

> **What does this mean?**
> Instead of saying sorry, the Ammonites prepare for war. They pay foreign armies.
> The Bible says “1,000 talents of silver.” A talent is about 75 pounds.
> The number of 32,000 chariots is very high. 2 Samuel 10:6 has 33,000 men instead.
> Medeba lay east of the Dead Sea. Mesopotamia here literally means “Aram of the two rivers,” that is, the area along the upper Euphrates.

## Joab and Abishai (Verses 10–15)

[10] Joab saw that the enemy was ready to attack him from the front and from behind.
So he chose some of the best soldiers of Israel.
He lined them up to fight against the Syrians.
[11] He put the rest of the army under the command of his brother Abishai.
They lined up to fight against the Ammonites.
[12] Joab said,
“If the Syrians are too strong for me, then you must help me.
But if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will help you.
[13] Be brave!
Let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our God.
May the LORD do what seems good to him.”
[14] Then Joab and his soldiers went forward to fight against the Syrians.
And the Syrians ran away from him.
[15] The Ammonites saw that the Syrians had run away.
So they too ran away from Joab’s brother Abishai.
They went back into the city.
Then Joab went back to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joab is surrounded: in front the Ammonites, behind the Syrians. He divides the army and agrees with his brother: we will help each other.
> His words are an example of courage and trust in God: we do what we can. And then: “May the LORD do what seems good to him.” You should give your best and leave the result to God.
> In Hebrew the “Syrians” are called “Arameans.”

---

## The victory over the Syrians (Verses 16–19)

[16] The Syrians saw that Israel had defeated them.
So they sent messengers.
They called for the Syrians from the other side of the River.
Shophach, the commander of Hadadezer’s army, led them.
[17] David heard about it.
So he gathered all Israel and crossed the Jordan.
He came to the Syrians and lined up his army against them.
David lined up for battle against the Syrians, and they fought against him.
[18] But the Syrians ran away from Israel.
David killed 7,000 Syrian chariot drivers and 40,000 foot soldiers.
He also killed Shophach, the commander of the army.
[19] The officials of Hadadezer saw that Israel had defeated them.
So they made peace with David and served him.
After that the Syrians did not want to help the Ammonites anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The River” is the Euphrates. The Syrians get help from there.
> In the end they make peace with David.
> 2 Samuel 10:18 says: 700 charioteers and 40,000 horsemen. Such differences in the numbers probably came about when the text was copied over the centuries. There the army commander is called “Shobach.”
