# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 19
### War with the Ammonites

---

## An insult (Verses 1–5)

[1] After this, Nahash the king of the children of Ammon died,
and his son reigned in his place.
[2] David said,
“I will show kindness to Hanun the son of Nahash,
because his father showed kindness to me.”
So David sent messengers to comfort him about his father.
David’s servants came into the land of the children of Ammon to Hanun to comfort him.
[3] But the princes of the children of Ammon said to Hanun,
“Do you think that David honors your father because he has sent comforters to you?
Haven’t his servants come to you
to search, to overthrow, and to spy out the land?”
[4] So Hanun took David’s servants,
shaved them,
and cut off their clothes in the middle at their buttocks,
and sent them away.
[5] Then some people went and told David how the men had been treated.
He sent people to meet them,
for the men were greatly humiliated.
The king said,
“Stay at Jericho until your beards have grown,
and then return.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David wants to be kind and honor an old friendship. But the advisers of the new king are suspicious and turn the kindness into a suspicion.
> The messengers are badly humiliated. Shaving off the beard and cutting the clothes off up to the buttocks was a great disgrace back then.
> David shows compassion for his men. He lets them wait until they can show themselves again. He protects their dignity.
> The story is also in 2 Samuel 10.

---

## Ammon looks for allies (Verses 6–9)

[6] When the children of Ammon saw that they had made themselves hated by David,
Hanun and the children of Ammon sent one thousand talents of silver
to hire chariots and horsemen out of Mesopotamia, out of Aram-maacah, and out of Zobah.
[7] So they hired for themselves 32,000 chariots,
and the king of Maacah with his people.
They came and camped near Medeba.
The children of Ammon gathered together from their cities and came to battle.
[8] When David heard of it,
he sent Joab with all the army of the mighty men.
[9] The children of Ammon came out and set up their battle lines at the gate of the city.
And the kings who had come were by themselves in the field.

> **What does this mean?**
> Instead of saying sorry, the Ammonites prepare for war. They pay foreign armies.
> The Bible says “one thousand talents of silver”. A talent is about 75 pounds.
> The number of 32,000 chariots is very high. 2 Samuel 10:6 has 33,000 men instead.
> Medeba lay east of the Dead Sea. Mesopotamia here literally means “Aram of the two rivers”, that is, the area along the upper Euphrates.

## Joab and Abishai (Verses 10–15)

[10] Now when Joab saw that the battle was set against him in front and behind,
he chose some of all the best men of Israel
and set them up against the Syrians.
[11] The rest of the people he put into the hand of Abishai his brother,
and they set themselves up against the children of Ammon.
[12] He said,
“If the Syrians are too strong for me, then you are to help me.
But if the children of Ammon are too strong for you, then I will help you.
[13] Be courageous,
and let’s be strong for our people and for the cities of our God.
May the LORD do what seems good to him.”
[14] So Joab and the people who were with him came near to the Syrians for the battle.
And they fled before him.
[15] When the children of Ammon saw that the Syrians had fled,
they also fled before Abishai his brother and went into the city.
Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joab is surrounded: in front the Ammonites, behind the Syrians. He divides the army and agrees with his brother: we will help each other.
> His words are an example of courage and trust in God: we do what we can. And then: “May the LORD do what seems good to him.” You should give your best and leave the result to God.
> In Hebrew the “Syrians” are called “Arameans”.

---

## The victory over the Syrians (Verses 16–19)

[16] When the Syrians saw that they had been defeated by Israel,
they sent messengers
and called out the Syrians who were beyond the River.
Shophach the captain of the army of Hadadezer led them.
[17] David was told this.
So he gathered all Israel together, crossed the Jordan, came to them,
and set up his battle lines against them.
When David had set up his battle lines against the Syrians, they fought with him.
[18] The Syrians fled before Israel.
David killed 7,000 charioteers and 40,000 foot soldiers of the Syrians.
He also killed Shophach the captain of the army.
[19] When the servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel,
they made peace with David and served him.
And the Syrians would not help the children of Ammon any more.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The River” is the Euphrates. The Syrians get help from there.
> In the end they make peace with David.
> 2 Samuel 10:18 says: 700 charioteers and 40,000 horsemen. Such differences in the numbers probably came about when the text was copied over the centuries. There the army commander is called “Shobach”.
