# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 28
### David’s last speech

---

## David speaks to all Israel (Verses 1–8)

[1] David called all the leaders of Israel together in Jerusalem.
These were the leaders of the tribes
and the commanders of the divisions that served the king.
There were the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds.
There were the men in charge of all the property and animals of the king and of his sons.
There were the officials and the mighty men, all the brave warriors.
[2] Then King David stood up on his feet.
He said, “Listen to me, my brothers and my people!
I wanted with all my heart to build a house of rest
for the ark of the LORD’s covenant.
It was to be the footstool of our God.
I had already made things ready for the building.
[3] But God said to me,
‘You must not build a house for my name.
You are a man of war, and you have shed blood.’
[4] But the LORD, the God of Israel, chose me out of my father’s whole family.
He chose me to be king over Israel forever.
He chose Judah to be the leader.
In the tribe of Judah, he chose my father’s family.
And among my father’s sons, he was pleased with me.
He made me king over all Israel.
[5] The LORD has given me many sons.
Out of all my sons, he has chosen my son Solomon.
Solomon will sit on the throne of the LORD’s kingdom over Israel.
[6] The LORD said to me,
‘Your son Solomon will build my house and my courtyards.
I have chosen him to be my son,
and I will be his father.
[7] I will make his kingdom firm forever,
if he keeps on doing my commandments and my laws, as he does today.’
[8] So now, all Israel is watching. You are the LORD’s assembly.
And our God is listening.
Keep all the commandments of the LORD your God, and search them out.
Then you will own this good land.
And you can pass it on to your children after you forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is old and weak. But now he stands up on his feet and gives a great farewell speech before the whole people.
> “The footstool of our God”: the ark was like the footstool for God’s invisible throne.
> David says honestly why he was not allowed to build the temple: because of all the blood.
> A special phrase: Solomon sits on “the throne of the LORD’s kingdom”. For Chronicles the real king of Israel is God himself. The human king sits on his throne only in his place.
> The promise has a condition: “if he keeps on doing my commandments”. God’s faithfulness invites faithfulness.

---

## David speaks to Solomon (Verses 9–10)

[9] “And you, my son Solomon,
know the God of your father.
Serve him with your whole heart and with a willing mind.
The LORD searches all hearts.
He understands every plan and every thought.
If you seek him, he will let you find him.
But if you turn away from him, he will reject you forever.
[10] Be careful now.
The LORD has chosen you
to build a house as his holy place.
Be strong, and do it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is David’s personal legacy to his son: “Know the God of your father.” Not only know about him, but know him personally.
> “With your whole heart and with a willing mind”: God does not want forced service, but an open, ready heart.
> “The LORD searches all hearts”: you cannot hide anything from him. That is serious, but also comforting: God understands us completely.
> “If you seek him, he will let you find him”: a wonderful promise. God does not hide from those who honestly seek him (compare Jeremiah 29:13–14).

## The plan of the temple (Verses 11–19)

[11] Then David gave his son Solomon the plans for the temple.
There were plans for the porch, for its buildings,
for its storerooms for treasure, for its upper rooms,
for its inner rooms,
and for the room of the mercy seat.
[12] He gave him the plans of everything the Spirit had put in his mind.
There were plans for the courtyards of the LORD’s house
and for all the rooms around them.
There were plans for the treasure rooms of God’s house
and for the treasure rooms of the things given to God.
[13] He also gave him the plans for the divisions of the priests and the Levites,
for all the work of serving in the LORD’s house,
and for all the things used for the service in the LORD’s house.
[14] He told him how much gold to use for all the gold things for every kind of service.
And he told him how much silver to use for all the silver things for every kind of service.
[15] He told him the weight of gold for the gold lampstands and their gold lamps.
He gave the weight for each lampstand and its lamps.
He told him the weight of silver for the silver lampstands and their lamps,
for each lampstand, depending on how it was used.
[16] He told him the weight of gold for the tables for the holy bread, for each table.
And he told him the weight of silver for the silver tables.
[17] The forks, the basins, and the cups were to be made of pure gold.
He gave the weight of gold for each gold bowl
and the weight of silver for each silver bowl.
[18] He gave the weight of refined gold for the altar of incense.
And he gave the gold for the plan of the chariot.
That means: the cherubim that spread out their wings and cover the ark of the LORD’s covenant.
[19] David said, “The LORD wrote all this down for me with his own hand.
He made me understand all the work of this plan.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David gives Solomon an exact building plan. He says: the Spirit put this in his mind, and the LORD wrote it down “with his own hand”. Just as Moses received the plan for the tabernacle from God (Exodus 25:9), David receives the plan for the temple.
> Everything is planned exactly, down to the weight of every bowl. The best should be given to God, with care.
> The “mercy seat” was the lid of the ark of the covenant. On the Day of Atonement blood was sprinkled there to make atonement for the guilt of the people (Leviticus 16:14–15).
> “The chariot” is a picture for the cherubim above the ark, on which God is invisibly enthroned, as on a royal chariot. Later the prophet Ezekiel saw a vision of God’s throne chariot (Ezekiel 1).

## Be strong and courageous (Verses 20–21)

[20] David said to his son Solomon,
“Be strong and courageous, and do it!
Don’t be afraid, and don’t lose courage.
The LORD God, my God, is with you.
He will not fail you or leave you
until all the work for the service of the LORD’s house is finished.
[21] Look, here are the divisions of the priests and the Levites for all the service of God’s house.
Every willing man who has skill for any kind of service will be with you in all the work.
And the commanders and all the people will do whatever you tell them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David encourages his son: you are not alone. God is with you. And many people help you.
> “He will not fail you or leave you”: God also gave this promise to Joshua (Joshua 1:5). It is taken up again in the New Testament (Hebrews 13:5). It is for everyone who has a great task ahead.
