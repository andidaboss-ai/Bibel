# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 28
### David’s last speech

---

## David speaks to all Israel (Verses 1–8)

[1] David gathered all the princes of Israel at Jerusalem:
the princes of the tribes,
the captains of the divisions who served the king,
the captains of thousands and the captains of hundreds,
the rulers over all the goods and possessions of the king and of his sons,
with the officers and the mighty men, all the brave warriors.
[2] Then King David stood up on his feet and said,
“Hear me, my brothers and my people!
As for me, it was in my heart to build a house of rest
for the ark of the LORD’s covenant
and for the footstool of our God.
And I had prepared for the building.
[3] But God said to me,
‘You shall not build a house for my name,
because you are a man of war and have shed blood.’
[4] But the LORD, the God of Israel, chose me out of all the house of my father
to be king over Israel forever.
For he has chosen Judah to be prince,
and in the house of Judah, the house of my father,
and among the sons of my father he was pleased with me
and made me king over all Israel.
[5] And of all my sons
(for the LORD has given me many sons),
he has chosen Solomon my son
to sit on the throne of the LORD’s kingdom over Israel.
[6] He said to me,
‘Solomon your son shall build my house and my courts.
For I have chosen him to be my son,
and I will be his father.
[7] I will make his kingdom firm forever,
if he continues to do my commandments and my rules, as he does today.’
[8] Now therefore, in the sight of all Israel, the LORD’s assembly,
and in the hearing of our God:
keep and seek out all the commandments of the LORD your God,
so that you may possess this good land
and leave it as an inheritance to your children after you forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is old and weak. But now he stands up on his feet and gives a great farewell speech before the whole people.
> “The footstool of our God”: the ark was like the footstool for God’s invisible throne.
> David says honestly why he was not allowed to build the temple: because of all the blood.
> A special phrase: Solomon sits on “the throne of the LORD’s kingdom”. For Chronicles the real king of Israel is God himself. The human king sits on his throne only in his place.
> The promise has a condition: “if he continues to do my commandments”. God’s faithfulness invites faithfulness.

---

## David speaks to Solomon (Verses 9–10)

[9] “And you, Solomon my son,
know the God of your father,
and serve him with a whole heart and with a willing mind.
For the LORD searches all hearts
and understands all the plans of the thoughts.
If you seek him, he will be found by you.
But if you abandon him, he will cast you off forever.
[10] Take care now,
for the LORD has chosen you
to build a house for the sanctuary.
Be strong, and do it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is David’s personal legacy to his son: “Know the God of your father.” Not only know about him, but know him personally.
> “With a whole heart and with a willing mind”: God does not want forced service, but an open, ready heart.
> “The LORD searches all hearts”: you cannot hide anything from him. That is serious, but also comforting: God understands us completely.
> “If you seek him, he will be found by you”: a wonderful promise. God does not hide from those who honestly seek him (compare Jeremiah 29:13–14).

## The plan of the temple (Verses 11–19)

[11] Then David gave Solomon his son the plans
for the porch of the temple, for its houses,
for its treasuries, for its upper rooms,
for its inner rooms,
and for the place of the mercy seat;
[12] and the plans of all that he had in mind by the Spirit:
for the courts of the LORD’s house,
for all the rooms around it,
for the treasuries of God’s house,
and for the treasuries of the dedicated things;
[13] also for the divisions of the priests and the Levites,
for all the work of the service of the LORD’s house,
and for all the vessels of service in the LORD’s house;
[14] gold by weight for all the gold vessels for every kind of service,
and silver by weight for all the silver vessels for every kind of service;
[15] also the weight for the gold lampstands and their gold lamps,
by weight for every lampstand and its lamps;
and for the silver lampstands, by weight for every lampstand and its lamps,
according to the use of every lampstand;
[16] and the gold by weight for the tables of show bread, for every table,
and silver for the silver tables;
[17] and the forks, the basins, and the cups, of pure gold;
and for the gold bowls by weight for every bowl,
and for the silver bowls by weight for every bowl;
[18] and for the altar of incense, refined gold by weight;
and gold for the plan of the chariot,
the cherubim that spread out their wings and cover the ark of the LORD’s covenant.
[19] “All this,” David said,
“I have been made to understand in writing from the LORD’s hand,
all the works of this pattern.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David gives Solomon an exact building plan. He says: I received this “by the Spirit” and “from the LORD’s hand”. Just as Moses received the plan for the tabernacle from God (Exodus 25:9), David receives the plan for the temple.
> Everything is planned exactly, down to the weight of every bowl. The best should be given to God, with care.
> The “mercy seat” was the lid of the ark of the covenant. On the Day of Atonement blood was sprinkled there to make atonement for the guilt of the people (Leviticus 16:14–15).
> “The chariot” is a picture for the cherubim above the ark, on which God is invisibly enthroned, as on a royal chariot. Later the prophet Ezekiel saw a vision of God’s throne chariot (Ezekiel 1).

## Be strong and courageous (Verses 20–21)

[20] David said to Solomon his son,
“Be strong and courageous, and do it!
Don’t be afraid, and don’t lose heart.
For the LORD God, my God, is with you.
He will not fail you nor forsake you,
until all the work for the service of the LORD’s house is finished.
[21] Look, there are the divisions of the priests and the Levites for all the service of God’s house.
And every willing man who has skill for any kind of service will be with you in all kinds of work.
Also the captains and all the people will be completely at your command.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David encourages his son: you are not alone. God is with you. And many people help you.
> “He will not fail you nor forsake you”: God also gave this promise to Joshua (Joshua 1:5). It is taken up again in the New Testament (Hebrews 13:5). It is for everyone who has a great task ahead.
