# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 3
### David’s family

---

## David’s sons from Hebron (Verses 1–4a)

[1] These were the sons of David who were born in Hebron:
The firstborn was Amnon.
His mother was Ahinoam, the woman from Jezreel.
The second was Daniel.
His mother was Abigail, the woman from Carmel.
[2] The third was Absalom.
His mother was Maacah, the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur.
The fourth was Adonijah.
His mother was Haggith.
[3] The fifth was Shephatiah.
His mother was Abital.
The sixth was Ithream.
His mother was Eglah, David’s wife.
[4] These six sons were born to David in Hebron.

> **What does this mean?**
> David first reigned in Hebron, over the tribe of Judah (2 Samuel 2:1–4). These six sons were born there. The list is also in 2 Samuel 3:2–5.
> In 2 Samuel 3:3 Daniel is called “Chileab”. He is not the prophet Daniel.
> Many of these sons have a sad story: Amnon did violence to his half sister Tamar and was killed by Absalom. Absalom rose up against his father and died. Adonijah wanted to become king and was killed.

---

## David’s sons from Jerusalem (Verses 4b–9)

David ruled there for seven years and six months.
Then he ruled for 33 years in Jerusalem.
[5] These sons were born to him in Jerusalem:
Shimea, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon.
These four were the sons of Bathshua, the daughter of Ammiel.
[6] Then there were Ibhar, Elishama, Eliphelet,
[7] Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia,
[8] Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet.
These were nine sons.
[9] All these were the sons of David.
David also had sons by his concubines.
Concubines are wives of lower rank.
Tamar was the sister of these sons.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Bathshua” is another form of the name Bathsheba. Her father Ammiel is called “Eliam” in 2 Samuel 11:3.
> Here Solomon is named as Bathsheba’s fourth son, although he was probably the oldest one who stayed alive. Perhaps the order is not by age.
> Nathan, another son of David, is important in the family tree of Jesus in Luke (Luke 3:31).
> Two names appear twice: Elishama and Eliphelet. Perhaps the first sons with this name died young, and later sons got the same name. Instead of the first Elishama, 2 Samuel 5:15 has “Elishua”.
> Tamar is the only daughter of David who is named. Her terrible story is in 2 Samuel 13.

## The kings of Judah (Verses 10–16)

[10] Solomon’s son was Rehoboam.
Rehoboam’s son was Abijah.
His son was Asa.
His son was Jehoshaphat.
[11] His son was Joram.
His son was Ahaziah.
His son was Joash.
[12] His son was Amaziah.
His son was Azariah.
His son was Jotham.
[13] His son was Ahaz.
His son was Hezekiah.
His son was Manasseh.
[14] His son was Amon.
His son was Josiah.
[15] The sons of Josiah were:
Johanan, the firstborn,
Jehoiakim, the second,
Zedekiah, the third,
and Shallum, the fourth.
[16] The sons of Jehoiakim were
his son Jeconiah
and his son Zedekiah.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the list of all the kings of Judah from David’s family, over almost 400 years. We know them from the books of Kings. Queen Athaliah is missing, because she did not belong to David’s family.
> In the books of Kings Abijah is called “Abijam”. Azariah is also called “Uzziah”.
> Shallum is probably the same as King Jehoahaz (2 Kings 23:31; Jeremiah 22:11). We know nothing else about Johanan.
> Jeconiah is another name for King Jehoiachin.
> The Zedekiah in verse 16 is probably a different one from King Zedekiah in verse 15. Or it is the same one after all, and “son” here means “successor”.

## David’s family after the exile (Verses 17–24)

[17] Jeconiah was taken away as a prisoner.
His sons were
his son Shealtiel,
[18] Malchiram, Pedaiah, Shenazzar, Jekamiah, Hoshama, and Nedabiah.
[19] The sons of Pedaiah were
Zerubbabel and Shimei.
The sons of Zerubbabel were
Meshullam and Hananiah.
Their sister was Shelomith.
[20] There were also five more:
Hashubah, Ohel, Berechiah, Hasadiah, and Jushab Hesed.
[21] The sons of Hananiah were
Pelatiah and Jeshaiah.
Then came the sons of Rephaiah,
the sons of Arnan,
the sons of Obadiah,
and the sons of Shecaniah.
[22] The son of Shecaniah was
Shemaiah.
The sons of Shemaiah were
Hattush, Igal, Bariah, Neariah, and Shaphat, six in all.
[23] The sons of Neariah were
Elioenai, Hizkiah, and Azrikam, three in all.
[24] The sons of Elioenai were
Hodaviah, Eliashib, Pelaiah, Akkub, Johanan, Delaiah, and Anani, seven in all.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an important message: David’s family did not perish in the exile! Jeconiah (Jehoiachin) was a prisoner in Babylon (2 Kings 25:27). But his descendants lived on.
> After the exile Zerubbabel led the first group of Jews back to Jerusalem and began to rebuild the temple (Ezra 3:2; Haggai 1:1). In other places he is called “son of Shealtiel”. Perhaps Shealtiel was his uncle or adoptive father, or there was a levirate marriage. That means: a man married the widow of his brother who had died without children.
> The list goes on for several generations after Zerubbabel. From this you can see that Chronicles was written only long after the return.
> Verse 22 says “six”, but only five names are given. Perhaps Shemaiah is counted too, or a name was lost when the text was copied.
> In Hebrew verse 21 is hard to understand. In the Greek translation the names form one continuous line of father and son.
> Matthew names Jeconiah, Shealtiel and Zerubbabel in the family tree of Jesus (Matthew 1:12). In this way Christians connect the hope for the Messiah from David’s family with Jesus. In Judaism the Messiah from the house of David is still expected.
