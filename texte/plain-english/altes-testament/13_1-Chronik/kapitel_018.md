# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 18
### David’s victories

---

## Victories on every side (Verses 1–8)

[1] After this, David defeated the Philistines and subdued them.
He took Gath and its towns out of the hand of the Philistines.
[2] He defeated Moab,
and the Moabites became servants to David and brought tribute.
[3] David defeated Hadadezer king of Zobah, toward Hamath,
as he went to make his rule firm by the river Euphrates.
[4] David took from him 1,000 chariots, 7,000 horsemen, and 20,000 foot soldiers.
David hamstrung all the chariot horses,
but kept enough of them for one hundred chariots.
[5] When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah,
David struck 22,000 men of the Syrians.
[6] Then David put garrisons in Syria of Damascus.
And the Syrians became servants to David and brought tribute.
The LORD gave victory to David wherever he went.
[7] David took the shields of gold that the servants of Hadadezer carried
and brought them to Jerusalem.
[8] From Tibhath and from Cun, cities of Hadadezer, David took very much bronze.
With it Solomon made the bronze sea, the pillars, and the vessels of bronze.

> **What does this mean?**
> In a short time David defeats all the enemies around: in the west the Philistines, in the east Moab, in the north the Arameans (Syrians). Israel becomes a small empire. The list is also in 2 Samuel 8.
> “Hamstrung the horses”: he cut their tendons so that they could no longer be used in war. Israel was not to trust in horses and chariots (Deuteronomy 17:16; Psalm 20:7).
> Chronicles stresses: the bronze from these victories was later used for the temple. David is already collecting material for the temple that Solomon will build.
> In Hebrew “Syria” is called “Aram”. Zobah was an Aramean kingdom north of Damascus.
> 2 Samuel 8:4 has 1,700 horsemen instead of 1,000 chariots and 7,000 horsemen. Such differences in numbers probably came about when the text was copied.
> In 2 Samuel 8:8 the cities are called “Betah” and “Berothai”.

## A gift from Hamath (Verses 9–13)

[9] When Tou king of Hamath heard
that David had struck all the army of Hadadezer king of Zobah,
[10] he sent Hadoram his son to King David
to greet him and to bless him,
because he had fought against Hadadezer and struck him.
For Hadadezer had been at war with Tou.
And Hadoram brought all kinds of vessels of gold and silver and bronze.
[11] King David also dedicated these to the LORD,
with the silver and the gold that he had carried away from all the nations:
from Edom, from Moab, from the children of Ammon, from the Philistines, and from Amalek.
[12] Abishai the son of Zeruiah struck 18,000 of the Edomites in the Valley of Salt.
[13] He put garrisons in Edom,
and all the Edomites became servants to David.
The LORD gave victory to David wherever he went.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king of Hamath is glad that his enemy has been defeated. He sends gifts.
> David does not keep the treasures for himself. He dedicates them to God, for the later temple.
> The sentence appears twice: “The LORD gave victory to David wherever he went.” Chronicles wants to say: David’s success is God’s gift.
> In 2 Samuel 8:13 the victory in the Valley of Salt is credited to David himself, in Psalm 60 to Joab. Here it is Abishai. Probably several army commanders took part.
> The numbers of the dead are high. War always means great suffering, even when the Bible tells it here from the point of view of the winners.

## David’s government (Verses 14–17)

[14] David reigned over all Israel,
and he did justice and righteousness for all his people.
[15] Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the army.
Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud was recorder.
[16] Zadok the son of Ahitub and Abimelech the son of Abiathar were priests.
Shavsha was scribe.
[17] Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites.
And the sons of David were chief officials serving the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 says what makes a good king: he does “justice and righteousness for all his people”. Not power and victories are most important, but justice for everyone.
> The list shows David’s most important officials. The “Cherethites and the Pelethites” were David’s bodyguard, probably foreign soldiers who were paid to fight.
> In the Hebrew text of 2 Samuel 8:18 it says that David’s sons were priests. Chronicles writes: they were “chief officials serving the king” (1 Chronicles 18:17). For Chronicles, only descendants of Aaron could be priests.
> In 2 Samuel 8:17 the names are “Ahimelech” instead of “Abimelech” and “Seraiah” instead of “Shavsha”.
