# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 14
### David becomes strong

---

## David’s house and family (Verses 1–7)

[1] Hiram, the king of Tyre, sent messengers to David.
He sent cedar wood, stoneworkers, and carpenters.
They were to build a house for David.
[2] David understood that the LORD had made him king over Israel and made his rule firm.
His kingdom was raised up very high,
for the sake of his people Israel.
[3] In Jerusalem David married more wives.
He became the father of more sons and daughters.
[4] These are the names of the children he had in Jerusalem:
Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,
[5] Ibhar, Elishua, Elpelet,
[6] Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia,
[7] Elishama, Beeliada, and Eliphelet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hiram was king of the Phoenicians in Tyre, in today’s Lebanon. He was a friend of David and later also of Solomon.
> An important sentence: David understands that God has made him great, and that it is “for the sake of his people Israel.” The king’s power is not there for himself, but for the people.
> David took more wives. That was usual for kings back then, but the law warned against it (Deuteronomy 17:17).
> The names of the sons are also in 1 Chronicles 3:5–8 and 2 Samuel 5:14–16, with small differences. “Beeliada” is called “Eliada” there. The “Baal” (lord) was often avoided later.

---

## Two victories over the Philistines (Verses 8–17)

[8] The Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over all Israel.
So all the Philistines went up to search for David.
David heard about it and went out to fight them.
[9] The Philistines had come and attacked the Valley of Rephaim.
[10] David asked God,
“Should I go up and fight the Philistines?
Will you give them into my power?”
The LORD answered him,
“Go up.
I will give them into your power.”
[11] So they went up to Baal Perazim.
There David defeated the Philistines.
David said,
“God has broken through my enemies by my hand,
like water that breaks through.”
That is why the place is called Baal Perazim.
[12] The Philistines left their gods there.
David gave an order, and the gods were burned.
[13] The Philistines attacked the valley again.
[14] So David asked God again.
God answered him,
“Do not go straight up after them.
Go around them.
Attack them in front of the mulberry trees.
[15] You will hear the sound of marching in the tops of the mulberry trees.
Then go out to battle.
For God has gone out ahead of you
to strike the army of the Philistines.”
[16] David did what God commanded him.
They struck down the army of the Philistines from Gibeon all the way to Gezer.
[17] David became famous in all lands.
And the LORD made all nations afraid of him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unlike Saul, David asks God before every battle, and each time anew. The second time he gets a completely different instruction. God’s way is not always the same. That is why it is worth asking again and again.
> “Baal Perazim” means “lord of breaking through.” God breaks through the enemies like a flood through a dam.
> The Philistines had taken the images of their gods with them into battle. David has them burned, as the law demands (Deuteronomy 7:25). 2 Samuel 5:21 only says that David and his men took them away.
> The sound in the treetops is a sign: God himself goes ahead of the army.
> “Mulberry trees”: it is not certain which trees exactly are meant. Some translate “baca trees” or “balsam trees”.
