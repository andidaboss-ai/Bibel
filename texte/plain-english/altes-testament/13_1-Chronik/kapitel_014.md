# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 14
### David becomes strong

---

## David’s house and family (Verses 1–7)

[1] Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David,
with cedar trees, masons, and carpenters,
to build him a house.
[2] David understood that the LORD had made him firmly king over Israel.
For his kingdom was lifted up very high,
for the sake of his people Israel.
[3] David took more wives in Jerusalem,
and David became the father of more sons and daughters.
[4] These are the names of the children whom he had in Jerusalem:
Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,
[5] Ibhar, Elishua, Elpelet,
[6] Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia,
[7] Elishama, Beeliada, and Eliphelet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hiram was king of the Phoenicians in Tyre, in today’s Lebanon. He was a friend of David and later also of Solomon.
> An important sentence: David understands that God has made him great, and that “for the sake of his people Israel”. The king’s power is not there for himself, but for the people.
> David took more wives. That was usual for kings back then, but the law warned against it (Deuteronomy 17:17).
> The names of the sons are also in 1 Chronicles 3:5–8 and 2 Samuel 5:14–16, with small differences. “Beeliada” is called “Eliada” there. The “Baal” (lord) was often avoided later.

---

## Two victories over the Philistines (Verses 8–17)

[8] When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over all Israel,
all the Philistines went up to look for David.
David heard of it and went out against them.
[9] Now the Philistines had come and made a raid in the valley of Rephaim.
[10] David asked God,
“Shall I go up against the Philistines?
Will you hand them over to me?”
The LORD said to him,
“Go up, for I will hand them over to you.”
[11] So they came up to Baal Perazim,
and David defeated them there.
David said,
“God has broken through my enemies by my hand,
like waters breaking out.”
That is why they called that place Baal Perazim.
[12] They left their gods there.
David gave a command,
and they were burned with fire.
[13] The Philistines made another raid in the valley.
[14] David asked God again.
God said to him,
“You shall not go up after them.
Turn away from them,
and come at them opposite the mulberry trees.
[15] When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the mulberry trees,
then go out to battle,
for God has gone out before you
to strike the army of the Philistines.”
[16] David did as God commanded him.
And they struck the army of the Philistines from Gibeon all the way to Gezer.
[17] The fame of David went out into all lands.
And the LORD made all nations afraid of him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unlike Saul, David asks God before every battle, and each time anew. The second time he gets a completely different instruction. God’s way is not always the same. That is why it is worth asking again and again.
> “Baal Perazim” means “lord of breaking through”. God breaks through the enemies like a flood through a dam.
> The Philistines had taken the images of their gods with them into battle. David has them burned, as the law demands (Deuteronomy 7:25). 2 Samuel 5:21 only says that David and his men took them away.
> The sound in the treetops is a sign: God himself goes ahead of the army.
> “Mulberry trees”: it is not certain which trees exactly are meant. Some translate “baca trees” or “balsam trees”.
