# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 27
### David’s army commanders and officials

---

## The twelve divisions of the army (Verses 1–15)

[1] This is the list of the Israelites who served the king.
They were heads of families,
commanders of thousands and of hundreds,
and their officers.
They served the king in all matters of the army divisions.
Each division came on duty for one month and then went off duty.
This went on every month of the year.
Each division had 24,000 men.
[2] Jashobeam, the son of Zabdiel, was in charge of the first division, for the first month.
His division had 24,000 men.
[3] He was a descendant of Perez.
He was the leader of all the army commanders for the first month.
[4] Dodai the Ahohite was in charge of the division for the second month.
Mikloth was the leader of his division.
His division had 24,000 men.
[5] The third army commander, for the third month, was Benaiah.
He was the son of Jehoiada, the chief priest.
His division had 24,000 men.
[6] This is the Benaiah who was a mighty man among the thirty.
He was in charge of the thirty.
His son Ammizabad was in his division.
[7] The fourth commander, for the fourth month, was Asahel, the brother of Joab.
His son Zebadiah came after him.
His division had 24,000 men.
[8] The fifth commander, for the fifth month, was Shamhuth the Izrahite.
His division had 24,000 men.
[9] The sixth commander, for the sixth month, was Ira, the son of Ikkesh, the Tekoite.
His division had 24,000 men.
[10] The seventh commander, for the seventh month, was Helez the Pelonite.
He was from the tribe of Ephraim.
His division had 24,000 men.
[11] The eighth commander, for the eighth month, was Sibbecai the Hushathite.
He was from the family of Zerah.
His division had 24,000 men.
[12] The ninth commander, for the ninth month, was Abiezer the Anathothite.
He was from the tribe of Benjamin.
His division had 24,000 men.
[13] The tenth commander, for the tenth month, was Maharai the Netophathite.
He was from the family of Zerah.
His division had 24,000 men.
[14] The eleventh commander, for the eleventh month, was Benaiah the Pirathonite.
He was from the tribe of Ephraim.
His division had 24,000 men.
[15] The twelfth commander, for the twelfth month, was Heldai the Netophathite.
He was from the family of Othniel.
His division had 24,000 men.

> **What does this mean?**
> David had no large standing army. Instead, every month one division served with the king, like a kind of military service in turns. So the men could work at home the rest of the time.
> The leaders are old friends: they are David’s mighty men from chapter 11.
> Asahel had fallen early in the war against Abner (2 Samuel 2:23). That is why it says here: “His son Zebadiah came after him” (1 Chronicles 27:7). The division probably kept his name.
> In verse 5 Jehoiada is called “chief priest.” The Hebrew text can also be read differently: “Benaiah, the son of Jehoiada the priest, as chief.”

## The leaders of the tribes (Verses 16–24)

[16] These were the leaders of the tribes of Israel:
Eliezer, the son of Zichri, was the leader of the Reubenites.
Shephatiah, the son of Maacah, led the Simeonites.
[17] Hashabiah, the son of Kemuel, led Levi.
Zadok led the descendants of Aaron.
[18] Elihu, one of David’s brothers, led Judah.
Omri, the son of Michael, led Issachar.
[19] Ishmaiah, the son of Obadiah, led Zebulun.
Jeremoth, the son of Azriel, led Naphtali.
[20] Hoshea, the son of Azaziah, led Ephraim.
Joel, the son of Pedaiah, led the half-tribe of Manasseh.
[21] Iddo, the son of Zechariah, led the half-tribe of Manasseh in Gilead.
Jaasiel, the son of Abner, led Benjamin.
[22] Azarel, the son of Jeroham, led Dan.
These were the leaders of the tribes of Israel.
[23] David did not count the men who were 20 years old or younger.
The LORD had promised to make Israel as many as the stars in the sky.
[24] Joab, the son of Zeruiah, began to count the people,
but he did not finish.
God’s anger came on Israel because of this counting.
So the number was not written in the official records of King David.

> **What does this mean?**
> Each tribe had a leader. Gad and Asher are missing in this list, but Levi and Aaron are named separately. So the number again comes to twelve, if you count the half-tribes.
> Elihu is probably Eliab, David’s oldest brother (1 Samuel 16:6).
> Jaasiel was a son of Abner, Saul’s former army commander. Saul’s people also got responsibility.
> Verses 23–24 remind us of the census (Chapter 21). God had promised Abraham to make his people as many as the stars (Genesis 15:5). People should not count again what God has promised.

## The managers of the king’s property (Verses 25–31)

[25] Azmaveth, the son of Adiel, was in charge of the king’s treasures.
Jonathan, the son of Uzziah, was in charge of the storehouses
in the fields, the cities, the villages, and the towers.
[26] Ezri, the son of Chelub, was in charge of the field workers
who farmed the land.
[27] Shimei the Ramathite was in charge of the vineyards.
Zabdi the Shiphmite was in charge of the grapes from the vineyards
and the wine cellars.
[28] Baal Hanan the Gederite was in charge of the olive trees and the sycamore fig trees
in the lowland.
Joash was in charge of the storerooms for olive oil.
[29] Shitrai the Sharonite was in charge of the cattle that grazed in Sharon.
Shaphat, the son of Adlai, was in charge of the cattle in the valleys.
[30] Obil the Ishmaelite was in charge of the camels.
Jehdeiah the Meronothite was in charge of the donkeys.
Jaziz the Hagrite was in charge of the sheep and goats.
[31] All these were the officials in charge of King David’s property.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king had much property of his own: fields, vineyards, olive trees and herds. For each area there was an expert.
> Remarkable: an Ishmaelite took care of the camels, a Hagrite of the flocks. The Arabs knew especially well how to handle camels and herds. David used people from other nations where they were good.
> In verse 30 the Hebrew says “female donkeys.”

---

## David’s advisers (Verses 32–34)

[32] Jonathan, David’s uncle, was an adviser.
He was a wise man and a writer.
Jehiel, the son of Hachmoni, took care of the king’s sons.
[33] Ahithophel was the king’s adviser.
Hushai the Archite was the king’s friend.
[34] After Ahithophel came Jehoiada, the son of Benaiah, and Abiathar.
Joab was the commander of the king’s army.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehiel was probably the teacher of the king’s sons.
> “The king’s friend” was a title of honor for a close and trusted adviser.
> Later Ahithophel betrayed David and joined Absalom. Hushai stayed faithful to David and defeated Ahithophel’s advice (2 Samuel 15–17). Chronicles mentions this only very briefly with the words “after Ahithophel.”
