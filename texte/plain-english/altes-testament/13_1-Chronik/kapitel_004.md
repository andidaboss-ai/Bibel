# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 4
### More families from Judah and the tribe of Simeon

---

## Families from Judah (Verses 1–8)

[1] The sons of Judah were
Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur, and Shobal.
[2] Reaiah, the son of Shobal, was the father of Jahath.
Jahath was the father of Ahumai and Lahad.
These are the families of the Zorathites.
[3] These were the sons of the father of Etam:
Jezreel, Ishma, and Idbash.
Their sister’s name was Hazzelelponi.
[4] Penuel was the father of Gedor.
Ezer was the father of Hushah.
These are the sons of Hur.
Hur was the firstborn of Ephrathah and the father of Bethlehem.
[5] Ashhur, the father of Tekoa, had two wives, Helah and Naarah.
[6] Naarah gave birth to his sons Ahuzzam, Hepher, Temeni, and Haahashtari.
These were the sons of Naarah.
[7] The sons of Helah were Zereth, Izhar, and Ethnan.
[8] Hakkoz was the father of Anub and Zobebah.
The families of Aharhel, the son of Harum, also came from him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The list in verse 1 is not a list of brothers, but a line over several generations: Perez was Judah’s son, Hezron his grandson, and so on.
> Many names are also place names: Etam, Jezreel, Gedor, Bethlehem, Tekoa. “Father of” here often means “founder of”.
> Here too Chronicles names women: Hazzelelponi, Helah and Naarah.
> In some Bibles “Hakkoz” is called “Koz”. The “ha” is the article in Hebrew.

## The prayer of Jabez (Verses 9–10)

[9] Jabez was more respected than his brothers.
His mother named him Jabez.
She said, “I gave birth to him in pain.”
[10] Jabez prayed to the God of Israel.
He said, “Please bless me richly!
Give me more land!
May your hand be with me.
Keep me safe from harm,
so that I do not cause pain!”
And God gave him what he asked for.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the long lists of names there is suddenly a little story with a prayer.
> In Hebrew the name Jabez sounds like “pain”. A name with a heavy meaning, like a shadow over his life.
> Jabez asks God: Bless me, be with me, and keep me safe from harm, so that I do not cause pain. He does not want his name to decide his fate.
> God hears him. The message: No one is bound to a hard beginning. You can ask God for a new way.
> Some years ago this prayer became very well known through a book. What matters is this: It is not a magic spell for riches, but a person’s request for God’s nearness and protection.

## More families from Judah (Verses 11–23)

[11] Chelub, the brother of Shuhah, was the father of Mehir.
Mehir was the father of Eshton.
[12] Eshton was the father of Beth Rapha, Paseah, and Tehinnah.
Tehinnah was the father of Ir Nahash.
These are the men of Recah.
[13] The sons of Kenaz were
Othniel and Seraiah.
The son of Othniel was
Hathath.
[14] Meonothai was the father of Ophrah.
Seraiah was the father of Joab.
Joab was the father of Ge Harashim.
They got this name because they were craftsmen.
[15] The sons of Caleb, the son of Jephunneh, were
Iru, Elah, and Naam.
The son of Elah was Kenaz.
[16] The sons of Jehallelel were
Ziph, Ziphah, Tiria, and Asarel.
[17] The sons of Ezrah were
Jether, Mered, Epher, and Jalon.
Mered’s wife gave birth to Miriam, Shammai, and Ishbah.
Ishbah was the father of Eshtemoa.
[18] His wife from Judah gave birth to Jered, the father of Gedor,
Heber, the father of Soco,
and Jekuthiel, the father of Zanoah.
These are the sons of Bithiah, the daughter of Pharaoh.
Mered had married her.
[19] Hodiah’s wife was the sister of Naham.
Her sons were the fathers of Keilah the Garmite
and of Eshtemoa the Maacathite.
[20] The sons of Shimon were
Amnon, Rinnah, Ben Hanan, and Tilon.
The sons of Ishi were
Zoheth and Ben Zoheth.
[21] The sons of Shelah, the son of Judah, were
Er, the father of Lecah,
and Laadah, the father of Mareshah.
The families of the linen workers in the house of Ashbea also came from him.
[22] So did Jokim, the men of Cozeba,
Joash, and Saraph, who ruled in Moab,
and Jashubilehem.
These records are very old.
[23] They were the potters.
They lived in Netaim and Gederah.
They lived there with the king and worked for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Othniel was the first judge of Israel (Judges 3:9–11). Caleb the son of Jephunneh was one of the two faithful spies (Numbers 14:6–9).
> “Ge Harashim” means “valley of craftsmen”. Chronicles also honors craftsmen, potters and linen workers. Every kind of work has its worth.
> Remarkable in verse 18: Mered married Bithiah, a daughter of Pharaoh. An Egyptian princess became part of the tribe of Judah. Her name “Bithiah” means “daughter of the LORD”. Jewish tradition connects her with the daughter of Pharaoh who saved Moses from the Nile.
> In Hebrew verses 17–18 are a little mixed up. The Greek translation puts the end of verse 18 before the words “Mered’s wife gave birth to Miriam” in verse 17.
> “These records are very old”: The writer honestly says that he draws from old sources.
> “Er” here is a grandson of Judah, named after Judah’s oldest son Er, who had died.

## The tribe of Simeon (Verses 24–33)

[24] The sons of Simeon were
Nemuel, Jamin, Jarib, Zerah, and Shaul.
[25] Then came his son Shallum,
his son Mibsam,
and his son Mishma.
[26] The descendants of Mishma were
his son Hammuel,
his son Zaccur,
and his son Shimei.
[27] Shimei had 16 sons and six daughters.
But his brothers did not have many children.
Their whole family did not grow as large as the tribe of Judah.
[28] They lived in Beersheba, Moladah, Hazarshual,
[29] Bilhah, Ezem, Tolad,
[30] Bethuel, Hormah, Ziklag,
[31] Beth Marcaboth, Hazar Susim, Beth Biri, and Shaaraim.
These were their cities until David became king.
[32] Their villages were Etam, Ain, Rimmon, Tochen, and Ashan, five towns.
[33] They also had all the villages around these towns, as far as Baal.
These were the places where they lived.
They kept a written record of their families.

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon was a small tribe. It lived in the middle of Judah’s land, in the south (Joshua 19:1–9). Over time it almost completely became part of Judah.
> Jacob had said about Simeon and Levi that they would be scattered in Israel (Genesis 49:7).
> In Genesis 46:10 Nemuel is called “Jemuel” and Jarib “Jachin”.

## Simeon looks for new pasture (Verses 34–43)

[34] Meshobab, Jamlech, Joshah the son of Amaziah,
[35] Joel, Jehu the son of Joshibiah, the son of Seraiah, the son of Asiel,
[36] Elioenai, Jaakobah, Jeshohaiah, Asaiah, Adiel, Jesimiel, Benaiah,
[37] and Ziza the son of Shiphi, the son of Allon, the son of Jedaiah, the son of Shimri, the son of Shemaiah:
[38] these men named here were leaders of their families.
Their families grew very large.
[39] They went to the entrance of Gedor,
to the east side of the valley.
They were looking for pasture for their flocks.
[40] They found rich, good pasture.
The land was wide, quiet, and peaceful.
The people who lived there before were descendants of Ham.
[41] The men whose names are written here came in the time of Hezekiah, king of Judah.
They attacked the tents of the people there and the Meunim they found there.
They destroyed them completely, and they are gone to this day.
Then they lived there in their place,
because there was pasture there for their flocks.
[42] 500 of them, of the descendants of Simeon, went to Mount Seir.
Their leaders were Pelatiah, Neariah, Rephaiah, and Uzziel, the sons of Ishi.
[43] They killed the rest of the Amalekites who had escaped.
They have lived there to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the time of King Hezekiah the families of Simeon need more room for their flocks. They move on into the south and drive out the people who lived there.
> This is a harsh report about driving people out and violence. The Bible tells how it happened back then. For the people who lived there peacefully it was an injustice and great suffering. These verses are no example for taking other people’s land.
> The Meunim were probably a people from the area south of the Dead Sea.
> The Amalekites had been enemies of Israel since the time in the wilderness (Exodus 17:8–16).
