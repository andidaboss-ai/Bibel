# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 4
### More families from Judah and the tribe of Simeon

---

## Families from Judah (Verses 1–8)

[1] The sons of Judah:
Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur, and Shobal.
[2] Reaiah the son of Shobal became the father of Jahath,
and Jahath became the father of Ahumai and Lahad.
These are the families of the Zorathites.
[3] These were the sons of the father of Etam:
Jezreel, Ishma, and Idbash.
The name of their sister was Hazzelelponi.
[4] Penuel was the father of Gedor,
and Ezer was the father of Hushah.
These are the sons of Hur, the firstborn of Ephrathah, the father of Bethlehem.
[5] Ashhur the father of Tekoa had two wives: Helah and Naarah.
[6] Naarah bore him Ahuzzam, Hepher, Temeni, and Haahashtari.
These were the sons of Naarah.
[7] The sons of Helah were Zereth, Izhar, and Ethnan.
[8] Hakkoz became the father of Anub, Zobebah,
and the families of Aharhel the son of Harum.

> **What does this mean?**
> The list in verse 1 is not a list of brothers, but a line over several generations: Perez was Judah’s son, Hezron his grandson, and so on.
> Many names are also place names: Etam, Jezreel, Gedor, Bethlehem, Tekoa. “Father of” here often means “founder of”.
> Here too Chronicles names women: Hazzelelponi, Helah and Naarah.
> In some Bibles “Hakkoz” is called “Koz”. The “ha” is the article in Hebrew.

---

## The prayer of Jabez (Verses 9–10)

[9] Jabez was more honorable than his brothers.
His mother named him Jabez,
for she said,
“Because I bore him with sorrow.”
[10] Jabez called on the God of Israel:
“Oh that you would bless me indeed,
and enlarge my border!
May your hand be with me,
and may you keep me from evil,
so that I may not cause pain!”
And God gave him what he asked for.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the long lists of names there is suddenly a little story with a prayer.
> In Hebrew the name Jabez sounds like “pain”. A name with a heavy meaning, like a shadow over his life.
> Jabez asks God: Bless me, be with me, and keep me from evil, so that I may not cause pain. He does not want his name to decide his fate.
> God hears him. The message: No one is bound to a hard beginning. You can ask God for a new way.
> Some years ago this prayer became very well known through a book. What matters is this: It is not a magic spell for riches, but a person’s request for God’s nearness and protection.

## More families from Judah (Verses 11–23)

[11] Chelub the brother of Shuhah became the father of Mehir.
He was the father of Eshton.
[12] Eshton became the father of Beth Rapha, Paseah, and Tehinnah the father of Ir Nahash.
These are the men of Recah.
[13] The sons of Kenaz:
Othniel and Seraiah.
The sons of Othniel:
Hathath.
[14] Meonothai became the father of Ophrah.
And Seraiah became the father of Joab the father of Ge Harashim,
for they were craftsmen.
[15] The sons of Caleb the son of Jephunneh:
Iru, Elah, and Naam.
The son of Elah: Kenaz.
[16] The sons of Jehallelel:
Ziph, Ziphah, Tiria, and Asarel.
[17] The sons of Ezrah:
Jether, Mered, Epher, and Jalon.
And Mered’s wife bore Miriam, Shammai, and Ishbah the father of Eshtemoa.
[18] His wife the Jewish woman bore Jered the father of Gedor,
Heber the father of Soco,
and Jekuthiel the father of Zanoah.
These are the sons of Bithiah the daughter of Pharaoh, whom Mered took as his wife.
[19] The sons of the wife of Hodiah, the sister of Naham,
were the fathers of Keilah the Garmite
and Eshtemoa the Maacathite.
[20] The sons of Shimon:
Amnon, Rinnah, Ben Hanan, and Tilon.
The sons of Ishi:
Zoheth and Ben Zoheth.
[21] The sons of Shelah the son of Judah:
Er the father of Lecah,
Laadah the father of Mareshah,
and the families of the house of those who worked fine linen, of the house of Ashbea;
[22] and Jokim, and the men of Cozeba,
and Joash, and Saraph, who ruled in Moab,
and Jashubilehem.
These records are ancient.
[23] These were the potters and the people who lived in Netaim and Gederah.
They lived there with the king for his work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Othniel was the first judge of Israel (Judges 3:9–11). Caleb the son of Jephunneh was one of the two faithful spies (Numbers 14:6–9).
> “Ge Harashim” means “valley of craftsmen”. Chronicles also honors craftsmen, potters and linen workers. Every kind of work has its worth.
> Remarkable in verse 18: Mered married Bithiah, a daughter of Pharaoh. An Egyptian princess became part of the tribe of Judah. Her name “Bithiah” means “daughter of the LORD”. Jewish tradition connects her with the daughter of Pharaoh who saved Moses from the Nile.
> In Hebrew verses 17–18 are a little mixed up. The Greek translation puts the end of verse 18 before the words “And Mered’s wife bore Miriam” in verse 17.
> “These records are ancient”: The writer honestly says that he draws from old sources.
> “Er” here is a grandson of Judah, named after Judah’s oldest son Er, who had died.

## The tribe of Simeon (Verses 24–33)

[24] The sons of Simeon:
Nemuel, Jamin, Jarib, Zerah, Shaul;
[25] Shallum his son,
Mibsam his son,
and Mishma his son.
[26] The sons of Mishma:
Hammuel his son,
Zaccur his son,
Shimei his son.
[27] Shimei had sixteen sons and six daughters.
But his brothers didn’t have many children.
All their family didn’t multiply like the children of Judah.
[28] They lived at Beersheba, Moladah, Hazarshual,
[29] at Bilhah, at Ezem, at Tolad,
[30] at Bethuel, at Hormah, at Ziklag,
[31] at Beth Marcaboth, Hazar Susim, at Beth Biri, and at Shaaraim.
These were their cities until David’s reign.
[32] Their villages were Etam, Ain, Rimmon, Tochen, and Ashan, five cities,
[33] and all their villages that were around these cities, as far as Baal.
These were their settlements,
and they kept their family records.

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon was a small tribe. It lived in the middle of Judah’s land, in the south (Joshua 19:1–9). Over time it almost completely became part of Judah.
> Jacob had said about Simeon and Levi that they would be scattered in Israel (Genesis 49:7).
> In Genesis 46:10 Nemuel is called “Jemuel” and Jarib “Jachin”.

## Simeon looks for new pasture (Verses 34–43)

[34] Meshobab, Jamlech, Joshah the son of Amaziah,
[35] Joel, Jehu the son of Joshibiah, the son of Seraiah, the son of Asiel,
[36] Elioenai, Jaakobah, Jeshohaiah, Asaiah, Adiel, Jesimiel, Benaiah,
[37] and Ziza the son of Shiphi, the son of Allon, the son of Jedaiah, the son of Shimri, the son of Shemaiah—
[38] these mentioned by name were princes in their families.
Their fathers’ houses grew very much.
[39] They went to the entrance of Gedor,
even to the east side of the valley,
to look for pasture for their flocks.
[40] They found rich, good pasture.
The land was wide, quiet, and peaceful,
for those who lived there before were descended from Ham.
[41] These, written down by name, came in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah.
They struck their tents and the Meunim who were found there,
and they destroyed them completely to this day.
And they lived in their place,
because there was pasture there for their flocks.
[42] Some of them, of the sons of Simeon,
five hundred men, went to Mount Seir.
Their captains were Pelatiah, Neariah, Rephaiah, and Uzziel, the sons of Ishi.
[43] They struck the remnant of the Amalekites who had escaped,
and they have lived there to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the time of King Hezekiah the families of Simeon need more room for their flocks. They move on into the south and drive out the people who lived there.
> This is a harsh report about driving people out and violence. The Bible tells how it happened back then. For the people who lived there peacefully it was an injustice and great suffering. These verses are no example for taking other people’s land.
> The Meunim were probably a people from the area south of the Dead Sea.
> The Amalekites had been enemies of Israel since the time in the wilderness (Exodus 17:8–16).
