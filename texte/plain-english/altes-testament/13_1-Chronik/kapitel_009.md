# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 9
### The people of Jerusalem after the return

---

## Who lived in the land again? (Verses 1–9)

[1] So all Israel was listed in family records.
These records are written in the book of the kings of Israel.
Judah was taken away to Babylon as prisoners
because of their disobedience.
[2] Later the first people came back to their own land and their own cities.
They were Israelites, priests, Levites, and temple servants.
[3] Some people of Judah, of Benjamin, and of Ephraim and Manasseh
lived in Jerusalem.
These were:
[4] Uthai, the son of Ammihud.
Ammihud was the son of Omri, Omri the son of Imri, and Imri the son of Bani.
They were descendants of Perez, the son of Judah.
[5] From the Shilonites:
Asaiah, the firstborn, and his sons.
[6] From the sons of Zerah:
Jeuel and their relatives.
There were 690 of them.
[7] From the tribe of Benjamin:
Sallu, the son of Meshullam.
Meshullam was the son of Hodaviah, and Hodaviah the son of Hassenuah.
[8] Also Ibneiah, the son of Jeroham.
Also Elah, the son of Uzzi. Uzzi was the son of Michri.
Also Meshullam, the son of Shephatiah.
Shephatiah was the son of Reuel, and Reuel the son of Ibnijah.
[9] With them were their relatives, listed by their generations.
There were 956 of them.
All these men were heads of families in their clans.

> **What does this mean?**
> With verse 1, Chronicles closes the long family trees. Then it makes a big jump: from the exile to the return.
> “Because of their disobedience”: Chronicles honestly says why Judah had to go into exile.
> Then it shows the hope: people come back and live in Jerusalem again. This list is similar to Nehemiah 11.
> Remarkable: not only Judah and Benjamin, but also people from Ephraim and Manasseh, that is, from the old northern kingdom, live in Jerusalem again. For Chronicles, “all Israel” belongs together.
> “Temple servants” (in Hebrew “Nethinim”) were helpers in the temple service who did not belong to the Levites.
> “Shilonites” are probably descendants of Shelah, the son of Judah.

---

## The priests (Verses 10–13)

[10] From the priests:
Jedaiah, Jehoiarib, and Jachin.
[11] Also Azariah, the son of Hilkiah.
Hilkiah was the son of Meshullam, Meshullam the son of Zadok,
Zadok the son of Meraioth, and Meraioth the son of Ahitub.
Azariah was the ruler of God’s house.
[12] Also Adaiah, the son of Jeroham.
Jeroham was the son of Pashhur, and Pashhur the son of Malchijah.
Also Maasai, the son of Adiel.
Adiel was the son of Jahzerah, Jahzerah the son of Meshullam,
Meshullam the son of Meshillemith, and Meshillemith the son of Immer.
[13] With them were their relatives, the heads of their clans.
There were 1,760 of them.
They were very able men.
They did the work of serving in God’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests come back too. Jedaiah and Jehoiarib are names of groups of priests (1 Chronicles 24:7). The Maccabees, a famous Jewish family in the 2nd century before Christ, came from the group of Jehoiarib.
> “The ruler of God’s house”: that was a high office in the temple, perhaps the high priest or his deputy.

---

## The Levites and the gatekeepers (Verses 14–27)

[14] From the Levites:
Shemaiah, the son of Hasshub.
Hasshub was the son of Azrikam, and Azrikam the son of Hashabiah.
They were descendants of Merari.
[15] Also Bakbakkar, Heresh, and Galal.
Also Mattaniah, the son of Mica.
Mica was the son of Zichri, and Zichri the son of Asaph.
[16] Also Obadiah, the son of Shemaiah.
Shemaiah was the son of Galal, and Galal the son of Jeduthun.
Also Berechiah, the son of Asa. Asa was the son of Elkanah.
Berechiah lived in the villages of the Netophathites.
[17] The gatekeepers were:
Shallum, Akkub, Talmon, Ahiman, and their relatives.
Shallum was their chief.
[18] Until then they had kept watch at the King’s Gate on the east side.
They were the gatekeepers for the camp of the Levites.
[19] Shallum was the son of Kore.
Kore was the son of Ebiasaph, and Ebiasaph the son of Korah.
Shallum and his relatives from his family, the Korahites, were in charge of the work.
They guarded the doorways of the tent.
Their ancestors had been in charge of the LORD’s camp.
They had guarded the entrance.
[20] In earlier times Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, was their leader.
And the LORD was with him.
[21] Zechariah, the son of Meshelemiah, was the gatekeeper at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[22] All the men chosen to guard the doorways were 212.
They were listed in the family records of their villages.
David and Samuel the seer had given them this position of trust.
A seer is a prophet.
[23] So they and their descendants were in charge of the gates of the LORD’s house.
That is the house of the tent.
They kept watch there.
[24] The gatekeepers stood on all four sides:
on the east, the west, the north, and the south.
[25] Their relatives lived in their villages.
From time to time they had to come for seven days and help them.
[26] The four chief gatekeepers were Levites.
They had a position of trust.
They were in charge of the rooms and the treasuries in God’s house.
[27] They spent the night around God’s house,
because it was their duty to guard it.
And it was their duty to open it every morning.

> **What does this mean?**
> The gatekeepers were important: they guarded the temple day and night, opened it in the morning and made sure that nothing unclean came in. They also had oversight over the treasures.
> Chronicles traces their service back to very old times: to the time in the wilderness, to Phinehas, to Samuel and David. In this way it shows: the worship after the return stands in a long, faithful tradition.
> They worked in turns: those who lived in the villages came to Jerusalem to serve for seven days at a time.
> Jeduthun was one of David’s great singers (1 Chronicles 16:41).

## More tasks in the temple (Verses 28–34)

[28] Some of them took care of the things used in the service.
They counted them when they brought them in.
And they counted them when they took them out.
[29] Others were in charge of the furniture
and of all the holy objects of the sanctuary.
They also took care of the fine flour, the wine, the oil, the frankincense, and the spices.
[30] Some of the sons of the priests mixed the spices.
[31] Mattithiah was one of the Levites.
He was the firstborn son of Shallum the Korahite.
He had the position of trust over the things that were baked in pans.
[32] Some of their relatives from the Kohathites
were in charge of the showbread.
They prepared it every Sabbath.
[33] These are the singers.
They were heads of Levite families.
They lived in the rooms of the temple.
They did not have to do other work,
because they were busy with their own work day and night.
[34] These were the heads of the Levite families, listed by their generations.
They were leaders.
They lived in Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the temple there were many different tasks: counting and keeping the vessels, taking care of flour, wine, oil and frankincense, mixing spices for the holy anointing oil, baking bread.
> The “showbread” was twelve loaves of bread that were put fresh on a table in the sanctuary every week, one for each tribe of Israel (Leviticus 24:5–9).
> The singers praised God “day and night.” That reminds us of Psalm 134: “Praise the LORD, all you servants of the LORD, who stand in the LORD’s house at night!”
> Every task has its place, whether gatekeeper, baker or singer. Together they all serve God.

## Once more: the family of Saul (Verses 35–44)

[35] Jeiel, the father of Gibeon, lived in Gibeon.
His wife’s name was Maacah.
[36] His firstborn son was Abdon.
Then came Zur, Kish, Baal, Ner, Nadab,
[37] Gedor, Ahio, Zechariah, and Mikloth.
[38] Mikloth became the father of Shimeam.
They also lived in Jerusalem, near their relatives.
[39] Ner became the father of Kish.
Kish became the father of Saul.
Saul became the father of Jonathan, Malchishua, Abinadab, and Eshbaal.
[40] Jonathan’s son was Merib-baal.
Merib-baal became the father of Micah.
[41] The sons of Micah were
Pithon, Melech, Tahrea, and Ahaz.
[42] Ahaz became the father of Jarah.
Jarah became the father of Alemeth, Azmaveth, and Zimri.
Zimri became the father of Moza.
[43] Moza became the father of Binea.
Binea’s son was Rephaiah.
Rephaiah’s son was Eleasah.
Eleasah’s son was Azel.
[44] Azel had six sons. These are their names:
Azrikam, Bocheru, Ishmael, Sheariah, Obadiah, and Hanan.
These were the sons of Azel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The family of Saul is named here a second time (compare 1 Chronicles 8:29–38). This is not a mistake: this list forms the bridge to the next chapter. There the real story begins, with the death of Saul.
> Small differences from Chapter 8: here the son of Micah is called “Tahrea” instead of “Tarea,” the son of Ahaz “Jarah” instead of “Jehoaddah,” and the son of Binea “Rephaiah” instead of “Raphah.” Such differences came about when the text was copied over the centuries.
> With this the family trees end. From Chapter 10 on, Chronicles tells the story of David.
