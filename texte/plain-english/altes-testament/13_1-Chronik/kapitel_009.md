# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 9
### The people of Jerusalem after the return

---

## Who lived in the land again? (Verses 1–9)

[1] So all Israel were listed in family records.
Look, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel.
But Judah was carried away captive to Babylon because of their disobedience.
[2] Now the first people who lived again in their possessions, in their cities,
were Israel, the priests, the Levites, and the temple servants.
[3] In Jerusalem there lived some of the children of Judah, of the children of Benjamin,
and of the children of Ephraim and Manasseh:
[4] Uthai the son of Ammihud, the son of Omri, the son of Imri, the son of Bani,
of the children of Perez the son of Judah.
[5] Of the Shilonites:
Asaiah the firstborn and his sons.
[6] Of the sons of Zerah:
Jeuel and their brothers, 690.
[7] Of the sons of Benjamin:
Sallu the son of Meshullam, the son of Hodaviah, the son of Hassenuah;
[8] and Ibneiah the son of Jeroham,
and Elah the son of Uzzi, the son of Michri,
and Meshullam the son of Shephatiah, the son of Reuel, the son of Ibnijah;
[9] and their brothers, according to their generations, 956.
All these men were heads of fathers’ households by their fathers’ houses.

> **What does this mean?**
> With verse 1 Chronicles closes the long family trees. Then it makes a big jump: from the exile to the return.
> “Because of their disobedience”: Chronicles honestly says why Judah had to go into exile.
> Then it shows the hope: people come back and live in Jerusalem again. This list is similar to Nehemiah 11.
> Remarkable: not only Judah and Benjamin, but also people from Ephraim and Manasseh, that is, from the old northern kingdom, live in Jerusalem again. For Chronicles “all Israel” belongs together.
> “Temple servants” (in Hebrew “Nethinim”) were helpers in the temple service who did not belong to the Levites.
> “Shilonites” are probably descendants of Shelah, the son of Judah.

---

## The priests (Verses 10–13)

[10] Of the priests:
Jedaiah, Jehoiarib, Jachin,
[11] and Azariah the son of Hilkiah, the son of Meshullam, the son of Zadok,
the son of Meraioth, the son of Ahitub,
the ruler of God’s house;
[12] and Adaiah the son of Jeroham, the son of Pashhur, the son of Malchijah;
and Maasai the son of Adiel, the son of Jahzerah,
the son of Meshullam, the son of Meshillemith, the son of Immer;
[13] and their brothers, heads of their fathers’ houses, 1,760,
very able men for the work of the service of God’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests come back too. Jedaiah and Jehoiarib are names of groups of priests (1 Chronicles 24:7). The Maccabees, a famous Jewish family in the 2nd century before Christ, came from the group of Jehoiarib.
> “The ruler of God’s house”: that was a high office in the temple, perhaps the high priest or his deputy.

---

## The Levites and the gatekeepers (Verses 14–27)

[14] Of the Levites:
Shemaiah the son of Hasshub, the son of Azrikam, the son of Hashabiah,
of the sons of Merari;
[15] and Bakbakkar, Heresh, Galal,
and Mattaniah the son of Mica, the son of Zichri, the son of Asaph,
[16] and Obadiah the son of Shemaiah, the son of Galal, the son of Jeduthun;
and Berechiah the son of Asa, the son of Elkanah,
who lived in the villages of the Netophathites.
[17] The gatekeepers:
Shallum, Akkub, Talmon, Ahiman, and their brothers.
Shallum was the chief.
[18] Until then they had served in the king’s gate to the east.
They were the gatekeepers for the camp of the children of Levi.
[19] Shallum was the son of Kore, the son of Ebiasaph, the son of Korah.
He and his brothers of his father’s house, the Korahites,
were over the work of the service,
keepers of the thresholds of the tent.
Their fathers had been over the LORD’s camp,
keepers of the entrance.
[20] Phinehas the son of Eleazar was ruler over them in time past,
and the LORD was with him.
[21] Zechariah the son of Meshelemiah was gatekeeper of the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[22] All these who were chosen to be gatekeepers at the thresholds were 212.
They were listed in the family records in their villages.
David and Samuel the seer had appointed them to their office of trust.
[23] So they and their children had the oversight of the gates of the LORD’s house,
the house of the tent, as guards.
[24] The gatekeepers were on the four sides:
toward the east, west, north, and south.
[25] Their brothers in their villages had to come in from time to time for seven days
to be with them.
[26] For the four chief gatekeepers, who were Levites, were in an office of trust.
They were over the rooms and over the treasuries in God’s house.
[27] They stayed around God’s house through the night,
because that was their duty.
And it was their duty to open it every morning.

> **What does this mean?**
> The gatekeepers were important: they guarded the temple day and night, opened it in the morning and made sure that nothing unclean came in. They also had oversight over the treasures.
> Chronicles traces their service back to very old times: to the time in the wilderness, to Phinehas, to Samuel and David. In this way it shows: the worship after the return stands in a long, faithful tradition.
> They worked in turns: those who lived in the villages came to Jerusalem for service for seven days at a time.
> Jeduthun was one of David’s great singers (1 Chronicles 16:41).

## More tasks in the temple (Verses 28–34)

[28] Some of them were in charge of the vessels of the service,
for these were counted when they were brought in, and counted when they were taken out.
[29] Some of them were also appointed over the furniture,
and over all the holy vessels,
over the fine flour, the wine, the oil, the frankincense, and the spices.
[30] Some of the sons of the priests prepared the mixing of the spices.
[31] Mattithiah, one of the Levites, the firstborn of Shallum the Korahite,
had the office of trust over the things that were baked in pans.
[32] Some of their brothers, of the sons of the Kohathites,
were over the show bread,
to prepare it every Sabbath.
[33] These are the singers, heads of fathers’ households of the Levites.
They lived in the rooms and were free from other service,
for they were busy with their work day and night.
[34] These were heads of fathers’ households of the Levites, throughout their generations, chief men.
They lived at Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the temple there were many different tasks: counting and keeping the vessels, taking care of flour, wine, oil and frankincense, mixing spices for the holy anointing oil, baking bread.
> The “show bread” was twelve loaves of bread that were put fresh on a table in the sanctuary every week, one for each tribe of Israel (Leviticus 24:5–9).
> The singers praised God “day and night”. That reminds us of Psalm 134: “Praise the LORD, all you servants of the LORD, who stand by night in the LORD’s house!”
> Every task has its place, whether gatekeeper, baker or singer. Together they all serve God.

## Once more: the family of Saul (Verses 35–44)

[35] Jeiel the father of Gibeon lived in Gibeon.
His wife’s name was Maacah.
[36] His firstborn son was Abdon,
then Zur, Kish, Baal, Ner, Nadab,
[37] Gedor, Ahio, Zechariah, and Mikloth.
[38] Mikloth became the father of Shimeam.
They also lived with their relatives in Jerusalem, near their relatives.
[39] Ner became the father of Kish.
Kish became the father of Saul.
Saul became the father of Jonathan, Malchishua, Abinadab, and Eshbaal.
[40] The son of Jonathan was Merib-baal.
Merib-baal became the father of Micah.
[41] The sons of Micah:
Pithon, Melech, Tahrea, and Ahaz.
[42] Ahaz became the father of Jarah.
Jarah became the father of Alemeth, Azmaveth, and Zimri.
Zimri became the father of Moza.
[43] Moza became the father of Binea,
Rephaiah his son,
Eleasah his son,
and Azel his son.
[44] Azel had six sons. These are their names:
Azrikam, Bocheru, Ishmael, Sheariah, Obadiah, and Hanan.
These were the sons of Azel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The family of Saul is named here a second time (compare 1 Chronicles 8:29–38). This is not a mistake: this list forms the bridge to the next chapter. There the real story begins, with the death of Saul.
> Small differences from chapter 8: here the son of Micah is called “Tahrea” instead of “Tarea”, the son of Ahaz “Jarah” instead of “Jehoaddah”, and the son of Binea “Rephaiah” instead of “Raphah”. Such differences came about when the text was copied over the centuries.
> With this the family trees end. From Chapter 10 on, Chronicles tells the story of David.
