# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 21
### David’s census and the altar on the threshing floor

---

## The census (Verses 1–7)

[1] Satan stood up against Israel
and moved David to take a census of Israel.
[2] David said to Joab and to the princes of the people,
“Go, count Israel from Beersheba to Dan,
and bring me word, so that I may know how many there are.”
[3] Joab said,
“May the LORD make his people a hundred times as many as they are!
But, my lord the king, aren’t they all my lord’s servants?
Why does my lord ask for this?
Why should he bring guilt on Israel?”
[4] But the king’s word won out against Joab.
So Joab left,
went throughout all Israel,
and then came to Jerusalem.
[5] Joab gave David the total of the count of the people.
All Israel had 1,100,000 men who drew a sword,
and Judah had 470,000 men who drew a sword.
[6] But he didn’t count Levi and Benjamin among them,
for the king’s word was disgusting to Joab.
[7] God was displeased with this thing.
So he struck Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> David wants to know how many warriors he has. That sounds harmless, but it shows: he trusts in the number of his soldiers, not in God. He treats the people as if they belonged to him, not to God.
> Even Joab, who is otherwise not squeamish, warns David.
> Here it says “Satan”. The Hebrew word means “adversary” or “accuser”. In this place 2 Samuel 24:1 says: “Again the anger of the LORD burned against Israel. And he moved David against them.” Chronicles, which was written later, says it differently: not God himself, but Satan led David astray. Both texts say: behind the temptation there is a power that is greater than David. And both say: David is still responsible himself.
> The numbers are different from 2 Samuel 24:9 (there 800,000 and 500,000).
> Levi was not counted because the Levites did not serve in war (Numbers 1:47–49).

## David is sorry (Verses 8–13)

[8] David said to God,
“I have sinned greatly, because I have done this thing.
But now, please take away the guilt of your servant,
for I have acted very foolishly.”
[9] The LORD spoke to Gad, David’s seer:
[10] “Go and tell David,
‘The LORD says,
“I offer you three things.
Choose one of them, that I may do it to you.”’”
[11] So Gad came to David and said to him,
“The LORD says,
‘Take your choice:
[12] either three years of famine,
or three months of being swept away before your enemies,
while the sword of your enemies catches up with you,
or else three days of the sword of the LORD,
a deadly disease in the land,
and the LORD’s angel destroying throughout all the borders of Israel.’
Now think about what answer I shall give to him who sent me.”
[13] David said to Gad,
“I am in great distress.
Please let me fall into the LORD’s hand,
for his mercies are very great.
But don’t let me fall into the hands of people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David sees his mistake and confesses it honestly: “I have sinned greatly.”
> He must choose between three terrible results. He chooses the one where he is completely in God’s hand. His reason: “His mercies are very great.” He trusts that God is more merciful than people.
> 2 Samuel 24:13 has “seven years” of famine instead of three.
> Gad was a prophet who was with David.

## The plague and the angel over Jerusalem (Verses 14–17)

[14] So the LORD sent a deadly disease on Israel.
And 70,000 men of Israel fell.
[15] God sent an angel to Jerusalem to destroy it.
As he was about to destroy it, the LORD saw,
and he was sorry about the disaster.
He said to the destroying angel,
“It is enough! Now withdraw your hand.”
The LORD’s angel was standing by the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.
[16] David lifted up his eyes
and saw the LORD’s angel standing between earth and sky,
with a drawn sword in his hand stretched out over Jerusalem.
Then David and the elders, dressed in sackcloth, fell on their faces.
[17] David said to God,
“Isn’t it I who commanded the people to be counted?
It is I who have sinned and done very wickedly.
But these sheep, what have they done?
Please, LORD my God, let your hand be against me and against my father’s house,
but not against your people, so that they are struck by the plague.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a very hard story. Many people die because of the guilt of their king. That seems unfair to us. The Bible does not keep quiet about this question. David himself asks it: “These sheep, what have they done?”
> David is ready to carry the punishment himself. He wants God to strike him and not the people. A king should stand in front of his people, like a shepherd in front of his sheep.
> God has mercy: “It is enough!” He stops the angel before Jerusalem is destroyed. God’s mercy is greater than his anger, as David had hoped.
> Christians see here a picture of Jesus: as the “good shepherd” he took the guilt of others on himself (John 10:11).
> In 2 Samuel 24:16 “Ornan” is called “Araunah”.

## The altar on the threshing floor (Verses 18–30)

[18] Then the LORD’s angel commanded Gad to tell David
that David should go up
and set up an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.
[19] David went up at the word of Gad,
which he spoke in the LORD’s name.
[20] Ornan turned back and saw the angel.
And his four sons who were with him hid themselves.
Ornan was threshing wheat.
[21] As David came to Ornan,
Ornan looked and saw David.
He went out of the threshing floor
and bowed down to David with his face to the ground.
[22] Then David said to Ornan,
“Sell me the place of this threshing floor,
so that I may build an altar to the LORD on it.
Sell it to me for the full price,
so that the plague may be stopped from striking the people.”
[23] Ornan said to David,
“Take it for yourself,
and let my lord the king do what is good in his eyes.
Look, I give the oxen for burnt offerings,
and the threshing sledges for wood,
and the wheat for the grain offering.
I give it all.”
[24] King David said to Ornan,
“No, I will certainly buy it for the full price.
For I will not take what is yours for the LORD,
nor offer a burnt offering that costs me nothing.”
[25] So David gave Ornan 600 shekels of gold by weight for the place.
[26] David built an altar to the LORD there
and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings.
He called on the LORD,
and he answered him from the sky by fire on the altar of burnt offering.
[27] Then the LORD commanded the angel,
and he put his sword back into its sheath.
[28] At that time,
when David saw that the LORD had answered him on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite,
he sacrificed there.
[29] For the LORD’s tabernacle, which Moses made in the wilderness,
and the altar of burnt offering were at that time in the high place at Gibeon.
[30] But David couldn’t go before it to ask God,
for he was afraid because of the sword of the LORD’s angel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The place where the angel stops becomes the place of making peace with God. There David builds an altar.
> Ornan wants to give David everything. But David says a famous sentence: “I will not … offer a burnt offering that costs me nothing.” True devotion to God costs something.
> The Bible says “600 shekels of gold”. A shekel is about 0.4 ounces. 2 Samuel 24:24 says “50 shekels of silver”. Perhaps the 50 shekels were only for the threshing floor and the oxen, and the 600 shekels for the whole place.
> God answers with fire from the sky, as with Elijah on Mount Carmel. This shows: God accepts the offering.
> Most important: later the temple is built on this place (1 Chronicles 22:1; 2 Chronicles 3:1). In 2 Chronicles 3:1 the place is called “Mount Moriah”, like the mountain on which Abraham was to sacrifice Isaac (Genesis 22:2). A place of guilt becomes a place of grace.
> To this day this place in Jerusalem, the Temple Mount, is holy for Jews, Christians and Muslims.
