# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 22
### David prepares for building the temple

---

## The place for the temple (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then David said,
“This is the place for the house of the LORD God.
And this is the altar of burnt offering for Israel.”
[2] David gave orders to gather the foreigners who lived in the land of Israel.
He made them stoneworkers.
They were to cut stone blocks for building God’s house.
[3] David got ready a large amount of iron
for the nails for the doors of the gates and for the clamps.
He also got so much bronze that no one could weigh it.
[4] He got more cedar logs than anyone could count.
The people of Sidon and Tyre had brought David a great many cedar logs.
[5] David said,
“My son Solomon is young and has no experience.
The house to be built for the LORD must be very beautiful and great.
It must be famous and glorious in all countries.
So I will get everything ready for it.”
So David prepared a great deal before he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the story of the angel and the altar (Chapter 21), David knows: here, on Ornan’s threshing floor, the temple is to stand.
> David is not allowed to build the temple himself. But he prepares everything: stones, iron, bronze and wood. He does what he can so that his son has it easier. Sometimes it is our task to prepare for others what we cannot finish ourselves.
> “The foreigners”: people from other nations who lived in Israel. They had to work on the building (compare 2 Chronicles 2:17–18).

---

## David gives Solomon the task (Verses 6–16)

[6] Then David called his son Solomon.
He told him to build a house for the LORD, the God of Israel.
[7] David said to his son Solomon,
“I myself wanted to build a house for the name of the LORD my God.
[8] But the LORD said to me,
‘You have shed a lot of blood.
You have fought great wars.
You must not build a house for my name.
You have shed so much blood on the earth before my eyes.
[9] But you will have a son.
He will be a man of peace.
I will give him rest from all his enemies around him.
His name will be Solomon.
During his rule I will give Israel peace and quiet.
[10] He will build a house for my name.
He will be my son, and I will be his father.
I will make the throne of his kingdom over Israel firm forever.’
[11] Now, my son,
may the LORD be with you and give you success.
May you build the house of the LORD your God,
just as he said about you.
[12] May the LORD give you wisdom and understanding
when he puts you in charge of Israel.
Then you can keep the law of the LORD your God.
[13] You will have success
if you carefully obey the laws and rules
that the LORD gave Moses for Israel.
Be strong and brave.
Do not be afraid.
Do not lose heart.
[14] Look, in my hardship I have prepared these things for the LORD’s house:
100,000 talents of gold
and 1,000,000 talents of silver.
I have also prepared so much bronze and iron that no one can weigh it.
There is a great amount of it.
I have also prepared wood and stone.
And you may add more.
[15] You also have many workers:
stonecutters, stoneworkers, and woodworkers,
and all kinds of people who are skilled in every kind of work.
[16] There is more gold, silver, bronze, and iron than anyone can count.
Now get up and start working!
May the LORD be with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is the reason why David was not allowed to build the temple: he had shed much blood. God’s house is to be a house of peace. That fits Solomon, whose name means “the peaceful one” (the Hebrew word “shalom” means “peace”).
> David gives his son advice for life: keep God’s law. “Be strong and brave. Do not be afraid.” Moses had also spoken like this to Joshua (Joshua 1:6–9).
> “In my hardship”: David gathered the material in spite of many difficulties.
> The Bible says “100,000 talents of gold” and “1,000,000 talents of silver.” A talent is about 75 pounds. Those would be unimaginably large amounts, much more than all the gold there was in the whole region at that time. The numbers probably want to say: it was beyond measure. Perhaps they were also changed when the text was copied.

## David calls on the princes (Verses 17–19)

[17] David also ordered all the leaders of Israel to help his son Solomon.
He said,
[18] “The LORD your God is with you!
He has given you peace on every side.
He has put the people of the land into my power.
The land is now under the control of the LORD and his people.
[19] Now set your heart and your soul to follow the LORD your God.
Get up and build the holy house of the LORD God.
Then the ark of the LORD’s covenant and the holy objects of God
can be brought into the house that will be built for the LORD’s name.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple is not only Solomon’s task. Everyone is to help.
> The most important thing is not the building, but the heart: “Set your heart and your soul to follow the LORD your God.” A house for God without a heart for God would be empty.
