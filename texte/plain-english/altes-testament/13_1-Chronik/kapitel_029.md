# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 29
### Willing gifts, David’s prayer and his death

---

## David’s own gift (Verses 1–5)

[1] David the king said to all the assembly,
“Solomon my son, whom alone God has chosen,
is still young and tender,
and the work is great.
For the palace is not for man,
but for the LORD God.
[2] Now I have prepared with all my might for the house of my God:
the gold for the things of gold,
the silver for the things of silver,
the bronze for the things of bronze,
iron for the things of iron,
and wood for the things of wood,
also onyx stones, stones to be set,
stones for inlaid work of various colors,
all kinds of precious stones,
and marble stones in abundance.
[3] In addition, because I have set my affection on the house of my God,
I give my own treasure of gold and silver to the house of my God,
over and above all that I have prepared for the holy house:
[4] three thousand talents of gold, of the gold of Ophir,
and seven thousand talents of refined silver,
to overlay the walls of the houses with them;
[5] gold for the things of gold, and silver for the things of silver,
and for all kinds of work to be made by the hands of artisans.
Who then offers willingly to consecrate himself today to the LORD?”

> **What does this mean?**
> David does not only give from the royal treasury, but also from his own property. “Because I have set my affection on the house of my God”: where the heart is, there you give gladly.
> He goes ahead with a good example, and then he invites the others to give willingly.
> A talent is about 75 pounds. So these numbers are also very high.
> In the Hebrew text it says literally “to fill your hand”. This is an old phrase for: to consecrate yourself to God, to give yourself for his service.

---

## The people give willingly (Verses 6–9)

[6] Then these offered willingly:
the princes of the fathers’ households,
the princes of the tribes of Israel,
the captains of thousands and of hundreds,
and the rulers over the king’s work.
[7] They gave for the service of God’s house:
of gold five thousand talents and ten thousand darics,
of silver ten thousand talents,
of bronze eighteen thousand talents,
and of iron one hundred thousand talents.
[8] People who had precious stones gave them to the treasure of the LORD’s house,
under the hand of Jehiel the Gershonite.
[9] Then the people rejoiced, because they had offered willingly.
For with a whole heart they offered willingly to the LORD.
And David the king also rejoiced with great joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> The great men of the people follow David’s example. And the most important thing: they give willingly and from the heart. That is why there is joy among the people. Giving willingly makes you glad. Later Paul writes: “God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7).
> “Darics” were Persian gold coins. They did not exist yet in David’s time, but only about 500 years later. The writer of Chronicles changed the value into the money of his own time, so that his readers could understand it. 10,000 darics would be about 185 pounds of gold.

## David’s prayer of praise (Verses 10–19)

[10] So David blessed the LORD before all the assembly.
David said,
“You are blessed, LORD, the God of Israel our father,
forever and ever.
[11] Yours, LORD, is the greatness, the power,
the glory, the victory, and the majesty!
For all that is in the heavens and on the earth is yours.
Yours is the kingdom, LORD,
and you are exalted as head above all.
[12] Both riches and honor come from you,
and you rule over all!
In your hand is power and might!
It is in your hand to make anyone great and to give strength to all!
[13] Now therefore, our God, we thank you
and praise your glorious name.
[14] But who am I, and what is my people,
that we should be able to offer so willingly as this?
For all things come from you,
and we have given you of your own.
[15] For we are strangers before you and foreigners, as all our fathers were.
Our days on the earth are like a shadow,
and there is no remaining.
[16] LORD our God,
all this wealth that we have prepared
to build you a house for your holy name
comes from your hand,
and is all your own.
[17] I know also, my God, that you test the heart
and have pleasure in uprightness.
As for me, in the uprightness of my heart
I have willingly offered all these things.
And now I have seen with joy
how your people, who are present here, offer willingly to you.
[18] LORD, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Israel, our fathers,
keep this desire forever in the thoughts of the heart of your people,
and prepare their heart for you;
[19] and give to Solomon my son a whole heart,
to keep your commandments, your testimonies, and your statutes,
and to do all these things,
and to build the palace for which I have made provision.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most beautiful prayers in the Bible.
> Verse 11 sounds familiar: “Yours, LORD, is the greatness, the power, the glory … Yours is the kingdom, LORD”. Similar words stand at the end of the Lord’s Prayer, as it is prayed in many churches. This ending is based on David’s prayer.
> Verse 14 is the heart of it: “All things come from you, and we have given you of your own.” When we give something to God, we only give back to him what he gave us first. This sentence is still spoken in many church services today at the offering.
> Verse 15: “We are strangers before you and foreigners … Our days on the earth are like a shadow.” The mighty king knows: life is short. Everything we have is only lent to us.
> At the end David does not pray for more wealth, but for a heart that stays turned toward God, for the people and for his son.

## Solomon becomes king (Verses 20–25)

[20] Then David said to all the assembly,
“Now bless the LORD your God!”
And all the assembly blessed the LORD, the God of their fathers.
They bowed down their heads
and bowed down before the LORD and the king.
[21] On the next day after that day
they offered sacrifices and burnt offerings to the LORD:
one thousand bulls, one thousand rams, and one thousand lambs,
with their drink offerings,
and sacrifices in abundance for all Israel.
[22] They ate and drank before the LORD on that day with great gladness.
They made Solomon the son of David king the second time,
and anointed him before the LORD to be prince,
and Zadok to be priest.
[23] Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father.
He prospered, and all Israel obeyed him.
[24] All the princes, the mighty men,
and also all the sons of King David
submitted themselves to Solomon the king.
[25] The LORD made Solomon very great in the sight of all Israel,
and gave him such royal majesty
as no king before him in Israel had had.

> **What does this mean?**
> A great feast with sacrifices, eating and drinking, “with great gladness”.
> “The second time”: Solomon had already been made king in 1 Chronicles 23:1. Now he is solemnly confirmed before all the people.
> Chronicles tells about the change of kings as a peaceful one. 1 Kings 1 tells that there was a fight about who would be the next king before that, because Adonijah wanted to become king himself. Here Chronicles stresses that all the sons of David submitted themselves to Solomon.
> Again: Solomon sits on “the throne of the LORD”.

## David’s death (Verses 26–30)

[26] So David the son of Jesse reigned over all Israel.
[27] The time that he reigned over Israel was forty years.
He reigned seven years in Hebron,
and he reigned thirty-three years in Jerusalem.
[28] He died at a good old age,
full of days, riches, and honor.
And Solomon his son reigned in his place.
[29] Now the acts of David the king, first and last,
look, they are written in the history of Samuel the seer,
in the history of Nathan the prophet,
and in the history of Gad the seer,
[30] with all his reign and his might,
and the events that came over him, over Israel, and over all the kingdoms of the lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> David dies “full of days”, after a long, full life. He reigned from about 1010 to 970 BC.
> Chronicles names its sources: records of the prophets Samuel, Nathan and Gad. These books are lost today. Some of it probably went into the books of Samuel.

## Looking back: What have we read in 1 Chronicles?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The family trees (Chapters 1–9):** The book begins with Adam and leads through Abraham and the twelve tribes to the people who came back to Jerusalem after the exile. The message to the first readers: you belong. You stand in a long story with God. No one is forgotten, every name counts.
> **David (Chapters 10–29):** After Saul’s death David becomes king over all Israel. All the tribes come to him. He conquers Jerusalem and brings the ark into the city with joy and music.
> **God’s promise:** God says to David: “The LORD will build you a house.” David’s family is to last forever. That is why Jews expect the Messiah from David’s family, and Christians see the promise fulfilled in Jesus.
> **The temple:** David is not allowed to build the temple himself, because he has shed much blood. But he prepares everything: the place, the material, the building plan, the priests, the Levites, the singers and the gatekeepers. Everything is in order and fairly shared out by lot.
> **The heart:** Again and again it is about the heart: “Serve him with a whole heart.” “If you seek him, he will be found by you.” The people give willingly and with joy.
> **The lesson of the book:** The most important thing in the life of a people and of a person is worship, that is, the relationship with God. David’s prayer sums it up: “All things come from you, and we have given you of your own.”
> **What comes next?** 2 Chronicles tells about Solomon, the building of the temple, and the kings of Judah until the exile and the return.
