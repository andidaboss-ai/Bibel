# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 29
### Willing gifts, David’s prayer and his death

---

## David’s own gift (Verses 1–5)

[1] King David said to the whole assembly,
“God has chosen my son Solomon, and only him.
But he is still young and inexperienced,
and the work is great.
This palace is not for a human being.
It is for the LORD God.
[2] I have worked with all my strength to get things ready for the house of my God.
I have gathered gold for the things of gold,
silver for the things of silver,
bronze for the things of bronze,
iron for the things of iron,
and wood for the things of wood.
I have also gathered onyx stones and stones to be set,
stones of different colors for inlaid work,
all kinds of precious stones,
and a great amount of marble.
[3] And I love the house of my God very much.
So I am also giving my own treasure of gold and silver to the house of my God.
This is on top of everything I have gathered for the holy house.
[4] I give 3,000 talents of gold (about 225,000 pounds), gold from Ophir,
and 7,000 talents of refined silver (about 525,000 pounds).
They are to cover the walls of the buildings.
[5] The gold is for the things of gold, and the silver is for the things of silver.
It is for every kind of work that the craftsmen will make.
Now who is willing to give himself to the LORD today?”

> **What does this mean?**
> David does not only give from the royal treasury, but also from his own property. “I love the house of my God very much”: where the heart is, there you give gladly.
> He goes ahead with a good example, and then he invites the others to give willingly.
> A talent is about 75 pounds. So these numbers are also very high.
> In the Hebrew text it says literally “to fill your hand”. This is an old phrase for: to consecrate yourself to God, to give yourself for his service.

---

## The people give willingly (Verses 6–9)

[6] Then these men gave willingly:
the leaders of the families,
the leaders of the tribes of Israel,
the commanders of thousands and of hundreds,
and the officials in charge of the king’s work.
[7] They gave this for the service of God’s house:
5,000 talents (about 375,000 pounds) and 10,000 darics of gold,
10,000 talents of silver (about 750,000 pounds),
18,000 talents of bronze (about 1.35 million pounds),
and 100,000 talents of iron (about 7.5 million pounds).
[8] Everyone who had precious stones gave them to the treasury of the LORD’s house.
Jehiel the Gershonite was in charge of it.
[9] The people were full of joy, because they had given willingly.
They had given to the LORD willingly, with their whole heart.
And King David was also full of great joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> The great men of the people follow David’s example. And the most important thing: they give willingly and from the heart. That is why there is joy among the people. Giving willingly makes you glad. Later Paul writes: “God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7).
> “Darics” were Persian gold coins. They did not exist yet in David’s time, but only about 500 years later. The writer of Chronicles changed the value into the money of his own time, so that his readers could understand it. 10,000 darics would be about 185 pounds of gold.

## David’s prayer of praise (Verses 10–19)

[10] So David praised the LORD in front of the whole assembly.
David said,
“Praise be to you, LORD, the God of our father Israel,
forever and ever.
[11] Yours, LORD, is the greatness and the power,
the glory, the victory, and the majesty!
Everything in heaven and on earth is yours.
Yours is the kingdom, LORD.
You are lifted high as the head over all.
[12] Riches and honor come from you.
You rule over everything!
Power and strength are in your hand!
You have the power to make anyone great and to give strength to everyone!
[13] So now, our God, we thank you.
We praise your glorious name.
[14] But who am I? And who are my people?
How could we give so willingly like this?
Everything comes from you.
We have only given you what is already yours.
[15] We are strangers and foreigners before you, as all our ancestors were.
Our days on earth are like a shadow.
Nothing stays.
[16] LORD our God,
we have gathered all this wealth
to build a house for your holy name.
But it all comes from your hand.
It all belongs to you.
[17] My God, I also know that you test the heart.
You are pleased when people are honest.
I have given all these things willingly, with an honest heart.
And now I have seen with joy
how your people who are here give willingly to you.
[18] LORD, God of our ancestors Abraham, Isaac, and Israel,
keep this desire in the hearts and thoughts of your people forever.
Make their hearts faithful to you.
[19] And give my son Solomon a whole heart.
Help him keep your commandments, your teachings, and your laws.
Help him do all these things.
And help him build the palace that I have gathered everything for.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most beautiful prayers in the Bible.
> Verse 11 sounds familiar: “Yours, LORD, is the greatness and the power, the glory … Yours is the kingdom, LORD”. Similar words stand at the end of the Lord’s Prayer, as it is prayed in many churches. This ending is based on David’s prayer.
> Verse 14 is the heart of it: “Everything comes from you. We have only given you what is already yours.” When we give something to God, we only give back to him what he gave us first. Words like these are still spoken in many church services today at the offering.
> Verse 15: “We are strangers and foreigners before you … Our days on earth are like a shadow.” The mighty king knows: life is short. Everything we have is only lent to us.
> At the end David does not pray for more wealth, but for a heart that stays turned toward God, for the people and for his son.

## Solomon becomes king (Verses 20–25)

[20] Then David said to the whole assembly,
“Now praise the LORD your God!”
And the whole assembly praised the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
They bowed their heads.
They bowed down to the ground before the LORD and the king.
[21] On the next day they offered sacrifices to the LORD.
They offered burnt offerings to the LORD:
1,000 bulls, 1,000 rams, and 1,000 lambs,
with their drink offerings.
They offered many sacrifices for all Israel.
[22] On that day they ate and drank before the LORD with great joy.
They made Solomon, the son of David, king a second time.
They anointed him before the LORD to be the ruler.
And they anointed Zadok to be priest.
[23] Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD.
He became king in place of his father David.
He was successful, and all Israel obeyed him.
[24] All the leaders and the mighty men,
and also all of King David’s sons,
promised to obey King Solomon.
[25] The LORD made Solomon very great in the eyes of all Israel.
He gave him royal majesty.
No king in Israel before him had ever had such majesty.

> **What does this mean?**
> A great feast with sacrifices, eating and drinking, “with great joy”.
> “A second time”: Solomon had already been made king in 1 Chronicles 23:1. Now he is solemnly confirmed before all the people.
> Chronicles tells about the change of kings as a peaceful one. 1 Kings 1 tells that there was a fight about who would be the next king before that, because Adonijah wanted to become king himself. Here Chronicles stresses that all the sons of David promised to obey Solomon.
> Again: Solomon sits on “the throne of the LORD”.

## David’s death (Verses 26–30)

[26] So David, the son of Jesse, ruled over all Israel.
[27] He was king over Israel for 40 years.
He ruled 7 years in Hebron.
And he ruled 33 years in Jerusalem.
[28] He died at a good old age.
He had a long life, riches, and honor.
And his son Solomon became king in his place.
[29] Everything King David did, from first to last, is written down.
It is in the records of Samuel the seer,
in the records of Nathan the prophet,
and in the records of Gad the seer.
[30] They tell about his whole reign and his power.
They tell about the events that happened to him, to Israel, and to all the kingdoms of the lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> David dies after a long, full life. He reigned from about 1010 to 970 BC.
> Chronicles names its sources: records of the prophets Samuel, Nathan and Gad. These books are lost today. Some of it probably went into the books of Samuel.

## Looking back: What have we read in 1 Chronicles?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The family trees (Chapters 1–9):** The book begins with Adam and leads through Abraham and the 12 tribes to the people who came back to Jerusalem after the exile. The message to the first readers: you belong. You stand in a long story with God. No one is forgotten, every name counts.
> **David (Chapters 10–29):** After Saul’s death David becomes king over all Israel. All the tribes come to him. He conquers Jerusalem and brings the ark into the city with joy and music.
> **God’s promise:** God says to David: “The LORD will build a house for you.” David’s family is to last forever. That is why Jews expect the Messiah from David’s family, and Christians see the promise fulfilled in Jesus.
> **The temple:** David is not allowed to build the temple himself, because he has shed much blood. But he prepares everything: the place, the material, the building plan, the priests, the Levites, the singers and the gatekeepers. Everything is in order and fairly shared out by lot.
> **The heart:** Again and again it is about the heart: “Serve him with your whole heart.” “If you seek him, he will let you find him.” The people give willingly and with joy.
> **The lesson of the book:** The most important thing in the life of a people and of a person is worship, that is, the relationship with God. David’s prayer sums it up: “Everything comes from you. We have only given you what is already yours.”
> **What comes next?** 2 Chronicles tells about Solomon, the building of the temple, and the kings of Judah until the exile and the return.
