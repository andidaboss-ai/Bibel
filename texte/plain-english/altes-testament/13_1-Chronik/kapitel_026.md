# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 26
### Doorkeepers, treasurers and officials

---

## The families of the doorkeepers (Verses 1–12)

[1] The divisions of the doorkeepers:
Of the Korahites:
Meshelemiah the son of Kore, of the sons of Asaph.
[2] Meshelemiah had sons:
Zechariah the firstborn,
Jediael the second,
Zebadiah the third,
Jathniel the fourth,
[3] Elam the fifth,
Jehohanan the sixth,
and Eliehoenai the seventh.
[4] Obed-Edom had sons:
Shemaiah the firstborn,
Jehozabad the second,
Joah the third,
Sacar the fourth,
Nethanel the fifth,
[5] Ammiel the sixth,
Issachar the seventh,
and Peullethai the eighth.
For God blessed him.
[6] Sons were also born to Shemaiah his son.
They ruled over their father’s house,
for they were mighty men of courage.
[7] The sons of Shemaiah:
Othni, Rephael, Obed, and Elzabad,
whose relatives were brave men, Elihu and Semachiah.
[8] All these were of the sons of Obed-Edom,
they and their sons and their brothers,
able men, strong for the service:
sixty-two of Obed-Edom.
[9] Meshelemiah had sons and brothers, eighteen brave men.
[10] Also Hosah, of the children of Merari, had sons:
Shimri the chief
(though he was not the firstborn, his father made him chief),
[11] Hilkiah the second,
Tebaliah the third,
and Zechariah the fourth.
All the sons and brothers of Hosah were thirteen.
[12] These were the divisions of the doorkeepers, by their chief men.
They had duties like their brothers, to serve in the LORD’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> The doorkeepers guarded the entrances of the temple.
> Obed-Edom is blessed here with many sons: “For God blessed him.” The ark had stayed in his house for three months (1 Chronicles 13:14). Its blessing passed on to his whole family.
> “Of the sons of Asaph” in verse 1 is probably a different Asaph from the singer, namely Ebiasaph (1 Chronicles 9:19).
> In verse 10 Hosah makes not the firstborn, but another son the chief. Perhaps the firstborn had died or was not suitable.

---

## The gates are given out by lot (Verses 13–19)

[13] They cast lots, the small as well as the great, by their fathers’ houses, for every gate.
[14] The lot for the east fell to Shelemiah.
Then they cast lots for Zechariah his son, a wise counselor,
and his lot came out for the north.
[15] To Obed-Edom the south,
and to his sons the storehouse.
[16] To Shuppim and Hosah the west,
by the gate of Shallecheth, at the road that goes up,
watchman next to watchman.
[17] To the east were six Levites,
to the north four a day,
to the south four a day,
and for the storehouse two and two.
[18] For Parbar to the west:
four at the road, and two at Parbar.
[19] These were the divisions of the doorkeepers,
of the sons of the Korahites and of the sons of Merari.

> **What does this mean?**
> Each side of the temple had its doorkeepers. In the east, where the main entrance was, the most stood.
> Shelemiah is another form of Meshelemiah.
> “Parbar” is a rare word. It was probably an open building or courtyard on the west side of the temple.
> We do not know what the gate “Shallecheth” was.

## The treasurers (Verses 20–28)

[20] Of the Levites, Ahijah was over the treasures of God’s house
and over the treasures of the dedicated things.
[21] The sons of Ladan,
the sons of the Gershonites belonging to Ladan,
the heads of the fathers’ households belonging to Ladan the Gershonite:
Jehieli.
[22] The sons of Jehieli:
Zetham, and Joel his brother.
They were over the treasures of the LORD’s house.
[23] Of the Amramites, of the Izharites, of the Hebronites, of the Uzzielites:
[24] Shebuel the son of Gershom, the son of Moses,
was ruler over the treasuries.
[25] His brothers:
of Eliezer,
Rehabiah his son,
Jeshaiah his son,
Joram his son,
Zichri his son,
and Shelomoth his son.
[26] This Shelomoth and his brothers were over all the treasuries of the dedicated things,
which David the king,
the heads of the fathers’ households,
the captains over thousands and hundreds,
and the captains of the army had dedicated.
[27] They dedicated some of the plunder won in battles
to repair the LORD’s house.
[28] All that Samuel the seer,
Saul the son of Kish,
Abner the son of Ner,
and Joab the son of Zeruiah had dedicated,
whoever had dedicated anything,
it was in the care of Shelomoth and his brothers.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple had great treasures. Honest and reliable Levites took care of them. Here too it shows: order and honesty with money are important in worship.
> Again descendants of Moses are named, Shebuel and Shelomoth. They had responsible tasks.
> Even Samuel, Saul, Abner and Joab had dedicated gifts for the temple, long before it was built.

## Officials in the whole land (Verses 29–32)

[29] Of the Izharites,
Chenaniah and his sons were appointed to the outside business over Israel,
as officers and judges.
[30] Of the Hebronites,
Hashabiah and his brothers, 1,700 brave men,
had the oversight of Israel beyond the Jordan westward,
for all the business of the LORD and for the service of the king.
[31] Of the Hebronites, Jerijah was the chief of the Hebronites,
by their generations and fathers’ households.
In the fortieth year of the reign of David they were searched for,
and mighty men of courage were found among them at Jazer of Gilead.
[32] His relatives, brave men, were 2,700 heads of fathers’ households.
King David made them overseers over the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of the Manassites,
for every matter concerning God and for the affairs of the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Not all Levites worked in the temple. Many served in the whole land as officials and judges. They took care of religious questions and of tasks for the king.
> “Beyond the Jordan westward”: this means the land west of the Jordan. For someone standing in the east, it lay “beyond”.
> In the fortieth year, that is, in David’s last year, he still put all this in order.
