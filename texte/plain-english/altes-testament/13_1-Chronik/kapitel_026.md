# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 26
### Doorkeepers, treasurers and officials

---

## The families of the doorkeepers (Verses 1–12)

[1] These were the groups of the doorkeepers:
From the Korahites:
Meshelemiah, the son of Kore, from the sons of Asaph.
[2] Meshelemiah had sons:
Zechariah was the firstborn,
Jediael was second,
Zebadiah was third,
Jathniel was fourth,
[3] Elam was fifth,
Jehohanan was sixth,
and Eliehoenai was seventh.
[4] Obed-Edom had sons:
Shemaiah was the firstborn,
Jehozabad was second,
Joah was third,
Sacar was fourth,
Nethanel was fifth,
[5] Ammiel was sixth,
Issachar was seventh,
and Peullethai was eighth.
For God blessed him.
[6] Obed-Edom’s son Shemaiah also had sons.
They were leaders in their family,
because they were brave, mighty men.
[7] The sons of Shemaiah were
Othni, Rephael, Obed, and Elzabad.
Their relatives Elihu and Semachiah were also brave men.
[8] All these were descendants of Obed-Edom.
They, their sons, and their relatives were able men.
They were strong enough for the work.
There were 62 from Obed-Edom.
[9] Meshelemiah had sons and relatives.
They were 18 brave men.
[10] Hosah, one of the descendants of Merari, also had sons.
Shimri was the leader.
He was not the firstborn, but his father made him the leader.
[11] Hilkiah was second,
Tebaliah was third,
and Zechariah was fourth.
Hosah’s sons and relatives were 13 in all.
[12] These were the groups of the doorkeepers, with their leaders.
Like their relatives, they had duties.
They served in the LORD’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> The doorkeepers guarded the entrances of the temple.
> Obed-Edom is blessed here with many sons: “For God blessed him.” The ark had stayed in his house for three months (1 Chronicles 13:14). Its blessing passed on to his whole family.
> “Of the sons of Asaph” in verse 1 is probably a different Asaph from the singer, namely Ebiasaph (1 Chronicles 9:19).
> In verse 10 Hosah makes not the firstborn, but another son the chief. Perhaps the firstborn had died or was not suitable.

## The gates are given out by lot (Verses 13–19)

[13] They drew lots for every gate, family by family.
The young and the old were treated the same.
[14] The lot for the east gate fell to Shelemiah.
Then they drew lots for his son Zechariah.
Zechariah was a wise adviser.
His lot was the north gate.
[15] Obed-Edom got the south gate.
His sons got the storehouse.
[16] Shuppim and Hosah got the west gate.
It was by the Shallecheth Gate, on the road that goes up.
Guard stood across from guard.
[17] On the east side there were six Levites each day.
On the north side there were four each day.
On the south side there were four each day.
At the storehouse there were two at each place.
[18] At the Parbar on the west side,
there were four on the road and two at the Parbar.
[19] These were the groups of the doorkeepers
from the descendants of Korah and of Merari.

> **What does this mean?**
> Each side of the temple had its doorkeepers. In the east, where the main entrance was, the most stood.
> Shelemiah is another form of Meshelemiah.
> “Parbar” is a rare word. It was probably an open building or courtyard on the west side of the temple.
> We do not know what the gate “Shallecheth” was.

## The treasurers (Verses 20–28)

[20] Some of the Levites were in charge of the treasures.
Ahijah took care of the treasures of God’s house
and the treasures of the dedicated gifts.
Dedicated gifts are things that people gave to God.
[21] Some were descendants of Ladan.
They were Gershonites through Ladan.
They were heads of the families of Ladan the Gershonite.
One of them was Jehieli.
[22] Jehieli’s sons were Zetham and his brother Joel.
They were in charge of the treasures of the LORD’s house.
[23] There were also leaders from the families of Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel:
[24] Shebuel was the son of Gershom, the son of Moses.
He was the chief officer over the treasuries.
[25] His relatives came from Eliezer.
Eliezer’s son was Rehabiah.
Rehabiah’s son was Jeshaiah.
Jeshaiah’s son was Joram.
Joram’s son was Zichri.
Zichri’s son was Shelomoth.
[26] This Shelomoth and his relatives
were in charge of all the treasuries of the dedicated gifts.
These gifts were given by King David,
by the heads of the families,
by the commanders of thousands and of hundreds,
and by the commanders of the army.
[27] They gave part of the plunder they had taken in battles.
It was to be used to repair the LORD’s house.
[28] Shelomoth and his relatives also took care of everything
that Samuel the seer had dedicated,
and Saul, the son of Kish,
and Abner, the son of Ner,
and Joab, the son of Zeruiah.
They took care of everything that anyone had dedicated.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple had great treasures. Honest and reliable Levites took care of them. Here too it shows: order and honesty with money are important in worship.
> Again descendants of Moses are named, Shebuel and Shelomoth. They had responsible tasks.
> Even Samuel, Saul, Abner and Joab had dedicated gifts for the temple, long before it was built.

## Officials in the whole land (Verses 29–32)

[29] From the Izharites:
Chenaniah and his sons were given work outside the temple.
They were officials and judges over Israel.
[30] From the Hebronites:
Hashabiah and his relatives were 1,700 brave men.
They were in charge of Israel on the west side of the Jordan.
They took care of all the LORD’s work and of the king’s service.
[31] Jerijah was the leader of the Hebronites,
listed by their generations and families.
In the fortieth year of David’s rule,
the records of the Hebronites were searched.
They found brave, mighty men among them at Jazer in Gilead.
[32] Jerijah had 2,700 relatives.
They were brave men and heads of families.
King David put them in charge of the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh.
They took care of everything that had to do with God
and everything that had to do with the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Not all Levites worked in the temple. Many served in the whole land as officials and judges. They took care of religious questions and of tasks for the king.
> “On the west side of the Jordan”: the Hebrew text says “beyond the Jordan westward.” For someone standing in the east, this land lay “beyond” the river.
> In the fortieth year, that is, in David’s last year, he still put all this in order.
