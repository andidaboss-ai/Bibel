# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 11
### David becomes king and conquers Jerusalem

---

## David becomes king over all Israel (Verses 1–3)

[1] Then all Israel gathered to David at Hebron and said,
“Look, we are your bone and your flesh.
[2] In times past, even when Saul was king,
it was you who led Israel out to war and brought them back home.
And the LORD your God said to you,
‘You shall be shepherd of my people Israel,
and you shall be prince over my people Israel.’”
[3] So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron.
And David made a covenant with them in Hebron before the LORD.
They anointed David king over Israel,
according to the LORD’s word by Samuel.

> **What does this mean?**
> “We are your bone and your flesh” means: we are your relatives, we belong together.
> Chronicles tells it briefly. It leaves out the seven years in which David reigned only over Judah and was in conflict with Saul’s family (2 Samuel 2–4). What matters to Chronicles is this: “all Israel” stands behind David.
> “Shepherd” means: someone who leads and provides like a shepherd. David himself had been a shepherd. A king should take care of his people like a good shepherd takes care of his sheep.
> The covenant shows: the king too has duties before God and before the people.

---

## The conquest of Jerusalem (Verses 4–9)

[4] David and all Israel went to Jerusalem (also called Jebus).
The Jebusites, the people of the land, were there.
[5] The people of Jebus said to David,
“You will not come in here!”
But David took the stronghold of Zion.
That is David’s city.
[6] David had said,
“Whoever strikes the Jebusites first shall be chief and captain.”
Joab the son of Zeruiah went up first
and was made chief.
[7] David lived in the stronghold.
That is why they called it David’s city.
[8] He built the city all around, from Millo all the way around.
And Joab repaired the rest of the city.
[9] David grew greater and greater,
for the LORD of Armies was with him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jerusalem was an old city of the Jebusites, on a hill with steep slopes. People thought no one could conquer it. David conquers it and makes it his capital (compare 2 Samuel 5:6–10).
> Jerusalem lay between the lands of Judah and Benjamin, that is, between south and north. A good choice to unite the whole people.
> “Zion” was first the name of the stronghold. Later it became the name for all of Jerusalem and a picture for the place where God lives.
> “Millo” was probably a raised mound of earth or a terrace on which buildings stood.
> Chronicles explains why Joab became army commander: he was the first in the attack.
> The most important sentence: “The LORD of Armies was with him.” David’s success came from God.

## David’s mighty men: the three (Verses 10–14)

[10] Now these are the chief of the mighty men whom David had,
who stood strongly with him in his kingdom,
together with all Israel, to make him king,
according to the LORD’s word concerning Israel.
[11] This is the number of the mighty men whom David had:
Jashobeam, the son of a Hachmonite, the chief of the thirty.
He lifted up his spear against three hundred
and killed them at one time.
[12] After him was Eleazar the son of Dodo, the Ahohite.
He was one of the three mighty men.
[13] He was with David at Pasdammim.
There the Philistines were gathered together for battle.
There was a plot of ground full of barley.
The people fled from the Philistines.
[14] But they stood in the middle of the plot,
defended it, and killed the Philistines.
And the LORD saved them by a great victory.

> **What does this mean?**
> David had a group of especially brave warriors: “the three” and “the thirty”. The list is also in 2 Samuel 23:8–39.
> There Jashobeam is called “Josheb Basshebeth”. And there he kills 800 instead of 300.
> In 2 Samuel 23 a third mighty man is named, Shammah, who also defended a field. Chronicles has shortened this part. That is why here the field is “full of barley”, while in 2 Samuel 23:11 it is a field full of lentils.
> Here too the text stresses: not the mighty men alone, but the LORD gave the victory.

## The water from Bethlehem (Verses 15–19)

[15] Three of the thirty chief men went down to the rock,
to David, into the cave of Adullam.
The army of the Philistines was camped in the valley of Rephaim.
[16] David was then in the stronghold,
and the garrison of the Philistines was in Bethlehem at that time.
[17] David longed for water and said,
“Oh, that someone would give me water to drink
from the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate!”
[18] The three broke through the army of the Philistines,
drew water out of the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate,
took it, and brought it to David.
But David would not drink any of it,
but poured it out to the LORD
[19] and said,
“My God forbid me that I should do this!
Shall I drink the blood of these men who have put their lives in danger?”
For they had risked their lives to bring it.
So he would not drink it.
The three mighty men did these things.

> **What does this mean?**
> David longs for the water from his hometown Bethlehem. Three of his men risk their lives to bring it to him.
> But David does not drink it. He pours it out as an offering for God. He says: this water is as precious as the life of my men. I must not simply take it for myself.
> A beautiful story about faithfulness, friendship and respect for the lives of others.

## Abishai and Benaiah (Verses 20–25)

[20] Abishai, the brother of Joab, was chief of the three.
He lifted up his spear against three hundred and killed them.
And he had a name among the three.
[21] Of the three, he was more honored than the two,
and he was made their captain.
But he was not counted among the three.
[22] Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, the son of a brave man of Kabzeel,
who had done mighty deeds:
He killed the two sons of Ariel of Moab.
He also went down and killed a lion in the middle of a pit on a snowy day.
[23] He killed an Egyptian, a very tall man, about seven and a half feet tall.
In the Egyptian’s hand was a spear like a weaver’s beam.
Benaiah went down to him with a staff,
pulled the spear out of the Egyptian’s hand,
and killed him with his own spear.
[24] Benaiah the son of Jehoiada did these things.
And he had a name among the three mighty men.
[25] Look, he was more honored than the thirty,
but he did not reach the three.
And David put him over his guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Hebrew verses 20 and 21 are hard to understand. Abishai was a captain, but did not belong to the first three. Perhaps he was the leader of a second group of three.
> Later, under Solomon, Benaiah became the highest army commander (1 Kings 2:35).
> The Bible says “five cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> A “weaver’s beam” was a thick wooden bar on a loom. Goliath’s spear is described in the same way (1 Samuel 17:7).

## The thirty (Verses 26–47)

[26] The mighty men of the armies also included:
Asahel the brother of Joab,
Elhanan the son of Dodo of Bethlehem,
[27] Shammoth the Harorite,
Helez the Pelonite,
[28] Ira the son of Ikkesh the Tekoite,
Abiezer the Anathothite,
[29] Sibbecai the Hushathite,
Ilai the Ahohite,
[30] Maharai the Netophathite,
Heled the son of Baanah the Netophathite,
[31] Ithai the son of Ribai of Gibeah of the children of Benjamin,
Benaiah the Pirathonite,
[32] Hurai of the brooks of Gaash,
Abiel the Arbathite,
[33] Azmaveth the Baharumite,
Eliahba the Shaalbonite,
[34] the sons of Hashem the Gizonite,
Jonathan the son of Shagee the Hararite,
[35] Ahiam the son of Sacar the Hararite,
Eliphal the son of Ur,
[36] Hepher the Mecherathite,
Ahijah the Pelonite,
[37] Hezro the Carmelite,
Naarai the son of Ezbai,
[38] Joel the brother of Nathan,
Mibhar the son of Hagri,
[39] Zelek the Ammonite,
Naharai the Berothite (the armor bearer of Joab the son of Zeruiah),
[40] Ira the Ithrite,
Gareb the Ithrite,
[41] Uriah the Hittite,
Zabad the son of Ahlai,
[42] Adina the son of Shiza the Reubenite (a chief of the Reubenites), and thirty with him,
[43] Hanan the son of Maacah,
Joshaphat the Mithnite,
[44] Uzzia the Ashterathite,
Shama and Jeiel the sons of Hotham the Aroerite,
[45] Jediael the son of Shimri,
and Joha his brother, the Tizite,
[46] Eliel the Mahavite,
and Jeribai and Joshaviah, the sons of Elnaam,
and Ithmah the Moabite,
[47] Eliel, Obed, and Jaasiel the Mezobaite.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here all the mighty men are named. Many are otherwise unknown. But what they did is not forgotten.
> Among them is also Uriah the Hittite. He was faithful, and David did him a great wrong: he took his wife Bathsheba from him and had him killed in the war (2 Samuel 11). Chronicles does not tell this story, but it names Uriah in the list of the mighty men.
> There are also foreigners among the mighty men: an Ammonite, a Moabite and a Hittite. They firmly belonged to David’s men.
> The list is longer than in 2 Samuel 23. The names from verse 42 on are only here. Many names are a little different from 2 Samuel 23, for example “Shammoth the Harorite” instead of “Shammah the Harodite”.
