# 1 Chronicles – Chapter 1
### From Adam to Esau

---

## Before we begin: What is the first book of Chronicles?

The books of Chronicles tell the history of Israel once more, from Adam to the end of the exile in Babylon. In the beginning, 1 and 2 Chronicles were one single book.
We already know much of it from the books of Samuel and Kings. But Chronicles tells it from a different point of view. It was probably written after the return from Babylon, around 400 to 300 before Christ. At that time people asked: Who are we? Do we still belong to God’s people? Has God forgotten us?
The answer of Chronicles is: You belong. You stand in a long line that goes back to Adam. God has never given up his people.
That is why the book begins with long lists of names (Chapters 1–9). For us today this is unusual. But for the people back then every name was important: here stood their family, their origin, their place in God’s people. That is why we do not leave out a single name here either.
After that, the first book of Chronicles tells mainly about King David (Chapters 10–29). Especially important are the temple, the priests, the Levites and the music in worship.
In Hebrew the book is called “Dibre ha-yamim”, which means “events of the days”. The word “Chronicles” comes from the Greek word for time.

## From Adam to Noah (Verses 1–4)

[1] Adam, Seth, Enosh,
[2] Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared,
[3] Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech,
[4] Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chronicles begins at the very beginning: with Adam, the first human. In this way it shows: the history of Israel is part of the history of all people.
> The names come from Genesis 5. There you can read more about these people. Enoch, for example, “walked with God”, and Methuselah became very old.
> Noah had three sons: Shem, Ham and Japheth. According to the Bible, all the peoples of the earth come from them.

---

## The descendants of Japheth (Verses 5–7)

[5] The sons of Japheth were
Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras.
[6] The sons of Gomer were
Ashkenaz, Diphath, and Togarmah.
[7] The sons of Javan were
Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim, and Rodanim.

> **What does this mean?**
> These names come from Genesis 10, the so-called “Table of Nations”. Many names stand for peoples or countries.
> As people thought back then, the descendants of Japheth lived mainly in the north and west, for example in Asia Minor (today Turkey) and on the islands of the Mediterranean Sea.
> “Javan” is probably the old name for the Greeks (Ionians). “Madai” are the Medes. “Kittim” are the people of Cyprus.
> In Genesis 10:3 it says “Riphath” instead of Diphath, and in Genesis 10:4 “Dodanim” instead of Rodanim. In Hebrew the letters D and R look almost the same.

---

## The descendants of Ham (Verses 8–16)

[8] The sons of Ham were
Cush, Mizraim, Put, and Canaan.
[9] The sons of Cush were
Seba, Havilah, Sabta, Raama, and Sabteca.
The sons of Raamah were
Sheba and Dedan.
[10] Cush was the father of Nimrod.
Nimrod was the first mighty ruler on the earth.
[11] Mizraim was the father of the Ludim, Anamim, Lehabim, Naphtuhim,
[12] Pathrusim, Casluhim, and Caphtorim.
The Philistines came from the Casluhim.
[13] Canaan was the father of Sidon, his firstborn son, and of Heth.
[14] He was also the father of the Jebusites, the Amorites, the Girgashites,
[15] the Hivites, the Arkites, the Sinites,
[16] the Arvadites, the Zemarites, and the Hamathites.

> **What does this mean?**
> As people thought back then, the descendants of Ham lived mainly in the south: in Africa and Arabia, and in Canaan.
> “Cush” is the land south of Egypt (today Sudan). “Mizraim” is the Hebrew name for Egypt. “Put” is perhaps Libya.
> Nimrod was a famous hunter and ruler in Mesopotamia (Genesis 10:8–12).
> The names in verses 14–16 are the peoples who lived in Canaan before Israel came.
> Important: This list orders peoples by regions and relationships, not by skin color or worth. The Bible teaches that all people come from one human couple and are of equal worth. Sadly, the list was misused in the past to justify racism. That is wrong and against the meaning of the Bible.

## The descendants of Shem (Verses 17–23)

[17] The sons of Shem were
Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, Aram, Uz, Hul, Gether, and Meshech.
[18] Arpachshad was the father of Shelah.
Shelah was the father of Eber.
[19] Eber had two sons.
One was named Peleg, because in his time the earth was divided.
His brother was named Joktan.
[20] Joktan was the father of Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah,
[21] Hadoram, Uzal, Diklah,
[22] Ebal, Abimael, Sheba,
[23] Ophir, Havilah, and Jobab.
All these were the sons of Joktan.

> **What does this mean?**
> From Shem come the “Semites”: the peoples of the Middle East, among them the Arameans, the Arabs and the Israelites.
> Eber is probably the one who gave his name to the “Hebrews”.
> “Peleg” means “division”. Perhaps this means the scattering of the peoples after the tower of Babel (Genesis 11).
> In Genesis 10:23, Uz, Hul, Gether and Meshech are sons of Aram. Chronicles names them here in short form.
> Joktan’s descendants lived mainly in Arabia. Ophir was famous for its gold (1 Kings 9:28).

---

## From Shem to Abraham (Verses 24–27)

[24] Shem, Arpachshad, Shelah,
[25] Eber, Peleg, Reu,
[26] Serug, Nahor, Terah,
[27] Abram, who is also called Abraham.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the line becomes narrower. Of all the descendants of Shem, only one line is followed: the line to Abraham.
> God gave Abram a new name: Abraham, “father of many nations” (Genesis 17:5). With him God’s special history with Israel begins.

---

## The descendants of Abraham: Ishmael and Keturah (Verses 28–33)

[28] The sons of Abraham were
Isaac and Ishmael.
[29] These are their families:
Nebaioth was Ishmael’s firstborn son.
After him came Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,
[30] Mishma, Dumah, Massa, Hadad, Tema,
[31] Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.
These are the sons of Ishmael.
[32] Keturah was Abraham’s concubine.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
Keturah gave birth to Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.
The sons of Jokshan were
Sheba and Dedan.
[33] The sons of Midian were
Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah.
All these were the sons of Keturah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ishmael, the older son of Abraham, also had twelve sons, just as God had promised (Genesis 17:20). Many Arabs and Muslims see Ishmael as their ancestor.
> Keturah was another wife of Abraham after Sarah’s death (Genesis 25:1–4). From her son Midian come the Midianites. Moses’ wife Zipporah was a Midianite.
> Chronicles does not forget Abraham’s other sons. God had promised Abraham that all nations would be blessed through him.

---

## The descendants of Esau (Verses 34–37)

[34] Abraham was the father of Isaac.
The sons of Isaac were
Esau and Israel.
[35] The sons of Esau were
Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam, and Korah.
[36] The sons of Eliphaz were
Teman, Omar, Zephi, Gatam, Kenaz, Timna, and Amalek.
[37] The sons of Reuel were
Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaac had two sons: Esau and Jacob. Here Jacob is called “Israel”, with the name God gave him (Genesis 32:28).
> From Esau come the Edomites. The list comes from Genesis 36.
> In Genesis 36:12, Timna is the concubine of Eliphaz and the mother of Amalek. Chronicles counts her here in short form among his descendants.

---

## The people of Seir (Verses 38–42)

[38] The sons of Seir were
Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon, Anah, Dishon, Ezer, and Dishan.
[39] The sons of Lotan were
Hori and Homam.
Lotan’s sister was Timna.
[40] The sons of Shobal were
Alian, Manahath, Ebal, Shephi, and Onam.
The sons of Zibeon were
Aiah and Anah.
[41] The son of Anah was
Dishon.
The sons of Dishon were
Hamran, Eshban, Ithran, and Cheran.
[42] The sons of Ezer were
Bilhan, Zaavan, and Jaakan.
The sons of Dishan were
Uz and Aran.

> **What does this mean?**
> Seir is the hill country where the Edomites lived. Before them the Horites lived there. Here their families are named (Genesis 36:20–28).
> Esau’s family mixed with them. Esau married Oholibamah, a granddaughter of Zibeon (Genesis 36:2).

---

## The kings and chiefs of Edom (Verses 43–54)

[43] These are the kings who ruled in the land of Edom,
before any king ruled over the people of Israel:
Bela, the son of Beor.
His city was called Dinhabah.
[44] When Bela died,
Jobab, the son of Zerah, from Bozrah, became king in his place.
[45] When Jobab died,
Husham, from the land of the Temanites, became king in his place.
[46] When Husham died,
Hadad, the son of Bedad, became king in his place.
Hadad defeated Midian in the country of Moab.
His city was called Avith.
[47] When Hadad died,
Samlah, from Masrekah, became king in his place.
[48] When Samlah died,
Shaul, from Rehoboth by the River, became king in his place.
[49] When Shaul died,
Baal Hanan, the son of Achbor, became king in his place.
[50] When Baal Hanan died,
Hadad became king in his place.
His city was called Pai.
His wife’s name was Mehetabel.
She was the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab.
[51] Then Hadad died.
The chiefs of Edom were:
chief Timna, chief Aliah, chief Jetheth,
[52] chief Oholibamah, chief Elah, chief Pinon,
[53] chief Kenaz, chief Teman, chief Mibzar,
[54] chief Magdiel, and chief Iram.
These are the chiefs of Edom.

> **What does this mean?**
> Edom already had kings long before Israel had a king. The list comes from Genesis 36:31–43.
> Interesting: these kings did not form one family. Each one came from a different city.
> “Chiefs” were the leaders of the tribes or regions of Edom. Some names are probably rather names of areas.
> In Genesis 36:39 the city is called “Pau” instead of “Pai”.
> In Genesis 10:28 Ebal is called “Obal”.
> With this the look at the other peoples ends. From Chapter 2 on, it is only about Israel.
