# Jeremiah – Chapter 27
### The yoke on the neck

---

## A message to the neighboring kings (Verses 1–11)

[1] At the beginning of the rule of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah,
this word came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] The LORD says to me:
“Make straps and bars,
and put them on your neck.
[3] Then send them to the king of Edom, to the king of Moab,
to the king of the people of Ammon, to the king of Tyre, and to the king of Sidon,
through the messengers who come to Jerusalem to Zedekiah, king of Judah.
[4] Give them a message for their masters. Say,
‘The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says,
“Tell your masters:
[5] ‘I have made the earth,
and the people and the animals that are on the face of the earth,
by my great power and by my outstretched arm.
I give it to whoever I think is right.
[6] Now I have given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, my servant.
I have also given him the wild animals to serve him.
[7] All the nations will serve him,
and his son, and his grandson,
until the time comes for his own land.
Then many nations and great kings will make him their slave.
[8] “‘“‘If a nation or a kingdom will not serve this Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
and will not put its neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon,
I will punish that nation,’ says the LORD,
‘with the sword, with famine, and with disease,
until I have destroyed them by his hand.
[9] But as for you, don’t listen to your prophets,
to your fortune-tellers, to your dreams,
to those who read signs, or to your sorcerers,
who say to you,
“You will not serve the king of Babylon.”
[10] For they prophesy a lie to you.
It will only take you far away from your land,
so that I will drive you out,
and you will die.
[11] But the nation that puts its neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon,
and serves him,
I will let that nation stay in its own land,’ says the LORD.
‘They will farm it and live in it.’”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The WEB, our English source text, names King
 Jehoiakim here, like the Hebrew text. But from the rest of the chapter (verses 3 and 12) it becomes clear that this is about the time of King Zedekiah. That is why many translations have “Zedekiah” here.
> Verse 2: Another acted-out sign: Jeremiah puts a yoke on his neck, like the yoke that is put on oxen. A yoke means: You have to serve someone.
> Verse 3: Messengers from the neighboring countries were in Jerusalem to plan an uprising against Babylon together. Jeremiah sends them a message: Don’t do it!
> Verses 5–7: God is the Creator of the whole world. He gives power to whoever he wants. Now he has given it to Nebuchadnezzar, but not forever: After three generations, it will be Babylon’s turn too.
> Verses 9–11: Jeremiah’s advice is politically wise: Submit to Babylon, then you may stay in your land and live. An uprising only leads to ruin.

## Words to King Zedekiah and to the priests (Verses 12–22)

[12] I spoke all these same words to Zedekiah, king of Judah. I said,
“Put your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon,
and serve him and his people, and live.
[13] Why should you die, you and your people,
by the sword, by famine, and by disease,
as the LORD has said about the nation that will not serve the king of Babylon?
[14] Don’t listen to the words of the prophets who say to you,
‘You will not serve the king of Babylon.’
For they prophesy a lie to you.
[15] For I have not sent them,” says the LORD,
“but they prophesy lies in my name.
The result will be that I drive you out,
and you will die,
you and the prophets who prophesy to you.”
[16] I also spoke to the priests and to all this people. I said,
The LORD says,
“Don’t listen to the words of your prophets who prophesy to you and say,
‘Look, the objects of the LORD’s house will soon be brought back from Babylon.’
For they prophesy a lie to you.
[17] Don’t listen to them.
Serve the king of Babylon, and live.
Why should this city become a ruin?
[18] But if they are prophets,
and if the LORD’s word is with them,
let them now plead with the LORD of Armies,
so that the objects which are left in the LORD’s house,
in the house of the king of Judah, and in Jerusalem,
don’t go to Babylon.
[19] For the LORD of Armies speaks about the pillars,
about the Sea, about the stands,
and about the rest of the objects that are left in this city,
[20] which Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, didn’t take
when he carried away from Jerusalem to Babylon as captives
Jeconiah, the son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah,
and all the nobles of Judah and Jerusalem.
[21] Yes, the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says this about the objects
that are left in the LORD’s house,
and in the house of the king of Judah, and in Jerusalem:
[22] ‘They will be carried to Babylon,
and there they will stay,
until the day that I come for them,’ says the LORD.
‘Then I will bring them up,
and bring them back to this place.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–15: Jeremiah says the same thing to his own king, Zedekiah: Submit, then you will live. The false prophets who preach resistance are lying.
> Verse 16: When the first group was carried away in 597 BC, the Babylonians had taken many precious objects from the temple with them. False prophets said: They will come back soon!
> Verse 18: Jeremiah’s answer: If you are real prophets, then pray instead that the rest will not be taken away too!
> Verse 19: The “pillars” (Jachin and Boaz), the “Sea” (a huge water basin made of bronze) and the “stands” were large bronze objects in the temple (1 Kings 7).
> Verse 22: But here too there is hope: One day God will bring the objects back. That really happened under King Cyrus (Ezra 1:7–11).
