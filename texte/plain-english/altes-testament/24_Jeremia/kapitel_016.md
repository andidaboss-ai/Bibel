# Jeremiah – Chapter 16
### Jeremiah’s lonely life

---

## Don’t marry! (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then the LORD’s word came to me. He said,
[2] “You must not take a wife,
and you must not have sons or daughters in this place.”
[3] For the LORD says this about the sons and the daughters who are born in this place,
and about their mothers who gave birth to them,
and about their fathers who fathered them in this land:
[4] “They will die terrible deaths.
No one will mourn for them,
and they will not be buried.
They will be like manure on the surface of the ground.
They will be destroyed by the sword and by famine.
Their dead bodies will be food for the birds of the sky
and for the animals of the earth.”
[5] For the LORD says,
“Don’t go into a house where people are mourning.
Don’t go there to mourn,
and don’t show them sympathy.
For I have taken my peace away from this people,” says the LORD,
“and my faithful love and my compassion.
[6] Both great and small will die in this land.
They will not be buried.
No one will mourn for them,
or cut themselves, or shave their heads for them.
[7] No one will break bread for those who mourn,
to comfort them for the dead.
No one will give them the cup of comfort to drink
for their father or for their mother.
[8] “You must not go into a house where people are feasting,
to sit with them, to eat and to drink.”
[9] For the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Look, before your eyes and in your days,
I will put an end in this place
to the sound of joy and the sound of gladness,
the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah’s whole life becomes a message:
> Verse 2: He is not to marry or have children. At that time, this was very unusual. Jeremiah’s loneliness shows: The time that is coming is so terrible that people should not start a family.
> Verses 5–7: He is not to go to funerals or comfort anyone. At that time it was customary to bring bread and a “cup of comfort” to people who were mourning.
> Verse 6: At that time some people cut their skin out of grief. The law of Israel forbade this (Leviticus 19:28). If you hurt yourself today out of despair, get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 8–9: He is also not to go to feasts. For soon there will be no more weddings.
> Giving all this up was very hard for Jeremiah. It shows how seriously he took his calling.

## Why is God doing this? (Verses 10–13)

[10] When you tell this people all these words,
they will ask you,
‘Why has the LORD announced all this great disaster against us?’
or ‘What is our guilt?’
or ‘What sin have we committed against the LORD our God?’
[11] Then tell them,
‘Because your ancestors have left me,’ says the LORD,
‘and have followed other gods,
have served them, have worshiped them,
have left me, and have not kept my law.
[12] You have done more evil than your ancestors,
for look, each of you follows the stubbornness of his evil heart,
so that you don’t listen to me.
[13] That is why I will throw you out of this land
into a land that you have not known,
neither you nor your ancestors.
There you will serve other gods day and night,
for I will show you no kindness.’

> **What does this mean?**
> The people ask in surprise: What have we done wrong? They do not even notice their own guilt.
> God answers: Your ancestors left me, and you are even worse.
> Verse 13: A bitter irony: You wanted to serve foreign gods; now you can do that day and night in a foreign land.

## A new exodus (Verses 14–21)

[14] “So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when people will no longer say,
‘As surely as the LORD lives,
who brought the people of Israel up out of the land of Egypt.’
[15] Instead they will say,
‘As surely as the LORD lives,
who brought the people of Israel up from the land of the north,
and from all the countries where he had driven them.’
I will bring them back into their land that I gave to their ancestors.
[16] “Look, I will send for many fishermen,” says the LORD,
“and they will catch them like fish.
Afterward I will send for many hunters,
and they will hunt them from every mountain, from every hill,
and out of the cracks in the rocks.
[17] For my eyes are on all their ways.
They are not hidden from me.
Their guilt is not hidden from my eyes.
[18] First I will pay them back double for their guilt and their sin,
because they have made my land unclean
with the dead bodies of their hateful idols,
and have filled my inheritance with their disgusting things.”
[19] LORD, my strength, my fortress,
and my refuge in the day of trouble,
the nations will come to you from the ends of the earth,
and will say,
“Our ancestors inherited nothing but lies,
worthless things that do no good.
[20] Can a person make gods for himself,
which are not gods at all?”
[21] “So look, I will make them know.
This time I will make them know my hand and my power.
Then they will know that my name is the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: In the middle of the message of judgment, a ray of light: God will bring his people back from exile. This freedom will be so great that people will celebrate it even more than the exodus from Egypt.
> Verses 16–17: Fishermen and hunters are a picture of judgment here: No one can hide from God. (Later, Jesus calls his disciples “fishers for people,” but in a completely different, good sense: Mark 1:17.)
> Verses 19–20: A wonderful prayer of Jeremiah: God is his strength and refuge. And he sees ahead: One day all nations will realize that the idols are nothing, and they will come to God.
