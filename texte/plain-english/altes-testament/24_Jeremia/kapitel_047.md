# Jeremiah – Chapter 47
### Words about the Philistines

---

## A flood from the north (Verses 1–7)

[1] This is the LORD’s word that came to Jeremiah the prophet about the Philistines,
before Pharaoh attacked Gaza.
[2] The LORD says:
“Look, waters are rising out of the north.
They will become a flooding river.
They will flood the land and everything in it,
the city and those who live in it.
The people will cry out,
and everyone who lives in the land will wail.
[3] At the noise of the stamping hoofs of his strong horses,
at the rushing of his chariots,
at the rumbling of his wheels,
fathers don’t look back for their children,
because their hands hang so weak.
[4] This is because of the day that is coming
to destroy all the Philistines,
to cut off from Tyre and Sidon
every helper who is left.
For the LORD will destroy the Philistines,
those who are left from the island of Caphtor.
[5] Gaza has shaved its head bald.
Ashkelon is silenced.
You who are left in their valley,
how long will you cut yourselves?
[6] Oh, you sword of the LORD,
how long will it be before you are quiet?
Put yourself back into your sheath!
Rest, and be still!
[7] But how can it be quiet,
since the LORD has given it a command?
Against Ashkelon and against the seashore,
there he has appointed it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Philistines lived on the coast in the southwest of Israel, in cities such as Gaza and Ashkelon. They were often enemies of Israel. They originally came from the island of Caphtor, probably Crete.
> Verse 2: The Babylonian army comes like a flood wave from the north.
> Verse 3: A shocking detail: The fathers are so full of fear that they do not even look for their children. War destroys even the love between parents and children.
> Verse 5: Heads shaved bald and cuts in the skin were signs of mourning.
> Verse 6: A moving cry: “Oh, you sword of the LORD, how long will it be before you are quiet? Put yourself back into your sheath!” Even in the middle of the announcement of judgment, you can hear the longing for the war to finally stop. This cry is still relevant today, wherever there is war.
