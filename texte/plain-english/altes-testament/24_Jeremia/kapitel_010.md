# Jeremiah – Chapter 10
### The LORD is the true God

---

## Idols can do nothing (Verses 1–16)

[1] Hear the word which the LORD speaks to you, house of Israel!
[2] The LORD says,
“Don’t learn the way of the nations,
and don’t be terrified by the signs in the sky,
even though the nations are terrified by them.
[3] For the customs of the peoples are worthless.
Someone cuts down a tree in the forest,
and a craftsman works it with his ax.
[4] They decorate it with silver and with gold.
They fasten it with nails and with hammers,
so that it can’t fall over.
[5] They are like a palm tree made on a lathe, and they don’t speak.
They must be carried, because they can’t walk.
Don’t be afraid of them,
for they can’t do harm,
and they can’t do good either.”
[6] There is no one like you, LORD.
You are great,
and your name is great in power.
[7] Who would not fear you, King of the nations?
For you deserve it.
Among all the wise men of the nations,
and in all their kingdoms,
there is no one like you.
[8] But they are all stupid and foolish.
What they learn from idols is worthless.
It is just wood.
[9] Silver is beaten into thin plates and brought from Tarshish,
and gold from Uphaz.
It is the work of the carver and of the goldsmith’s hands.
Their clothing is blue and purple.
They are all the work of skillful men.
[10] But the LORD is the true God.
He is the living God, and a King forever.
When he is angry, the earth shakes.
The nations can’t stand up to his fury.
[11] “Say this to them:
‘The gods that have not made the heavens and the earth
will disappear from the earth, and from under the heavens.’”
[12] God has made the earth by his power.
He has set the world in place by his wisdom.
By his understanding he has stretched out the heavens.
[13] When he speaks, the waters in the heavens roar.
He makes the clouds rise from the ends of the earth.
He makes lightning for the rain,
and brings the wind out of his storehouses.
[14] Every person has become stupid and without knowledge.
Every goldsmith is put to shame by his carved image,
for his cast image is a lie,
and there is no breath in them.
[15] They are worthless, a work to be laughed at.
In the time of their punishment they will be destroyed.
[16] The portion of Jacob is not like these,
for he is the maker of all things,
and Israel is the tribe of his inheritance.
The LORD of Armies is his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: The Babylonians interpreted the stars and were afraid of signs in the sky like eclipses of the sun. God says: Don’t be afraid of them! I made the stars.
> Verses 3–5: Again mockery of the idols: A tree is cut down, carved, covered with gold and nailed down so that it does not fall over. It cannot speak and cannot walk. Other translations here compare the idol to a scarecrow in a cucumber field. “Don’t be afraid of them, for they can’t do harm, and they can’t do good either.”
> Verses 6–7: A song of praise: “There is no one like you, LORD.”
> Verse 10: “The LORD is the true God. He is the living God.” That is the opposite of the dead idols.
> Verse 11: This verse is written in Aramaic, not in Hebrew. Aramaic was the language that people understood at that time in Babylon and throughout the Near East. Probably the exiles were meant to be able to say this sentence to the Babylonians in their own language.
> Verses 12–13: God is the Creator. He makes rain, lightning and wind.
> Verse 16: Israel’s “portion,” that is, its treasure, is God himself, the Creator of everything.

## My wound is serious (Verses 17–25)

[17] Gather up your belongings from the land,
you who live under siege.
[18] For the LORD says,
“Look, this time I will throw out the people who live in the land, as with a sling.
I will bring distress on them,
so that they may feel it.”
[19] How terrible for me because of my injury!
My wound is serious.
But I said, “Truly, this is my suffering,
and I must bear it.”
[20] My tent is destroyed,
and all my ropes are broken.
My children have gone away from me,
and they are no more.
There is no one left to put up my tent again,
or to hang up my curtains.
[21] For the shepherds have become stupid,
and have not asked the LORD for advice.
That is why they have not succeeded,
and all their flocks are scattered.
[22] Listen, the news is coming:
a great noise from the land of the north,
to make the cities of Judah a waste,
a place where jackals live.
[23] LORD, I know that a person’s way is not in his own power.
It is not up to the one who walks to direct his steps.
[24] LORD, correct me, but gently,
not in your anger,
or you will make me nothing.
[25] Pour out your anger on the nations that don’t know you,
and on the families that don’t call on your name.
For they have eaten up Jacob.
Yes, they have eaten him up and destroyed him,
and have ruined his home.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: Pack your things! The exile is coming.
> Verses 19–20: Jerusalem speaks like a mother whose tent is destroyed and whose children are gone. “Truly, this is my suffering, and I must bear it.”
> Verse 21: The “shepherds,” the leaders of the people, did not ask God for advice. That is why the flock is scattered.
> Verse 23 is a well-known verse: “It is not up to the one who walks to direct his steps.” We do not have our life completely under control. We depend on God.
> Verse 24: A humble prayer: God, correct me, but gently, not in anger.
> Verse 25: A prayer that God’s anger may hit the nations that destroyed Israel. This is a cry of the oppressed for justice, as in some psalms (Psalm 79:6–7). It is addressed to God and leaves the judgment to him. It is not permission to do violence to other nations.
