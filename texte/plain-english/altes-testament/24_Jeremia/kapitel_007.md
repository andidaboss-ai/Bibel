# Jeremiah – Chapter 7
### The temple speech

---

## Don’t trust in lying words (Verses 1–15)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] “Stand in the gate of the LORD’s house,
and call out this word there. Say,
‘Hear the LORD’s word, all you people of Judah
who go in through these gates to worship the LORD.’”
[3] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says,
“Change your ways and your deeds,
and I will let you live in this place.
[4] Don’t trust in lying words that say,
‘This is the LORD’s temple, the LORD’s temple, the LORD’s temple.’
[5] For if you really change your ways and your deeds,
if you really do justice between one person and another,
[6] if you don’t oppress the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow,
and don’t shed innocent blood in this place,
and don’t follow other gods, which only harms you,
[7] then I will let you live in this place,
in the land that I gave to your ancestors,
from long ago and forever.
[8] Look, you trust in lying words that are of no use.
[9] Will you steal, murder, commit adultery, swear falsely,
burn incense to Baal,
and follow other gods that you have not known,
[10] and then come and stand in front of me in this house,
which is called by my name, and say, ‘We are safe,’
so that you can go on doing all these disgusting things?
[11] Has this house, which is called by my name,
become a den of robbers in your eyes?
Look, I myself have seen it,” says the LORD.
[12] “But go now to my place which was in Shiloh,
where I first made my name live,
and see what I did to it because of the evil of my people Israel.
[13] Now, you have done all these things,” says the LORD.
“I spoke to you again and again,
but you didn’t listen.
I called you, but you didn’t answer.
[14] That is why I will do to the house which is called by my name,
which you trust in,
and to the place which I gave to you and to your ancestors,
what I did to Shiloh.
[15] I will throw you out of my sight,
as I have thrown out all your brothers,
all the descendants of Ephraim.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is Jeremiah’s famous “temple speech.” He stands at the gate of the temple, where everyone goes in, and speaks there.
> Verse 4: People thought: As long as the temple stands, nothing can happen to us. After all, God lives here! Three times they say “the LORD’s temple” like a magic formula. Jeremiah says: These are lying words. A building does not protect you.
> Verses 5–7: What matters: doing justice, not oppressing foreigners, orphans and widows, not shedding innocent blood.
> Verses 9–10: The people break almost all of the Ten Commandments and then come into the temple and say: “We are safe!”
> Verse 11: “Has this house … become a den of robbers?” After their raids, robbers go back to their den, where they are safe. That is how people used the temple. Jesus quotes this verse when he drives the traders out of the temple (Mark 11:17).
> Verses 12–14: Shiloh was earlier the place where the ark of the covenant stood (1 Samuel 1–4). Shiloh was later destroyed. Jeremiah says: The same can happen to the temple in Jerusalem. Jeremiah was almost killed for this speech (Chapter 26).

## Don’t pray for this people (Verses 16–20)

[16] “So don’t pray for this people.
Don’t raise a cry or prayer for them,
and don’t plead with me for them,
for I will not hear you.
[17] Don’t you see what they do in the cities of Judah
and in the streets of Jerusalem?
[18] The children gather wood,
the fathers light the fire,
and the women knead the dough,
to make cakes for the queen of the sky.
And they pour out drink offerings to other gods,
to make me angry.
[19] Is it me they make angry?” says the LORD.
“Isn’t it themselves they hurt, to their own shame?”
[20] That is why the Lord GOD says:
“Look, my anger and my fury will be poured out on this place,
on people, on animals,
on the trees of the field, and on the fruit of the ground.
It will burn and will not be put out.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: A shocking sentence: God says to Jeremiah: Don’t pray for this people anymore. This shows how serious the situation is. Even so, Jeremiah later prayed and lamented for his people again and again.
> Verse 18: Whole families take part in idol worship: children, fathers and mothers. The “queen of the sky” was a goddess who was worshiped in the ancient Near East (probably Ishtar or Astarte). People baked cakes for her.
> Verse 19: Whoever leaves God harms above all themselves in the end.

## Obedience instead of sacrifice (Verses 21–28)

[21] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Add your burnt offerings to your sacrifices,
and eat the meat.
[22] For on the day that I brought your ancestors out of the land of Egypt,
I didn’t speak to them or command them about burnt offerings or sacrifices.
[23] But this is what I commanded them:
‘Listen to my voice,
and I will be your God,
and you will be my people.
Walk in all the way that I command you,
so that it may go well with you.’
[24] But they didn’t listen or pay attention.
They followed their own plans
and the stubbornness of their evil heart.
They went backward, and not forward.
[25] Since the day that your ancestors came out of the land of Egypt until today,
I have sent you all my servants the prophets,
sending them every day, again and again.
[26] Yet they didn’t listen to me or pay attention,
but they made their necks stiff.
They did worse than their ancestors.

[27] “You will speak all these words to them,
but they will not listen to you.
You will call to them,
but they will not answer you.
[28] Tell them,
‘This is the nation that has not listened to the voice of the LORD their God,
and has not accepted correction.
Truth has died.
It has disappeared from their mouth.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: An ironic sentence: You might as well eat your sacrifices yourselves; they mean nothing to me.
> Verses 22–23: God says: When I brought you out of Egypt, it was not sacrifices that mattered most to me, but that you listen to me. “I will be your God, and you will be my people.” That is the heart of the covenant. The laws about sacrifices came later and were not the most important thing.
> Verse 24: They “went backward, and not forward.” A strong picture.
> Verse 28: “Truth has died.” A sad judgment on a society.

## The valley of slaughter (Verses 29–34)

[29] Cut off your hair and throw it away.
Sing a song of mourning on the bare hilltops,
for the LORD has rejected and left
the generation that made him angry.
[30] “For the people of Judah have done what is evil in my eyes,” says the LORD.
“They have put their disgusting idols in the house which is called by my name,
to make it unclean.
[31] They have built the high places of Topheth,
which is in the valley of the son of Hinnom,
to burn their sons and their daughters in the fire.
I didn’t command it,
and it never came into my mind.
[32] So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when it will no longer be called ‘Topheth’
or ‘The valley of the son of Hinnom,’
but ‘The valley of Slaughter.’
For they will bury in Topheth
until there is no room left to bury.
[33] The dead bodies of this people will be food for the birds of the sky
and for the animals of the earth.
No one will scare them away.
[34] Then I will put an end in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem
to the sound of joy and the sound of gladness,
the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride,
for the land will become a ruin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29: Cutting off your hair was a sign of mourning.
> Verse 31: The most terrible thing: In the valley of the son of Hinnom near Jerusalem, some people burned their own children as sacrifices to foreign gods. God says very clearly: I never commanded this, “it never came into my mind.” Sacrificing children or doing violence to them is a crime that God hates.
> Verse 32: The valley becomes “The valley of Slaughter.” From the name “Ge-Hinnom” (valley of Hinnom) later came the word “Gehenna,” which in the New Testament refers to hell.
> Verse 34: No more weddings, no more joy, no more laughter. This is the picture of a completely destroyed city.
