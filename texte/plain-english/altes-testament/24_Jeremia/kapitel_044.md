# Jeremiah – Chapter 44
### The dispute about the queen of the sky

---

## Have you learned nothing? (Verses 1–14)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah
about all the Jews who lived in the land of Egypt,
who lived at Migdol, at Tahpanhes, at Memphis,
and in the region of Pathros. He said,
[2] “The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘You have seen all the disaster that I have brought on Jerusalem
and on all the cities of Judah.
Look, today they are in ruins,
and no one lives in them,
[3] because of the evil they did to provoke me to anger.
They went to burn incense and to serve other gods
whom they did not know,
neither they, nor you, nor your ancestors.
[4] Yet I sent all my servants the prophets to you,
early and tirelessly, to say,
“Oh, don’t do this detestable thing that I hate!”
[5] But they didn’t listen or pay attention.
They didn’t turn from their evil,
to stop burning incense to other gods.
[6] So my fury and my anger were poured out,
and burned in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem.
They have become ruins and wasteland, as they are today.’
[7] So now the LORD, the God of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘Why are you doing such great harm to yourselves?
You are cutting off from Judah man and woman,
child and nursing baby,
so that you leave no one remaining.
[8] You provoke me to anger with what your hands have made,
by burning incense to other gods in the land of Egypt,
where you have gone to live.
So you will be cut off,
and become a curse and a disgrace among all the nations of the earth.
[9] Have you forgotten the evil of your ancestors,
the evil of the kings of Judah,
the evil of their wives,
your own evil,
and the evil of your wives,
which they did in the land of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem?
[10] To this day they have not humbled themselves.
They have not feared me,
nor followed my law and my rules,
which I set before you and before your ancestors.’
[11] So the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘Look, I will set my face against you for disaster,
to cut off all Judah.
[12] I will take those who are left of Judah,
who were determined to go to the land of Egypt to live there,
and they will all come to an end.
They will fall in the land of Egypt.
They will come to an end by the sword and by famine.
They will die, from the least to the greatest,
by the sword and by famine.
They will become an object of horror and shock,
a curse and a disgrace.
[13] For I will punish those who live in the land of Egypt,
as I have punished Jerusalem,
by the sword, by famine, and by disease.
[14] None of those who are left of Judah,
who have gone to live in the land of Egypt,
will escape or survive to return to the land of Judah,
though they long to return and live there.
For no one will return except a few who escape.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> By now Jews were living in various places in Egypt: Migdol and Tahpanhes in the north, Memphis in the middle, and Pathros, that is, Upper Egypt in the south.
> Verses 2–6: Jeremiah reminds them: You saw how Jerusalem was destroyed. And why? Because of the worship of idols.
> Verse 4: A touching sentence from God: “Oh, don’t do this detestable thing that I hate!” God is almost pleading.
> Verses 7–10: And yet in Egypt they go on in exactly the same way. They have learned nothing.

---

## The people’s answer (Verses 15–19)

[15] Then all the men who knew that their wives burned incense to other gods,
and all the women standing there, a great crowd,
all the people who lived in Pathros in the land of Egypt,
answered Jeremiah. They said,
[16] “As for the message that you have spoken to us in the LORD’s name,
we will not listen to you.
[17] Instead, we will certainly do everything we have said we would do.
We will burn incense to the queen of the sky,
and pour out drink offerings to her,
as we used to do, we and our ancestors,
our kings and our leaders,
in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem.
For then we had plenty of food,
and were well off, and saw no disaster.
[18] But since we stopped burning incense to the queen of the sky,
and pouring out drink offerings to her,
we have lacked everything,
and have been coming to an end by the sword and by famine.”
[19] And the women said,
“When we burned incense to the queen of the sky,
and poured out drink offerings to her,
did we make cakes for her to worship her,
and pour out drink offerings to her,
without our husbands?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people’s answer is shockingly open: We will not listen to you!
> Verses 17–18: They have a completely different explanation for their misfortune: In the past, when we worshiped the queen of the sky, things went well for us. Since King Josiah banned this cult (2 Kings 23), things have gone badly for us. So stopping was to blame!
> This shows: The same events can be interpreted in completely different ways. Jeremiah says: The disaster came because of the worship of idols. The people say: It came because we stopped worshiping idols.
> Verse 19: The women stress: We didn’t do this alone; our husbands agreed. Men and women share the responsibility together. The text does not put the blame on the women alone.

## Jeremiah’s answer (Verses 20–30)

[20] Then Jeremiah said to all the people,
to the men and to the women,
to all the people who had given him this answer:
[21] “The incense that you burned in the cities of Judah
and in the streets of Jerusalem,
you and your ancestors, your kings and your leaders,
and the people of the land,
didn’t the LORD remember it?
Didn’t it come to his mind?
[22] The LORD could no longer bear
the evil of your deeds
and the detestable things you had done.
That is why your land has become a ruin,
an object of shock and a curse,
without anyone living in it, as it is today.
[23] You burned incense,
and you sinned against the LORD.
You did not obey the LORD’s voice,
nor follow his law, his rules, and his instructions.
That is why this disaster has happened to you, as it is today.”
[24] Jeremiah also said to all the people, including all the women,
“Hear the LORD’s word, all you people of Judah who are in the land of Egypt!
[25] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘You and your wives have said it with your mouths,
and done it with your hands.
You said, “We will surely carry out the vows we have made,
to burn incense to the queen of the sky,
and to pour out drink offerings to her.”
Then keep your vows, and carry out your vows!’
[26] So hear the LORD’s word, all you people of Judah who live in the land of Egypt:
‘Look, I have sworn by my great name,’ says the LORD,
‘that my name will never again be spoken
by the mouth of any man of Judah in all the land of Egypt,
saying, “As the Lord GOD lives.”
[27] Look, I am watching over them for disaster, and not for good.
All the men of Judah who are in the land of Egypt
will come to an end by the sword and by famine,
until they are all gone.
[28] Those who escape the sword
will return from the land of Egypt to the land of Judah,
but they will be few in number.
Then all who are left of Judah,
who have gone to live in the land of Egypt,
will know whose word will stand, mine or theirs.
[29] This will be the sign to you,’ says the LORD,
‘that I will punish you in this place,
so that you may know that my words against you
will surely come true for disaster.’
[30] The LORD says:
‘Look, I will hand Pharaoh Hophra, king of Egypt,
over to his enemies
and to those who want to kill him,
just as I handed Zedekiah, king of Judah,
over to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
his enemy, who wanted to kill him.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–23: Jeremiah disagrees: No, God remembered your worship of idols. That is why the disaster came.
> Verse 25: A bitter, ironic sentence from God: “Then keep your vows, and carry out your vows!” Anyone who will not listen, God lets go their own way.
> Verse 26: The Jews in Egypt will no longer use God’s name in their oaths, because they have turned away from him.
> Verse 28: The people from Judah will recognize “whose word will stand, mine or theirs.” Time will tell.
> Verse 30: As a sign, Jeremiah names this: Pharaoh Hophra will be overthrown. Hophra (in Greek, Apries) was in fact overthrown in an uprising around 570 BC.
> These are the last recorded words of Jeremiah. The Bible does not tell how he died.
