# Jeremiah – Chapter 9
### Whoever wants to boast

---

## Oh, if only my head were water! (Verses 1–11)

[1] Oh, if only my head were water,
and my eyes a spring of tears,
so that I could weep day and night
for those who were killed among the daughter of my people!
[2] Oh, if only I had a place to stay in the wilderness,
a shelter for travelers,
so that I could leave my people and go away from them!
For they are all adulterers,
a crowd of unfaithful people.
[3] “They bend their tongue like a bow to shoot lies.
They have grown strong in the land, but not for the truth.
For they go from one evil to the next,
and they don’t know me,” says the LORD.
[4] “Everyone, watch out for your neighbor,
and don’t trust any brother.
For every brother cheats,
and every neighbor goes around spreading lies.
[5] Friends deceive each other,
and will not speak the truth.
They have taught their tongue to speak lies.
They wear themselves out doing wrong.
[6] You live in the middle of deceit.
Because of their deceit, they refuse to know me,” says the LORD.
[7] That is why the LORD of Armies says,
“Look, I will melt them and test them,
for what else can I do with the daughter of my people?
[8] Their tongue is a deadly arrow.
It speaks deceit.
With his mouth a person speaks peacefully to his neighbor,
but in his heart he plans to ambush him.
[9] Shouldn’t I punish them for these things?” says the LORD.
“Shouldn’t I take revenge on a nation like this?
[10] I will weep and wail for the mountains,
and sing a song of mourning for the pastures of the wilderness,
because they are burned up, so that no one passes through.
No one hears the sound of the livestock.
Both the birds of the sky and the animals have fled.
They are gone.
[11] “I will make Jerusalem heaps of ruins,
a place where jackals live.
I will make the cities of Judah a waste,
with no one living in them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Note: In some Bibles, verse 1 of this chapter is the last verse of chapter 8 (8:23). That is why all the verses of chapter 9 are shifted by one there. Here we follow the numbering of the WEB, our English source text.
> Verse 1: Jeremiah wishes his head were full of water and his eyes a spring, so that he could weep enough over the many dead. That is why he is called the “weeping prophet.”
> Verse 2: At the same time, he would like most of all to run away, far away into the wilderness. Many people know what it is like to feel both at the same time: compassion and the wish to run away.
> Verses 3–8: No one can trust anyone anymore. Friends and brothers and sisters lie to each other. People speak in a friendly way but plan evil. Lies and slander destroy a community from the inside.
> Verse 10: God laments too. He weeps over the burned mountains and the empty pastures.

## Why is the land destroyed? (Verses 12–16)

[12] Who is wise enough to understand this?
To whom has the LORD spoken, so that he can explain it?
Why is the land ruined and burned up like a wilderness,
so that no one passes through?
[13] The LORD says,
“Because they have left my law, which I set before them,
and have not obeyed my voice or walked in my ways.
[14] Instead, they have followed the stubbornness of their own heart,
and the Baals, as their ancestors taught them.”
[15] That is why the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says,
“Look, I will feed this people with wormwood,
and give them poisoned water to drink.
[16] I will scatter them among nations
that neither they nor their ancestors have known.
I will send the sword after them,
until I have destroyed them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The big question: Why did all this happen?
> Verses 13–14: The answer: Because the people left God’s law and followed idols.
> Verse 15: Wormwood is a very bitter plant. Life becomes as bitter as wormwood.
> Verse 16: The exile is announced: being scattered among foreign nations.

## Call for the mourning women! (Verses 17–22)

[17] The LORD of Armies says,
“Think about it, and call for the mourning women to come.
Send for the most skillful women to come.
[18] Let them hurry and start wailing for us,
so that our eyes run with tears,
and our eyelids pour out water.
[19] For a sound of wailing is heard out of Zion:
‘How we are ruined!
We are deeply ashamed, because we have left the land,
because they have thrown down our homes.’”
[20] Yet hear the LORD’s word, you women.
Let your ears take in the word of his mouth.
Teach your daughters to wail.
Each of you, teach her neighbor a song of mourning.
[21] For death has climbed in through our windows.
It has come into our palaces,
to take away the children from the streets,
and the young men from the town squares.
[22] Say, “The LORD says,
‘The dead bodies of people will fall like manure on the open field,
and like grain left behind by the harvester.
No one will gather them.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: In the ancient Near East, there were mourning women. They were experts in grief and helped others to express their pain. God himself calls them: You must lament now!
> Verse 20: The women are to teach their daughters and neighbors to lament. Grief needs words and community.
> Verse 21: “Death has climbed in through our windows” like a thief. It takes away children and young people.
> These verses show: It is right to express grief and pain out loud. The Bible has a whole book full of laments. Whoever mourns may weep. And no one has to be alone with it: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## What to be proud of (Verses 23–26)

[23] The LORD says,
“Don’t let the wise man boast about his wisdom.
Don’t let the strong man boast about his strength.
Don’t let the rich man boast about his riches.
[24] But whoever boasts, let him boast about this:
that he understands and knows me,
that I am the LORD, who shows faithful love, justice, and righteousness on the earth,
for I delight in these things,” says the LORD.
[25] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will punish all those who are circumcised only in their body:
[26] Egypt, Judah, Edom, the people of Ammon, Moab,
and all who cut the hair at the sides of their head,
who live in the wilderness.
For all the nations are uncircumcised,
and the whole house of Israel is uncircumcised in heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–24 are among the most important sentences in Jeremiah: Don’t be proud of your cleverness, your strength or your money. If you want to be proud of something, be proud that you know God, the God who shows faithful love, justice and righteousness and delights in them. Righteousness means: living right before God.

 Paul takes this up: “He who boasts, let him boast in the Lord” (1 Corinthians 1:31).
> Verses 25–26: Many nations at that time were circumcised, not only Israel. Jeremiah says: The outward sign alone does no good. If someone has an “uncircumcised heart,” that is, a heart that is closed to God, the sign on the body does not help them either. This applies to all nations and also to Israel. Cutting the hair at the sides of the head was a hairstyle of certain Arab tribes.
