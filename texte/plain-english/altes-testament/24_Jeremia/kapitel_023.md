# Jeremiah – Chapter 23
### The LORD is our righteousness

---

## The bad shepherds and the righteous Branch (Verses 1–8)

[1] “How terrible for the shepherds
who destroy and scatter the sheep of my pasture!” says the LORD.
[2] That is why the LORD, the God of Israel,
says this against the shepherds who take care of my people:
“You have scattered my flock,
and driven them away,
and have not looked after them.
Look, I will punish you for the evil of your deeds,” says the LORD.
[3] “I myself will gather what is left of my flock
out of all the countries where I have driven them.
I will bring them back to their pastures,
and they will have many young and become many.
[4] I will set up shepherds over them who will take care of them.
They will no longer be afraid or terrified,
and none of them will be missing,” says the LORD.
[5] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will raise up for David a righteous Branch.
He will rule as king and act wisely,
and he will do justice and righteousness in the land.
[6] In his days Judah will be saved,
and Israel will live in safety.
This is the name by which he will be called:
The LORD our righteousness.
[7] “So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when people will no longer say,
‘As surely as the LORD lives,
who brought the people of Israel up out of the land of Egypt.’
[8] Instead they will say,
‘As surely as the LORD lives,
who brought up and led the descendants of the house of Israel
out of the land of the north,
and from all the countries where I had driven them.’
Then they will live in their own land.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: The “shepherds” are the kings and leaders. They did not take care of the people, but led them into disaster.
> Verses 3–4: God himself will be the good shepherd. He gathers the scattered sheep and gives them good shepherds, so that none of them is missing anymore.
> Verses 5–6: A great promise: From David’s family a “righteous Branch” will come, a king who rules wisely and justly. His name: “The LORD our righteousness” (in Hebrew: “Yahweh Tsidkenu”). Interesting: The last king was called Zedekiah, which means “My righteousness is the LORD.” But he did not live up to his name. The coming king will.
> In Judaism, this promise is applied to the Messiah who is still to come. Christians see it fulfilled in Jesus, the descendant of David.
> Verses 7–8: The return from exile will be a new exodus, greater than the one from Egypt (as already in 16:14–15).

## Against the false prophets (Verses 9–22)

[9] About the prophets:
My heart is broken inside me.
All my bones shake.
I am like a drunk man,
like a man who has had too much wine,
because of the LORD,
and because of his holy words.
[10] “For the land is full of adulterers.
Because of the curse, the land mourns.
The pastures of the wilderness have dried up.
They run on an evil course,
and they use their power wrongly.
[11] For both prophet and priest are godless.
Yes, even in my house I have found their evil,” says the LORD.
[12] “That is why their way will be like slippery paths in the dark.
They will be pushed and fall there.
For I will bring disaster on them
in the year of their punishment,” says the LORD.
[13] “I have seen foolish things among the prophets of Samaria.
They prophesied by Baal,
and led my people Israel astray.
[14] Among the prophets of Jerusalem I have also seen a horrible thing:
They commit adultery and live in lies.
They strengthen the hands of evil people,
so that no one turns away from his evil.
They have all become like Sodom to me,
and the people who live there like Gomorrah.”
[15] That is why the LORD of Armies says this about the prophets:
“Look, I will feed them with wormwood,
and make them drink poisoned water.
For from the prophets of Jerusalem,
godlessness has spread through the whole land.”
[16] The LORD of Armies says,
“Don’t listen to the words of the prophets who prophesy to you.
They fill you with empty hopes.
They speak a vision from their own heart,
and not from the mouth of the LORD.
[17] Again and again they say to those who despise me,
‘The LORD has said, “You will have peace.”’
And to everyone who follows the stubbornness of his own heart they say,
‘No disaster will come on you.’
[18] For who has stood in the council of the LORD,
so that he has seen and heard his word?
Who has paid attention to my word and heard it?
[19] Look, the LORD’s storm, his anger, has gone out.
Yes, a whirling storm!
It will burst on the head of the wicked.
[20] The LORD’s anger will not turn back
until he has done and carried out what his heart has planned.
In the last days you will understand it clearly.
[21] I didn’t send these prophets,
yet they ran.
I didn’t speak to them,
yet they prophesied.
[22] But if they had stood in my council,
then they would have let my people hear my words,
and would have turned them away from their evil way,
and from the evil of their deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Jeremiah is shaken. God’s words stir him up so much that he trembles like a drunk man.
> Verse 14: The prophets in Jerusalem themselves live immorally and encourage others to do evil. They are worse than the prophets of Baal in the north.
> Verses 16–17: How do you recognize false prophets? They say to people who despise God: “You will have peace.” And: “No disaster will come on you.” They confirm people in their wrong way.
> Verses 18 and 22: A true prophet has stood in the “council of the LORD,” that is, he has really listened to God. A true prophet calls people to turn back. A false prophet only calms people down.
> Verse 21: “I didn’t send these prophets, yet they ran.” Not everyone who speaks in God’s name has really been sent by God.

## God’s word is like a hammer (Verses 23–32)

[23] “Am I only a God who is near,” says the LORD,
“and not a God who is far away?
[24] Can anyone hide himself in secret places
so that I can’t see him?” says the LORD.
“Don’t I fill heaven and earth?” says the LORD.
[25] “I have heard what the prophets have said,
who prophesy lies in my name.
They say, ‘I had a dream! I had a dream!’
[26] How long will this go on in the heart of the prophets who prophesy lies,
the prophets who speak the deceit of their own heart?
[27] They plan to make my people forget my name
through their dreams, which they tell one another,
just as their fathers forgot my name because of Baal.
[28] The prophet who has a dream, let him tell his dream.
But the one who has my word, let him speak my word faithfully.
What does straw have to do with wheat?” says the LORD.
[29] “Isn’t my word like fire?” says the LORD,
“and like a hammer that breaks the rock into pieces?
[30] “So look, I am against the prophets,” says the LORD,
“who steal my words from one another.
[31] Look, I am against the prophets,” says the LORD,
“who use their own tongues, and say, ‘He says.’
[32] Look, I am against those who prophesy lying dreams,” says the LORD,
“who tell them, and lead my people astray
by their lies and by their empty bragging.
Yet I didn’t send them or command them.
They don’t help this people at all,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–24: God is not only a “God who is near,” whom people can shape to fit their wishes. He is also far away, great, and fills heaven and earth. No one can hide from him.
> Verses 25–28: The false prophets tell their dreams and claim they come from God. God says: A dream is a dream. But my word is something different. The difference is like the difference between straw and wheat: Straw looks similar, but it does not feed you.
> Verse 29 is a famous verse: God’s word is like fire that burns, and like a hammer that breaks rocks. It is not comfortable, but powerful. It can break open hard hearts.
> Verse 30: Some prophets simply “stole” the words of others and passed them off as their own message.

## The “message” of the LORD (Verses 33–40)

[33] “When this people, or a prophet, or a priest asks you,
‘What is the message from the LORD?’
then tell them,
‘“What message? I will throw you off,” says the LORD.’
[34] As for the prophet, the priest, and the people who say,
‘The message from the LORD,’
I will punish that man and his household.
[35] This is what each of you will say to his neighbor and to his brother:
‘What has the LORD answered?’
and, ‘What has the LORD said?’
[36] You will not mention ‘the message from the LORD’ anymore,
for each man’s own word has become his message.
For you have twisted the words of the living God,
of the LORD of Armies, our God.
[37] This is what you will say to the prophet:
‘What has the LORD answered you?’
and, ‘What has the LORD said?’
[38] But if you say, ‘The message from the LORD,’
then the LORD says:
‘Because you say this word, “The message from the LORD,”
even though I sent word to you, telling you not to say, “The message from the LORD,”
[39] look, I will completely forget you,
and I will throw you away from my presence,
together with the city that I gave to you and to your fathers.
[40] I will bring on you a disgrace that lasts forever,
and a shame that never ends,
which will not be forgotten.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> There is a play on words here in Hebrew: The word “massa” means both “message, saying” and “burden.” The WEB translates it as “message.” Many other Bibles translate it as “burden,” so that the play on words can be understood.
> People asked Jeremiah mockingly: “What is the LORD’s ‘message’ (or ‘burden’) today?” They made fun of his messages.
> God answers with the play on words: You ask about the “burden”? You yourselves are the burden! “I will throw you off.”
> Verses 35–36: Instead of talking mockingly about the “burden,” the people should seriously ask: “What has the LORD answered?” and, “What has the LORD said?” God’s word should be treated with respect, not as a joke.
