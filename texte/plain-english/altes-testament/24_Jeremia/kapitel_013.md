# Jeremiah – Chapter 13
### The ruined belt

---

## An acted-out sign with a belt (Verses 1–11)

[1] The LORD said to me,
“Go and buy yourself a linen belt.
Put it around your waist,
and don’t put it in water.”
[2] So I bought a belt, as the LORD had said,
and put it around my waist.
[3] The LORD’s word came to me a second time. He said,
[4] “Take the belt that you have bought, which is around your waist.
Get up, go to the Euphrates,
and hide it there in a crack in the rock.”
[5] So I went and hid it by the Euphrates,
as the LORD commanded me.
[6] After many days, the LORD said to me,
“Get up, go to the Euphrates,
and take from there the belt which I commanded you to hide there.”
[7] Then I went to the Euphrates and dug.
I took the belt from the place where I had hidden it.
And look, the belt was ruined.
It was good for nothing.
[8] Then the LORD’s word came to me. He said,
[9] “The LORD says,
‘In the same way I will ruin the pride of Judah,
and the great pride of Jerusalem.
[10] This evil people refuse to listen to my words.
They follow the stubbornness of their heart.
They have followed other gods to serve them and to worship them.
They will be like this belt,
which is good for nothing.
[11] For as the belt clings to a man’s waist,
so I made the whole house of Israel and the whole house of Judah cling to me,’ says the LORD,
‘so that they would be my people,
and bring me fame, praise, and glory.
But they would not listen.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah carries out an “acted-out sign”: He acts out God’s message so that people see it and do not only hear it.
> He wears a new linen belt, then hides it by the Euphrates. When he gets it again, it has rotted. (Some interpreters think that instead of the far-away Euphrates, a place called Parah near Anathoth is meant. In Hebrew both names sound the same.)
> Verse 11: The meaning: Israel was as closely connected to God as a belt is to the body. At that time, a belt was a beautiful, important piece of clothing. But through idol worship, the people have “rotted.” The Euphrates points to Babylon: That is where the people will be in exile.

## The wine jars (Verses 12–14)

[12] “So speak this word to them:
‘The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
“Every jar should be filled with wine.”’
They will say to you,
‘Don’t we know very well that every jar should be filled with wine?’
[13] Then tell them, ‘The LORD says,
“Look, I will fill all the people who live in this land with drunkenness:
the kings who sit on David’s throne,
the priests, the prophets,
and all the people of Jerusalem.
[14] I will smash them against each other,
the fathers and the sons together,” says the LORD.
“I will not pity them, or spare them, or have compassion on them.
Nothing will keep me from destroying them.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah says something that sounds like a drinking toast: “Every jar should be filled with wine.” The people answer: “Yes, of course, we know that!”
> But Jeremiah means it differently: The people themselves are like jars. They will be made “drunk,” that is: confused and without direction. Then they will smash each other like jars that bump into each other. A picture of quarreling and chaos that destroys a people from the inside.

## Give God glory before it gets dark (Verses 15–27)

[15] Listen, and pay attention.
Don’t be proud, for the LORD has spoken.
[16] Give glory to the LORD your God,
before he brings darkness,
and before your feet stumble on the dark mountains.
While you are looking for light,
he will turn it into the shadow of death,
and make it deep darkness.
[17] But if you will not listen,
I will weep in secret because of your pride.
My eyes will weep bitterly and run with tears,
because the LORD’s flock has been taken captive.
[18] Say to the king and to the queen mother,
“Make yourselves low.
Sit down,
for your crowns have fallen from your heads,
your beautiful crowns.
[19] The cities of the South are shut up,
and there is no one to open them.
Judah is carried away as captives, all of it.
They are completely carried away as captives.
[20] Lift up your eyes,
and look at those who come from the north.
Where is the flock that was given to you,
your beautiful flock?
[21] What will you say when he puts over you as rulers
those you yourself taught to be your friends?
Won’t pains seize you,
like a woman in labor?
[22] And if you say in your heart,
“Why have these things happened to me?”
It is because your sins are so great
that your skirts are lifted up,
and your heels suffer violence.
[23] Can the Ethiopian change his skin,
or the leopard its spots?
Then you could also do good,
you who are used to doing evil.
[24] “So I will scatter them like straw
that is blown away by the desert wind.
[25] This is your lot,
the share I have measured out to you,” says the LORD,
“because you have forgotten me,
and trusted in lies.”
[26] So I will also lift your skirts up over your face,
and your shame will be seen.
[27] I have seen your disgusting deeds,
your adulteries and your neighing,
your shameful prostitution
on the hills in the fields.
How terrible for you, Jerusalem!
You will not be made clean.
How much longer will it be?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Give God glory while it is still light! Like a hiker who has to get down from the mountains before it gets dark.
> Verse 17: Jeremiah will weep in secret if the people do not listen. He is not a prophet who is glad when others suffer.
> Verse 18: The king is probably Jehoiachin, the queen mother is his mother Nehushta. Both were carried off to Babylon in 597 BC (2 Kings 24:8–15).
> Verse 21: Judah had earlier looked to Babylon as an ally. Now Babylon becomes the ruler over Judah.
> Verses 22 and 26: Jerusalem is pictured as a woman who is publicly put to shame. This is how people at that time spoke about the humiliation of a conquered city. These pictures are a picture for a city, not a justification for putting women to shame or doing violence to them. If you experience violence: Domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 23: “Can the Ethiopian change his skin, or the leopard its spots?” The Hebrew word here is “Cushite.” The WEB has “Ethiopian.” Cush lay south of Egypt, and the people there had dark skin. All that is meant is: Something that is deeply rooted is not easy to change. The people are so used to evil that they can no longer change themselves. The verse says nothing bad about dark skin. In other places, the Bible speaks very respectfully of people from Cush, for example of Ebedmelech, who saves Jeremiah’s life (Chapter 38).
> Verse 27: “How much longer will it be?” God is still waiting for his people to become clean.
