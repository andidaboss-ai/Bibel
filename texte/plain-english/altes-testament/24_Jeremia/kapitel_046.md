# Jeremiah – Chapter 46
### Words about Egypt

---

## The battle of Carchemish (Verses 1–12)

[1] This is the LORD’s word which came to Jeremiah the prophet about the nations.
[2] About Egypt:
about the army of Pharaoh Necoh, king of Egypt,
which was by the Euphrates River at Carchemish,
and which Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, defeated
in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah.
[3] “Get your small shields and large shields ready,
and march out to battle!
[4] Harness the horses!
Mount up, you riders!
Take your positions with your helmets on!
Polish the spears!
Put on your armor!
[5] Why do I see this?
They are terrified and are turning back.
Their warriors are beaten down.
They flee in haste and don’t look back.
There is terror on every side,” says the LORD.
[6] “Don’t let the swift flee away,
or the warrior escape.
In the north, by the Euphrates River,
they have stumbled and fallen.
[7] Who is this who rises like the Nile,
like rivers whose waters surge?
[8] Egypt rises like the Nile,
like rivers whose waters surge.
Egypt says, ‘I will rise up.
I will cover the earth.
I will destroy cities and the people who live in them.’
[9] Charge, you horses!
Rush wildly, you chariots!
Let the warriors march out:
Cush and Put, who carry the shield,
and the Ludim, who handle and bend the bow.
[10] For that day belongs to the Lord, the LORD of Armies.
It is a day of vengeance,
when he takes vengeance on his enemies.
The sword will devour and be satisfied,
and will drink its fill of their blood.
For the Lord, the LORD of Armies, has a sacrifice
in the land of the north by the Euphrates River.
[11] Go up to Gilead and get balm,
virgin daughter of Egypt.
You use many medicines in vain.
There is no healing for you.
[12] The nations have heard of your shame.
The earth is full of your cry.
For warrior has stumbled against warrior,
and they both fall together.”

> **What does this mean?**
> With Chapter 46 the “words about the nations” begin (Chapters 46–51). God had, after all, “appointed you as a prophet to the nations” (1:5). God is not only the God of Israel, but Lord over the whole world.
> Verse 2: The battle of Carchemish on the Euphrates in the year 605 BC was one of the most important battles of the ancient world. The young Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptian army. After that, Babylon was the greatest power.
> Verses 3–6: A vivid picture: First the Egyptians proudly arm themselves for battle. Then they flee in panic.
> Verses 7–8: Egypt wanted to spread out like the Nile in flood.
> Verse 9: Cush, Put, and Lud were peoples from Africa who fought in the Egyptian army.
> Verse 10: “The sword will devour and be satisfied, and will drink its fill of their blood.” This is the harsh language of war of that time. It describes God’s judgment on an arrogant power. It is not a call to violence and not a justification of war.
> Verse 11: Like Judah (8:22), Egypt also finds no balm that heals.

## Nebuchadnezzar will come to Egypt (Verses 13–26)

[13] This is the word that the LORD spoke to Jeremiah the prophet,
about how Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, would come and strike the land of Egypt:
[14] “Announce it in Egypt,
proclaim it in Migdol,
and proclaim it in Memphis and in Tahpanhes.
Say, ‘Take your positions and get ready,
for the sword has devoured those around you.’
[15] Why are your strong ones swept away?
They didn’t stand,
because the LORD pushed them down.
[16] He made many stumble.
Yes, they fell over one another.
They said, ‘Get up!
Let’s go back to our own people,
to the land where we were born,
away from the oppressor’s sword.’
[17] There they cried out,
‘Pharaoh, king of Egypt, is only a noise.
He has let the right moment pass by.’
[18] As I live,” says the King,
whose name is the LORD of Armies,
“surely, like Tabor among the mountains,
and like Carmel by the sea,
so he will come.
[19] You daughter who lives in Egypt,
pack your bags to go into captivity!
For Memphis will become a wasteland.
It will be burned down,
without anyone living in it.
[20] Egypt is a very beautiful young cow,
but destruction from the north has come.
It has come.
[21] Even her hired soldiers among her
are like fattened calves,
for they too have turned back.
They have fled together.
They didn’t stand,
for the day of their disaster has come on them,
the time of their punishment.
[22] Her sound will go like a snake,
for the enemy will march with an army,
and come against her with axes,
like woodcutters.
[23] They will cut down her forest,” says the LORD,
“though it is impossible to get through,
because they are more numerous than locusts,
and cannot be counted.
[24] The daughter of Egypt will be put to shame.
She will be handed over to the people of the north.”
[25] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Look, I will punish Amon of No,
and Pharaoh, and Egypt,
with her gods and her kings,
Pharaoh and those who trust in him.
[26] I will hand them over to those who want to kill them,
to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
and to his officials.
Afterward it will be inhabited again, as in the days of old,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: The very cities to which the Jews had fled (Migdol, Tahpanhes, Memphis) are named.
> Verse 17: A mocking name for Pharaoh: “Only a noise,” a lot of talk, but nothing behind it.
> Verse 18: Nebuchadnezzar will come as surely as the mountains Tabor and Carmel stand out above the plain.
> Verse 25: “Amon of No”: Amon was the chief god of the city of No, which today is called Thebes (Luxor).
> Verse 26: At the end, a surprising hope even for Egypt: “Afterward it will be inhabited again, as in the days of old.” God’s judgment is not the last word, not even for other nations.

## Don’t be afraid, Jacob (Verses 27–28)

[27] “But don’t be afraid, Jacob my servant.
Don’t be terrified, Israel.
For look, I will save you from far away,
and save your descendants from the land of their captivity.
Jacob will come back,
and will be quiet and at ease.
No one will make him afraid.
[28] Don’t be afraid, Jacob my servant,” says the LORD,
“for I am with you.
I will make a complete end of all the nations where I have driven you,
but I will not make a complete end of you.
I will correct you with fairness,
but I will not leave you unpunished.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses appear almost word for word already in Jeremiah 30:10–11. In the middle of the words about the great world powers, God reminds his small people: Don’t be afraid! I am with you. The great powers pass away, but God’s people will remain.
