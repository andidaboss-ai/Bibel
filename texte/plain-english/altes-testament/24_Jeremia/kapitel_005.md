# Jeremiah – Chapter 5
### Is there anyone who does what is right?

---

## Look for one righteous person! (Verses 1–9)

[1] “Run back and forth through the streets of Jerusalem.
Look now, and find out.
Search in its town squares
to see if you can find one person,
if there is anyone who acts with justice,
who looks for the truth.
Then I will forgive the city.
[2] Even though they say, ‘As surely as the LORD lives,’
they certainly swear falsely.”
[3] LORD, don’t your eyes look for truth?
You have struck them, but they felt no pain.
You have crushed them, but they have refused to accept correction.
They have made their faces harder than a rock.
They have refused to turn back.
[4] Then I said, “These are only the poor.
They are foolish,
for they don’t know the LORD’s way,
or the law of their God.
[5] I will go to the important men and speak to them,
for they know the way of the LORD,
and the law of their God.”
But all of them together have broken the yoke
and torn off the chains.
[6] That is why a lion out of the forest will kill them.
A wolf of the evening will destroy them.
A leopard will lie in wait near their cities.
Everyone who goes out there will be torn to pieces,
because their rebellions are many,
and they have turned away more and more.
[7] “How can I forgive you?
Your children have left me,
and sworn by things that are no gods.
When I had fed them until they were full,
they committed adultery,
and crowded together at the houses of prostitutes.
[8] They were like well-fed horses running around.
Each one neighed after his neighbor’s wife.
[9] Shouldn’t I punish them for these things?” says the LORD.
“Shouldn’t I take revenge on such a nation as this?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A famous verse: God says: Search all of Jerusalem for one single person who is righteous and looks for the truth, then I will forgive the whole city. This is a reminder of Abraham, who bargained for Sodom (Genesis 18).
> Verses 4–5: At first Jeremiah thinks: The simple people don’t know any better. I will go to the educated and powerful. But it is exactly these people who have most ignored God’s commandments.
> Verse 6: Lion, wolf and leopard are pictures of the enemies who will come.
> Verses 7–8: There is unfaithfulness toward God, and also real unfaithfulness in marriages. God sees both. Faithfulness in marriage is a high value. If you experience violence or abuse in your marriage, you may get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233.
> Verse 9: “Revenge” here means that God restores justice. It is not permission for people to take revenge.

## They deny the LORD (Verses 10–19)

[10] “Go up on her walls and destroy,
but don’t make a complete end.
Take away her branches,
for they do not belong to the LORD.
[11] For the house of Israel and the house of Judah
have been very unfaithful to me,” says the LORD.
[12] They have denied the LORD and said,
“He won’t do anything.
Disaster won’t come on us.
We won’t see sword or famine.
[13] The prophets are nothing but wind,
and the word is not in them.
Let this happen to them.”
[14] That is why the LORD, the God of Armies, says,
“Because you speak this way,
look, I will make my words in your mouth fire,
and this people wood,
and it will burn them up.
[15] Look, I will bring a nation against you from far away, house of Israel,” says the LORD.
“It is a mighty nation.
It is an ancient nation,
a nation whose language you don’t know.
You don’t understand what they say.
[16] Their quiver is like an open grave.
They are all mighty warriors.
[17] They will eat up your harvest and your bread,
which your sons and your daughters should eat.
They will eat up your flocks and your herds.
They will eat up your vines and your fig trees.
With the sword they will break down your fortified cities,
the cities you trust in.
[18] “But even in those days,” says the LORD,
“I will not make a complete end of you.
[19] When you ask, ‘Why has the LORD our God done all these things to us?’
then you will say to them,
‘Just as you have left me and served foreign gods in your land,
so you will serve strangers in a land that is not yours.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The enemies will come, but God sets a limit: “Don’t make a complete end.”
> Verses 12–13: The people say: God won’t do anything to us. The prophets are only talking hot air.
> Verse 14: God answers: Jeremiah’s words will be like fire, and the people like wood.
> Verses 15–17: The foreign nation with the unknown language is the Babylonians. They will take away everything people need to live.
> Verse 18: Again the hope: “I will not make a complete end of you.” God leaves a remnant.
> Verse 19: Whoever serves foreign gods will have to serve foreigners. The punishment fits the guilt: the exile in Babylon.

## A people with eyes that do not see (Verses 20–31)

[20] “Announce this in the house of Jacob,
and make it known in Judah. Say:
[21] ‘Hear this now, you foolish people without understanding,
who have eyes, but don’t see,
who have ears, but don’t hear:
[22] Don’t you fear me?’ says the LORD.
‘Won’t you tremble in front of me?
I have placed the sand as the border of the sea,
by a law that lasts forever, so that it can’t cross it.
Even if its waves toss, they can’t win.
Even if they roar, they still can’t cross over it.’
[23] “But this people has a stubborn and rebellious heart.
They have turned away and gone off.
[24] They don’t say in their heart,
‘Let’s fear the LORD our God,
who gives rain, both the autumn rain and the spring rain, at the right time,
who keeps for us the weeks set for the harvest.’
[25] “Your wrongdoings have turned these things away.
Your sins have kept good things from you.
[26] For wicked men are found among my people.
They watch like bird hunters lying in wait.
They set a trap.
They catch people.
[27] As a cage is full of birds,
so their houses are full of deceit.
That is how they have become powerful and rich.
[28] They have grown fat.
They are sleek.
Yes, they go beyond all limits in doing evil.
They don’t plead the case of the orphan,
so that it may go well for him.
And they don’t defend the rights of the needy.
[29] “Shouldn’t I punish for these things?” says the LORD.
“Shouldn’t I take revenge on such a nation as this?
[30] “Something shocking and horrible has happened in the land.
[31] The prophets prophesy lies,
and the priests rule as they please,
and my people love it this way.
What will you do when the end comes?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: The people have eyes and ears, but they do not want to see or hear.
> Verse 22: Even the wild sea keeps to the border God has set for it. But God’s people keep to no border.
> Verse 24: No one thanks God for the rain and the harvest.
> Verses 26–28: Sharp social criticism: Rich people set traps for others like bird hunters. They become rich and fat through deceit. But they do not help orphans and the poor. God sees this. Injustice toward the weak matters to him.
> Verse 31: The worst thing: Prophets lie, priests do what they want, “and my people love it this way.” People would rather hear pleasant lies than uncomfortable truth.
