# Jeremiah – Chapter 38
### In the cistern

---

## Jeremiah is thrown into the cistern (Verses 1–6)

[1] Shephatiah, the son of Mattan,
Gedaliah, the son of Pashhur,
Jucal, the son of Shelemiah,
and Pashhur, the son of Malchijah,
heard the words that Jeremiah was speaking to all the people. He said,
[2] “The LORD says,
‘Whoever stays in this city will die by the sword, by famine, and by disease.
But whoever goes out to the Chaldeans will live.
He will escape with his life, and he will live.’
[3] The LORD says,
‘This city will surely be handed over to the army of the king of Babylon,
and he will capture it.’”
[4] Then the princes said to the king,
“Please have this man put to death.
For by speaking such words,
he is making the soldiers who are left in this city lose courage,
and all the people too.
This man is not looking for the good of this people,
but for their harm.”
[5] King Zedekiah said,
“Look, he is in your hands,
for the king can’t do anything against you.”
[6] Then they took Jeremiah
and threw him into the cistern of Malchijah, the king’s son,
which was in the courtyard of the guard.
They let Jeremiah down with ropes.
There was no water in the cistern, only mud,
and Jeremiah sank into the mud.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: The princes say: Jeremiah is destroying the soldiers’ courage. He is a traitor. In times of war, uncomfortable voices are often called traitors.
> Verse 5: King Zedekiah is so weak that he simply hands Jeremiah over: “Look, he is in your hands, for the king can’t do anything against you.”
> Verse 6: Jeremiah is thrown into an empty cistern, a deep hole in the rock in which rainwater was collected. At the bottom there is only mud. Jeremiah sinks into it. They leave him there to die, without laying a hand on him themselves.

## Ebedmelech saves Jeremiah (Verses 7–13)

[7] Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, a court official, was in the king’s palace.
He heard that they had put Jeremiah into the cistern.
At that time the king was sitting in the Benjamin Gate.
[8] Ebedmelech went out of the king’s palace,
and spoke to the king. He said,
[9] “My lord the king,
these men have done evil in everything they have done to Jeremiah the prophet.
They have thrown him into the cistern.
He will die of hunger where he is,
for there is no more bread in the city.”
[10] Then the king commanded Ebedmelech the Ethiopian:
“Take thirty men from here with you,
and pull Jeremiah the prophet up out of the cistern before he dies.”
[11] So Ebedmelech took the men with him,
and went into the king’s palace, to a room under the treasury.
From there he took old rags and worn-out clothes,
and let them down with ropes into the cistern to Jeremiah.
[12] Ebedmelech the Ethiopian said to Jeremiah,
“Put these old rags and worn-out clothes under your armpits,
under the ropes.”
Jeremiah did so.
[13] So they pulled Jeremiah up with the ropes,
and lifted him out of the cistern.
And Jeremiah stayed in the courtyard of the guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> The hero of this story is Ebedmelech. His name means “servant of the king.” He is a Cushite, that is, an African from the land south of Egypt (the WEB has “the Ethiopian”). He is a foreigner and a eunuch, that is, a court official, who often had little respect. Yet he is the one who has the courage that the king and the princes do not have.
> Verse 9: He goes straight to the king and says publicly: What these men have done is evil!
> Verses 11–12: A beautiful detail: Ebedmelech thinks of everything. He gets old rags so that the ropes will not cut into Jeremiah’s armpits. He does not only save his life; he is also caring and gentle.
> Ebedmelech shows: Humanity and courage do not depend on where you come from, the color of your skin or your position. God rewards him for it later (39:15–18).

## A secret conversation with the king (Verses 14–28)

[14] Then King Zedekiah sent for Jeremiah the prophet,
and had him brought to him at the third entrance of the LORD’s house.
The king said to Jeremiah,
“I will ask you something.
Don’t hide anything from me.”
[15] Then Jeremiah said to Zedekiah,
“If I tell you, won’t you surely put me to death?
And if I give you advice, you will not listen to me.”
[16] So King Zedekiah swore secretly to Jeremiah. He said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, who gave us our life,
I will not put you to death,
and I will not hand you over to these men who want to kill you.”
[17] Then Jeremiah said to Zedekiah,
“The LORD, the God of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘If you will surrender to the princes of the king of Babylon,
then your life will be spared,
and this city will not be burned with fire.
You and your family will live.
[18] But if you will not surrender to the princes of the king of Babylon,
then this city will be handed over to the Chaldeans.
They will burn it with fire,
and you won’t escape out of their hand.’”
[19] King Zedekiah said to Jeremiah,
“I am afraid of the Jews who have deserted to the Chaldeans.
They might hand me over to them,
and they will make fun of me.”
[20] But Jeremiah said, “They won’t hand you over.
Please obey the LORD’s voice in what I am telling you.
Then it will go well with you,
and your life will be spared.
[21] But if you refuse to surrender,
this is what the LORD has shown me:
[22] ‘Look, all the women who are left in the palace of the king of Judah
will be brought out to the princes of the king of Babylon.
Those women will say,
“Your close friends have deceived you,
and they have won against you.
Now that your feet are sunk in the mud,
they have turned away from you.”
[23] They will bring out all your wives and your children to the Chaldeans.
You won’t escape out of their hand,
but you will be captured by the king of Babylon.
And you will be the cause of this city being burned with fire.’”
[24] Then Zedekiah said to Jeremiah,
“Don’t let anyone know about these words,
and you won’t die.
[25] But if the princes hear that I have talked with you,
and they come to you and say to you,
‘Tell us now what you said to the king.
Don’t hide it from us, and we will not put you to death.
Also tell us what the king said to you,’
[26] then tell them,
‘I was begging the king
not to send me back to Jonathan’s house to die there.’”
[27] Then all the princes came to Jeremiah and asked him,
and he told them exactly what the king had commanded.
So they stopped questioning him,
for no one had heard the conversation.
[28] So Jeremiah stayed in the courtyard of the guard
until the day that Jerusalem was captured.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–16: Once more a secret meeting. The king wants to hear the truth, but Jeremiah is careful: Will you kill me? Will you even listen?
> Verses 17–18: Jeremiah’s advice is clear: Surrender, then you will save your life, your family and the city.
> Verse 19: Zedekiah’s real problem is fear, fear of mockery and humiliation. He knows what would be right, but he does not have the courage to do it.
> Verse 22: A mocking song: “Your feet are sunk in the mud.” That is bitter: Jeremiah really had sunk into the mud. Now the king himself will be “stuck in the mud.”
> Verses 24–27: The king is more afraid of his own princes than of God. Jeremiah tells the princes only part of the truth to protect his life.
