# Jeremiah – Chapter 49
### Words about Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar, and Elam

---

## About Ammon (Verses 1–6)

[1] About the people of Ammon.
The LORD says:
“Has Israel no sons?
Has he no heir?
Why then has Malcam taken over Gad,
and why do his people live in its cities?
[2] So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will make the battle cry heard
against Rabbah of the people of Ammon.
It will become a heap of ruins,
and its villages will be burned with fire.
Then Israel will take over the land of those who took over his land,”
says the LORD.
[3] “Wail, Heshbon, for Ai is laid waste!
Cry out, you villages of Rabbah!
Put on sackcloth.
Mourn, and run back and forth among the walls,
for Malcam will go into captivity,
his priests and his leaders together.
[4] Why do you boast about your valleys,
your valley overflowing with plenty,
you unfaithful daughter?
You trusted in your treasures, saying,
‘Who will come to me?’
[5] Look, I will bring terror on you,”
says the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
“from all who are around you.
Each of you will be driven out headlong,
and no one will gather the refugees.
[6] But afterward I will turn around the fate of the people of Ammon,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ammon lay east of the Jordan, around its capital Rabbah (today Amman, the capital of Jordan).
> Verse 1: Malcam (also Milcom) was the god of the Ammonites. When the northern kingdom of Israel was conquered by Assyria, the Ammonites took over the territory of the tribe of Gad. God asks: Does Israel have no heirs left?
> Verse 4: Ammon was proud of its fertile valleys and felt safe: “Who will come to me?”
> Verse 6: Here too, at the end: God will turn around the fate of Ammon. His mercy is also for foreign peoples.

## About Edom (Verses 7–22)

[7] About Edom.
The LORD of Armies says:
“Is there no more wisdom in Teman?
Has good advice disappeared from the wise?
Has their wisdom vanished?
[8] Flee! Turn back!
Hide in deep places, you who live in Dedan!
For I will bring disaster on Esau
when I punish him.
[9] If grape pickers came to you,
wouldn’t they leave a few grapes?
If thieves came by night,
wouldn’t they steal only as much as they wanted?
[10] But I have stripped Esau bare.
I have uncovered his hiding places,
and he will not be able to hide.
His descendants are destroyed,
and his relatives and his neighbors,
and he is no more.
[11] Leave your fatherless children.
I will keep them alive.
And let your widows trust in me.”
[12] For the LORD says:
“Look, those who did not deserve to drink the cup
will certainly drink it.
Are you the one who will go completely unpunished?
You won’t go unpunished,
but you will surely drink it.
[13] For I have sworn by myself,” says the LORD,
“that Bozrah will become an object of shock and disgrace,
a ruin and a curse.
All its cities will be ruins forever.”
[14] I have heard news from the LORD.
A messenger has been sent among the nations to say,
“Gather together, and come against her!
Get up for battle!”
[15] “For look, I have made you small among the nations,
and despised among people.
[16] The terror you cause
and the pride of your heart have deceived you,
you who live in the clefts of the rock,
who hold the height of the hill.
Even if you build your nest as high as the eagle,
I will bring you down from there,” says the LORD.
[17] “Edom will become an object of horror.
Everyone who passes by will be shocked,
and will hiss at all its wounds.
[18] As when Sodom and Gomorrah and the towns around them were overthrown,”
says the LORD,
“no one will live there,
and no human being will stay there.
[19] Look, someone will come up like a lion
from the thickets of the Jordan
against the strong pasture.
For I will suddenly make Edom run away from it,
and I will appoint over it whoever I choose.
For who is like me?
Who can summon me?
What shepherd can stand against me?”
[20] So hear the plan that the LORD has made against Edom,
and what he has decided against those who live in Teman:
Surely the little ones of the flock will be dragged away.
Surely he will make their pasture a wasteland because of them.
[21] The earth trembles at the noise of their fall.
There is a cry!
Its noise is heard at the Red Sea.
[22] Look, the enemy will rise and swoop down like an eagle,
and spread out his wings over Bozrah.
On that day the hearts of Edom’s warriors
will be like the heart of a woman in labor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Edom lay south of the Dead Sea. The Edomites were descendants of Esau, Jacob’s brother. Teman and Bozrah were important cities. Edom was famous for its wisdom (compare Job 2:11) and for its mountain fortresses in the rocks.
> Verses 9–10: Even grape pickers and thieves leave something behind. But in Edom nothing will be left.
> Verse 11 is a surprisingly tender verse in the middle of the judgment: “Leave your fatherless children. I will keep them alive. And let your widows trust in me.” God cares for the weakest, even among an enemy people.
> Verse 16: Edom felt as safe in its rock cities as an eagle in its nest. But pride deceives.
> Many verses here are similar to the small book of Obadiah, which is entirely about Edom.

## About Damascus (Verses 23–27)

[23] About Damascus.
“Hamath and Arpad are confused,
for they have heard bad news.
They melt away in fear.
There is worry like a restless sea
that cannot be calm.
[24] Damascus has grown weak.
She turns to flee,
and trembling has seized her.
Pain and anguish have taken hold of her,
like a woman giving birth.
[25] How is it that the city of praise is not abandoned,
the city of my joy?
[26] So her young men will fall in her streets,
and all her soldiers will be silenced on that day,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[27] “I will set fire to the wall of Damascus,
and it will burn up the palaces of Ben Hadad.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Damascus was the capital of Syria (Aram). Hamath and Arpad were cities in the north.
> Verse 25: Damascus is called the “city of praise” and the “city of my joy,” a beautiful, famous city.
> Verse 27: Ben Hadad was a common name of the kings of Damascus.
> Sadly, Damascus has also been struck by war and suffering in our own time. The Bible takes the suffering of all cities seriously.

## About Kedar and Hazor (Verses 28–33)

[28] About Kedar and the kingdoms of Hazor,
which Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, defeated.
The LORD says:
“Get up, go up against Kedar,
and destroy the people of the east.
[29] They will take their tents and their flocks.
They will carry off their tent curtains,
all their goods, and their camels.
They will shout to them,
‘Terror on every side!’
[30] Flee! Run far away!
Hide in deep places, you who live in Hazor,” says the LORD,
“for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, has made a plan against you,
and has decided to attack you.
[31] Get up! Go up against a nation that is at ease,
that lives without worry,” says the LORD,
“that has neither gates nor bars,
that lives alone.
[32] Their camels will be taken as loot,
and their many cattle as plunder.
I will scatter to every wind
those who cut the corners of their hair,
and I will bring disaster on them from every side,” says the LORD.
[33] “Hazor will become a home for jackals,
a wasteland forever.
No one will live there,
and no human being will stay there.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Kedar was a tribe of Arab nomads with tents and camels. Hazor here is probably a name for settlements in the Arabian desert, not the well-known city in Galilee.
> Verse 31: These people lived peacefully, without walls and gates. They felt safe because they were far away. But Babylon reached them too.

## About Elam (Verses 34–39)

[34] This is the LORD’s word that came to Jeremiah the prophet about Elam,
at the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah, king of Judah. He said,
[35] “The LORD of Armies says:
‘Look, I will break the bow of Elam,
the source of their strength.
[36] I will bring against Elam the four winds
from the four corners of the sky,
and will scatter them to all those winds.
There will be no nation
where the refugees from Elam will not go.
[37] I will make Elam terrified before their enemies,
and before those who want to kill them.
I will bring disaster on them,
my burning anger,’ says the LORD.
‘I will send the sword after them,
until I have finished them off.
[38] I will set my throne in Elam,
and destroy its king and leaders there,’ says the LORD.
[39] ‘But in the last days I will turn around the fate of Elam,’
says the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Elam lay far to the east, in today’s Iran. The Elamites were famous as archers.
> Verse 38: “I will set my throne in Elam”: God is Lord even over the most distant lands.
> Verse 39: And again at the end: God will “turn around the fate of Elam.” In the Acts of the Apostles (2:9), “Elamites” are also present at Pentecost, hearing God’s great deeds in their own language.
> Altogether, Chapter 49 shows: God is the Lord of all nations. He judges pride and injustice everywhere, but he also promises foreign nations a future (Verses 6 and 39).
