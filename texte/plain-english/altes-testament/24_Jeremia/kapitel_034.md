# Jeremiah – Chapter 34
### The broken promise of freedom

---

## A word to King Zedekiah (Verses 1–7)

[1] This is the word which came to Jeremiah from the LORD,
when Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
with all his army,
and all the kingdoms of the earth that were under his rule,
and all the peoples,
were fighting against Jerusalem and against all its cities:
[2] “The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Go and speak to Zedekiah, king of Judah, and tell him,
The LORD says,
“Look, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon,
and he will burn it with fire.
[3] You won’t escape out of his hand,
but you will surely be captured and handed over to him.
You will see the king of Babylon with your own eyes,
and he will speak with you face to face.
You will go to Babylon.”’
[4] “Yet hear the LORD’s word, Zedekiah, king of Judah.
The LORD says this about you:
‘You won’t die by the sword.
[5] You will die in peace.
As people burned spices in honor of your fathers,
the earlier kings who came before you,
so they will burn spices in your honor.
They will mourn for you and say, “Oh, lord!”
For I have spoken the word,’ says the LORD.”
[6] Then Jeremiah the prophet spoke all these words to Zedekiah, king of Judah, in Jerusalem,
[7] while the army of the king of Babylon was fighting against Jerusalem,
and against all the cities of Judah that were left,
against Lachish and against Azekah.
For these were the only fortified cities of Judah that were left.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3: Again Jeremiah tells King Zedekiah: The city will fall, and you will be taken prisoner.
> Verses 4–5: But Jeremiah also has a gentle word for him: Zedekiah will not die by the sword, but in peace, with an honorable burial. Burning spices was a custom of honor at the burial of kings (2 Chronicles 16:14).
> Verse 7: Only three fortified cities were still holding out: Jerusalem, Lachish and Azekah. In Lachish, archaeologists have found pieces of pottery with letters from this time. In one of them an officer writes that the fire signals of Azekah can no longer be seen.

---

## The slaves are set free – and taken back (Verses 8–22)

[8] The word came to Jeremiah from the LORD
after King Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people who were in Jerusalem,
to announce freedom to them:
[9] Everyone was to set free his male and female slaves
who were Hebrews, men or women,
so that no one would keep a fellow Jew as a slave.
[10] All the princes and all the people who had entered into the covenant obeyed.
Everyone set free his male and female slaves,
so that no one would keep them as slaves anymore.
They obeyed and let them go.
[11] But afterward they changed their minds.
They brought back the male and female slaves whom they had set free,
and forced them to be slaves again.
[12] That is why the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah from the LORD. He said,
[13] “The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘I made a covenant with your fathers
on the day that I brought them out of the land of Egypt,
out of the house of slavery. I said:
[14] At the end of seven years, each of you must set free his fellow Hebrew
who has been sold to you and has served you for six years.
You must let him go free.
But your fathers didn’t listen to me or pay attention.
[15] Now you had turned back,
and had done what is right in my eyes,
by each of you announcing freedom to his neighbor.
You had made a covenant in front of me
in the house which is called by my name.
[16] But then you turned around and dishonored my name.
Each of you brought back his male and female slaves,
whom you had set free to go wherever they wanted.
You forced them to be your slaves again.’”
[17] That is why the LORD says:
“You have not listened to me
and announced freedom, each to his brother and to his neighbor.
Look, now I announce a ‘freedom’ for you,” says the LORD,
“freedom for the sword, for disease, and for famine.
I will make you a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth.
[18] I will hand over the men who have broken my covenant,
who have not kept the words of the covenant which they made in front of me,
when they cut the calf in two and walked between its halves:
[19] the princes of Judah, the princes of Jerusalem,
the court officials, the priests, and all the people of the land,
who walked between the halves of the calf.
[20] I will give them into the hand of their enemies,
and into the hand of those who want to kill them.
Their dead bodies will be food for the birds of the sky
and for the animals of the earth.
[21] “I will give Zedekiah, king of Judah, and his princes
into the hands of their enemies,
into the hands of those who want to kill them,
and into the hands of the army of the king of Babylon,
which has withdrawn from you.
[22] Look, I will give the command,” says the LORD,
“and bring them back to this city.
They will fight against it, capture it, and burn it with fire.
I will make the cities of Judah a waste,
with no one living in them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–10: During the siege, King Zedekiah had all Hebrew slaves set free. At that time, someone who could not pay his debts could sell himself as a servant. According to God’s law, he had to be set free after six years (Exodus 21:2; Deuteronomy 15:12).
> Verse 11: But then the Babylonians withdrew for a short time (compare verse 21; 37:5), and the rich simply took their slaves back. A broken promise as soon as the danger seemed to be over.
> Verse 17: God answers with a bitter play on words: You have not announced “freedom”? Then I will announce a “freedom” for you, namely for the sword, disease and famine.
> Verse 18: When a solemn covenant was made, an animal was cut in two and people walked between the halves. That meant: May the same happen to me if I break the covenant (compare Genesis 15:9–17).
> Important: The Bible shows here that God is on the side of those who are not free, and that people must not be exploited. Today we know: No human being may belong to another. Human trafficking and forced labor still exist today. If you are affected by it or notice something, you can go to the police: In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
