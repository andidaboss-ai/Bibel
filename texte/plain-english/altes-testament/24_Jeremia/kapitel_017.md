# Jeremiah – Chapter 17
### Like a tree by the water

---

## Sin is written on the heart (Verses 1–4)

[1] “The sin of Judah is written with an iron pen,
and with the point of a diamond.
It is carved on the tablet of their heart,
and on the horns of your altars.
[2] Even their children remember their altars and their Asherah poles
by the green trees on the high hills.
[3] My mountain in the open country,
I will give away your wealth and all your treasures as plunder,
and your high places too,
because of the sin throughout all your territory.
[4] You yourself will lose your inheritance
that I gave you.
I will make you serve your enemies
in a land which you don’t know,
for you have lit a fire by my anger
which will burn forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Sin is not just on the surface. It is deeply carved, as if with a diamond into stone, on the “tablet of their heart.” This is a reminder of the tablets of the law from Sinai. Instead of God’s commandments, sin is written in the heart. (In Jeremiah 31:33 God promises to write his law on the heart in a new way.)
> Verse 2: Even the children know the idol altars. The Asherah poles were wooden poles for the goddess Asherah.

---

## Cursed and blessed (Verses 5–11)

[5] The LORD says:
“Cursed is the man who trusts in human beings,
who relies on human strength,
and whose heart turns away from the LORD.
[6] For he will be like a bush in the desert.
He will not see it when good comes.
He will live in the dry places in the wilderness,
in a salty land where no one lives.
[7] “Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD,
and whose confidence is in the LORD.
[8] For he will be like a tree planted by the water,
that spreads out its roots by the stream.
It will not be afraid when heat comes.
Its leaves will stay green.
It will not worry in the year of drought.
It will not stop producing fruit.
[9] The heart is deceitful above all things,
and it is very sick.
Who can understand it?
[10] “I, the LORD, search the mind.
I test the heart,
to give each person what his ways deserve,
what the fruit of his deeds deserves.”
[11] Like the partridge that sits on eggs which she has not laid,
so is the one who gets rich in the wrong way.
In the middle of his life his riches will leave him.
At his end, he will be a fool.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–8 are among the most beautiful texts in Jeremiah. They are similar to Psalm 1.
> Whoever trusts only in people and in their own strength is like a dry bush in the desert.
> But whoever trusts in God is like a tree by a stream. Its roots reach down to the water. Even when heat and drought come, its leaves stay green. It always produces fruit.
> This does not mean that you may not trust other people. It means: Your deepest support in life should be God, not people or power.
> Verse 9: “The heart is deceitful.” We humans can deceive ourselves. Often we do not fully know our own heart.
> Verse 10: But God knows it. He sees into the depths. Where the WEB, our English source text, says “mind,”
 the Hebrew says “kidneys.” At that time, the kidneys were seen as the place of the feelings.
> Verse 11: People at that time believed that the partridge sometimes hatches other birds’ eggs. When the chicks hatch, they run away. It is the same with riches gained wrongly: They do not stay.

## Heal me, LORD! (Verses 12–18)

[12] A glorious throne, set on high from the beginning,
is the place of our sanctuary.
[13] LORD, the hope of Israel,
all who leave you will be put to shame.
Those who turn away from me will be written in the dust,
because they have left the LORD,
the spring of living water.
[14] Heal me, LORD, and I will be healed.
Save me, and I will be saved,
for you are the one I praise.
[15] Look, they ask me,
“Where is the LORD’s word?
Let it come true now!”
[16] As for me, I have not run away from being a shepherd who follows you.
I haven’t wanted the day of disaster.
You know it.
What came out of my lips was in front of you.
[17] Don’t be a terror to me.
You are my refuge in the day of disaster.
[18] Let those who persecute me be put to shame,
but don’t let me be put to shame.
Let them be terrified,
but don’t let me be terrified.
Bring on them the day of disaster,
and destroy them with double destruction.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Whoever leaves God will be “written in the dust,” that is, quickly forgotten, like writing in the sand. Once Jesus wrote with his finger in the dust when people wanted to stone a woman (John 8:6–8). Some interpreters see this as a reference to this verse.
> Verse 14: A prayer that many people pray in sickness and trouble: “Heal me, LORD, and I will be healed. Save me, and I will be saved.”
> Verse 15: The people mock: Where is the disaster you keep announcing?
> Verse 16: Jeremiah says: I did not wish for the disaster. I only said what God told me to say.
> Verse 18: Jeremiah asks God to punish his persecutors. He is hurt and angry and says so openly in prayer. But he leaves it to God and does not take revenge into his own hands.

## Keep the Sabbath holy (Verses 19–27)

[19] The LORD said this to me:
“Go and stand in the People’s Gate,
through which the kings of Judah come in and go out,
and in all the gates of Jerusalem.
[20] Tell them, ‘Hear the LORD’s word,
you kings of Judah, all Judah,
and all the people of Jerusalem
who come in through these gates.
[21] The LORD says,
“Be careful,
and carry no load on the Sabbath day,
and don’t bring it in through the gates of Jerusalem.
[22] Don’t carry a load out of your houses on the Sabbath day.
Don’t do any work,
but keep the Sabbath day holy,
as I commanded your ancestors.
[23] But they didn’t listen or pay attention.
They made their necks stiff,
so that they would not hear
and would not accept correction.
[24] If you really listen to me,” says the LORD,
“and bring no load through the gates of this city on the Sabbath day,
but keep the Sabbath day holy,
and do no work on it,
[25] then kings and princes who sit on David’s throne
will come in through the gates of this city,
riding in chariots and on horses,
they and their princes,
the men of Judah and the people of Jerusalem.
And this city will be lived in forever.
[26] People will come from the cities of Judah,
and from the places around Jerusalem,
from the land of Benjamin,
from the lowland, from the hill country, and from the South.
They will bring burnt offerings, sacrifices, grain offerings, and incense,
and they will bring thank offerings to the LORD’s house.
[27] But if you will not listen to me,
to keep the Sabbath day holy,
and not to carry a load and come in through the gates of Jerusalem on the Sabbath day,
then I will set its gates on fire,
and the fire will burn up the palaces of Jerusalem.
It will not be put out.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah is to speak at the city gates about the Sabbath. On the Sabbath, the seventh day of the week, no one was supposed to work. The traders were not supposed to bring goods into the city.
> The Sabbath is a gift: a day of rest for everyone, also for male and female servants and for animals (Deuteronomy 5:14). Whoever ignores it shows that money and business are more important to them than God and people.
> Verses 24–26: If the people keep the Sabbath, Jerusalem will be lived in forever, and people from all regions will come to the temple.
> Verse 27: If not, fire will destroy the city. That happened in the year 587 BC.
