# Jeremiah – Chapter 2
### The spring of living water

---

## I remember the love of your youth (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD spoke his word to me.
He said,
[2] “Go and call out so that the people of Jerusalem hear it.
Say: ‘This is what the LORD says:
“I remember how kind you were when you were young.
I remember how you loved me as a bride.
You followed me in the wilderness,
in a land where no one planted seeds.
[3] Israel was holy to the LORD.
Israel was like the first part of his harvest.
Everyone who eats it up will be held guilty.
Disaster will come on them,”’ says the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God remembers the beginning with his people, the journey through the wilderness after the exodus from Egypt. Back then, Israel was like a young bride who follows her bridegroom everywhere, even into the wilderness.
> Verse 3: Israel was holy to God, like the first fruits of the harvest that belonged to God. Whoever attacked Israel became guilty.
> This loving memory is the background for the complaint that follows: What has become of this love?

---

## My people have left me (Verses 4–13)

[4] House of Jacob, hear the word of the LORD!
All you families of the house of Israel, hear it!
[5] This is what the LORD says:
“What wrong did your ancestors find in me?
Why did they go so far away from me?
They followed worthless idols.
And they became worthless themselves.
[6] They did not ask,
‘Where is the LORD?
He brought us up out of the land of Egypt.
He led us through the wilderness,
through a land of deserts and pits,
through a land of drought and deep darkness,
through a land where no one travels
and where no one lives.’
[7] I brought you into a rich land.
You could eat its fruit and its good things.
But when you came in, you made my land unclean.
You made the land I gave you disgusting.
[8] The priests did not ask, ‘Where is the LORD?’
Those who teach the law did not know me.
The rulers turned against me.
The prophets spoke in the name of Baal.
They followed gods that do not help.
[9] “That is why I will still bring charges against you,” says the LORD.
“And I will bring charges against your grandchildren.
[10] Go over to the islands of Kittim and look.
Send someone to Kedar and think about it carefully.
See if anything like this has ever happened.
[11] Has a nation ever exchanged its gods?
And their gods are not even real gods!
But my people have traded their glory
for something that does not help.
[12] “Heavens, be shocked at this!
Shake with horror!
Be completely empty,” says the LORD.
[13] “My people have done two evil things.
They have left me, the spring of living water.
And they have dug water tanks for themselves.
But those tanks are cracked.
They cannot hold water.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: God asks like a disappointed lover: What did I do to you, that you have left me?
> Verses 6–8: No one asked about God, not the people, not the priests, not the rulers, not the prophets. They forgot who freed them from Egypt.
> Verses 10–11: Kittim is Cyprus in the west, Kedar is a people in Arabia in the east. God says: Look around everywhere! No nation changes its gods, even though they are not even real gods. But Israel has left the true God.
> Verse 13 is the best-known picture of this chapter: God is like a fresh spring that always flows. The people have left this spring and dug cisterns (water tanks) for themselves, that is, holes in the rock in which rainwater is collected. But these cisterns are cracked and hold no water. Whoever leaves God looks for happiness where it cannot be found.

## What do you gain? (Verses 14–19)

[14] Is Israel a slave?
Was he born a slave?
Then why has he become a prisoner?
[15] Young lions have roared at him.
They have roared loudly.
They have turned his land into a wasteland.
His cities are burned down.
No one lives in them.
[16] The people of Memphis and Tahpanhes
have also broken the crown of your head.
[17] “You brought this on yourself, didn’t you?
You left the LORD your God,
while he was leading you on the way.
[18] Now what do you gain by going to Egypt
to drink the water of the Shihor?
Why do you go on the road to Assyria
to drink the water of the River?
[19] “Your own evil will punish you.
Your turning away from me will correct you.
So know it and see it:
It is evil and bitter that you have left the LORD your God.
It is evil and bitter that you do not fear me,” says the Lord, the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–16: Israel was free, but now it is being robbed by enemies, as if by lions. Memphis and Tahpanhes are cities in Egypt. Verse 16: “Broken the crown of your head” means: They have hurt you badly.
> Verse 18: The Shihor is a branch of the Nile in Egypt, “the River” is the Euphrates in Assyria. Judah looked for help sometimes from Egypt, sometimes from Assyria, instead of from God. That is like drinking water from foreign rivers instead of from your own spring.
> Verse 19: The consequences of your own mistakes are themselves the punishment. Whoever leaves God realizes at some point how bitter that is.

## Like a wild vine (Verses 20–28)

[20] “Long ago I broke the yoke off your neck.
I tore off your chains.
But you said, ‘I will not serve you.’
On every high hill and under every green tree
you bowed down and acted like a prostitute.
[21] I had planted you as a fine vine.
I planted you from pure and good seed.
So how did you turn into the wild, spoiled branches
of a foreign vine?
[22] You may wash yourself with lye.
You may use a lot of soap.
But your guilt is still a stain in front of me,” says the Lord GOD.
Lye is a strong cleaning powder.
[23] “How can you say, ‘I am not unclean.
I have not followed the Baals’?
Look at what you did in the valley.
Admit what you have done.
You are like a fast young camel
that runs this way and that way.
[24] You are like a wild donkey that is used to the wilderness.
She sniffs the wind in her strong desire.
When she is in heat, who can hold her back?
All who look for her do not need to wear themselves out.
In her season, they will find her.
[25] “Keep your feet from going bare.
Keep your throat from getting dry.
But you said, ‘It is no use.
No! I love strangers, and I will follow them.’
[26] A thief is ashamed when he is caught.
In the same way, the house of Israel is ashamed:
they, their kings, their princes, their priests and their prophets.
[27] They say to a piece of wood, ‘You are my father.’
They say to a stone, ‘You gave birth to me.’
They have turned their backs to me, not their faces.
But when they are in trouble, they will say,
‘Get up and save us!’
[28] “But where are the gods you made for yourselves?
Let them get up, if they can save you when you are in trouble!
Judah, you have as many gods as you have towns.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: The Bible often describes idol worship with the picture of unfaithfulness in marriage or prostitution. What is meant is: The people have become unfaithful to God. It is a picture of the relationship with God, not a judgment on women or on people in prostitution. Foreign gods were worshiped “on every high hill and under every green tree.”
> Verse 21: God had planted Israel as a noble vine (as in Isaiah 5). But it has become a wild, spoiled vine.
> Verse 22: Guilt cannot simply be washed off with soap.
> Verses 23–24: Strong pictures from the animal world: The people run after foreign gods like an animal driven by its urges.
> Verse 27: Bitter mockery: They say “father” to a piece of wood. But when they are in trouble, they still call on God for help.
> Verse 28: Every town in Judah had its own god. But none of them can help.

## I have not sinned? (Verses 29–37)

[29] “Why do you argue with me?
All of you have turned against me,” says the LORD.
[30] “I punished your children, but it was no use.
They did not accept correction.
Your own sword has killed your prophets,
like a lion that tears its prey apart.
[31] People of today, pay attention to the word of the LORD!
Have I been like a wilderness to Israel?
Have I been like a land of deep darkness?
Why do my people say, ‘We are free now.
We will never come back to you’?
[32] “Can a young woman forget her jewelry?
Can a bride forget her wedding clothes?
But my people have forgotten me
for more days than anyone can count.
[33] You know so well how to find your way to lovers!
You have even taught your ways to wicked women.
[34] And on the edges of your clothes
there is the blood of innocent, poor people.
You did not catch them breaking into your house.
But it is because of all these things.
[35] “Still you say, ‘I am innocent.
Surely his anger has turned away from me.’
“Look, I will judge you,
because you say, ‘I have not sinned.’
[36] Why do you keep running around and changing your ways?
You will be ashamed of Egypt too,
just as you were ashamed of Assyria.
[37] You will leave that place too, with your hands on your head.
The LORD has rejected those you trust.
They will not help you succeed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 30: The people even killed prophets who called them to turn back.
> Verse 31: God asks: Was I really so bad to you, like a wilderness? Why do you not want to have anything to do with me anymore?
> Verse 32: A bride does not forget her wedding jewelry. But God’s people have forgotten God.
> Verse 34: A serious accusation: The blood of poor, innocent people sticks to their clothes. These people were killed even though they had done nothing. Injustice against the poor is a crime in God’s eyes.
> Verse 35: The worst thing is when someone says, “I have not sinned.” Whoever does not see their guilt cannot turn back.
> Verse 37: To leave “with your hands on your head” shows grief and shame.
