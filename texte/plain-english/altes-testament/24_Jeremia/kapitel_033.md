# Jeremiah – Chapter 33
### Call to me, and I will answer you

---

## I will heal the city (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD’s word came to Jeremiah a second time,
while he was still locked up in the courtyard of the guard. He said,
[2] “The LORD who does it,
the LORD who shapes it to make it firm,
the LORD is his name, says:
[3] ‘Call to me, and I will answer you,
and I will show you great and hidden things,
which you don’t know.’
[4] For the LORD, the God of Israel,
says this about the houses of this city,
and about the houses of the kings of Judah,
which have been torn down to defend against the siege ramps and against the sword:
[5] ‘People come to fight against the Chaldeans,
but they will only fill these houses with the dead bodies of people
whom I have struck down in my anger and in my fury.
For I have hidden my face from this city
because of all their evil.
[6] But look, I will bring it health and healing,
and I will heal them.
I will show them great peace and faithfulness.
[7] I will turn around the fate of Judah and Israel,
and I will build them as they were at the beginning.
[8] I will make them clean from all their guilt
by which they have sinned against me.
I will forgive all their sins
by which they have sinned against me
and rebelled against me.
[9] This city will bring me fame and joy,
praise and glory,
in front of all the nations of the earth.
They will hear about all the good that I do for them.
They will be afraid and tremble
because of all the good and all the peace that I give to it.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah is still in prison, and outside the war is raging.
> Verse 3 is a very popular verse: “Call to me, and I will answer you, and I will show you great and hidden things.” God invites us to talk with him. He has answers that we cannot think up ourselves.
> Verses 4–5: During the siege, houses are torn down so that their stones can strengthen the walls. The situation is hopeless.
> Verses 6–9: And yet: God will heal the city, give it peace and faithfulness and forgive all its guilt. Jerusalem will be a place of joy again.

---

## Sounds of joy again (Verses 10–13)

[10] The LORD says:
“About this place you say,
‘It is a waste, without people and without animals.’
But in the cities of Judah,
and in the streets of Jerusalem,
that are ruined, without people, without anyone living there, and without animals,
there will again be heard
[11] the sound of joy and the sound of gladness,
the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride,
the voice of those who say,
‘Give thanks to the LORD of Armies,
for the LORD is good,
for his faithful love endures forever!’
as they bring thank offerings into the LORD’s house.
For I will turn around the fate of the land
and make it as it was at the beginning,” says the LORD.
[12] The LORD of Armies says:
“In this place, which is a waste, without people and without animals,
and in all its cities,
there will again be pastures
where shepherds let their flocks lie down.
[13] In the cities of the hill country,
in the cities of the lowland,
in the cities of the South,
in the land of Benjamin,
in the places around Jerusalem,
and in the cities of Judah,
the flocks will again pass under the hands of the one who counts them,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: Jeremiah had announced several times that the voices of the bride and bridegroom would fall silent (7:34; 16:9; 25:10). Now this is reversed: People will celebrate weddings again and praise God: “Give thanks to the LORD of Armies, for the LORD is good, for his faithful love endures forever!” (as in Psalm 136). Jewish wedding blessings still recall these verses today.
> Verses 12–13: The shepherds are counting their sheep again, a picture of peace and normal life.

## The righteous Branch and God’s faithfulness (Verses 14–26)

[14] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will fulfill the good promise
which I have spoken about the house of Israel and about the house of Judah.
[15] “In those days and at that time,
I will make a righteous Branch grow up for David.
He will do justice and righteousness in the land.
[16] In those days Judah will be saved,
and Jerusalem will live in safety.
This is the name by which she will be called:
The LORD our righteousness.”
[17] For the LORD says:
“David will never be without a man
to sit on the throne of the house of Israel.
[18] The Levitical priests will never be without a man in front of me
to offer burnt offerings,
to burn grain offerings,
and to offer sacrifices every day.”
[19] The LORD’s word came to Jeremiah. He said,
[20] “The LORD says:
‘If you can break my covenant with the day and my covenant with the night,
so that day and night no longer come at their right time,
[21] then my covenant with David my servant could also be broken,
so that he would have no son to rule on his throne,
and my covenant with the Levitical priests, who serve me.
[22] As the stars of the sky can’t be counted,
and the sand of the sea can’t be measured,
so I will make the descendants of David my servant many,
and the Levites who serve me.’”
[23] The LORD’s word came to Jeremiah. He said,
[24] “Haven’t you noticed what this people has said?
‘The LORD has rejected the two families which he chose.’
That is how they despise my people,
as if they were no longer a nation in their eyes.”
[25] The LORD says:
“If I have not made my covenant with day and night,
if I have not set the laws of heaven and earth,
[26] then I will also reject the descendants of Jacob and of David my servant,
so that I will not take any of his descendants
to rule over the descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
But I will turn around their fate,
and I will have mercy on them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–16: The promise of David’s “righteous Branch” from chapter 23:5–6 is repeated. This time Jerusalem itself is called “The LORD our righteousness.” In Judaism this is applied to the coming Messiah; Christians apply it to Jesus.
> Verses 17–18: God promises that there will always be a king from David’s family and priests from the tribe of Levi. After the exile there was no king for a long time, and the temple was later destroyed. That is why Jews understand this promise as a hope for the future. Christians see it fulfilled in Jesus, whom the New Testament describes as a king from David’s family and as high priest (Hebrews 7).
> Verses 20–22: God’s covenant is as sure as day and night. No one can break it.
> Verses 24–26: Some said: God has rejected Israel and Judah. God answers: Never! As surely as there is day and night, I will have mercy on my people. This is a clear rejection of everyone who thinks God has cast off the Jewish people.
