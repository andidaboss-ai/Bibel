# Jeremiah – Chapter 11
### The broken covenant

---

## Hear the words of this covenant (Verses 1–17)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] “Hear the words of this covenant,
and speak to the men of Judah and to the people of Jerusalem.
[3] Say to them, The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘Cursed is the man who doesn’t listen to the words of this covenant,
[4] which I commanded your fathers on the day that I brought them out of the land of Egypt,
out of the iron furnace.
I said: Obey my voice,
and do everything that I command you.
So you will be my people,
and I will be your God.
[5] Then I will keep the oath which I swore to your fathers,
to give them a land flowing with milk and honey,’
as it is today.”
Then I answered, “Amen, LORD.”
[6] The LORD said to me,
“Call out all these words in the cities of Judah,
and in the streets of Jerusalem. Say,
‘Hear the words of this covenant, and do them.
[7] For I warned your fathers seriously
on the day that I brought them up out of the land of Egypt,
and until this day I warned them again and again. I said,
“Obey my voice.”
[8] Yet they didn’t obey or pay attention.
Each one followed the stubbornness of his evil heart.
So I brought on them all the words of this covenant,
which I commanded them to do, but they didn’t do them.’”
[9] The LORD said to me,
“A conspiracy has been found among the men of Judah,
and among the people of Jerusalem.
[10] They have turned back to the sins of their ancestors,
who refused to listen to my words.
They have followed other gods to serve them.
The house of Israel and the house of Judah
have broken my covenant which I made with their fathers.
[11] That is why the LORD says,
‘Look, I will bring disaster on them,
which they will not be able to escape.
They will cry to me,
but I will not listen to them.
[12] Then the cities of Judah and the people of Jerusalem
will go and cry to the gods they burn incense to,
but these will not save them at all in the time of their trouble.
[13] For you have as many gods as you have cities, Judah.
And as many streets as Jerusalem has,
so many altars you have set up to the shameful thing,
altars to burn incense to Baal.’
[14] “So don’t pray for this people.
Don’t raise a cry or prayer for them,
for I will not listen when they cry to me because of their trouble.
[15] What is my beloved doing in my house,
since she has done shameful things with many?
Can holy meat take your guilt away?
When you do evil, you are happy.”
[16] The LORD called you by the name
“A green olive tree, beautiful, with good fruit.”
With the noise of a great roar he has set it on fire,
and its branches are broken.
[17] For the LORD of Armies, who planted you,
has announced disaster against you,
because of the evil of the house of Israel and of the house of Judah,
which they have done to themselves.
They made me angry by burning incense to Baal.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–5: God reminds them of the covenant at Sinai. The heart of it is: “Obey my voice … So you will be my people, and I will be your God.” Egypt is called the “iron furnace,” a picture of the hard slavery there.
> Verse 5: Jeremiah answers with “Amen.” “Amen” means: That is true, so be it.
> Perhaps this chapter is connected with the reform of King Josiah. At that time a book of the law was found in the temple, and the covenant was renewed (2 Kings 22–23).
> Verses 9–10: But the people broke the covenant and turned to other gods again.
> Verse 14: Again God says to Jeremiah: Don’t pray for this people (as in 7:16). This shows how serious the situation is.
> Verse 16: Israel was like a beautiful, green olive tree. Now it is struck by fire. Later, Paul uses the picture of the olive tree for Israel (Romans 11:17–24).

## Like a lamb led to the slaughter (Verses 18–23)

[18] The LORD let me know it, and I knew it.
Then you showed me what they were doing.
[19] But I was like a gentle lamb that is led to the slaughter.
I didn’t know that they had made plans against me. They said,
“Let’s destroy the tree with its fruit,
and let’s cut him off from the land of the living,
so that no one remembers his name anymore.”
[20] But, LORD of Armies, you judge rightly.
You test the heart and the mind.
Let me see your revenge on them,
for I have put my case in your hands.
[21] “That is why the LORD says this about the men of Anathoth,
who want to kill you, and say,
‘Don’t prophesy in the LORD’s name,
or you will die by our hand.’
[22] That is why the LORD of Armies says,
‘Look, I will punish them.
The young men will die by the sword.
Their sons and their daughters will die by famine.
[23] None of them will be left,
for I will bring disaster on the men of Anathoth,
in the year of their punishment.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 18–19: Jeremiah learns that men from his own home village Anathoth want to kill him, perhaps even relatives (compare 12:6). He suspected nothing, like a lamb that is led to the slaughter. This picture is a reminder of the servant of God in Isaiah 53:7.
> Verse 20: Jeremiah does not take revenge himself. He hands his case over to God, who judges rightly and sees into the heart. He asks God for justice. This is an important difference: He does not take revenge into his own hands.
> Verses 21–23: God announces the judgment on the men of Anathoth.
