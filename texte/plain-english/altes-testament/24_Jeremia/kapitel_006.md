# Jeremiah – Chapter 6
### Peace, peace – when there is no peace

---

## Jerusalem is besieged (Verses 1–8)

[1] “Run for safety, you people of Benjamin,
out of the middle of Jerusalem!
Blow the trumpet in Tekoa,
and raise a signal on Beth Haccherem,
for disaster looks out from the north,
with a great destruction.
[2] I will destroy the beautiful and delicate one,
the daughter of Zion.
[3] Shepherds with their flocks will come to her.
They will set up their tents against her all around.
Each one will graze his flock in his own place.”
[4] “Get ready for war against her!
Get up! Let’s attack at noon.
How terrible for us! For the day is ending,
and the shadows of the evening are growing long.
[5] Get up! Let’s attack by night,
and let’s destroy her palaces.”
[6] For the LORD of Armies said,
“Cut down trees,
and build a ramp of earth against Jerusalem.
This is the city that must be punished.
She is full of oppression inside.
[7] As a well pours out its water,
so she pours out her evil.
Violence and destruction are heard in her.
Sickness and wounds are always in front of me.
[8] Accept the warning, Jerusalem,
or I will turn away from you,
or I will make you a waste,
a land where no one lives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Tekoa and Beth Haccherem are places south of Jerusalem. The people are to flee south, because the enemy comes from the north.
> Verse 3: The “shepherds with their flocks” are the enemy commanders with their soldiers.
> Verses 4–5: We hear the enemies talking: They are so eager that they even want to attack at noon and at night.
> Verses 6–7: God says: Jerusalem is full of oppression and violence inside. Evil pours out of the city like water out of a well.
> Verse 8: Here too there is a last warning: Accept the warning before it is too late!

## They say “peace,” but there is no peace (Verses 9–15)

[9] The LORD of Armies says,
“They will completely pick clean what is left of Israel, like a vine.
Pass your hand over the branches again,
like someone who gathers grapes into baskets.”
[10] Who should I speak to and warn,
so that they may hear?
Look, their ears are uncircumcised,
and they can’t listen.
Look, the LORD’s word has become something they mock.
They have no joy in it.
[11] That is why I am full of the LORD’s anger.
I am tired of holding it in.
“Pour it out on the children in the street,
and on the young men gathered together.
For even the husband with the wife will be taken,
the old with the very old.
[12] Their houses will be given to others,
their fields and their wives together,
for I will stretch out my hand against the people who live in the land,” says the LORD.
[13] “For from the least to the greatest of them,
everyone is greedy for gain.
From the prophet to the priest,
everyone acts falsely.
[14] They have treated the wound of my people as if it were not serious.
They say, ‘Peace, peace!’
when there is no peace.
[15] Were they ashamed when they had done disgusting things?
No, they were not ashamed at all.
They didn’t even know how to blush.
That is why they will fall among those who fall.
When I punish them, they will be thrown down,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Jeremiah is in despair: No one listens. Their ears are “uncircumcised,” that is, closed.
> Verse 11: Jeremiah can no longer hold back God’s message. The disaster will hit everyone, children, young and old. This is not Jeremiah’s wish, but a painful announcement.
> Verse 13: From the least to the greatest, everyone only thinks about their own gain.
> Verse 14 is a famous verse: The false prophets say “Peace, peace!” when there is no peace. They cover a deep wound with only a bandage. They tell people what they want to hear. Real peace needs honesty and justice.
> Verse 15: They are not even ashamed anymore of the evil they do.

## Ask for the old paths (Verses 16–21)

[16] The LORD says,
“Stand at the crossroads and look.
Ask for the old paths:
‘Where is the good way?’
Walk in it,
and you will find rest for your souls.
But they said, ‘We will not walk in it.’
[17] I set watchmen over you, and said,
‘Listen to the sound of the trumpet!’
But they said, ‘We will not listen!’
[18] So hear, you nations,
and know, you assembly, what will happen to them.
[19] Hear, earth!
Look, I will bring disaster on this people,
the fruit of their own plans,
because they have not listened to my words,
and they have rejected my law.
[20] What good is it to me when incense comes from Sheba,
and sweet-smelling cane from a far country?
Your burnt offerings are not acceptable,
and your sacrifices do not please me.”
[21] That is why the LORD says,
“Look, I will put stumbling blocks in front of this people.
Fathers and sons together will stumble over them.
Neighbors and friends will die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16 is a beautiful, often quoted verse: If you don’t know where to go, stand still, look around, and ask for the proven paths. Walk on the good way, and you will find rest for your soul. Jesus says something similar: “And you will find rest for your souls” (Matthew 11:29).
> But the people answer: “We will not!”
> Verse 17: The “watchmen” are the prophets who warn. But no one listens to them.
> Verse 20: Expensive offerings from far countries are of no use if the heart is not with God.

## A cruel people from the north (Verses 22–30)

[22] The LORD says,
“Look, a people is coming from the land of the north.
A great nation will be stirred up from the ends of the earth.
[23] They hold bow and spear.
They are cruel and have no mercy.
Their voice roars like the sea.
They ride on horses,
each one lined up like a man ready for battle,
against you, daughter of Zion.”
[24] We have heard the news about them.
Our hands hang weak.
Anguish has seized us,
pains like those of a woman in labor.
[25] Don’t go out into the field,
or walk along the road,
for the enemy has a sword,
and there is terror on every side.
[26] Daughter of my people, put on sackcloth,
and roll in ashes!
Mourn as for an only son,
with the most bitter weeping,
for the destroyer will suddenly come on us.
[27] “I have made you a tester of metals among my people, like a fortress,
so that you may know and test their way.
[28] They are all the worst kind of rebels.
They go around spreading lies about others.
They are bronze and iron.
All of them act corruptly.
[29] The bellows blow hard.
The lead is burned up in the fire.
They go on refining for nothing,
because the wicked are not removed.
[30] People will call them rejected silver,
because the LORD has rejected them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–23: The army from the north is cruel and without mercy. Jeremiah honestly describes how terrible war is.
> Verses 24–26: The people are paralyzed with fear. No one dares to go outside anymore. “There is terror on every side” is an expression that appears more often in Jeremiah. The grief is as deep as the grief for an only child.
> Verses 27–30: God makes Jeremiah a “tester,” like someone who tests metal in the fire. When the metal is melted, the pure silver is supposed to be separated from what is impure. But no matter how much is melted, nothing pure is left. A sad result.
