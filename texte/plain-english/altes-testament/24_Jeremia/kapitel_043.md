# Jeremiah – Chapter 43
### The journey to Egypt

---

## They do not listen and go to Egypt (Verses 1–7)

[1] Jeremiah finished telling all the people
all the words of the LORD their God,
all the words that the LORD their God had sent him to tell them.
[2] Then Azariah, the son of Hoshaiah,
Johanan, the son of Kareah,
and all the proud men said to Jeremiah,
“You are lying!
The LORD our God has not sent you to say,
‘You must not go to Egypt to live there.’
[3] Instead, Baruch, the son of Neriah, has turned you against us,
to hand us over to the Chaldeans,
so that they can put us to death,
or carry us away as captives to Babylon.”
[4] So Johanan, the son of Kareah,
and all the army commanders,
and all the people
didn’t obey the LORD’s voice to stay in the land of Judah.
[5] Instead, Johanan, the son of Kareah, and all the army commanders
took all the people who were left of Judah,
who had come back from all the nations where they had been driven,
to live in the land of Judah:
[6] the men, the women, the children, the king’s daughters,
and everyone whom Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard,
had left with Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan,
and also Jeremiah the prophet,
and Baruch, the son of Neriah.
[7] They went into the land of Egypt,
for they didn’t obey the LORD’s voice.
They came as far as Tahpanhes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3: Even though they had promised to listen to God, they now call Jeremiah a liar. And they blame Baruch: He supposedly influenced Jeremiah. When people don’t want to hear a message, they often attack the messenger.
> Verse 6: Jeremiah and Baruch are taken along against their will. Jeremiah, the old prophet, has to leave his homeland and probably dies in Egypt.
> Verse 7: Tahpanhes was an Egyptian border town in the Nile Delta.

---

## Stones in front of Pharaoh’s palace (Verses 8–13)

[8] Then the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah in Tahpanhes. He said,
[9] “Take some large stones in your hands,
and hide them in the mortar of the brick pavement
at the entrance to Pharaoh’s palace in Tahpanhes,
while the men of Judah are watching.
[10] Tell them: The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘Look, I will send for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, my servant.
I will set his throne on these stones that I have hidden,
and he will spread his royal canopy over them.
[11] He will come and strike the land of Egypt.
Those who are meant for death will go to death.
Those who are meant for captivity will go into captivity.
Those who are meant for the sword will go to the sword.
[12] I will set fire to the temples of the gods of Egypt.
He will burn them, and carry their gods away as captives.
He will wrap himself in the land of Egypt,
as a shepherd wraps himself in his cloak,
and he will leave there in peace.
[13] He will also break the sacred pillars of Beth Shemesh in the land of Egypt,
and he will burn the temples of the gods of Egypt with fire.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Another symbolic act: Jeremiah buries large stones in front of Pharaoh’s palace. One day Nebuchadnezzar will set up his throne there.
> The message: Egypt is not a safe place. Babylon will come here too. The people fled from what they feared, and it will catch up with them.
> Verse 13: Beth Shemesh means *house of the sun*. This refers to the Egyptian city of Heliopolis, with its sun temple and its obelisks (stone pillars).
> Nebuchadnezzar did in fact attack Egypt later (around 568 BC).
