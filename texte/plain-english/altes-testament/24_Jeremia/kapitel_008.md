# Jeremiah – Chapter 8
### Is there no balm in Gilead?

---

## The bones are dug up (Verses 1–3)

[1] “At that time,” says the LORD,
“they will bring out of their graves
the bones of the kings of Judah,
the bones of its princes,
the bones of the priests,
the bones of the prophets,
and the bones of the people of Jerusalem.
[2] They will spread them out in front of the sun, the moon, and all the stars of the sky,
which they have loved, which they have served,
which they have followed, which they have looked for,
and which they have worshiped.
The bones will not be gathered or buried.
They will be like manure on the surface of the ground.
[3] All who are left of this evil family,
who are left in all the places where I have driven them,
will choose death rather than life,” says the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> A gruesome picture: The graves are opened, and the bones lie in front of the sun, moon and stars that the people worshiped. These heavenly bodies cannot help them now.
> In the ancient Near East, it was a great disgrace not to be buried.
> Verse 3: The survivors in exile will be so desperate that they would rather die. This shows how deep the suffering is. ## Even the stork knows its time (Verses 4–13)

[4] “Tell them also, ‘The LORD says:
“‘When people fall, don’t they get up again?
When someone turns away, doesn’t he come back?
[5] Why then have the people of Jerusalem turned away,
and keep on turning away?
They hold on to deceit.
They refuse to come back.
[6] I listened and heard,
but they didn’t say what is right.
No one is sorry for his evil and says,
“What have I done?”
Everyone runs his own way,
like a horse that charges into battle.
[7] Yes, the stork in the sky knows her seasons.
The turtledove, the swallow, and the crane
keep the time of their return.
But my people don’t know the LORD’s law.
[8] “‘How can you say, “We are wise,
and the LORD’s law is with us”?
But look, the lying pen of the scribes
has turned it into a lie.
[9] The wise men are put to shame.
They are terrified and trapped.
Look, they have rejected the LORD’s word.
What kind of wisdom do they have?
[10] So I will give their wives to others,
and their fields to new owners.
For everyone from the least to the greatest is greedy for gain.
From the prophet to the priest, everyone acts falsely.
[11] They have treated the wound of the daughter of my people
as if it were not serious.
They say, “Peace, peace,”
when there is no peace.
[12] Were they ashamed when they had done disgusting things?
No, they were not ashamed at all.
They didn’t know how to blush.
That is why they will fall among those who fall.
When the time of their punishment comes, they will be thrown down, says the LORD.
[13] “‘I will completely sweep them away, says the LORD.
There will be no grapes on the vine,
and no figs on the fig tree,
and the leaves will wither.
What I have given them will be taken away from them.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: A simple question: Whoever falls gets up again! Whoever goes the wrong way turns back! Why do my people not do this?
> Verse 6: No one asks himself, “What have I done?” This question is the beginning of every turning back.
> Verse 7: A beautiful picture from nature: Migrating birds like storks, swallows and cranes know exactly when they must return. But God’s people do not know when it is time to return to God.
> Verse 8: The scribes say: But we have the law! But they have explained it in such a way that it became a lie.
> Verses 10–12: These verses repeat Jeremiah 6:12–15 almost
 word for word: “Peace, peace,” when there is no peace.
> Verse 13: A fig tree without fruit. Jesus uses a similar picture (Mark 11:13–14; Luke 13:6–9).

## The harvest is over (Verses 14–22)

[14] “Why are we sitting still?
Gather together!
Let’s go into the fortified cities,
and let’s be silent there.
For the LORD our God has silenced us,
and given us poisoned water to drink,
because we have sinned against the LORD.
[15] We hoped for peace, but nothing good came.
We hoped for a time of healing, but there was terror!
[16] The snorting of his horses is heard from Dan.
The whole land shakes at the sound of the neighing of his strong horses.
For they have come and have eaten up the land and everything in it,
the city and those who live in it.”
[17] “For look, I will send snakes among you,
poisonous snakes that can’t be charmed.
They will bite you,” says the LORD.
[18] Oh, if only I could find comfort in my sorrow!
My heart is sick inside me.
[19] Listen, the cry of the daughter of my people
from a land very far away:
“Isn’t the LORD in Zion?
Isn’t her King in her?”
“Why have they made me angry with their carved images,
and with foreign idols?”
[20] “The harvest is over.
The summer has ended,
and we are not saved.”
[21] Because the daughter of my people is broken, I am broken.
I mourn.
Horror has seized me.
[22] Is there no balm in Gilead?
Is there no doctor there?
Why then hasn’t the daughter of my people been healed?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–16: The people now realize that disaster is coming. They flee into the fortresses, but without hope.
> Verse 18: Now Jeremiah himself is speaking. His heart is sick with sorrow.
> Verse 19: You can already hear the voices from exile: “Isn’t the LORD in Zion?” And God answers sadly: Why have they hurt me with idols?
> Verse 20: A famous verse: “The harvest is over. The summer has ended, and we are not saved.” The time when they could still have turned back is over.
> Verse 21: Jeremiah suffers with his people. He is “broken” because his people are broken.
> Verse 22: Gilead (east of the Jordan) was famous for its healing balm, a resin. Jeremiah asks in despair: Is there no medicine and no doctor for my people? An African American spiritual answers: *There Is a Balm in Gilead*, a balm that heals the wounded.
> Balm is a healing ointment.
