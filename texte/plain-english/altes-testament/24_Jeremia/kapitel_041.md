# Jeremiah – Chapter 41
### The murder of Gedaliah

---

## Ishmael kills Gedaliah (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the seventh month,
Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, the son of Elishama,
who was from the royal family and one of the king’s chief officers,
came with 10 men to Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, at Mizpah.
There at Mizpah they ate a meal together.
[2] Then Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, and the 10 men who were with him got up.
They struck down Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, with the sword,
and killed him,
the man whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the land.
[3] Ishmael also killed all the Jews who were with Gedaliah at Mizpah,
and the Chaldean soldiers who were there.
[4] On the second day after he had killed Gedaliah,
before anyone knew about it,
[5] 80 men came from Shechem, from Shiloh, and from Samaria.
Their beards were shaved, their clothes were torn,
and they had cut themselves.
They had grain offerings and frankincense in their hands,
to bring them to the LORD’s house.
[6] Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, went out from Mizpah to meet them,
weeping all the way as he went.
When he met them, he said to them,
“Come to Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam.”
[7] When they came into the middle of the city,
Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, and the men who were with him
killed them and threw them into a pit.
[8] But there were 10 men among them who said to Ishmael,
“Don’t kill us!
For we have supplies hidden in the field:
wheat, barley, oil, and honey.”
So he held back, and didn’t kill them along with their companions.
[9] Ishmael threw all the dead bodies of the men he had killed
along with Gedaliah into a large pit.
King Asa had made it when he was afraid of Baasha, king of Israel.
Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, filled it with those he had killed.
[10] Then Ishmael took captive all the rest of the people who were in Mizpah,
including the king’s daughters,
all the people who remained in Mizpah,
whom Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, had put in the care of Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam.
Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, took them captive,
and set out to go over to the people of Ammon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Ishmael comes as a guest, eats a meal with Gedaliah, and then murders him. Abusing hospitality was considered especially shameful. Ishmael came from the royal family. Perhaps he wanted to rule himself, or he saw Gedaliah as a traitor because he worked together with Babylon.
> Verse 5: A group of 80 pilgrims from the north come in mourning to offer sacrifices at the destroyed temple. Ishmael lures them into the city with false tears and murders them. A cruel, senseless crime.
> Verse 8: The 10 men save their lives because they offer supplies. Greed is stronger than the lust to kill.
> To this day, Jews remember this murder with a day of fasting, the “Fast of Gedaliah,” shortly after the Jewish New Year. It destroyed the last hope for a life in the land after the destruction of Jerusalem.
> Violence breeds new violence. No political goal justifies murder.

## Johanan frees the captives (Verses 11–18)

[11] But Johanan, the son of Kareah, and all the army commanders who were with him
heard about all the evil that Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, had done.
[12] So they took all their men,
and went to fight against Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah.
They found him by the great pool in Gibeon.
[13] When all the people who were with Ishmael
saw Johanan, the son of Kareah, and all the army commanders who were with him,
they were glad.
[14] So all the people whom Ishmael had taken captive from Mizpah
turned around and came back,
and went over to Johanan, the son of Kareah.
[15] But Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, escaped from Johanan with eight men,
and went to the people of Ammon.
[16] Then Johanan, the son of Kareah, and all the army commanders who were with him
took all the rest of the people from Mizpah,
whom he had rescued from Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah,
after Ishmael had killed Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam:
the soldiers, the women, the children, and the eunuchs,
whom he had brought back from Gibeon.
[17] They set out and stayed at Geruth Chimham, near Bethlehem,
on their way to go to Egypt,
[18] because of the Chaldeans.
For they were afraid of them,
because Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, had killed Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam,
whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–15: Johanan pursues Ishmael and frees the captives. Ishmael himself escapes.
> Verses 16–18: Now the survivors are very afraid: The Babylonians could punish them for the murder of their governor. They want to flee to Egypt.
> Verse 16: “Eunuchs” were officials at the royal court.
