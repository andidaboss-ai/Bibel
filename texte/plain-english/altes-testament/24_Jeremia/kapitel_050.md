# Jeremiah – Chapter 50
### Babylon has fallen

---

## Babylon is captured, Israel comes home (Verses 1–10)

[1] This is the word that the LORD spoke about Babylon,
about the land of the Chaldeans,
through Jeremiah the prophet.
[2] “Announce it among the nations and proclaim it!
Raise a signal flag!
Proclaim it, and don’t hide it.
Say, ‘Babylon is captured!
Bel is put to shame!
Merodach is terrified!
Her images are put to shame.
Her idols are terrified.’
[3] For a nation is coming against her out of the north.
It will make her land a wasteland,
and no one will live in it.
Both people and animals have fled.
They are gone.
[4] In those days, and at that time,” says the LORD,
“the people of Israel will come,
they and the people of Judah together.
They will weep as they walk along,
and will seek the LORD their God.
[5] They will ask the way to Zion,
with their faces turned toward it.
They will say, ‘Come, let’s join ourselves to the LORD
in a covenant that lasts forever and will never be forgotten.’
[6] My people have been lost sheep.
Their shepherds have led them astray.
They have let them wander on the mountains.
They have gone from mountain to hill.
They have forgotten their resting place.
[7] All who found them have devoured them.
Their enemies said, ‘We are not guilty,
because they have sinned against the LORD,
the true pasture of righteousness,
the LORD, the hope of their ancestors.’
[8] Flee out of the middle of Babylon!
Go out of the land of the Chaldeans,
and be like the male goats that lead the flock.
[9] For look, I will stir up a crowd of great nations from the land of the north,
and bring them up against Babylon.
They will take up their positions against her.
From there she will be captured.
Their arrows will be like those of a skilled warrior,
who never comes back empty-handed.
[10] Chaldea will be plundered.
All who plunder her will have their fill,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 50–51 are the longest words about a nation: about Babylon, the great power that destroyed Jerusalem.
> Verse 2: Bel and Merodach (Marduk) were the chief gods of Babylon. They are “put to shame.”
> Verse 3: A nation “out of the north” will conquer Babylon. These were the Medes and Persians under Cyrus in the year 539 BC.
> Verses 4–5: In the middle of the announcement about Babylon, a wonderful scene: Israel and Judah come back together, weeping, and seek God. They want “a covenant that lasts forever and will never be forgotten.”
> Verse 6: God’s people were like a lost flock because the shepherds (the leaders) failed. Jesus has compassion on the people, “because they were like sheep without a shepherd” (Mark 6:34).
> Verse 7: The enemies thought they could oppress Israel without punishment because Israel had sinned. But God also sees their guilt.

---

## Payback for Babylon (Verses 11–20)

[11] “You are glad and you rejoice,
you who plunder my inheritance.
You frisk about like a young cow threshing grain,
and neigh like stallions.
[12] So your mother will be deeply ashamed.
She who gave birth to you will be disgraced.
Look, she will be the least of the nations,
a wilderness, a dry land, and a desert.
[13] Because of the LORD’s anger, no one will live in her.
She will be completely deserted.
Everyone who passes by Babylon will be shocked,
and will hiss at all her wounds.
[14] Take up your positions against Babylon on every side,
all you who bend the bow.
Shoot at her!
Spare no arrows,
for she has sinned against the LORD.
[15] Raise the battle cry against her on every side!
She has surrendered.
Her towers have fallen.
Her walls have been torn down.
For this is the LORD’s vengeance.
Take vengeance on her.
As she has done, do to her.
[16] Cut off from Babylon the one who sows,
and the one who swings the sickle at harvest time.
For fear of the oppressor’s sword,
each will return to his own people,
and each will flee to his own land.
[17] Israel is a scattered sheep.
The lions have driven him away.
First the king of Assyria devoured him,
and now at last Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, has crushed his bones.”
[18] So the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Look, I will punish the king of Babylon and his land,
as I punished the king of Assyria.
[19] I will bring Israel back to his pasture,
and he will graze on Carmel and Bashan.
He will eat his fill
on the hills of Ephraim and in Gilead.
[20] In those days, and at that time,” says the LORD,
“people will search for Israel’s guilt,
but there will be none,
and for Judah’s sins,
but they will not be found.
For I will forgive those whom I leave as a remnant.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Babylon had rejoiced over the plundering of Jerusalem. Anyone who rejoices in the misfortune of others will be held accountable.
> Verse 15: “As she has done, do to her.” God’s judgment matches what Babylon did to others. This language of war describes God’s judgment on a cruel power. It is not general permission for revenge among people. Jesus teaches: Do not repay evil with evil (Matthew 5:38–44; Romans 12:17–21).
> Verse 17: Israel is like a sheep hunted by lions: first by Assyria, then by Babylon.
> Verse 19: Israel returns to its green pastures.
> Verse 20 is a wonderful verse: People will search for Israel’s guilt and find nothing, “For I will forgive those whom I leave as a remnant.” When God forgives, the guilt is truly gone.

## The hammer of the whole earth is broken (Verses 21–32)

[21] “Go up against the land of Merathaim,
against it, and against the people of Pekod.
Kill them and destroy them completely,” says the LORD,
“and do everything that I have commanded you.
[22] The noise of battle is in the land,
and of great destruction.
[23] How the hammer of the whole earth
is cut down and broken!
How Babylon has become a wasteland
among the nations!
[24] I set a trap for you,
and you were caught, Babylon,
before you knew it.
You were found and captured,
because you fought against the LORD.
[25] The LORD has opened his armory,
and has brought out the weapons of his anger.
For the Lord, the LORD of Armies, has work to do
in the land of the Chaldeans.
[26] Come against her from far away.
Open her storehouses.
Pile her up like heaps of grain.
Destroy her completely.
Let nothing of her be left.
[27] Kill all her young bulls.
Let them go down to the slaughter.
How terrible for them!
For their day has come,
the time of their punishment.
[28] Listen! Refugees and survivors are fleeing from the land of Babylon,
to announce in Zion the vengeance of the LORD our God,
the vengeance for his temple.
[29] Call together the archers against Babylon,
all those who bend the bow.
Camp against her on every side.
Let no one escape.
Pay her back for what she has done.
Do to her everything she has done to others.
For she has been arrogant against the LORD,
against the Holy One of Israel.
[30] So her young men will fall in her streets.
All her soldiers will be silenced on that day,” says the LORD.
[31] “Look, I am against you, you arrogant one,”
says the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
“for your day has come,
the time when I will punish you.
[32] The arrogant one will stumble and fall,
and no one will help him up.
I will set fire to his cities,
and it will burn up everything around him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: “Merathaim” and “Pekod” are plays on words. Merathaim means *double rebellion*, and Pekod sounds like *punishment*. Both are also names of regions in Babylonia.
> Verse 23: Babylon was like a “hammer” that smashed the whole world. Now the hammer itself is broken.
> Verse 28: The refugees from Babylon announce in Jerusalem: God has not forgotten the destruction of his temple.
> Verses 31–32: The main reason for Babylon’s fall: arrogance, pride against God. “The arrogant one will stumble and fall.”

## The strong Redeemer (Verses 33–40)

[33] The LORD of Armies says:
“The people of Israel and the people of Judah
are oppressed together.
All who took them captive hold on to them tightly.
They refuse to let them go.
[34] Their Redeemer is strong.
The LORD of Armies is his name.
He will surely take up their case,
so that he may give rest to the earth,
and unrest to the people of Babylon.
[35] A sword against the Chaldeans,” says the LORD,
“and against the people of Babylon,
against her leaders,
and against her wise men!
[36] A sword against the boasters,
and they will become fools!
A sword against her warriors,
and they will be terrified!
[37] A sword against their horses and their chariots,
and against all the foreign troops among her,
and they will become like women!
A sword against her treasures,
and they will be plundered!
[38] A drought against her waters,
and they will dry up!
For it is a land of carved images,
and they go mad over their idols.
[39] So desert animals will live there with hyenas.
Ostriches will live there.
No one will ever live there again.
No one will settle there for all generations.
[40] As when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah
and the towns around them,” says the LORD,
“so no one will live there,
and no human being will stay there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 33–34: Israel and Judah are captive, and their oppressors refuse to let them go, as Pharaoh did back then in Egypt. But their “Redeemer” is strong. He takes up their case like a lawyer in court.
> Verses 35–38: Five times it says, “A sword against …”: against the leaders, the wise men, the boasters, the warriors, the horses, and the treasures. Everything Babylon was proud of will be broken.
> Verse 37: “They will become like women” is a saying of that time for soldiers who lose their courage. It reflects the thinking of that time. Today we know: Women are not weaker or less brave than men.
> Verse 38: Babylon was a land full of images of gods. The canals that watered the land will also dry up.
> Verses 39–40: Over the centuries Babylon was in fact abandoned. Today only ruins are left in Iraq.

## A people from the north (Verses 41–46)

[41] “Look, a people is coming from the north.
A great nation and many kings
will be stirred up from the ends of the earth.
[42] They hold bow and spear.
They are cruel and have no mercy.
Their voice roars like the sea.
They ride on horses,
each one lined up like a man ready for battle,
against you, daughter of Babylon.
[43] The king of Babylon has heard the news about them,
and his hands hang weak.
Anguish has seized him,
pains like those of a woman in labor.
[44] Look, the enemy will come up like a lion
from the thickets of the Jordan
against the strong pasture.
For I will suddenly make them run away from it,
and I will appoint over it whoever I choose.
For who is like me?
Who can summon me?
What shepherd can stand against me?”
[45] So hear the plan that the LORD has made against Babylon,
and what he has decided against the land of the Chaldeans:
Surely the little ones of the flock will be dragged away.
Surely he will make their pasture a wasteland because of them.
[46] The earth trembles at the noise of Babylon’s capture.
The cry is heard among the nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 41–43: These verses repeat almost word for word what Jeremiah had said earlier about the army coming against Jerusalem (6:22–24). But now it is directed against Babylon. Whoever brought terror to others now experiences it themselves.
> Verses 44–46: These verses were also already spoken about Edom (49:19–21). Now they apply to Babylon. No power in the world can stand before God.
