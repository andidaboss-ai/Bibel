# Jeremiah – Chapter 26
### Jeremiah on trial

---

## Jeremiah must die (Verses 1–9)

[1] At the beginning of the rule of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah,
this word came from the LORD:
[2] “The LORD says:
‘Stand in the courtyard of the LORD’s house,
and speak to all the people of the cities of Judah
who come to worship in the LORD’s house.
Tell them all the words that I command you to speak to them.
Don’t leave out a word.
[3] Perhaps they will listen,
and each one will turn back from his evil way.
Then I will change my mind about the disaster
which I plan to bring on them because of the evil of their deeds.’
[4] Tell them, “The LORD says:
‘If you will not listen to me,
and walk in my law which I have set before you,
[5] and listen to the words of my servants the prophets,
whom I send to you early and tirelessly,
though you have not listened,
[6] then I will make this house like Shiloh,
and I will make this city a curse to all the nations of the earth.’”
[7] The priests, the prophets, and all the people heard Jeremiah
speaking these words in the LORD’s house.
[8] When Jeremiah had finished speaking everything
that the LORD had commanded him to speak to all the people,
the priests, the prophets, and all the people seized him and said,
“You must die!
[9] Why have you prophesied in the LORD’s name,
‘This house will be like Shiloh,
and this city will be a ruin, with no one living in it’?”
All the people crowded around Jeremiah in the LORD’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter tells what happened after the temple speech in chapter 7.
> Verse 2: “Don’t leave out a word.” Jeremiah is to pass on God’s message completely, even if it is uncomfortable. (That is exactly what we want with this Bible too: not to leave out a word.)
> Verse 3: “Perhaps they will listen.” God still hopes that they will turn back. The threat is not meant to destroy, but to move people to turn back.
> Verse 8: The reaction is fierce: “You must die!” To the people, it was blasphemy to say that the temple could be destroyed.

---

## The trial (Verses 10–19)

[10] When the princes of Judah heard these things,
they came up from the king’s house to the LORD’s house,
and they sat down at the entrance of the New Gate of the LORD’s house.
[11] Then the priests and the prophets spoke to the princes and to all the people. They said,
“This man deserves to die,
for he has prophesied against this city,
as you have heard with your own ears.”
[12] Then Jeremiah spoke to all the princes and to all the people. He said,
“The LORD sent me to prophesy against this house and against this city
all the words that you have heard.
[13] So now, change your ways and your deeds,
and obey the voice of the LORD your God.
Then the LORD will change his mind about the disaster
that he has announced against you.
[14] But as for me, look, I am in your hands.
Do with me whatever you think is good and right.
[15] Only know for sure that if you put me to death,
you will bring innocent blood on yourselves,
on this city, and on the people who live in it.
For it is true: The LORD has sent me to you
to speak all these words so that you can hear them.”
[16] Then the princes and all the people said to the priests and to the prophets:
“This man does not deserve to die,
for he has spoken to us in the name of the LORD our God.”
[17] Then some of the elders of the land stood up,
and spoke to the whole assembly of the people. They said,
[18] “Micah the Morashtite prophesied in the days of Hezekiah, king of Judah.
He spoke to all the people of Judah and said,
‘The LORD of Armies says:
“‘Zion will be plowed like a field,
and Jerusalem will become heaps of ruins,
and the mountain of the temple will become like hills covered with forest.’
[19] Did Hezekiah, king of Judah, and all Judah put him to death?
Didn’t he fear the LORD and ask the LORD for mercy?
And the LORD changed his mind about the disaster
which he had announced against them.
We would be doing great harm to ourselves that way!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The princes, that is, the king’s officials, come to the temple gate as judges.
> Verses 12–15: Jeremiah’s defense is brave: He takes nothing back. He repeats: Turn back! And he says: I am in your hands. But if you kill me, you will shed innocent blood.
> Verse 16: The princes and the people decide: Jeremiah is innocent.
> Verses 17–19: The elders remember the prophet Micah (Micah 3:12). About 100 years earlier he had said something similar. King Hezekiah did not kill him, but turned back, and God spared Jerusalem. A beautiful example of how people can learn from history.

## The prophet Uriah is killed (Verses 20–24)

[20] There was also another man who prophesied in the LORD’s name:
Uriah, the son of Shemaiah, from Kiriath Jearim.
He prophesied against this city and against this land,
just as Jeremiah had.
[21] When King Jehoiakim, with all his warriors and all the princes, heard his words,
the king tried to put him to death.
But when Uriah heard about it, he was afraid.
He fled and went to Egypt.
[22] Then King Jehoiakim sent Elnathan, the son of Achbor,
and some men with him to Egypt.
[23] They brought Uriah out of Egypt to King Jehoiakim,
who killed him with the sword,
and threw his dead body into the graves of the common people.
[24] But Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, protected Jeremiah,
so that they didn’t hand him over to the people to be put to death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–23: Another prophet, Uriah, had the same message as Jeremiah. He fled to Egypt, but King Jehoiakim had him brought back and killed. This shows how dangerous Jeremiah’s situation was. Prophets who told the truth were persecuted, and that still happens today in many countries.
> Verse 24: Jeremiah survived because Ahikam protected him. Ahikam was a high official. His family (Shaphan) helped Jeremiah again and again. Ahikam’s son Gedaliah later became governor (Chapter 40). Courage and protection from influential people can save lives.
