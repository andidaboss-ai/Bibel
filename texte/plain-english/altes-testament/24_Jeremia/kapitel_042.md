# Jeremiah – Chapter 42
### Should we go to Egypt?

---

## The people ask Jeremiah for advice (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then all the army commanders,
Johanan, the son of Kareah,
Jezaniah, the son of Hoshaiah,
and all the people, from the least to the greatest, came near.
[2] They said to Jeremiah the prophet,
“Please listen to our request,
and pray for us to the LORD your God,
for all of us who are left.
For only a few of us are left out of many,
as you can see with your own eyes.
[3] Pray that the LORD your God may show us the way we should go,
and what we should do.”
[4] Then Jeremiah the prophet said to them,
“I have heard you.
Look, I will pray to the LORD your God as you have asked.
Whatever the LORD answers you, I will tell you.
I will keep nothing back from you.”
[5] Then they said to Jeremiah,
“May the LORD be a true and faithful witness among us,
if we don’t do everything that the LORD your God sends you to tell us.
[6] Whether it is good or bad,
we will obey the voice of the LORD our God, to whom we are sending you,
so that it may go well with us
when we obey the voice of the LORD our God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The survivors come to Jeremiah and ask him: Ask God for us what we should do.
> Verses 5–6: They solemnly promise: Whatever God says, whether we like it or not, we will obey.
> A beautiful promise. But you can tell: They have actually already decided to go to Egypt (41:17).

---

## God’s answer: Stay in the land! (Verses 7–22)

[7] After 10 days, the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah.
[8] Then he called Johanan, the son of Kareah,
and all the army commanders who were with him,
and all the people, from the least to the greatest.
[9] He said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, to whom you sent me to bring your request, says:
[10] ‘If you will stay in this land,
then I will build you up, and not tear you down.
I will plant you, and not pull you up.
For I am sorry about the disaster that I have brought on you.
[11] Don’t be afraid of the king of Babylon,
whom you fear.
Don’t be afraid of him,’ says the LORD,
‘for I am with you to save you,
and to rescue you from his hand.
[12] I will show you mercy,
so that he will have mercy on you
and let you return to your own land.
[13] But suppose you say, “We will not stay in this land,”
and so you don’t obey the voice of the LORD your God.
[14] Suppose you say, “No, we will go to the land of Egypt,
where we will see no war,
nor hear the sound of the trumpet,
nor go hungry for bread.
We will live there.”
[15] Then hear the LORD’s word, you who are left of Judah!
The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“If you are determined to go to Egypt,
and go to live there,
[16] then the sword that you fear
will catch up with you there in the land of Egypt.
The famine that you are afraid of
will follow close behind you there in Egypt,
and you will die there.
[17] So it will be with all the men who are determined to go to Egypt to live there.
They will die by the sword, by famine, and by disease.
None of them will survive or escape
from the disaster that I will bring on them.”
[18] For the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Just as my anger and my fury were poured out
on the people of Jerusalem,
so my fury will be poured out on you
when you go to Egypt.
You will become an object of horror and shock,
a curse and a disgrace,
and you will never see this place again.”’
[19] The LORD has spoken to you, you who are left of Judah:
‘Don’t go to Egypt!’
Know for certain that I have warned you today.
[20] For you have deceived yourselves.
You sent me to the LORD your God, saying,
‘Pray for us to the LORD our God.
Tell us everything the LORD our God says,
and we will do it.’
[21] I have told you today,
but you have not obeyed the voice of the LORD your God
in anything he has sent me to tell you.
[22] So now know for certain
that you will die by the sword, by famine, and by disease
in the place where you want to go to live.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Jeremiah waits 10 days for God’s answer. He does not answer from his own opinion, but waits patiently for God.
> Verse 10: God’s answer is full of kindness: Stay in the land, then I will build you up. “I am sorry about the disaster that I have brought on you.” God shows compassion for his suffering people.
> Verses 11–12: Don’t be afraid of Babylon! I am with you.
> Verse 14: Egypt seemed to be the safe place: no war, no hunger. But God warns: What you fear will catch up with you there.
> Verses 20–21: Jeremiah realizes that they never really wanted to listen. They had already decided. You cannot ask God for advice and then only do what you wanted to do anyway.
> Note: This text is not a general statement against people who flee from war and hardship. It is about a specific situation in which God had expressly told the people to stay in the land. In many places the Bible tells of flight and of finding shelter in a foreign land, for example of Jesus and his parents, who flee to Egypt (Matthew 2:13–15).
