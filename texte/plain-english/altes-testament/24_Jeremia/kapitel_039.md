# Jeremiah – Chapter 39
### Jerusalem falls

---

## The city is captured (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the ninth year of Zedekiah, king of Judah, in the tenth month,
Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, and his whole army came against Jerusalem,
and besieged it.
[2] In the eleventh year of Zedekiah, in the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month,
the city wall was broken through.
[3] All the princes of the king of Babylon came in,
and sat down in the Middle Gate:
Nergal Sharezer, Samgarnebo,
Sarsechim the Rabsaris,
Nergal Sharezer the Rabmag,
and all the rest of the princes of the king of Babylon.
[4] When Zedekiah, king of Judah, and all the soldiers saw them,
they fled and left the city by night,
by way of the king’s garden,
through the gate between the two walls.
The king went out toward the Arabah.
[5] But the army of the Chaldeans chased them,
and caught up with Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho.
They captured him and brought him up
to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, at Riblah in the land of Hamath.
There Nebuchadnezzar passed judgment on him.
[6] Then the king of Babylon killed Zedekiah’s sons in Riblah,
in front of his eyes.
The king of Babylon also killed all the nobles of Judah.
[7] He also put out Zedekiah’s eyes,
and bound him in chains to take him to Babylon.
[8] The Chaldeans burned the king’s palace and the people’s houses with fire,
and broke down the walls of Jerusalem.
[9] Then Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard,
carried away to Babylon as captives
the rest of the people who were left in the city,
and the deserters who had gone over to him,
and the rest of the people who were left.
[10] But Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard,
left some of the poor people, who had nothing, in the land of Judah.
At that time he gave them vineyards and fields.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now what Jeremiah had announced for 40 years happens.
> Verses 1–2: The siege lasted about a year and a half (588–587 BC). Then the Babylonians broke through the wall.
> Verse 3: “Rabsaris” and “Rabmag” are Babylonian titles, something like “chief court official” and “chief of the magicians” (advisers).
> Verses 4–5: King Zedekiah flees during the night, but is captured near Jericho.
> Verses 6–7: What happens next is cruel: Zedekiah has to watch his sons being killed. After that, his eyes are put out. The last thing he saw was the death of his children. The Bible reports this cruelty soberly. It shows how terrible war is. Violence against prisoners and children is a crime.
> Verse 8: The royal palace and the houses are burned, and the walls are torn down. (The destruction of the temple is told in chapter 52.)
> Verse 10: A surprising turn: The poor, who had nothing, stay in the land and even receive vineyards and fields. Things are turned around.
> Anyone who has been traumatized by war and violence needs help. Help is available: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Jeremiah is set free (Verses 11–14)

[11] Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, gave Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard,
a command about Jeremiah. He said,
[12] “Take him, and look after him.
Do him no harm,
but do for him whatever he asks you.”
[13] So Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard,
Nebushazban the Rabsaris,
Nergal Sharezer the Rabmag,
and all the chief officers of the king of Babylon
[14] sent and took Jeremiah out of the courtyard of the guard.
They handed him over to Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan,
to take him home.
So he lived among the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Of all people, it is the enemy king who treats Jeremiah well. Perhaps he had heard that Jeremiah had advised surrender.
> Verse 14: Jeremiah is handed over to Gedaliah. Gedaliah is the son of Ahikam, who had already saved Jeremiah earlier (26:24). The family of Shaphan stays loyal to Jeremiah. Gedaliah soon becomes governor of the land (Chapter 40).
> (Jeremiah 40:1–6 tells about this release somewhat differently and in more detail.)

## A promise for Ebedmelech (Verses 15–18)

[15] The LORD’s word had come to Jeremiah
while he was locked up in the courtyard of the guard. He said,
[16] “Go and speak to Ebedmelech the Ethiopian. Say,
‘The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Look, I will make my words come true against this city,
for disaster and not for good.
On that day they will happen in front of your eyes.
[17] But I will rescue you on that day,” says the LORD.
“You will not be handed over to the men you are afraid of.
[18] For I will surely save you.
You won’t fall by the sword,
but you will escape with your life,
because you have put your trust in me,” says the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> God does not forget what Ebedmelech did for Jeremiah. The foreign man from Cush who had the courage to pull Jeremiah out of the mud is saved.
> Verse 18: The reason: “Because you have put your trust in me.” Ebedmelech was not an Israelite, but he trusted God. God’s rescue is for everyone who trusts him, no matter where they come from.
