# Jeremiah – Chapter 37
### Jeremiah in prison

---

## The Babylonians withdraw for a short time (Verses 1–10)

[1] Zedekiah, the son of Josiah, ruled as king
in the place of Coniah, the son of Jehoiakim.
Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, had made him king in the land of Judah.
[2] But neither he, nor his servants, nor the people of the land
listened to the LORD’s words,
which he spoke through the prophet Jeremiah.
[3] King Zedekiah sent Jehucal, the son of Shelemiah,
and Zephaniah, the son of Maaseiah, the priest,
to the prophet Jeremiah. They said,
“Please pray to the LORD our God for us.”
[4] At that time Jeremiah could still come and go among the people,
for they had not put him in prison yet.
[5] Pharaoh’s army had come out of Egypt.
When the Chaldeans who were besieging Jerusalem heard the news about them,
they withdrew from Jerusalem.
[6] Then the LORD’s word came to the prophet Jeremiah. He said,
[7] “The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Tell the king of Judah, who sent you to me to ask me:
“Look, Pharaoh’s army, which has come out to help you,
will go back to Egypt, to its own land.
[8] The Chaldeans will come back,
and fight against this city.
They will capture it and burn it with fire.”’
[9] “The LORD says,
‘Don’t deceive yourselves by saying,
“The Chaldeans will surely go away from us,”
for they will not go away.
[10] For even if you had defeated the whole army of the Chaldeans who fight against you,
and only wounded men were left among them,
each of them would get up in his tent
and burn this city with fire.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Zedekiah asks Jeremiah to pray. He respects Jeremiah, but he still does not listen to him. He is a weak king who cannot make up his mind.
> Verse 5: Suddenly hope rises: Egypt sends an army, and the Babylonians withdraw. Everyone thinks: We are saved!
> Verses 7–10: But Jeremiah says: Don’t deceive yourselves! The Egyptians will turn back, and the Babylonians will return. Even if only wounded men were left, they would capture the city. God’s plan stands firm.

---

## Jeremiah is arrested (Verses 11–21)

[11] When the army of the Chaldeans had withdrawn from Jerusalem
out of fear of Pharaoh’s army,
[12] Jeremiah set out from Jerusalem to go to the land of Benjamin,
to receive his share of land there among the people.
[13] When he was at the Benjamin Gate,
a captain of the guard was there whose name was Irijah,
the son of Shelemiah, the son of Hananiah.
He seized Jeremiah the prophet and said,
“You are deserting to the Chaldeans!”
[14] Jeremiah said, “That is a lie!
I am not deserting to the Chaldeans.”
But Irijah didn’t listen to him.
He seized Jeremiah and brought him to the princes.
[15] The princes were angry with Jeremiah.
They beat him and put him in prison in the house of Jonathan the scribe,
for they had turned it into a prison.
[16] Jeremiah was put into a dungeon, into the cells,
and he stayed there many days.
[17] Then King Zedekiah sent for him and had him brought out.
The king asked him secretly in his palace,
“Is there any word from the LORD?”
Jeremiah said, “There is.”
And he said, “You will be handed over to the king of Babylon.”
[18] Jeremiah also said to King Zedekiah,
“What wrong have I done against you, against your servants, or against this people,
that you have put me in prison?
[19] Where are your prophets now who prophesied to you and said,
‘The king of Babylon will not come against you or against this land’?
[20] Now please listen, my lord the king.
Please accept my request,
and don’t send me back to the house of Jonathan the scribe,
or I will die there.”
[21] Then King Zedekiah gave the command,
and they kept Jeremiah in the courtyard of the guard.
Every day they gave him a loaf of bread from the Bakers’ Street,
until all the bread in the city was gone.
So Jeremiah stayed in the courtyard of the guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Jeremiah wants to use the short break to settle a family matter in his home area of Benjamin, perhaps an inheritance.
> Verses 13–15: But he is suspected of deserting to the enemy. Because he had always said that people should surrender, no one believes him. He is beaten and thrown into an underground prison.
> Verse 17: The king secretly sends for him and asks: “Is there any word from the LORD?” Jeremiah stays honest, even though it is dangerous: “There is.” … “You will be handed over to the king of Babylon.”
> Verse 19: Jeremiah rightly asks: Where are the prophets now who said that Babylon would not come?
> Verses 20–21: Jeremiah asks not to have to go back to the terrible underground prison. The king has pity and has him kept in the courtyard of the guard, with one loaf of bread a day.
> Anyone today who is persecuted or locked up though innocent can recognize themselves in Jeremiah. Many people in many countries still suffer in prison today because of their faith or their opinion.
