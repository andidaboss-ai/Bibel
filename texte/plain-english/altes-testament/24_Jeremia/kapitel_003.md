# Jeremiah – Chapter 3
### Return, you unfaithful children!

---

## Come back to me (Verses 1–5)

[1] “They say, ‘If a man sends his wife away,
and she leaves him and becomes another man’s wife,
should he go back to her again?’
Wouldn’t that land be made very unclean?
But you have acted like a prostitute with many lovers.
Yet come back to me,” says the LORD.
[2] “Lift up your eyes to the bare hilltops, and look!
Where have you not been slept with?
You have sat by the road waiting for them,
like an Arab in the wilderness.
You have made the land unclean
with your prostitution and with your evil.
[3] That is why the showers have been held back,
and there has been no spring rain.
Yet you have had the forehead of a prostitute,
and you refused to be ashamed.
[4] From now on, won’t you call out to me,
‘My Father, you are the friend of my youth!’?
[5] “‘Will he stay angry forever?
Will he keep it up to the end?’
Look, you have talked like this,
and you have done evil things,
and you have had your way.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: According to the law (Deuteronomy 24:1–4), a man was not allowed to marry his divorced wife again if she had married someone else in the meantime. God says: According to this law, a return would be impossible. And yet God invites his unfaithful people: “Come back to me!” God’s love is greater than the law.
> Verses 2–3: Again the picture of unfaithfulness and prostitution for idol worship. It is about the people’s unfaithfulness to God, not a judgment on individual women. The drought is understood as a consequence of this unfaithfulness.
> Verses 4–5: The people do call out “My Father!”, but they keep doing evil. They think: God will surely not be angry forever.

---

## The two sisters Israel and Judah (Verses 6–11)

[6] In the days of King Josiah, the LORD also said to me,
“Have you seen what unfaithful Israel has done?
She has gone up on every high mountain and under every green tree,
and has acted like a prostitute there.
[7] After she had done all these things, I said,
‘She will come back to me.’
But she didn’t come back,
and her unfaithful sister Judah saw it.
[8] I saw that, because unfaithful Israel had committed adultery,
I had sent her away and given her a certificate of divorce.
Yet her sister, unfaithful Judah, was not afraid.
She also went and acted like a prostitute.
[9] Because she took her prostitution lightly,
the land was made unclean,
and she committed adultery with stones and with wood.
[10] Yet in spite of all this, her unfaithful sister Judah
has not come back to me with her whole heart,
but only in pretense,” says the LORD.
[11] The LORD said to me,
“Unfaithful Israel has shown herself more righteous than unfaithful Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here two sisters are compared: Israel (the northern kingdom) and Judah (the southern kingdom with Jerusalem). The northern kingdom had already been destroyed by Assyria about 100 years earlier (722 BC).
> Verse 8: The “certificate of divorce” is a picture of the exile of the northern kingdom.
> Verse 9: “Adultery with stones and with wood” means worshiping images of gods made of stone and wood.
> Verse 10: Under King Josiah there was a great reform (2 Kings 22–23). But Jeremiah says: Many only went along with it on the outside, not with their heart.
> Verse 11: Surprisingly, Judah is worse than Israel, because Judah saw what happened to her sister and still learned nothing.

---

## Return, you unfaithful children! (Verses 12–18)

[12] Go, and call out these words toward the north, and say,
‘Come back, unfaithful Israel,’ says the LORD.
‘I will not look at you in anger,
for I am merciful,’ says the LORD.
‘I will not stay angry forever.
[13] Only admit your guilt:
that you have rebelled against the LORD your God,
and have run here and there to strangers under every green tree,
and you have not obeyed my voice,’” says the LORD.
[14] “Return, unfaithful children,” says the LORD,
“for I am a husband to you.
I will take one of you from a city,
and two from a family,
and I will bring you to Zion.
[15] I will give you shepherds who follow my heart.
They will feed you with knowledge and understanding.
[16] In those days, when you have become many in the land,” says the LORD,
“they will no longer say, ‘the ark of the LORD’s covenant!’
It will not come to mind.
They won’t remember it.
They won’t miss it,
and no other will be made.
[17] At that time they will call Jerusalem ‘The LORD’s Throne.’
All the nations will be gathered to it,
to the LORD’s name, to Jerusalem.
They will no longer follow the stubbornness of their evil heart.
[18] In those days the house of Judah will walk with the house of Israel.
Together they will come out of the land of the north
to the land that I gave to your fathers as an inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–13: God even calls back the northern kingdom that was destroyed long ago: “Come back, unfaithful Israel … for I am merciful.” Only one thing is needed: to admit your own guilt.
> Verse 14: “Return, unfaithful children!” God is ready to bring home even the few, “one of you from a city, and two from a family.” Every single person counts.
> Verse 15: God promises good shepherds, that is, leaders who guide the people with knowledge and understanding.
> Verse 16: Amazing: People will no longer miss the ark of the covenant, the holiest object in the temple. For all of Jerusalem will be God’s throne (verse 17). God’s nearness will then no longer be tied to an object. We do not know what later happened to the ark of the covenant. After the destruction of Jerusalem, the Bible does not mention it anymore.
> Verses 17–18: All nations come to Jerusalem, and Israel and Judah are united again.

## A prayer of turning back (Verses 19–25)

[19] “But I said,
‘How I want to put you among my children,
and give you a pleasant land,
the most beautiful inheritance among the nations!’
And I said, ‘You will call me “My Father,”
and you will not turn away from following me.’
[20] “But as a wife is unfaithful and leaves her husband,
so you have been unfaithful to me, house of Israel,” says the LORD.
[21] A voice is heard on the bare hilltops:
the weeping and the pleading of the people of Israel,
because they have twisted their way.
They have forgotten the LORD their God.
[22] Return, unfaithful children,
and I will heal your unfaithfulness.
“Look, we come to you,
for you are the LORD our God.
[23] Truly, the help from the hills,
the noise on the mountains, is useless.
Truly, the salvation of Israel is in the LORD our God.
[24] But the shameful thing has eaten up what our fathers worked for, since our youth:
their flocks and their herds,
their sons and their daughters.
[25] Let us lie down in our shame,
and let our disgrace cover us.
For we have sinned against the LORD our God,
we and our fathers,
from our youth until this day.
We have not obeyed the voice of the LORD our God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: God had wanted so much for his people to call him “My Father” and to stay with him.
> Verse 21: On the hills where foreign gods used to be worshiped, weeping and praying can now be heard.
> Verse 22: God calls: “Return, unfaithful children, and I will heal your unfaithfulness.” And the people answer: “Look, we come to you!” A beautiful example of what turning back looks like.
> Verse 23: The people realize: The idols on the hills could not help. Only God saves.
> Verse 24: “The shameful thing” is a scornful name for the idol Baal. Idol worship has eaten up everything, even sons and daughters. This probably also refers to the terrible sacrifices of children (compare Jeremiah 7:31).
> Verse 25: An honest confession of guilt. It is the first step toward a new beginning.
