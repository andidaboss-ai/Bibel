# Jeremiah – Chapter 1
### Before I formed you in the womb

---

## Before we begin: Who was Jeremiah?

Jeremiah lived about 100 years after Isaiah, roughly from 627 until after 587 BC. He came from a family of priests from the village of Anathoth, not far from Jerusalem.
Jeremiah lived through a very hard time: The empire of Babylon became more and more powerful. For decades Jeremiah warned: If you do not turn back to God, Jerusalem will be destroyed. Many people did not want to hear this. Jeremiah was mocked, beaten, thrown into prison and put into a cistern. In the year 587 BC, Jerusalem really was destroyed by the Babylonians.
Jeremiah is the prophet whose feelings we learn the most about. He laments, weeps and argues with God. That is why he is sometimes called the “weeping prophet.”
But Jeremiah also brings great hope: God promises a “new covenant,” in which he writes his law on people’s hearts (Chapter 31).
The book has 52 chapters. It contains speeches, prayers, laments and stories from Jeremiah’s life. Jeremiah’s scribe was called Baruch. He wrote down many of Jeremiah’s words.
Jews and Christians read Jeremiah as God’s word in a time of collapse. His message: God is not far away, even in judgment, and he gives a new beginning.

## Jeremiah is called (Verses 1–10)

[1] These are the words of Jeremiah, the son of Hilkiah.
He was one of the priests who lived in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin.
[2] The LORD’s word came to him in the days of Josiah,
the son of Amon, king of Judah,
in the thirteenth year of his rule.
[3] It came also in the days of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah,
until the end of the eleventh year of Zedekiah, the son of Josiah, king of Judah,
until Jerusalem was carried away as captives in the fifth month.
[4] The LORD’s word came to me. He said:
[5] “Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you.
Before you were born, I made you holy.
I have appointed you as a prophet to the nations.”
[6] Then I said, “Oh, Lord GOD!
Look, I don’t know how to speak,
because I am a child.”
[7] But the LORD said to me,
“Don’t say, ‘I am a child.’
For you must go to everyone I send you to,
and you must say everything I command you.
[8] Don’t be afraid of them,
for I am with you to rescue you,” says the LORD.
[9] Then the LORD stretched out his hand and touched my mouth.
And the LORD said to me,
“Look, I have put my words in your mouth.
[10] Look, today I have set you over the nations and over the kingdoms,
to pull up and to tear down,
to destroy and to overthrow,
to build and to plant.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Jeremiah worked for about 40 years: from the 13th year of King Josiah (627 BC) until the destruction of Jerusalem (587 BC).
> Verse 5: A wonderful verse: God already knew Jeremiah before he was born. Every person is wanted and known by God, even before birth.
> Verse 6: Jeremiah resists: “I don’t know how to speak, because I am a child.” Many people feel too small for a big task. Moses also hesitated like this (Exodus 4:10).
> Verses 7–8: God’s answer: “Don’t say, ‘I am a child.’ … Don’t be afraid of them, for I am with you.” God trusts young people with something too.
> Verse 9: God touches Jeremiah’s mouth and puts his words into it.
> Verse 10: Six verbs describe Jeremiah’s task: Four of them are hard (pull up, tear down, destroy, overthrow), two are full of hope (build, plant). The whole book will be like this: much judgment, but a new beginning at the end.

## Two pictures: an almond branch and a boiling pot (Verses 11–19)

[11] The LORD’s word came to me again. He said,
“Jeremiah, what do you see?”
I said, “I see a branch of an almond tree.”
[12] Then the LORD said to me,
“You have seen well,
for I watch over my word to make it happen.”
[13] The LORD’s word came to me a second time. He said,
“What do you see?”
I said, “I see a boiling pot,
and it is tipping away from the north.”
[14] Then the LORD said to me,
“Out of the north, disaster will break out
on all the people who live in the land.
[15] For look, I will call all the families of the kingdoms of the north,” says the LORD.
“They will come, and each of them will set up his throne
at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem,
and against all its walls all around,
and against all the cities of Judah.
[16] I will speak my judgments against them
because of all their evil,
because they have left me,
and have burned incense to other gods,
and have worshiped the works of their own hands.
[17] “But you, put your belt around your waist.
Get up, and tell them everything that I command you.
Don’t be terrified of them,
or I will terrify you in front of them.
[18] For look, today I have made you a fortified city,
an iron pillar, and bronze walls against the whole land:
against the kings of Judah, against its princes,
against its priests, and against the people of the land.
[19] They will fight against you,
but they will not defeat you,
for I am with you,” says the LORD, “to rescue you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: A play on words in Hebrew: The almond tree is called “shaked,” which means “the watchful one,” because in spring it is the first tree to bloom, as if it were already awake. God says: I am “shoked,” I watch over my word. What I say happens.
> Verses 13–15: The boiling pot that boils over from the north is a picture of the enemies who will come from the north. Babylon actually lay to the east, but the armies came to Israel by way of the north, because they had to go around the desert.
> Verse 16: The reason for the disaster: The people have left God and worshiped idols.
> Verses 17–19: Jeremiah will meet a lot of opposition: from kings, priests and the people. But God makes him strong like a fortress, an iron pillar. “I am with you … to rescue you.” This does not mean that Jeremiah will not suffer, but he will not be destroyed.
