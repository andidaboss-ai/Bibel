# Jeremiah – Chapter 45
### A word for Baruch

---

## Are you looking for great things? (Verses 1–5)

[1] This is the message that Jeremiah the prophet spoke to Baruch, the son of Neriah,
when he wrote these words in a book as Jeremiah dictated them,
in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah. He said,
[2] “The LORD, the God of Israel, says to you, Baruch:
[3] ‘You said, “Woe is me now!
For the LORD has added sorrow to my pain!
I am weary with my groaning,
and I find no rest.”’
[4] Tell him: The LORD says:
‘Look, what I have built, I will tear down,
and what I have planted, I will pull up,
and that in the whole land.
[5] Are you looking for great things for yourself?
Don’t look for them!
For look, I will bring disaster on all people,’ says the LORD,
‘but I will let you escape with your life wherever you go.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This short chapter is a personal word to Baruch, Jeremiah’s faithful scribe. It comes from the time when Baruch wrote the first scroll (Chapter 36, in the year 605 BC).
> Verse 3: Baruch is exhausted: “Woe is me now! … I am weary with my groaning, and I find no rest.” The prophets’ helpers also suffered under the heavy message. Anyone who helps others can become tired and exhausted themselves.
> Verse 4: God answers: It hurts me too. I have to tear down what I myself have built.
> Verse 5: “Are you looking for great things for yourself? Don’t look for them!” Perhaps Baruch had hoped for respect or a career. God says: In this hard time, that is not important. But I promise you: You will survive, wherever you go.
> A comforting word for all who feel exhausted: Great successes are not what counts, but that God protects your life. If you feel burned out, you may get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
