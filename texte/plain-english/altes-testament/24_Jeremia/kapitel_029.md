# Jeremiah – Chapter 29
### A letter to the exiles

---

## Seek the peace of the city (Verses 1–9)

[1] These are the words of the letter that Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem
to the elders who were left among the captives,
and to the priests, to the prophets,
and to all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had carried away as captives
from Jerusalem to Babylon.
[2] (This was after King Jeconiah, the queen mother, the court officials,
the princes of Judah and Jerusalem, the craftsmen, and the metalworkers
had left Jerusalem.)
[3] He sent it with Elasah, the son of Shaphan,
and Gemariah, the son of Hilkiah,
whom Zedekiah, king of Judah, sent to Babylon,
to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.
The letter said:
[4] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel,
says to all the captives whom I have had carried away from Jerusalem to Babylon:
[5] “Build houses and live in them.
Plant gardens and eat their fruit.
[6] Marry and have sons and daughters.
Find wives for your sons,
and give your daughters in marriage,
so that they have sons and daughters.
Become many there, and don’t become fewer.
[7] Seek the peace of the city where I have had you carried away as captives,
and pray to the LORD for it,
for in its peace you will have peace.”
[8] For the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Don’t let your prophets and your fortune-tellers who are among you deceive you.
Don’t listen to the dreams that you encourage them to dream.
[9] For they prophesy lies to you in my name.
I have not sent them,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah writes a letter to the people who had been carried off to Babylon in 597 BC. The letter is taken along by messengers whom the king sends to Babylon.
> Verses 5–6: Jeremiah’s advice is very practical: Settle in! Build houses, plant gardens, marry, have children. The exile will last a long time. Don’t live as if you were just waiting, but live your life.
> Verse 7 is one of the most important verses in Jeremiah: “Seek the peace of the city … and pray to the LORD for it, for in its peace you will have peace.” The exiles are to pray for Babylon, for the city of their enemies! That is revolutionary. To this day, this verse is a guiding word for people who live in a foreign land, for migrants and refugees, and for everyone who works for the common good. The Hebrew word for peace, “shalom,” also means well-being.
> Verses 8–9: In Babylon too there are false prophets who say: Soon we will go home! Jeremiah warns: Don’t believe them.

---

## Thoughts of peace (Verses 10–14)

[10] For the LORD says,
“When 70 years are over for Babylon,
I will come to you,
and keep my good promise to you,
to bring you back to this place.
[11] For I know the thoughts that I think toward you,” says the LORD,
“thoughts of peace, and not of disaster,
to give you hope and a future.
[12] You will call on me,
and you will come and pray to me,
and I will listen to you.
[13] You will seek me and find me,
when you search for me with all your heart.
[14] I will let you find me,” says the LORD,
“and I will turn your fate around.
I will gather you from all the nations,
and from all the places where I have driven you,” says the LORD.
“I will bring you back to the place
from which I had you carried away as captives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11 is one of the most loved verses in the whole Bible: “For I know the thoughts that I think toward you … thoughts of peace, and not of disaster, to give you hope and a future.” It has given courage to many people in hard times.
> It was first spoken to people who had lost everything and were living in a foreign land. It is to them in particular that God says: I have a good plan for you.
> Verse 13: “You will seek me and find me, when you search for me with all your heart.” God can be found in Babylon too, even without a temple. That was an important insight: People can worship God everywhere.

## About those who stayed in Jerusalem (Verses 15–19)

[15] Because you have said,
“The LORD has raised up prophets for us in Babylon,”
[16] the LORD says this about the king who sits on David’s throne,
and about all the people who live in this city,
your brothers who haven’t gone with you into captivity.
[17] The LORD of Armies says:
“Look, I will send the sword, the famine, and disease against them.
I will make them like rotten figs
that are so bad they can’t be eaten.
[18] I will chase them with the sword, with famine, and with disease.
I will make them be tossed back and forth among all the kingdoms of the earth.
They will be a horror, something that shocks people, something people hiss at,
and a disgrace among all the nations where I have driven them,
[19] because they have not listened to my words,” says the LORD,
“which I sent to them through my servants the prophets, early and tirelessly.
But you would not listen,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The exiles hoped that Jerusalem would remain standing and that they could soon return. Jeremiah says: It is Jerusalem that will fall. Those who stayed there are the “bad figs” from chapter 24.

## False prophets in Babylon (Verses 20–32)

[20] So hear the LORD’s word,
all you captives whom I have sent away from Jerusalem to Babylon.
[21] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says this
about Ahab, the son of Kolaiah,
and about Zedekiah, the son of Maaseiah,
who prophesy a lie to you in my name:
“Look, I will hand them over to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
and he will kill them in front of your eyes.
[22] Because of them, all the captives of Judah who are in Babylon
will use this curse:
‘May the LORD make you like Zedekiah and like Ahab,
whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire.’
[23] For they have done shameful things in Israel.
They have committed adultery with their neighbors’ wives,
and have spoken lies in my name,
which I didn’t command them.
I am the one who knows, and I am a witness,” says the LORD.
[24] Say this about Shemaiah the Nehelamite:
[25] “The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says,
‘You have sent letters in your own name
to all the people who are in Jerusalem,
and to Zephaniah, the son of Maaseiah, the priest,
and to all the priests. You wrote:
[26] “The LORD has made you priest in the place of Jehoiada the priest,
so that there are officers in the LORD’s house.
They must deal with every man who is crazy and acts like a prophet.
You should put him in the stocks and in chains.
[27] So why have you not punished Jeremiah from Anathoth,
who acts like a prophet among you?
[28] For he has sent us a message in Babylon:
The captivity will be long.
Build houses, and live in them.
Plant gardens, and eat their fruit.”’”
[29] Zephaniah the priest read this letter
so that Jeremiah the prophet heard it.
[30] Then the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah. He said,
[31] “Send this message to all the captives:
‘The LORD says this about Shemaiah the Nehelamite:
“Shemaiah has prophesied to you,
though I didn’t send him,
and he has made you trust in a lie.”

[32] That is why the LORD says,
“Look, I will punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite and his descendants.
No man of his family will be left to live among this people.
He won’t see the good that I will do for my people,” says the LORD,
“because he has spoken rebellion against the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–23: Two false prophets in Babylon, Ahab and Zedekiah, told lies and committed adultery. The Babylonian king had them executed, probably because they called for an uprising. The Bible mentions this cruel punishment as part of the history; it does not approve of it.
> Verses 24–28: Another man in Babylon, Shemaiah, was angry about Jeremiah’s letter. He wrote to the priest in Jerusalem: Why do you let Jeremiah run around freely? Put him in the stocks!
> Verse 29: Zephaniah reads this letter out to Jeremiah. Perhaps he wanted to warn Jeremiah.
> Verses 31–32: God says: Shemaiah will not live to see the return home.
> This chapter shows: Even in a foreign land, there was argument about who speaks in God’s name. The uncomfortable truth was harder to bear than the beautiful lie.
