# Jeremiah – Chapter 14
### The great drought

---

## No rain in the land (Verses 1–6)

[1] This is the LORD’s word that came to Jeremiah about the drought:
[2] “Judah mourns,
and its gates are weak.
They sit in black on the ground.
The cry of Jerusalem goes up.
[3] Their important people send their servants for water.
They come to the cisterns and find no water.
They come back with empty jars.
They are disappointed and ashamed,
and they cover their heads.
[4] Because the ground is cracked,
because there has been no rain in the land,
the farmers are disappointed.
They cover their heads.
[5] Yes, even the doe in the field gives birth
and leaves her young,
because there is no grass.
[6] The wild donkeys stand on the bare hilltops.
They gasp for air like jackals.
Their eyes grow dim,
because there are no plants.

> **What does this mean?**
> A terrible drought hits the land. Jeremiah describes it exactly:
> Verse 3: The servants find no water in the cisterns and come back with empty jars.
> Verse 4: The ground is cracked; the farmers cannot sow anything.
> Verses 5–6: Even the animals suffer: A doe leaves her newborn because she cannot find food. Wild donkeys gasp for air.
> Today too, many people and animals suffer from droughts. The Bible takes this suffering seriously.

---

## A prayer in distress (Verses 7–12)

[7] Even though our sins speak against us,
act for the sake of your name, LORD.
For we have turned away from you many times.
We have sinned against you.
[8] You hope of Israel,
its Savior in the time of trouble,
why are you like a foreigner in the land,
and like a traveler who stops only to stay for a night?
[9] Why are you like a man who is taken by surprise,
like a warrior who can’t save?
Yet you, LORD, are among us,
and we are called by your name.
Don’t leave us.
[10] The LORD says this about this people:
“They have loved to wander like this.
They have not held back their feet.
That is why the LORD does not accept them.
Now he will remember their guilt,
and punish them for their sins.”
[11] The LORD said to me,
“Don’t pray for the good of this people.
[12] When they fast, I will not listen to their cry.
When they offer burnt offerings and grain offerings, I will not accept them.
Instead, I will destroy them by the sword, by famine, and by disease.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–9: A beautiful prayer of the people: “You hope of Israel … why are you like a foreigner in the land, and like a traveler who stops only to stay for a night? … You, LORD, are among us … Don’t leave us.” People can still pray words like these in times of trouble today.
> Verses 10–12: But God’s answer is hard: The people have gone too far away. Fasting and sacrifices alone do not help now if their life does not change. For the third time God says to Jeremiah: Don’t pray for this people.

---

## The false prophets (Verses 13–18)

[13] Then I said, “Oh, Lord GOD!
Look, the prophets tell them,
‘You will not see the sword,
and you will not have famine.
I will give you lasting peace in this place.’”
[14] Then the LORD said to me,
“The prophets are prophesying lies in my name.
I didn’t send them.
I didn’t command them.
I didn’t speak to them.
They prophesy to you a false vision,
fortune-telling, worthless things,
and the deceit of their own heart.
[15] That is why the LORD says this about the prophets
who prophesy in my name, though I didn’t send them,
and who say, ‘There will be no sword and famine in this land’:
Those prophets will be destroyed by sword and famine.
[16] The people they prophesy to will be thrown out into the streets of Jerusalem,
because of the famine and the sword.
There will be no one to bury them,
not them, their wives, their sons, or their daughters,
for I will pour out their own evil on them.
[17] “Say this word to them:
‘Let my eyes run with tears night and day,
and let them not stop.
For the virgin daughter of my people is broken with a great break,
with a very serious wound.
[18] If I go out into the field,
look, there are those killed by the sword!
If I go into the city,
look, there are those sick from hunger!
For both the prophet and the priest wander through the land,
and they don’t know what they are doing.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Jeremiah tries to excuse his people: The false prophets deceived them! They promised peace.
> Verse 14: God says: These prophets did not speak on my behalf. They made up their message themselves. It is important to test whether someone really speaks in God’s name or only says what people want to hear.
> Verses 17–18: Jeremiah weeps day and night. In the field he sees those who were killed, in the city those who are starving. A shocking picture of war.

---

## Don’t reject us! (Verses 19–22)

[19] Have you completely rejected Judah?
Do you hate Zion?
Why have you struck us,
so that there is no healing for us?
We hoped for peace, but nothing good came.
We hoped for a time of healing, but there was terror!
[20] LORD, we admit our evil,
and the guilt of our ancestors,
for we have sinned against you.
[21] Do not hate us, for the sake of your name.
Do not dishonor the throne of your glory.
Remember, and don’t break your covenant with us.
[22] Can any of the worthless idols of the nations make it rain?
Or can the sky give showers?

Isn’t it you, LORD our God?
So we will hope in you,
for you have made all these things.

> **What does this mean?**
> A second prayer, even more urgent than the first.
> Verse 20: The people honestly confess their guilt.
> Verse 21: They remind God of his own name and of his covenant: God, you promised to be faithful!
> Verse 22: A strong confession: None of the idols can make rain. Only you, God. “So we will hope in you.” Even in the deepest distress, the people do not give up their hope in God.
