# Jeremiah – Chapter 12
### Why do the wicked do so well?

---

## Jeremiah argues with God (Verses 1–6)

[1] You are righteous, LORD,
when I argue with you.
Yet I want to bring my case to you:
Why does the way of the wicked succeed?
Why do all who are very unfaithful live in comfort?
[2] You have planted them.
Yes, they have taken root.
They grow.
Yes, they produce fruit.
You are near in their mouth,
but far from their heart.
[3] But you, LORD, know me.
You see me, and test how my heart is toward you.
Drag them away like sheep to be slaughtered,
and set them apart for the day of slaughter.
[4] How long will the land mourn,
and the plants of the whole country wither?
Because of the evil of those who live in it,
the animals and birds are swept away,
because the people said, “He won’t see what happens to us in the end.”
[5] “If you have run with men on foot,
and they have tired you out,
then how can you race against horses?
If you feel safe only in a land of peace,
then what will you do in the thick forest of the Jordan?
[6] For even your brothers,
and the family of your father,
even they have been unfaithful to you!
Even they have shouted loudly after you!
Don’t believe them,
even though they speak beautiful words to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Jeremiah asks a question that people still ask today: Why do evil people do so well? They talk piously, but their heart is far away from God. And still they are successful. Job and Psalm 73 also ask this question.
> Verse 1: Jeremiah begins with “You are righteous,” and yet he argues with God. Faith and honest questions belong together.
> Verse 3: Jeremiah is so hurt that he asks God to punish the evil people. He expresses his anger openly before God, but he does not act himself.
> Verse 5: God’s answer is surprising: He does not explain anything, but says: It is going to get even harder! If you get tired running with people on foot, how will you keep up with horses? The “thick forest of the Jordan” was a dangerous jungle full of lions.
> Verse 6: Even Jeremiah’s own family betrayed him. An especially painful experience.

---

## God laments over his inheritance (Verses 7–13)

[7] “I have left my house.
I have abandoned my inheritance.
I have given the beloved of my soul
into the hands of her enemies.
[8] My inheritance has become to me like a lion in the forest.
She has roared against me.
That is why I have hated her.
[9] Is my inheritance to me like a speckled bird of prey?
Are the birds of prey against her all around?
Go, gather all the wild animals.
Bring them to eat.
[10] Many shepherds have destroyed my vineyard.
They have trampled my portion under their feet.
They have made my pleasant portion a ruined wilderness.
[11] They have made it a ruin.
It lies ruined and mourns before me.
The whole land is ruined,
because no one cares.
[12] Destroyers have come on all the bare hilltops in the wilderness,
for the sword of the LORD eats up the land from one end to the other end.
No one has peace.
[13] They have sown wheat, and have harvested thorns.
They have worn themselves out, and gained nothing.
You will be ashamed of your harvest,
because of the LORD’s fierce anger.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here God himself speaks, and you can feel his pain: “I have given the beloved of my soul into the hands of her enemies.” God does not do this gladly. It hurts him like someone who has lost their love.
> Verse 8: “That is why I have hated her” is the language of hurt love. The people have turned against God like a roaring lion.
> Verses 10–11: The “shepherds” here are the enemy commanders who destroy the land. And no one cares.
> Verse 13: They sow wheat and harvest thorns. All their work is for nothing.

---

## There is hope for the neighboring nations too (Verses 14–17)

[14] The LORD says,
“About all my evil neighbors,
who attack the inheritance which I gave my people Israel:
Look, I will pull them up out of their land,
and I will pull up the house of Judah from among them.
[15] After I have pulled them up,
I will turn back and have compassion on them.
I will bring them back again,
each one to his inheritance,
and each one to his land.
[16] And if they will carefully learn the ways of my people,
to swear by my name, ‘As surely as the LORD lives,’
just as they taught my people to swear by Baal,
then they will be built up among my people.
[17] But if they will not listen,
then I will pull up that nation,
pull it up and destroy it,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: The neighboring nations that attacked Israel will themselves be pulled out of their land.
> Verse 15: But then the surprise: God will also have compassion on them and bring them back to their land! God’s mercy is not only for Israel.
> Verse 16: If the other nations learn to honor the true God, they will get a place among God’s people. The door is open to everyone.
> Here the words from Jeremiah’s call come back: “pull up” and “build” (1:10).
