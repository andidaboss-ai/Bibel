# Jeremiah – Chapter 51
### Babylon will sink

---

## Flee from Babylon! (Verses 1–14)

[1] The LORD says:
“Look, I will stir up a destroying wind
against Babylon,
and against those who live in Lebkamai.
[2] I will send strangers to Babylon,
who will winnow her like grain.
They will empty her land.
For in the day of trouble
they will be against her on every side.
[3] Let the archer bend his bow against the one who bends his bow,
and against the one who stands up proudly in his armor!
Don’t spare her young men!
Destroy her whole army completely!
[4] They will fall down dead in the land of the Chaldeans,
pierced through in her streets.
[5] For Israel and Judah have not been abandoned
by their God, the LORD of Armies,
even though their land is full of guilt
against the Holy One of Israel.
[6] Flee out of the middle of Babylon!
Everyone, save your own life!
Don’t be destroyed because of her guilt,
for it is the time of the LORD’s vengeance.
He will pay her back what she deserves.
[7] Babylon has been a golden cup in the LORD’s hand,
which made the whole earth drunk.
The nations have drunk her wine.
That is why the nations have gone mad.
[8] Babylon has suddenly fallen and been shattered!
Wail for her!
Get balm for her pain.
Perhaps she can be healed.
[9] ‘We tried to heal Babylon,
but she could not be healed.
Leave her, and let’s each go to our own country.
For her judgment reaches up to heaven,
and rises up to the skies.’
[10] ‘The LORD has brought out our righteousness.
Come, let’s tell in Zion
what the LORD our God has done.’
[11] Sharpen the arrows!
Hold the shields firmly!
The LORD has stirred up the spirit of the kings of the Medes,
because his plan is against Babylon, to destroy it.
For this is the LORD’s vengeance,
the vengeance for his temple.
[12] Raise a signal flag against the walls of Babylon!
Strengthen the guard!
Post the watchmen!
Prepare the ambushes!
For the LORD has both planned and done
what he said about the people of Babylon.
[13] You who live by many waters,
rich in treasures,
your end has come,
the measure of your greed.
[14] The LORD of Armies has sworn by himself:
‘I will surely fill you with men, as with locusts,
and they will raise a battle cry against you.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Lebkamai” is a secret name for Chaldea (Babylonia), again formed by swapping letters, like “Sheshach” (25:26).
> Verses 3–4: Harsh language of war. It describes the end of the cruel great power Babylon. It is not permission for people to wage war or to take revenge.
> Verse 5: A comforting sentence: Israel and Judah have not been “abandoned” by their God. The Hebrew word makes us think of a widow who is left alone. God has not abandoned his people, even though it has become guilty.
> Verse 7: Babylon was like a golden cup from which the nations drank an intoxicating wine. The Book of Revelation takes up this picture (Revelation 17:4; 18:3).
> Verses 8–9: People try to heal Babylon, but it is no longer possible.
> Verse 11: The Medes (together with the Persians) conquered Babylon in 539 BC.
> Verse 13: Babylon lay “by many waters”: on the Euphrates with many canals.

## The Creator and the idols (Verses 15–19)

[15] “God has made the earth by his power.
He has set the world in place by his wisdom.
By his understanding he has stretched out the heavens.
[16] When he speaks, the waters in the heavens roar.
He makes the clouds rise from the ends of the earth.
He makes lightning for the rain,
and brings the wind out of his storehouses.
[17] Every person has become stupid and without knowledge.
Every goldsmith is put to shame by his carved image,
for his cast images are a lie,
and there is no breath in them.
[18] They are worthless, a work of deception.
In the time of their punishment they will be destroyed.
[19] The portion of Jacob is not like these,
for he is the maker of all things,
including the tribe of his inheritance.
The LORD of Armies is his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses appear almost word for word already in Jeremiah 10:12–16. In the middle of the words about Babylon, Jeremiah reminds: The gods of Babylon are dead images. The God of Israel is the Creator of heaven and earth.

## The smashing hammer (Verses 20–26)

[20] “You are my battle ax, my weapon for battle.
With you I smash nations.
With you I destroy kingdoms.
[21] With you I smash the horse and its rider.
[22] With you I smash the chariot and its driver.
With you I smash man and woman.
With you I smash the old and the young.
With you I smash the young man and the young woman.
[23] With you I smash the shepherd and his flock.
With you I smash the farmer and his team of oxen.
With you I smash governors and officials.
[24] But I will pay back Babylon and all the people of Chaldea
for all the evil they have done in Zion,
before your very eyes,” says the LORD.
[25] “Look, I am against you, you destroying mountain,” says the LORD,
“you who destroy the whole earth.
I will stretch out my hand against you,
roll you down from the cliffs,
and make you a burned-out mountain.
[26] No one will take a cornerstone from you,
nor a stone for a foundation.
You will be a wasteland forever,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–23: Babylon was God’s “battle ax,” his hammer, with which he smashed nations. Nine times it says, “With you I smash …” These verses show how terrible Babylon’s wars were for all people: for men and women, old and young, shepherds and farmers.
> Verses 24–25: But now the hammer itself is smashed, because it has done so much evil. Babylon, the “destroying mountain,” becomes a burned-out mountain.
> Verse 26: Not even a stone will be left for new buildings.
> These verses describe how God can use powers and then judge them again. They do not justify violence against innocent people, but show it as a terrible injustice for which Babylon is held responsible.

## The nations are called (Verses 27–33)

[27] “Raise a signal flag in the land!
Blow the trumpet among the nations!
Prepare the nations for war against her!
Call together against her the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni, and Ashkenaz!
Appoint a commander against her!
Bring up the horses like swarming locusts!
[28] Prepare the nations for war against her:
the kings of the Medes,
their governors and all their officials,
and all the lands they rule!
[29] The land trembles and writhes in pain,
for the LORD’s plans against Babylon stand firm:
to make the land of Babylon a wasteland
where no one lives.
[30] Babylon’s warriors have stopped fighting.
They stay in their fortresses.
Their strength has failed.
They have become like women.
Her houses are set on fire.
The bars of her gates are broken.
[31] One runner runs to meet another,
and one messenger to meet another,
to tell the king of Babylon
that his city is captured from end to end.
[32] The river crossings are seized.
The reeds are burned with fire.
The soldiers are terrified.”
[33] For the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“The daughter of Babylon is like a threshing floor
at the time it is trampled down.
In just a little while,
the time of harvest will come for her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: Ararat, Minni, and Ashkenaz were peoples in the north, in the area of today’s Armenia and northern Iran.
> Verse 30: “They have become like women” is a saying of that time for soldiers who lose their courage (like 50:37). Today we know: Courage does not depend on whether you are a man or a woman.
> Verse 31: A vivid picture: Messengers run from all sides to the king to report to him: The city has fallen!
> Verse 33: The threshing floor is a hard-packed place for threshing grain. Soon the “harvest” comes for Babylon, that is, the judgment.

## God takes up his people’s case (Verses 34–44)

[34] “Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, has devoured me.
He has crushed me.
He has made me an empty jar.
Like a monster, he has swallowed me up.
He has filled his stomach with my delicacies.
He has spit me out.
[35] May the violence done to me and to my body be on Babylon,”
the people of Zion will say.
“May my blood be on the people of Chaldea,”
Jerusalem will say.
[36] So the LORD says:
“Look, I will take up your case,
and take vengeance for you.
I will dry up her sea,
and make her springs run dry.
[37] Babylon will become heaps of ruins,
a home for jackals,
an object of horror and hissing,
without anyone living there.
[38] They will roar together like young lions.
They will growl like lion cubs.
[39] When they are heated up,
I will prepare a feast for them,
and make them drunk,
so that they celebrate,
and then sleep forever and never wake up,” says the LORD.
[40] “I will bring them down like lambs to the slaughter,
like rams and male goats.
[41] How Sheshach is captured!
How the pride of the whole earth is seized!
How Babylon has become an object of horror
among the nations!
[42] The sea has risen over Babylon.
She is covered by its many waves.
[43] Her cities have become a wasteland,
a dry land and a desert,
a land where no one lives,
and through which no human being passes.
[44] I will punish Bel in Babylon,
and make him spit out what he has swallowed.
The nations will no longer stream to him.
Yes, the wall of Babylon will fall.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 34–35: Jerusalem laments: Nebuchadnezzar has devoured me like a monster and spit me out again. The city asks that the injustice not be forgotten.
> Verse 36: God answers: “I will take up your case.” God himself becomes Jerusalem’s lawyer. The victims do not have to take revenge themselves.
> Verse 39: A feast after which no one wakes up again. The Greek historians tell that Babylon was captured during a night of feasting (compare also Daniel 5).
> Verse 44: Bel, the god of Babylon, has to “spit out” what he has swallowed: the treasures and the captives of the nations.

## My people, go out! (Verses 45–58)

[45] “My people, go out of the middle of her!
Each of you, save yourselves
from the LORD’s burning anger.
[46] Don’t lose heart,
and don’t be afraid of the news that will be heard in the land.
For news will come one year,
and after that, more news in another year,
and violence in the land,
ruler against ruler.
[47] So look, the days are coming
when I will punish the carved idols of Babylon.
Her whole land will be put to shame,
and all her dead will fall in the middle of her.
[48] Then heaven and earth and everything in them
will shout for joy over Babylon,
for the destroyers will come against her from the north,” says the LORD.
[49] “Just as Babylon caused the dead of Israel to fall,
so the dead of the whole land will fall at Babylon.
[50] You who have escaped the sword,
go! Don’t stand still!
Remember the LORD from far away,
and let Jerusalem come to your mind.”
[51] “We are ashamed, because we have heard insults.
Shame has covered our faces,
for strangers have entered the holy places of the LORD’s house.”
[52] “So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will punish her carved idols,
and throughout her land the wounded will groan.
[53] Even if Babylon climbs up to the sky,
and even if she fortifies her high stronghold,
destroyers will still come against her from me,” says the LORD.
[54] “Listen! A cry from Babylon,
the sound of great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans!
[55] For the LORD is laying Babylon waste.
He is silencing her loud noise.
The waves of the enemy roar like many waters.
The noise of their voices rings out.
[56] For the destroyer has come against her, against Babylon.
Her warriors are captured.
Their bows are broken,
for the LORD is a God who pays back.
He will surely repay.
[57] I will make her leaders and her wise men drunk,
her governors, her officials, and her warriors.
They will sleep forever and never wake up,”
says the King, whose name is the LORD of Armies.
[58] The LORD of Armies says:
“The thick walls of Babylon will be completely torn down.
Her high gates will be burned with fire.
The peoples will work hard for nothing,
and the nations will wear themselves out
only for the fire.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: “My people, go out!” The Book of Revelation takes up this call (Revelation 18:4).
> Verse 46: Comforting advice for unsettled times: Don’t be afraid of all the news of war and power struggles! God keeps history in his hand. That is true today too, when we hear bad news every day.
> Verse 50: “Remember the LORD from far away, and let Jerusalem come to your mind.” Even in exile, people should not forget their homeland and their God (compare Psalm 137:5).
> Verse 53: Even if Babylon built up to the sky (compare the Tower of Babel, Genesis 11), it could not protect itself from God.
> Verse 58: Everything the nations worked for to make Babylon great was in vain.

## The scroll in the Euphrates (Verses 59–64)

[59] This is the message that Jeremiah the prophet gave to Seraiah,
the son of Neriah, the son of Mahseiah,
when he went with Zedekiah, king of Judah, to Babylon
in the fourth year of his reign.
Seraiah was the officer in charge of the king’s quarters on the journey.
[60] Jeremiah wrote in a book all the disaster that would come on Babylon,
all these words that are written about Babylon.
[61] Jeremiah said to Seraiah,
“When you come to Babylon,
make sure you read all these words aloud.
[62] Then say,
‘LORD, you have said about this place
that you will destroy it,
so that no one will live in it,
neither people nor animals,
but that it will be a wasteland forever.’
[63] When you have finished reading this book,
tie a stone to it,
and throw it into the middle of the Euphrates.
[64] Then say,
‘In the same way Babylon will sink,
and will never rise again,
because of the disaster that I will bring on her.
And they will be worn out.’”
This is the end of the words of Jeremiah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 59: Seraiah was Baruch’s brother (both were sons of Neriah). In the year 594/593 BC, he traveled with King Zedekiah to Babylon.
> Verses 60–64: One last symbolic act: Seraiah is to read the words about Babylon aloud in Babylon, then tie a stone to the scroll and throw it into the Euphrates. Just as the scroll sinks, Babylon will sink.
> The Book of Revelation also takes up this picture: An angel throws a large stone into the sea and says: “In the same way Babylon, the great city, will be thrown down with violence” (Revelation 18:21).
> “This is the end of the words of Jeremiah.” The last chapter (52) is a historical appendix that tells about the fall of Jerusalem.
