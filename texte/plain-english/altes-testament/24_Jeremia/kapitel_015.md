# Jeremiah – Chapter 15
### Your words became my food

---

## Not even Moses and Samuel could help (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then the LORD said to me,
“Even if Moses and Samuel stood in front of me,
my heart would not turn toward this people.
Throw them out of my sight,
and let them go away!
[2] When they ask you, ‘Where should we go?’
then tell them, ‘The LORD says:
“Those meant for death, to death.
Those meant for the sword, to the sword.
Those meant for famine, to famine.
Those meant for captivity, to captivity.”’
[3] “I will send four kinds of destroyers against them,” says the LORD:
“the sword to kill,
the dogs to tear apart,
and the birds of the sky and the animals of the earth,
to eat and to destroy.
[4] I will make them be tossed back and forth among all the kingdoms of the earth,
because of Manasseh, the son of Hezekiah, king of Judah,
because of what he did in Jerusalem.
[5] For who will have pity on you, Jerusalem?
Who will mourn for you?
Who will stop to ask how you are?
[6] You have rejected me,” says the LORD.
“You have gone backward.
That is why I have stretched out my hand against you and destroyed you.
I am tired of showing compassion.
[7] I have separated them with a winnowing fork
in the gates of the land.
I have taken their children from them.
I have destroyed my people.
They didn’t turn back from their ways.
[8] Their widows have become more than the sand of the seas.
At noon I have brought a destroyer
against the mother of the young men.
I have made anguish and terror fall on her suddenly.
[9] She who had seven children grows weak.
She has breathed her last.
Her sun has gone down while it was still day.
She has been put to shame and disgraced.
Those who are left of them I will hand over to the sword
in front of their enemies,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Moses and Samuel were the great intercessors of Israel. They often prayed for the people, and God heard them (Exodus 32; 1 Samuel 7). But now, God says, not even their prayer would help. That is how bad the situation is.
> Verse 4: King Manasseh ruled about 50 years before Jeremiah. He was especially bad: He brought in idol worship and had much innocent blood shed (2 Kings 21). The consequences of his deeds lasted a long time.
> Verse 7: A winnowing fork is a farm tool. With it, farmers separated the grain from the chaff.
> Verses 8–9: A moving picture: A mother who had seven children loses them all. “Her sun has gone down while it was still day.” That is what endless grief feels like.
> Important: The text explains Jerusalem’s disaster as a consequence of guilt. This does not mean that every person who suffers or loses a child is to blame for it. In other places the Bible clearly rejects this idea (Job; John 9:1–3). If you are grieving, you can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## How terrible for me, my mother, that you gave birth to me! (Verses 10–14)

[10] How terrible for me, my mother,
that you gave birth to me,
a man everyone fights with,
a man the whole land argues with!
I have not lent money,
and no one has lent money to me.
Yet every one of them curses me.
[11] The LORD said,
“I will certainly make you strong for good.
I will certainly make the enemy beg you for help
in the time of disaster and in the time of trouble.
[12] Can anyone break iron,
iron from the north, and bronze?
[13] I will give away your wealth and your treasures as plunder, without payment,
because of all your sins,
in all your territory.
[14] I will make them go with your enemies

in a land which you don’t know,
for a fire has been lit by my anger,
and it will burn against you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Jeremiah is at the end of his strength. He laments: “How terrible for me, my mother, that you gave birth to me.” He is a man everyone fights with. He has not lent anyone money or owed anyone money (which often led to quarrels), and yet everyone curses him. He is hated only because he speaks God’s word.
> Verses 11–12: God comforts him: I will make you strong. The “iron from the north” is Babylon. No one can stop it.
> Verses 13–14: These verses are addressed to the people: The treasures will be robbed, and the people will be led into exile.
> If you feel like Jeremiah here, so alone and desperate that you wish you had never been born, talk to someone. Help is there day and night: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Your words became my food (Verses 15–21)

[15] LORD, you know.
Remember me, take care of me,
and pay back those who persecute me.
You are patient, so don’t take me away.
Know that I have suffered insults for your sake.
[16] Your words were found, and I ate them.
Your words were a joy to me,
and the delight of my heart,
for I am called by your name,
LORD, God of Armies.
[17] I didn’t sit with the people who celebrate and have fun.
I sat alone because your hand was on me,
for you have filled me with anger.
[18] Why does my pain never end,
and why is my wound incurable?
It refuses to be healed.
Will you really be to me like a stream that dries up,
like water that is not there when you need it?
[19] That is why the LORD says,
“If you turn back, then I will bring you back,
so that you may stand in front of me.
If you separate what is precious from what is worthless,
you will be my mouth.
They will turn to you,
but you will not turn to them.
[20] I will make you a fortified bronze wall against this people.
They will fight against you,
but they will not defeat you,
for I am with you to save you and to rescue you,” says the LORD.
[21] “I will rescue you out of the hand of the wicked,
and I will free you out of the hand of the cruel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: Jeremiah asks God: Remember me! I am suffering for you.
> Verse 16 is a beautiful verse: “Your words were found, and I ate them. Your words were a joy to me.” God’s word was like food for Jeremiah. It filled him and made him happy.
> Verse 17: But his calling also makes him lonely. He cannot celebrate happily with the others.
> Verse 18: Jeremiah’s most bitter accusation: God, are you like a stream that dries up in summer when you need it? Earlier, God had called himself “the spring of living water” (2:13). Now Jeremiah feels abandoned by this spring. The Bible allows such honest words.
> Verse 19: God does not answer with pity, but with an invitation: Turn back, Jeremiah! Let go of your despair. Then you can be my mouth again.
> Verses 20–21: God repeats the promise from Jeremiah’s call (1:18–19): “I am with you to save you and to rescue you.”
