# Jeremiah – Chapter 32
### Jeremiah buys a field

---

## Jeremiah in prison (Verses 1–5)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD
in the tenth year of Zedekiah, king of Judah,
which was the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar.
[2] At that time the army of the king of Babylon was besieging Jerusalem.
Jeremiah the prophet was locked up in the courtyard of the guard,
which was in the palace of the king of Judah.
[3] For Zedekiah, king of Judah, had locked him up. He had said,
“Why do you prophesy and say,
‘The LORD says,
“Look, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon,
and he will capture it.
[4] Zedekiah, king of Judah, won’t escape out of the hand of the Chaldeans,
but he will surely be handed over to the king of Babylon.
He will speak with him face to face,
and see him with his own eyes.
[5] He will take Zedekiah to Babylon,
and he will be there until I come for him,” says the LORD.
“Even if you fight against the Chaldeans,
you will not succeed”’?”

> **What does this mean?**
> It is the year 588/587 BC. Jerusalem is under siege, shortly before it falls. King Zedekiah has locked Jeremiah up in the “courtyard of the guard” of the palace, because he keeps announcing: The city will fall.

---

## A purchase contract in the middle of war (Verses 6–15)

[6] Jeremiah said, “The LORD’s word came to me. He said,
[7] ‘Look, Hanamel, the son of your uncle Shallum, will come to you and say,
“Buy my field that is in Anathoth,
for you have the right to buy it back for the family.”’
[8] “So my cousin Hanamel came to me in the courtyard of the guard,
just as the LORD had said, and said to me,
‘Please buy my field that is in Anathoth, in the land of Benjamin,
for you have the right of inheritance,
and the right to buy it back is yours.
Buy it for yourself.’
“Then I knew that this was the LORD’s word.
[9] I bought the field in Anathoth from my cousin Hanamel,
and weighed out the money for him: 17 shekels of silver (about 7 ounces).
[10] I signed the deed and sealed it.
I called witnesses,
and weighed out the money for him on the scales.
[11] Then I took the deed of purchase,
both the sealed copy, which contained the terms and conditions,
and the open copy.
[12] I gave the deed of purchase to Baruch, the son of Neriah, the son of Mahseiah,
in front of my cousin Hanamel,
and in front of the witnesses who had signed the deed of purchase,
in front of all the Jews who were sitting in the courtyard of the guard.
[13] “In front of them I commanded Baruch:
[14] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘Take these deeds,
this deed of purchase which is sealed,
and this deed which is open,
and put them in a clay jar,
so that they last for a long time.’
[15] For the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
‘Houses and fields and vineyards will again be bought in this land.’

> **What does this mean?**
> An amazing story: In the middle of the siege, while the enemies stand at the gates and the land seems lost, Jeremiah buys a field in his home village Anathoth. The village was already in the area held by the Babylonians.
> Verses 7–8: According to the law, a close relative had the right and the duty to buy a family plot of land so that it would stay in the family (Leviticus 25:25).
> Verses 10–14: Jeremiah does everything very properly: contract, seal, witnesses, money. There are two copies: one sealed, one open to read. They are put into a clay jar so that they will last a long time. (The Dead Sea Scrolls were also kept like this.)
> Verse 15: The message: There is a future! People will buy houses and fields in this land again. Jeremiah invests in hope, even though everything seems lost.
> Shekels were pieces of silver that were weighed out as money. One shekel is about 0.4 ounces.
> Here Baruch is mentioned for the first time, Jeremiah’s faithful scribe and friend.

## Jeremiah’s prayer: Nothing is too hard for you (Verses 16–25)

[16] After I had given the deed of purchase to Baruch, the son of Neriah,
I prayed to the LORD. I said,
[17] “Oh, Lord GOD!
Look, you have made the heavens and the earth
by your great power and by your outstretched arm.
There is nothing too hard for you.
[18] You show faithful love to thousands,
but you pay back the guilt of the ancestors
into the lap of their children after them.
The great and mighty God, the LORD of Armies, is your name.
[19] You are great in your plans and mighty in your deeds.
Your eyes are open to all the ways of people,
to give each person what his ways deserve,
and what the fruit of his deeds deserves.
[20] You did signs and wonders in the land of Egypt,
and you still do them to this day,
both in Israel and among other people.
You have made a name for yourself, as it is today.
[21] You brought your people Israel out of the land of Egypt
with signs, with wonders, with a strong hand,
with an outstretched arm, and with great terror.
[22] You gave them this land,
which you swore to their ancestors to give them,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
[23] They came in and took it over,
but they didn’t obey your voice,
and didn’t walk in your law.
They have done nothing of all that you commanded them to do.
That is why you have brought all this disaster on them.
[24] “Look, siege ramps have been built against the city to capture it.
Because of the sword, the famine, and disease,
the city is handed over to the Chaldeans who are fighting against it.
What you have said has happened.
Look, you see it.
[25] Yet you have said to me, Lord GOD,
‘Buy the field for money, and call witnesses,’
while the city is handed over to the Chaldeans.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: “There is nothing too hard for you.” Jeremiah trusts God’s power.
> Verses 18–23: He remembers God’s great deeds in history, the exodus from Egypt and the land that God gave. But the people did not listen.
> Verses 24–25: And then the honest question: God, the enemies are standing outside the city, and you tell me to buy a field? How does that fit together? Jeremiah obeys, but he does not fully understand it.

---

## God’s answer: judgment (Verses 26–35)

[26] Then the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah. He said,
[27] “Look, I am the LORD, the God of all people.
Is there anything too hard for me?
[28] So the LORD says:
Look, I will give this city into the hand of the Chaldeans,
and into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
and he will capture it.
[29] The Chaldeans who are fighting against this city will come
and set this city on fire.
They will burn it down,
with the houses on whose roofs people have burned incense to Baal,
and poured out drink offerings to other gods,
to make me angry.
[30] “For the people of Israel and the people of Judah
have done only what was evil in my eyes, since their youth.
The people of Israel have only made me angry
with the work of their hands, says the LORD.
[31] For this city has stirred up my anger and my fury
from the day that they built it until today,
so that I must remove it from my sight,
[32] because of all the evil of the people of Israel and of the people of Judah,
which they have done to make me angry:
they, their kings, their princes,
their priests, their prophets,
the men of Judah, and the people of Jerusalem.
[33] They have turned their backs to me, and not their faces.
I taught them early and tirelessly,
but they have not listened or accepted correction.
[34] Instead, they put their disgusting idols in the house which is called by my name,
to make it unclean.
[35] They built the high places of Baal in the valley of the son of Hinnom,
to make their sons and their daughters pass through the fire to Molech.
I didn’t command them to do it.
It didn’t even come into my mind
that they should do this disgusting thing,
and make Judah sin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: God takes up Jeremiah’s words: “Is there anything too hard for me?” Rescue after the judgment is not too hard for God either.
> Verses 28–35: But first comes the judgment. God lists once more why: idol worship, making the temple unclean, and the worst crime, sacrificing children to the god Molech. God says again: That never came into my mind. Killing children is something disgusting to God.

---

## God’s answer: hope (Verses 36–44)

[36] So now the LORD, the God of Israel, says this about this city,
about which you say,
“It is handed over to the king of Babylon
by the sword, by famine, and by disease”:
[37] “Look, I will gather them out of all the countries
where I have driven them in my anger, in my fury, and in great rage.
I will bring them back to this place,
and I will let them live in safety.
[38] Then they will be my people,
and I will be their God.
[39] I will give them one heart and one way,
so that they will fear me forever,
for their own good and the good of their children after them.
[40] I will make a covenant with them that lasts forever:
I will not turn away from them,
but I will do them good.
I will put the fear of me in their hearts,
so that they will not turn away from me.
[41] Yes, I will be glad to do them good,
and I will plant them firmly in this land
with my whole heart and with my whole soul.”
[42] For the LORD says:
“Just as I have brought all this great disaster on this people,
so I will bring on them all the good that I have promised them.
[43] Fields will be bought in this land,
about which you say,
‘It is a waste, without people or animals.
It is handed over to the Chaldeans.’
[44] People will buy fields for money,
sign the deeds, seal them, and call witnesses,
in the land of Benjamin,
and in the places around Jerusalem,
in the cities of Judah,
in the cities of the hill country,
in the cities of the lowland,
and in the cities of the South.
For I will turn around their fate,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the judgment comes hope:
> Verses 37–38: God will bring his people back. “Then they will be my people, and I will be their God.”
> Verses 39–40: “A covenant … that lasts forever,” similar to the new covenant in chapter 31: God gives a new heart so that the people stay with him.
> Verse 41 is especially beautiful: God is glad to do good. He says: I do it “with my whole heart and with my whole soul.” Here God speaks of his whole heart and his whole soul, just as people are to love God with all their heart and all their soul (Deuteronomy 6:5).
> Verses 43–44: And now it becomes clear why Jeremiah was to buy the field: It was a sign that life goes on. One day people will buy fields again all over the land.
