# Jeremiah – Chapter 21
### The way of life and the way of death

---

## King Zedekiah asks Jeremiah (Verses 1–7)

[1] This is the word which came to Jeremiah from the LORD,
when King Zedekiah sent Pashhur, the son of Malchijah,
and Zephaniah, the son of Maaseiah, the priest, to him. They said,
[2] “Please ask the LORD for us,
for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, is making war against us.
Perhaps the LORD will do for us one of all his wonderful works,
so that he goes away from us.”
[3] Then Jeremiah said to them, “Tell Zedekiah:
[4] ‘The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
“Look, I will turn back the weapons of war that are in your hands,
with which you fight against the king of Babylon,
and against the Chaldeans who are besieging you outside the walls.
I will gather them into the middle of this city.
[5] I myself will fight against you
with an outstretched hand and with a strong arm,
in anger, in fury, and in great rage.
[6] I will strike the people who live in this city,
both people and animals.
They will die of a terrible disease.
[7] Afterward,” says the LORD,
“I will hand over Zedekiah, king of Judah, his servants, and the people,
those who are left in this city after the disease, the sword, and the famine,
into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
into the hand of their enemies,
and into the hand of those who want to kill them.
He will strike them with the edge of the sword.
He will not spare them, or pity them, or have mercy on them.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the story jumps forward about 20 years: It is the time of the last siege of Jerusalem (588–587 BC). Zedekiah is the last king of Judah.
> This Pashhur is a different man from the Pashhur in chapter 20.
> Verse 2: Zedekiah hopes for a miracle, as back then, when God saved Jerusalem from the Assyrians (2 Kings 19).
> Verses 4–7: But Jeremiah’s answer is hard: This time God will not help. The city will fall.

---

## Choose between life and death (Verses 8–10)

[8] “Say to this people,
‘The LORD says:
“Look, I set before you the way of life and the way of death.
[9] Whoever stays in this city will die by the sword, by famine, and by disease.
But whoever goes out and surrenders to the Chaldeans who are besieging you will live.
He will escape with his life.
[10] For I have set my face against this city for disaster, and not for good,” says the LORD.
“It will be handed over to the king of Babylon,
and he will burn it with fire.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: “Look, I set before you the way of life and the way of death.” This is a reminder of Moses (Deuteronomy 30:15–19). But here it is very concrete.
> Verse 9: Jeremiah’s advice is shocking: Whoever wants to live should surrender to the enemy. To many people this sounded like treason. But Jeremiah wanted to save lives. He saw that resistance would only bring even more deaths.
> “He will escape with his life” means: He will lose everything, but he will get away alive.

---

## A word to the royal house (Verses 11–14)

[11] “To the house of the king of Judah: Hear the LORD’s word!
[12] House of David, the LORD says,
‘Do justice in the morning,

and rescue the one who has been robbed
out of the hand of the oppressor.
Otherwise my anger will go out like fire,
and burn so that no one can put it out,
because of the evil of your deeds.
[13] Look, I am against you, you who live in the valley,
on the rock of the plain,’ says the LORD.
‘You say, “Who could come down against us?”
or, “Who could get into our homes?”
[14] I will punish you as the fruit of your deeds deserves,’ says the LORD.
‘I will set her forest on fire,
and it will burn up everything around her.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The most important task of a king: to make sure there is justice every morning and to protect those who have been robbed from the oppressors. This still applies today to everyone who has power.
> Verse 13: Jerusalem felt safe on its rocks and hills. The people said: “Who could come down against us?” But God says: This false security will not protect you.
> Verse 14: The “forest” is probably the royal palace, which was built with a lot of cedar wood (it was called the “House of the Forest of Lebanon,” 1 Kings 7:2).
