# Jeremiah – Chapter 31
### The new covenant

---

## Loved with an everlasting love (Verses 1–6)

[1] “At that time,” says the LORD,
“I will be the God of all the families of Israel,
and they will be my people.”
[2] The LORD says,
“The people who survived the sword
found favor in the wilderness,
Israel, when I went to give him rest.”
[3] Long ago the LORD appeared to me and said,
“Yes, I have loved you with an everlasting love.
That is why I have drawn you to me with faithful love.
[4] I will build you again, and you will be built,
virgin Israel.
Again you will put on your tambourines,
and go out in the dances of those who celebrate.
[5] Again you will plant vineyards on the mountains of Samaria.
Those who plant them will plant,
and will enjoy the fruit.
[6] For a day will come when the watchmen on the hills of Ephraim will call out,
‘Get up! Let’s go up to Zion, to the LORD our God.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: As back then in the wilderness after the exodus from Egypt, God will show favor to his people again.
> Verse 3 is one of the most beautiful love verses in the Bible: “I have loved you with an everlasting love. That is why I have drawn you to me with faithful love.” God’s love never ends.
> Verses 4–5: The future looks joyful: dancing, music, vineyards, harvest.
> Verse 6: Samaria and Ephraim belonged to the northern kingdom, which had separated from Jerusalem. Now the people from the north too will come back to Jerusalem to the temple. The split will be overcome.

---

## They will come with weeping (Verses 7–14)

[7] For the LORD says,
“Sing with joy for Jacob,
and shout for the greatest of the nations.
Announce it, praise, and say,
‘LORD, save your people,
what is left of Israel!’
[8] Look, I will bring them from the land of the north,
and gather them from the ends of the earth.
Among them will be the blind and the lame,
the pregnant woman and the woman in labor together.
They will come back as a great crowd.
[9] They will come with weeping.
I will lead them as they pray.
I will lead them along streams of water,
on a straight path where they won’t stumble,
for I am a father to Israel.
Ephraim is my firstborn.
[10] “Hear the LORD’s word, you nations,
and announce it in the islands far away.
Say, ‘He who scattered Israel will gather him,
and guard him, as a shepherd guards his flock.’
[11] For the LORD has set Jacob free,
and rescued him from the hand of someone stronger than he was.
[12] They will come and sing on the heights of Zion.
They will stream to the good gifts of the LORD:
to the grain, to the new wine, to the oil,
and to the young of the flock and of the herd.
Their life will be like a watered garden.
They will not grieve anymore at all.
[13] Then the young women will be glad and dance,
and the young men and the old together.
For I will turn their mourning into joy,
and I will comfort them,
and give them joy instead of sorrow.
[14] I will fill the priests with rich food,
and my people will be satisfied with my good gifts,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: A wonderful picture: God brings everyone home, especially the weak: the blind, the lame, pregnant women, women in labor. No one is left behind.
> Verse 9: They come weeping, out of joy and deep emotion. God leads them like a father. He calls Ephraim (the northern kingdom) his “firstborn,” his favorite child.
> Verse 13: “I will turn their mourning into joy.” Young and old dance together. A picture full of hope for all who mourn.

---

## Rachel weeps for her children (Verses 15–22)

[15] The LORD says:
“A voice is heard in Ramah,
mourning and bitter weeping:
Rachel is weeping for her children.
She refuses to be comforted for her children,
because they are no more.”
[16] The LORD says:
“Stop your weeping,
and hold back the tears from your eyes,
for your work will be rewarded,” says the LORD.
“They will come back from the land of the enemy.
[17] There is hope for your future,” says the LORD.
“Your children will come back to their own land.
[18] “I have surely heard Ephraim mourning like this:
‘You have disciplined me,
and I was disciplined,
like a calf that is not trained.
Bring me back, and I will come back,
for you are the LORD my God.
[19] Truly, after I turned away, I was sorry.
After I understood, I struck my thigh in grief.
I was ashamed, yes, even disgraced,
because I carried the disgrace of my youth.’
[20] Is Ephraim my dear son?
Is he the child I delight in?
For as often as I speak against him,
I still remember him with love.
That is why my heart longs for him.
I will surely have mercy on him,” says the LORD.
[21] “Set up road signs.
Put up guideposts.
Pay attention to the highway,
the way by which you went.
Come back, virgin Israel.
Come back to these cities of yours.
[22] How long will you wander here and there,
you unfaithful daughter?
For the LORD has created a new thing on the earth:
a woman will surround a man.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: Rachel was Jacob’s favorite wife and the ancestral mother of Ephraim and Benjamin. Her grave was near Ramah (Genesis 35:19; 1 Samuel 10:2). Jeremiah imagines Rachel weeping for her children who are being led into exile. Ramah was a gathering place for the prisoners (compare 40:1). The Gospel of Matthew quotes this verse in the story of the killing of the children in Bethlehem (Matthew 2:18).
> Verses 16–17: God comforts Rachel: Stop weeping! There is hope. Your children will come back.
> If you are grieving for a child, you will find in Rachel someone who knows this pain. Help is available, for example: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 18–19: Ephraim is sorry and prays: “Bring me back, and I will come back.” Turning back is God’s gift.
> Verse 20: A moving verse: God speaks like a father who is disappointed by his child, but still cannot forget it. “My heart longs for him.”
> Verse 22: “A woman will surround a man” is a puzzling sentence. Many understand it like this: Things will be turned around; something completely new will happen. Interpreters do not agree on the exact meaning.

## A refreshing sleep (Verses 23–30)

[23] The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“When I turn around their fate,
people in the land of Judah and in its cities will say these words again:
‘May the LORD bless you, home of righteousness, holy mountain.’
[24] Judah and all its cities will live there together,
the farmers and those who move around with flocks.
[25] For I have refreshed the tired soul,
and I have filled every sad soul.”
[26] At this I woke up and looked around.
My sleep had been sweet to me.
[27] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will sow the house of Israel and the house of Judah
with the seed of people and with the seed of animals.
[28] Just as I have watched over them to pull up and to break down,
to overthrow, to destroy, and to bring disaster,
so I will watch over them to build and to plant,” says the LORD.
[29] “In those days they will no longer say,
“‘The fathers have eaten sour grapes,
and the children’s teeth are set on edge.’
[30] But everyone will die for his own guilt.
Every person who eats the sour grapes,
his own teeth will be set on edge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: “I have refreshed the tired soul.” God refreshes the exhausted.
> Verse 26: Jeremiah wakes up and says: “My sleep had been sweet to me.” After all the hard messages, this message was like a beautiful dream.
> Verse 28: Again the words from Jeremiah’s call (1:10): God has pulled up and broken down. Now he will build and plant.
> Verses 29–30: A saying at that time went: The fathers have sinned, and the children must pay for it. God says: This will no longer apply. Everyone is responsible for his own deeds. (Ezekiel 18 says this too.)

## The new covenant (Verses 31–34)

[31] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will make a new covenant
with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.
[32] It will not be like the covenant that I made with their fathers
on the day that I took them by the hand
to bring them out of the land of Egypt.
They broke my covenant,
even though I was a husband to them,” says the LORD.
[33] “But this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days,” says the LORD:
“I will put my law inside them,
and I will write it on their heart.
I will be their God,
and they will be my people.
[34] They will no longer teach each other, neighbor and brother, and say,
‘Know the LORD.’
For they will all know me,
from the least of them to the greatest,” says the LORD,
“for I will forgive their guilt,
and I will remember their sin no more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important texts in the whole Old Testament.
> Verses 31–32: God promises a “new covenant” with Israel and Judah. The old covenant from Sinai was broken by the people, although God was faithful like a husband.
> Verse 33: What is new: God’s law is no longer only on tablets of stone, but is written on the heart. People will then do God’s will no longer because they are forced to, but from the inside, because they want to.
> Verse 34: Everyone will know God, from the least to the greatest. No one will need a teacher anymore. And the foundation is forgiveness: “I will remember their sin no more.”
> In Judaism this promise is applied to Israel: God renews his covenant with his people, and the law (the Torah) is written on the heart.
> In the New Testament this text is applied to Jesus. At the Last Supper Jesus says: “This cup is the new covenant in my blood” (Luke 22:20). The letter to the Hebrews quotes the whole passage (Hebrews 8:8–12).
> Important: The new covenant is made explicitly with Israel and Judah. God has not rejected his people Israel (compare verses 35–37; Romans 11:1–2).

## As sure as the sun and the moon (Verses 35–40)

[35] The LORD gives the sun as a light by day,
and sets the order of the moon and of the stars as a light by night.
He stirs up the sea, so that its waves roar.
The LORD of Armies is his name.
He says:
[36] “If this order ever disappears from in front of me,” says the LORD,
“then the descendants of Israel will also stop being a nation in front of me forever.”
[37] The LORD says:
“If the heavens above can be measured,
and the foundations of the earth below can be explored,
then I will also reject all the descendants of Israel
for all that they have done,” says the LORD.
[38] “Look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when the city will be rebuilt for the LORD,
from the Tower of Hananel to the Corner Gate.
[39] The measuring line will go out farther, straight ahead to the hill Gareb,
and will turn toward Goah.
[40] The whole valley of the dead bodies and of the ashes,
and all the fields as far as the Kidron Valley,
to the corner of the Horse Gate toward the east,
will be holy to the LORD.
It will never be pulled up or thrown down again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 35–37: God’s faithfulness to Israel is as sure as the order of the sun, moon and stars. Only if these stopped would God reject Israel, that is, never. And just as no one can measure the heavens, God will never cast off his people.
> Verses 38–40: Jerusalem will be rebuilt bigger. Even the “valley of the dead bodies and of the ashes,” probably the valley of the son of Hinnom, where children were sacrificed, will be holy to God. The place of the greatest horror will be transformed.
