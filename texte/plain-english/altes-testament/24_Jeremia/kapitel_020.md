# Jeremiah – Chapter 20
### Like a burning fire

---

## Jeremiah in the stocks (Verses 1–6)

[1] Now Pashhur, the son of Immer, the priest,
who was the chief officer in the LORD’s house,
heard Jeremiah prophesying these things.
[2] Then Pashhur struck Jeremiah the prophet,
and put him in the stocks that were in the upper Benjamin Gate,
which was in the LORD’s house.
[3] The next day, Pashhur let Jeremiah out of the stocks.
Then Jeremiah said to him,
“The LORD has not called your name Pashhur,
but Magormissabib.
[4] For the LORD says,
‘Look, I will make you a terror to yourself and to all your friends.
They will fall by the sword of their enemies,
and your eyes will see it.
I will give all Judah into the hand of the king of Babylon.
He will carry them away as captives to Babylon,
and will kill them with the sword.
[5] I will also give all the wealth of this city,
all it has worked for, and all its precious things,
yes, all the treasures of the kings of Judah,
into the hand of their enemies.
They will rob them, take them, and carry them to Babylon.
[6] You, Pashhur, and everyone who lives in your house
will go into captivity.
You will come to Babylon,
and there you will die,
and there you will be buried,
you and all your friends,
to whom you have prophesied lies.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Pashhur, a high priest in the temple, has Jeremiah beaten and put in the “stocks” for a night. That was a wooden frame in which feet, hands or the neck were locked. It was painful and humiliating, and everyone could see the prisoner.
> Verse 3: Jeremiah gives Pashhur a new name: “Magormissabib,” which means “Terror on every side.” Pashhur himself will experience what he did to Jeremiah.
> Verse 6: Pashhur had acted as a prophet himself and told lies, probably that everything would be fine.
> Here Babylon is named openly as the enemy for the first time.

## Jeremiah’s lament: You persuaded me (Verses 7–13)

[7] LORD, you persuaded me,
and I let myself be persuaded.
You are stronger than I am,
and you have won.
I have become a joke all day long.
Everyone mocks me.
[8] For every time I speak, I cry out.
I shout, “Violence and destruction!”
For the LORD’s word has brought me insults
and mockery all day long.
[9] If I say, “I will not mention him,
or speak anymore in his name,”
then there is something in my heart like a burning fire,
shut up in my bones.
I am tired of holding it in.
I can’t.
[10] For I have heard many people whispering:
“Terror on every side!
Report him, and we will report him!”
All my close friends say this,
those who watch for me to fall.
“Perhaps he will be tricked,
and we will win against him,
and we will take our revenge on him.”
[11] But the LORD is with me like a mighty warrior who inspires fear.
That is why those who persecute me will stumble,
and they won’t win.
They will be completely put to shame,
because they have not acted wisely.
Their disgrace will last forever.
It will never be forgotten.
[12] But LORD of Armies, you test the righteous.
You see the heart and the mind.
Let me see your revenge on them,
for I have put my case in your hands.
[13] Sing to the LORD!
Praise the LORD,
for he has rescued the life of the needy
from the hand of evil people.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most honest and moving laments in the Bible.
> Verse 7: Jeremiah accuses God: You persuaded me to become a prophet. You were stronger than I was. And now everyone laughs at me.
> Verse 8: His message is always just “Violence and destruction!” He has no good news, and that is why he is mocked.
> Verse 9 is famous: Jeremiah wants to stop speaking God’s word. But he cannot. It burns inside him like a fire shut up in his bones. God’s word does not let him go.
> Verse 10: Even his friends are waiting for him to make a mistake. They mock him with his own words: “Terror on every side!”
> Verses 11–13: And then suddenly a turn: “The LORD is with me like a mighty warrior.” Jeremiah trusts again and even sings a song of praise. God rescues the poor.

## Cursed is the day I was born (Verses 14–18)

[14] Cursed is the day on which I was born.
Don’t let the day on which my mother gave birth to me be blessed.
[15] Cursed is the man who brought the news to my father,
and said, “A son is born to you,”
and made him very glad.
[16] Let that man be like the cities
which the LORD destroyed without changing his mind.
Let him hear a cry in the morning,
and shouting at noon,

[17] because he didn’t kill me before I was born.
Then my mother would have been my grave,
and her womb would have stayed pregnant forever.
[18] Why did I come out of the womb
to see trouble and sorrow,
so that my days are used up in shame?

> **What does this mean?**
> Right after the song of praise, Jeremiah falls back into the deepest despair. He curses the day of his birth. He wishes he had never been born.
> This shows what it is like for people in a crisis: Hope and despair take turns, sometimes very quickly. Job also lamented like this (Job 3).
> The Bible does not hide such feelings. It shows: Even a great prophet, a man of God, can be this desperate. And God does not let him fall. After this lament, Jeremiah lived and worked for many more years.
