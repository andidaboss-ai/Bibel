# Jeremiah – Chapter 48
### Words about Moab

---

## Moab is laid waste (Verses 1–10)

[1] About Moab.
The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“How terrible for Nebo! For it is laid waste.
Kiriathaim is put to shame.
It is captured.
Misgab is put to shame and broken down.
[2] Moab’s glory is no more.
In Heshbon they have planned disaster against her:
‘Come! Let’s cut her off from being a nation.’
You too, Madmen, will be silenced.
The sword will chase you.
[3] Listen! A cry from Horonaim:
ruin and great destruction!
[4] Moab is destroyed.
Her little ones cry out.
[5] For they go up the slope of Luhith,
weeping as they go.
For on the road down to Horonaim
they have heard the cries of distress over the destruction.
[6] Flee! Save your lives!
Be like a juniper bush in the wilderness.
[7] For because you trusted in your works and in your treasures,
you too will be captured.
Chemosh will go into captivity,
his priests and his leaders together.
[8] The destroyer will come against every city,
and no city will escape.
The valley also will be ruined,
and the plain will be destroyed,
as the LORD has said.
[9] Give wings to Moab,
so that she may fly away.
Her cities will become a wasteland,
without anyone living in them.
[10] Cursed is anyone who does the LORD’s work carelessly,
and cursed is anyone who holds back his sword from blood.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moab lay east of the Dead Sea, in today’s Jordan. The Moabites were related to Israel (Genesis 19:37). Ruth, David’s great-grandmother, was a Moabite. Still, there was often conflict and war between the two peoples.
> Many cities are named; this makes it clear: The whole land is affected.
> Verse 7: Moab trusted in its treasures and in its god Chemosh. But even Chemosh will “go into captivity,” meaning his statue will be carried away.
> Verse 10: A very harsh verse. It is directed at the Babylonians, whom God uses as a tool of judgment on Moab: They are to carry out their task. This verse is not a general instruction and gives no one the right to use violence. Sadly, people in history have misused such verses to justify violence. That goes against the will of God, who loves “faithful love, justice, and righteousness” (9:24).

## Wine that was never poured off (Verses 11–17)

[11] “Moab has been at ease from his youth.
He has settled on his dregs like wine,
and has not been poured from jar to jar.
He has not gone into captivity.
So his flavor remains in him,
and his smell has not changed.
[12] So look, the days are coming,” says the LORD,
“when I will send to him people who pour off wine,
and they will pour him off.
They will empty his jars,
and smash his containers to pieces.
[13] Moab will be ashamed of Chemosh,
just as the people of Israel were ashamed of Bethel,
in which they trusted.
[14] How can you say, ‘We are warriors,
strong men for battle’?
[15] Moab is laid waste.
The enemy has gone up into his cities.
His best young men have gone down to the slaughter,”
says the King, whose name is the LORD of Armies.
[16] “Moab’s disaster is near,
and his misery is coming quickly.
[17] All you who live around him, mourn for him!
All you who know his name, say,
‘How the strong scepter is broken,
the glorious staff!’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: A picture from winemaking: Wine that lies on its yeast for a long time and is never poured into another jar keeps its old taste. Moab never experienced war or deportation. It has become self-satisfied and has never changed. Now it is being “poured off,” that is, torn out of its comfort.
> Verse 13: Bethel was a shrine in the northern kingdom of Israel with a golden bull statue (1 Kings 12:28–29). It could not save Israel. Neither will Chemosh save Moab.
> Verse 17: The neighbors are to feel pity. Even in judgment, compassion can be heard.

## Lament over the cities of Moab (Verses 18–28)

[18] You daughter who lives in Dibon,
come down from your glory,
and sit on the thirsty ground.
For the destroyer of Moab has come up against you.
He has destroyed your fortresses.
[19] You who live in Aroer,
stand by the road and watch.
Ask the man who flees and the woman who escapes.
Say, ‘What has happened?’
[20] Moab is put to shame,
for it is broken down.
Wail and cry out!
Announce it by the Arnon
that Moab is laid waste.
[21] Judgment has come on the plateau:
on Holon, on Jahzah, on Mephaath,
[22] on Dibon, on Nebo, on Beth Diblathaim,
[23] on Kiriathaim, on Beth Gamul, on Beth Meon,
[24] on Kerioth, on Bozrah,
and on all the cities of the land of Moab, far and near.
[25] Moab’s horn is cut off,
and his arm is broken,” says the LORD.
[26] “Make him drunk,
for he has made himself great against the LORD.
Moab will roll around in his vomit,
and he too will be laughed at.
[27] For wasn’t Israel laughed at by you?
Was he caught among thieves,
that every time you speak of him, you shake your head?
[28] You who live in Moab,
leave the cities, and live among the rocks.
Be like the dove that makes her nest
in the sides of the mouth of a gorge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–24: A long list of cities: Every single one is struck by the judgment. Dibon is today Dhiban in Jordan. There, in 1868, the famous “Mesha Stone” was found, an inscription of King Mesha of Moab from the 9th century BC.
> Verse 25: The “horn” is a picture for strength and power.
> Verses 26–27: Moab had made fun of Israel and shaken its head when Israel was doing badly. Now Moab itself is mocked. Anyone who mocks others in their misfortune will become a laughingstock themselves.
> Verse 28: The people are to hide in the rocks like doves in the cliffs.

## Moab’s pride (Verses 29–39)

[29] “We have heard of Moab’s pride.
He is very proud:
his loftiness, his pride, his arrogance,
and the haughtiness of his heart.
[30] I know his rage,” says the LORD,
“but it is nothing.
His boasting has achieved nothing.
[31] That is why I will wail for Moab.
Yes, I will cry out for all Moab.
People will mourn for the men of Kir Heres.
[32] With more than the weeping of Jazer
I will weep for you, vine of Sibmah.
Your branches reached across the sea.
They reached as far as the sea of Jazer.
The destroyer has fallen on your summer fruit
and on your grape harvest.
[33] Gladness and joy are taken away
from the fruitful land of Moab.
I have made the wine stop flowing from the winepresses.
No one will tread the grapes with shouts of joy.
The shouting will be no shouting of joy.
[34] From the cry of Heshbon as far as Elealeh,
as far as Jahaz, they have raised their voices,
from Zoar as far as Horonaim,
to Eglath Shelishiyah.
For even the waters of Nimrim will dry up.
[35] I will also put an end in Moab,” says the LORD,
“to anyone who offers sacrifices on the shrine on the hill
and burns incense to his gods.
[36] That is why my heart moans for Moab like flutes,
and my heart moans like flutes for the men of Kir Heres.
That is why the wealth he gained has been lost.
[37] For every head is shaved bald,
and every beard is cut off.
There are cuts on all the hands,
and sackcloth around the waist.
[38] On all the rooftops of Moab
and in its streets
there is mourning everywhere.
For I have broken Moab
like a jar that no one wants,” says the LORD.
[39] “How it is broken down!
How they wail!
How Moab has turned its back in shame!
So Moab will become an object of laughter and terror
to all who live around him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 29–30: The reason for the judgment is Moab’s pride. It is described here with many words one after another. Moab was proud and arrogant. But its boasting was empty.
> Verses 31–32 and 36: The amazing thing: God (or the prophet) weeps for Moab! “That is why my heart moans for Moab like flutes.” Flutes were played at funerals. God does not rejoice over the misfortune of a people, not even an enemy people. He mourns with them.
> Verses 32–33: Moab was famous for its wine. Now there is no joyful grape harvest anymore.
> Verse 37: Heads shaved bald, cuts, mourning clothes: signs of deep grief.
> Many verses in this chapter are similar to Isaiah 15–16.

## Terror, pit, and trap (Verses 40–47)

[40] For the LORD says:
“Look, the enemy will swoop down like an eagle,
and spread out his wings over Moab.
[41] Kerioth is captured,
and the fortresses are seized.
On that day the hearts of Moab’s warriors
will be like the heart of a woman in labor.
[42] Moab will be destroyed and no longer be a people,
because he has made himself great against the LORD.
[43] Terror, the pit, and the trap are coming on you,
you who live in Moab,” says the LORD.
[44] “Whoever flees from the terror
will fall into the pit.
Whoever climbs out of the pit
will be caught in the trap.
For I will bring on Moab
the year of their punishment,” says the LORD.
[45] “Those who fled stand without strength
in the shadow of Heshbon.
For a fire has gone out from Heshbon,
a flame from the house of Sihon.
It has burned up the forehead of Moab,
the skulls of the boastful.
[46] How terrible for you, Moab!
The people of Chemosh are lost.
For your sons are taken away as captives,
and your daughters into captivity.
[47] Yet in the last days I will turn around the fate of Moab,” says the LORD.
This is the end of the judgment on Moab.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 40: Babylon comes like an eagle swooping down on its prey.
> Verses 43–44: “Terror, the pit, and the trap”: In Hebrew these three words sound alike (pachad, pachat, pach). There is no escape: Whoever escapes one falls into the next.
> Verse 45: Heshbon used to be the city of the Amorite king Sihon (Numbers 21:26–28). This old song is taken up here.
> Verse 47: And at the end, after all the judgment, the surprising hope: “I will turn around the fate of Moab.” God does not give up even on the hostile neighboring people. His mercy reaches beyond Israel.
