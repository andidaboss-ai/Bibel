# Jeremiah – Chapter 30
### I will heal you

---

## Write everything in a book (Verses 1–3)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] “The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Write all the words that I have spoken to you in a book.
[3] For look, the days are coming,’ says the LORD,
‘when I will turn around the fate of my people Israel and Judah,’ says the LORD.
‘I will bring them back to the land that I gave to their fathers,
and they will own it.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> With chapter 30 a special part of the book of Jeremiah begins: the “Book of Comfort” (Chapters 30–33). After so many words about judgment, now come words of hope.
> Verse 2: Jeremiah is to write these words down so that later generations can read them. The hope is also for the future.
> Verse 3: God will “turn around the fate” of his people: The exiles will return home, both from the northern kingdom of Israel and from Judah.

---

## A time of fear, but rescue (Verses 4–11)

[4] These are the words that the LORD spoke about Israel and about Judah.
[5] For the LORD says:
“We have heard a sound of trembling,
a sound of fear, and not of peace.
[6] Ask now, and see:
Can a man give birth to a child?
Then why do I see every man with his hands on his belly,
like a woman in labor?
Why have all faces turned pale?
[7] Oh, that day is great!
There is no other day like it.
It is the time of Jacob’s trouble,
but he will be saved out of it.
[8] On that day,” says the LORD of Armies,
“I will break his yoke off your neck,
and I will tear off your chains.
Foreigners will no longer make them their slaves.
[9] Instead they will serve the LORD their God,
and David their king, whom I will raise up for them.
[10] So don’t be afraid, Jacob my servant,” says the LORD.
“Don’t be terrified, Israel.
For look, I will save you from far away,
and save your descendants from the land of their captivity.
Jacob will come back,
and will be quiet and at ease.
No one will make him afraid.
[11] For I am with you to save you,” says the LORD.
“I will make a complete end of all the nations where I have scattered you,
but I will not make a complete end of you.
I will correct you with fairness,
but I will not leave you unpunished.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: The fear is so great that even strong men bend over like women in labor.
> Verse 7: “It is the time of Jacob’s trouble, but he will be saved out of it.” The suffering is great, but it is not the end.
> Verses 8–9: God will break the yoke, the yoke of Babylon from chapters 27–28. And the people will serve a king from David’s family. In Judaism this is applied to the Messiah; Christians see it fulfilled in Jesus.
> Verses 10–11: “So don’t be afraid, Jacob my servant … For I am with you.” These words are a reminder of Isaiah 41 and 43. God punishes “with fairness,” not without limits, and he never lets his people be completely destroyed.

---

## I will heal you (Verses 12–17)

[12] For the LORD says,
“Your injury is incurable.
Your wound is serious.
[13] There is no one to plead your case,
no one to bandage you.
You have no medicine that heals.
[14] All your lovers have forgotten you.
They don’t care about you.
For I have wounded you as an enemy would,
with the punishment of someone cruel,
because your guilt is so great,
because your sins have become so many.
[15] Why do you cry over your injury?
Your pain is incurable.
Because your guilt is so great,
because your sins have become so many,
I have done these things to you.
[16] Therefore all those who eat you up will be eaten up.
All your enemies, every one of them, will go into captivity.
Those who rob you will be robbed.
I will make all who prey on you become prey.
[17] For I will make you healthy again,
and I will heal your wounds,” says the LORD,
“because they have called you an outcast and said,
‘It is Zion, whom no one cares about.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–15: The people’s wound seems incurable. No doctor, no medicine, no friends. And God says honestly: This wound comes from your guilt.
> Verses 16–17: But then the surprising turn: “Therefore” – precisely because the wound is incurable and no one cares – “I will heal your wounds.” What is impossible for people, God can do.
> Verse 17: The others said about Zion: “It is Zion, whom no one cares about.” That is exactly why God cares about her. God turns to those whom everyone else has given up on.

---

## You will be my people (Verses 18–24)

[18] The LORD says:
“Look, I will turn around the fate of Jacob’s tents,
and have compassion on his homes.
The city will be built again on its own hill of ruins,
and the palace will stand in its right place.
[19] Songs of thanks will come out of them,
with the sound of people celebrating.
I will make them many, and they will not be few.
I will give them honor, and they will not be looked down on.
[20] Their children will be as they were before,
and their community will be firmly established in front of me.
I will punish all who oppress them.
[21] Their prince will be one of their own,
and their ruler will come from among them.
I will bring him near, and he will come close to me.
For who would dare on his own to come close to me?” says the LORD.
[22] “You will be my people,
and I will be your God.
[23] Look, the LORD’s storm, his anger, has gone out,
a sweeping storm.
It will burst on the head of the wicked.
[24] The fierce anger of the LORD will not turn back
until he has done and carried out what his heart has planned.
In the last days you will understand it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: Jerusalem will be rebuilt on its own hill of ruins. Old cities in the Near East were often rebuilt on the ruins of the earlier city.
> Verses 19–20: Instead of laments, thanks and laughter will be heard again. Children will play in the streets again.
> Verse 21: The new ruler will not be a foreign king, but one of their own people. And he may come very close to God, like a priest.
> Verse 22: “You will be my people, and I will be your God.” That is the covenant formula, the heart of the relationship between God and Israel. It is being renewed.
> Verses 23–24: These verses already appear almost word for word in 23:19–20. God’s judgment hits the wicked, not his people, whom he heals.
