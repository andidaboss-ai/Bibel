# Jeremiah – Chapter 36
### The king burns the scroll

---

## Baruch writes and reads aloud (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the fourth year of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah,
this word came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] “Take a scroll,
and write in it all the words that I have spoken to you
against Israel, against Judah, and against all the nations,
from the day I first spoke to you, in the days of Josiah, until today.
[3] Perhaps the house of Judah will hear
about all the disaster which I plan to bring on them,
so that they turn back, each from his evil way.
Then I can forgive their guilt and their sin.”
[4] Then Jeremiah called Baruch, the son of Neriah.
As Jeremiah spoke, Baruch wrote on a scroll
all the words that the LORD had spoken to him.
[5] Jeremiah commanded Baruch:
“I am kept away.
I can’t go into the LORD’s house.
[6] So you go,
and read the LORD’s words from the scroll which you have written as I spoke.
Read them to the people in the LORD’s house on the day of fasting.
Also read them to all the people of Judah who come in from their cities.
[7] Perhaps they will bring their prayer for mercy to the LORD,
and each will turn back from his evil way.
For the LORD has announced great anger and fury against this people.”
[8] Baruch, the son of Neriah, did everything that Jeremiah the prophet commanded him.
He read the LORD’s words from the book in the LORD’s house.
[9] In the fifth year of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah, in the ninth month,
all the people in Jerusalem,
and all the people who came from the cities of Judah to Jerusalem,
announced a fast before the LORD.
[10] Then Baruch read the words of Jeremiah from the book in the LORD’s house,
in the room of Gemariah, the son of Shaphan, the scribe,
in the upper courtyard, at the entrance of the New Gate of the LORD’s house,
so that all the people heard it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3: Jeremiah is to write down all the messages of the last 23 years. The reason: “Perhaps the house of Judah will hear … so that they turn back … Then I can forgive their guilt and their sin.” God does not want to punish; he wants to forgive.
> Verse 4: Jeremiah dictates, Baruch writes. That is probably how part of the book of Jeremiah came into being.
> Verse 5: Jeremiah is no longer allowed into the temple, probably after the temple speech (Chapters 7 and 26).
> Verses 6 and 9: Baruch reads the scroll aloud on a day of fasting, when many people are in the temple. Perhaps the fast had been announced because of the threat from Babylon.

---

## The officials listen (Verses 11–19)

[11] Micaiah, the son of Gemariah, the son of Shaphan,
heard all the LORD’s words from the book.
[12] Then he went down to the king’s palace, into the scribe’s room.
And look, all the princes were sitting there:
Elishama the scribe,
Delaiah the son of Shemaiah,
Elnathan the son of Achbor,
Gemariah the son of Shaphan,
Zedekiah the son of Hananiah,
and all the other princes.
[13] Then Micaiah told them all the words that he had heard
when Baruch read the book to the people.
[14] So all the princes sent Jehudi, the son of Nethaniah,
the son of Shelemiah, the son of Cushi, to Baruch.
They told him, “Take the scroll that you read to the people,
and come.”
So Baruch, the son of Neriah, took the scroll in his hand,
and came to them.
[15] They said to him,
“Sit down now, and read it to us.”
So Baruch read it to them.
[16] When they had heard all the words,
they turned to one another in fear,
and said to Baruch,
“We must tell the king all these words.”
[17] They asked Baruch,
“Tell us now, how did you write all these words?
Did he dictate them to you?”
[18] Baruch answered them,
“He spoke all these words to me,
and I wrote them in the book with ink.”
[19] Then the princes said to Baruch,
“You and Jeremiah, go and hide.
Don’t let anyone know where you are.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The officials are shocked by what they hear. They take the words seriously.
> Verses 17–18: They want to know exactly whether these are really Jeremiah’s words. Baruch explains: Jeremiah dictated; I wrote with ink.
> Verse 19: The officials know their king. They know that he is dangerous (compare the murder of Uriah, 26:20–23). That is why they warn Baruch and Jeremiah: Hide!

## The king burns the scroll (Verses 20–26)

[20] They went in to the king in the courtyard,
but they had left the scroll in the room of Elishama the scribe.
They told the king all the words.
[21] So the king sent Jehudi to get the scroll,
and he took it out of the room of Elishama the scribe.
Jehudi read it to the king,
and to all the princes who stood beside the king.
[22] The king was sitting in the winter house.
It was the ninth month,
and a fire was burning in the fire pot in front of him.
[23] Whenever Jehudi had read three or four columns,
the king cut them off with a knife,
and threw them into the fire in the fire pot,
until the whole scroll was burned up in the fire.
[24] The king and his servants who heard all these words were not afraid,
and they didn’t tear their clothes.
[25] Elnathan, Delaiah, and Gemariah had begged the king
not to burn the scroll,
but he would not listen to them.
[26] The king commanded Jerahmeel, the king’s son,
Seraiah, the son of Azriel,
and Shelemiah, the son of Abdeel,
to arrest Baruch the scribe and Jeremiah the prophet.
But the LORD hid them.

> **What does this mean?**
> A dramatic scene: It is winter; the king is sitting by the warm fire pot. Jehudi reads aloud. After each part, the king cuts off what has been read with a knife and throws it into the fire. Piece by piece, until the whole scroll is burned.
> Verse 24: No one is afraid, no one tears his clothes (a sign of being sorry). What a contrast to Jehoiakim’s father Josiah: When a book of the law was read to him, he tore his clothes in shock and turned back (2 Kings 22:11).
> Verse 25: Only three officials have the courage to ask the king not to burn the scroll.
> Verse 26: The king wants to have Jeremiah and Baruch arrested, but God has hidden them.

---

## A new scroll (Verses 27–32)

[27] After the king had burned the scroll
with the words which Baruch had written as Jeremiah spoke,
the LORD’s word came to Jeremiah. He said,
[28] “Take another scroll,
and write in it all the words that were in the first scroll,
which Jehoiakim, king of Judah, has burned.
[29] And say about Jehoiakim, king of Judah,
‘The LORD says:
“You have burned this scroll, and said,
‘Why have you written in it:
“The king of Babylon will certainly come and destroy this land,
and will wipe out both people and animals from it”?’”
[30] That is why the LORD says this about Jehoiakim, king of Judah:
“He will have no one to sit on David’s throne.
His dead body will be thrown out,
into the heat by day and into the frost by night.
[31] I will punish him, his descendants, and his servants for their guilt.
I will bring on them, on the people of Jerusalem, and on the men of Judah,
all the disaster that I have announced against them.
But they didn’t listen.”’”
[32] Then Jeremiah took another scroll,
and gave it to Baruch the scribe, the son of Neriah.
As Jeremiah spoke, Baruch wrote in it
all the words of the book which Jehoiakim, king of Judah, had burned in the fire.
And many similar words were added to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 28: God’s word cannot be burned. Jeremiah and Baruch write everything down again.
> Verse 30: Jehoiakim’s son Jehoiachin ruled for only three months, then he was carried off (compare 22:30). And Jehoiakim himself was not to have an honorable burial (compare 22:19).
> Verse 32: The new scroll was even longer than the first. You can burn a book, but not the truth that is in it. In the course of history, powerful people have burned books and Bibles again and again, but God’s word has remained.
