# Jeremiah – Chapter 4
### Disaster comes from the north

---

## Break up new ground! (Verses 1–4)

[1] “If you will return, Israel,” says the LORD,
“if you will return to me,
and if you will put your disgusting idols out of my sight,
then you will not be driven away.
[2] You will swear, ‘As surely as the LORD lives,’
in truth, in justice, and in righteousness.
Then the nations will bless themselves in him,
and they will be proud of him.”
[3] For the LORD says to the men of Judah and to Jerusalem,
“Break up your unplowed ground,
and don’t sow among thorns.
[4] Circumcise yourselves for the LORD.
Take away the foreskins of your heart,
you men of Judah and people of Jerusalem.
Otherwise my anger will go out like fire,
and burn so that no one can put it out,
because of the evil of your deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: God invites them once more: If you turn back, you can stay. And then even the other nations will be blessed through you, as was promised to Abraham (Genesis 12:3).
> Verse 3: A picture from farming: A field that has not been plowed for a long time is hard and full of thorns. You first have to plow it up before you sow. In the same way, the heart must first be “plowed up” so that God’s word can grow.
> Verse 4: Circumcision was the sign of the covenant with God. But Jeremiah says: It is not only the outward sign that matters, but the heart. The heart should be open to God (compare Deuteronomy 10:16; Romans 2:29).

---

## The lion has set out (Verses 5–18)

[5] Announce it in Judah, and make it known in Jerusalem.
Say, ‘Blow the trumpet in the land!’
Shout loudly and say,
‘Gather together! Let’s go into the fortified cities!’
[6] Raise a signal flag toward Zion.
Run for safety! Don’t wait!
For I will bring disaster from the north,
and a great destruction.”
[7] A lion has come up from his thicket,
a destroyer of nations.
He is on his way.
He has gone out from his place
to make your land a waste,
so that your cities will be ruined, with no one living in them.
[8] Because of this, put on sackcloth.
Mourn and wail,
for the fierce anger of the LORD hasn’t turned away from us.
[9] “On that day,” says the LORD,
“the king will lose his courage,
and so will the princes.
The priests will be shocked,
and the prophets will be amazed.”
[10] Then I said, “Oh, Lord GOD!
Surely you have badly deceived this people and Jerusalem,
by saying, ‘You will have peace,’
while the sword reaches to the throat.”
[11] At that time it will be said to this people and to Jerusalem,
“A hot wind blows from the bare hilltops in the wilderness
toward the daughter of my people.
It is not a wind for separating grain from chaff,
and not for cleaning.
[12] A wind too strong for that will come at my command.
Now I will also speak judgments against them.”
[13] Look, he will come up like clouds.
His chariots will be like the whirlwind.
His horses are faster than eagles.
How terrible for us! For we are ruined.
[14] Jerusalem, wash your heart clean of evil,
so that you may be saved.
How long will your evil thoughts stay inside you?
[15] For a voice announces it from Dan,
and makes disaster known from the hills of Ephraim:
[16] “Tell the nations, look,
announce it against Jerusalem:
‘Besiegers are coming from a far country.
They raise their voices against the cities of Judah.
[17] Like guards of a field,
they surround her on every side,
because she has rebelled against me,’” says the LORD.
[18] “Your way and your deeds have brought these things on you.
This is your evil.
Yes, it is bitter.
Yes, it reaches to your heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: The horn (shofar) is blown, an alarm signal. Everyone is to flee into the fortified cities.
> Verse 7: The “lion” is the army of Babylon, which swallows up whole nations.
> Verse 10: Jeremiah accuses God: Have you deceived the people? There were prophets who had promised peace in God’s name. Jeremiah is in despair because these promises were false. He dares to express his despair openly before God.
> Verses 11–13: The enemy comes like a burning desert wind, like a storm, faster than eagles.
> Verse 14: In the middle of the warning, another invitation: “Jerusalem, wash your heart clean of evil, so that you may be saved.”
> Verse 15: Dan lies in the far north of the land, Ephraim in the middle. The news of the enemy is coming closer and closer.
> Verse 18: The disaster is the result of their own deeds.

## My anguish, my anguish! (Verses 19–31)

[19] My anguish, my anguish!
I am in pain deep in my heart!
My heart pounds inside me.
I can’t keep quiet,
because you have heard, my soul,
the sound of the trumpet,
the alarm of war.
[20] Destruction after destruction is announced,
for the whole land is ruined.
Suddenly my tents are destroyed,
and my curtains are gone in a moment.
[21] How long must I see the signal flag
and hear the sound of the trumpet?
[22] “For my people are foolish.
They don’t know me.
They are foolish children,
and they have no understanding.
They are skillful at doing evil,
but they don’t know how to do good.”
[23] I looked at the earth, and it was without form and empty.
I looked at the heavens, and they had no light.
[24] I looked at the mountains, and they were shaking,
and all the hills were rocking back and forth.
[25] I looked, and there was no human being,
and all the birds of the sky had fled.
[26] I looked, and the fruitful land was a wilderness.
All its cities were broken down before the LORD,
before his fierce anger.
[27] For the LORD says,
“The whole land will be a waste.
Yet I will not make a complete end.
[28] Because of this the earth will mourn,
and the heavens above will be black,
because I have spoken it.
I have planned it, and I have not changed my mind.
I will not turn back from it.”
[29] Every city runs away at the noise of the horsemen and archers.
They go into the thickets and climb up on the rocks.
Every city is abandoned,
and no one lives in them.
[30] And you, when you are ruined, what will you do?
Even if you dress in scarlet,
even if you put on gold jewelry,
even if you make your eyes bigger with makeup,
you make yourself beautiful for nothing.
Your lovers despise you.
They want to kill you.
[31] For I have heard a cry like that of a woman in labor,
the anguish like that of a woman giving birth to her first child.
It is the cry of the daughter of Zion,
who gasps for breath, who stretches out her hands, and says,
“How terrible for me now!
For I am fainting before the murderers.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–21: Jeremiah is not a cold prophet of judgment. He suffers with his people. He hears the war horn, and his heart hurts. He cannot keep quiet. Anyone who has ever been very afraid understands these words.
> Verse 22: God laments: My people are skillful at doing evil, but they have forgotten how to do good.
> Verses 23–26: A shocking vision: Jeremiah sees the earth “without form and empty,” the same words as in Genesis 1:2 before creation. No light, no human being, no birds. It is as if creation were being undone. That is how terrible war is.
> Verse 27: And yet: “I will not make a complete end.” A small spark of hope.
> Verse 30: Jerusalem is described as a woman who makes herself beautiful to win foreign lovers (foreign powers like Egypt). But they turn against her.
> Verse 31: The daughter of Zion cries out like a woman in hard labor, without hope. A picture of the distress of people in war. Sadly, there are such cries in many wars to this day.
