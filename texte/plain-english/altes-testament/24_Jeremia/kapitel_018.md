# Jeremiah – Chapter 18
### At the potter’s house

---

## Like clay in the potter’s hand (Verses 1–12)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD:
[2] “Get up, and go down to the potter’s house,
and there I will let you hear my words.”
[3] So I went down to the potter’s house.
And look, he was working at the wheel.
[4] When the pot that he was making from the clay was spoiled in the potter’s hand,
he made it into another pot,
as the potter thought best.
[5] Then the LORD’s word came to me. He said,
[6] “House of Israel, can’t I do with you as this potter does?” says the LORD.
“Look, as the clay is in the potter’s hand,
so are you in my hand, house of Israel.
[7] At one moment I may speak about a nation or a kingdom,
that I will pull it up, break it down, and destroy it.
[8] But if that nation I spoke about turns away from its evil,
I will change my mind about the disaster that I planned to do to it.
[9] At another moment I may speak about a nation or a kingdom,
that I will build it and plant it.
[10] But if it does what is evil in my eyes,
and does not obey my voice,
then I will change my mind about the good that I said I would do for it.
[11] “So now, speak to the men of Judah and to the people of Jerusalem. Say,
‘The LORD says:
“Look, I am shaping disaster against you,
and making a plan against you.
Now, each of you, turn back from your evil way,
and change your ways and your deeds.”’
[12] But they say,
‘It is no use!
For we will follow our own plans,
and each of us will follow the stubbornness of his evil heart.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeremiah goes to a potter and watches him at work. When a pot does not turn out well, the potter does not throw the clay away, but makes a new pot out of it.
> Verse 6: That is how Israel is in God’s hand. God can make something new out of the “spoiled” people.
> Verses 7–10: An important thought: God’s announcements are not fixed forever. If a nation turns back, God does not bring the disaster he announced (as with Nineveh in the book of Jonah). And if a nation does evil, God takes back his good promise. God responds to what people do. The future is open.
> Verse 11: That is why the invitation: Turn back! It is not too late yet.
> Verse 12: But the people answer: “It is no use! For we will follow our own plans.”
> The picture of the potter is comforting: Even if something in our life goes wrong, God can shape something new out of it.

## My people have forgotten me (Verses 13–17)

[13] That is why the LORD says:
“Ask now among the nations,
‘Who has heard of such things?’
The virgin Israel has done a very horrible thing.
[14] Does the snow of Lebanon ever disappear from the rocky slopes?
Do the cold waters that flow down from far away ever dry up?
[15] But my people have forgotten me.
They have burned incense to false gods.
They have been made to stumble in their ways,
on the ancient paths,
to walk on side paths,
on a road that is not built up.
[16] They make their land a horror,
something people will always hiss at.
Everyone who passes by it will be horrified,
and shake his head.
[17] I will scatter them in front of the enemy,
as with an east wind.
I will show them my back, and not my face,
on the day of their disaster.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Nature is faithful: The snow on the mountains of Lebanon stays; the cold mountain streams always flow. But God’s people have become unfaithful.
> Verse 15: They have left the old, good paths and are walking on bumpy side paths (compare 6:16).
> Verse 16: Whoever passes by the destroyed land will shake their head in horror. “Hissing” was an expression of horror at that time.
> Verse 17: The east wind is a hot desert wind. God will turn away: “I will show them my back, and not my face.”

## A plot against Jeremiah (Verses 18–23)

[18] Then they said,
“Come! Let’s make plans against Jeremiah.
For the priest will not run out of teaching,
the wise man will not run out of advice,
and the prophet will not run out of words.
Come, let’s attack him with our tongues,
and let’s not pay attention to any of his words.”
[19] Pay attention to me, LORD,
and listen to what those who argue against me are saying.
[20] Should evil be paid back for good?
For they have dug a pit for me.
Remember how I stood in front of you
to speak good for them,
to turn your anger away from them.
[21] So hand over their children to famine,
and give them over to the power of the sword.
Let their wives become childless and widows.
Let their men be killed,
and their young men struck down by the sword in battle.
[22] Let a cry be heard from their houses,
when you suddenly bring an army against them.
For they have dug a pit to catch me,
and hidden traps for my feet.
[23] Yet, LORD, you know all their plans against me to kill me.
Don’t forgive their guilt.
Don’t wipe out their sin from your sight.
Let them be thrown down in front of you.
Deal with them in the time of your anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: Jeremiah’s opponents say: We don’t need him. We have our priests, wise men and prophets. Let’s finish him off with words, by slander.
> Verse 20: Jeremiah is deeply hurt. He even prayed for these people, and now they want to kill him.
> Verses 21–23: Jeremiah prays a harsh prayer of cursing against his enemies and their families. These words are frightening. They show how desperate and hurt Jeremiah was. The Bible does not hide such feelings. It shows a person who brings all his anger before God, instead of using violence himself.
> Important: These words are not an instruction to wish evil on others or to do evil to them. Jesus showed another way: “Love your enemies … And pray for those who treat you badly and persecute you” (Matthew 5:44). On the cross he prayed: “Father, forgive them” (Luke 23:34).
