# Jeremiah – Chapter 40
### Gedaliah, the governor

---

## Jeremiah may choose (Verses 1–6)

[1] This is the word which came to Jeremiah from the LORD,
after Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, had let him go from Ramah.
He had taken him there bound in chains
among all the captives of Jerusalem and Judah
who were being carried away to Babylon.
[2] The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him,
“The LORD your God announced this disaster against this place.
[3] And the LORD has brought it about,
and has done just as he said.
Because you have sinned against the LORD,
and have not obeyed his voice,
this has happened to you.
[4] Now look, today I am setting you free
from the chains on your hands.
If you want to come with me to Babylon, come,
and I will take care of you.
But if you don’t want to come with me to Babylon, don’t.
Look, the whole land is in front of you.
Go wherever you think it is good and right to go.”
[5] Before Jeremiah turned to go, Nebuzaradan said,
“Go back to Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan,
whom the king of Babylon has made governor over the cities of Judah,
and live with him among the people.
Or go wherever you think it is right to go.”
So the captain of the guard gave him food and a gift,
and let him go.
[6] Then Jeremiah went to Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, at Mizpah,
and lived with him among the people who were left in the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Jeremiah had first been put in chains with the other prisoners and taken as far as Ramah. Ramah was a gathering place for those being carried off (compare 31:15: Rachel weeps in Ramah).
> Verses 2–3: Amazing: A Babylonian officer says what Jeremiah had always said: This happened because the people did not listen to God. Even a foreigner sees it.
> Verse 4: Jeremiah may choose: to go to Babylon and be taken care of, or to stay in the destroyed land. Jeremiah decides to stay with his people, with the poor in the land.
> (Jeremiah 39:14 tells more briefly that Jeremiah was taken out of the courtyard of the guard and handed over to Gedaliah. Probably he afterward ended up among the lines of prisoners and was set free again in Ramah.)

## Gedaliah gathers the people (Verses 7–12)

[7] All the army commanders who were out in the countryside, they and their men,
heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, governor in the land.
They heard that he had put him in charge of men, women, and children,
and of the poorest people of the land,
those who had not been carried away to Babylon.
[8] So they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah:
Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah,
Johanan and Jonathan, the sons of Kareah,
Seraiah, the son of Tanhumeth,
the sons of Ephai the Netophathite,
and Jezaniah, the son of the Maacathite,
they and their men.
[9] Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan,
swore an oath to them and to their men. He said,
“Don’t be afraid to serve the Chaldeans.
Live in the land,
and serve the king of Babylon,
and it will go well with you.
[10] As for me, look, I will live at Mizpah,
to represent you in front of the Chaldeans who come to us.
But you, gather wine, summer fruit, and oil,
and put them in your storage jars,
and live in your towns that you have taken over.”
[11] All the Jews who were in Moab,
and among the people of Ammon,
and in Edom,
and in all the other countries,
also heard that the king of Babylon had left some people in Judah,
and that he had put Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, over them.
[12] Then all the Jews came back from all the places where they had been driven.
They came to the land of Judah, to Gedaliah at Mizpah,
and gathered a very large harvest of wine and summer fruit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: After the war, groups of soldiers are still scattered throughout the land. Their leaders come to Gedaliah.
> Verses 9–10: Gedaliah gives the same advice Jeremiah gave: Submit to Babylon and live in peace. Harvest, lay up supplies, build a life for yourselves.
> Verses 11–12: Many refugees return from the neighboring countries. There is a rich harvest. For a short time it looks as if a new beginning could succeed in the destroyed land.

## A warning that is not heeded (Verses 13–16)

[13] Johanan, the son of Kareah,
and all the army commanders who had been out in the countryside,
came to Gedaliah at Mizpah,
[14] and said to him,
“Do you know that Baalis, the king of the people of Ammon,
has sent Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah, to kill you?”
But Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, didn’t believe them.
[15] Then Johanan, the son of Kareah, spoke to Gedaliah secretly in Mizpah. He said,
“Please let me go, and I will kill Ishmael, the son of Nethaniah.
No one will know about it.
Why should he kill you,
so that all the Jews who have gathered around you are scattered,
and the rest of Judah is destroyed?”
[16] But Gedaliah, the son of Ahikam, said to Johanan, the son of Kareah,
“You must not do this,
for what you are saying about Ishmael is not true.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gedaliah is warned of a plot to murder him. Johanan offers to kill Ishmael secretly beforehand.
> Gedaliah refuses. He does not want anyone to be killed on mere suspicion, and he believes in the good in Ishmael. That is honorable, but sadly too trusting, as the next chapter shows.
