# Jeremiah – Chapter 25
### 70 years and the cup of anger

---

## For 23 years you have not listened (Verses 1–7)

[1] This is the word that came to Jeremiah about all the people of Judah,
in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, king of Judah.
(This was the first year of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.)
[2] Jeremiah the prophet spoke it to all the people of Judah,
and to all the people of Jerusalem:
[3] From the thirteenth year of Josiah, the son of Amon, king of Judah,
until today, for these 23 years,
the LORD’s word has come to me.
I have spoken to you early and tirelessly,
but you have not listened.
[4] The LORD has sent you all his servants the prophets,
early and tirelessly,
but you have not listened or paid attention.
[5] They said, “Now, each of you, turn back from your evil way,
and from the evil of your deeds.
Then you can live in the land that the LORD has given to you and to your ancestors,
from long ago and forever.
[6] Don’t follow other gods to serve them or worship them,
and don’t make me angry with the work of your hands.
Then I will do you no harm.”
[7] “Yet you have not listened to me,” says the LORD.
“You have made me angry with the work of your hands,
and that only harms you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The fourth year of Jehoiakim is 605 BC. In that year Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptians at Carchemish and became king of Babylon. A turning point in world history.
> Verse 3: Jeremiah has been preaching for 23 years, “early and tirelessly,” but no one listens. What endurance!
> Verses 5–6: The message was always the same and very simple: Turn back, then you may stay in the land.

---

## 70 years (Verses 8–14)

[8] That is why the LORD of Armies says:
“Because you have not listened to my words,
[9] look, I will send for all the families of the north,” says the LORD,
“and I will send for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, my servant.
I will bring them against this land,
and against the people who live in it,
and against all these nations around it.
I will completely destroy them,
and make them a horror,
something people hiss at,
and ruins that last forever.
[10] I will take away from them
the sound of joy and the sound of gladness,
the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride,
the sound of the millstones,
and the light of the lamp.
[11] This whole land will be a ruin and a horror,
and these nations will serve the king of Babylon for 70 years.
[12] “When the 70 years are over,
I will punish the king of Babylon and that nation for their guilt,” says the LORD.
“I will make the land of the Chaldeans a waste forever.
[13] I will bring on that land all the words which I have spoken against it,
everything that is written in this book,
which Jeremiah has prophesied against all the nations.
[14] For many nations and great kings will make slaves of them too.
I will pay them back for their deeds,
and for the work of their hands.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Amazing: God calls Nebuchadnezzar, the enemy king, “my servant.” He is a tool in God’s hand without knowing it.
> Verse 10: A very moving picture of a destroyed land: No more laughter, no more weddings, no more sound of the hand mills that ground flour every morning, no more light in the houses in the evening. Ordinary everyday life stops.
> Verses 11–12: The “70 years” are a round number for a human lifetime. From the first carrying away (605 BC) until the return home after the decree of Cyrus (538 BC), about 70 years passed. Later, Daniel (Daniel 9:2) and Chronicles (2 Chronicles 36:21) thought about this number.
> Verses 12–14: Babylon too will be called to account. No power lasts forever.

## The cup of the wine of anger (Verses 15–29)

[15] For the LORD, the God of Israel, says to me:
“Take this cup of the wine of anger from my hand,
and make all the nations I send you to drink it.
[16] They will drink, and stagger back and forth,
and go out of their minds,
because of the sword that I will send among them.”
[17] Then I took the cup from the LORD’s hand,
and made all the nations drink it that the LORD had sent me to:
[18] Jerusalem and the cities of Judah,
with its kings and its princes,
to make them a ruin, a horror,
something people hiss at and curse,
as it is today;
[19] Pharaoh, king of Egypt,
with his servants, his princes, and all his people;
[20] and all the mixed peoples,
and all the kings of the land of Uz,
all the kings of the Philistines,
Ashkelon, Gaza, Ekron, and what is left of Ashdod;
[21] Edom, Moab, and the people of Ammon;
[22] and all the kings of Tyre,
all the kings of Sidon,
and the kings of the islands across the sea;
[23] Dedan, Tema, Buz,
and all who cut off the corners of their beards;
[24] and all the kings of Arabia,
and all the kings of the mixed peoples who live in the wilderness;
[25] and all the kings of Zimri,
all the kings of Elam,
and all the kings of the Medes;
[26] and all the kings of the north, far and near, one after another;
and all the kingdoms of the world on the face of the earth.
The king of Sheshach will drink after them.
[27] “Tell them, ‘The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says:
“Drink, and get drunk, and vomit,
and fall, and don’t get up again,
because of the sword which I will send among you.”’
[28] If they refuse to take the cup from your hand to drink,
then tell them, ‘The LORD of Armies says:
“You must drink it!
[29] For look, I am beginning to bring disaster on the city which is called by my name.
Should you go completely unpunished?
You will not go unpunished,
for I am calling for a sword against all the people who live on the earth,
says the LORD of Armies.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> The “cup of the wine of anger” is a picture: Whoever drinks from it staggers like a drunk person. What is meant is the disaster of war that comes on many nations.
> Verses 18–26: A long list of nations around Israel: Egypt, the Philistines, Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre, Sidon, Arab tribes, Elam and the Medes in the east. All of them will feel the power of Babylon.
> Verse 26: “Sheshach” is a secret name for Babylon. To make it, the letters of the Hebrew alphabet were swapped: the first with the last, the second with the second to last, and so on. That is how “Babel” becomes “Sheshach.” (“Babel” is the Hebrew name for Babylon.) Babylon’s turn comes last.
> Verse 29: If God begins the judgment with his own city, then the other nations will certainly not be spared. (Compare 1 Peter 4:17: “For the time has come for judgment to begin with the household of God.”)

## The LORD roars like a lion (Verses 30–38)

[30] “So prophesy all these words against them, and tell them,
‘The LORD will roar from on high,
and raise his voice from his holy home.
He will roar loudly against his pasture.
He will shout, like those who stamp on grapes,
against all the people who live on the earth.
[31] A noise will reach the end of the earth,
for the LORD has a case against the nations.
He will go to court with all people.
As for the wicked, he will hand them over to the sword,’ says the LORD.”

[32] The LORD of Armies says,
“Look, disaster will go out from nation to nation,
and a great storm will be stirred up from the ends of the earth.”
[33] On that day, those killed by the LORD will lie
from one end of the earth to the other end of the earth.
No one will mourn for them.
They won’t be gathered or buried.
They will be like manure on the surface of the ground.
[34] Wail, you shepherds, and cry out.
Roll in the dust, you leaders of the flock,
for the days of your slaughter have come,
and you will be scattered.
You will fall and break like a precious jar.
[35] The shepherds will have no place to run to.
The leaders of the flock will have no escape.
[36] Listen to the cry of the shepherds,
and the wailing of the leaders of the flock,
for the LORD is destroying their pasture.
[37] The peaceful pastures are silenced
because of the fierce anger of the LORD.
[38] He has left his hiding place like a lion,
for their land has become a horror
because of the fierce oppression,
and because of his fierce anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 30–31: God “roars” like a lion. He has a case in court against all nations. All the injustice of the world is brought before God.
> Verses 34–36: The “shepherds,” the kings and the powerful who led their nations badly, will be held responsible.
> These verses describe judgment as God’s business. They are not a call for people to wage war. War is described here as a terrible disaster, not as something good.
