# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 8
### No one can find out God’s work

---

## People and the king (Verses 1–9)

[1] Who is like the wise person?
And who knows the meaning of a thing?
A person’s wisdom makes his face shine,
and the hardness of his face is changed.
[2] I say, “Keep the king’s command!”
because of the oath before God.
[3] Don’t be in a hurry to leave his presence.
Don’t stay with an evil thing,
for he does whatever he pleases,
[4] for the king’s word has power.
Who can say to him, “What are you doing?”
[5] Whoever keeps the command will not come to harm,
and a wise heart will know the right time and the right way.
[6] For there is a time and a way for every purpose,
even though a person’s misery lies heavily on him.
[7] For he does not know what will be.
For who can tell him how it will be?
[8] No one has power over the spirit to hold back the spirit.
He also has no power over the day of death.
There is no release from service in war,
and wickedness will not save those who do it.
[9] I have seen all this,
and I set my mind on every work that is done under the sun.
There is a time when one person has power over another to his harm.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Wisdom makes a face friendly. A wise person does not look hard, but shines.
> Verses 2–5: A wise way of dealing with the powerful: be careful, take no hasty action. That does not mean that you have to obey every command, not even when it demands something wrong. The Bible tells of people who obeyed God rather than kings (Daniel 3; Acts 5:29).
> Verse 8: A sentence full of humility: No one can hold back the spirit, and no one can decide the day of his death. The Hebrew word for “spirit” can also mean “wind” or “breath.” No one can hold back the wind either.
> Verse 9: Power is often misused to harm others.

---

## When punishment is slow to come (Verses 10–15)

[10] So I saw the wicked buried.
Yes, they even came from the holy place.
They went away and were forgotten in the city where they had done this.
This too is vanity.
[11] Because the sentence against an evil deed is not carried out quickly,
the heart of people is fully set on doing evil.
[12] Even if a sinner commits crimes 100 times and lives long,
yet I surely know that it will go better with those who fear God,
who have reverence before him.
[13] But it will not go well with the wicked,
and he will not make his days longer like a shadow,
because he does not fear God.
[14] There is a vanity that happens on the earth:
There are righteous people to whom things happen as if they had acted like the wicked.
And there are wicked people to whom things happen as if they had acted like the righteous.
I said that this too is vanity.
[15] Then I praised joy,
for a person has nothing better under the sun
than to eat, to drink, and to be joyful.
For that will go with him in his hard work
all the days of his life that God has given him under the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: This sentence is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: Evil people, who even went in and out of the temple, received an honorable burial. Then people forgot what they had done.
> Verse 11: When crimes are not punished quickly, people feel encouraged to keep doing evil. That is still true today for courts and society.
> Verses 12–13: Still the Preacher believes: In the end, it will go well with those who fear God.
> Verse 14: But he also sees the opposite: Good people do badly, evil people do well. He bears both side by side, without an easy answer.
> Verse 15: His answer is again: Enjoy the life that God gives you.

---

## God’s work cannot be found out (Verses 16–17)

[16] When I set my heart on knowing wisdom
and on seeing the busy activity that happens on the earth
(even though the eyes see no sleep day or night),
[17] then I saw all the work of God:
that people cannot find out the work that is done under the sun.
For however hard a person works to search it out,
he will not find it.
Yes, even if a wise person thinks he can understand it,
he will not be able to find it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Preacher has thought about it day and night. His result: No one can fully understand what God does, not even the wisest person.
> This is not despair, but humility. We don’t have to understand everything in order to trust God. Paul says: “No one can search out his judgments. No one can trace his ways” (Romans 11:33).
