# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 4
### Two are better than one

---

## The tears of the oppressed (Verses 1–3)

[1] Then I turned and saw all the oppression
that is done under the sun:
There were the tears of those who were oppressed,
and they had no comforter.
On the side of their oppressors there was power,
but they had no comforter.
[2] So I praised the dead who have been dead a long time
more than the living who are still alive.
[3] Yes, better than both of them is the one who has not yet been,
who has not seen the evil work
that is done under the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Preacher sees the suffering of the oppressed. Twice he says: “They had no comforter.” No one helps them.
> Verses 2–3: In despair over so much suffering, he says: The dead are better off, and best of all would be never to have been born. That is an expression of deep grief over the injustice of the world, as with Job (Job 3). It is not a call to end your life.
> In other places, the Bible answers this cry: God himself wants to be the comforter of the oppressed (Isaiah 40:1; 2 Corinthians 1:3–4). And we are to become comforters for others.
> If you are in despair: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Envy and quietness (Verses 4–8)

[4] Then I saw all the hard work and success,
that it comes from one person’s envy of another.
This too is vanity and a chasing after wind.
[5] The fool folds his hands
and ruins himself.
[6] Better one handful with quietness
than two handfuls with hard work and chasing after wind.
[7] Then I turned and saw vanity under the sun.
[8] There is someone who is alone,
and he has neither son nor brother.
Yet there is no end to all his hard work,
and his eyes are not satisfied with wealth.
“For whom am I working so hard
and keeping my soul from enjoyment?”
This too is vanity.
Yes, it is a miserable business.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: A lot of hard work and success comes from competition and envy: People want to be better than their neighbor. That does not make anyone happy.
> Verses 5–6: Two extremes: The lazy person does nothing and is ruined. The stressed person works with both hands and has no rest. The middle is better: one handful, but with quietness.
> Verse 8: A sad picture: A person works and works, becomes richer and richer, but he is completely alone. For whom, really? He allows himself nothing and has no one to share with.

## Two are better than one (Verses 9–12)

[9] Two are better than one,
because they have a good reward for their hard work.
[10] For if they fall, the one will help his friend up.
But how terrible for the one who is alone when he falls,
and there is no one else to help him up.
[11] Also, if two lie down together, they keep warm.
But how can one keep warm alone?
[12] If someone overpowers one person who is alone,
two will stand up against him.
And a cord of three strands is not quickly broken.

> **What does this mean?**
> A wonderful text about community and friendship. It is often read at weddings.
> Two together are stronger: They help each other up, keep each other warm, protect each other.
> Verse 12: “A cord of three strands”: Three threads twisted together are much stronger than one. At weddings, people often say: The third strand is God, who holds the couple together.
> This text is a comfort for everyone who has community, and an invitation for everyone who is lonely: Look for people who will walk with you. And be there for others yourself.

## The young king and the old king (Verses 13–16)

[13] Better a poor and wise young man
than an old and foolish king
who no longer knows how to accept a warning.
[14] For he came out of prison to become king.
Yes, he was even born poor in his kingdom.
[15] I saw all the living who walk under the sun,
that they were with the young man, the other one,
who took his place.
[16] There was no end to all the people,
to all those over whom he was set.
And yet those who come later will not be glad about him.
Surely this too is vanity and a chasing after wind.

> **What does this mean?**
> A little story: An old king does not listen to anyone anymore. A poor young man, who was even in prison, becomes king in his place. Everyone cheers for him.
> But his popularity does not last forever either. The next generation has already forgotten him.
> Fame and popularity are fleeting. This too is “a chasing after wind.”
