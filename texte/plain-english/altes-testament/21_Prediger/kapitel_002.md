# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 2
### The great experiment

---

## I tried pleasure (Verses 1–11)

[1] I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with joy.
So enjoy pleasure!”
But this too was vanity.
[2] I said about laughter, “It is foolishness,”
and about joy, “What does it achieve?”
[3] I searched in my heart
how I could cheer my body with wine,
while my heart was still guiding me with wisdom,
and how I could hold on to foolishness,
until I could see what is good for people
to do under heaven
all the days of their lives.
[4] I made great works for myself.
I built houses for myself.
I planted vineyards for myself.
[5] I made gardens and parks for myself,
and I planted trees with all kinds of fruit in them.
[6] I made pools of water for myself,
to water the forest where trees were growing.
[7] I bought male servants and female servants,
and I had servants who were born in my house.
I also had great possessions of herds and flocks,
more than all who were before me in Jerusalem.
[8] I also gathered silver and gold for myself
and the treasures of kings and provinces.
I got male and female singers for myself,
and the delights of people:
musical instruments of all kinds.
[9] So I became great and richer than all
who were before me in Jerusalem.
My wisdom also stayed with me.
[10] Whatever my eyes wanted, I did not keep from them.
I did not hold my heart back from any joy,
for my heart was glad because of all my work,
and this was my share from all my work.
[11] Then I looked at all the works that my hands had done,
and at the hard work I had worked so hard at,
and all was vanity and a chasing after wind,
and there was no profit under the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Preacher carries out a big experiment: Does pleasure make you happy? He has everything: houses, gardens, parks, pools, servants, herds, gold, music, wine. He allows himself every wish.
> The result: emptiness. Nothing is left that really counts.
> This is still true today: Many rich and famous people say that possessions and success did not make them happy.
> Verse 7: Owning slaves was normal back then. Today we know: No person may belong to another.

## The wise person dies like the fool (Verses 12–17)

[12] I turned to look at wisdom, madness, and foolishness.
For what can the king’s successor do?
Only what has already been done long ago.
[13] Then I saw that wisdom is better than foolishness,
as light is better than darkness.
[14] The wise person has his eyes in his head,
and the fool walks in darkness.
And yet I saw that the same thing happens to them all.
[15] Then I said in my heart,
“What happens to the fool will happen to me too.
Why then have I been so much wiser?”
Then I said in my heart that this too is vanity.
[16] For the wise person, just like the fool,
will not be remembered forever,
for in the days to come everything will long be forgotten.
Yes, the wise person must die just like the fool!
[17] So I hated life,
for the work that is done under the sun was painful to me,
for all is vanity and a chasing after wind.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: Wisdom is better than foolishness, as light is better than darkness. The Preacher says that too.
> But: In the end both die, the wise person and the fool. And both are forgotten.
> Verse 17: “So I hated life”: a shockingly honest sentence. The Preacher does not hide his despair. The Bible has room for feelings like these.
> If you feel this way yourself and can no longer bear life: Please talk with someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## What is it all for? (Verses 18–23)

[18] I hated all my hard work that I had worked at under the sun,
because I must leave it to the person who comes after me.
[19] Who knows whether he will be wise or a fool?
And yet he will rule over all my hard work
that I worked at and in which I showed myself wise under the sun.
This too is vanity.
[20] So I began to let my heart despair
over all the hard work that I had worked at under the sun.
[21] For there is a person who works with wisdom, knowledge, and skill,
and yet he must leave it as a share to a person
who did not work for it.
This too is vanity and a great evil.
[22] For what does a person get from all his hard work
and from the striving of his heart
that he works at under the sun?
[23] For all his days are pain,
and his work is grief.
Yes, even at night his heart finds no rest.
This too is vanity.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Preacher is upset: I worked my whole life and built everything up wisely. And who gets it after my death? Maybe a fool who throws everything away.
> Verse 23: He describes what many people know: stress, worries, sleepless nights because of work. And in the end you ask yourself: What for?

---

## Eat, drink and enjoy (Verses 24–26)

[24] There is nothing better for a person
than to eat and drink
and let his soul enjoy good things in his work.
I also saw this, that it comes from the hand of God.
[25] For who can eat, or who can enjoy things, more than I?
[26] For to the person who pleases him,
God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy.
But to the sinner he gives the trouble of gathering and piling up,
to give it to the one who pleases God.
This too is vanity and a chasing after wind.

> **What does this mean?**
> After all the despair comes a surprising turn: Enjoy the simple things! Eating, drinking, joy in your work. This is not a reward that you earn, but a gift from God’s hand.
> This is a basic thought of the book: We cannot hold on to life or understand it completely. But we can accept the good moments thankfully from God’s hand.
