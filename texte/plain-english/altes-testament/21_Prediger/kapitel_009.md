# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 9
### Eat your bread with joy

---

## The same thing happens to everyone (Verses 1–6)

[1] For I took all this to heart, to search out all this:
that the righteous and the wise and their works are in God’s hand.
Whether it is love or hatred, people do not know.
Everything lies before them.
[2] Everything comes alike to all.
There is one fate for the righteous and the wicked,
for the good, for the clean and the unclean,
for the one who sacrifices and the one who does not sacrifice.
As it goes for the good, so it goes for the sinner,
for the one who takes an oath as for the one who is afraid of an oath.
[3] This is an evil in all that is done under the sun,
that the same fate comes to all.
Yes, also the heart of people is full of evil,
and madness is in their heart while they live,
and after that they go to the dead.
[4] For whoever is joined with all the living has hope,
for a living dog is better than a dead lion.
[5] For the living know that they will die,
but the dead don’t know anything,
and they have no more reward,
for the memory of them is forgotten.
[6] Their love, their hatred, and their envy are also long gone,
and they will never again have a share
in anything that is done under the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: All people are in God’s hand. But no one knows what the future will bring, love or hatred.
> Verses 2–3: A hard thought: Death comes to everyone alike, good and evil people.
> Verse 4: “A living dog is better than a dead lion.” Back then, dogs were looked down on, and lions were admired. But life itself is precious. As long as you live, there is hope.
> Verses 5–6: The Preacher looks at death soberly. He does not yet know about a resurrection. Later in the Bible, this hope grows (Daniel 12:2; 1 Corinthians 15). Christians believe that Jesus has defeated death.

## Enjoy life (Verses 7–10)

[7] Go, eat your bread with joy,
and drink your wine with a glad heart,
for God has already accepted your works.
[8] Let your clothes always be white,
and don’t let your head lack oil.
[9] Enjoy life with the wife whom you love
all the days of your life of vanity,
which he has given you under the sun,
all your days of vanity,
for that is your share in life
and in your hard work that you work at under the sun.
[10] Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your strength,
for in Sheol, the world of the dead, where you are going,
there is no work, no plan, no knowledge, and no wisdom.

> **What does this mean?**
> One of the most beautiful passages of the book: Because life is short, live it with joy!
> Verse 7: “God has already accepted your works”: You don’t first have to earn God’s goodwill. Enjoy life.
> Verse 8: White clothes and oil on the head were signs of feasts and joy. Make every day a little bit of a feast.
> Verse 9: Enjoy life with the person you love.
> Verse 10: Whatever you do, do it with your whole heart. Don’t put anything off. Time is limited.

## Time and chance (Verses 11–12)

[11] I saw again under the sun
that the race is not won by the fast,
and the battle is not won by the strong,
and bread does not go to the wise,
and riches do not go to people of understanding,
and favor does not go to the skilled,
but time and chance happen to them all.
[12] For people do not know their time either.
Like fish that are caught in a cruel net,
and like birds that are caught in a trap,
so people are trapped in an evil time,
when it falls on them suddenly.

> **What does this mean?**
> A very honest look: The best does not always win. Sometimes luck and chance decide. The fastest can stumble, the wisest can stay poor.
> Verse 12: Misfortune often comes suddenly, like a net over a fish. No one can protect himself from it completely.
> That is no reason to despair, but a reason to be humble. We do not have everything in our hands.

## The poor wise man whom no one knows (Verses 13–18)

[13] I have also seen this wisdom under the sun,
and it seemed great to me:
[14] There was a small city, and there were few men in it.
A great king came against it,
surrounded it, and built great siege works against it.
[15] In it there was a poor, wise man,
and he saved the city by his wisdom.
Yet no one remembered that poor man.
[16] Then I said, “Wisdom is better than strength.”
But the poor man’s wisdom is despised,
and no one listens to his words.
[17] The words of the wise, heard in quiet,
are better than the shouting of someone who rules among fools.
[18] Wisdom is better than weapons of war,
but one sinner destroys much good.

> **What does this mean?**
> A little story: A poor, wise man saves his city from a mighty enemy. But afterward, everyone forgets him.
> Verse 16: Wisdom is stronger than violence. But sadly, people often do not listen to the poor, even when they are wise.
> Verse 17: Quiet, wise words are better than the loud shouting of a leader.
> Verse 18: Wisdom is better than weapons. But a single person who does evil can destroy a lot of good.
