# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 5
### Whoever loves money is never satisfied

---

## Few words before God (Verses 1–7)

[1] Watch your steps when you go to God’s house.
For coming near to listen is better
than giving the sacrifice of fools,
for they do not know that they are doing evil.
[2] Don’t be hasty with your mouth,
and don’t let your heart rush to say anything before God,
for God is in heaven, and you are on earth.
So let your words be few.
[3] For as a dream comes with many worries,
so a fool’s speech comes with many words.
[4] When you make a vow to God, don’t delay in keeping it,
for he has no pleasure in fools.
Keep what you vow.
[5] It is better not to make a vow
than to make a vow and not keep it.
[6] Don’t let your mouth lead you into sin.
Don’t say before the messenger that it was a mistake.
Why should God be angry at your voice
and destroy the work of your hands?
[7] For where there are many dreams, there are empty things,
and also where there are many words.
But you must fear God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: In worship, listening is more important than religious rituals without the heart.
> Verse 2: “God is in heaven, and you are on earth. So let your words be few.” God does not need long, beautiful prayers. Jesus says something similar (Matthew 6:7–8).
> Verses 4–5: Keep what you promise God. Better not to promise anything at all than to break a promise.
> Verse 6: “The messenger” is probably the priest before whom a vow was made. You should not try to talk your way out: “That was only a mistake.”

---

## Injustice in the state (Verses 8–9)

[8] If you see that the poor are oppressed in a district,
and justice and righteousness are taken away by force,
don’t be surprised at the matter,
for one official is watched by a higher one,
and there are even higher officials over them.
[9] Besides, the profit of the land is for all.
The king profits from the field.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Preacher describes soberly how things go in a big state: One official stands above another. Everyone wants a share. In the end, the poor suffer.
> “Don’t be surprised” does not mean “accept it.” It means: Don’t be shocked. That is how the world is. Other parts of the Bible clearly call for standing up against such injustice (for example Amos 5:24).
> Verse 9 is hard to understand in Hebrew. Maybe it means: Everyone lives from what the land produces, even the king.

---

## Riches do not satisfy (Verses 10–17)

[10] Whoever loves silver will not be satisfied with silver,
and whoever loves abundance will not be satisfied with what he earns.
This too is vanity.
[11] When possessions increase, those who eat them increase too.
And what good are they to the owner,
except that his eyes can feast on them?
[12] The sleep of a worker is sweet,
whether he eats little or much,
but the abundance of the rich person does not let him sleep.
[13] There is a terrible evil that I have seen under the sun:
riches kept by their owner to his own harm.
[14] Those riches are lost through a misfortune,
and if he has a son,
there is nothing in his hand.
[15] As he came out of his mother’s womb,
so he will go again naked, as he came,
and he will take nothing for his hard work
that he could carry away in his hand.
[16] This too is a terrible evil:
Exactly as he came, so he will go.
And what does he gain, he who works hard for the wind?
[17] Also, all his days he eats in darkness.
He is full of frustration and has sickness and anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: A timeless sentence: Whoever loves money never has enough. It is like drinking salt water: The more you drink, the thirstier you become.
> Verse 11: Whoever has more also has more people around him who want to live from it.
> Verse 12: A simple worker sleeps well. A rich person lies awake and worries about his money.
> Verse 15: “So he will go again naked, as he came”: We cannot take anything with us. Job (Job 1:21) and Paul (1 Timothy 6:7) say something similar. There is also a saying: Shrouds have no pockets.

---

## Joy as a gift from God (Verses 18–20)

[18] Look, this is what I have seen to be good and right:
that a person eats and drinks
and enjoys good things in all his hard work
that he works at under the sun,
all the days of his life that God has given him,
for this is his share.
[19] And every person to whom God has given riches and wealth,
and to whom he has given the ability to eat of it,
to take his share and to be glad in his work,
that is the gift of God.
[20] For he will not often think about the days of his life,
because God keeps him busy with the joy of his heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the Preacher’s answer: Enjoy what God gives you. It is not money itself that makes you happy, but the ability to enjoy it thankfully. This ability too is a gift from God.
> Verse 20: Whoever enjoys life does not keep brooding over how short it is. God fills his heart with joy.
