# Ecclesiastes – Chapter 7
### Better than …

---

## What is better? (Verses 1–6)

[1] A good name is better than fine perfume,
and the day of death better than the day of birth.
[2] It is better to go to a house of mourning
than to go to a house of feasting,
for that is the end of all people,
and the living should take it to heart.
[3] Sorrow is better than laughter,
for by a sad face the heart is made good.
[4] The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning,
but the heart of fools is in the house of fun.
[5] It is better to hear the correction of the wise
than for someone to hear the song of fools.
[6] For like the crackling of thorns under a pot,
so is the laughter of the fool.
This too is vanity.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins a series of “better than” sayings.
> Verse 1: In Hebrew, a play on words: “shem” (name) and “shemen” (oil). A good reputation is worth more than expensive perfume.
> Verses 1–4: Surprising: Death and mourning are supposed to be better than birth and celebration? The Preacher means: Whoever goes to a funeral thinks about life. He becomes more serious and wiser. At a party, people don’t think about it.
> That does not mean that we should not celebrate. In other places, the Preacher praises joy. But whoever never thinks about death lives on the surface.
> Verse 6: Thorns burn loudly and quickly, but they give little warmth. That is what the loud laughter of fools is like: a lot of noise about nothing.

---

## Patience and wisdom (Verses 7–14)

[7] Surely taking money by force makes the wise person foolish,
and a bribe destroys understanding.
[8] The end of a thing is better than its beginning.
The patient person is better than the proud person.
[9] Don’t be quick in your spirit to become angry,
for anger rests in the lap of fools.
[10] Don’t say, “Why were the former days better than these?”
For you do not ask this out of wisdom.
[11] Wisdom is as good as an inheritance.
Yes, it is an advantage for those who see the sun.
[12] For wisdom is a protection, just as money is a protection,
but the advantage of knowledge is
that wisdom keeps alive the one who has it.
[13] Think about the work of God,
for who can make straight what he has made crooked?
[14] On the day of good fortune, be joyful,
and on the day of misfortune, think:
Yes, God has put the one beside the other,
so that people cannot find out anything that comes after them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: You should not judge a thing before its end. Patience pays off.
> Verse 9: Anger lives with fools. Whoever is wise does not get angry quickly.
> Verse 10: “Why were the former days better than these?” People in every generation ask this. They mean: Everything was better in the past. The Preacher says: That is not a wise question. Every time has its good and its bad.
> Verse 14: Wise advice: When things go well for you, be glad! When things go badly for you, think! Both good and hard days belong to life, and both come from God’s hand.

## Not too righteous, not too wicked (Verses 15–22)

[15] I have seen all this in my days of vanity:
There is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness,
and there is a wicked person who lives long in his evil.
[16] Don’t be overly righteous,
and don’t make yourself overly wise.
Why should you destroy yourself?
[17] Don’t be too wicked, and don’t be a fool.
Why should you die before your time?
[18] It is good that you hold on to the one thing.
Yes, and don’t take your hand away from the other either,
for whoever fears God will come through it all.
[19] Wisdom makes the wise person stronger
than 10 rulers who are in a city.
[20] Surely there is not a righteous person on earth
who does good and does not sin.
[21] Also, don’t pay attention to every word that is spoken,
or you may hear your servant curse you,
[22] for your own heart often knows
that you too have cursed others.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The Preacher looks honestly: Sometimes good people die young, and evil people live long. That does not fit the simple rule “Whoever is good does well.”
> Verses 16–17: Unusual advice: Don’t be “overly righteous.” This probably means: Don’t be self-righteous and hard on yourself and others, as if you could earn everything by being religious. And of course, don’t be wicked either.
> Verse 18: Whoever fears God finds the right way between the extremes.
> Verse 20: “There is not a righteous person on earth who does good and does not sin.” Paul quotes this thought (Romans 3:10–12).
> Verses 21–22: Wise advice: Don’t listen to every bit of gossip about you. And remember: You too have already spoken badly about others.

## What I looked for and did not find (Verses 23–29)

[23] I have tested all this with wisdom.
I said, “I will be wise,”
but it was far from me.
[24] What exists is far away and very deep.
Who can find it out?
[25] I turned, and my heart tried to know and to search out,
and to seek wisdom and the meaning of things,
and to know that wickedness is stupidity,
and that foolishness is madness.
[26] I find more bitter than death the woman
whose heart is snares and traps,
and whose hands are chains.
Whoever pleases God will escape from her,
but the sinner will be caught by her.
[27] “This is what I have found,” says the Preacher,
“adding one thing to another to find an explanation,
[28] which my soul is still looking for, but I have not found.
I have found one man among 1,000,
but I have not found a woman among all those.
[29] This is the only thing I have found:
that God made people upright,
but they look for many schemes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–24: The Preacher admits: I wanted to become wise, but the truth is too deep for me.
> Verse 26: Here he warns against a seductive woman, as Proverbs does (Proverbs 5 and 7). It is about a certain kind of woman, not about all women.
> Verse 28: This verse sounds hostile to women and has often been misused that way. Many interpreters understand it like this: Among men too, the Preacher found almost no one who was really wise and upright, “one man among 1,000.” He describes his personal, limited experience, probably from a royal court with many women who were not free partners. It is no judgment of God about women.
> Other parts of the Bible praise many wise and good women, for example Ruth, Abigail, Deborah and the capable woman in Proverbs 31.
> Verse 29: The most important result: God created people, man and woman, good and upright. But people have thought up many excuses and wrong ways. This is true for everyone alike.
