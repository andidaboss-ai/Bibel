# Leviticus – Chapter 15
### Uncleanness from fluids of the body

---

## When a man has a discharge because of illness (Verses 1–12)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘When any man has a discharge from his body,
he is unclean because of his discharge.
A discharge is a fluid that flows out of the body.
[3] This is how his uncleanness is with his discharge:
Whether his body lets the discharge run, or his body holds it back,
it is his uncleanness.

[4] “‘Every bed that the man with the discharge lies on shall be unclean.
And everything he sits on shall be unclean.
[5] Whoever touches his bed shall wash his clothes
and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[6] Whoever sits on anything that the man with the discharge sat on
shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[7] “‘Whoever touches the body of the man with the discharge
shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[8] “‘If the man with the discharge spits on someone who is clean,
that person shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[9] “‘Every saddle that the man with the discharge rides on shall be unclean.
[10] Whoever touches anything that was under him shall be unclean until the evening.
Whoever carries those things shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[11] “‘If the man with the discharge touches someone without first rinsing his hands in water,
that person shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[12] “‘A clay pot that the man with the discharge touches shall be broken.
And every container made of wood shall be rinsed with water.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is about fluids that come from the sexual organs. This is a very personal subject. The Bible speaks about it openly.
> First it means a discharge in a man that comes from illness, for example from an infection.
> Many of these rules protect against infection: The sick person’s bed, saddle and dishes are cleaned. Whoever touches him washes himself. And the sick person should wash his hands before he touches others (verse 11). This is a very good rule of hygiene, thousands of years before people knew about bacteria.
> Important: “Unclean” does not mean “sinful.” It means: For a while, a person may not go into the holy tent.

## When the man is healed (Verses 13–15)

[13] “‘When the man with the discharge has become clean from his discharge,
he shall count seven days for his cleansing.
He shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in running water.
Then he will be clean.
[14] “‘On the eighth day he shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons,
come before the LORD to the door of the Tent of Meeting,
and give them to the priest.
[15] The priest shall bring them, the one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.
So the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD because of his discharge.

---

## Release of semen and sexual relations (Verses 16–18)

[16] “‘If a man has a release of semen,
he shall bathe his whole body in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
Semen is the fluid that comes out of a man’s penis.
[17] Every piece of clothing and every piece of leather that has semen on it
shall be washed with water, and it will be unclean until the evening.
[18] If a man sleeps with a woman and there is a release of semen,
they shall both bathe themselves in water, and they will be unclean until the evening.

> **What does this mean?**
> A release of semen is completely natural. Love between a man and a woman is good too. The Bible has a whole book that celebrates love: the Song of Songs.
> Still, afterward a person counts as unclean until the evening. This only means: A person should not go into the holy tent right afterward. Sexuality and worship should be kept apart. Among Israel’s neighbors, there were religions in which sex played a part in the temple. That was not supposed to happen in Israel.
> The uncleanness only lasts until the evening. You bathe, and then everything is fine again.

## A woman’s monthly bleeding (Verses 19–24)

[19] “‘If a woman has a discharge, and the discharge from her body is blood,
she shall be in her impurity for seven days.
Whoever touches her shall be unclean until the evening.
[20] “‘Everything that she lies on in her impurity shall be unclean.
And everything that she sits on shall be unclean.
[21] Whoever touches her bed shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[22] Whoever touches anything that she sits on
shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.
[23] If it is on the bed, or on anything she sits on, when he touches it,
he will be unclean until the evening.
[24] “‘If any man sleeps with her, and some of her monthly bleeding gets on him,
he will be unclean for seven days.
And every bed he lies on shall be unclean.

> **What does this mean?**
> The monthly bleeding, the period, is something completely natural. God created a woman’s body this way. It is not a sin and nothing dirty.
> In the Bible, blood stands for life. That is why a person counts as unclean for a while when blood flows out of the body.
> For the woman, this time could also be a time of rest, when she was free from some duties.
> Sadly, these rules were sometimes used later to look down on women. That is not what this text means. Here very similar rules apply to men and women.

## When a woman bleeds outside her normal time (Verses 25–30)

[25] “‘If a woman has a flow of blood for many days, outside the time of her period,
or if her bleeding lasts longer than her period,
then all the days of her unclean flow shall be as in the days of her period. She is unclean.
[26] Every bed that she lies on during all the days of her flow
shall be to her like the bed during her period.
And everything she sits on shall be unclean, like the uncleanness of her period.
[27] Whoever touches these things shall be unclean.
He shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and he will be unclean until the evening.

[28] “‘But if she has become clean from her flow,
she shall count seven days. After that she will be clean.
[29] On the eighth day she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons
and bring them to the priest, to the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[30] The priest shall bring the one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.
So the priest shall make atonement for her before the LORD because of her unclean flow.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is about an illness: bleeding that does not stop.
> The New Testament tells about a woman who suffered from bleeding like this for twelve years. The whole time she was “unclean” and shut out. Full of trust, she touched Jesus’ clothes and was healed. Jesus did not scold her for touching him. He said lovingly to her: My daughter, your faith has saved you. Go in peace (Mark 5:25–34).

---

## The purpose of these rules (Verses 31–33)

[31] “‘This is how you shall keep the people of Israel away from their uncleanness.
Otherwise they will die in their uncleanness when they make my tabernacle unclean, which is among them.’”
[32] This is the law for the man who has a discharge,
and for the man who has a release of semen that makes him unclean,
[33] and for the woman who has her period,
and for a man or a woman who has a discharge,
and for the man who sleeps with a woman who is unclean.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31 explains the reason for all these rules: God lives in the middle of the camp. His home is holy. That is why the people must take care to be clean.
> These rules applied above all as long as the holy tent and the temple existed. In Judaism, some of them are still kept today, for example the woman’s ritual bath (mikveh) after her period. Most Christians no longer keep these rules, because they believe that Jesus has made people completely clean.
