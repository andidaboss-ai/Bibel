# Leviticus – Chapter 20
### Punishments for serious wrongs

---

## Child sacrifice for Molech and calling up the dead (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Also tell the people of Israel,
‘Someone from the people of Israel,
or one of the foreigners who live in Israel,
may give one of his children to Molech.
Then he must be put to death.
The people of the land shall stone him with stones.
[3] And I myself will turn against that person
and cut him off from his people.
For he has given one of his children to Molech.
So he has made my holy place unclean and dishonored my holy name.
[4] But the people of the land may close their eyes to that person
when he gives one of his children to Molech,
and not put him to death.
[5] Then I will turn against that man and against his family.
I will cut him off from his people.
I will also cut off all who follow him
and are unfaithful to me with Molech, like a prostitute.

[6] “‘Someone may turn to people who call up the dead, and to fortune-tellers,
and be unfaithful to me with them, like a prostitute.
I will turn against that person
and cut him off from his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 18 many things were forbidden. In Chapter 20 the punishments for them are given. Many of these punishments are very harsh. Often it is the death penalty. That is hard for us to read today.
> At the very beginning is the worst crime: burning children as a sacrifice for the god Molech.
> Verse 4 is important: Whoever looks away shares the guilt. A community must not stay silent when violence is done to children.
> Whoever turns to foreign gods or fortune-tellers is compared to an unfaithful wife. God and his people are like a married couple.

---

## Be holy (Verses 7–8)

[7] “‘So make yourselves holy, and be holy, for I am the LORD your God.
[8] You shall keep my rules and do them.
I am the LORD, who makes you holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am the LORD, who makes you holy.” Holiness is not only something people achieve. God himself makes his people holy. People answer by living by God’s commandments.

## The punishments (Verses 9–21)

[9] “‘Everyone who curses his father or his mother must be put to death.
He has cursed his father or his mother.
His blood shall be on himself.

[10] “‘A man may commit adultery with another man’s wife,
with the wife of his neighbor.
Then the man and the woman who committed adultery must be put to death.

[11] “‘A man may sleep with his father’s wife.
Then he has dishonored his father.
Both of them must be put to death.
Their blood shall be on themselves.
[12] “‘If a man sleeps with his daughter-in-law, both of them must be put to death.
They have committed a perversion.
Their blood shall be on themselves.
[13] “‘If a man sleeps with a man the way one sleeps with a woman,
both of them have done something disgusting.
They must be put to death.
Their blood shall be on themselves.
[14] “‘If a man marries a woman and her mother, it is wicked.
They shall be burned with fire, both he and the two women,
so that there is no wickedness among you.
[15] “‘If a man sleeps with an animal, he must be put to death.
And you shall kill the animal too.
[16] “‘If a woman goes to an animal to have sex with it,
you shall kill the woman and the animal.
They must be put to death.
Their blood shall be on them.

[17] “‘A man may take his sister, his father’s daughter or his mother’s daughter.
He sees her naked, and she sees him naked.
That is a shameful thing.
They shall be cut off in front of the people of their nation.
He has had sex with his sister.
He shall carry his guilt.
[18] “‘A man may sleep with a woman during her monthly period and have sex with her.
Then he has uncovered the source of her blood,
and she has uncovered the source of her blood.
Both of them shall be cut off from their people.
[19] “‘You shall not have sex with your mother’s sister or your father’s sister.
For whoever does that has uncovered his close relative.
They shall carry their guilt.
[20] “‘If a man sleeps with his uncle’s wife,
he has dishonored his uncle.
They shall carry their sin. They shall die without children.
[21] “‘If a man takes his brother’s wife, it is something unclean.
He has dishonored his brother.
They shall be without children.

> **What does this mean?**
> “His blood shall be on himself” means: He himself is to blame for his death. No one else carries the guilt for it.
> These punishments are shocking to us today. They must be understood from their own time. Back then there were no prisons. The family was the foundation of the whole society. Whoever destroyed it put the whole people in danger.
> In later Judaism, the teachers set up very strict rules before anyone could be sentenced to death: for example, two eyewitnesses and a warning beforehand. So the death penalty was almost never carried out. A court that sentenced one person to death in 70 years was called a “murderous court.”
> The New Testament tells how a woman is caught in adultery and brought to Jesus. People want to stone her according to this law. Jesus says: “He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her.” One after another they go away. And Jesus says to her: “Neither do I condemn you. Go your way. From now on, sin no more” (John 8:1–11).
> For verse 13, the same is true as for Leviticus 18:22: Jews and Christians understand this passage differently today. But it must never justify hatred or violence against people. The large Jewish and Christian communities today reject carrying out such punishments.
> “Die without children” is not a punishment that people carry out. It means: God himself will judge this.

## God has set you apart from the nations (Verses 22–26)

[22] “‘So you shall keep all my rules and all my laws, and do them.
Then the land where I am bringing you to live will not vomit you out.
[23] You shall not live by the customs of the nation that I am driving out ahead of you.
For they did all these things, and that is why I came to hate them.
[24] But I have said to you,
“You shall take their land as your own.
I will give it to you to own, a land flowing with milk and honey.”
I am the LORD your God, who has set you apart from the nations.

[25] “‘So you shall tell the difference between clean and unclean animals,
and between unclean and clean birds.
Do not make yourselves disgusting by animals, by birds,
or by anything that swarms on the ground,
which I have set apart for you as unclean.
[26] You shall be holy to me, for I, the LORD, am holy.
And I have set you apart from the nations so that you belong to me.

> **What does this mean?**
> A beautiful play on words: Just as Israel tells the difference between clean and unclean animals, God has chosen Israel from all the nations and “set it apart.” Israel belongs to God.
> That does not mean that Israel is better than other nations. It means: Israel has a special task. It is to show what a life with God looks like.

---

## People who call up the dead (Verse 27)

[27] “‘A man or a woman who calls up the dead or is a fortune-teller must be put to death.
They shall be stoned with stones.
Their blood shall be on themselves.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This punishment is very harsh too. Sadly, in the course of history, passages like this were misused, for example in the witch hunts in Europe. There, many innocent people, mostly women, were tortured and killed. That was a grave injustice and a misuse of the Bible.
> The point of this passage is: Israel is not to rely on spirits and the dead, but only on the living God.
