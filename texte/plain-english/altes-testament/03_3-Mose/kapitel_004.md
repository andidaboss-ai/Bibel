# Leviticus – Chapter 4
### The sin offering

---

## When the high priest sins (Verses 1–12)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘If anyone sins without meaning to,
and does any of the things that the LORD has commanded not to be done –
[3] if the anointed priest sins and so brings guilt on the people,
then for the sin he has done, he shall bring the LORD a young bull without any fault as a sin offering.
[4] He shall bring the bull to the door of the Tent of Meeting, before the LORD.
He shall lay his hand on the head of the bull and kill the bull before the LORD.
[5] The anointed priest shall take some of the bull’s blood and bring it into the Tent of Meeting.
[6] The priest shall dip his finger in the blood
and sprinkle some of the blood seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil of the sanctuary.
[7] The priest shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar for sweet incense,
which stands before the LORD in the Tent of Meeting.
He shall pour out all the rest of the bull’s blood at the bottom of the altar for burnt offerings,
which stands at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

[8] He shall take all the fat from the bull of the sin offering:
the fat that covers the inner parts, and all the fat that is on the inner parts,
[9] the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is by the lower back,
and the covering on the liver. He shall take that away together with the kidneys,
[10] just as it is taken away from the bull of the sacrifice of peace offerings.
The priest shall burn them on the altar for burnt offerings.
[11] But the bull’s skin and all its meat, with its head and its legs, its inner parts and its dung –
[12] that is, all the rest of the bull –
he shall carry outside the camp, to a clean place where the ashes are poured out.
There he shall burn it on wood with fire.
It shall be burned where the ashes are poured out.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: The anointed priest is the priest on whose head holy oil has been poured.
> Verse 7: The horns are the raised corners of the altar.
> Now it is about the “sin offering.” It is for sins that someone did without meaning to: by accident, because he did not know, or because he was not careful.
> Such mistakes count too. They harm the relationship with God and with other people. But God gives a way for them to be forgiven.
> The chapter goes step by step: What does the high priest do? What does the whole people do? What does a leader do? What does an ordinary person do?
> The “anointed priest” is the high priest. If he makes a mistake, it affects the whole people. For he stands before God for the people. That is why he must bring a big offering: a young bull.
> The blood is even brought into the holy tent, right up to the veil in front of the Most Holy Place. The more important the person, the heavier his guilt.
> Seven is the number of completeness. The atonement is meant to be whole and complete.
> No one may eat the meat here. It is burned outside the camp. The high priest may not eat from his own sin offering.

## When the whole people sins (Verses 13–21)

[13] “‘If the whole community of Israel sins, and the assembly does not notice it,
and they do any of the things that the LORD has commanded not to be done, and so they become guilty,
[14] and then the sin that they have done becomes known,
the assembly shall bring a young bull as a sin offering.
They shall bring it in front of the Tent of Meeting.
[15] The elders of the community shall lay their hands on the head of the bull before the LORD.
And the bull shall be killed before the LORD.
[16] The anointed priest shall bring some of the bull’s blood into the Tent of Meeting.
[17] The priest shall dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle it seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil.
[18] He shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar that stands before the LORD in the Tent of Meeting.
He shall pour out all the rest of the blood at the bottom of the altar for burnt offerings, which stands at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[19] He shall take all its fat from it and burn it on the altar.
[20] This is what he shall do with the bull.
As he did with the bull of the sin offering, so he shall do with this one.
So the priest shall make atonement for them, and they will be forgiven.
[21] He shall carry the bull outside the camp
and burn it, just as he burned the first bull.
It is the sin offering for the assembly.

> **What does this mean?**
> A whole people can also become guilty together without noticing it. For example, when everyone takes part in a wrong, or when everyone looks away.
> The elders lay their hands on the animal in place of everyone.
> “They will be forgiven” – that is the goal of the offering. God wants to forgive.

## When a leader sins (Verses 22–26)

[22] “‘When a ruler sins,
and without meaning to does any of the things that the LORD his God has commanded not to be done, and so he becomes guilty,
[23] and then the sin that he has done is made known to him,
he shall bring a male goat as his offering, a male animal without any fault.
[24] He shall lay his hand on the head of the goat
and kill it in the place where the burnt offering is killed before the LORD.
It is a sin offering.
[25] The priest shall take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger
and put it on the horns of the altar for burnt offerings.
He shall pour out the rest of its blood at the bottom of the altar for burnt offerings.
[26] He shall burn all its fat on the altar, like the fat of the sacrifice of peace offerings.
So the priest shall make atonement for him because of his sin, and he will be forgiven.

> **What does this mean?**
> A ruler is, for example, the head of a tribe. He brings a male goat, that is, a little less than the high priest.
> Here the blood is only put on the altar outside. It is not sprinkled inside the holy tent.

---

## When an ordinary person sins (Verses 27–35)

[27] “‘If anyone of the ordinary people sins without meaning to,
by doing any of the things that the LORD has commanded not to be done, and so he becomes guilty,
[28] and then the sin that he has done is made known to him,
he shall bring a goat as his offering for the sin he has done, a female animal without any fault.
[29] He shall lay his hand on the head of the sin offering
and kill the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering.
[30] The priest shall take some of its blood with his finger
and put it on the horns of the altar for burnt offerings.
He shall pour out all the rest of its blood at the bottom of the altar.
[31] He shall take away all its fat, just as the fat is taken away from the sacrifice of peace offerings.
The priest shall burn it on the altar as a pleasant smell to the LORD.
So the priest shall make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.

[32] “‘If he brings a lamb as his offering for a sin offering,
he shall bring a female animal without any fault.
[33] He shall lay his hand on the head of the sin offering
and kill it as a sin offering in the place where the burnt offering is killed.
[34] The priest shall take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger
and put it on the horns of the altar for burnt offerings.
He shall pour out all the rest of its blood at the bottom of the altar.
[35] He shall take away all its fat, just as the fat of the lamb is taken away from the sacrifice of peace offerings.
The priest shall burn them on the altar, on top of the LORD’s offerings made by fire.
So the priest shall make atonement for him because of the sin he has done, and he will be forgiven.

> **What does this mean?**
> An ordinary person can also be forgiven. He brings a goat or a lamb.
> Four times this chapter says: “and he will be forgiven” or “and they will be forgiven.” Whether high priest, the whole people, a leader or an ordinary person – there is forgiveness for everyone.
> Important: These offerings are for sins done “without meaning to.” For sins that someone does knowingly and on purpose, other rules apply (Numbers 15:30). Later the prophets say clearly: God does not want only offerings. He wants an honest heart that is sorry (Psalm 51:16–17; Hosea 6:6).
