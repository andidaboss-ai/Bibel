# Leviticus – Chapter 11
### Clean and unclean animals

---

## Land animals (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said to them,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘These are the living things that you may eat, of all the animals on the earth:
[3] Any animal that has a split hoof, divided all the way through, and that chews the cud, you may eat.
To chew the cud means: The animal brings its food back up from its stomach and chews it again.

[4] “‘But of those that chew the cud or have a split hoof, you shall not eat these:
the camel, because it chews the cud but does not have a split hoof. It is unclean to you.
[5] The hyrax, because it chews the cud but does not have a split hoof. It is unclean to you.
[6] The hare, because it chews the cud but does not have a split hoof. It is unclean to you.
[7] The pig, because it has a split hoof, divided all the way through, but it does not chew the cud. It is unclean to you.
[8] You shall not eat their meat, and you shall not touch their dead bodies.
They are unclean to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a big part of the book about “clean” and “unclean” begins (Chapters 11–15). First it is about food.
> “Unclean” here does not mean dirty, disgusting or evil. It means: The Israelites should not eat these animals. All animals are created by God and are good (Genesis 1:31).
> Land animals are allowed if they have two marks: a split hoof and chewing the cud. These include cattle, sheep, goats and deer.
> A hyrax is a small animal that lives among rocks. It is about the size of a rabbit. The hyrax and the hare do not really chew the cud. But when they eat, they move their mouths as if they were chewing the cud. The Bible describes them the way people saw them back then.
> Why these rules? Jews and Christians have thought about this a lot. Some say: for reasons of health. Others say: They are signs so that Israel is different from other peoples. The most important reason is at the end of the chapter: “Be holy, because I am holy.”
> To this day, many Jews eat “kosher,” that is, by these rules. Muslims do not eat pork either. Most Christians do not keep these food rules. Jesus said that it is not food that makes a person unclean, but what comes out of his heart (Mark 7:18–23). Peter also had a vision about this (Acts 10).

## Animals in the water (Verses 9–12)

[9] “‘Of all the animals in the water, you may eat these:
Anything in the water that has fins and scales, in the seas and in the rivers, you may eat.
[10] But anything that does not have fins and scales, in the seas and in the rivers,
anything that moves in the water, and all the living creatures in the water –
they are something disgusting to you.
[11] They shall be disgusting to you.
You shall not eat their meat.
And you shall find their dead bodies disgusting.
[12] Anything in the water that has no fins and no scales is something disgusting to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Fish with fins and scales are allowed, for example carp, salmon or herring. Not allowed are, for example, eels, mussels, crabs and squid.

## Birds (Verses 13–19)

[13] “‘You shall find these birds disgusting.
They shall not be eaten, because they are something disgusting:
the eagle, the vulture, the black vulture,
[14] the red kite, every kind of black kite,
[15] every kind of raven,
[16] the horned owl, the screech owl, the gull, every kind of hawk,
[17] the little owl, the cormorant, the great owl,
[18] the white owl, the desert owl, the osprey,
[19] the stork, every kind of heron, the hoopoe and the bat.

> **What does this mean?**
> Today we no longer know for sure which birds are meant exactly. The old Hebrew names cannot always be translated exactly. That is why different Bibles have slightly different names.
> It stands out that many of these birds are birds of prey or eat dead animals. They have to do with death and blood.
> The bat is not a bird, of course. But back then, everything that flies was counted together.

## Insects (Verses 20–23)

[20] “‘All flying insects that walk on all fours are something disgusting to you.
[21] But you may eat these:
of all the winged creeping animals that walk on all fours,
those that have long jointed legs for hopping on the ground.
[22] Of these you may eat:
every kind of locust, every kind of katydid,
every kind of cricket and every kind of grasshopper.
[23] But all other winged creeping animals that have four feet are something disgusting to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Walk on all fours” is a simple description. Insects actually have six legs. With grasshoppers, you see four legs for walking and two big legs for jumping.
> In the Middle East, locusts were normal food, especially for poor people. John the Baptist also ate locusts and wild honey (Mark 1:6).

## Whoever touches dead animals (Verses 24–28)

[24] “‘You will become unclean by these:
Whoever touches their dead body shall be unclean until the evening.
[25] Whoever carries any part of their dead body shall wash his clothes and be unclean until the evening.

[26] “‘Every animal that has a split hoof that is not divided all the way through,
or that does not chew the cud, is unclean to you.
Everyone who touches them shall be unclean.
[27] All animals that walk on paws, of all the animals that walk on all fours, are unclean to you.
Whoever touches their dead body shall be unclean until the evening.
[28] Whoever carries their dead body shall wash his clothes and be unclean until the evening.
They are unclean to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever touches a dead unclean animal becomes “unclean” himself. That means: For a while, he may not go into the holy tent or touch holy things.
> But this lasts only “until the evening.” Then, by the Jewish way of counting, a new day begins. Whoever washes his clothes is clean again afterward.
> Animals that “walk on paws” are, for example, dogs, cats, lions and bears.

## Small animals that creep on the ground (Verses 29–38)

[29] “‘These are unclean to you among the small animals that creep on the ground:
the weasel, the rat, every kind of large lizard,
[30] the gecko, the monitor lizard, the wall lizard, the skink and the chameleon.
[31] These are unclean to you among all the creeping animals.
Whoever touches them when they are dead shall be unclean until the evening.

[32] Anything that one of them falls on when it is dead shall be unclean:
any container made of wood, any clothing, any leather, any sack,
any object that is used for work.
It must be put into water.
It shall be unclean until the evening. Then it will be clean.
[33] Every clay pot that one of them falls into, and everything in it, shall be unclean.
You shall break it.
[34] All food that may be eaten and that is soaked in water shall be unclean.
And every drink in such a pot shall be unclean.
[35] Everything that a part of their dead body falls on shall be unclean.
An oven or a stove for pots shall be broken in pieces.
They are unclean and shall be unclean to you.
[36] But a spring or a cistern where water is gathered shall stay clean.
A cistern is a large hole in the ground for storing water.
But whatever touches their dead body shall be unclean.
[37] If a part of their dead body falls on seed that is to be planted, it is clean.
[38] But if water has been put on the seed
and a part of their dead body falls on it,
it is unclean to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> To us, these rules seem like rules for hygiene. Mice, rats and lizards often came into houses. If such an animal fell dead into a pot, everything had to be cleaned or thrown away.
> Interesting: Flowing water in a spring or a cistern stays clean. And dry seed does too. But wet seed, or food made with water, becomes unclean. People thought that wetness passes on uncleanness.
> Simple clay pots could not be cleaned well. That is why they had to be broken.

---

## When an allowed animal dies by itself (Verses 39–40)

[39] “‘If one of the animals that you may eat dies,
whoever touches its dead body shall be unclean until the evening.
[40] Whoever eats from its dead body shall wash his clothes and be unclean until the evening.
Whoever carries its dead body shall also wash his clothes and be unclean until the evening.

## You shall be holy (Verses 41–47)

[41] “‘Every small animal that creeps on the ground is something disgusting.
It shall not be eaten.
[42] Whatever crawls on its belly,
whatever walks on all fours,
and whatever has many feet,
all the small animals that creep on the ground –
you shall not eat them.
For they are something disgusting.
[43] Do not make yourselves disgusting with any creeping animal.
Do not make yourselves unclean with them, so that you are not made impure by them.
[44] For I am the LORD your God.
So make yourselves holy, and be holy, because I am holy.
Do not make yourselves unclean with any kind of creeping animal that moves on the ground.
[45] For I am the LORD who brought you up out of the land of Egypt, to be your God.
So you shall be holy, because I am holy.

[46] “‘This is the law about the land animals, the birds,
every living creature that moves in the water,
and every creature that creeps on the ground,
[47] so that you can tell the difference between the unclean and the clean,
between the animals that may be eaten and the animals that may not be eaten.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is the most important reason for all these rules: “Be holy, because I am holy.” This sentence appears here for the first time. It is repeated often in Leviticus.
> “Holy” means: belonging to God, set apart for God, being different. Even when they eat, that is, every day, Israel should remember: We belong to God.
> This sentence is also quoted in the New Testament. Peter writes: “So you also are to be holy in all your way of life. For it is written, ‘You shall be holy, for I am holy’” (1 Peter 1:15–16).
