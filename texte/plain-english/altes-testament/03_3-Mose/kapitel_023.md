# Leviticus – Chapter 23
### The feasts of the LORD

---

## The Sabbath (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘These are the fixed feasts of the LORD, which you shall announce as holy gatherings.
These are my feasts.

[3] “‘Work shall be done for six days.
But the seventh day is a Sabbath, a day of complete rest, a holy gathering.
You shall not do any kind of work.
It is a Sabbath for the LORD, everywhere you live.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is like a calendar of feasts. It names all the feasts that Israel is to celebrate every year.
> At the beginning is the Sabbath, the feast of every week. It is the foundation of all the other feasts.
> “Holy gathering” means: The people come together to celebrate and worship God.
> The feasts are “feasts of the LORD.” They belong to God. They are times when God and his people meet.

---

## Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread (Verses 4–8)

[4] “‘These are the fixed feasts of the LORD, the holy gatherings,
which you shall announce at their fixed times:
[5] In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, in the evening, is the LORD’s Passover.
[6] On the fifteenth day of the same month is the Feast of Unleavened Bread for the LORD.
For seven days you shall eat bread without yeast.
[7] On the first day you shall have a holy gathering.
You shall do no ordinary work.
[8] For seven days you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
On the seventh day is a holy gathering.
You shall do no ordinary work.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Unleavened bread is bread made without yeast.
> Passover is a reminder of the night when Israel was set free from Egypt (Exodus 12). The first month is in the spring, in March or April.
> To this day, Jews celebrate the Passover feast, in Hebrew “Pesach,” with a special evening meal, the Seder.
> Jesus celebrated the Passover meal with his disciples before he died. That became the Lord’s Supper. To this day, Easter falls around the time of Passover.

---

## The first bundle of the harvest (Verses 9–14)

[9] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[10] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘You will come into the land that I am giving you, and you will bring in its harvest.
Then you shall bring the first bundle of grain from your harvest to the priest.
[11] He shall wave the bundle back and forth before the LORD, so that you are accepted.
The priest shall wave it on the day after the Sabbath.
[12] On the day you wave the bundle,
you shall offer a one-year-old male lamb without defect as a burnt offering to the LORD.
[13] The meal offering with it shall be about 4½ quarts of fine flour mixed with oil.
It is an offering by fire to the LORD, a pleasant smell.
And the drink offering with it shall be about 1 quart of wine.
[14] You shall not eat bread, roasted grain, or fresh grain
until this very day, until you have brought the offering for your God.
This is a rule forever, for all your generations, everywhere you live.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the measures are given like this: two tenths of an ephah of flour and a fourth of a hin of wine. An ephah is about 6 gallons. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> A “bundle” of grain (in older English: a sheaf) is a bundle of cut stalks of grain. The very first bundle of the barley harvest belongs to God. Only after that may people eat from the new grain themselves. That is how they say thank you to God for the whole harvest.
> Paul calls Jesus “the first fruit of those who are asleep” (1 Corinthians 15:20). Asleep means here: those who have died. Christians see a connection here: Jesus rose from the dead on the day after the Sabbath, exactly on the day of the first bundle.

---

## The Feast of Weeks: fifty days later (Verses 15–22)

[15] “‘You shall count from the day after the Sabbath,
from the day you brought the bundle as a wave offering:
There shall be seven full weeks.
[16] You shall count fifty days, up to the day after the seventh Sabbath.
Then you shall bring a new meal offering to the LORD.
[17] From your homes you shall bring two loaves of bread as a wave offering.
They shall be made from about 4½ quarts of fine flour.
They shall be baked with yeast, as first fruits for the LORD.
[18] With the bread you shall offer seven one-year-old lambs without defect,
one young bull, and two rams.
They shall be a burnt offering to the LORD, with their meal offering and their drink offerings.
It is an offering by fire, a pleasant smell for the LORD.
[19] You shall offer one male goat as a sin offering,
and two one-year-old male lambs as a peace offering.
[20] The priest shall wave them back and forth before the LORD as a wave offering, with the bread of the first fruits,
together with the two lambs.
They shall be holy to the LORD and belong to the priest.
[21] On that same day you shall announce that there is a holy gathering for you.
You shall do no ordinary work.
This is a rule forever, everywhere you live, for all your generations.

[22] “‘When you bring in the harvest of your land,
you shall not harvest your field all the way to the corners.
And you shall not pick up the grain that is left lying after the harvest.
You shall leave it for the poor and for the foreigner.
I am the LORD your God.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Seven weeks after the first bundle, the wheat harvest is over. Then people celebrate the Feast of Weeks, in Hebrew “Shavuot.” At this feast, Jews also remember that God gave the Torah at Mount Sinai.
> “Fiftieth” in Greek is “pentekoste.” That is where the word “Pentecost” comes from. At this feast, the Holy Spirit came on the disciples of Jesus (Acts 2).
> In the middle of the calendar of feasts is the rule for the poor again (as in Leviticus 19:9–10). Whoever celebrates a feast is not to forget the poor.

---

## The Feast of Trumpets (Verses 23–25)

[23] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[24] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, you shall have a day of rest.
It is a day to remember, with the blowing of trumpets, a holy gathering.
[25] You shall do no ordinary work.
And you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The seventh month is in the fall. On the first day of this month, a horn is blown. This is the “shofar,” a horn from a ram.
> Today Jews celebrate their New Year on this day, “Rosh Hashanah.” It is the beginning of ten days of reflection and turning back to God, up to the Day of Atonement.

---

## The Day of Atonement (Verses 26–32)

[26] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[27] “But the tenth day of this seventh month is the Day of Atonement.
It shall be a holy gathering for you.
You shall humble yourselves and bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
[28] You shall not do any kind of work on that day.
For it is the Day of Atonement, when atonement is made for you before the LORD your God.
[29] For whoever does not humble himself on that day shall be cut off from his people.
[30] And whoever does any kind of work on that day, I will destroy that person from among his people.
[31] You shall not do any kind of work.
This is a rule forever, for all your generations, everywhere you live.
[32] It shall be a Sabbath for you, a day of complete rest.
And you shall humble yourselves.
You shall keep your Sabbath from the evening of the ninth day of the month, from evening to evening.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: To humble yourself here means: to go without food and comforts.
> The Day of Atonement, “Yom Kippur,” was already described in detail in Chapter 16.
> “From evening to evening” – in the Jewish calendar, every day begins in the evening, when the sun goes down. It is already like this in the story of creation: “There was evening, and there was morning” (Genesis 1:5).

---

## The Feast of Booths (Verses 33–44)

[33] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[34] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘The fifteenth day of this seventh month is the Feast of Booths, for seven days, for the LORD.
[35] On the first day there shall be a holy gathering.
You shall do no ordinary work.
[36] For seven days you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
On the eighth day you shall have a holy gathering.
You shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
It is a solemn gathering. You shall do no ordinary work.

[37] “‘These are the fixed feasts of the LORD, which you shall announce as holy gatherings,
to bring offerings by fire to the LORD:
burnt offerings, meal offerings, sacrifices, and drink offerings, each on its own day.
[38] These are in addition to the Sabbaths of the LORD,
and in addition to your gifts, to all your promises,
and to all your free-will gifts that you give to the LORD.

[39] “‘So on the fifteenth day of the seventh month,
when you have brought in the crops of the land,
you shall celebrate the feast of the LORD for seven days.
The first day shall be a day of rest, and the eighth day shall be a day of rest.
[40] On the first day you shall take fruit from splendid trees,
palm branches, branches from leafy trees, and willows from the brook.
And you shall be joyful before the LORD your God for seven days.
[41] You shall celebrate it as a feast for the LORD, seven days in the year.
This is a rule forever, for all your generations.
You shall celebrate it in the seventh month.
[42] You shall live in huts for seven days.
Everyone born in Israel shall live in huts.
[43] That way your descendants will know
that I had the people of Israel live in huts when I brought them out of the land of Egypt.
I am the LORD your God.’”
[44] So Moses told the people of Israel about the fixed feasts of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 34: Booths are simple huts made of branches.
> The Feast of Booths, in Hebrew “Sukkot,” is the great harvest festival in the fall. All the crops have been brought in.
> For seven days, people live in simple huts made of branches. That way they remember the time in the wilderness, when Israel had no solid houses and had to trust God completely.
> “You shall be joyful” – this is even a commandment! God wants his people to celebrate with joy.
> To this day, Jews build huts in the garden or on the balcony for this feast. They take a festive bundle made of a palm branch, myrtle, willow, and a citrus fruit, the etrog.
> Many Christians celebrate a harvest thanksgiving in the fall. That is also a reminder that everything we have is a gift from God.
