# Leviticus – Chapter 24
### The light, the bread, and a man who curses God

---

## The oil for the lampstand (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Command the people of Israel to bring you pure olive oil,
made from crushed olives, for the light,
so that a lamp burns all the time.
[3] Aaron shall keep it burning in the Tent of Meeting,
outside the curtain in front of the Testimony, from evening to morning, before the LORD, all the time.
This is a rule forever, for all your generations.
[4] He shall keep the lamps on the lampstand of pure gold in order before the LORD, all the time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: The Testimony is the tablets with God’s covenant written on them.
> This was already commanded in Exodus 27:20–21. Now it is repeated.
> The light in the holy place is never to go out. It is a sign: God is always there, even at night. And his people are always to think of him.

---

## The twelve loaves of bread (Verses 5–9)

[5] “Take fine flour and bake twelve loaves from it.
Each loaf shall be made from about 4½ quarts of flour.
[6] Set them in two rows, six in each row, on the table of pure gold before the LORD.
[7] Put pure frankincense on each row.
It shall be a reminder part for the bread, an offering by fire to the LORD.
[8] Every Sabbath day he shall set the bread in order before the LORD, all the time.
It is a covenant forever on behalf of the people of Israel.
[9] The bread shall belong to Aaron and his sons.
They shall eat it in a holy place.
For it is most holy for him, from the LORD’s offerings by fire, as a rule forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Frankincense is a sweet-smelling resin that is burned.
> The Bible says: two tenths of an ephah for each loaf. An ephah is about 6 gallons. These were large loaves.
> This bread is called the “showbread.” There are twelve loaves, for the twelve tribes of Israel. They lie before God all the time. That way the whole people is always present before God.
> Every week on the Sabbath, new bread is put there. The priests eat the old bread.
> In the New Testament, Jesus reminds people that David once ate from this holy bread when he was in great need (Mark 2:25–26; 1 Samuel 21). With this Jesus says: A person’s need matters more to God than a rule.

---

## A man curses God (Verses 10–12)

[10] There was a son of an Israelite woman. His father was an Egyptian.
He went out among the people of Israel.
And the son of the Israelite woman got into a fight with a man of Israel in the camp.
[11] The son of the Israelite woman spoke against the Name and cursed.
So they brought him to Moses.
His mother’s name was Shelomith. She was the daughter of Dibri, from the tribe of Dan.
[12] They kept him under guard until the LORD would make his will clear to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a story interrupts the many laws. It is one of the few stories in the book of Leviticus.
> “Speaking against the Name” means: insulting, cursing, or mocking the name of God. The name of God here is “the LORD.” In our English source text it is “Yahweh,” in Hebrew YHWH.
> Moses does not decide by himself. He waits until God says what is to happen. In serious cases, people are not to judge too quickly.

---

## God’s judgment and the same law for everyone (Verses 13–22)

[13] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[14] “Bring the man who cursed outside the camp.
All who heard him shall lay their hands on his head.
And the whole community shall stone him.
[15] And tell the people of Israel,
‘Whoever curses his God shall carry his sin.
[16] Whoever speaks against the name of the LORD must be put to death.
The whole community shall stone him.
The foreigner as well as the one born in Israel:
whoever speaks against the Name must be put to death.

[17] “‘Whoever strikes a person and kills him must be put to death.
[18] Whoever strikes an animal and kills it shall pay for it: a life for a life.
[19] If someone injures his neighbor,
it shall be done to him as he has done:
[20] a broken bone for a broken bone, an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth.
It shall be done to him just as he has injured someone.
[21] Whoever kills an animal shall pay for it.
And whoever kills a person shall be put to death.
[22] You shall have one and the same law, for the foreigner as well as for the one born in Israel.
For I am the LORD your God.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The punishment for cursing God is very harsh. Back then, the name of God was seen as the holiest thing of all. Whoever cursed it attacked the whole people and its covenant with God.
> Out of deep respect, Jews still do not say the name YHWH aloud today. Instead they say “Adonai” (the Lord) or “HaShem” (the Name). That is why many Bibles, this one too, have “the LORD” in capital letters.
> “An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth” sounds cruel. But back then it was a step forward: The punishment must not be greater than the harm. It was about limiting revenge. In Judaism, it was understood very early as payment in money for the harm: Whoever injures an eye pays as much as an eye is worth.
> Jesus went even further. He said: “You have heard that it was said: ‘An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth.’ But I tell you: Do not resist the person who does evil to you” and “Love your enemies” (Matthew 5:38–44).
> Verse 22 is very important: The same law applies to foreigners and to those born in the land. No one is to be treated unfairly in court because he is a foreigner.

---

## The punishment is carried out (Verse 23)

[23] Moses spoke to the people of Israel.
And they brought the man who had cursed outside the camp and stoned him with stones.
The people of Israel did as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a sad ending. The Bible tells it without making it look better than it is.
> Many people have asked: Why does the Bible tell something like this? It shows how seriously people took God’s holiness back then. For us today it is clear: No one may kill people for cursing God. The large Jewish and Christian communities also reject this. Sadly, there are still countries today where people are persecuted for blasphemy. That goes against the dignity that every person has as an image of God.
