# Leviticus – Chapter 12
### The cleansing of a mother after giving birth

---

## After the birth of a son or a daughter (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘If a woman becomes pregnant and gives birth to a son,
she shall be unclean for seven days.
She shall be unclean as in the days of her monthly period.
[3] On the eighth day, the flesh of the boy’s foreskin shall be circumcised.
To circumcise means: to cut off the foreskin of the penis.
[4] Then she shall stay in the blood of her cleansing for 33 more days.
She shall not touch anything holy, and she shall not come into the holy place,
until the days of her cleansing are over.
[5] But if she gives birth to a daughter,
she shall be unclean for two weeks, as in her period.
And then she shall stay in the blood of her cleansing for 66 more days.

> **What does this mean?**
> After giving birth, a woman keeps bleeding for a few weeks. In the Bible, blood stands for life. That is why a person is seen as “unclean” when blood flows out of the body.
> Important: “Unclean” does not mean dirty or sinful. Giving birth is not a sin. Children are a blessing from God (Psalm 127:3). It only means: For a while, the woman should not go into the holy tent.
> This was also a protection. The mother had time to recover and did not have to do any religious duties.
> Circumcision on the eighth day is the sign of the covenant that God made with Abraham (Genesis 17:10–12). Jesus was also circumcised on the eighth day (Luke 2:21).
> The Bible does not say why the time after the birth of a daughter is twice as long. There are different guesses about this. Some Jewish teachers think: Because the girl herself will one day give life, her birth is honored in a special way. We do not know for sure. But it does not mean that a girl is worth less.

---

## The offering after the cleansing (Verses 6–8)

[6] “‘When the days of her cleansing for a son or for a daughter are over,
she shall bring to the priest at the door of the Tent of Meeting
a lamb one year old as a burnt offering,
and a young pigeon or a turtledove as a sin offering.
[7] He shall bring it before the LORD and make atonement for her.
Then she will be clean from the flow of her blood.

“‘This is the law for a woman who gives birth, whether to a boy or to a girl.
[8] If she cannot afford a lamb,
she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons,
one as a burnt offering and the other as a sin offering.
So the priest shall make atonement for her, and she will be clean.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The “sin offering” here does not mean that the woman has sinned. It is an offering for cleansing. After it, she may come into the holy tent again and take part in worship.
> The burnt offering is also a thank-you to God for the child.
> Mary brought exactly this offering after Jesus was born. She and Joseph were poor. That is why they brought two doves (Luke 2:22–24).
