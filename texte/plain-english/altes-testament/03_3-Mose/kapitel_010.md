# Leviticus – Chapter 10
### Nadab and Abihu – strange fire

---

## The death of Nadab and Abihu (Verses 1–7)

[1] Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, each took his censer.
A censer is a small pan for burning incense.
They put fire in it and laid incense on it.
So they brought strange fire before the LORD, which he had not commanded them.
[2] Then fire came out from before the LORD and burned them up.
And they died before the LORD.

[3] Then Moses said to Aaron,
“This is what the LORD spoke about when he said,
‘I will show myself holy among those who come near me.
And before all the people I will be honored.’”
And Aaron was silent.

[4] Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, the sons of Uzziel, Aaron’s uncle.
He said to them,
“Come here. Carry your brothers away from the holy place, out of the camp.”
[5] So they came and carried them in their tunics out of the camp, as Moses had said.

[6] Moses said to Aaron and to his sons Eleazar and Ithamar,
“Do not let the hair of your heads hang loose, and do not tear your clothes.
Otherwise you will die, and he will be angry with the whole community.
But your brothers, the whole house of Israel, shall weep over the burning that the LORD has started.
[7] Do not go out from the door of the Tent of Meeting, or you will die.
For the LORD’s anointing oil is on you.”
And they did what Moses said.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most shocking stories in the Bible. On the day of great joy, right after God’s fire accepted the offering (Chapter 9), two sons of Aaron die by this fire.
> The Bible does not say very clearly what exactly their mistake was. They brought “strange fire,” “which he had not commanded them.” Maybe they took fire that did not come from the altar. Maybe they went into the holy place on their own and at the wrong time. Some Jewish teachers think that they were drunk, because right after this comes the rule against alcohol for priests (verse 9).
> The message is serious: God is holy. You cannot serve him just the way you like. The closer you are to God, the more responsibility you carry.
> “And Aaron was silent” – a father loses two sons in one day and says nothing. This silence shows deep pain, and maybe also that he accepts God’s will.
> As high priest, Aaron may not show the usual signs of mourning: letting his hair hang loose and tearing his clothes. He may not stop his service. Instead, the whole people is to mourn for him.
> For us today, this story is hard to understand. It remains a warning not to come to God carelessly.

## No wine in the holy place (Verses 8–11)

[8] The LORD spoke to Aaron. He said,
[9] “You and your sons may not drink wine or strong drink
when you go into the Tent of Meeting.
Otherwise you will die.
This is a rule forever, for all your generations.
[10] You must tell the difference between what is holy and what is common,
and between what is unclean and what is clean.
[11] And you must teach the people of Israel all the rules
that the LORD has spoken to them through Moses.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the only time in Leviticus that God speaks directly to Aaron alone.
> Whoever serves God needs a clear head. He must be able to tell what is holy and what is not.
> The priests had two tasks: to serve at the holy tent and to teach the people. They were also the teachers of the people.

## What the priests shall eat (Verses 12–15)

[12] Moses spoke to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar, his sons who were left. He said,
“Take the grain offering that is left over from the LORD’s offerings made by fire,
and eat it without yeast beside the altar.
For it is most holy.
[13] You shall eat it in a holy place.
For it is your share and your sons’ share of the LORD’s offerings made by fire.
That is what I have been commanded.
[14] The breast that is waved and the thigh that is lifted up you shall eat in a clean place,
you and your sons and your daughters with you.
For they are given as your share and your sons’ share,
from the sacrifices of the peace offerings of the people of Israel.
[15] They shall bring the thigh that is lifted up and the breast that is waved
together with the fat of the offerings made by fire,
to wave it before the LORD as a wave offering.
It shall belong to you and to your sons with you, as a share forever,
as the LORD has commanded.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Even in the middle of the grief, the service goes on. The other priests are to eat what is theirs.
> The family of the priest may also eat the breast and the thigh, the daughters too. The other part, the “most holy” part, may only be eaten by the priests themselves in the holy place.

## Moses’ anger and Aaron’s answer (Verses 16–20)

[16] Moses asked carefully about the goat of the sin offering.
And look: It had been burned.
Then he was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, the sons of Aaron who were left. He said,
[17] “Why have you not eaten the sin offering in the holy place?
It is most holy, after all.
He has given it to you so that you carry the guilt of the community
and make atonement for them before the LORD.
[18] Look: Its blood was not brought into the inner part of the holy place.
You certainly should have eaten it in the holy place, as I commanded.”
[19] Aaron answered Moses,
“Look: Today they brought their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD.
And things like these have happened to me.
If I had eaten the sin offering today,
would the LORD have been pleased with that?”
[20] When Moses heard that, he was satisfied.

> **What does this mean?**
> According to the rules in Leviticus 6:26, the priests should have eaten the meat of the sin offering. Instead, they burned it. Moses is angry. After the death of Nadab and Abihu, he is afraid that another mistake will happen.
> Aaron explains: How could I eat the holy offering meal on a day like this, full of grief? Would God have been pleased with that?
> And Moses understands him. He agrees with Aaron.
> This small story shows: God’s rules are important. But they should not be followed without a heart. God sees what is going on inside a person. Grief and honesty have their place before him.
