# Leviticus – Chapter 16
### The great Day of Atonement – the scapegoat

---

## Not at just any time into the Most Holy Place (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron,
who came near before the LORD and died.
[2] The LORD said to Moses,
“Tell your brother Aaron that he may not come at just any time
into the Most Holy Place behind the curtain,
in front of the mercy seat that is on the ark.
Otherwise he will die.
For I will appear in the cloud above the mercy seat.
The ark is the holy chest with the Ten Commandments in it. The mercy seat is the golden lid on top of the ark.

[3] “This is how Aaron shall come into the holy place:
with a young bull as a sin offering and a ram as a burnt offering.
[4] He shall put on the holy linen tunic.
He shall have the linen underpants on his body,
tie on the linen sash
and put on the linen turban.
These are the holy clothes.
He shall bathe his body in water and then put them on.
[5] From the community of the children of Israel he shall take two male goats as a sin offering,
and one ram as a burnt offering.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is the heart of Leviticus. It describes the great Day of Atonement, in Hebrew “Yom Kippur.” To this day it is the holiest day of the Jewish year.
> It begins by remembering Nadab and Abihu (Chapter 10). You cannot simply come close to God however you like. But now God shows a way to do it.
> On this day the high priest does not wear his splendid clothes of gold and precious stones. He wears simple white linen clothes. He comes before God humbly, like a simple servant.

---

## The two goats (Verses 6–10)

[6] “Aaron shall bring the bull for the sin offering, which is for himself.
So he shall make atonement for himself and for his family.
[7] He shall take the two goats
and set them before the LORD at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[8] Aaron shall cast lots for the two goats:
one lot for the LORD and the other lot for the scapegoat.
Casting lots is a way of choosing, a little like rolling dice.
[9] Aaron shall bring the goat on which the lot for the LORD falls, and offer it as a sin offering.
[10] But the goat on which the lot for the scapegoat falls
shall be set alive before the LORD, to make atonement with it.
He shall send it away into the wilderness as the scapegoat.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Hebrew it says here “for Azazel.” We do not know for sure what this word means. Maybe it means “the goat that goes away.” That is where the English translation “scapegoat” comes from. Maybe Azazel is also the name of a place in the wilderness, or of a desert demon.
> We still use the word “scapegoat” today. Today it means someone who is unfairly blamed for something. Here in the Bible it is different: The goat carries the guilt of the people away, so that the people become free.

---

## Aaron goes into the Most Holy Place (Verses 11–14)

[11] “Aaron shall bring the bull for the sin offering, which is for himself.
He shall make atonement for himself and for his family.
And he shall kill the bull for the sin offering, which is for himself.
[12] He shall take a censer full of burning coals from the altar that stands before the LORD,
and two handfuls of sweet incense, crushed fine.
And he shall bring it behind the curtain.
[13] He shall put the incense on the fire before the LORD.
The cloud of the incense shall cover the mercy seat that is on the covenant.
Then he will not die.
[14] He shall take some of the bull’s blood
and sprinkle it with his finger on the front of the mercy seat, toward the east.
And in front of the mercy seat he shall sprinkle some of the blood with his finger seven times.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only on this one day of the year may a person enter the Most Holy Place, and only the high priest.
> The cloud of incense covers the mercy seat. So the high priest does not see God’s presence directly. For no person can see God and stay alive (Exodus 33:20).
> The blood is sprinkled on the mercy seat. Here, right above the Ten Commandments in the ark, the guilt is covered.

---

## Atonement for the people and for the holy place (Verses 15–19)

[15] “Then he shall kill the goat for the sin offering, which is for the people.
He shall bring its blood behind the curtain.
He shall do with its blood just as he did with the bull’s blood,
and sprinkle it on the mercy seat and in front of the mercy seat.
[16] So he shall make atonement for the Holy Place,
because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel and because of their wrongdoings, all their sins.
He shall do the same for the Tent of Meeting,
which lives with them in the middle of their uncleanness.
[17] No one shall be in the Tent of Meeting
when he goes in to make atonement in the Holy Place,
until he comes out and has made atonement
for himself, for his family and for the whole assembly of Israel.

[18] “He shall go out to the altar that stands before the LORD and make atonement for it.
He shall take some of the bull’s blood and some of the goat’s blood
and put it all around on the horns of the altar.
[19] He shall sprinkle some of the blood on it with his finger seven times.
So he shall make it clean and make it holy from the uncleanness of the children of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Not only the people are made clean, but also the holy place itself. All year long, the guilt of the people has piled up in the holy place like dirt. Now everything is made clean. So God can go on living in the middle of his people.
> “In the middle of their uncleanness” – God does not live with perfect people. He lives among people with faults. And he gives a way for this to stay possible.

---

## The scapegoat carries the guilt away (Verses 20–22)

[20] “When he has finished making atonement for the Holy Place, the Tent of Meeting and the altar,
he shall bring the living goat.
[21] Aaron shall lay both his hands on the head of the living goat.
Over it he shall confess all the guilt of the children of Israel,
all their wrongdoings, all their sins.
He shall put them on the head of the goat.
And he shall send it away into the wilderness with a man who is ready for this.
[22] So the goat shall carry all their guilt on itself into a lonely land.
And the man shall let the goat go free in the wilderness.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a very vivid picture. All the sins of the people are said out loud and laid on the goat. Then the goat is led far away, into the wilderness. The guilt is gone. It does not come back.
> Psalm 103:12 says: As far as the east is from the west, that far God has taken our wrongdoings away from us. Wrongdoings are sins.
> Christians see here a picture of Jesus. John the Baptist said about him: Look, here is the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). The letter to the Hebrews in the New Testament compares Jesus in detail with the high priest on the Day of Atonement (Hebrews 9).

---

## After the service in the Most Holy Place (Verses 23–28)

[23] “Aaron shall go into the Tent of Meeting.
He shall take off the linen clothes that he put on when he went into the Holy Place,
and leave them there.
[24] Then he shall bathe himself in water in a holy place and put on his clothes.
He shall come out and bring his burnt offering and the burnt offering of the people.
So he shall make atonement for himself and for the people.
[25] He shall burn the fat of the sin offering on the altar.

[26] “The man who let the goat go as the scapegoat
shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water.
After that he may come into the camp.
[27] The bull for the sin offering and the goat for the sin offering,
whose blood was brought into the Holy Place to make atonement,
shall be carried outside the camp.
They shall burn their skins, their meat and their dung with fire.
[28] Whoever burns them shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water.
After that he may come into the camp.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the service, Aaron puts on his splendid clothes again (his “clothes” in verse 24). He brings the burnt offerings. Now the people are at peace with God and can give themselves completely to him.

---

## Yom Kippur – a day for all time (Verses 29–34)

[29] “This shall be a rule for you forever:
In the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, you shall humble yourselves.
And you shall do no work at all,
whether you were born in the land or are a foreigner who lives among you.
[30] For on this day atonement will be made for you, to make you clean.
You will be clean from all your sins before the LORD.
[31] It is a Sabbath for you, a day of complete rest.
The Sabbath is the holy day of rest.
And you shall humble yourselves.
This is a rule forever.

[32] The priest who is anointed and set apart to serve as priest in his father’s place
shall make the atonement.
He shall put on the linen clothes, the holy clothes.
[33] He shall make atonement for the Most Holy Place.
He shall make atonement for the Tent of Meeting and for the altar.
And he shall make atonement for the priests and for all the people of the assembly.
[34] “This shall be a rule for you forever:
to make atonement for the children of Israel once a year because of all their sins.”
And it was done as the LORD commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The seventh month is in the fall, in September or October.
> “Humbling yourselves” means above all: fasting. To this day, Jews fast for 25 hours on Yom Kippur. They do not eat or drink anything. They pray in the synagogue, think about their lives and ask God and other people for forgiveness.
> “You will be clean from all your sins” – that is the great promise of this day. Once a year, everyone may start completely new.
> The foreigner who lives in Israel is also to rest on this day. God’s atonement is for everyone.
