# Leviticus – Chapter 26
### Blessing and curse – God does not forget his covenant

---

## No idols, keep the Sabbath (Verses 1–2)

[1] “‘You shall not make idols for yourselves.
You shall not set up a carved image or a stone pillar.
And you shall not put any stone with pictures carved on it in your land, to bow down to it.
For I am the LORD your God.

[2] “‘You shall keep my Sabbaths.
And you shall honor my sanctuary.
I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of the many commandments in Leviticus, the most important things are said once more: serve only the one God and keep the Sabbath.
> Verse 2: A sanctuary is a holy place where God is worshiped.

---

## The blessing for obeying (Verses 3–13)

[3] “‘If you live by my statutes, keep my commandments and do them,
[4] then I will give you rain at the right time.
The land will give its harvest.
And the trees of the field will give their fruit.
[5] Your threshing will go on until the grape harvest.
And the grape harvest will go on until the time for sowing.
You will eat your bread until you are full.
And you will live in your land in safety.

[6] “‘I will give peace in the land.
You will lie down, and nobody will make you afraid.
I will remove dangerous animals from the land.
And no sword will go through your land.
[7] You will chase your enemies.
And they will fall before you by the sword.
[8] Just 5 of you will chase 100.
And 100 of you will chase 10,000.
And your enemies will fall before you by the sword.

[9] “‘I will turn to you with kindness.
I will make you fruitful and make you many.
And I will make my covenant with you stand firm.
[10] You will eat old supplies that were stored for a long time.
And you will have to move out the old to make room for the new.
[11] I will set up my tent among you.
And I will not reject you in disgust.
[12] I will walk among you.
I will be your God, and you will be my people.
[13] I am the LORD your God.
I brought you out of the land of Egypt, so that you would no longer be their slaves.
I have broken the bars of your yoke.
And I have made you walk upright.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, agreements between kings and nations often ended like this: with a blessing for those who keep the agreement and a curse for those who break it. God’s covenant with Israel also ends this way.
> The blessing is described beautifully: rain, good harvests, so much that you can never finish it all. Peace, safety, no fear.
> But the greatest thing is verse 12: “I will walk among you.” This is like the Garden of Eden, where God walked in the garden (Genesis 3:8). God wants to be as close to his people as at the beginning.
> Verse 13 is a strong picture: A yoke is a wooden beam that is put on the necks of animals that pull a plow or a cart. Whoever carries a yoke walks bent over. God has broken the yoke. Now Israel can walk upright, as a free people.
> Verse 5: Threshing means: beating the grain to get the seeds out.
> Verse 9: A covenant is a binding agreement.

---

## What happens if you do not obey (Verses 14–17)

[14] “‘But suppose you do not listen to me and do not do all these commandments.
[15] Suppose you reject my statutes.
Suppose you hate my rules, so that you do not do all my commandments, but break my covenant.
[16] Then I will also do this to you:
I will bring terror on you,
wasting disease and fever that ruin the eyes and make life fade away.
You will sow your seed for nothing, for your enemies will eat it.
[17] I will turn against you.
You will be beaten by your enemies.
Those who hate you will rule over you.
And you will run away even when nobody is chasing you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the other side: What happens if Israel breaks the covenant? This part is much longer than the blessing. It sounds very harsh.
> What is important: God does not threaten because he enjoys punishing. It is a warning, like a father who warns his child about a danger. He wants Israel to turn back before it is too late.
> “And you will run away even when nobody is chasing you” – whoever leaves God loses his inner support and lives in fear.

---

## Worse and worse (Verses 18–26)

[18] “‘If you still do not listen to me after all this,
then I will punish you seven times more for your sins.
[19] I will break the pride you have in your power.
I will make your sky like iron and your soil like bronze.
[20] Your strength will be used up for nothing.
For your land will not give its harvest.
And the trees of the land will not give their fruit.

[21] “‘If you go against me and do not want to listen to me,
then I will bring seven times more plagues on you, as your sins deserve.
[22] I will send the wild animals among you.
They will take away your children and destroy your livestock.
And they will make you few in number.
Your roads will be empty.

[23] “‘If these things do not bring you back to me,
but you keep going against me,
[24] then I will also go against you.
I myself will strike you seven times for your sins.
[25] I will bring a sword on you that will carry out the punishment for breaking the covenant.
You will gather together inside your cities.
And I will send disease among you.
You will be handed over to the enemy.
[26] I will break your staff of bread.
Then 10 women will bake your bread in a single oven.
They will weigh out your bread and hand it to you.
You will eat and not be full.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Seven times more” means: worse and worse, until the very end. Each new step is a new chance to turn back. Again and again it says: “If you still do not listen to me …” God waits patiently for an answer.
> “Sky like iron and soil like bronze” is a picture of a drought: The sky gives no rain. The ground is so hard that nothing grows.
> “Break your staff of bread” means: take the bread away. Bread was like a staff that you lean on to stay alive.
> In verse 26, 10 women bake in a single oven: There is so little flour that a single oven is enough for 10 families.

---

## The worst of all (Verses 27–39)

[27] “‘If you still do not listen to me after all this, but go against me,
[28] then I will go against you in anger.
I will also punish you seven times for your sins.
[29] You will eat the flesh of your sons.
And you will eat the flesh of your daughters.
[30] I will destroy your high places and cut down your incense altars.
I will throw your dead bodies on the bodies of your idols.
And I will reject you in disgust.
[31] I will turn your cities into ruins and make your sanctuaries empty and waste.
And I will not enjoy the sweet smell of your offerings.
[32] I will make the land a wasteland.
Your enemies who live in it will be shocked by it.
[33] I will scatter you among the nations.
And I will draw the sword and chase you.
Your land will be a wasteland, and your cities will be ruins.

[34] “‘Then the land will enjoy its Sabbaths
as long as it lies empty and you are in the land of your enemies.
Then the land will rest and enjoy its Sabbaths.
[35] As long as it lies empty, it will rest.
It gets the rest that it did not have in your Sabbaths when you lived on it.

[36] “‘As for those of you who are left, I will make their hearts weak with fear
in the lands of their enemies.
The sound of a leaf blown by the wind will make them run away.
They will run as people run from the sword.
And they will fall even when nobody is chasing them.
[37] They will trip over one another, as if the sword were behind them, even when nobody is chasing them.
You will have no power to stand against your enemies.
[38] You will die among the nations.
The land of your enemies will eat you up.
[39] Those of you who are left will waste away because of their guilt in the lands of your enemies.
And they will also waste away because of the guilt of their ancestors, like them.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are terrible. Verse 29 describes people in a famine who are so desperate that they eat their own children. That is the most horrible thing anyone can imagine. Sadly, such things really happened later in wars, for example when Jerusalem was under siege (Lamentations 4:10).
> Much of this really happened in the history of Israel. In the year 586 BC, the Babylonians conquered Jerusalem. They destroyed the temple and took many people away to Babylon. The prophets saw this as the fulfillment of this warning.
> Verses 34–35 are interesting: Because Israel did not keep the Sabbath years, the land makes up for its rest while the people are in exile. Exile means: living far from home in a foreign land. This is exactly what 2 Chronicles 36:21 also says about the exile in Babylon.
> These verses must never be used to say that the suffering of the Jewish people in history is a punishment from God. That has happened often, and it has led to terrible hatred of Jews. The Bible itself says something very different in the next section.
> Verse 30: High places were places on hills where people worshiped idols.

---

## But God does not forget his covenant (Verses 40–46)

[40] “‘But suppose they confess their guilt and the guilt of their ancestors,
the unfaithfulness they showed against me,
and also that they went against me,
[41] so that I also went against them
and brought them into the land of their enemies.
If then their uncircumcised heart is humbled,
and they accept the punishment for their guilt,
[42] then I will remember my covenant with Jacob
and my covenant with Isaac.
And I will also remember my covenant with Abraham.
And I will remember the land.
[43] The land will also be left empty by them.
It will enjoy its Sabbaths while it lies empty without them.
And they will accept the punishment for their guilt,
because they rejected my rules
and hated my statutes.

[44] “‘But in spite of all that, when they are in the land of their enemies,
I will not reject them, and I will not turn away from them in disgust.
I will not destroy them completely or break my covenant with them.
For I am the LORD their God.
[45] But for their sake I will remember the covenant with their ancestors.
I brought them out of the land of Egypt while the nations watched, so that I might be their God.
I am the LORD.’”

[46] These are the statutes, the rules and the laws
that the LORD made between himself and the people of Israel on Mount Sinai through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the most important part of the chapter. After all the harsh words comes hope.
> If Israel confesses its guilt and turns back, then God remembers his covenant. He made a promise to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. This promise lasts forever.
> Verse 44 is like a light in the darkness: “But in spite of all that … I will not reject them.” Even if Israel breaks the covenant, God does not break it. God’s faithfulness is greater than the unfaithfulness of people.
> An “uncircumcised heart” is a heart that is closed to God. When it is “humbled,” it opens up again.
> In the New Testament, Paul writes about the Jewish people: God has not rejected his people (Romans 11:1–2). And: God’s gifts and his calling can never be canceled (Romans 11:29). That means: God does not take them back.
> Verse 46 is an ending: Here the great laws that God gave at Sinai come to an end. Chapter 27 is an addition.
