# Leviticus – Chapter 17
### The blood is the life

---

## Sacrifices only at the holy tent (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to Aaron, to his sons, and to all the people of Israel.
Tell them,
‘This is what the LORD has commanded:
[3] Someone from the house of Israel might kill a bull, a lamb, or a goat
in the camp or outside the camp.
[4] If he does not bring it to the door of the Tent of Meeting
to give it as an offering to the LORD in front of the LORD’s tabernacle,
then that man is guilty of blood.
He has shed blood.
That man shall be cut off from his people.
Cut off means: He no longer belongs to the people of God.
[5] This rule is there so that the people of Israel bring their sacrifices to the LORD.
Until now they have sacrificed them out in the open field.
Now they shall bring them to the door of the Tent of Meeting, to the priest.
There they shall sacrifice them to the LORD as peace offerings.
[6] The priest shall sprinkle the blood on the LORD’s altar at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
He shall burn the fat as a pleasant smell for the LORD.
[7] They shall no longer bring their sacrifices to the goat idols.
They have been unfaithful to God with them, like a prostitute.
This shall be a rule for them forever, for all their generations.’

[8] “Tell them,
‘Someone from the house of Israel,
or one of the foreigners who live among them,
might offer a burnt offering or another sacrifice.
[9] If he does not bring it to the door of the Tent of Meeting to sacrifice it to the LORD,
then that man shall be cut off from his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the wilderness, everyone lived close to the holy tent. If someone wanted to kill a bull, a sheep, or a goat, he was to bring it to the holy tent as a peace offering. So every meal with meat became a meal with God.
> Back then, many people believed in spirits in the wilderness that looked like male goats. Our English source text says “goat idols.” That means idols in the shape of a goat. Some Israelites secretly made sacrifices to these spirits. That is to stop.
> Later, when Israel lived in the whole land, people were also allowed to kill animals at home (Deuteronomy 12:15–16). But they were still not allowed to eat the blood.

---

## Why no one may eat blood (Verses 10–14)

[10] “‘Someone from the house of Israel,
or one of the foreigners who live among them,
might eat any kind of blood.
Then I will turn against that person who eats blood.
I will cut him off from his people.
[11] For the life of the body is in the blood.
I have given it to you on the altar, to make atonement for your lives.
Atonement means: the guilt is taken away, and there is peace with God again.
For it is the blood that makes atonement, because the life is in it.
[12] That is why I have said to the people of Israel,
“No one among you may eat blood.
And no foreigner who lives among you may eat blood.”

[13] “‘Someone from the people of Israel,
or one of the foreigners who live among them,
might hunt an animal or a bird that may be eaten.
Then he shall pour out its blood and cover it with dust.
[14] For the life of every living body is in its blood.
Its blood is its life.
That is why I said to the people of Israel,
“You shall not eat the blood of any kind of living body.
For the life of every living body is its blood.
Whoever eats it shall be cut off.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11 is one of the most important verses about the sacrifices: “For the life of the body is in the blood.” The blood stands for life. And life belongs to God, not to people.
> That is why the blood is so important in the sacrifices: One life is given for another life. That is how atonement happens.
> Even someone who hunts an animal is to honor the blood. He is to cover it with dust, almost like a burial. That shows respect for the life of the animal.
> Christians see a pointer to Jesus here. The New Testament says: “And without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness” (Hebrews 9:22). And at the Last Supper, Jesus says about the wine: “this is my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins” (Matthew 26:28).

## Someone who eats an animal that died by itself (Verses 15–16)

[15] “‘Someone might eat an animal that died by itself,
or one that wild animals have torn.
It does not matter whether he was born in Israel or is a foreigner.
He shall wash his clothes and bathe in water.
He is unclean until the evening.
Then he is clean.
[16] But if he does not wash his clothes or bathe his body,
then he shall carry his guilt.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> An animal that died by itself has not been drained of its blood. The blood is still in it. That is why it makes a person unclean.
> Someone who ate it anyway, for example because of hunger, can wash and is clean again in the evening. The only bad thing is to simply ignore the cleansing.
