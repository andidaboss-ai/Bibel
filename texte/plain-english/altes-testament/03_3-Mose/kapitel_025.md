# Leviticus – Chapter 25
### The Sabbath year and the Year of Jubilee

---

## The land shall rest (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai.
He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘You will come into the land that I give you.
Then the land shall keep a Sabbath for the LORD.
[3] For six years you shall sow seed in your field.
For six years you shall prune your vineyard and gather in its fruit.
[4] But in the seventh year the land shall have a Sabbath, a time of complete rest, a Sabbath for the LORD.
You shall not sow your field.
You shall not prune your vineyard.
[5] What grows by itself after your harvest, you shall not harvest.
And you shall not pick the grapes of your vine that you have not pruned.
It shall be a year of complete rest for the land.
[6] What the land gives in the Sabbath year shall be food for you:
for yourself, for your servant, for your maid, for your hired worker,
and for the stranger who lives as a foreigner with you.
[7] And all that the land gives shall also be food for your livestock
and for the animals that live in your land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: To prune means: to cut back the branches.
> People rest every seventh day. In the same way, the land shall rest every seventh year. Nobody sows, and nobody harvests.
> This is good for the soil. It can recover. Modern farmers know: If you always use a field, the soil gets worn out.
> But it is about even more: The land belongs to God. In this year, what grows by itself belongs to everyone equally. The owner, the servant, the stranger and even the wild animals may eat from it. For one year, everyone is equal.

---

## The Year of Jubilee: freedom in the whole land (Verses 8–13)

[8] “‘You shall count 7 Sabbath years, 7 times 7 years.
The time of 7 Sabbath years shall be 49 years for you.
[9] Then you shall have the loud trumpet sounded on the tenth day of the seventh month.
On the Day of Atonement you shall sound the trumpet throughout all your land.
[10] You shall make the fiftieth year holy.
And you shall proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants.
It shall be a jubilee for you.
Each of you shall go back to his own property.
And each of you shall go back to his family.
[11] That fiftieth year shall be a jubilee for you.
In it you shall not sow.
You shall not harvest what grows by itself.
And you shall not pick the grapes from the vines that were not pruned.
[12] For it is a jubilee. It shall be holy to you.
You shall eat what the field gives by itself.
[13] In this Year of Jubilee each of you shall go back to his property.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Holy means: It belongs to God in a special way.
> Verse 10: “Proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants” means: Announce freedom for all the people who live in the land.
> After seven times seven years comes the fiftieth year, the “Year of Jubilee.” The name comes from the Hebrew word “yobel.” That is the ram’s horn that is blown on this day.
> In the Year of Jubilee, everything goes back to how it was at the beginning. Whoever had to sell his land gets it back. Whoever had to sell himself as a servant becomes free. Every family gets a new beginning.
> So a few rich people could not keep getting richer and buy up all the land while others stayed poor forever. Every 50 years, the inequality was balanced out again.
> The Year of Jubilee begins on the Day of Atonement. First the guilt before God is forgiven. Then the debts between people are also canceled.
> The words “proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants” are written on the famous Liberty Bell in America.
> Jesus spoke about this year. He said that God had sent him “to tell the prisoners that they will be free … to announce the year when the Lord shows his favor” (Luke 4:18–19).
> In the Catholic Church there is still a “Holy Year” or “Jubilee Year” today. In the year 2000 there was a worldwide movement called “Jubilee 2000.” It asked that poor countries have their debts canceled.

## Buying and selling fairly (Verses 14–17)

[14] “‘When you sell something to your neighbor, or buy something from your neighbor,
you shall not cheat one another.
[15] You shall buy from your neighbor according to the number of years since the Jubilee.
He shall sell to you according to the number of years of harvests.
[16] If there are many years, you shall make the price higher.
If there are few years, you shall make the price lower.
For he is only selling you the number of harvests.
[17] You shall not cheat one another.
But you shall fear your God.
For I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Because the land is given back in the Year of Jubilee, it is not really sold forever. You only sell the harvests until the next Year of Jubilee. It is more like a lease or rent.
> That is why the price depends on how many years are left until the Year of Jubilee. So nobody shall be cheated.

## God provides for the Sabbath year (Verses 18–22)

[18] “‘So you shall follow my rules.
You shall keep my laws and do them.
Then you will live in the land in safety.
[19] The land will give its fruit.
You will eat until you are full and live in it in safety.
[20] Maybe you will say:
“What shall we eat in the seventh year?
Look: We will not sow, and we will not gather in our harvest.”
[21] Then I will send my blessing to you in the sixth year.
The land will give enough fruit for three years.
[22] In the eighth year you will sow.
And you will still eat from the old harvest until the ninth year.
Until its new harvest comes in, you will eat from the old harvest.

> **What does this mean?**
> The question is understandable: What shall we live on if we harvest nothing for a whole year? God answers: Trust me. In the sixth year I will give you so much that it is enough for three years.
> This is like the manna in the desert. On the sixth day there was twice as much, so that nobody had to gather it on the Sabbath (Exodus 16:22–30).
> The Sabbath year is a practice in trust. Jesus says: “So don’t worry, saying, ‘What will we eat?’ … Your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things” (Matthew 6:31–32).

## The land belongs to God (Verses 23–28)

[23] “‘The land shall not be sold forever.
For the land is mine.
You are only strangers and foreigners living with me.
[24] In all the land that you own, you shall allow the land to be bought back.

[25] “‘Your brother might become poor and sell some of his property.
Then his closest relative shall come and buy back what his brother has sold.
[26] But maybe a man has nobody to buy it back.
And then he himself has money again and has enough to buy it back.
[27] Then he shall count the years since he sold it.
He shall pay back the rest to the man he sold it to.
Then he goes back to his property.
[28] But maybe he is not able to get it back himself.
Then what he sold stays with the man who bought it until the Year of Jubilee.
In the Jubilee it becomes free, and he goes back to his property.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23 is the basis for everything: “For the land is mine. You are only strangers and foreigners living with me.” Nobody really owns the land forever. God is the true owner. The people only rent it from him.
> If someone in need had to sell his land, there were three ways to get it back:
> 1. A close relative buys it back. In Hebrew he is called “go’el.” That means “redeemer” – someone who buys something back.
> 2. The seller has money again and buys it back himself.
> 3. In the Year of Jubilee at the latest, he gets it back for free.
> In the book of Ruth, Boaz is such a “redeemer.” He buys back the land for Naomi and marries Ruth (Ruth 4).

## Houses in cities and villages (Verses 29–34)

[29] “‘If a man sells a house to live in, in a city with walls,
then he can buy it back within a whole year after he sold it.
For a full year he has the right to buy it back.
[30] If it is not bought back within a full year,
then the house in the walled city shall belong forever to the man who bought it,
and to his descendants after him.
It does not become free in the Jubilee.
[31] But the houses in the villages that have no wall around them shall be treated like the fields in the country.
They can be bought back, and they become free in the Jubilee.

[32] “‘But for the cities of the Levites this is the rule:
The Levites may buy back the houses in the cities that belong to them at any time.
[33] If a Levite has sold a house in the city that belongs to him, it can be bought back.
And it becomes free in the Jubilee.
For the houses in the cities of the Levites are their property among the people of Israel.
[34] But the pasture land around their cities may not be sold.
For it is their property forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> A house in a city is something different from a field. A family lives from its field. That is why the field shall always go back to the family. A house in the city is more like goods that are bought and sold.
> But a house in a village is part of the farm. That is why the same rules apply to it as to the field.
> The Levites did not get their own land like the other tribes, only some cities with pasture land (Numbers 35). That is why they are especially protected.

## Do not take interest from the poor (Verses 35–38)

[35] “‘Your brother might become poor and no longer be able to support himself among you.
Then you shall help him.
He shall live with you like a foreigner and a guest.
[36] Take no interest from him and no profit.
But fear your God, so that your brother can live among you.
[37] You shall not lend him your money for interest.
And you shall not give him your food for profit.
[38] I am the LORD your God.
I brought you out of the land of Egypt
to give you the land of Canaan and to be your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 36: Interest means: extra money that someone has to pay back on top of a loan.
> Whoever is poor and needs help shall be helped without anyone making money from it. Whoever lends money to a poor person shall not take any interest.
> Back then, people usually did not borrow money to do business. They borrowed because they were in need and had nothing to eat. You shall not make money from someone else’s need.
> In the Middle Ages, Christians and Jews concluded from this that interest is forbidden. Islam also forbids interest. Today this passage is mainly understood like this: Do not take advantage of other people’s need.

## An Israelite shall not serve as a slave (Verses 39–43)

[39] “‘Your brother might become poor among you and sell himself to you.
Then you shall not make him work like a slave.
[40] He shall be with you like a hired worker and like a guest.
He shall work for you until the Year of Jubilee.
[41] Then he shall leave you, he and his children with him.
He shall go back to his own family and to the property of his ancestors.
[42] For they are my servants.
I brought them out of the land of Egypt.
They shall not be sold as slaves.
[43] You shall not rule over him harshly.
But you shall fear your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Someone could be so poor that he had to sell himself. Then he should not be treated like a slave, but like a worker who gets paid.
> The reason: “For they are my servants.” God freed Israel from slavery. Israel belongs to God. That is why no Israelite may make another Israelite his slave.

## Slaves from other nations (Verses 44–46)

[44] “‘Your male and female slaves may come from the nations that are around you.
From them you may buy male and female slaves.
[45] You may also buy from the children of the foreigners who live among you,
and from their families who are with you, who were born in your land.
They will be your property.
[46] You may leave them to your children after you as an inheritance, so that they own them.
You may keep them as slaves forever.
But over your brothers, the people of Israel, you shall not rule harshly, one over another.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are very hard for us to read today. They allow people to buy people from other nations as slaves and to pass them on to their children.
> We have to say it honestly: Back then there was slavery in all the countries of the world. Here the Bible describes that world. It did not end slavery. But it made rules to limit it. In other places it also protects foreign slaves: Whoever injures his slave must set him free (Exodus 21:26–27). And a slave who runs away may not be sent back (Deuteronomy 23:15–16).
> Sadly, people in the 18th and 19th centuries used these verses to defend the slavery of Africans in America. That was a terrible misuse of the Bible.
> Other Christians fought against slavery precisely because of the Bible. For the Bible also teaches: Every person is created in God’s image (Genesis 1:27). And the New Testament says: “There is neither slave nor free … For you are all one in Christ Jesus” (Galatians 3:28).
> Today Jews and Christians agree: Slavery is wrong.

## When an Israelite sells himself to a foreigner (Verses 47–55)

[47] “‘A foreigner or a guest living with you might become rich.
And your brother beside him might become poor.
He sells himself to the foreigner or guest living among you,
or to a member of the foreigner’s family.
[48] After he is sold, he may be bought back.
One of his brothers may buy him back.
[49] Or his uncle or his uncle’s son may buy him back.
Or any close relative from his family may buy him back.
Or if he himself becomes rich, he may buy himself back.
[50] He shall count the time with the man who bought him,
from the year he sold himself to him until the Year of Jubilee.
The price of his sale shall depend on the number of years.
His time with him shall be counted like the time of a hired worker.
[51] If there are still many years,
then he shall pay back a large part of the money he was bought for.
That is the price to buy him back.
[52] If only a few years are left until the Year of Jubilee,
then he shall count with him.
He shall pay the price to buy him back according to his years of service.
[53] He shall be with him like a worker who is hired year by year.
The man shall not rule over him harshly while you watch.
[54] If he is not bought back in these ways,
then he shall become free in the Year of Jubilee, he and his children with him.
[55] For the people of Israel are my servants.
They are my servants whom I brought out of the land of Egypt.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> A foreigner could also become rich in Israel. If a poor Israelite sold himself to him, his family was supposed to buy him free. In the Year of Jubilee at the latest, he became free.
> “Buying free” or “redeeming” is a very practical word here: Someone pays the price so that another person becomes free.
> Later, Christians applied this word to Jesus. He is the “redeemer” who buys people free (Mark 10:45; 1 Peter 1:18–19).
> The whole chapter shows: God does not want people to be trapped in poverty and dependence forever. Everyone shall get a chance for a new beginning.
