# Leviticus – Chapter 6
### Making things right – instructions for the priests

---

## When someone cheats his neighbor (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “If anyone sins and is unfaithful to the LORD
by cheating his neighbor:
in something that was given to him to keep safe,
in a business deal, or by robbery,
or if he has oppressed his neighbor,
[3] or if he has found something that was lost and lied about it,
and has sworn a false oath –
in any of these things that a person does and so sins –
[4] if he has sinned in this way and is guilty,
he shall give back
what he took by robbery,
or what he got by oppression,
or what was given to him to keep safe,
or the lost thing that he found,
[5] or anything about which he swore falsely.
He shall pay it back in full and add a fifth more to it.
He shall give it back to the one it belongs to, on the day he is found guilty.
[6] And he shall bring his trespass offering to the LORD:
a ram without any fault from the flock, by your estimate, as a trespass offering to the priest.
[7] So the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD.
And he will be forgiven for whatever he did that made him guilty.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, verses 1–7 of this chapter come at the end of Chapter 5 (as 5:20–26). That is why the verse numbers in Chapter 6 are different there. Here we follow the numbering of the WEB.
> Whoever cheats another person sins not only against that person, but also against God. That is why it says: He “is unfaithful to the LORD.”
> The order is important: First you give back to the person what you took from him, and you add a fifth. Only then do you bring the offering to God.
> Jesus says something very similar: If you bring your gift to the altar and remember there that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there. First go and make peace with your brother. Then come and bring your gift (Matthew 5:23–24).
> A good example is Zacchaeus in the New Testament. He even gave back four times as much (Luke 19:8).

## The fire on the altar must never go out (Verses 8–13)

[8] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[9] “Command Aaron and his sons and say,
‘This is the law for the burnt offering:
The burnt offering shall stay on the fireplace on the altar all night until morning.
And the fire of the altar shall be kept burning on it.
[10] The priest shall put on his linen robe,
and he shall put on his linen underpants on his body.
Linen is a fine cloth made from a plant called flax.
He shall take away the ashes that are left when the fire has burned up the burnt offering on the altar.
And he shall put them beside the altar.
[11] Then he shall take off his clothes and put on other clothes,
and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place.
[12] The fire on the altar shall be kept burning on it. It shall not go out.
Every morning the priest shall burn wood on it.
He shall lay the burnt offering neatly on it
and burn the fat of the peace offerings on it.
[13] Fire shall be kept burning on the altar all the time. It shall not go out.

> **What does this mean?**
> From here on, the instructions are for the priests: How should they handle the offerings?
> The fire on the altar should never go out, day and night. This is said three times. It is a sign that the bond between God and his people should never be broken.
> Even carrying away the ashes is a holy service. The priest wears special clothes for it.
> Many Christians also see the fire that never goes out as a picture of faith: It should always burn in us and never go out.

## The law for the meal offering (Verses 14–18)

[14] “‘This is the law for the meal offering:
The sons of Aaron shall bring it before the LORD, in front of the altar.
[15] He shall take from it a handful of the fine flour of the meal offering and of its oil,
and all the frankincense that is on the meal offering.
He shall burn it on the altar as a pleasant smell, as its memorial part for the LORD.
[16] Aaron and his sons shall eat what is left of it.
It shall be eaten without yeast in a holy place.
They shall eat it in the courtyard of the Tent of Meeting.
[17] It shall not be baked with yeast.
I have given it to them as their share of my offerings made by fire.
It is most holy, like the sin offering and the trespass offering.
[18] Every male among the children of Aaron shall eat from it.
This is their share forever, for all your generations, from the LORD’s offerings made by fire.
Whoever touches them shall be holy.’”

## The meal offering of the priests (Verses 19–23)

[19] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[20] “This is the offering of Aaron and his sons,
which they shall bring to the LORD on the day when he is anointed:
about 2 quarts of fine flour as a meal offering that is always given,
half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening.
[21] It shall be made with oil on a griddle.
When it is soaked through, you shall bring it in.
You shall bring the meal offering in baked pieces, as a pleasant smell to the LORD.
[22] The anointed priest from among his sons who takes his place shall bring it.
It is a rule forever. It shall be burned completely for the LORD.
[23] Every meal offering of a priest shall be burned completely.
It shall not be eaten.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: a tenth of an ephah of flour, that is, about 2 quarts.
> When an ordinary person brings a meal offering, the priests eat the rest. But when a priest brings a meal offering himself, he may not eat any of it. Otherwise he would be giving a present to himself.

## The law for the sin offering (Verses 24–30)

[24] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[25] “Speak to Aaron and his sons and say,
‘This is the law for the sin offering:
In the place where the burnt offering is killed, the sin offering shall also be killed before the LORD.
It is most holy.
[26] The priest who brings it for sin shall eat it.
It shall be eaten in a holy place, in the courtyard of the Tent of Meeting.
[27] Whatever touches its meat shall be holy.
If any of its blood is sprinkled on a piece of clothing,
you shall wash the thing it was sprinkled on in a holy place.
[28] But the clay pot in which it is boiled shall be broken.
And if it is boiled in a bronze pot, the pot shall be scrubbed and rinsed with water.
[29] Every male among the priests shall eat from it. It is most holy.
[30] But no sin offering shall be eaten if any of its blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting
to make atonement in the Holy Place.
It shall be burned with fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> For the smaller sin offerings (for leaders and ordinary people), the priests eat the meat. For the big sin offerings whose blood is brought into the holy tent (for the high priest and the whole people), everything is burned (see Leviticus 4).
> A clay pot soaks up some of the meat. That is why it must be broken. A bronze pot can be cleaned completely.
> What is holy should not be mixed with everyday things.
