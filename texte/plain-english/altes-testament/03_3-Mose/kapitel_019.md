# Leviticus – Chapter 19
### Be holy – love your neighbor as yourself

---

## You shall be holy (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to the whole community of the people of Israel and tell them,
‘You shall be holy, because I, the LORD your God, am holy.

[3] “‘Each one of you shall respect his mother and his father.
And you shall keep my Sabbaths.
I am the LORD your God.
[4] “‘Do not turn to idols.
And do not make gods out of melted metal for yourselves.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapter 19 is one of the most important chapters in the whole Bible. It shows what “being holy” means in everyday life.
> Being holy does not only mean being devout at worship. It means doing what is good, fair, and loving in all of life: at work, in the field, in court, with neighbors, with foreigners, with old people, and with people with disabilities.
> Many commandments here are a reminder of the Ten Commandments: honor your parents, keep the Sabbath, no idols.
> Again and again it says: “I am the LORD.” That is the reason: Because God is like this, we are to be like this too.

---

## The peace offering (Verses 5–8)

[5] “‘When you bring a peace offering to the LORD,
you shall offer it in the right way, so that you are accepted.
[6] It shall be eaten on the day you offer it, and on the next day.
Whatever is left over until the third day shall be burned with fire.
[7] If someone still eats some of it on the third day, it is disgusting.
It will not be accepted.
[8] And everyone who eats it shall carry his guilt.
For he has dishonored what is holy to the LORD.
That person shall be cut off from his people.

---

## Leave something for the poor (Verses 9–10)

[9] “‘When you bring in the harvest of your land,
you shall not harvest your field all the way to the corners.
And you shall not pick up the grain that is left lying after the harvest.
[10] You shall not go over your vineyard a second time.
And you shall not pick up the grapes that have fallen down.
You shall leave them for the poor and for the foreigner.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a social rule: The farmers are not to keep everything for themselves. The edges of the field and what is left lying belong to the poor and the foreigners. That way, people without land of their own could also find something to eat.
> The poor do not just receive a handout. They work and gather for themselves. That way they keep their dignity.
> The book of Ruth tells how Ruth, a poor foreign woman, gathers grain in the field of Boaz (Ruth 2). That is how a wonderful story begins.

---

## Deal honestly and fairly with each other (Verses 11–16)

[11] “‘You shall not steal.
“‘You shall not lie.
“‘You shall not cheat one another.
[12] “‘You shall not swear falsely by my name
and so dishonor the name of your God.
I am the LORD.

[13] “‘You shall not oppress your neighbor or rob him.
“‘You shall not keep the pay of a hired worker with you overnight until the morning.

[14] “‘You shall not curse a deaf person.
And you shall not put something in the way of a blind person so that he trips.
But you shall fear your God.
I am the LORD.

[15] “‘You shall not be unfair when you judge.
You shall not favor the poor, and you shall not favor the powerful.
You shall judge your neighbor fairly.

[16] “‘You shall not go around spreading lies about others among your people.
“‘You shall not put the life of your neighbor in danger.
I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A hired worker here is a worker who is hired for only one day. He needs his pay that same evening to buy food for his family. That is why he is to be paid right away.
> A deaf person does not hear the curse. A blind person does not see what is in the way. You could think: Nobody will notice. But God sees it. That is why it says: “But you shall fear your God.” Whoever mocks or harms weak people acts against God.
> In court, everyone is to be treated the same. The rich are not to be favored. But the poor are not to be favored out of pity either. Justice applies to everyone.
> “Put the life of your neighbor in danger” may also mean: to stand by and do nothing when someone is in danger. Jews understand this verse like this: You must help when another person’s life is in danger.

---

## Love your neighbor as yourself (Verses 17–18)

[17] “‘You shall not hate your brother in your heart.
You shall openly correct your neighbor,
so that you do not carry guilt because of him.
[18] “‘You shall not take revenge.
And you shall not hold a grudge against the people of your nation.
But you shall love your neighbor as yourself.
I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is one of the most famous sentences in the Bible: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
> Jesus called it one of the two most important commandments, together with the commandment to love God with all your heart (Mark 12:29–31). The famous Jewish teacher Rabbi Akiva also said: This is the most important principle of the Torah.
> The order is interesting: First it says: Do not hate your brother in your heart. Then it says: Speak openly with your neighbor. If someone has hurt you, you are to tell him, instead of holding a secret grudge. Honest words are better than silent hate.
> “As yourself” – this assumes that you may also accept yourself.
> Who is my neighbor? Jesus answered this with the story of the good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37). And already in this chapter, in verse 34, the commandment is widened to include the foreigner.

---

## Do not mix things (Verse 19)

[19] “‘You shall keep my rules.
“‘You shall not breed two different kinds of animals with each other.
“‘You shall not sow your field with two different kinds of seed.
“‘Do not wear clothes made of two different kinds of material.

> **What does this mean?**
> Why these rules? The Bible does not explain it. Maybe it is meant to remind people of creation, where God created everything “each according to its kind” (Genesis 1). The order of creation is to be respected.
> Later it is explained that the two materials are wool and linen (Deuteronomy 22:11). Some Jews still pay attention to this today.

## A case with a slave woman (Verses 20–22)

[20] “‘A man might sleep with a woman who is a slave.
She is promised in marriage to another man,
but she has not yet been bought free or given her freedom.
Then they shall be punished.
But they shall not be put to death, because she was not free.
[21] He shall bring his trespass offering to the LORD, to the door of the Tent of Meeting.
It shall be a ram as a trespass offering.
[22] With the ram of the trespass offering, the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD
for the sin he has committed.
Then the sin he has committed will be forgiven him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a difficult case from a time when there was slavery. If a free woman who was engaged slept with another man, it counted as adultery, and the punishment was death (Deuteronomy 22:23–24). With a slave woman the situation was different, because she could not freely decide for herself.
> For us today it is hard to bear that people were slaves. Here the Bible describes the world as it was back then. It tries to give rules for justice in that world. Slavery itself is not an ideal of the Bible. Israel was always to remember that it had been a slave in Egypt itself.

## The fruit of young trees (Verses 23–25)

[23] “‘When you come into the land and plant all kinds of fruit trees,
you shall treat their fruit as forbidden.
For three years it shall be forbidden to you. It shall not be eaten.
[24] But in the fourth year all its fruit shall be holy, to praise the LORD.
[25] In the fifth year you may eat its fruit.
So the trees will give you more and more fruit.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Young trees bear only a few small fruits in their first years. If you do not pick them, the trees grow stronger. That is also a good rule for growing fruit.
> The first good fruit in the fourth year belongs to God, as a thank-you. Only after that may you eat from it yourself.

## No pagan customs (Verses 26–31)

[26] “‘You shall not eat any meat with the blood still in it.
You shall not use magic spells, and you shall not practice sorcery.
[27] “‘You shall not cut the hair on the sides of your head.
And you shall not trim the edge of your beard.
[28] “‘You shall not make any cuts in your skin for the dead.
And you shall not tattoo any marks on yourselves.
I am the LORD.
[29] “‘Do not dishonor your daughter by making her a prostitute.
Otherwise the land will turn to prostitution, and the land will become full of wickedness.
[30] “‘You shall keep my Sabbaths and honor my holy place.
I am the LORD.
[31] “‘Do not turn to people who call up the dead, or to fortune-tellers.
Do not go to them, so that you do not become unclean through them.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is about customs that the nations around Israel had. When they mourned, they cut their skin and cut their hair in a special way to honor the gods of the dead. Israel is not to do this.
> Verse 27 is where some devout Jews get the custom of wearing long curls at the temples, called “payot.”
> Verse 28 also mentions tattoos. Back then they were often signs of foreign gods or marks for slaves. Jews and Christians think differently about whether this ban also applies to tattoos today.
> Verse 29 protects daughters. Fathers in need sometimes sold their daughters into prostitution. This is strictly forbidden here.
> Magic, fortune-telling, and calling up the dead are forbidden. Whoever trusts God does not need fortune-tellers to learn about the future.

## Old people and foreigners (Verses 32–34)

[32] “‘You shall stand up in front of a gray head, and you shall honor old people.
And you shall fear your God.
I am the LORD.

[33] “‘If a foreigner lives with you in your land, you shall not do him wrong.
[34] The foreigner who lives with you shall be to you like someone born among you.
And you shall love him as yourself.
For you yourselves lived as foreigners in the land of Egypt.
I am the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Old people are to be honored. A “gray head” is a person with gray hair. When an old person comes into the room, you stand up. That is a sign of respect.
> Verse 34 is very special: The commandment to love your neighbor also applies to the foreigner, the person from another country. “And you shall love him as yourself.”
> The reason: “For you yourselves lived as foreigners in the land of Egypt.” Israel knows how it feels to be oppressed as a foreigner. That is why they are to treat foreigners well. This commandment, in these or similar words, is in the Torah more than 30 times.

## Honest measures and weights (Verses 35–37)

[35] “‘You shall not be unfair when you judge,
or when you measure length, weight, or amount.
[36] You shall have honest scales and honest weights,
an honest measure for dry things and an honest measure for liquids.
I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt.
[37] “‘You shall keep all my rules and all my laws, and do them.
I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible names the measures ephah (for grain) and hin (for liquids) here. An ephah is about 6 gallons. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> Back then, traders often cheated with false weights and measures. For example, someone who used a slightly lighter weight sold less than the customer paid for. God says: That is wrong. Honesty in business is part of being holy.
> The chapter ends the way it began: with God, who set his people free. Because God set Israel free, Israel is to live in a fair and loving way.
