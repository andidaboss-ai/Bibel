# Leviticus – Chapter 3
### The peace offering

---

## The peace offering from cattle (Verses 1–5)

[1] “‘If his offering is a sacrifice of peace offerings,
and he brings it from the cattle, a male or a female animal,
he shall bring it before the LORD without any fault.
[2] He shall lay his hand on the head of his offering
and kill it at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
Aaron’s sons, the priests, shall sprinkle the blood all around on the altar.
[3] From the sacrifice of peace offerings he shall bring an offering made by fire to the LORD:
the fat that covers the inner parts, and all the fat that is on the inner parts,
[4] the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is by the lower back,
and the covering on the liver.
He shall take that away together with the kidneys.
[5] Aaron’s sons shall burn it on the altar, on top of the burnt offering
that is on the wood over the fire.
It is an offering made by fire, a pleasant smell to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A sacrifice is an animal that is killed and given to God as an offering.
> The peace offering is also called the “fellowship offering.” In Hebrew, its name contains the word “shalom”: peace, well-being, being whole.
> In this offering, only the fat and a few inner parts are burned. The people eat most of the meat themselves – at a feast with family and friends (Leviticus 7:15). One part belongs to the priests.
> It is like a shared meal with God. People celebrate that they live in peace with God and with one another. People brought it, for example, to thank God, or when they had kept a promise.
> Unlike the burnt offering, it may also be a female animal.

## The peace offering from sheep (Verses 6–11)

[6] “‘If his offering for a sacrifice of peace offerings to the LORD is from the flock,
a male or a female animal,
he shall bring it without any fault.
[7] If he brings a lamb as his offering, he shall bring it before the LORD.
[8] He shall lay his hand on the head of his offering
and kill it in front of the Tent of Meeting.
Aaron’s sons shall sprinkle its blood all around on the altar.
[9] From the sacrifice of peace offerings he shall bring an offering made by fire to the LORD:
its fat, the whole fat tail, which he shall cut off close to the backbone,
the fat that covers the inner parts, and all the fat that is on the inner parts,
[10] the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is by the lower back,
and the covering on the liver.
He shall take that away together with the kidneys.
[11] The priest shall burn it on the altar.
It is the food of the offering made by fire to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “fat tail” belongs to a kind of sheep in the Middle East that stores a lot of fat in its tail. It could weigh many pounds and was seen as an especially good piece.
> “The food of the offering to the LORD” is a picture. God does not need food. But the offering is like an invitation: God sits at the table with us.

## The peace offering from goats (Verses 12–17)

[12] “‘If his offering is a goat, he shall bring it before the LORD.
[13] He shall lay his hand on its head and kill it in front of the Tent of Meeting.
The sons of Aaron shall sprinkle its blood all around on the altar.
[14] From it he shall bring his offering, an offering made by fire to the LORD:
the fat that covers the inner parts, and all the fat that is on the inner parts,
[15] the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is by the lower back,
and the covering on the liver.
He shall take that away together with the kidneys.
[16] The priest shall burn them on the altar.
It is the food of the offering made by fire, a pleasant smell.
All the fat belongs to the LORD.

[17] “‘This shall be a rule that lasts forever, for all your generations, everywhere you live:
You shall not eat any fat or any blood.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The fat was seen as the best part of the animal. That is why it belongs to God.
> This means the fat around the inner organs, not the fat inside the meat.
> People may not eat blood, because the life is in the blood (Leviticus 17:11). Life belongs to God. This rule already existed in the time of Noah (Genesis 9:4).
> To this day, Jews make sure that kosher meat has had the blood drained out. The first Christians also kept the rule against blood (Acts 15:20).
