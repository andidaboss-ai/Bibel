# Leviticus – Chapter 7
### More laws for the offerings – the share of the priests

---

## The law for the trespass offering (Verses 1–6)

[1] “‘This is the law for the trespass offering: It is most holy.
[2] In the place where they kill the burnt offering, he shall also kill the trespass offering.
And he shall sprinkle its blood all around on the altar.
[3] He shall bring all its fat:
the fat tail and the fat that covers the inner parts,
[4] and he shall take away the two kidneys and the fat on them, which is by the lower back,
and the covering on the liver, together with the kidneys.
[5] The priest shall burn them on the altar as an offering made by fire to the LORD.
It is a trespass offering.
[6] Every male among the priests may eat from it.
It shall be eaten in a holy place. It is most holy.

---

## What belongs to the priests (Verses 7–10)

[7] “‘The trespass offering is like the sin offering.
There is one law for both.
It belongs to the priest who makes atonement with it.
[8] When a priest brings someone’s burnt offering,
the skin of the burnt offering that he has brought belongs to him.
[9] Every meal offering that is baked in the oven,
and everything that is prepared in a pan or on a griddle,
belongs to the priest who brings it.
[10] Every meal offering, whether mixed with oil or dry,
belongs to all the sons of Aaron, to one as much as to another.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests had no land of their own where they could grow food. They lived from the offerings. That is why this tells exactly what belongs to them: meat, skins and bread.
> In this way the whole people cared, through their offerings, for the priests who served for everyone.

---

## The law for the peace offering (Verses 11–21)

[11] “‘This is the law for the sacrifice of peace offerings that someone brings to the LORD:
[12] If he brings it to give thanks,
then together with the sacrifice of thanks he shall bring cakes without yeast, mixed with oil,
thin wafers without yeast, spread with oil,
and cakes of fine flour mixed with oil.
[13] Together with the sacrifice of his peace offerings for thanks,
he shall also bring cakes of bread made with yeast.
[14] From these he shall bring one of each kind as a heave offering to the LORD.
A heave offering is a gift that is lifted up for God.
It shall belong to the priest who sprinkles the blood of the peace offerings.
[15] The meat of the sacrifice of his peace offerings for thanks
shall be eaten on the day he brings his offering.
He shall not leave any of it until the morning.

[16] “‘But if the sacrifice he brings is for a vow, or is a freewill offering,
it shall be eaten on the day he brings his sacrifice.
A vow is a solemn promise to God.
What is left of it may also be eaten on the next day.
[17] But whatever is left of the meat of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burned with fire.
[18] If any of the meat of the sacrifice of his peace offerings is eaten on the third day,
it will not be accepted.
It will not be counted for the one who brings it.
It will be something disgusting to God.
And the person who eats any of it will carry his guilt.

[19] “‘Meat that touches anything unclean shall not be eaten.
It shall be burned with fire.
As for the other meat, everyone who is clean may eat it.
[20] But if a person eats from the meat of the sacrifice of peace offerings that belongs to the LORD
while he is unclean,
that person shall be cut off from his people.
That means: He no longer belongs to the people of God.
[21] When anyone touches anything unclean –
something unclean from a human being, or an unclean animal, or anything unclean and disgusting –
and then eats some of the meat of the sacrifice of peace offerings that belongs to the LORD,
that person shall be cut off from his people.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> People could bring the peace offering for three reasons: to thank God, to keep a promise they had made to God, or simply freely, out of joy.
> With the thank offering there is a feast. The meat must be eaten on the same day. That means: You invite many guests – family, friends, poor people. You should share your joy about God’s help.
> Interesting: Bread made with yeast is part of it here too. It is not burned on the altar. It is eaten at the feast.
> After three days, the meat has gone bad in the heat. That is why it may not be eaten then anymore.
> There is more about “being cut off” in the explanation for Exodus 30:33.

## No fat and no blood (Verses 22–27)

[22] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[23] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘You shall not eat any fat, whether of cattle, or sheep, or goats.
[24] The fat of an animal that died by itself,
and the fat of an animal that was torn by wild animals,
may be used for anything else.
But you shall never eat it.
[25] For whoever eats the fat of an animal that people bring to the LORD as an offering made by fire,
that person shall be cut off from his people.
[26] And you shall not eat any blood, whether of birds or of other animals, anywhere you live.
[27] Whoever eats any blood, that person shall be cut off from his people.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This means the fat around the inner organs, which is burned on the altar with the offerings. It belongs to God.
> But people could use this fat for other things, for example for lamps or to care for leather.
> People may not eat blood, because the life is in the blood (Leviticus 17:11).

## The priests’ share of the peace offering (Verses 28–36)

[28] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[29] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘Whoever brings the sacrifice of his peace offerings to the LORD
shall bring his gift to the LORD from the sacrifice of his peace offerings.
[30] He shall bring the LORD’s offerings made by fire with his own hands.
He shall bring the fat together with the breast.
The breast shall be waved before the LORD as a wave offering.
That means: It is moved back and forth in front of the altar.
[31] The priest shall burn the fat on the altar.
But the breast shall belong to Aaron and his sons.
[32] You shall give the right thigh to the priest as a heave offering from the sacrifices of your peace offerings.
[33] The one among the sons of Aaron who brings the blood of the peace offerings and the fat
shall have the right thigh as his share.
[34] For I have taken the breast that is waved and the thigh that is lifted up
from the people of Israel, from the sacrifices of their peace offerings.
And I have given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons
as their share forever from the people of Israel.’”

[35] This is the holy share of Aaron and the holy share of his sons
from the LORD’s offerings made by fire,
from the day when he brought them near to serve the LORD as priests.
[36] The LORD commanded that the people of Israel give this to them,
from the day that he anointed them.
It is their share forever, for all their generations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever brings an offering should bring the gift “with his own hands.” He should not leave it to others. It is a personal present to God.
> In the “wave offering,” the gift is moved back and forth in front of the altar. In the “heave offering,” it is lifted up. Both show: This gift is meant for God. God then passes it on to the priests.

---

## Summary (Verses 37–38)

[37] This is the law for the burnt offering, the meal offering, the sin offering, the trespass offering, the consecration offering, and the sacrifice of peace offerings,
[38] which the LORD commanded Moses on Mount Sinai,
on the day when he commanded the people of Israel to bring their offerings to the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.
The consecration offering is the offering when the priests are set apart for their service.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the laws for the offerings end (Chapters 1–7). There are five kinds of offerings: the burnt offering, the meal offering, the sin offering, the trespass offering and the peace offering. In addition, there is the “consecration offering” when the priests are set apart. The next chapter tells about that.
