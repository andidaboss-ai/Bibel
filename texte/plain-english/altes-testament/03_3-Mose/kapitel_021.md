# Leviticus – Chapter 21
### Special rules for the priests

---

## Mourning for the dead (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and tell them,
‘A priest shall not make himself unclean for a dead person among his people.
[2] But he may do it for his closest relatives:
for his mother, his father, his son, his daughter, his brother,
[3] and for his sister who is still a virgin and close to him, because she has no husband.
For her he may make himself unclean.
[4] He is a leader among his people.
So he shall not make himself unclean and dishonor himself.

[5] “‘The priests shall not shave their heads bald,
or shave off the edges of their beards,
or make any cuts in their skin.
[6] They shall be holy to their God
and not dishonor the name of their God.
For they offer the LORD’s offerings made by fire, the food of their God.
So they shall be holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever touches a dead person or is in the same room becomes unclean (Numbers 19:11–14). The priests are to avoid this as much as they can, so that they can do their service. Serving God has to do with life, not with death.
> But for his closest relatives a priest may mourn and make himself unclean. God understands grief.
> The sister is named separately when she is not married. Then she still belongs to his family. When she is married, her husband takes care of her burial.
> “The food of their God” is a picture for the offerings, like a meal you prepare for a guest.

---

## The marriage of the priests (Verses 7–9)

[7] “‘They shall not marry a woman who is a prostitute or who has been dishonored.
And a priest shall not marry a woman who is divorced from her husband.
For he is holy to his God.
[8] So you shall treat him as holy, for he offers the food of your God.
He shall be holy to you, for I, the LORD, who make you holy, am holy.
[9] “‘If a priest’s daughter dishonors herself by becoming a prostitute,
she dishonors her father.
She shall be burned with fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Stricter rules apply to priests than to the rest of the people. The closer you serve God, the higher the demands.
> These rules say nothing about the worth of divorced women. Other Israelites were allowed to marry divorced women (Deuteronomy 24:2).
> The punishment in verse 9 is terribly harsh. It shows how closely the honor of the priest’s family was tied to the holy place back then. Here, too: In later Judaism, strict court rules made such punishments almost impossible.

---

## The high priest (Verses 10–15)

[10] “‘The high priest is the one among his brothers
on whose head the anointing oil was poured.
He has been set apart to wear the holy clothes.
He shall not let the hair of his head hang loose, and he shall not tear his clothes.
[11] He must not go near any dead body.
He must not make himself unclean even for his father or his mother.
[12] He shall not leave the holy place,
and he shall not dishonor the holy place of his God.
For the anointing oil of his God is on him like a crown.
I am the LORD.

[13] “‘He shall marry a woman who is still a virgin.
[14] He shall not marry a widow, a divorced woman, a woman who has been dishonored, or a prostitute.
He shall marry a virgin from his own people.
[15] He shall not dishonor his children among his people,
for I am the LORD, who makes him holy.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The strictest rules apply to the high priest. He may not even go to his parents’ funeral. He belongs completely to the holy place.
> That sounds harsh. But it shows: His service for the whole people is so important that he is not to interrupt it, even when he is grieving. Let us remember Aaron, who was not allowed to mourn after the death of his sons (Leviticus 10:6).
> “He shall not leave the holy place” probably means here: He is not to interrupt his service in the holy place to take part in a funeral.

## Priests with physical disabilities (Verses 16–24)

[16] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[17] “Say to Aaron,
‘None of your descendants, in all their generations, who has a physical defect
may come near to offer the food of his God.
[18] For no man who has a defect shall come near:
no one who is blind or lame, no one with a flat nose or any other deformity,
[19] no man with an injured foot or an injured hand,
[20] no one with a hunched back, no one who is very short, no one with a defect in his eye,
no one with an itching rash or scabs,
and no one with damaged testicles.
[21] No man among the descendants of Aaron the priest who has a defect
shall come near to offer the LORD’s offerings made by fire.
Because he has a defect, he shall not come near to offer the food of his God.
[22] He may eat the food of his God,
from the most holy offerings and from the holy offerings.
[23] But he shall not go near the curtain or come near the altar, because he has a defect.
That way he will not dishonor my holy places.
For I am the LORD, who makes them holy.’”
[24] So Moses spoke to Aaron, to his sons, and to all the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a passage that is very hard for us today. People with disabilities may not serve at the altar.
> It must be understood from its own time: The animals for the offerings had to be “without defect” (Leviticus 22). The priests who stood at the altar were to be like that too. It was about an outward picture of God’s perfection.
> But verse 22 is important: A priest with a disability stays a priest. He belongs to the priest family. He may eat from the most holy offerings, which is the holiest food a priest may eat. He is not thrown out. He does not lose his place with God. He only has a different task.
> This rule applied only to serving at the altar. It says nothing about the worth of a person before God. Elsewhere the Bible says: “For man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7).
> Jesus turned especially to the blind, the lame, and the sick. He healed them and invited them to his table (Luke 14:13, 21).
