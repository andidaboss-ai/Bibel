# Leviticus – Chapter 5
### Sin offerings for special cases – the trespass offering

---

## Four cases (Verses 1–4)

[1] “‘If anyone hears a public call to testify and sins in this way:
he is a witness, because he has seen something or knows something,
but he does not report it,
then he shall carry his guilt.

[2] “‘Or if anyone touches anything unclean,
whether it is the dead body of an unclean wild animal,
or the dead body of unclean livestock,
or the dead body of an unclean creeping animal,
and he does not notice it, but he is unclean,
then he shall be guilty.

[3] “‘Or if he touches something unclean from a human being,
whatever the uncleanness is that makes a person unclean,
and he does not notice it,
then he shall be guilty as soon as he knows about it.

[4] “‘Or if anyone swears with his lips without thinking, to do something bad or something good –
whatever a person might swear without thinking –
and he does not notice it,
then he will be guilty in one of these cases as soon as he knows about it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here four cases are named:
> 1. Someone has seen something, but he stays silent in court. Whoever knows the truth and stays silent shares in the guilt.
> 2. and 3. Someone has touched something “unclean” without noticing it. Chapters 11 to 15 explain what “unclean” means. Unclean does not mean dirty or evil. It means: For a while, a person is not ready to come into the holy tent.
> 4. Someone has sworn something carelessly and then forgotten it. Words are important. Later Jesus says: “Let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No’” (Matthew 5:37).

---

## Confessing and bringing an offering (Verses 5–6)

[5] “‘When he is guilty in one of these cases,
he shall confess what he has sinned in.
To confess means: to say openly what you have done wrong.
[6] And he shall bring his trespass offering to the LORD for the sin he has done:
a female animal from the flock, a lamb or a goat, as a sin offering.
So the priest shall make atonement for him because of his sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is something very important: First a person should confess the sin, that is, say openly what he has done wrong. The offering alone is not enough. A person must also honestly face his guilt.
> The New Testament says about God: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous. He forgives us our sins” (1 John 1:9).

---

## For those who are poor (Verses 7–13)

[7] “‘If he cannot afford a lamb,
he shall bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the LORD as his trespass offering for what he has sinned in:
one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.
[8] He shall bring them to the priest.
The priest shall first bring the bird for the sin offering.
He shall twist its head at the neck, but he shall not tear it off completely.
[9] He shall sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar.
The rest of the blood shall be drained out at the bottom of the altar.
It is a sin offering.
[10] He shall bring the second bird as a burnt offering, as the rule says.
So the priest shall make atonement for him because of the sin he has done, and he will be forgiven.

[11] “‘But if he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons either,
he shall bring as his offering for what he has sinned in
about 2 quarts of fine flour as a sin offering.
He shall not pour any oil on it, and he shall not put any frankincense on it.
For it is a sin offering.
[12] He shall bring it to the priest.
The priest shall take a handful of it as the memorial part
and burn it on the altar, on top of the LORD’s offerings made by fire.
It is a sin offering.
[13] So the priest shall make atonement for him because of the sin he has done in any of these cases, and he will be forgiven.
The rest shall belong to the priest, like the meal offering.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: a tenth of an ephah of flour. An ephah is about 6 gallons.
> This is a wonderful rule: No one is too poor for God’s forgiveness. Whoever has no lamb brings doves. Whoever has no doves brings a handful of flour. Forgiveness does not depend on your wallet.
> In the sin offering there is no oil and no frankincense. Oil and frankincense stand for joy and celebration. But the sin offering is about being sorry.

---

## The trespass offering: when you owe God something (Verses 14–16)

[14] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[15] “If anyone is unfaithful and sins without meaning to against the LORD’s holy things,
he shall bring his trespass offering to the LORD:
a ram without any fault from the flock.
A ram is a male sheep.
You shall set its value in shekels of silver, by the shekel of the holy place, as a trespass offering.
[16] He shall pay back what he did wrong with the holy thing.
He shall add a fifth to it and give it to the priest.
So the priest shall make atonement for him with the ram of the trespass offering, and he will be forgiven.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “trespass offering” is for cases where damage has been done that can be made good again. For example: Someone has eaten by mistake some of the gifts that belong to God or to the priests.
> Then an offering is not enough. He must pay for the damage and add one fifth more, that is, 20 percent.
> Forgiveness from God and making things good again belong together.
> A shekel is about 0.4 ounces of silver.

---

## When you did not know it (Verses 17–19)

[17] “If anyone sins and does any of the things that the LORD has commanded not to be done,
even if he did not know it,
he is still guilty and shall carry his guilt.
[18] He shall bring a ram without any fault from the flock, by your estimate, to the priest as a trespass offering.
So the priest shall make atonement for him because of what he did wrong without knowing it, and he will be forgiven.
[19] It is a trespass offering. He is truly guilty before the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Even if you did not know something, you can do damage. We know this today too: If you damage someone else’s car by accident, you still have to pay for it.
> The Bible takes guilt seriously, even guilt that we are not aware of. But it always shows a way to forgiveness too.
