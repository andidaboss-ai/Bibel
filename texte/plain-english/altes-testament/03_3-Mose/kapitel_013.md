# Leviticus – Chapter 13
### Leprosy on the skin and on clothes

---

## The priest examines the skin (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[2] “When a person gets a swelling on his skin, or a scab, or a bright spot,
and it becomes the disease of leprosy on his skin,
he shall be brought to Aaron the priest, or to one of his sons, the priests.
[3] The priest shall examine the sore on the skin.
If the hair in the sore has turned white,
and the sore looks deeper than the skin,
it is the disease of leprosy.
The priest shall examine him and declare him unclean.

[4] If the bright spot on his skin is white,
but it does not look deeper than the skin,
and the hair on it has not turned white,
then the priest shall keep the sick person apart for seven days.
[5] On the seventh day the priest shall examine him.
If, as far as he can see, the sore has stayed the same
and has not spread on the skin,
then the priest shall keep him apart for seven more days.
[6] On the seventh day the priest shall examine him again.
If the sore has faded
and has not spread on the skin,
then the priest shall declare him clean.
It is only a scab.
He shall wash his clothes, and he will be clean.

[7] But if the scab spreads on the skin
after he has shown himself to the priest to be declared clean,
he shall show himself to the priest again.
[8] The priest shall examine him.
If the scab has spread on the skin,
then the priest shall declare him unclean.
It is leprosy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many English Bibles say “leprosy” here. But this does not only mean the illness that we call leprosy today. The Hebrew word “tzaraat” means different skin diseases with spots, scales or open sores. Even mold on clothes and houses is called by this name (from verse 47 and in Chapter 14).
> The priest is not a doctor here. He does not heal. He looks carefully and decides: May this person go on taking part in the community and in worship, or not?
> He decides carefully and not too quickly. If he is not sure, he waits seven days and looks again. Sometimes another seven days. No one should be shut out unfairly.
> “Keep apart” means: The sick person stays by himself for a while, as in a quarantine.

## Old leprosy (Verses 9–17)

[9] “When a person has the disease of leprosy, he shall be brought to the priest.
[10] The priest shall examine him.
If there is a white swelling on the skin,
and it has turned the hair white,
and there is raw flesh in the swelling,
[11] it is an old leprosy on the skin of his body.
The priest shall declare him unclean.
He does not need to keep him apart, because he is already unclean.

[12] “If the leprosy breaks out all over the skin,
and the leprosy covers all the skin of the sick person, from his head to his feet,
as far as the priest can see,
[13] then the priest shall examine him.
If the leprosy has covered his whole body,
he shall declare him clean from the disease.
It has all turned white: He is clean.
[14] But whenever raw flesh can be seen on him, he shall be unclean.
[15] The priest shall examine the raw flesh and declare him unclean.
The raw flesh is unclean. It is leprosy.
[16] But if the raw flesh changes again and turns white,
he shall come to the priest.
[17] The priest shall examine him.
If the sore has turned white,
then the priest shall declare him clean from the disease. He is clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> This sounds strange: Whoever is completely white is clean. Whoever has raw flesh is unclean.
> Probably it means: When the whole skin is white and peeling, the disease is healing. The new skin comes out underneath. But when open, raw flesh can be seen, the disease is still active.

## After a boil (Verses 18–23)

[18] “When someone had a boil on his skin, and it has healed,
[19] and in the place of the boil there is a white swelling,
or a bright spot, reddish-white,
then it shall be shown to the priest.
[20] The priest shall examine it.
If it looks deeper than the skin, and the hair on it has turned white,
then the priest shall declare him unclean.
It is the disease of leprosy. It has broken out in the boil.
A boil is a painful, swollen sore with pus.
[21] But if the priest examines it,
and there are no white hairs on it,
and it is not deeper than the skin, but it is pale,
then the priest shall keep him apart for seven days.
[22] If it spreads on the skin,
then the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a disease.
[23] But if the bright spot stays in its place and has not spread,
it is the scar from the boil.
The priest shall declare him clean.

---

## After a burn (Verses 24–28)

[24] “Or when someone has a burn from fire on his skin,
and the raw flesh of the burn becomes a bright spot, reddish-white or white,
[25] then the priest shall examine it.
If the hair on the bright spot has turned white,
and it looks deeper than the skin,
it is leprosy. It has broken out in the burn.
The priest shall declare him unclean. It is the disease of leprosy.
[26] But if the priest examines it,
and there is no white hair on the bright spot,
and it is not deeper than the skin, but it has faded,
then the priest shall keep him apart for seven days.
[27] On the seventh day the priest shall examine him.
If it has spread on the skin,
then the priest shall declare him unclean. It is the disease of leprosy.
[28] If the bright spot stays in its place
and has not spread on the skin, but it is faded,
it is the swelling from the burn.
The priest shall declare him clean.
For it is the scar from the burn.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priest looks very carefully: Is this only a scar that is healing well? Or is a disease spreading? The most important sign is always: Is it spreading or not?

## On the head or in the beard (Verses 29–37)

[29] “When a man or a woman has a sore on the head or in the beard,
[30] then the priest shall examine the sore.
If it looks deeper than the skin,
and the hair on it is yellow and thin,
then the priest shall declare him unclean.
It is an itch, a leprosy of the head or of the beard.

[31] If the priest examines the sore with the itch,
and it does not look deeper than the skin,
and there is no black hair on it,
then the priest shall keep the person with the itch apart for seven days.
[32] On the seventh day the priest shall examine the sore.
If the itch has not spread,
and there is no yellow hair on it,
and the itch does not look deeper than the skin,
[33] then the person shall shave himself, but he shall not shave the place with the itch.
And the priest shall keep the person with the itch apart for seven more days.
[34] On the seventh day the priest shall examine the itch.
If the itch has not spread on the skin,
and it does not look deeper than the skin,
then the priest shall declare him clean.
He shall wash his clothes, and he will be clean.

[35] But if the itch spreads on the skin after he has been declared clean,
[36] then the priest shall examine him.
If the itch has spread on the skin,
the priest does not need to look for the yellow hair. He is unclean.
[37] But if, as far as he can see, the itch has stayed the same,
and black hair has grown on it,
then the itch is healed. He is clean.
The priest shall declare him clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Itch” here means an itching, scaly rash on the skin of the head.
> When normal, black hair grows again, it is a sign of healing.

## Harmless spots and bald heads (Verses 38–44)

[38] “When a man or a woman has bright spots on the skin, white bright spots,
[39] then the priest shall examine them.
If the spots on the skin are a dull white,
it is a harmless rash that has broken out on the skin.
He is clean.

[40] “If a man’s hair has fallen out from his head, he is bald. He is clean.
[41] If his hair has fallen out from the front of his head, his forehead is bald. He is clean.
[42] But if there is a reddish-white sore on the bald head or on the bald forehead,
it is leprosy breaking out on his bald head or on his bald forehead.
[43] Then the priest shall examine him.
If the swelling of the sore on his bald head or on his bald forehead is reddish-white,
and it looks like leprosy on the skin,
[44] he has leprosy. He is unclean.
The priest shall certainly declare him unclean.
The disease is on his head.

> **What does this mean?**
> Being bald is not a disease. Whoever is bald is clean. Here the Bible protects people from being shut out because of something harmless.

---

## How a person with leprosy must live (Verses 45–46)

[45] “The person with leprosy who has the disease shall wear torn clothes.
He shall let the hair of his head hang loose.
He shall cover his upper lip and call out, ‘Unclean! Unclean!’
[46] As long as he has the disease, he shall be unclean.
He is unclean. He shall live alone.
He shall live outside the camp.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is very hard. The person with leprosy must wear the signs of mourning: torn clothes, loose hair, a covered mouth. He must warn others and live alone outside the camp.
> This protected the community from infection. But for the sick person, it meant loneliness and great suffering.
> That makes what Jesus does later all the more moving: He touches people with leprosy and heals them (Mark 1:40–42). He is not afraid of them. He brings them back into the community.
> Important: The Bible does not say that the disease is a punishment for sin. Today, too, we should not shut out or judge people who are sick.

## Leprosy on clothes (Verses 47–59)

[47] “If the disease of leprosy is on a piece of clothing,
whether it is clothing of wool or clothing of linen,
[48] in the threads that run lengthwise or in the threads that run across, of linen or of wool,
or on leather, or on anything made of leather,
[49] and if the spot is greenish or reddish,
on the clothing or on the leather, in the lengthwise threads or in the crosswise threads,
or on anything made of leather,
it is the disease of leprosy.
It shall be shown to the priest.

[50] The priest shall examine the spot
and keep the thing that has the spot apart for seven days.
[51] On the seventh day he shall examine the spot.
If the spot has spread,
on the clothing, in the lengthwise threads or in the crosswise threads, or on the leather,
whatever the leather is used for,
then the spot is a destructive mildew. It is unclean.
Mildew is a kind of mold.
[52] He shall burn the clothing,
the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads, of wool or of linen,
or anything of leather that has the spot on it.
For it is a destructive mildew.
It shall be burned in the fire.

[53] “If the priest examines it,
and the spot has not spread,
on the clothing, in the lengthwise threads or in the crosswise threads, or on anything of leather,
[54] then the priest shall command that they wash the thing that has the spot on it.
And he shall keep it apart for seven more days.
[55] Then the priest shall examine it after the spot has been washed.
If the spot has not changed its color,
even if the spot has not spread,
it is unclean. You shall burn it in the fire.
It is a spot of mildew, whether the worn place is on the inside or on the outside.

[56] If the priest looks,
and the spot has faded after it was washed,
then he shall tear it out:
out of the clothing, or out of the leather, or out of the lengthwise threads, or out of the crosswise threads.
[57] If it then appears again on the clothing,
in the lengthwise threads or in the crosswise threads, or on anything of leather,
it is spreading.
You shall burn the thing that has the spot on it with fire.
[58] But the clothing, the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads, or whatever is made of leather,
that you have washed, and the spot has gone away from it,
shall be washed a second time. Then it will be clean.”

[59] This is the law about the disease of mildew
on clothing of wool or of linen,
in the lengthwise threads or in the crosswise threads, or on anything of leather,
to declare it clean or to declare it unclean.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “lengthwise threads” and the “crosswise threads” are the threads that cloth is woven from. Maybe this also means yarn that has not been woven yet.
> This is about mold or fungus on cloth and leather. For this, too, the Bible uses the same word as for leprosy.
> Here, too, everything is checked carefully: First the thing is washed, and they wait. Only if the mildew does not go away is the clothing burned. Clothes were very valuable back then. People did not throw them away carelessly.
