# Leviticus – Chapter 14
### The cleansing after leprosy – mildew in houses

---

## The healed person is cleansed: the two birds (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “This shall be the law for the person with leprosy on the day he is cleansed:
He shall be brought to the priest.
[3] The priest shall go out of the camp.
The priest shall examine him.
If the person with leprosy is healed from the disease of leprosy,
[4] then the priest shall command them to take for the one who is to be cleansed
two living clean birds,
and cedar wood, scarlet cloth and hyssop.
Scarlet is a bright red color.
[5] The priest shall command them to kill one of the birds
in a clay pot over running water.
[6] He shall take the living bird,
and the cedar wood, the scarlet cloth and the hyssop.
He shall dip them, together with the living bird, in the blood of the bird
that was killed over the running water.
[7] He shall sprinkle seven times on the one who is to be cleansed from the leprosy,
and declare him clean.
And he shall let the living bird fly away into the open field.

> **What does this mean?**
> God heals. The priest only makes sure that the healing has happened. He goes out of the camp to the sick person. He does not wait until the sick person comes to him.
> “Running water” is fresh, living water from a spring or a stream, not water that stands still.
> Hyssop is a small plant with many branches. It was good for sprinkling. Cedar wood is a wood that does not rot. The red cloth is a reminder of blood, that is, of life.
> One bird dies, the other flies away free. This is a strong picture: The disease and the uncleanness are carried away. The person is free and can live a new life.
> Jesus said to people with leprosy whom he healed: “Go and show yourselves to the priests” (Luke 17:14). In this way, their healing was to be officially confirmed, following exactly this law.

---

## Washing, shaving and waiting seven days (Verses 8–9)

[8] “The one who is to be cleansed shall wash his clothes,
shave off all his hair and bathe himself in water.
Then he will be clean.
After that he may come into the camp.
But for seven days he shall live outside his tent.
[9] On the seventh day he shall shave off all his hair:
the hair on his head, his beard and his eyebrows.
He shall shave off all his hair.
He shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water.
Then he will be clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> The healed person starts completely new, almost like a newborn child without hair. He comes back step by step: first into the camp, and after seven days into his own tent.

---

## The offerings on the eighth day (Verses 10–20)

[10] “On the eighth day he shall take two male lambs without any fault,
one female lamb one year old without any fault,
about 7 quarts of fine flour mixed with oil as a meal offering,
and about two-thirds of a pint of oil.
[11] The priest who cleanses him shall set the man who is to be cleansed, and these things,
before the LORD, at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

[12] “The priest shall take one of the male lambs
and bring it as a trespass offering, together with the oil.
He shall wave them before the LORD as a wave offering.
[13] He shall kill the male lamb in the place where they kill the sin offering and the burnt offering, in the holy place.
For just as the sin offering belongs to the priest, so does the trespass offering.
It is most holy.
[14] The priest shall take some of the blood of the trespass offering.
The priest shall put it on the tip of the right ear of the one who is to be cleansed,
on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot.

[15] The priest shall take some of the oil
and pour it into the palm of his own left hand.
[16] The priest shall dip his right finger in the oil that is in his left hand.
And with his finger he shall sprinkle some of the oil seven times before the LORD.
[17] From the rest of the oil in his hand, the priest shall put some
on the tip of the right ear of the one who is to be cleansed,
on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot,
on top of the blood of the trespass offering.
[18] The rest of the oil in the priest’s hand he shall put on the head of the one who is to be cleansed.
So the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD.

[19] “The priest shall bring the sin offering
and make atonement for the one who is to be cleansed from his uncleanness.
After that he shall kill the burnt offering.
[20] Then the priest shall bring the burnt offering and the meal offering on the altar.
So the priest shall make atonement for him, and he will be clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible gives the measures like this: three tenths of an ephah of flour and one log of oil. A log is about two-thirds of a pint.
> Blood and oil on the ear, the hand and the foot – this is exactly how the priests were set apart too (Leviticus 8:23–24). The healed person is treated like a priest. That is a great honor. Whoever was shut out for a long time is now welcomed back with a ceremony. He fully belongs again, to God and to the people.

## When the healed person is poor (Verses 21–32)

[21] “If he is poor and cannot afford so much,
he shall take one male lamb as a trespass offering, to be waved, to make atonement for him,
and about 2 quarts of fine flour mixed with oil as a meal offering,
and about two-thirds of a pint of oil,
[22] and two turtledoves or two young pigeons, whatever he can afford.
One shall be a sin offering and the other a burnt offering.

[23] “On the eighth day he shall bring them for his cleansing to the priest,
to the door of the Tent of Meeting, before the LORD.
[24] The priest shall take the lamb of the trespass offering and the oil.
And the priest shall wave them before the LORD as a wave offering.
[25] He shall kill the lamb of the trespass offering.
The priest shall take some of the blood of the trespass offering
and put it on the tip of the right ear of the one who is to be cleansed,
on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot.
[26] The priest shall pour some of the oil into the palm of his own left hand.
[27] And with his right finger the priest shall sprinkle some of the oil that is in his left hand
seven times before the LORD.
[28] Then the priest shall put some of the oil that is in his hand
on the tip of the right ear of the one who is to be cleansed,
on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot,
on the place where the blood of the trespass offering is.
[29] The rest of the oil in the priest’s hand he shall put on the head of the one who is to be cleansed,
to make atonement for him before the LORD.
[30] He shall bring one of the turtledoves or of the young pigeons, whichever he can afford,
[31] of the kind he can afford:
the one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, together with the meal offering.
So the priest shall make atonement before the LORD for the one who is to be cleansed.”
[32] This is the law for the person who had the disease of leprosy
and who cannot afford the offering for his cleansing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever was sick for a long time often could not work and was poor. That is why there is a rule for poor people: Instead of three lambs, one lamb and two doves are enough.
> But the steps are exactly the same. The poor person is welcomed back with just as much honor as the rich person.

## Mildew in houses (Verses 33–47)

[33] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[34] “When you have come into the land of Canaan, which I give to you as your own,
and I put a spreading mildew in a house in the land you own,
[35] then the owner of the house shall come and tell the priest,
‘It seems to me that there is some kind of disease in the house.’
[36] The priest shall command that they empty the house
before the priest goes in to examine the disease.
That way nothing in the house will become unclean.
After that the priest shall go in to examine the house.

[37] He shall examine the disease.
If the disease is in the walls of the house,
with hollow streaks, greenish or reddish,
and it looks deeper than the wall,
[38] then the priest shall go out of the house to the door of the house
and shut up the house for seven days.
[39] On the seventh day the priest shall come again and look.
If the disease has spread in the walls of the house,
[40] then the priest shall command that they take out the stones that have the disease in them,
and throw them into an unclean place outside the city.
[41] He shall have the inside of the house scraped all over.
They shall pour out the plaster that they scraped off into an unclean place outside the city.
[42] They shall take other stones and put them in the place of those stones.
And he shall take other plaster and plaster the house.

[43] “If the disease comes back and breaks out in the house
after he has taken out the stones,
and after he has scraped the house, and after it was plastered,
[44] then the priest shall come in and look.
If the disease has spread in the house,
it is a destructive mildew in the house. It is unclean.
[45] He shall tear down the house, its stones, its wood and all the plaster of the house.
And he shall carry them out of the city into an unclean place.

[46] “Also, whoever goes into the house while it is shut up shall be unclean until the evening.
[47] Whoever lies down in the house shall wash his clothes.
And whoever eats in the house shall wash his clothes.

> **What does this mean?**
> This rule only applies to the time when Israel lives in solid houses in the land of Canaan. In the wilderness, they lived in tents.
> “I put a spreading mildew in a house” – in the Bible, everything in the end comes from God’s hand, even mildew. But this does not mean that the owner has done something evil.
> The priest has the house emptied first. That way the owner does not have to lose his things. The Bible thinks very practically here.
> Here, too, things are repaired first: Stones are replaced, and the walls are plastered again. Only if that does not help is the house torn down. This is still a good rule today for dangerous mold.

---

## The cleansing of the house (Verses 48–53)

[48] “If the priest comes in and examines it,
and the disease has not spread in the house after the house was plastered,
then the priest shall declare the house clean.
For the disease is healed.
[49] To cleanse the house, he shall take two birds, cedar wood, scarlet cloth and hyssop.
[50] He shall kill one of the birds in a clay pot over running water.
[51] He shall take the cedar wood, the hyssop, the scarlet cloth and the living bird.
He shall dip them in the blood of the bird that was killed, and in the running water.
And he shall sprinkle the house seven times.
[52] So he shall cleanse the house
with the blood of the bird and with the running water,
with the living bird, with the cedar wood, with the hyssop and with the scarlet cloth.
[53] But he shall let the living bird fly away out of the city into the open field.
So he shall make atonement for the house, and it will be clean.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A house is cleansed in the same way as a person, with the two birds. A house, too, can get a new beginning.

---

## Summary (Verses 54–57)

[54] This is the law for every disease of leprosy and for an itch,
[55] for the destructive mildew on clothing and on a house,
[56] for a swelling, for a scab and for a bright spot,
[57] to teach when something is unclean and when it is clean.
This is the law about leprosy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 13 and 14 belong together. They show: God cares about the health of his people, about their bodies, their clothes and their homes.
> And they show: Whoever had to be shut out should not stay shut out forever. There is a way back into the community.
