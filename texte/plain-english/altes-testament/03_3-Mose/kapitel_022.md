# Leviticus – Chapter 22
### The holy gifts and the animals for the offerings

---

## When a priest is unclean (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Tell Aaron and his sons to be careful with the holy gifts of the people of Israel,
which they set apart for me.
They shall not dishonor my holy name.
I am the LORD.
[3] Tell them,
‘One of all your descendants, in all your generations,
might come near the holy gifts that the people of Israel set apart for the LORD
while he is unclean.
Then that person shall be cut off from my presence.
I am the LORD.

[4] “‘One of the descendants of Aaron might have leprosy or a discharge from his body.
He shall not eat from the holy gifts until he is clean.
Someone might touch anything that has become unclean through a dead person,
or a man might have an emission of semen,
[5] or someone might touch a small creeping animal that makes him unclean,
or a person who makes him unclean, whatever kind of uncleanness it is.
[6] Whoever touches anything like this is unclean until the evening.
He shall not eat from the holy gifts unless he has bathed his body in water.
[7] When the sun has gone down, he is clean.
After that he may eat from the holy gifts, because that is his food.
[8] He shall not eat an animal that died by itself or was torn by wild animals,
and so make himself unclean.
I am the LORD.
[9] “‘So they shall keep my command.
Otherwise they will carry sin for it and die because of it, because they dishonored what is holy.
I am the LORD, who makes them holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests lived from the offerings. But this applies to them too: Whoever is unclean may not eat from the holy gifts. He must wait until he is clean again.
> “Because that is his food” – the offerings are the daily bread of the priests. God provides for them. But they are to handle the offerings with deep respect.

---

## Who may eat from the holy gifts (Verses 10–16)

[10] “‘No outsider may eat from the holy gifts.
A foreigner who lives with a priest, or a hired worker, shall not eat from the holy gifts.
[11] But if a priest buys a slave with his money, the slave may eat from them.
And those who are born in his house may eat from his food.
[12] If a priest’s daughter marries a man who is not a priest,
she shall not eat from the heave offering of the holy gifts.
[13] But a priest’s daughter might become a widow or be divorced and have no child.
She might return to her father’s house, as when she was young.
Then she may eat from her father’s food.
But no outsider shall eat any of it.

[14] “‘If someone eats something holy by mistake,
he shall add one fifth of its value
and give the holy thing back to the priest.
[15] The priests shall not dishonor the holy gifts that the people of Israel bring to the LORD.
[16] Otherwise they will bring guilt on the people when they eat their holy gifts.
For I am the LORD, who makes them holy.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> “An outsider” here means: someone who does not belong to the priest’s family.
> A slave whom a priest has bought belongs to the family and may eat too. A hired worker or a guest who is there only for a short time may not. In the world back then, slaves were part of the household. There is more about this in the explanation of Exodus 21.
> A daughter who marries a man then belongs to his family. But if she becomes a widow and comes back without children, she is provided for again. That way she does not fall into poverty. This protects women who have no one to provide for them.

## Only animals without defect (Verses 17–25)

[17] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[18] “Speak to Aaron, to his sons, and to all the people of Israel, and tell them,
‘Someone from the house of Israel, or one of the foreigners in Israel, might bring his offering.
It may be because he made a promise to God, or it may be a free-will gift.
He brings it to the LORD as a burnt offering.
[19] So that you are accepted, you shall bring a male animal without defect,
from the bulls, the sheep, or the goats.
[20] But you shall not offer anything that has a defect.
For it will not be accepted for you.

[21] “‘Someone might bring a peace offering to the LORD to keep a promise,
or as a free-will gift from the cattle or from the sheep and goats.
Then it must be perfect to be accepted.
It shall have no defect.
[22] You shall not offer to the LORD anything that is blind,
injured, or maimed,
or that has a wart, is festering, or has an open sore.
You shall not put any of these on the altar as an offering by fire to the LORD.
[23] A bull or a lamb that has a deformity or is missing a body part
you may offer as a free-will gift.
But it will not be accepted to keep a promise.
[24] You shall not offer to the LORD an animal whose testicles are bruised, crushed, torn, or cut.
You shall not do this in your land.
[25] You shall also not take any of these animals from a foreigner and offer them as the food of your God.
For they are spoiled. They have a defect.
They will not be accepted for you.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> You are to give God the best, not what you no longer need yourself. Offering a sick or injured animal would be like a gift from the trash.
> Later the prophet Malachi complained that people offered blind and sick animals. He asked: “Try giving it to your governor! Will he be pleased with you?” (Malachi 1:8).
> Foreigners may also bring offerings. God’s altar is open to everyone.

## Care for the animals (Verses 26–28)

[26] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[27] “When a bull, a sheep, or a goat is born,
it shall stay with its mother for seven days.
From the eighth day on, it will be accepted as an offering by fire to the LORD.
[28] You shall not kill a cow or a ewe on the same day as its young.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 28: A ewe is a female sheep.
> Here the Bible shows compassion for the animals. A newborn animal is to be allowed to stay with its mother for a week. And mother and young are not to be killed on the same day.
> Animals are also creatures of God. They are not to be treated cruelly, even when people eat them or offer them.

---

## The thank offering and the end (Verses 29–33)

[29] “When you bring a thank offering to the LORD, you shall offer it so that you are accepted.
[30] It shall be eaten on the same day.
You shall leave none of it until the morning.
I am the LORD.
[31] “So you shall keep my commandments and do them.
I am the LORD.
[32] You shall not dishonor my holy name.
But I will be treated as holy among the people of Israel.
I am the LORD, who makes you holy,
[33] who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God.
I am the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “But I will be treated as holy among the people of Israel.” When people live in a good and fair way, God’s name is honored. When they do evil, his name is “dishonored,” that is, dragged through the mud.
> In Judaism, when someone brings honor to God by the way they act, it is called “Kiddush Hashem,” the making holy of the Name. In the Lord’s Prayer, Christians pray: “may your name be kept holy” (Matthew 6:9).
