# Daniel – Chapter 1
### Faithful in a foreign land

---

## Before we begin: Who was Daniel?

The book of Daniel tells about a young man from Judah. He was taken away to Babylon and lived there at the king’s court. So he lived at the same time as Ezekiel and Jeremiah.
His name means “God is my judge.”
The book has 12 chapters and two parts:
– Chapters 1–6: Stories about Daniel and his three friends. They stay faithful to God, even when that is dangerous: in the fiery furnace, in the lions’ den.
– Chapters 7–12: Visions about the kingdoms of the world and the coming kingdom of God.
A special feature: Part of the book (Daniel 2:4 to 7:28) is not written in Hebrew but in Aramaic. Aramaic was the international language of that time.
When was the book written? According to tradition, Daniel himself wrote it, in the 6th century before Christ. Many scholars think that it got its present form only in the 2nd century before Christ. At that time, King Antiochus IV persecuted the Jews and wanted to forbid their faith. Then the stories of Daniel gave courage to the people who were persecuted. Believers hold both views.
In the Jewish Bible, Daniel is not among the Prophets but among the “Writings.” In Christian Bibles, it is among the Prophets. Jesus calls Daniel a prophet (Matthew 24:15). And he calls himself the “Son of Man,” after Daniel 7:13.
The basic message: The powerful people of the world may seem great and threatening. But God is the true Lord of history. His kingdom will last.

## Taken away to Babylon (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the third year that Jehoiakim was king of Judah,
Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.
He put the city under siege.
That means: His army surrounded the city.
[2] The Lord handed Jehoiakim, the king of Judah, over to him.
He also handed over some of the objects from the house of God.
Nebuchadnezzar carried them away to the land of Shinar,
to the house of his god.
He put the objects into the treasure house of his god.
[3] The king spoke to Ashpenaz, the chief of his eunuchs.
He told him to bring some of the people of Israel,
from the royal family and from the noble families.
[4] They were to be young men without any defect,
good-looking,
skillful in all kinds of wisdom,
gifted with knowledge,
with understanding for learning,
young men who were able to serve in the king’s palace.
Ashpenaz was to teach them the writing and the language of the Chaldeans.
[5] The king gave them a daily share
of the king’s fine food
and of the wine that he drank.
They were to be trained for three years.
After that, they were to serve the king.
[6] Among them were these men from Judah:
Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
[7] The chief of the eunuchs gave them new names:
He called Daniel Belteshazzar,
Hananiah he called Shadrach,
Mishael he called Meshach,
and Azariah he called Abednego.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Nebuchadnezzar conquers Jerusalem and takes objects from the temple with him. He puts them in the temple of his own god. That was meant to show: The god of Babylon is stronger. But the text says: “The Lord handed Jehoiakim, the king of Judah, over to him.” God is in control, even in defeat.
> The “third year of Jehoiakim” would be about 606/605 before Christ. Scholars disagree about the exact date of this first deportation.
> “Shinar” is an old name for Babylonia (compare Genesis 11:2, the tower of Babel).
> Verses 3–5: The young men from noble families are to be retrained as Babylonian officials: a new language, a new education, new food. The WEB calls Ashpenaz the “master of his eunuchs.” Eunuchs were court officials. Often they had been castrated. The text does not say whether Daniel and his friends were also made eunuchs.
> Verse 7: Even their names are changed. Their Hebrew names reminded people of God (for example, “Daniel” = “God is my judge,” “Hananiah” = “The LORD is gracious”). The new names remind people of Babylonian gods. Someone wanted to wipe out who they were.

## Daniel does not want to make himself unclean (Verses 8–16)

[8] But Daniel decided in his heart
that he would not make himself unclean
with the king’s fine food
or with the wine that he drank.
Unclean means: not fit to come before God.
So Daniel asked the chief of the eunuchs
to let him not make himself unclean.
[9] God made the chief of the eunuchs
kind to Daniel and full of compassion for him.
[10] The chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel,
“I am afraid of my lord the king.
He has decided what you eat and what you drink.
What if he sees that your faces look worse
than the faces of the young men of your own age?
Then you would put my head in danger with the king.”
[11] Then Daniel spoke to the guard
whom the chief of the eunuchs had put in charge of Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
[12] Daniel said,
“Please test your servants for ten days.
Let them give us vegetables to eat and water to drink.
[13] Then look at our faces
and at the faces of the young men
who eat the king’s fine food.
Then do with your servants what you think is right.”
[14] So the guard listened to them in this matter.
He tested them for ten days.
[15] At the end of the ten days,
their faces looked better and healthier.
They were better fed
than all the young men who ate the king’s fine food.
[16] So the guard took away their fine food
and the wine they were supposed to drink.
And he gave them vegetables.

> **What does this mean?**
> Daniel does not want to eat the king’s food. Why? Probably because the meat was not prepared according to the Jewish food laws, or because it had first been offered to idols. Daniel wanted to stay faithful to God, even in a foreign land.
> It is remarkable how Daniel does this: He does not rebel loudly. He asks politely and suggests a fair test. He looks for a solution that is also good for the guard.
> Verse 9: “God made the chief of the eunuchs kind to Daniel.” God works in the background.
> Verse 15: After ten days of vegetables and water, the four look healthier than the others. This is not a general rule for healthy eating. It shows: God blesses faithfulness.
> For today: We too can live in a place where people have other values. Daniel shows how to stay true to what you believe without looking down on others.

## Ten times wiser (Verses 17–21)

[17] God gave these four young men knowledge and skill
in all kinds of learning and wisdom.
And Daniel could understand all visions and dreams.
[18] The king had set a time for bringing them in.
At the end of that time,
the chief of the eunuchs brought them before Nebuchadnezzar.
[19] The king talked with them.
Among them all, there was no one like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
So they began to serve the king.
[20] The king asked them about many things
that needed wisdom and understanding.
In every matter, he found them ten times better
than all the magicians and enchanters in his whole kingdom.
Enchanters are people who try to use magic words.
[21] Daniel stayed there until the first year of King Cyrus.

> **What does this mean?**
> The four learn all the knowledge of Babylon, and they are excellent at it. They do not refuse the foreign culture. They learn and work along with others, but they do not give up their faith.
> Verse 20: “Ten times better” – God gives them wisdom that is greater than the wisdom of the Babylonian magicians.
> Verse 21: Daniel lives longer than the whole Babylonian Empire. He is still there when the Persian king Cyrus conquers Babylon (539 before Christ) and allows the Jews to return home. Kings come and go, but God’s faithful servant remains.
