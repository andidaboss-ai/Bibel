# Daniel – Chapter 5
### The writing on the wall

---

## Belshazzar’s feast (Verses 1–4)

[1] King Belshazzar gave a great feast for a thousand of his nobles.
He drank wine in front of the thousand.
[2] While Belshazzar was tasting the wine,
he gave an order to bring the gold and silver cups and bowls
that his father Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem.
He wanted the king and his nobles,
his wives and his concubines to drink from them.
Concubines are women who live with a man like wives, but with fewer rights.
[3] So they brought the gold cups and bowls
that had been taken from the temple, the house of God in Jerusalem.
And the king and his nobles,
his wives and his concubines drank from them.
[4] They drank wine
and praised the gods made of gold and silver,
of bronze, iron, wood, and stone.

> **What does this mean?**
> Belshazzar was the last king of Babylon. He holds a great feast, even though (as we know from other sources) the Persians were already outside the city.
> Verse 2: The text calls Nebuchadnezzar Belshazzar’s “father.” According to Babylonian inscriptions, Belshazzar was the son of King Nabonidus and ruled in Babylon in his place. But in the Bible, “father” can also mean “the king before him” or “ancestor.”
> Verses 3–4: Belshazzar drinks from the holy cups of the temple and praises idols while he does it. He is mocking the God of Israel on purpose.

---

## The hand writes on the wall (Verses 5–9)

[5] At that very hour, the fingers of a human hand appeared.
They wrote on the plaster of the wall of the king’s palace,
near the lampstand.
And the king saw the part of the hand that was writing.
[6] Then the king’s face turned pale.
His thoughts frightened him.
His legs became weak,
and his knees knocked together.
[7] The king shouted
for them to bring in the enchanters, the Chaldeans, and the fortune-tellers.
The king said to the wise men of Babylon,
“Whoever reads this writing and tells me what it means
will be dressed in purple
and will wear a gold chain around his neck.
He will be the third ruler in the kingdom.”
Purple clothes were a sign of high honor.
[8] Then all the king’s wise men came in.
But they could not read the writing,
and they could not tell the king what it means.
[9] Then King Belshazzar was very frightened.
His face turned pale,
and his nobles did not know what to do.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the feast, a hand appears and writes on the wall. Suddenly the reckless king turns pale and shakes.
> To this day, people say “the writing is on the wall” when they mean a clear warning sign.
> Verse 7: “The third ruler in the kingdom” – this fits history exactly: Nabonidus was the first, Belshazzar the second. The third place was the highest place that Belshazzar could give away.

---

## The queen reminds the king of Daniel (Verses 10–16)

[10] The queen heard what the king and his nobles were saying.
So she came into the banquet hall.
The queen said,
“O king, live forever!
Do not let your thoughts frighten you.
Do not let your face turn pale.
[11] There is a man in your kingdom
who has the spirit of the holy gods in him.
In the days of your father,
he was found to have light, understanding, and wisdom, like the wisdom of the gods.
King Nebuchadnezzar, your father,
yes, the king, your father,
made him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and fortune-tellers.
[12] For this Daniel,
whom the king named Belteshazzar,
was found to have an excellent spirit,
knowledge, and understanding.
He could explain dreams,
make riddles clear,
and solve hard problems.
Now call Daniel,
and he will tell you what it means.”
[13] Then Daniel was brought to the king.
The king said to Daniel,
“Are you that Daniel, one of the captives from Judah
whom the king, my father, brought from Judah?
[14] I have heard about you,
that the spirit of the gods is in you,
and that you have light, understanding, and excellent wisdom.
[15] The wise men, the enchanters, have just been brought to me
to read this writing and tell me what it means.
But they could not tell me what the matter means.
[16] But I have heard about you,
that you can explain things and solve hard problems.
If you can read the writing and tell me what it means,
you will be dressed in purple
and will wear a gold chain around your neck.
You will be the third ruler in the kingdom.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The “queen” is probably the queen mother. She remembers Daniel, whom the young king had obviously forgotten. By now, Daniel is an old man, over 80 years old.
> A wise woman saves the situation. She keeps a clear head while the men do not know what to do.
> Verse 12: “Solve hard problems” means: answer difficult questions and riddles. The WEB says “dissolving of doubts.”

## Daniel preaches to the king (Verses 17–24)

[17] Then Daniel answered the king,
“Keep your gifts for yourself,
and give your rewards to someone else.
But I will read the writing to the king
and tell him what it means.
[18] “O king, the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar, your father,
the kingdom, greatness, glory, and majesty.
[19] Because of the greatness that God gave him,
all the peoples, nations, and people of every language
trembled with fear before him.
He killed whomever he wanted to kill,
and he let live whomever he wanted to let live.
He raised up whomever he wanted to raise up,
and he brought down whomever he wanted to bring down.
[20] But his heart became proud,
and his spirit became hard, so that he acted with pride.
Then he was removed from his royal throne,
and his glory was taken away from him.
[21] He was driven away from people.
His heart was made like the heart of an animal.
He lived with the wild donkeys.
He was fed grass like cattle.
His body got wet with the dew of the sky,
until he knew that the Most High God rules over the kingdoms of human beings
and puts over them whomever he wants.
[22] “But you, his son Belshazzar,
have not humbled your heart,
even though you knew all this.
[23] Instead, you have set yourself up against the Lord of heaven.
The cups and bowls of his house were brought to you.
You and your nobles, your wives and your concubines
drank wine from them.
You praised the gods made of silver and gold,
of bronze, iron, wood, and stone.
They cannot see or hear or know anything.
But you have not honored the God
who holds your breath in his hand
and to whom all your ways belong.
[24] That is why he sent the hand,
and this writing was written.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Daniel does not want any gifts. He cannot be bought.
> Verses 18–21: Daniel reminds the king of the story of Nebuchadnezzar in Chapter 4: Even the greatest king was humbled until he accepted God.
> Verse 22: The heaviest accusation: “But you, his son Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, even though you knew all this.” Belshazzar knew the story, but he learned nothing from it.
> Verse 23: A strong contrast: Belshazzar praises gods that “cannot see or hear or know anything.” But he does not honor the God “who holds your breath in his hand.” Every breath is a gift from God.

## Mene, mene, tekel, upharsin (Verses 25–31)

[25] “This is the writing that was written:
‘MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN.’
[26] “This is what it means:
MENE: God has counted the days of your kingdom
and brought it to an end.
[27] TEKEL: You have been weighed on the scales
and found too light.
[28] PERES: Your kingdom is divided
and given to the Medes and Persians.”
[29] Then Belshazzar gave an order.
They dressed Daniel in purple
and put a gold chain around his neck.
And they announced
that he would be the third ruler in the kingdom.
[30] That same night, Belshazzar, the Chaldean king, was killed.
[31] And Darius the Mede received the kingdom.
He was about sixty-two years old.

> **What does this mean?**
> The four words are Aramaic names of weights (mina, shekel, half a mina). At the same time, they sound like verbs:
> – MENE = “counted”: Your days as king are counted.
> – TEKEL = “weighed”: You have been weighed and found too light.
> – PERES (the singular of “parsin”; the “u” in “upharsin” means “and”) = “divided,” and it sounds like “Paras” = Persia.
> Verse 30: That same night, Belshazzar dies. In the year 539 before Christ, the Persians under Cyrus conquered Babylon. Greek historians report that the city was taken during a feast.
> Verse 31: To this day, it is not clear who “Darius the Mede” was exactly. He is not known by this name in other sources. Some think of a governor under Cyrus (for example, Gubaru). Others think it is another name for Cyrus himself.
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, verse 31 is already the first verse of Chapter 6.
> The message: Every person, every ruler too, will one day be “weighed.” What counts in the end? Not power and wealth, but whether we have honored God and done what is right.
