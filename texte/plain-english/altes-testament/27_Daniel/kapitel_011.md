# Daniel – Chapter 11
### The kings of the north and of the south

---

## From Persia to Alexander (Verses 1–4)

[1] “As for me, in the first year of Darius the Mede,
I stood up to support him and to make him strong.
[2] “Now I will show you the truth.
Three more kings will rise up in Persia.
The fourth will be much richer than all of them.
When he has become strong through his riches,
he will stir up everyone against the kingdom of Greece.
[3] Then a mighty king will rise up.
He will rule with great power
and do whatever he wants.
[4] But as soon as he has risen up,
his kingdom will be broken.
It will be divided toward the four winds of the sky.
But it will not go to his descendants.
And it will not have the power with which he ruled.
For his kingdom will be pulled up by the roots,
and it will go to others besides these.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapter 11 is the most detailed picture of the future in the Bible. It describes the history from about 530 to 165 before Christ in coded language, but amazingly exactly.
> Verse 2: The Persian kings. The rich fourth king is probably Xerxes I, who went to war against Greece in 480 before Christ.
> Verses 3–4: The mighty king is Alexander the Great. After his early death, his empire was divided among his generals, not given to his sons.
> Some scholars date the book to the 2nd century before Christ. For them, Chapter 11 is history looking back, written in the style of a prophecy. For readers who date it to the 6th century, it is an exact prediction. Both views are held.

---

## Wars between north and south (Verses 5–20)

[5] “The king of the south will become strong.
But one of his princes will become stronger than he is.
He will rule,
and his rule will be a great rule.
[6] After some years, they will become allies.
The daughter of the king of the south
will come to the king of the north to make an agreement.
But she will not keep her power.
He will not last either, and neither will his power.
She will be handed over,
together with those who brought her,
and the one who became her father,
and the one who supported her in those times.
[7] “But a branch from her family roots
will rise up in his place.
He will march against the army.
He will go into the fortress of the king of the north.
He will fight against them and win.
[8] He will also carry their gods off to Egypt,
together with their metal idols
and their valuable objects of silver and gold.
For some years, he will leave the king of the north alone.
[9] Then the king of the north will come into the kingdom of the king of the south,
but he will go back to his own land.
[10] His sons will go to war.
They will gather a huge number of large armies.
These armies will come on like a flood and sweep through.
They will come back and make war
all the way to his fortress.
[11] “The king of the south will become very angry.
He will go out and fight against him,
against the king of the north.
The king of the north will send out a huge army,
but that army will be handed over to the king of the south.
[12] The army will be carried off,
and the heart of the king of the south will become proud.
He will kill tens of thousands,
but he will not stay in power.
[13] The king of the north will come back.
He will send out an army that is even bigger than the first one.
After some time, after some years,
he will come with a great army and with plenty of supplies.
[14] “In those times, many will rise up against the king of the south.
Also some violent men from your people
will rise up to make the vision come true.
But they will fall.
[15] So the king of the north will come.
He will build up a ramp of earth
and capture a city with strong walls.
The armies of the south will not be able to stand against him,
not even their best troops.
They will have no strength to resist.
[16] But the one who comes against him will do whatever he wants.
No one will be able to stand against him.
He will stand in the glorious land,
and he will have the power to destroy it.
[17] He will set his mind on coming
with the strength of his whole kingdom.
He will bring fair terms of peace with him,
and he will carry them out.
He will give him a daughter in marriage
to destroy the kingdom.
But she will not succeed,
and she will not be on his side.
[18] After this, he will turn to the islands
and capture many of them.
But a commander will put an end to his insults.
Yes, he will turn his insults back on him.
[19] Then he will turn back toward the fortresses of his own land.
But he will stumble and fall,
and he will not be seen anymore.
[20] “Then someone else will rise up in his place.
He will send a tax collector through the kingdom
to keep up its royal splendor.
But in a few days, he will be destroyed,
not in anger and not in battle.

> **What does this mean?**
> After Alexander’s death, two of the kingdoms that came after him fought against each other again and again:
> – the “king of the south”: the Ptolemies in Egypt,
> – the “king of the north”: the Seleucids in Syria.
> Israel lay right between them, in the “glorious land” (verse 16), and it became the battlefield of their wars again and again.
> Many details fit known events, for example:
> Verse 6: Around 250 before Christ, the Egyptian princess Berenice married the Syrian king Antiochus II. The alliance failed, and she was murdered.
> Verses 15–16: Around 200 before Christ, Antiochus III took the land of Israel from the Egyptians.
> Verse 17: Antiochus III gave his daughter Cleopatra I to the Egyptian king as his wife, to gain influence. But she stood by her husband and by Egypt.
> Verse 18: A Roman commander stopped Antiochus III.
> Verse 20: Seleucus IV sent an official (Heliodorus) to take the temple treasure in Jerusalem. Seleucus was murdered later.
> Verse 14: “Some violent men from your people” – people from Israel also tried to make the vision “come true” by violence, and they failed. God’s plans cannot be forced by violence.

## The despised king (Verses 21–35)

[21] “In his place, a despised man will rise up.
People had not given him the honor of being king.
But he will come at a time when people feel safe.
He will get the kingdom by flattering people.
[22] Huge armies will be swept away before him like a flood.
They will be broken.
Yes, the prince of the covenant will also be broken.
[23] After an agreement is made with him,
he will act with lies and tricks.
He will rise up
and become strong with only a few people.
[24] At a time when people feel safe,
he will come into the richest parts of the province.
He will do what his fathers did not do,
and what his fathers’ fathers did not do.
He will hand out loot, plunder, and riches among his followers.
Yes, he will make plans against the strong cities,
but only for a time.
[25] “He will stir up his power and his courage
against the king of the south
with a great army.
And the king of the south will go to war
with a very great and mighty army.
But he will not be able to stand,
because people will make plans against him.
[26] Yes, those who eat his fine food
will destroy him.
His army will be swept away.
Many will fall dead.
[27] Both these kings
will want in their hearts to do evil.
They will sit at one table and tell each other lies.
But it will not succeed,
for the end will still come at the set time.
[28] Then he will go back to his land with great riches.
His heart will be against the holy covenant.
He will act against it
and then go back to his own land.
[29] “At the set time, he will come back
and come into the south.
But this time it will not be like the first time.
[30] For ships of Kittim will come against him.
So he will lose courage
and turn back.
He will rage against the holy covenant
and act against it.
He will come back
and pay attention to those who leave the holy covenant.
[31] “His armies will make the holy place unclean, the fortress.
They will take away the daily burnt offering.
Then they will set up the disgusting thing that causes ruin.
[32] He will lead astray with flattery
those who act wickedly against the covenant.
But the people who know their God
will be strong and take action.
[33] “Those among the people who are wise will teach many.
But for many days, they will fall
by the sword and by fire,
by being taken captive and by being robbed.
[34] When they fall,
they will receive a little help.
But many will join them who are not honest.
[35] Some of the wise will fall,
so that they are refined, made pure, and made white,
until the time of the end.
For the end will still come at the set time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the “despised man,” who comes to power by flattery. Almost all interpreters see in him Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175–164 before Christ), the “little horn” from Chapters 7 and 8.
> Verse 22: The “prince of the covenant” is probably the high priest Onias III, who was removed from office and later murdered.
> Verse 30: The “ships of Kittim” are the Romans. In the year 168 before Christ, a Roman messenger forced Antiochus to leave Egypt. The humbled king took out his anger on Jerusalem.
> Verse 31: He made the temple unclean, stopped the daily offering, and set up the “disgusting thing that causes ruin,” an altar for the god Zeus (167 before Christ).
> Verse 32: But: “The people who know their God will be strong.” Many Jews stayed faithful, even when their lives were in danger.
> Verses 33–35: The “wise” teach the people, even when they suffer and die for it. Their suffering is not meaningless. The Bible says that it “refines” the community. Refining means: making pure, like metal in a fire. This does not mean that God wants suffering. But in God’s eyes it has meaning.
> Verse 34: “A little help” – many interpreters think of the uprising of the Maccabees.

## The king who lifts himself above everything (Verses 36–45)

[36] “The king will do whatever he wants.
He will lift himself up and make himself greater than every god.
He will say shocking things against the God of gods.
He will be successful until the time of God’s anger is over.
For what has been decided will happen.
[37] He will not care about the gods of his fathers,
or about the god that women love,
or about any god.
For he will make himself greater than all of them.
[38] Instead, he will honor the god of fortresses.
He will honor a god that his fathers did not know.
He will honor him with gold and silver,
with precious stones and costly gifts.
[39] With the help of a foreign god,
he will attack the strongest fortresses.
He will give great honor to those who accept him.
He will make them rulers over many people.
And he will divide up the land as a reward.
[40] “At the time of the end, the king of the south will fight against him.
And the king of the north will come at him like a storm,
with chariots, with horsemen, and with many ships.
He will invade the countries.
He will sweep through them like a flood.
[41] He will also come into the glorious land.
Many countries will be defeated.
But these will be rescued from his power:
Edom, Moab, and the leaders of the people of Ammon.
[42] He will also reach out his hand against the countries.
The land of Egypt will not escape.
[43] He will have power over the treasures of gold and silver
and over all the precious things of Egypt.
The Libyans and the Ethiopians will follow him.
[44] But news from the east and from the north
will frighten him.
He will go out in a great rage
to destroy many and wipe them out completely.
[45] He will set up his royal tents
between the sea and the glorious holy mountain.
But he will come to his end,
and no one will help him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king becomes more and more mad with power. He puts himself above all gods, even above the God of gods. Antiochus IV called himself “Epiphanes.” That means “the god who has appeared.”
> Verse 37: “The god that women love” probably means a god that women especially worshiped (maybe Tammuz or Adonis, compare Ezekiel 8:14). In the WEB, the words are: the desire of women.
> Verse 38: The “god of fortresses” – maybe Zeus, or simply: He worships only power and war.
> Verse 43: “Ethiopians” – this means the people of Cush, south of Egypt.
> Verses 40–45: These verses no longer fit exactly with what is known about Antiochus IV. He died of an illness in Persia in 164 before Christ. That is why many interpreters connect these verses with a time of the end that is still to come. Others see in them a general announcement of his downfall.
> The message is clear: Even the most powerful and cruel ruler “will come to his end, and no one will help him.”
