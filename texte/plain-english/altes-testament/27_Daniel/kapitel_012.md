# Daniel – Chapter 12
### The time of the end

---

## The resurrection (Verses 1–4)

[1] “At that time Michael will stand up,
the great prince who protects the people of your nation.
There will be a time of trouble,
worse than any time since there was a nation until that time.
But at that time your people will be rescued,
everyone whose name is found written in the book.
[2] Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth
will wake up.
Some will wake up to life that lasts forever.
Others will wake up to shame and to disgrace that lasts forever.
[3] Those who are wise will shine like the bright sky.
And those who lead many people to do what is right
will shine like the stars
forever and ever.
[4] But you, Daniel, close up the words
and seal the book
until the time of the end.
Many will run here and there,
and knowledge will increase.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important texts of the Old Testament!
> Verse 1: A hard time of trouble will come. But Michael, the protector of Israel, stands up. And everyone whose name is written “in the book” will be rescued. The “book of life” is a picture that God knows everyone who belongs to him (compare Revelation 20:12).
> Verse 2: Here, for the first time in the Bible, it is said very clearly: The dead will rise again. Death is a “sleep in the dust” from which people wake up, to life that lasts forever or to shame that lasts forever.
> That was a great hope, especially for the martyrs who had died for their faith (Daniel 11:33–35). Martyrs are people who are killed because of their faith. Their death is not the end. God will give them justice.
> Belief in the resurrection of the dead is a basic belief in Judaism (the daily prayer says: “Blessed are you, who gives life to the dead”) and in Christianity (Jesus speaks in a very similar way in John 5:28–29).
> Verse 3: A beautiful picture: Whoever is wise and leads others to what is good will shine like the stars. That is true for everyone who helps others find God and what is good: parents, teachers, friends.

## How long? (Verses 5–13)

[5] Then I, Daniel, looked,
and I saw two others standing there.
One was on the bank of the river on this side,
and the other was on the bank of the river on the other side.
[6] One of them said to the man dressed in linen,
who was above the waters of the river,
“How long will it be until the end of these amazing things?”
[7] I heard the man dressed in linen,
who was above the waters of the river.
He raised his right hand and his left hand toward heaven.
He made a promise by the one who lives forever.
He said: It will be for a time, times, and a half.
When the power of the holy people has been completely broken,
all these things will be finished.
[8] I heard it, but I did not understand it.
So I said,
“My lord, how will all these things end?”
[9] He said,
“Go your way, Daniel.
For the words are closed up and sealed
until the time of the end.
[10] Many will make themselves pure.
They will make themselves white and be refined.
But the wicked will keep doing what is wicked.
None of the wicked will understand.
But those who are wise will understand.
[11] “The daily burnt offering will be taken away,
and the disgusting thing that causes ruin will be set up.
From that time on, there will be one thousand two hundred ninety days.
[12] Happy is the one who waits patiently
and reaches the one thousand three hundred thirty-five days.
[13] “But you, go your way until the end.
You will rest.
And at the end of the days, you will stand up to receive your share.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: The question of everyone who suffers: “How long?” The answer: “A time, times, and a half,” that is, about three and a half years (compare 7:25). A limited time. The suffering has an end.
> Verse 8: An honest sentence: “I heard it, but I did not understand it.” Even Daniel does not understand everything. We do not have to understand every number and every picture in the Bible either.
> Verses 11–12: 1,290 days and 1,335 days. It is not clear what exactly these numbers mean. They are a little longer than three and a half years. Maybe it is meant to say: If it takes longer than you expect, do not give up. “Happy is the one who waits patiently.”
> Important: Again and again, people have tried to use these numbers to calculate the date of the end of the world. All these calculations have failed. Jesus says: “But no one knows that day or that hour” (Mark 13:32).
> Verse 13: The last verse of the book is a personal promise to Daniel: “But you, go your way until the end. You will rest. And at the end of the days, you will stand up to receive your share.” Many understand this as a promise of resurrection: Daniel will rise again and receive his place with God. A comforting word for everyone who has lived faithfully.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Daniel?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Faithful in a foreign land:** Daniel and his friends were taken away and lived at the court of Babylon. They learned the foreign culture and worked faithfully for the kings. But they did not give up their faith (Chapter 1).
> **Courage in danger:** The three friends would rather go into the fiery furnace than worship an idol: “But if not …” (Chapter 3). Daniel kept praying, even when it was forbidden, and was thrown into the lions’ den (Chapter 6). God was with them in the fire and with the lions.
> **God is Lord over the kings:** “He removes kings and sets up kings” (2:21). Proud Nebuchadnezzar was humbled and learned to praise the King of heaven (Chapter 4). Belshazzar was “weighed” and found “too light” (Chapter 5).
> **The world empires pass away:** The statue made of metals (Chapter 2) and the four animals (Chapter 7) show: All world empires, as powerful as they are, pass away.
> **God’s kingdom remains:** A stone, not made by human hands, becomes a mountain (Chapter 2). “One like a son of man” comes with the clouds and receives a kingdom that lasts forever (Chapter 7). Jesus called himself the “Son of Man.”
> **Prayer:** Daniel prayed three times a day (Chapter 6). He confessed the guilt of his people and said “we” (Chapter 9). God heard his prayers from the first day (10:12).
> **Hope beyond death:** “Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth will wake up” (12:2). The faithful teachers will shine like the stars.
> **Be careful with calculations:** The numbers and pictures in Daniel are often hard to understand. They are meant to give courage, not fear. No one can use them to calculate the end of the world.
> **For today:** Daniel shows how to stay faithful in a world with other values: polite, wise, hardworking, but firm in faith. And the book gives courage: No power in the world has the last word. God does.
> **What comes next?** Now come the twelve “Minor Prophets.” They are called “minor” (small) because their books are shorter, not because they are less important. The first is Hosea. His marriage to an unfaithful woman becomes a picture of God’s love for his unfaithful people: a love that does not give up.
