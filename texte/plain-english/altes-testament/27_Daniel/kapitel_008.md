# Daniel – Chapter 8
### The ram and the male goat

---

## The vision by the Ulai River (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the third year that King Belshazzar ruled,
I, Daniel, saw a vision.
It came after the vision that I had seen at first.
[2] I saw the vision.
In the vision, I was in the fortress of Susa,
which is in the province of Elam.
I saw in the vision
that I was by the Ulai River.
[3] Then I looked up,
and I saw a ram with two horns standing beside the river.
A ram is a male sheep.
The two horns were long,
but one was longer than the other.
The longer one grew up last.
[4] I saw the ram charging toward the west, the north, and the south.
No animal could stand against him.
No one could rescue anyone from his power.
He did whatever he wanted,
and he made himself great.
[5] As I was thinking about this,
I saw a male goat coming from the west.
He crossed the whole earth
without touching the ground.
The goat had one large horn between his eyes.
[6] He came to the ram with the two horns,
which I had seen standing beside the river.
He ran at him with all his furious strength.
[7] I saw him come close to the ram.
He was very angry with him.
He struck the ram
and broke his two horns.
The ram had no strength to stand against him.
The goat threw him to the ground
and trampled on him.
And no one could rescue the ram from his power.
[8] The male goat made himself very great.
But when he was at his strongest, the great horn was broken.
In its place, four large horns grew up.
They pointed toward the four winds of the sky.

> **What does this mean?**
> From Chapter 8 on, the book is written in Hebrew again.
> In a vision, Daniel sees himself in Susa, which later became the capital of the Persian Empire (in today’s Iran).
> Here, the meaning is given in the text itself (verses 20–21): The ram with two horns is the kingdom of the Medes and Persians. The longer horn that grew later is Persia, which became stronger than Media.
> The male goat from the west is Greece. He is so fast that he does not touch the ground. The great horn is the first king: Alexander the Great. He conquered the huge Persian Empire in only a few years (334–331 before Christ).
> Verse 8: At the height of his power, “the great horn was broken”: Alexander died when he was only 32 years old (323 before Christ). His empire was divided among his generals, into four parts.

## The little horn (Verses 9–14)

[9] Out of one of them came a little horn.
It grew very great
toward the south, toward the east,
and toward the glorious land.
[10] It grew so great that it reached the army of the sky.
It threw some of the army and some of the stars down to the earth
and trampled on them.
[11] Yes, it made itself great, even up to the prince of the army.
It took away from him the daily burnt offering.
A burnt offering is an animal that is burned completely on the altar for God.
And the place of his holy temple was thrown down.
[12] Because of rebellion against God,
the army was handed over to it,
together with the daily burnt offering.
It threw truth down to the ground.
It did whatever it wanted,
and it was successful.
[13] Then I heard a holy one speaking.
And another holy one asked the one who was speaking,
“How long will the vision last?
How long will it be about the daily burnt offering
and the rebellion that causes ruin?
How long will the holy place and the army be trampled underfoot?”
[14] He said to me,
“For two thousand three hundred evenings and mornings.
Then the holy place will be made clean again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Out of one of the four kingdoms grows a little horn that becomes very powerful. It attacks the “glorious land” (Israel), stops the daily offering in the temple, and makes the holy place unclean.
> Most interpreters, Jewish and Christian, see in it King Antiochus IV Epiphanes from the kingdom of the Seleucids (one of the four kingdoms that came after Alexander). In the year 167 before Christ, he forbade the Jewish worship of God and set up an altar for the Greek god Zeus in the temple.
> Verse 10: He even attacks “the stars,” that is, he rises up against God himself. A picture of pride without limits.
> Verse 14: 2,300 “evenings and mornings” – that is either 2,300 days (a little more than six years) or, if you count the evening and morning offerings separately, 1,150 days (a little more than three years). After that, the holy place is cleansed. In the year 164 before Christ, the Maccabees won back the temple and dedicated it again. The Jewish feast of Hanukkah remembers this.

## The angel Gabriel explains the vision (Verses 15–27)

[15] I, Daniel, had seen the vision,
and I was trying to understand it.
Then someone stood in front of me
who looked like a man.
[16] I heard a man’s voice between the banks of the Ulai.
It called out,
“Gabriel, help this man understand the vision.”
[17] So he came near the place where I was standing.
When he came, I was frightened and fell on my face.
But he said to me,
“Understand, son of man,
that the vision is about the time of the end.”
[18] While he was speaking with me,
I fell into a deep sleep
with my face to the ground.
But he touched me and stood me up.
[19] He said,
“Listen, I will tell you
what will happen later, in the time of God’s anger.
For the vision is about the set time of the end.
[20] You saw the ram with the two horns.
They are the kings of Media and Persia.
[21] The hairy male goat is the king of Greece.
The great horn between his eyes is the first king.
[22] The horn was broken, and four horns grew up in its place.
That means: Four kingdoms will rise from his nation,
but they will not have his power.
[23] “In the last part of their rule,
when the wrongdoers have become as bad as they can be,
a king will rise up.
He will have a fierce face,
and he will understand riddles.
[24] His power will be great,
but not by his own strength.
He will cause terrible destruction.
He will be successful in what he does.
He will destroy the mighty ones and the holy people.
[25] With his cleverness, he will make lies and tricks succeed.
In his heart he will think he is great.
He will destroy many people who feel safe.
He will even rise up against the Prince of princes.
But he will be broken,
and not by any human hand.
[26] “The vision about the evenings and the mornings
that has been told to you is true.
But seal up the vision.
That means: Keep it secret.
For it is about a time many days from now.”
[27] I, Daniel, fainted
and was sick for several days.
Then I got up
and did the king’s work again.
I was shocked by the vision,
but no one understood it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Here, for the first time in the Bible, an angel is called by name: Gabriel. His name means “God is strong” or “man of God.” In the New Testament, it is Gabriel who tells Mary that Jesus will be born (Luke 1:26–38).
> Verse 17: Gabriel calls Daniel “son of man,” just as God called Ezekiel.
> Verses 23–25: The king with “a fierce face” is again the cruel ruler (Antiochus IV). He is clever, deceitful, and violent. He even attacks God (“the Prince of princes”).
> Verse 25: “He will be broken, and not by any human hand.” Antiochus IV died of an illness in the year 164 before Christ, not in battle.
> Verse 27: The vision is so heavy that Daniel becomes sick. But then he gets up and goes back to his work. Faith also means to keep doing your daily work faithfully, even when you have heavy thoughts.
