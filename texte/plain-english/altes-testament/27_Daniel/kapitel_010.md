# Daniel – Chapter 10
### The man dressed in linen

---

## Daniel mourns for three weeks (Verses 1–3)

[1] In the third year of Cyrus, the king of Persia,
a message was shown to Daniel,
who was also called Belteshazzar.
The message was true.
It was about a great war.
He understood the message,
and he understood the vision.
[2] In those days, I, Daniel, was mourning
for three whole weeks.
[3] I did not eat any tasty food.
No meat and no wine came into my mouth.
I did not put any oil on my body at all,
until the three whole weeks were over.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 10–12 belong together. They are Daniel’s last and longest vision.
> The third year of Cyrus is about 536 BC. By then, many Jews had returned to Jerusalem. But Daniel, now very old, stayed in Babylon.
> Why does Daniel mourn? The text does not say exactly. Maybe because of the difficulties with rebuilding Jerusalem (compare Ezra 4). He fasts in part: no tasty food, no meat, no wine, no oil to care for his body.

---

## The vision by the Tigris (Verses 4–9)

[4] On the twenty-fourth day of the first month,
I was on the bank of the great river, the Hiddekel.
[5] I looked up,
and I saw a man dressed in linen.
Around his waist he wore a belt of pure gold from Uphaz.
[6] His body was like beryl.
His face looked like lightning.
His eyes were like burning torches.
His arms and his feet were like polished bronze.
And the sound of his words was like the noise of a huge crowd.
[7] Only I, Daniel, saw the vision.
The men who were with me did not see the vision.
But they began to shake with great fear,
and they ran away to hide.
[8] So I was left alone,
and I saw this great vision.
I had no strength left.
My face turned as pale as death,
and I had no strength anymore.
[9] But I heard the sound of his words.
When I heard the sound of his words,
I fell into a deep sleep on my face,
with my face to the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hiddekel is the Hebrew name for the Tigris.
> Daniel sees a shining man from heaven, dressed in linen, with a belt of gold, with eyes like fire and arms like bronze.
> The description is very similar to the appearance of the risen Christ in Revelation 1:13–15. The text does not say who the man in Daniel is. Many think of a high angel, maybe Gabriel.
> Verse 7: The men with Daniel see nothing, but they feel something and run away. Something similar happened to the men with Paul near Damascus (Acts 9:7).
> In verse 6, the WEB has “beryl,” a shimmering gemstone.

## Do not be afraid, you who are greatly loved (Verses 10–14)

[10] Then a hand touched me.
It helped me up onto my knees and the palms of my hands.
[11] He said to me,
“Daniel, you man who is greatly loved,
understand the words that I am speaking to you.
Stand up,
for I have now been sent to you.”
When he said this to me,
I stood up, trembling.
[12] Then he said to me,
“Do not be afraid, Daniel!
From the first day
that you set your heart on understanding
and on humbling yourself before your God,
your words were heard.
And I have come because of your words.
[13] But the prince of the kingdom of Persia
stood against me for 21 days.
Then Michael, one of the chief princes,
came to help me,
because I was left there with the kings of Persia.
[14] Now I have come
to help you understand
what will happen to your people in the last days.
For the vision is about a time many days from now.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: A great comfort: God heard Daniel’s prayer from the first day! Only the answer arrived later.
> Verse 13: A mysterious look behind the scenes: The messenger was held up for 21 days, exactly the three weeks that Daniel fasted. The “prince of the kingdom of Persia” is an invisible power that stands behind the Persian Empire. Only when Michael came could the messenger go on.
> Michael (the name means “Who is like God?”) is an archangel, a chief angel. Here he is named as the protector of Israel. In the New Testament, he appears in the letter of Jude (verse 9) and in Revelation (12:7).
> The message: Behind the events on earth there is an invisible reality. When an answer to prayer takes a long time, that does not mean that God has not heard.

## Be strong! (Verses 15–21)

[15] When he had spoken these words to me,
I bowed my face to the ground,
and I could not speak.
[16] Then one who looked like a human being
touched my lips.
Then I opened my mouth.
I spoke to the one who was standing in front of me.
I said,
“My lord, because of the vision, pain has come over me,
and I have no strength left.
[17] How can I, your servant, my lord,
talk with you, my lord?
For right now I have no strength left in me,
and there is no breath left in me.”
[18] Then the one who looked like a man touched me again,
and he gave me strength.
[19] He said,
“You man who is greatly loved, do not be afraid!
Peace be with you!
Be strong! Yes, be strong!”
When he spoke to me, I became stronger.
I said,
“Let my lord speak, for you have given me strength.”
[20] Then he said,
“Do you know why I have come to you?
Now I will go back
to fight against the prince of Persia.
And when I leave,
the prince of Greece will come.
[21] But I will tell you
what is written in the book of truth.
No one stands with me against them
except Michael, your prince.

> **What does this mean?**
> Daniel is completely exhausted. Three times he is touched and given strength: on his body (verse 10), on his lips (verse 16), and once more (verse 18).
> Verse 19: Beautiful words: “You man who is greatly loved, do not be afraid! Peace be with you! Be strong! Yes, be strong!” God also speaks like this to us when we are weak and exhausted.
> Verse 20: The world empires (Persia, Greece) have their invisible “princes.” The fight goes on, but God sees everything: It is written in the “book of truth” (verse 21).
