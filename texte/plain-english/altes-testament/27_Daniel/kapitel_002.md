# Daniel – Chapter 2
### The dream of the great statue

---

## The king demands the impossible (Verses 1–13)

[1] In the second year that Nebuchadnezzar was king,
Nebuchadnezzar had dreams.
His spirit was troubled,
and he could not sleep anymore.
[2] Then the king gave an order
to call the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans.
They were to tell the king his dreams.
So they came and stood in front of the king.
[3] The king said to them,
“I have had a dream.
My spirit is troubled,
and I want to know the dream.”
[4] Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in the Syrian language,
“O king, live forever!
Tell your servants the dream,
and we will explain what it means.”
[5] The king answered the Chaldeans,
“The matter has gone from me.
If you do not tell me the dream and what it means,
you will be cut to pieces.
And your houses will be turned into piles of garbage.
[6] But if you tell me the dream and what it means,
you will receive gifts, rewards, and great honor from me.
So tell me the dream and what it means.”
[7] They answered a second time,
“Let the king tell his servants the dream.
Then we will explain what it means.”
[8] The king answered,
“I know for sure that you are trying to gain time.
For you see that the matter has gone from me.
[9] If you do not tell me the dream,
there is only one law for you.
For you have agreed to speak lying and evil words to me
until the situation changes.
So tell me the dream.
Then I will know that you can also tell me what it means.”
[10] The Chaldeans answered the king,
“There is no one on earth
who can do what the king asks.
For no king, lord, or ruler
has ever asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter, or Chaldean.
[11] What the king asks is very hard.
No one can tell it to the king
except the gods.
And the gods do not live among human beings.”
[12] Because of this, the king became angry and very furious.
He ordered that all the wise men of Babylon be killed.
[13] So the order went out,
and the wise men were to be killed.
They also looked for Daniel and his friends to kill them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king has a troubling dream. He puts his dream interpreters to the test: They are to tell him not only what it means, but first the dream itself. That way he can check whether they really have knowledge from beyond this world, or whether they are just making something up.
> Verse 4: From here to Daniel 7:28, the book is written in Aramaic. The WEB calls the language “Syrian.” It means Aramaic.
> Verse 11: The wise men admit it themselves: Only the gods could know this, and they do not live among human beings. Without knowing it, they prepare Daniel’s answer: There is a God who reveals secrets (verse 28).
> Verse 12: A cruel order out of anger. The text shows how unfair and dangerous the power of a tyrant is.

## Daniel prays with his friends (Verses 14–23)

[14] Then Daniel spoke with wisdom and good sense
to Arioch, the captain of the king’s guard.
Arioch had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.
[15] Daniel said to Arioch, the king’s captain,
“Why is the king’s order so urgent?”
Then Arioch explained the matter to Daniel.
[16] Daniel went in and asked the king
to give him some time.
Then he would tell the king what the dream means.
[17] Then Daniel went to his house.
He told the matter to his friends Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
[18] He asked them to pray to the God of heaven for mercy
about this secret,
so that Daniel and his friends would not die
with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.
[19] Then the secret was shown to Daniel in a vision in the night.
And Daniel praised the God of heaven.
[20] Daniel said,
“May the name of God be praised forever and ever,
for wisdom and power belong to him.
[21] He changes the times and the seasons.
He removes kings and sets up kings.
He gives wisdom to the wise
and knowledge to those who have understanding.
[22] He reveals what is deep and secret.
He knows what is in the darkness,
and the light lives with him.
[23] I thank you and praise you, O God of my ancestors.
You have given me wisdom and power.
And now you have shown me what we asked you for.
For you have shown us what the king wants to know.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Daniel stays calm. He speaks wisely with the officer of the guard, asks the king for time, and then prays with his friends. Together they ask God for help.
> Verses 19–23: When God shows him the secret, Daniel first gives thanks. He praises God before he goes to the king. His prayer is a beautiful song of praise:
> “He removes kings and sets up kings.” That is the theme of the whole book: Not the rulers decide history, but God.
> “The light lives with him.” God also sees into the darkness.

## There is a God in heaven (Verses 24–30)

[24] So Daniel went to Arioch.
The king had given Arioch the job of killing the wise men of Babylon.
Daniel went and said to him,
“Do not kill the wise men of Babylon.
Bring me to the king,
and I will tell the king what the dream means.”
[25] Then Arioch quickly brought Daniel to the king.
He said to him,
“I have found a man among the captives from Judah
who will tell the king what the dream means.”
[26] The king said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar,
“Can you tell me the dream that I saw
and what it means?”
[27] Daniel answered the king,
“Wise men, enchanters, magicians, and fortune-tellers
cannot tell the king the secret
that the king has asked about.
[28] But there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets.
He has shown King Nebuchadnezzar
what will happen in the last days.
Your dream and the visions in your head as you lay on your bed were these:
[29] “O king, as you lay on your bed,
you had thoughts about what would happen in the future.
And he who reveals secrets has shown you what will happen.
[30] But this secret was not shown to me
because I am wiser than anyone else alive.
It was shown to me so that the king would learn what the dream means,
and so that you would know the thoughts of your heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Daniel does not think only of himself. First he asks that the other wise men are not killed either, even though they are not believers in his God.
> Verses 27–28: Daniel does not take the honor for himself. He says: No human being can do this. “But there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets.”
> Verse 30: Daniel is humble: “This secret was not shown to me because I am wiser than anyone else alive.” That means: I am not smarter than others. Everything is God’s gift.

## The dream (Verses 31–35)

[31] “O king, you looked,
and you saw a great statue.
This statue was huge,
and it shone brightly.
It stood in front of you,
and it looked terrifying.
[32] The head of this statue was made of fine gold.
Its chest and its arms were made of silver.
Its belly and its thighs were made of bronze.
[33] Its legs were made of iron.
Its feet were partly iron and partly clay.
[34] As you watched,
a stone was cut out, but not by human hands.
It struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay
and broke them to pieces.
[35] Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold
were all broken to pieces together.
They became like chaff on the threshing floors in summer.
The wind carried them away,
so that no trace of them was found.
But the stone that struck the statue
became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king saw a huge statue made of different metals. From top to bottom, they are less and less valuable (gold, silver, bronze, iron). And at the end it stands on feet of iron and clay, so it is not stable.
> Then a stone comes, “but not by human hands.” So it is not made by human beings. It smashes the whole statue. And the stone becomes a mountain that fills the whole world.
> Verse 35: Chaff is the dry husk of grain that the wind blows away.

## The meaning: four kingdoms and the kingdom of God (Verses 36–45)

[36] “That was the dream.
Now we will tell the king what it means.
[37] O king, you are the king of kings.
The God of heaven has given you the kingdom,
the power, the strength, and the glory.
[38] Wherever people live,
he has handed over to you the animals of the field and the birds of the sky.
He has made you ruler over them all.
You are the head of gold.
[39] “After you, another kingdom will rise.
It will be weaker than you.
Then a third kingdom, of bronze, will come.
It will rule over the whole earth.
[40] The fourth kingdom will be as strong as iron.
For iron breaks everything to pieces and defeats everything.
Like iron that crushes all these things,
it will break and crush them all.
[41] You saw the feet and toes,
partly of potter’s clay and partly of iron.
It will be a divided kingdom.
But some of the strength of iron will be in it,
because you saw the iron mixed with soft, muddy clay.
[42] The toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay.
In the same way, the kingdom will be partly strong and partly brittle.
[43] You saw the iron mixed with soft, muddy clay.
They will mix with other people through marriage.
But they will not hold together,
just as iron does not mix with clay.
[44] “In the days of those kings,
the God of heaven will set up a kingdom
that will never be destroyed.
Its rule will never be handed over to another people.
It will break all these kingdoms to pieces and put an end to them.
But it will last forever.
[45] For you saw a stone cut out of the mountain, but not by human hands.
And it broke to pieces the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold.
The great God has shown the king
what will happen in the future.
The dream is certain,
and its meaning can be trusted.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The statue stands for four world empires that come one after the other. The head of gold is Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar.
> Verse 42: Brittle means: It breaks easily.
> Which empires are the others? There are two main explanations:
> – Many interpreters: Babylon, Media, Persia, Greece (the empire of Alexander the Great and of the kings after him. They joined their families through marriage, but they stayed divided, verse 43).
> – An old explanation that many people hold (already among Jewish and Christian interpreters in ancient times): Babylon, Media-Persia, Greece, Rome.
> Verse 44: The central message: All world empires, as powerful as they are, will pass away. At the end there is a kingdom that God himself sets up and that lasts forever.
> Jews expect this kingdom with the coming of the Messiah. Christians see in it the kingdom of God that Jesus announced. Jesus said that the kingdom of God is near (Mark 1:15). It begins small like a stone and grows until it fills the whole world.
> For today: No power in the world lasts forever. Dictators, world empires, and superpowers come and go. God’s kingdom remains.

## The king honors Daniel’s God (Verses 46–49)

[46] Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face
and bowed down to Daniel in worship.
He ordered that an offering and sweet-smelling incense be offered to him.
[47] The king said to Daniel,
“Truly, your God is the God of gods
and the Lord of kings.
He reveals secrets,
for you were able to reveal this secret.”
[48] Then the king made Daniel great.
He gave him many great gifts.
He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon
and the chief governor over all the wise men of Babylon.
[49] Daniel made a request to the king.
So the king put Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego
in charge of the business of the province of Babylon.
But Daniel stayed at the king’s gate.
That means: He served at the king’s court.

> **What does this mean?**
> The most powerful king in the world falls down before a young captive. And he confesses: “Your God is the God of gods and the Lord of kings.”
> The text does not say whether Daniel accepted the worship. But the king understands: The honor really belongs to Daniel’s God.
> Verse 49: Daniel does not forget his friends. He makes sure that they also get important positions.
