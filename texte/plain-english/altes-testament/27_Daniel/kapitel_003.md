# Daniel – Chapter 3
### The three men in the fiery furnace

---

## The golden statue (Verses 1–7)

[1] King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold.
It was sixty cubits high, that is about 90 feet.
It was six cubits wide, that is about 9 feet.
He set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.
[2] Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent messengers
to gather the local governors, the deputies, and the governors,
the judges, the treasurers, the advisers, the sheriffs,
and all the rulers of the provinces.
They were to come to the dedication of the statue
that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
A dedication is a celebration where something is given its special purpose.
[3] So the local governors, the deputies, and the governors,
the judges, the treasurers, the advisers, the sheriffs,
and all the rulers of the provinces gathered
for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
And they stood in front of the statue
that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
[4] Then the herald called out loudly.
A herald is a man who announces the king’s orders.
He said, “Peoples, nations, and people of every language,
this is your order:
[5] As soon as you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre,
harp, pipe, and all kinds of music,
you must fall down and worship the golden statue
that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.
[6] Whoever does not fall down and worship
will be thrown that same hour
into the middle of a burning, fiery furnace.”
A furnace is a very large oven.
[7] So at that time,
as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre,
harp, pipe, and all kinds of music,
all the peoples, nations, and people of every language fell down.
They worshiped the golden statue
that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nebuchadnezzar has a huge golden statue built: 60 cubits high (about 90 feet) and only 6 cubits wide. So it was more like a pillar or an obelisk, maybe only covered with gold. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> Maybe the king misunderstood his dream from Chapter 2: There, only the head was made of gold. Now he makes the whole statue of gold, as if he wanted to say: My kingdom will last forever.
> The many repetitions (again and again the list of officials and the list of instruments) seem almost funny. They show how boastful and bureaucratic this power is.
> Verse 6: Anyone who does not go along is killed. A dictatorship that demands the same worship from everyone.
> In the 20th century, Christians and Jews under dictatorships (for example, under the Nazis) read this chapter as encouragement not to bow down.

## The accusation (Verses 8–12)

[8] So at that time, some Chaldeans came forward
and accused the Jews.
[9] They said to King Nebuchadnezzar,
“O king, live forever!
[10] O king, you have given an order:
Everyone who hears the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre,
harp, pipe, and all kinds of music
must fall down and worship the golden statue.
[11] And whoever does not fall down and worship
will be thrown into the middle of a burning, fiery furnace.
[12] There are some Jews whom you put in charge of the business of the province of Babylon:
Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.
These men, O king, have not respected you.
They do not serve your gods.
And they do not worship the golden statue
that you have set up.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jealous officials use the chance to get rid of their successful Jewish colleagues. They stress: “There are some Jews whom you put in charge …”
> Here we see how hatred against a minority often grows out of jealousy and the hunger for power. Sadly, hatred of Jews has a long and terrible history, up to today. This text clearly takes the side of those who are persecuted.

---

## “But if not …” (Verses 13–18)

[13] Then Nebuchadnezzar became angry and furious.
He ordered that Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego be brought.
So these men were brought to the king.
[14] Nebuchadnezzar said to them,
“Is it true, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego,
that you do not serve my gods
and do not worship the golden statue that I have set up?
[15] Now, when you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre,
harp, pipe, and all kinds of music,
if you are ready to fall down and worship the statue that I have made, good.
But if you do not worship,
you will be thrown that same hour
into the middle of a burning, fiery furnace.
And who is the god who can rescue you from my hands?”
[16] Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered the king,
“Nebuchadnezzar, we do not need
to answer you in this matter.
[17] If it happens,
our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the burning, fiery furnace.
And he will rescue us from your hand, O king.
[18] But if not,
you should know, O king,
that we will not serve your gods
or worship the golden statue that you have set up.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The king gives them a second chance, and he challenges God: “Who is the god who can rescue you from my hands?”
> Verses 17–18: One of the bravest answers in the Bible: “Our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the burning, fiery furnace … But if not, you should know, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden statue that you have set up.”
> That is real faith: It does not depend on whether God solves the problem. The three trust God, even though they do not know how it will end. They are ready to die for what they believe.
> Many people have drawn strength from this “But if not …”, for example when they stood up against injustice.

## In the fiery furnace (Verses 19–23)

[19] Then Nebuchadnezzar was full of fury.
The look on his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.
He gave an order
to heat the furnace seven times hotter than usual.
[20] He ordered some strong men in his army
to tie up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego
and to throw them into the burning, fiery furnace.
[21] So these men were tied up
in their pants, their tunics, their cloaks, and their other clothes.
And they were thrown into the middle of the burning, fiery furnace.
[22] The king’s order was urgent,
and the furnace was extremely hot.
So the flames of the fire killed the men
who carried Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego up.
[23] These three men, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego,
fell, tied up, into the middle of the burning, fiery furnace.

> **What does this mean?**
> In his rage, the king loses all good sense. He has the furnace heated so much that even his own soldiers die. Blind rage also destroys your own people.
> Note: In Catholic and Orthodox Bibles, there is a longer section after verse 23: the prayer of Azariah and the song of praise of the three men in the furnace. It has come down to us only in Greek and is not in the Hebrew-Aramaic Bible. The WEB does not include it here.

## The fourth man in the fire (Verses 24–27)

[24] Then King Nebuchadnezzar was amazed.
He quickly stood up.
He said to his advisers,
“Didn’t we throw three men, tied up, into the middle of the fire?”
They answered the king,
“That is true, O king.”
[25] He answered,
“Look! I see four men walking around free in the middle of the fire.
And they are not hurt.
And the fourth one looks like a son of the gods.”
[26] Then Nebuchadnezzar came near to the opening of the burning, fiery furnace.
He said,
“Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego,
servants of the Most High God,
come out! Come here!”
Then Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego came out of the middle of the fire.
[27] The local governors, the deputies, the governors, and the king’s advisers
gathered and looked at these men.
The fire had had no power over their bodies.
The hair on their heads was not burned.
Their pants had not changed.
There was not even the smell of fire on them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Suddenly the king sees four men in the fire, free and unhurt. The ropes are burned, but not the men.
> Verse 25: Who is the fourth? The king says: “The fourth one looks like a son of the gods.” Later he calls him an “angel” (verse 28). Many Christians have seen in the fourth man an appearance of Christ. In Judaism, he is usually understood as an angel of God.
> The most important message: God was with them in the fire. He did not keep them from the fire, but he kept them safe in the fire. Compare Isaiah 43:2. There God promises: When you walk through the fire, you will not be burned.
> Verse 27: Not even the smell of smoke! The rescue is complete.

---

## The king praises the God of the three men (Verses 28–30)

[28] Nebuchadnezzar said,
“Praise be to the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego!
He has sent his angel
and rescued his servants
who trusted in him.
They went against the king’s word.
They were ready to give up their bodies
so that they would not serve or worship any god except their own God.
[29] So I give this order:
Any people, nation, or people of any language
that says anything evil against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego
will be cut to pieces.
And their houses will be turned into piles of garbage.
For there is no other god who can rescue like this.”
[30] Then the king gave Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego higher positions
in the province of Babylon.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king praises God. He admits: These men risked their lives to stay faithful to their God.
> Verse 29: But the king has not really understood yet. Now he threatens the same violence, only in the other direction. Faith cannot be forced by threats. God does not need violence to protect him.
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, the first three verses of the next chapter (in the WEB 4:1–3) still belong to Chapter 3 (as 3:31–33).
> For today: All over the world, people are persecuted because of their faith. This chapter encourages us to stay faithful. And it reminds us to stand up for freedom of religion for all people.
