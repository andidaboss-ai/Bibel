# Daniel – Chapter 7
### Four animals and the Son of Man

---

## Four animals come up out of the sea (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the first year that Belshazzar was king of Babylon,
Daniel had a dream.
He saw visions in his head as he lay on his bed.
Then he wrote down the dream
and told the main points of it.
[2] Daniel said,
“I looked in my vision at night,
and I saw the four winds of the sky
stirring up the great sea.
[3] Four great animals came up out of the sea.
Each one was different from the others.
[4] “The first was like a lion,
and it had the wings of an eagle.
As I watched, its wings were pulled off.
It was lifted up from the ground
and made to stand on two feet like a human being.
And it was given a human heart.
[5] “Then I saw another animal, a second one.
It looked like a bear.
It was raised up on one side.
It had three ribs in its mouth between its teeth.
It was told,
‘Get up! Eat a lot of meat!’
[6] “After this I looked,
and I saw another animal. It looked like a leopard.
On its back it had four wings like a bird.
The animal also had four heads.
And it was given power to rule.
[7] “After this I looked in the visions at night,
and I saw a fourth animal.
It was terrifying, powerful, and very strong.
It had large iron teeth.
It ate and crushed its victims,
and it stamped on what was left with its feet.
It was different from all the animals before it.
It had 10 horns.
[8] “I was thinking about the horns.
Then I saw another horn come up among them, a little one.
Three of the first horns were pulled out by the roots to make room for it.
This horn had eyes like the eyes of a human being
and a mouth that spoke proudly.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Chapter 7, the second part of the book begins: Daniel’s own visions. They are full of strange pictures, like a nightmare. Texts like this are called “apocalyptic” (from a Greek word that means “revealing”): They show in pictures what is going on behind history.
> The “great sea” stands for chaos and unrest. Out of this chaos come four wild animals: a lion with wings, a bear, a leopard with four heads, and a terrible fourth animal with iron teeth and 10 horns.
> The animals are like the four metals of the statue in Chapter 2: four world empires. Here, world empires are shown as beasts of prey, dangerous and greedy.
> In the Bible, horns are a picture of power and of kings.
> Verse 8: The little horn with human eyes and a big mouth is an especially proud ruler.

---

## The Ancient of Days on the throne (Verses 9–12)

[9] “As I watched,
thrones were set in place,
and one who was the Ancient of Days sat down.
His clothes were as white as snow.
The hair on his head was like pure wool.
His throne was flames of fire,
and its wheels were burning fire.
[10] A river of fire flowed out from in front of him.
Thousands and thousands served him.
10,000 times 10,000 stood in front of him.
The court sat down to judge,
and the books were opened.
[11] “Then I kept watching because of the proud words
that the horn was speaking.
I watched until the animal was killed.
Its body was destroyed
and thrown into the fire to be burned.
[12] The power of the other animals was taken away.
But they were allowed to live a while longer,
for a set time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Suddenly the scene changes: a courtroom in heaven. God himself sits on the throne. He is called “the Ancient of Days” (that means: the one who is very old), because he is eternal. His white hair and clothes stand for wisdom and purity.
> The throne has wheels of fire, like the throne chariot in Ezekiel (Ezekiel 1).
> Verse 10: Countless angels serve him. “The court sat down to judge, and the books were opened.” That means: God judges what the powerful have done.
> Verse 11: The fourth animal with the proud horn is destroyed. God’s judgment ends the rule of violence.

## One like a son of man (Verses 13–14)

[13] “I looked in the visions at night,
and I saw one like a son of man
coming with the clouds of the sky.
He came to the Ancient of Days,
and he was brought to him.
[14] He was given power to rule, glory, and a kingdom,
so that all the peoples, nations, and people of every language would serve him.
His rule is a rule that lasts forever.
It will never pass away.
And his kingdom will never be destroyed.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important verses in the whole Bible!
> After the wild animals out of the sea, now comes “one like a son of man,” that is, like a human being. And he does not come out of the sea, but with the clouds of the sky. The animal kingdoms are violent. The kingdom of this “human being” is humane, and it comes from God. It will never pass away.
> Who is this “son of man”?
> – Verses 18 and 27 say that “the saints of the Most High” receive the kingdom. That is why many Jewish interpreters understand the son of man as a picture of God’s faithful people.
> – Other Jewish interpreters, already in ancient times, saw in him the Messiah.
> – In the New Testament, Jesus calls himself the “Son of Man” again and again. Before the high council, he says: “You will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of the sky” (Mark 14:62). For Christians, Jesus is this Son of Man.

## The meaning (Verses 15–28)

[15] “As for me, Daniel, my spirit was troubled inside me.
The visions in my head frightened me.
[16] I went to one of those who were standing there.
I asked him what all this really meant.
“So he told me
and explained to me what these things mean:
[17] ‘These four great animals
are four kings who will rise from the earth.
[18] But the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom.
They will have the kingdom forever,
yes, forever and ever.’
[19] “Then I wanted to know the truth about the fourth animal.
It was different from all the others
and very terrifying.
Its teeth were made of iron, and its claws of bronze.
It ate and crushed its victims,
and it stamped on what was left with its feet.
[20] I also wanted to know about the 10 horns on its head,
and about the other horn that came up.
Three horns fell to make room for it.
That horn had eyes
and a mouth that spoke proudly,
and it looked bigger than the others.
[21] As I watched,
this horn made war against the saints
and defeated them,
[22] until the Ancient of Days came.
Then judgment was given in favor of the saints of the Most High.
And the time came when the saints took over the kingdom.
[23] “He said,
‘The fourth animal will be a fourth kingdom on earth.
It will be different from all the other kingdoms.
It will eat up the whole earth.
It will trample it down and crush it.
[24] As for the 10 horns:
10 kings will rise out of this kingdom.
After them, another king will rise.
He will be different from the kings before him.
And he will bring down three kings.
[25] He will speak words against the Most High.
He will wear down the saints of the Most High.
He will plan to change the times and the law.
And the saints will be handed over to him
for a time, and times, and half a time.
[26] “‘But the court will sit down to judge.
His power will be taken away from him.
It will be completely wiped out and destroyed forever.
[27] Then the kingdom and the power to rule
and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole sky
will be given to the people of the saints of the Most High.
His kingdom is a kingdom that lasts forever.
And all rulers will serve him and obey him.’
[28] “This is the end of the matter.
As for me, Daniel, my thoughts frightened me very much,
and my face turned pale.
But I kept the matter in my heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> An angel explains: The four animals are four kingdoms. But in the end, “the saints of the Most High” receive the kingdom that lasts forever.
> Verse 18: Saints are people who belong to God.
> Verse 25: The proud ruler (the little horn) persecutes the believers. He plans “to change the times and the law.” That means: He wants to get rid of God’s feasts and commandments.
> “A time, and times, and half a time” is probably three and a half years (1 + 2 + ½), a limited time of suffering. The persecution has an end.
> Who is the little horn?
> – Many interpreters think of King Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175–164 BC). He forbade the Jews to keep the Sabbath and to circumcise their sons, and he made the temple in Jerusalem unclean. After about three and a half years, the temple was dedicated to God again (Jews still celebrate this today at the feast of Hanukkah).
> – Others, especially in the Christian tradition, take the fourth animal as Rome and the little horn as an enemy of God at the end of time.
> Important: Again and again, people have tried to apply these pictures to persons or countries of their own time, often with wrong results. The main message is clear and comforting: Even the worst tyrants have only a limited time. God’s kingdom will come and will last.
