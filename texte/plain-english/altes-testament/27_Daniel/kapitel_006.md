# Daniel – Chapter 6
### Daniel in the lions’ den

---

## A plot against Daniel (Verses 1–9)

[1] Darius decided
to put 120 local governors over the kingdom.
They were to be all over the kingdom.
[2] Over them he put three officials,
and Daniel was one of them.
The local governors were to report to them,
so that the king would not lose anything.
[3] This Daniel stood out above the other officials and the local governors,
because there was an excellent spirit in him.
And the king thought about putting him over the whole kingdom.
[4] Then the officials and the local governors looked for a reason to accuse Daniel
in the way he did his work for the kingdom.
But they could not find any reason or any fault,
because he was faithful.
No mistake and no fault was found in him.
[5] Then these men said,
“We will not find any reason to accuse this Daniel,
unless we find it in the law of his God.”
[6] So these officials and local governors went together to the king.
They said to him,
“King Darius, live forever!
[7] All the officials of the kingdom,
the deputies and the local governors,
the advisers and the governors,
have talked together.
They want you to make a royal law and a strict order:
For 30 days, whoever asks anything of any god or any human being,
except of you, O king,
will be thrown into the lions’ den.
[8] Now, O king, make this order
and sign the document,
so that it cannot be changed.
That is the law of the Medes and Persians,
which cannot be changed.”
[9] So King Darius signed the document and the order.

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, the verse numbers in this chapter are one higher (6:2–29), because there 5:31 is already counted as 6:1.
> By now, Daniel is an old man, and again he has a high position, this time under the new Persian Empire. He is so good and honest that his colleagues become jealous.
> Verse 4: High praise: They do not find a single fault in Daniel. He does not take bribes. He is not lazy and not dishonest.
> Verse 5: So they look for his only “weak spot”: his faith. They know that Daniel will stay faithful to God, no matter what happens.
> Verses 7–8: They flatter the king and get him to make a law that can no longer be changed. The king does not notice that he is being used.

## Daniel keeps praying (Verses 10–15)

[10] Daniel learned that the document had been signed.
Then he went into his house.
In his room, the windows were open toward Jerusalem.
Three times a day he knelt down on his knees.
He prayed and gave thanks to his God,
just as he had done before.
[11] Then these men came together.
They found Daniel praying and asking his God for help.
[12] Then they went to the king
and spoke to him about the king’s order:
“Didn’t you sign an order
that for 30 days, anyone who asks anything of any god or any human being,
except of you, O king,
will be thrown into the lions’ den?”
The king answered,
“That is true.
It is the law of the Medes and Persians,
which cannot be changed.”
[13] Then they said to the king,
“That Daniel, one of the captives from Judah,
does not respect you, O king,
or the order that you have signed.
He still prays three times a day.”
[14] When the king heard these words,
he was very upset.
He set his heart on saving Daniel.
Until the sun went down, he worked hard to rescue him.
[15] Then these men went together to the king.
They said to the king,
“You know, O king,
that it is a law of the Medes and Persians
that no order or law that the king makes
may be changed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Daniel does not change his habit. He keeps praying three times a day, with his windows open toward Jerusalem, “just as he had done before.” He does not make a show of it, but he does not hide either.
> Praying toward Jerusalem reminds us of Solomon’s prayer (1 Kings 8:48). To this day, Jews pray toward Jerusalem. And praying three times a day is still part of Jewish life today (compare Psalm 55:17).
> Verse 14: The king likes Daniel and tries all day to rescue him. But he is trapped by his own law. Even the most powerful man is powerless against an unjust law that he made himself.

## In the lions’ den (Verses 16–18)

[16] Then the king gave the order.
They brought Daniel and threw him into the lions’ den.
The king said to Daniel,
“Your God, whom you serve all the time,
will rescue you.”
[17] A stone was brought
and put over the opening of the den.
The king sealed it with his own signet ring
and with the signet rings of his nobles.
A signet ring is a ring with a stamp, used like a signature.
That way, nothing could be changed in Daniel’s case.
[18] Then the king went to his palace.
He spent the night without eating.
No musical instruments were brought to him,
and he could not sleep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: The king believes in other gods, but he hopes that Daniel’s God will rescue him.
> Verse 17: The den is closed with a stone and sealed. For Christians, this is a reminder of the tomb of Jesus. It was also closed with a stone and sealed (Matthew 27:66).
> Verse 18: The king cannot sleep. He fasts and worries. Fasting means: not eating for a time.

## God shut the lions’ mouths (Verses 19–24)

[19] Then the king got up very early in the morning.
He hurried to the lions’ den.
[20] When he came near the den where Daniel was,
he called out with a worried voice.
The king said to Daniel,
“Daniel, servant of the living God,
has your God, whom you serve all the time,
been able to rescue you from the lions?”
[21] Then Daniel said to the king,
“O king, live forever!
[22] My God has sent his angel,
and he has shut the lions’ mouths.
They have not hurt me,
because God found me innocent.
And I have not done anything wrong to you either, O king.”
[23] Then the king was very glad.
He ordered that Daniel be lifted out of the den.
So Daniel was lifted out of the den.
No injury of any kind was found on him,
because he had trusted in his God.
[24] Then the king gave an order.
They brought the men who had accused Daniel.
And they threw them into the lions’ den,
them, their children, and their wives.
The lions attacked them
and crushed all their bones
even before they reached the bottom of the den.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the morning, the king calls out, full of fear. And Daniel answers calmly: “My God has sent his angel, and he has shut the lions’ mouths.”
> Verse 23: The reason for the rescue: “because he had trusted in his God.” Compare Hebrews 11:33. There it says about people who trusted God: “Through faith they … stopped the mouths of lions.”
> Verse 24: This verse is very shocking. The king punishes not only the accusers, but also their innocent wives and children. That was the cruel custom of some rulers at that time. The Bible reports it, but it does not approve of it. God’s law says clearly that fathers must not be put to death for their children, and children must not be put to death for their fathers (Deuteronomy 24:16; compare Ezekiel 18:20). Punishing children for what their parents did is wrong.

## The king praises Daniel’s God (Verses 25–28)

[25] Then King Darius wrote
to all the peoples, nations, and people of every language
who live in the whole earth:
“May you have more and more peace!
[26] “I give this order:
In every part of my kingdom,
people must tremble and fear before the God of Daniel.
“For he is the living God.
He stands firm forever.
His kingdom will never be destroyed.
His rule will last to the very end.
[27] He saves and rescues.
He does signs and wonders in heaven and on earth.
He has rescued Daniel from the power of the lions.”
[28] So this Daniel did well
during the rule of Darius
and during the rule of Cyrus the Persian.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like Nebuchadnezzar in Chapters 3 and 4, now the Persian king also praises the God of Israel: “He is the living God.” “He saves and rescues.”
> Here the first part of the book ends, the part with the stories. The message of all six chapters: God is faithful to those who are faithful to him, even in a foreign, hostile place. And his kingdom is greater than all the kingdoms of the world.
> For today: All over the world, people are persecuted and thrown into prison because of their faith. Daniel’s story encourages us to stay faithful. And it reminds us to stand up for freedom of religion for all people.
