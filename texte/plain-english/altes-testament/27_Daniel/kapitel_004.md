# Daniel – Chapter 4
### The pride of Nebuchadnezzar

---

## A letter from the king (Verses 1–3)

[1] From King Nebuchadnezzar
to all the peoples, nations, and people of every language
who live in the whole earth:
May you have more and more peace!
[2] I am glad to tell you
about the signs and wonders
that the Most High God has done for me.
[3] How great are his signs!
How mighty are his wonders!
His kingdom is a kingdom that lasts forever.
His rule goes on from generation to generation.

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, these three verses are at the end of Chapter 3 (3:31–33). That is why the verse numbers in this chapter of the WEB are three higher than in many other Bibles. We follow the numbering of the WEB.
> An unusual chapter: It is a letter that the pagan King Nebuchadnezzar himself writes to the whole world. Pagan means: He believed in other gods. He tells how God humbled him and then raised him up again. At the beginning and at the end, he praises God.

---

## The dream of the great tree (Verses 4–18)

[4] I, Nebuchadnezzar, was living in peace in my house.
Things were going well for me in my palace.
[5] Then I had a dream that made me afraid.
The thoughts I had on my bed
and the visions in my head frightened me.
[6] So I gave an order
to bring all the wise men of Babylon to me,
so that they would tell me what the dream means.
[7] Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans, and the fortune-tellers came in.
I told them the dream,
but they could not tell me what it means.
[8] But at last Daniel came to me.
His name is Belteshazzar, after the name of my god.
The spirit of the holy gods is in him.
I told him the dream.
I said,
[9] “Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians,
I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you,
and that no secret is too hard for you.
Tell me the visions of the dream I have seen,
and tell me what it means.
[10] These were the visions in my head as I lay on my bed:
I looked, and I saw a tree in the middle of the earth.
It was very tall.
[11] The tree grew and became strong.
It was so tall that it reached up to the sky.
It could be seen to the ends of the whole earth.
[12] Its leaves were beautiful,
and it had a lot of fruit.
There was food on it for everyone.
The animals of the field found shade under it.
The birds of the sky lived in its branches.
And every living creature was fed from it.
[13] “As I lay on my bed, I saw in the visions in my head
a holy watcher coming down from the sky.
A watcher is an angel who keeps watch.
[14] He called out loudly,
‘Cut down the tree!
Cut off its branches!
Shake off its leaves,
and scatter its fruit!
Let the animals run away from under it
and the birds from its branches.
[15] But leave the stump with its roots in the earth,
with a band of iron and bronze around it,
in the soft grass of the field.
Let it get wet with the dew of the sky.
Let him have his share with the animals in the grass of the earth.
[16] Let his heart be changed
so that it is no longer a human heart.
Let him be given the heart of an animal.
Then let seven times pass over him.
[17] “‘This sentence was decided by the watchers.
The decision comes from the word of the holy ones.
It is so that the living may know
that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of human beings.
He gives them to whomever he wants.
He even puts the lowest of people over them.’
[18] “This is the dream that I, King Nebuchadnezzar, have seen.
Now you, Belteshazzar, tell me what it means.
For all the wise men of my kingdom cannot tell me what it means.
But you can,
for the spirit of the holy gods is in you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In his dream, the king sees a huge tree that reaches up to the sky and gives shade and food to all living creatures. Then a “holy watcher,” an angel, calls out that it should be cut down. Only the stump stays, with a band of metal.
> Verse 16: Suddenly the tree becomes a “him”: The tree is a human being, and he is to be given the heart of an animal.
> “Seven times” are probably seven years, or simply a complete, long time.
> Verse 17: That is the theme: Everyone should know “that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of human beings.” God can even make the lowest person a ruler.
> Verses 8–9: Nebuchadnezzar believes in other gods. So he speaks of “the spirit of the holy gods.” He still understands God within his own view of the world.

## Daniel explains the dream (Verses 19–27)

[19] Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, could not speak for a while.
His thoughts frightened him.
The king said,
“Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its meaning frighten you.”
Belteshazzar answered,
“My lord, I wish the dream were for those who hate you,
and its meaning for your enemies.
[20] You saw the tree
that grew and became strong.
It was so tall that it reached up to the sky.
It could be seen over the whole earth.
[21] Its leaves were beautiful, and it had a lot of fruit.
There was food on it for everyone.
The animals of the field lived under it.
The birds of the sky had their homes in its branches.
[22] O king, that tree is you.
You have grown and become strong.
Your greatness has grown and reaches up to the sky.
Your rule reaches to the end of the earth.
[23] “The king saw a holy watcher coming down from the sky.
He said,
‘Cut down the tree and destroy it.
But leave the stump with its roots in the earth,
with a band of iron and bronze around it, in the soft grass of the field.
Let it get wet with the dew of the sky.
Let him have his share with the animals of the field,
until seven times pass over him.’
[24] “O king, this is what it means.
It is a decision of the Most High
that has come upon my lord the king:
[25] You will be driven away from people.
You will live with the animals of the field.
You will be made to eat grass like cattle.
You will get wet with the dew of the sky.
Seven times will pass over you,
until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of human beings
and gives them to whomever he wants.
[26] It was ordered that the stump of the tree with its roots be left.
That means: Your kingdom will be given back to you
after you learn that Heaven rules.
[27] So, O king, please accept my advice:
Stop sinning, and do what is right.
Stop your evil deeds, and show mercy to the poor.
Then maybe your peace will last longer.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Daniel is frightened. He likes the king and does not wish him any harm. He says honestly: “My lord, I wish the dream were for those who hate you, and its meaning for your enemies.” Daniel is not a cold prophet of doom.
> Verse 26: The stump stays. That is the hope: After he has been humbled, the king will get his kingdom back.
> Verse 27: Daniel gives the king brave advice: “Stop sinning, and do what is right. Stop your evil deeds, and show mercy to the poor.” Doing what is right and helping the poor are the way to turn back to God, for every person, also for the powerful.
> “Heaven rules” (verse 26) – here “Heaven” stands for God. Later, in Judaism, people often said “Heaven” instead of “God” out of deep respect (compare “the kingdom of heaven” in Matthew).

## It comes true (Verses 28–33)

[28] All this happened to King Nebuchadnezzar.
[29] Twelve months later, he was walking around in the royal palace of Babylon.
[30] The king said,
“Isn’t this great Babylon?
I have built it as a royal home
by my own great power
and for the glory of my majesty.”
[31] The words were still in the king’s mouth
when a voice came from the sky,
“King Nebuchadnezzar, this is said to you:
‘The kingdom has been taken away from you.
[32] You will be driven away from people.
You will live with the animals of the field.
You will be made to eat grass like cattle.
Seven times will pass over you,
until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of human beings
and gives them to whomever he wants.’”
[33] That same hour, it happened to Nebuchadnezzar.
He was driven away from people.
He ate grass like cattle.
His body got wet with the dew of the sky,
until his hair had grown long like eagles’ feathers,
and his nails like birds’ claws.

> **What does this mean?**
> God gives the king a whole year to turn back. But the king changes nothing.
> Verse 30: The king is on the roof of his palace. He looks out over his splendid city and says: “Isn’t this great Babylon? I have built it as a royal home by my own great power and for the glory of my majesty.” He talks only about himself: I, my power, my majesty. Babylon really was splendid, with the famous Ishtar Gate and many palaces.
> Verses 31–33: At exactly this moment of pride, he falls deeply. He loses his mind and lives like an animal.
> Some interpreters think of a rare mental illness in which people believe they are animals. Others connect the story with the last Babylonian king, Nabonidus, who lived for many years in the desert, far away from Babylon. Either way: The text shows how fragile human power is, and also the human mind.
> Important: Mental illnesses are not a punishment from God. People who are mentally ill need help and understanding, not blame.

## The king praises the Most High (Verses 34–37)

[34] At the end of that time, I, Nebuchadnezzar, looked up to heaven,
and my mind came back to me.
I praised the Most High.
I praised and honored him who lives forever.
For his rule lasts forever,
and his kingdom goes on from generation to generation.
[35] All the people who live on the earth are counted as nothing.
He does what he wants with the army of heaven
and with the people who live on the earth.
No one can stop his hand
or say to him, “What are you doing?”
[36] At that same time, my mind came back to me.
For the glory of my kingdom,
my majesty and my splendor came back to me.
My advisers and my nobles came looking for me.
I was set up again over my kingdom,
and I was given even greater power.
[37] Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise, lift up, and honor
the King of heaven.
For everything he does is true,
and his ways are just.
And he is able to bring down
those who walk in pride.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 34: That is the turning point. The king tells: “I, Nebuchadnezzar, looked up to heaven, and my mind came back to me.” That means: At the moment he looks up and accepts God, he becomes healthy again.
> Verse 37: At the end, the most powerful king in the world praises the “King of heaven.” And he has learned: “He is able to bring down those who walk in pride.”
> The message: God can humble even the most powerful. But he does it so that they come to understand, not to destroy them. God gives even the proud king a second chance.
> Compare Proverbs 16:18. There it says that pride comes before destruction, and a proud spirit comes before a fall. And Luke 1:52 says that God brings down rulers from their thrones and lifts up the lowly.
