# Ezra – Chapter 2
### The list of those who returned

---

## The leaders (Verses 1–2)

[1] These are the people of the province
who came back from captivity.
Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried them away to Babylon.
Now they returned to Jerusalem and Judah, each to his own city.
[2] They came with Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Seraiah, Reelaiah, Mordecai,
Bilshan, Mispar, Bigvai, Rehum, and Baanah.
The number of the men of the people of Israel:

> **What does this mean?**
> This long list appears again almost the same in Nehemiah 7. It shows: every single person was important. God knows the names.
> “The province” is Judah, now a small province in the great Persian Empire.
> Zerubbabel was a grandson of King Jehoiachin, so a descendant of David (1 Chronicles 3:17–19). Jeshua was the high priest. Both lead the return.
> The Nehemiah and the Mordecai in this list are probably not the same as the famous men from the books of Nehemiah and Esther. These names were common.
> The list names 11 leaders. In Nehemiah 7:7 there are 12, like the 12 tribes of Israel.

[3] The descendants of Parosh: 2,172.
[4] The descendants of Shephatiah: 372.
[5] The descendants of Arah: 775.
[6] The descendants of Pahathmoab, of the descendants of Jeshua and Joab: 2,812.
[7] The descendants of Elam: 1,254.
[8] The descendants of Zattu: 945.
[9] The descendants of Zaccai: 760.
[10] The descendants of Bani: 642.
[11] The descendants of Bebai: 623.
[12] The descendants of Azgad: 1,222.
[13] The descendants of Adonikam: 666.
[14] The descendants of Bigvai: 2,056.
[15] The descendants of Adin: 454.
[16] The descendants of Ater, of Hezekiah: 98.
[17] The descendants of Bezai: 323.
[18] The descendants of Jorah: 112.
[19] The descendants of Hashum: 223.
[20] The descendants of Gibbar: 95.

> **What does this mean?**
> These families are named after their ancestors. “Pahathmoab” means “governor of Moab.” Maybe an ancestor once held this office.
> The number 666 in verse 13 has no special meaning here. It is simply the number of people in this family.

## The people from the towns (Verses 21–35)

[21] The people of Bethlehem: 123.
[22] The men of Netophah: 56.
[23] The men of Anathoth: 128.
[24] The people of Azmaveth: 42.
[25] The people of Kiriath Arim, Chephirah, and Beeroth: 743.
[26] The people of Ramah and Geba: 621.
[27] The men of Michmas: 122.
[28] The men of Bethel and Ai: 223.
[29] The people of Nebo: 52.
[30] The people of Magbish: 156.
[31] The descendants of the other Elam: 1,254.
[32] The descendants of Harim: 320.
[33] The people of Lod, Hadid, and Ono: 725.
[34] The people of Jericho: 345.
[35] The descendants of Senaah: 3,630.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the people are listed by their home towns. They return to the towns their families came from.
> Bethlehem is the town of David. Anathoth is the home town of the prophet Jeremiah. Before the destruction he had bought a field there, as a sign of hope that people would come back again (Jeremiah 32). Now 128 men from Anathoth return.
> Kiriath Arim is probably Kiriath Jearim, where the ark of the covenant stayed for 20 years.
> Elam, Harim and maybe also Senaah are probably family names here, not places.

## The priests (Verses 36–39)

[36] The priests:
the descendants of Jedaiah, of the house of Jeshua: 973.
[37] The descendants of Immer: 1,052.
[38] The descendants of Pashhur: 1,247.
[39] The descendants of Harim: 1,017.

> **What does this mean?**
> More than 4,000 priests return. That is about one tenth of all who return. Worship was very important to those who returned.

---

## The Levites, singers and gatekeepers (Verses 40–42)

[40] The Levites:
the descendants of Jeshua and Kadmiel, of the descendants of Hodaviah: 74.
[41] The singers:
the descendants of Asaph: 128.
[42] The descendants of the gatekeepers:
the descendants of Shallum, the descendants of Ater, the descendants of Talmon,
the descendants of Akkub, the descendants of Hatita, the descendants of Shobai,
in all 139.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only a few Levites return, far fewer than priests. Maybe in Babylon they had little reason to hope that they would find a good task in Jerusalem. Later Ezra had to search especially for Levites (Chapter 8:15–20).

## The descendants of Solomon’s servants (Verses 55–58)

[55] The descendants of Solomon’s servants:
the descendants of Sotai, the descendants of Hassophereth, the descendants of Peruda,
[56] the descendants of Jaalah, the descendants of Darkon, the descendants of Giddel,
[57] the descendants of Shephatiah, the descendants of Hattil,
the descendants of Pochereth Hazzebaim, the descendants of Ami.
[58] All the temple servants and the descendants of Solomon’s servants were 392.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “temple servants” helped with simple work in the temple, for example fetching wood and carrying water. Some of their names do not sound Hebrew. Maybe their ancestors came from other peoples, for example from Gibeon (Joshua 9:27).
> The “servants of Solomon” too were probably descendants of workers from foreign peoples (1 Kings 9:20–21).
> They too now firmly belong to God’s people and return with the others. Every name is mentioned. No one is too small.
> The name Sisera in verse 53 is the same as that of the army commander in Judges 4. But here a family of temple servants is meant.

## Those without proof (Verses 59–63)

[59] These were the ones who went up from Tel Melah, Tel Harsha, Cherub, Addan, and Immer.
But they could not prove from their family records
that they were from Israel:
[60] the descendants of Delaiah, the descendants of Tobiah, the descendants of Nekoda: 652.
[61] Of the descendants of the priests:
the descendants of Habaiah, the descendants of Hakkoz,
and the descendants of Barzillai.
He had married one of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite
and was called by their name.
[62] These looked for their names among those who were listed in the family records,
but they were not found.
So they were not allowed to serve and were removed from the priesthood.
[63] The governor told them that they should not eat of the most holy things
until a priest stood up to serve with Urim and with Thummim.

> **What does this mean?**
> Some could not prove that they belonged to Israel. Their papers had been lost in the exile. Even so they returned with the others. They were not sent away.
> With the priests it was stricter: only someone who descended from Aaron was allowed to be a priest. Whoever could not prove this was not allowed to serve for the time being.
> Barzillai the Gileadite was a friend of David who had helped him in a hard time (2 Samuel 19:31–39). A priest had married his daughter and taken her family name.
> “Urim and Thummim” were lots with which the high priest used to ask for God’s will (Exodus 28:30). They had probably been lost. The decision was postponed until God himself would give clarity. That is a wise and fair solution.
> The “governor” is probably Zerubbabel or Sheshbazzar. Here a Persian word is used: “Tirshatha.”

## The total number and the gifts (Verses 64–70)

[64] The whole assembly together was 42,360,
[65] besides their male servants and their female servants,
of whom there were 7,337.
And they had 200 singing men and singing women.
[66] Their horses were 736, their mules 245,
[67] their camels 435, their donkeys 6,720.
[68] Some of the heads of the families,
when they came to the LORD’s house which is in Jerusalem,
gave willingly for God’s house, to set it up in its place.
[69] They gave according to their ability into the treasury for the work:
61,000 darics of gold (more than 1,100 pounds),
5,000 minas of silver (about 6,250 pounds),
and 100 priests’ garments.
[70] So the priests and the Levites, with some of the people,
the singers, the gatekeepers, and the temple servants, lived in their cities,
and all Israel lived in their cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> If you add up the single numbers of the list, you get only about 29,800. The total number is 42,360. Maybe the total number also counted women or other groups that do not appear one by one in the list. No one knows exactly.
> So in all almost 50,000 people return. That is only a small part of the people. Most stayed in Babylon.
> “Darics” were Persian gold coins of about 0.3 ounces. 61,000 darics are more than 1,100 pounds of gold. Silver: the Bible says “5,000 minas.” A mina is about 1¼ pounds.
> The people gave “according to their ability”: each one as much as he could (compare 2 Corinthians 8:12).
> The numbers here are a little different from Nehemiah 7:70–72. When such lists were copied, small differences often happened.
