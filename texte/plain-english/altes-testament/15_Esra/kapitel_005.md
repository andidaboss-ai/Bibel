# Ezra – Chapter 5
### The building goes on

---

## The prophets Haggai and Zechariah (Verses 1–2)

[1] The prophets Haggai and Zechariah, the son of Iddo,
spoke God’s message to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem.
They spoke to them in the name of the God of Israel.
[2] Then Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua, the son of Jozadak, got to work.
They began to build God’s house in Jerusalem again.
The prophets of God were with them and helped them.

> **What does this mean?**
> For 16 years, the people had not worked on the temple any more. They had settled down and built their own houses. Then God sends two prophets. Their words are in the books of Haggai and Zechariah.
> Haggai says: “Is it time for you yourselves to live in your houses with fine wooden walls, while this house lies in ruins?” (Haggai 1:4). And Zechariah says: “Not by might, and not by power, but by my Spirit … says the LORD of Armies” (Zechariah 4:6).
> The prophets do not only preach, they also help. God’s word and practical help belong together.
> The Aramaic says word for word: “in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them.” Many understand it like this: God watched over them.

---

## Tattenai asks questions (Verses 3–5)

[3] At that time, Tattenai came to them.
He was the governor of the land beyond the River.
Shetharbozenai and their officials came with him.
They asked the Jews,
“Who gave you permission to build this house and to finish this wall?”
[4] They also asked for the names of the men who were putting up this building.
[5] But God was watching over the elders of the Jews.
So the officials did not make them stop.
First the matter had to go to Darius,
and his answer had to come back in a letter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Tattenai was the Persian governor of the whole province west of the Euphrates. His name is also found in a Persian document from this time.
> Unlike the opponents in Chapter 4, Tattenai acts fairly: he asks questions, writes to the king, and lets the Jews go on building in the meantime.
> “God was watching over the elders”: the English source text says, “the eye of their God was on the elders.” That is why they were allowed to go on building.

---

## The letter to Darius (Verses 6–17)

[6] Tattenai, the governor of the land beyond the River,
Shetharbozenai, and his officials, the Apharsachites beyond the River,
sent a letter to King Darius.
Here is a copy of it.
[7] This is what they wrote to him:
“To King Darius: We wish you peace in every way.
[8] We want the king to know this:
We went to the province of Judah,
to the house of the great God.
It is being built with large stones.
Wooden beams are being set into the walls.
The work is being done with great care.
It is going well for them.
[9] We asked the elders there,
‘Who gave you permission to build this house and to finish this wall?’
[10] We also asked for their names.
We wanted to write down the names of their leaders for you.
[11] This is the answer they gave us:
‘We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth.
We are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago.
A great king of Israel built it and finished it.
[12] But our ancestors made the God of heaven angry.
So he handed them over to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, the Chaldean.
He destroyed this house
and took the people away to Babylon.
[13] But in his first year as king of Babylon,
King Cyrus gave an order to rebuild this house of God.
[14] Nebuchadnezzar had taken the gold and silver objects of God’s house
out of the temple in Jerusalem.
He had brought them into the temple in Babylon.
King Cyrus took them out of the temple in Babylon again.
He gave them to a man named Sheshbazzar,
whom he had made governor.
[15] Cyrus said to him,
“Take these objects.
Go and put them in the temple in Jerusalem.
Let God’s house be rebuilt in its old place.”
[16] So this Sheshbazzar came
and laid the foundations of God’s house in Jerusalem.
Since then, people have been building it until now.
But it is still not finished.’
[17] Now, if the king agrees,
please have a search made in the royal archives in Babylon.
Find out whether King Cyrus really gave an order
to rebuild this house of God in Jerusalem.
Then please let us know what the king decides about this.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The letter is factual and fair. It reports exactly what the Jews said.
> The answer of the elders is brave and honest:
> – They say openly who they are: servants of the God of heaven and earth.
> – They admit that their fathers were to blame for the destruction.
> – They refer to the order of Cyrus.
> Cyrus is called “king of Babylon” here, because he had conquered Babylon and also had this title.
> A great king of Israel: this means Solomon.
> “Apharsachites”: probably a Persian title for officials, something like “officers” or “messengers.”
