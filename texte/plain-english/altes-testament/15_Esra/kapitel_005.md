# Ezra – Chapter 5
### The building goes on

---

## The prophets Haggai and Zechariah (Verses 1–2)

[1] Now the prophets, Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo,
spoke as prophets to the Jews who were in Judah and Jerusalem.
They spoke to them in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them.
[2] Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and Jeshua the son of Jozadak rose up
and began to build God’s house in Jerusalem.
And the prophets of God were with them, helping them.

> **What does this mean?**
> For 16 years the people had not worked on the temple any more. They had settled down and built their own houses. Then God sends two prophets. Their words are in the books of Haggai and Zechariah.
> Haggai says: “Is it time for you yourselves to live in your houses with fine wooden walls, while this house lies in ruins?” (Haggai 1:4). And Zechariah says: “Not by might, and not by power, but by my Spirit … says the LORD of Armies” (Zechariah 4:6).
> The prophets do not only preach, they also help. God’s word and practical help belong together.
> The Aramaic says word for word: “in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them”. Many understand it like this: God watched over them.

---

## Tattenai asks questions (Verses 3–5)

[3] At the same time Tattenai, the governor beyond the River,
came to them with Shetharbozenai and their companions.
They asked them,
“Who gave you an order to build this house and to finish this wall?”
[4] They also asked for the names of the men who were putting up this building.
[5] But the eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews.
They didn’t make them stop until the matter could come to Darius,
and an answer could come back by letter about it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Tattenai was the Persian governor of the whole province west of the Euphrates. His name is also found in a Persian document from this time.
> Unlike the opponents in Chapter 4, Tattenai acts fairly: he asks questions, writes to the king, and lets the Jews go on building in the meantime.
> “The eye of their God was on the elders”: God watched over them. That is why they were allowed to go on building.

---

## The letter to Darius (Verses 6–17)

[6] This is the copy of the letter that Tattenai, the governor beyond the River,
and Shetharbozenai and his companions, the Apharsachites who were beyond the River,
sent to Darius the king.
[7] They sent him a letter, in which was written:
“To Darius the king, all peace.
[8] The king should know that we went into the province of Judah,
to the house of the great God.
It is being built with great stones, and timber is laid in the walls.
This work goes on with care and goes well in their hands.
[9] Then we asked those elders and said to them,
‘Who gave you an order to build this house and to finish this wall?’
[10] We also asked them their names, to inform you,
so that we could write down the names of the men who lead them.
[11] They gave us this answer:
‘We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth.
We are building the house that was built many years ago,
which a great king of Israel built and finished.
[12] But our fathers made the God of heaven angry.
So he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean,
who destroyed this house and carried the people away to Babylon.
[13] But in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon,
Cyrus the king gave an order to build this house of God.
[14] Nebuchadnezzar had taken the gold and silver objects of God’s house
out of the temple in Jerusalem and brought them into the temple of Babylon.
Cyrus the king also took them out of the temple of Babylon,
and they were handed over to a man named Sheshbazzar,
whom he had made governor.
[15] He said to him,
“Take these objects, go, put them in the temple in Jerusalem,
and let God’s house be built in its place.”
[16] Then this same Sheshbazzar came
and laid the foundations of God’s house in Jerusalem.
Since that time until now it has been under construction,
and still it is not finished.’
[17] Now, if it seems good to the king,
let a search be made in the king’s treasure house there in Babylon,
whether it is true that Cyrus the king gave an order
to build this house of God in Jerusalem.
And let the king send us his decision about this matter.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The letter is factual and fair. It reports exactly what the Jews said.
> The answer of the elders is brave and honest:
> – They say openly who they are: servants of the God of heaven and earth.
> – They admit that their fathers were to blame for the destruction.
> – They refer to the order of Cyrus.
> Cyrus is called “king of Babylon” here, because he had conquered Babylon and also had this title.
> A great king of Israel: this means Solomon.
> “Apharsachites”: probably a Persian title for officials, something like “officers” or “messengers”.
