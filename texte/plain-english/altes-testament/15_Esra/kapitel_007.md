# Ezra – Chapter 7
### Ezra comes to Jerusalem

---

## Who was Ezra? (Verses 1–10)

[1] Some time later, Artaxerxes was king of Persia.
At that time there was a man named Ezra.
Ezra was the son of Seraiah, the son of Azariah, the son of Hilkiah,
[2] the son of Shallum, the son of Zadok, the son of Ahitub,
[3] the son of Amariah, the son of Azariah, the son of Meraioth,
[4] the son of Zerahiah, the son of Uzzi, the son of Bukki,
[5] the son of Abishua, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eleazar,
the son of Aaron the chief priest.
[6] This Ezra came up from Babylon.
He was a scribe, an expert in the Law of Moses.
The LORD, the God of Israel, had given this law.
The king gave Ezra everything he asked for,
because the hand of the LORD his God was on him.
[7] Some of the people of Israel went up to Jerusalem with him.
Among them were priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, and temple servants.
This was in the seventh year that Artaxerxes was king.
[8] Ezra came to Jerusalem in the fifth month
of the king’s seventh year.
[9] He had left Babylon on the first day of the first month.
He arrived in Jerusalem on the first day of the fifth month,
because the good hand of his God was on him.
[10] Ezra had made up his mind to study the LORD’s law,
to obey it,
and to teach its rules and laws in Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Almost 60 years lie between Chapter 6 and 7. Ezra came to Jerusalem in the seventh year of Artaxerxes (Artaxerxes I), that is, 458 before Christ. The story of Esther takes place in the time between.
> The family tree shows: Ezra descends directly from Aaron, the first high priest. Seraiah was the last high priest before the destruction of Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:18). “Son” here also means “descendant.” Some generations are skipped.
> Ezra was a “scribe”. He knew God’s law very well. In Jewish tradition he counts as a second Moses, because he put the law back at the center.
> The journey took four months. The way from Babylon to Jerusalem is more than 600 miles long.
> Verse 10 is an example: Ezra wanted first to study God’s word, second to live by it himself, and third to teach others. In this order.
> “The good hand of his God was on him”: this sentence comes several times in the book of Ezra. Ezra knew that his success came from God.

## The king’s letter (Verses 11–26)

[11] King Artaxerxes gave a letter to Ezra the priest and scribe.
Ezra was an expert in the commandments of the LORD
and in the rules he gave to Israel.
This is a copy of the letter:
[12] “From Artaxerxes, king of kings,
to Ezra the priest, the scribe who teaches the law of the God of heaven.
Greetings.
[13] I give this order:
Any of the people of Israel in my kingdom may go with you to Jerusalem,
if they want to.
This includes their priests and the Levites.
[14] The king and his seven advisers are sending you.
You are to find out how things are in Judah and Jerusalem.
Do this by the law of your God, which you have with you.
[15] Take with you the silver and gold
that the king and his advisers have freely given to the God of Israel.
His home is in Jerusalem.
[16] Also take all the silver and gold you can get in the whole province of Babylon.
Take the gifts that the people and the priests freely give
for the house of their God in Jerusalem.
[17] With this money, be careful to buy bulls, rams, and lambs,
with their grain offerings and drink offerings.
Offer them on the altar of the house of your God in Jerusalem.
[18] You may do whatever you and your relatives think best with the rest of the silver and gold.
Do it according to the will of your God.
[19] You have been given objects for the service in the house of your God.
Bring all of them before the God of Jerusalem.
[20] Maybe you will need more for the house of your God.
Then you may take it from the king’s treasury.
[21] I, King Artaxerxes, give this order to all the treasurers in the land beyond the River:
Ezra the priest is a scribe of the law of the God of heaven.
Whatever he asks of you, do it carefully,
[22] up to 100 talents of silver, about 7,500 pounds,
100 cors of wheat, about 600 bushels,
100 baths of wine, about 600 gallons,
100 baths of olive oil,
and as much salt as he needs.
[23] Whatever the God of heaven commands for his house,
do it exactly.
Why should his anger come on the kingdom of the king and his sons?
[24] We also tell you this:
You may not make the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants,
or other workers of this house of God pay any taxes or fees.
[25] And you, Ezra, use the wisdom your God has given you.
Appoint officials and judges
to judge all the people in the land beyond the River,
all who know the laws of your God.
And teach those who do not know them.
[26] Some people will not obey the law of your God and the law of the king.
Punish them strictly.
They may be put to death,
or sent out of the country,
or lose their property,
or be put in prison.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This letter is again written in Aramaic, the official language of the Persian Empire (verses 12–26).
> “King of kings” was the title of the Persian great kings. The “seven advisers” were the king’s highest council (compare Esther 1:14).
> The Bible says “100 talents of silver,” “100 cors of wheat” and “100 baths of wine” and of oil. A talent is about 75 pounds, a cor about 6 bushels, a bath about 6 gallons.
> The king is very generous. Why? Verse 23 gives a reason: he does not want the God of Israel to be angry with his kingdom. The Persian kings wanted all the gods in their empire to be content.
> Ezra receives a great task: he is to make God’s law count in the province and to teach the people.
> Verse 26 names harsh punishments. That was the law of the Persian state. It shows the power that the king gave to Ezra. Today Jews and Christians say: faith must not be forced with the power of the state.

## Ezra’s praise (Verses 27–28)

[27] Praise the LORD, the God of our ancestors!
He has put it into the king’s heart
to make the LORD’s house in Jerusalem beautiful.
[28] He has shown me his loving kindness
before the king and his advisers
and before all the king’s powerful officials.
The hand of the LORD my God was on me,
and that gave me courage.
So I gathered leaders from Israel to go up with me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Ezra begins to tell the story himself, using “I”. This goes on until Chapter 9. These parts are called the “Ezra memoir”.
> Ezra is thankful. He knows: it was not his cleverness that convinced the king, but God put this into the king’s heart (compare Proverbs 21:1).
