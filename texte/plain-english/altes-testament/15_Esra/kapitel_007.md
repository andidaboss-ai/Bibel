# Ezra – Chapter 7
### Ezra comes to Jerusalem

---

## Who was Ezra? (Verses 1–10)

[1] After these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia,
there was Ezra the son of Seraiah, the son of Azariah, the son of Hilkiah,
[2] the son of Shallum, the son of Zadok, the son of Ahitub,
[3] the son of Amariah, the son of Azariah, the son of Meraioth,
[4] the son of Zerahiah, the son of Uzzi, the son of Bukki,
[5] the son of Abishua, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eleazar,
the son of Aaron the chief priest.
[6] This Ezra went up from Babylon.
He was a skilled scribe in the law of Moses,
which the LORD, the God of Israel, had given.
The king gave him all he asked for,
because the hand of the LORD his God was on him.
[7] Some of the children of Israel went up to Jerusalem with him,
among them some of the priests, the Levites, the singers, the gatekeepers, and the temple servants,
in the seventh year of Artaxerxes the king.
[8] He came to Jerusalem in the fifth month,
in the seventh year of the king.
[9] For on the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylon,
and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem,
according to the good hand of his God on him.
[10] For Ezra had set his heart to study the LORD’s law, and to do it,
and to teach rules and laws in Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Almost 60 years lie between Chapter 6 and 7. Ezra came to Jerusalem in the seventh year of Artaxerxes (Artaxerxes I), that is 458 before Christ. The story of Esther takes place in the time between.
> The family tree shows: Ezra descends directly from Aaron, the first high priest. Seraiah was the last high priest before the destruction of Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:18). “Son” here also means “descendant”. Some generations are skipped.
> Ezra was a “scribe”. He knew God’s law very well. In Jewish tradition he counts as a second Moses, because he put the law back at the center.
> The journey took four months. The way from Babylon to Jerusalem is more than 600 miles long.
> Verse 10 is an example: Ezra wanted first to study God’s word, second to live by it himself, and third to teach others. In this order.
> “The good hand of his God on him”: this sentence comes several times in the book of Ezra. Ezra knew that his success came from God.

## The king’s letter (Verses 11–26)

[11] This is the copy of the letter that King Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest, the scribe,
a scribe of the words of the LORD’s commandments and of his rules for Israel:
[12] “Artaxerxes, king of kings,
to Ezra the priest, the scribe of the law of the perfect God of heaven.
Now:
[13] I give an order that all of the people of Israel and their priests and the Levites in my kingdom
who want of their own free will to go to Jerusalem may go with you.
[14] For you are sent by the king and his seven advisers
to look into the situation in Judah and Jerusalem,
according to the law of your God which is in your hand,
[15] and to carry the silver and gold
which the king and his advisers have freely given to the God of Israel,
whose dwelling is in Jerusalem,
[16] and all the silver and gold that you will find in the whole province of Babylon,
with the freewill offering of the people and of the priests,
who give willingly for the house of their God in Jerusalem.
[17] So with this money you shall carefully buy bulls, rams, and lambs,
with their grain offerings and their drink offerings,
and offer them on the altar of the house of your God in Jerusalem.
[18] Whatever seems good to you and to your brothers to do with the rest of the silver and the gold,
do that according to the will of your God.
[19] Hand over the objects that are given to you for the service of the house of your God
before the God of Jerusalem.
[20] Whatever more is needed for the house of your God,
which you may have to provide,
give it out of the king’s treasure house.
[21] I, Artaxerxes the king, give an order to all the treasurers beyond the River:
whatever Ezra the priest, the scribe of the law of the God of heaven, asks of you,
it shall be done with all care,
[22] up to 100 talents of silver, about 7,500 pounds,
100 cors of wheat, about 600 bushels,
100 baths of wine, about 600 gallons,
100 baths of oil,
and salt without limit.
[23] Whatever is commanded by the God of heaven,
let it be done exactly for the house of the God of heaven.
For why should there be anger against the kingdom of the king and his sons?
[24] We also tell you
that no one is allowed to make any of the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants,
or workers of this house of God pay tribute, tax, or toll.
[25] And you, Ezra, according to the wisdom of your God that is in your hand,
appoint officials and judges
who may judge all the people beyond the River,
all who know the laws of your God.
And teach those who don’t know them.
[26] Whoever will not obey the law of your God and the law of the king,
let judgment be carried out on him with all care,
whether death, or banishment, or taking away his goods, or prison.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This letter is again written in Aramaic, the official language of the Persian Empire (verses 12–26).
> “King of kings” was the title of the Persian great kings. The “seven advisers” were the king’s highest council (compare Esther 1:14).
> The Bible says “100 talents of silver”, “100 cors of wheat” and “100 baths of wine and oil”. A talent is about 75 pounds, a cor about 6 bushels, a bath about 6 gallons.
> The king is very generous. Why? Verse 23 gives a reason: he does not want the God of Israel to be angry with his kingdom. The Persian kings wanted all the gods in their empire to be content.
> Ezra receives a great task: he is to make God’s law count in the province and to teach the people.
> Verse 26 names harsh punishments. That was the law of the Persian state. It shows the power that the king gave to Ezra. Today Jews and Christians say: faith must not be forced with the power of the state.

## Ezra’s praise (Verses 27–28)

[27] Blessed be the LORD, the God of our fathers,
who has put such a thing as this into the king’s heart,
to make beautiful the LORD’s house in Jerusalem,
[28] and who has shown loving kindness to me
before the king and his advisers,
and before all the king’s mighty princes.
I was strengthened, because the hand of the LORD my God was on me,
and I gathered leading men out of Israel to go up with me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Ezra begins to tell the story himself, using “I”. This goes on until Chapter 9. These parts are called the “Ezra memoir”.
> Ezra is thankful. He knows: it was not his cleverness that convinced the king, but God put this into the king’s heart (compare Proverbs 21:1).
