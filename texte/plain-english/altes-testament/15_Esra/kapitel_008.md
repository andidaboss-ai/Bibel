# Ezra – Chapter 8
### The journey to Jerusalem

---

## Who went with Ezra (Verses 1–14)

[1] These are the heads of their families,
and this is the family list of those who went up with me from Babylon,
in the reign of Artaxerxes the king:
[2] Of the descendants of Phinehas, Gershom.
Of the descendants of Ithamar, Daniel.
Of the descendants of David, Hattush.
[3] Of the descendants of Shecaniah, of the descendants of Parosh, Zechariah.
With him 150 men were listed in the family list.
[4] Of the descendants of Pahathmoab, Eliehoenai the son of Zerahiah,
and with him 200 men.
[5] Of the descendants of Shecaniah, the son of Jahaziel,
and with him 300 men.
[6] Of the descendants of Adin, Ebed the son of Jonathan,
and with him 50 men.
[7] Of the descendants of Elam, Jeshaiah the son of Athaliah,
and with him 70 men.
[8] Of the descendants of Shephatiah, Zebadiah the son of Michael,
and with him 80 men.
[9] Of the descendants of Joab, Obadiah the son of Jehiel,
and with him 218 men.
[10] Of the descendants of Shelomith, the son of Josiphiah,
and with him 160 men.
[11] Of the descendants of Bebai, Zechariah the son of Bebai,
and with him 28 men.
[12] Of the descendants of Azgad, Johanan the son of Hakkatan,
and with him 110 men.
[13] Of the descendants of Adonikam, who were the last,
these are their names: Eliphelet, Jeuel, and Shemaiah,
and with them 60 men.
[14] Of the descendants of Bigvai, Uthai and Zabbud,
and with them 70 men.

> **What does this mean?**
> Together there are about 1,500 men, and women and children came with them (verse 21).
> We already know many of these families from Chapter 2. Part of the family had already returned back then. Now the rest comes. “The last” in verse 13 probably means: the last members of this family now return home.
> Hattush was a descendant of David (1 Chronicles 3:22).
> In verses 5 and 10 a family name is probably missing in the Hebrew text. Old Greek translations also name the families Zattu and Bani there.

---

## Ezra looks for Levites (Verses 15–20)

[15] I gathered them together at the river that runs to Ahava,
and there we camped for three days.
Then I looked over the people and the priests,
and found none of the sons of Levi there.
[16] Then I sent for Eliezer, Ariel, Shemaiah, Elnathan, Jarib, Elnathan, Nathan, Zechariah, and Meshullam,
who were leading men,
and also for Joiarib and Elnathan, who were teachers.
[17] I sent them to Iddo, the leader at the place Casiphia.
I told them what they should say to Iddo and his brothers the temple servants at the place Casiphia,
so that they would bring us servants for the house of our God.
[18] Because the good hand of our God was on us,
they brought us a man of good sense,
of the descendants of Mahli, the son of Levi, the son of Israel,
namely Sherebiah, with his sons and his brothers, 18 men,
[19] and Hashabiah, and with him Jeshaiah of the descendants of Merari,
his brothers and their sons, 20 men,
[20] and of the temple servants, whom David and the princes had given to serve the Levites,
220 temple servants.
All of them were named by name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again Levites are missing (compare Chapter 2:40). Many had settled down in Babylon and did not want to go back.
> Ezra acts wisely: he sends respected men and teachers to convince the Levites. Casiphia was probably a place in Babylonia where many Levites and temple servants lived.
> In the end 38 Levites and 220 temple servants come along. Ezra sees God’s good hand in this.

## Fasting and prayer (Verses 21–23)

[21] Then I announced a fast there at the river Ahava,
so that we would humble ourselves before our God,
to ask him for a safe journey for us, for our little ones, and for all our possessions.
[22] For I was ashamed to ask the king for a troop of soldiers and horsemen
to help us against the enemy on the way.
For we had said to the king,
“The hand of our God is on all those who seek him, for good.
But his power and his anger are against all those who abandon him.”
[23] So we fasted and begged our God for this,
and he answered our prayer.

> **What does this mean?**
> The journey was dangerous. They carried a lot of gold and silver with them, and there were robbers.
> Ezra had told the king about God’s protection. Now he does not want to ask for soldiers. It would have seemed to him as if he himself did not believe what he had said.
> Ezra fasts and prays with everyone. He takes the danger seriously, but he trusts in God.
> Later Nehemiah let the king send soldiers with him (Nehemiah 2:9). Both can be right. God can protect through miracles and through human help.

## The treasures are entrusted (Verses 24–30)

[24] Then I set apart twelve of the leaders of the priests,
Sherebiah, Hashabiah, and ten of their brothers with them.
[25] I weighed out to them the silver, the gold, and the objects,
the offering for the house of our God,
which the king, his advisers, his princes, and all Israel who were there had given.
[26] I weighed into their hand 650 talents of silver, about 24 tons,
silver objects weighing 100 talents, about 7,500 pounds,
100 talents of gold, about 7,500 pounds,
[27] 20 bowls of gold worth 1,000 darics,
and two objects of fine shining bronze, as precious as gold.
[28] I said to them,
“You are holy to the LORD, and the objects are holy.
The silver and the gold are a freewill offering to the LORD, the God of your fathers.
[29] Watch over them and keep them
until you weigh them before the leaders of the priests, the Levites,
and the princes of the families of Israel in Jerusalem,
in the rooms of the LORD’s house.”
[30] So the priests and the Levites received the silver, the gold, and the objects by weight,
to bring them to Jerusalem to the house of our God.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says “650 talents of silver, 100 talents of silver objects, 100 talents of gold”. A talent is about 75 pounds. These are huge amounts. Some experts think that the numbers became larger in copying. What matters is: it was a great treasure.
> “1,000 darics”: darics were Persian gold coins of about 0.3 ounces.
> Ezra weighs everything exactly and hands it over to trustworthy men. In Jerusalem everything is weighed again. Whoever handles money for God should be honest and exact (compare 2 Corinthians 8:20–21).
> “You are holy to the LORD, and the objects are holy”: the men are set apart for God, just like the treasures.

## Arrival in Jerusalem (Verses 31–36)

[31] Then we left the river Ahava on the twelfth day of the first month,
to go to Jerusalem.
The hand of our God was on us,
and he saved us from the hand of the enemy and the robbers on the way.
[32] We came to Jerusalem and stayed there three days.
[33] On the fourth day the silver and the gold and the objects were weighed
in the house of our God into the hand of Meremoth the son of Uriah the priest.
With him was Eleazar the son of Phinehas,
and with them were the Levites Jozabad the son of Jeshua and Noadiah the son of Binnui.
[34] Everything was counted and weighed,
and the whole weight was written down at that time.
[35] The people who had come back from captivity, out of exile,
offered burnt offerings to the God of Israel:
twelve bulls for all Israel, 96 rams, 77 lambs,
and twelve male goats as a sin offering.
All this was a burnt offering to the LORD.
[36] They handed over the king’s orders to the king’s local governors
and to the governors beyond the River.
So these supported the people and God’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> God answered the prayer. They arrive safely, without soldiers.
> Again offerings are made “for all Israel”: 12 bulls and 12 male goats, for the twelve tribes. 96 is 8 times 12.
> Meremoth the son of Uriah later also helps to build the wall (Nehemiah 3:4).
> “The king’s local governors”: here the Hebrew uses a Persian word, “satraps”. That was the name for the governors of the large provinces in the Persian Empire.
