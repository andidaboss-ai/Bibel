# Ezra – Chapter 9
### Ezra’s prayer of repentance

---

## Shocking news (Verses 1–4)

[1] When these things were done, the princes came to me and said,
“The people of Israel, the priests, and the Levites
have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands.
They follow their disgusting customs,
the customs of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites,
the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Egyptians, and the Amorites.
[2] For they have taken some of their daughters as wives for themselves and for their sons.
So the holy offspring have mixed themselves with the peoples of the lands.
Yes, the princes and rulers have been the first in this unfaithfulness.”
[3] When I heard this,
I tore my clothes and my robe,
pulled out hair from my head and from my beard,
and sat down in shock.
[4] Then everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel gathered around me
because of the unfaithfulness of the exiles.
And I sat in shock until the evening offering.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter and the next one are among the most difficult parts of the book.
> What is it about? Many men, among them priests and leaders, had married women from the surrounding peoples who worshiped other gods. Ezra saw a great danger in this: the small people could lose its faith, as Solomon once did, whose foreign wives led him to foreign gods (1 Kings 11:1–8). That is exactly what had led to the exile.
> It was not about skin color or origin as such, but about faith. Ruth, a woman from Moab, became part of the people because she confessed the God of Israel (Ruth 1:16). She even became the great-grandmother of David.
> The list of peoples in verse 1 recalls Deuteronomy 7:1–4. Some of these peoples no longer existed at that time. Ezra uses the old names to say: it is the same danger as before.
> “Holy offspring” means: the people that God has set apart for himself.
> Ezra reacts with deep grief. Pulling out hair was a sign of the deepest despair.

## Ezra’s prayer (Verses 5–15)

[5] At the evening offering I got up from my humbling,
with my clothes and my robe torn.
I fell on my knees and spread out my hands to the LORD my God.
[6] I said,
“My God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to you, my God.
For our sins have risen higher than our heads,
and our guilt has grown up to the heavens.
[7] Since the days of our fathers we have been very guilty, until this day.
Because of our sins, we, our kings, and our priests
have been handed over to the kings of the lands,
to the sword, to captivity, to robbery, and to shame,
as it is today.
[8] Now for a little while the LORD our God has shown us grace.
He has left us a remnant that escaped,
and has given us a stake in his holy place,
so that our God may make our eyes shine
and give us a little new life in our slavery.
[9] For we are slaves.
Yet our God has not abandoned us in our slavery.
He has shown us loving kindness in the sight of the kings of Persia,
to give us new life, to set up the house of our God,
to repair its ruins,
and to give us a wall in Judah and in Jerusalem.
[10] Now, our God, what shall we say after this?
For we have abandoned your commandments,
[11] which you commanded through your servants the prophets.
You said,
‘The land which you are going in to possess is an unclean land,
through the uncleanness of the peoples of the lands,
through their disgusting customs,
which have filled it from one end to the other with their filth.
[12] So now don’t give your daughters to their sons,
and don’t take their daughters for your sons.
Never seek their peace or their prosperity,
so that you may be strong and eat the good things of the land,
and leave it as an inheritance to your children forever.’
[13] All this has come on us because of our evil deeds and our great guilt.
And yet you, our God, have punished us less than our sins deserve,
and have given us such a remnant.
[14] Shall we again break your commandments
and join ourselves by marriage to the peoples who do these disgusting things?
Wouldn’t you be angry with us until you had destroyed us,
so that there would be no remnant and no one to escape?
[15] LORD, God of Israel, you are righteous,
for we are left as a remnant that has escaped, as it is today.
Look, we are before you in our guilt.
For no one can stand before you because of this.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezra’s prayer is a confession of sin for the whole people. Remarkable: Ezra himself had done nothing wrong. But he says “we” and “our guilt”. He stands with his people and takes their side (compare Daniel 9:4–19; Nehemiah 1:6–7).
> He sees God’s grace: God has not completely destroyed the people. He has left a “remnant”. He has given them “a stake” in his holy place. That is a picture of a firm place, like a tent peg that is stuck firmly in the ground.
> The “wall” in verse 9 is probably a picture for protection. The real city wall was only built later under Nehemiah.
> Verses 11–12 are not a word-for-word quotation, but a summary of several passages from the law (for example Deuteronomy 7:1–3; 23:6).
> In this prayer Ezra does not even ask for forgiveness. He only confesses the guilt and says: “You are righteous.” He leaves everything to God.
> Chapter 10 explains how the story goes on and how this story can be understood today.
