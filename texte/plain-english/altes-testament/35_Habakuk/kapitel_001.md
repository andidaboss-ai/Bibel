# Habakkuk – Chapter 1
### How long, LORD?

---

## Before we begin: Who was Habakkuk?

We know almost nothing about Habakkuk, except that he was a prophet. He probably lived shortly before 600 BC in Judah, in the time of Jeremiah. At that time the Babylonians (in the Bible also called “Chaldeans”) were rising to become the new world power. Nineveh had just fallen (612 BC, see Nahum).
What is special about Habakkuk: He does not only speak to people on God’s behalf. He speaks to God, and he asks him uncomfortable questions: Why do you allow injustice? Why are you silent when wicked people swallow up the righteous?
The book is like a conversation between Habakkuk and God:
Chapter 1: Habakkuk complains, God answers, Habakkuk complains again.
Chapter 2: God’s answer: “The righteous will live by his faith,” and five cries of “How terrible” against the oppressor.
Chapter 3: A prayer and a song. It ends with great trust: “Even though the fig tree does not bloom … yet I will be glad in the LORD.”
Habakkuk shows: You may ask God questions, even questions full of doubt and complaint. The book has 3 chapters.

---

## Habakkuk’s first complaint (Verses 1–4)

[1] This is the message that Habakkuk the prophet saw.
[2] LORD, how long will I cry for help,
and you will not listen?
I cry out to you, “Violence!”
and will you not save?
[3] Why do you make me see wrongdoing?
Why do you look at what is wrong?
For destruction and violence are in front of me.
There is fighting, and quarrels rise up.
[4] That is why the law has no power,
and justice never wins.
For the wicked surround the righteous.
That is why justice comes out twisted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Habakkuk begins with a complaint, as in many psalms: “How long?” He sees violence, fighting, and injustice among his own people in Judah. The courts no longer work. The wicked win. The righteous lose. Righteous means: living right before God.
> And God seems to do nothing. Habakkuk accuses God: You do not listen! You do not save!
> That is allowed: In the Bible, people may be honest with God, also with their disappointment, their anger, and their doubts. God can bear it.

---

## God’s first answer: The Babylonians are coming (Verses 5–11)

[5] “Look among the nations and watch!
Be amazed, yes, be amazed!
For I am doing something in your days
that you would not believe even if someone told you.
[6] For look, I am making the Chaldeans rise up,
that bitter and fierce nation.
They march across the whole wide earth
to take homes that do not belong to them.
[7] People fear them and are terrified of them.
They make their own rules and decide their own honor.
[8] Their horses are faster than leopards
and fiercer than the wolves in the evening.
Their riders charge forward proudly.
Yes, their riders come from far away.
They fly like an eagle that swoops down to eat.
[9] All of them come to do violence.
Their crowds of soldiers push forward.
They gather prisoners like sand.
[10] Yes, they make fun of kings,
and princes are a joke to them.
They laugh at every fortress,
for they pile up a ramp of earth and capture it.
[11] Then they rush past like the wind and go on.
They are truly guilty,
for their own strength is their god.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers, but very differently from what Habakkuk expected: I am sending the Babylonians. They will punish the unjust people of Judah.
> Verse 5: This is so unbelievable that no one would believe it. Paul quotes this verse in a sermon (Acts 13:41).
> Verses 6–10: An impressive description of the army of Babylon: fast as leopards, fierce as wolves, like an eagle on the hunt. No fortress can stop them. They simply pile up a ramp of earth and climb over the walls.
> Verses 7 and 11: The Babylonians know no law except their own. Verse 11 says of them: “Their own strength is their god.” That means: They worship only their own power. That is why they are guilty too.

---

## Habakkuk’s second complaint (Verses 12–17)

[12] Aren’t you from everlasting, LORD,
my God, my Holy One?
We will not die.
LORD, you have chosen them to bring judgment.
You, Rock, have set him in place to punish.
[13] Your eyes are too pure to look at evil.
You cannot look at wrongdoing.
So why do you put up with people who betray?
Why are you silent when the wicked swallows up
someone who is more righteous than he is?
[14] Why do you make people like the fish of the sea,
like the crawling creatures that have no ruler?
[15] He pulls them all up with a hook.
He catches them in his net
and gathers them in his fishing net.
That is why he is happy and glad.
[16] That is why he offers sacrifices to his net
and burns incense to his fishing net.
For through them he lives in luxury,
and his food is rich.
[17] Will he keep on emptying his net like this,
and keep on killing the nations without mercy?

> **What does this mean?**
> Habakkuk is not satisfied with God’s answer. He has a new problem:
> Verse 12: He trusts God, the eternal “Rock.” He believes: Our people will not be completely destroyed. He also understands that God is using the Babylonians as a tool of punishment.
> Verse 13: But: God, you are holy, and you cannot look at evil. How can you then allow even worse people to swallow up people who are less bad? Judah is guilty, yes, but Babylon is much worse!
> Verses 14–17: A picture: Babylon catches people like fish with a hook and a net. And then it worships its own net, that is, its military power, which makes it rich. Will this go on forever?
> This is a question people still ask today: Why are violent people so often successful?
