# Malachi – Chapter 4
### The sun of righteousness

---

## The day is coming (Verses 1–3)

[1] “For look, the day is coming,
burning like an oven.
On that day all the proud and all who do evil
will be like straw.
The day that is coming will burn them up,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“so that it will leave them neither root nor branch.
[2] But for you who fear my name,
the sun of righteousness will rise,
with healing in its wings.
You will go out and jump for joy
like calves let out of the barn.
[3] You will trample on the wicked,
for they will be ashes under the soles of your feet
on the day that I am preparing,”
says the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, these verses are Malachi 3:19–24.
> Verse 1: The day of judgment is like a glowing oven. Everything proud and evil burns up like straw.
> Verse 2: But for those who honor God, the same day is a sunrise! “The sun of righteousness” rises. Its rays are like wings that bring healing. And the people jump for joy like young calves that are finally allowed out of the barn onto the pasture after the winter.
> Christians have applied the “sun of righteousness” to Jesus. This picture comes up in many Christmas songs. Zechariah, the father of John the Baptist, speaks of a special light. He calls it: “the dawn from on high” (Luke 1:78).
> Verse 3: The righteous will win over evil. This is a picture of the victory of good. It is not a call for people to use violence. Judgment belongs to God.

---

## Remember Moses, and Elijah will come (Verses 4–6)

[4] “Remember the law of my servant Moses,
which I commanded him at Horeb for all Israel,
the rules and the laws.
[5] Look, I will send you Elijah the prophet
before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.
[6] He will turn the hearts of the fathers to their children
and the hearts of the children to their fathers.
Otherwise I would come
and strike the land with a curse.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These are the last words of the Old Testament (in the Christian order):
> Verse 4: Remember the law of Moses! Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai, where God gave Moses the commandments.
> Verse 5: God will send the prophet Elijah. Elijah had not died, but had been taken up into heaven in a chariot of fire (2 Kings 2:11). That is why people expected him to come back.
> In Judaism, a cup is still set out for Elijah at the Passover meal today, and the door is opened for him, in the hope that he will come and announce the Messiah.
> In the New Testament, the angel says that John the Baptist will go ahead “in the spirit and power of Elijah,” “to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children” (Luke 1:17). Jesus himself says about John: “He is Elijah, who was to come” (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13).
> Verse 6: Elijah’s task: to bring the generations back together. Parents and children are to find their way to each other again. When families and generations are at peace with each other, God’s blessing can come instead of the curse.
> So the Old Testament ends with a hope that waits: God will come. It ends with an open door, and the New Testament begins with John the Baptist, who walks right through this door.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Malachi?

> **What does this mean?**
> **I have loved you:** God’s first word in this book is a declaration of love (1:2).
> **Giving God the best:** The priests sacrificed sick and lame animals. God does not want what is left over, but the heart (1:6–14).
> **One God for all:** “From where the sun rises to where it goes down, my name is great among the nations” (1:11). “Don’t we all have one father?” (2:10).
> **Faithfulness in marriage:** God is the witness of the marriage covenant, and he takes the side of the women who were left (2:14–16).
> **The messenger is coming:** “Look, I am sending my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me” (3:1).
> **God does not change:** His faithfulness is the reason why his people still exist (3:6).
> **Return to me:** “Return to me, and I will return to you” (3:7).
> **A book to remember them:** God forgets no one who is faithful to him (3:16).
> **The sun of righteousness:** For those who honor God, a sun rises, with healing in its wings (4:2).
> **Elijah will come:** He will turn the hearts of parents and children to each other (4:5–6).
> **For today:** Does God get the best from me, or what is left over? Am I faithful to God and to the people I am connected with?

## Looking back: What have we read in the whole Old Testament?

> **What does this mean?**
> **In the beginning:** God creates the world and human beings in his image, male and female (Genesis 1–2). But people turn away from God, and violence and suffering come into the world.
> **Abraham and the fathers of Israel:** God calls Abraham and promises: “Through you, all the families of the earth will be blessed” (Genesis 12:3). He leads Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph.
> **Freedom:** God sets his people free from slavery in Egypt. At Mount Sinai he gives the Ten Commandments: rules for a life in freedom with God and with each other.
> **In the promised land:** Under Joshua, the judges, and the kings Saul, David, and Solomon, Israel lives in the land. There are good times and bad times, faithfulness and unfaithfulness.
> **The prophets:** Again and again God sends prophets. They call people to turn back, stand up for the poor, and give hope: Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Amos, Hosea, Micah, and many others.
> **Exile and return:** Because the people do not listen, Israel and Judah are destroyed and taken into exile. But God does not give up on his people. They are allowed to come home and build the temple again.
> **Prayer and wisdom:** The Psalms show how people talk with God, in joy, in sorrow, and in doubt. Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs ask about the meaning of life, about suffering, and about love.
> **The great hope:** The prophets announce a new covenant, a king from the house of David, a servant of God who suffers for others, and a day when God will be King over the whole earth and all nations will live in peace.
> **For Jews and Christians:** These writings are the Holy Scriptures of the Jewish people, the “Tanakh.” For Christians, they are the Old Testament. Jesus himself knew these Scriptures, prayed them, and taught them. Whoever wants to understand the New Testament needs to know the Old.
> **What comes next?** Next, the New Testament begins with the Gospel of Matthew. It tells about Jesus of Nazareth, about his birth in Bethlehem, his life, his death, and his resurrection. And right at the beginning, John the Baptist appears, the messenger that Malachi announced.
