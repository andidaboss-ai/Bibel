# Malachi – Chapter 4
### The sun of righteousness

---

## The day is coming (Verses 1–3)

[1] “For look, the day is coming,
burning like an oven.
On that day all the proud and all who do evil
will be like straw.
The day that is coming will burn them up,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“so that it will leave them neither root nor branch.
[2] But for you who fear my name,
the sun of righteousness will rise,
with healing in its wings.
You will go out and jump for joy
like calves let out of the barn.
[3] You will trample on the wicked,
for they will be ashes under the soles of your feet
on the day that I am preparing,”
says the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, these verses are Malachi 3:19–24.
> Verse 1: The day of judgment is like a glowing oven. Everything proud and evil burns up like straw.
> Verse 2: But for those who honor God, the same day is a sunrise! “The sun of righteousness” rises. Its rays are like wings that bring healing. And the people jump for joy like young calves that are finally allowed out of the barn onto the pasture after the winter.
> Christians have applied the “sun of righteousness” to Jesus. This picture comes up in many Christmas songs. Zechariah, the father of John the Baptist, speaks of a special light. He calls it: “the dawn from on high” (Luke 1:78).
> Verse 3: The righteous will win over evil. This is a picture of the victory of good. It is not a call for people to use violence. Judgment belongs to God.

---

## Remember Moses, and Elijah will come (Verses 4–6)

[4] “Remember the law of my servant Moses,
which I commanded him at Horeb for all Israel,
the rules and the laws.
[5] Look, I will send you Elijah the prophet
before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.
[6] He will turn the hearts of the fathers to their children
and the hearts of the children to their fathers.
Otherwise I would come
and strike the land with a curse.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These are the last words of the Old Testament (in the Christian order):
> Verse 4: Remember the law of Moses! Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai, where God gave Moses the commandments.
> Verse 5: God will send the prophet Elijah. Elijah had not died, but had been taken up into heaven in a chariot of fire (2 Kings 2:11). That is why people expected him to come back.
> In Judaism, a cup is still set out for Elijah at the Passover meal today, and the door is opened for him, in the hope that he will come and announce the Messiah.
> In the New Testament, the angel says that John the Baptist will go ahead “in the spirit and power of Elijah,” “to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children” (Luke 1:17). Jesus himself says about John: “He is Elijah, who was to come” (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13).
> Verse 6: Elijah’s task: to bring the generations back together. Parents and children are to find their way to each other again. When families and generations are at peace with each other, God’s blessing can come instead of the curse.
> So the Old Testament ends with a hope that waits: God will come. It ends with an open door, and the New Testament begins with John the Baptist, who walks right through this door.
