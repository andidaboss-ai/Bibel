# Malachi – Chapter 3
### I am sending my messenger

---

## The messenger and the fire that makes metal pure (Verses 1–6)

[1] “Look, I am sending my messenger,
and he will prepare the way before me!
The Lord whom you are looking for will suddenly come to his temple.
Look, the messenger of the covenant, whom you long for, is coming!”
says the LORD of Armies.
[2] “But who can bear the day when he comes?
And who will stand when he appears?
For he is like the fire that makes metal pure,
and like the strong soap of those who wash clothes.
[3] He will sit like someone who melts silver and makes it pure.
He will make the sons of Levi pure.
He will make them pure like gold and silver.
Then they will bring offerings to the LORD in the right way.
[4] Then the offering of Judah and Jerusalem
will please the LORD,
as in the days of long ago,
and as in years long past.
[5] I will come near to you to judge.
I will be quick to speak as a witness
against those who practice magic, against those who break their marriage,
against those who lie under oath,
and against those who cheat the hired worker out of his wages,
who treat the widow and the orphan harshly,
and who take away the rights of the foreigner,
and who do not fear me,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[6] “For I, the LORD, do not change.
That is why you, sons of Jacob, have not been destroyed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The answer to the question “Where is the God of justice?” (2:17): God sends a messenger who prepares the way for him. Then the Lord himself suddenly comes to his temple.
> In the New Testament, this verse is applied to John the Baptist, who prepared the way for Jesus (Mark 1:2; Matthew 11:10; Luke 7:27). Jesus himself says this about John.
> Verses 2–3: But God’s coming is not only pleasant. He is like the fire that is used to make silver and gold pure, and like strong soap. He will remove everything unclean, first among the priests.
> Verse 5: Whom does the judgment strike? Not only people who sin in religious ways (those who practice magic), but also those who do wrong to others: people who break their marriage, people who lie in court, employers who hold back their workers’ wages, and all who treat widows, orphans, and foreigners harshly. For God, faith and justice belong together.
> Verse 6: “For I, the LORD, do not change.” God’s faithfulness is the reason why Israel still exists at all. He keeps his promises, even when his people are unfaithful.

## Return to me! (Verses 7–12)

[7] Since the days of your ancestors
you have turned away from my rules
and have not kept them.
Return to me,
and I will return to you,”
says the LORD of Armies.
“But you say, ‘How should we return?’
[8] Will a person rob God?
Yet you rob me!
But you say, ‘How have we robbed you?’
In the tithes and the offerings.
A tithe is a tenth part of what a person earns or harvests.
[9] You are under a curse,
for you rob me,
yes, this whole nation does.
[10] Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse,
so that there may be food in my house.
And test me in this,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“and see if I will not open the windows of heaven for you
and pour out a blessing on you,
until there is not enough room for it.
[11] For your sake I will stop the one who eats up your crops,
so that he will not destroy the fruit of your ground.
And your vine in the field will not lose its fruit before it is ripe,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[12] “All nations will call you happy,
for you will be a land that brings joy,”
says the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Again the invitation: “Return to me, and I will return to you” (as in Zechariah 1:3).
> Verses 8–9: The people ask: “How should we return?” God names a clear example: the tithe. According to the law, the Israelites were to give a tenth of their harvest. This was what the Levites lived on, since they had no land of their own. And the poor, widows, orphans, and foreigners were also cared for with it (Deuteronomy 14:28–29). So whoever did not give the tithe also took something away from the poor.
> Verse 10: “Test me!” This is one of the few places in the Bible where God tells people to put him to the test. Whoever gives generously will see that God blesses generously.
> Important: This verse is sometimes misused to put pressure on people or to take their money, for example with the promise: “If you give money, you will get rich.” That is not what it means. No one can buy God’s blessing. The New Testament says: “Let each one give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under pressure. For God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7).
> Verse 11: “The one who eats up your crops” probably means locusts or other pests.

## Is it worth it to serve God? (Verses 13–18)

[13] “Your words against me have been harsh,”
says the LORD.
“But you say, ‘What have we said against you?’
[14] You have said,
‘It is useless to serve God.
What good is it that we have kept his instructions
and have walked around sadly before the LORD of Armies?
[15] Now we call the proud people happy.
Yes, those who do evil do well.
Yes, they test God and get away with it.’”
[16] Then those who feared the LORD talked with each other.
And the LORD paid attention and heard it.
A book to remember them was written before him
for those who feared the LORD
and honored his name.
[17] “They will be mine,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“my own treasure, on the day that I am preparing.
I will spare them,
as a man spares his own son who serves him.
[18] Then you will again see the difference
between the righteous and the wicked,
between the one who serves God
and the one who does not serve him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: Many people ask this question: Is it even worth it to believe in God and to live the right way? Those who do evil are doing much better! Psalm 73 asks this question too.
> Verse 16: A quiet, beautiful scene: Those who are faithful to God talk with each other and encourage each other. And God listens! He writes their names in a “book to remember them.” No faithful person is forgotten.
> Verse 17: They are God’s “own treasure,” something special that belongs to him (compare Exodus 19:5). He cares for them like a father for his child.
> Verse 18: In the end it will be clear that it makes a difference whether people serve God or not.
