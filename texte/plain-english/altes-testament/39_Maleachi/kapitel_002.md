# Malachi – Chapter 2
### Do not be unfaithful

---

## A warning to the priests (Verses 1–9)

[1] “Now, you priests, this command is for you.
[2] If you will not listen,
and if you will not take it to heart
to give honor to my name,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“then I will send the curse on you,
and I will curse your blessings.
Yes, I have already cursed them,
because you do not take it to heart.
[3] Look, I will rebuke your children,
and I will spread dung on your faces,
the dung from your feasts.
And you will be carried away with it.
Dung is animal waste.
[4] Then you will know that I have sent you this command,
so that my covenant with Levi may continue,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[5] “My covenant with him was a covenant of life and peace.
I gave them to him so that he would honor me.
And he did honor me
and stood in awe of my name.
[6] The law of truth was in his mouth,
and nothing wrong was found on his lips.
He walked with me in peace and honesty,
and he turned many away from sin.
[7] For the lips of a priest should keep knowledge,
and people should look for teaching from his mouth,
for he is the messenger of the LORD of Armies.
[8] But you have turned away from the path.
You have made many people stumble by your teaching.
You have ruined the covenant of Levi,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[9] “That is why I have made you looked down on
and low before all the people,
because you have not kept my ways,
but have favored some people when you teach the law.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3: Hard words to the priests. Because they do not honor God, their blessing becomes a curse. The picture of dung on their faces is meant to be shocking: The waste of the animals for sacrifice was burned outside the camp (Exodus 29:14). That is how unclean the priests have become.
> Verses 4–7: God reminds them of the ideal: Levi, the ancestor of the priests, was faithful. A good priest speaks the truth, lives in peace with God, and helps others turn away from wrong. People are to find advice and teaching with him. He is a “messenger of the LORD.” In Hebrew this is the same word as “Malachi.”
> Verses 8–9: But these priests have failed. They have led many people the wrong way and were unfair: They “favored some people,” that is, they gave the rich and powerful better treatment.
> For today: A warning to all who are responsible in church and congregation: Speak the truth, be honest, and treat everyone the same.

## Unfaithful to God and to each other (Verses 10–12)

[10] Don’t we all have one father?
Hasn’t one God created us?
Why are we unfaithful to each other, each against his brother,
and make the covenant of our ancestors unholy?
[11] Judah has been unfaithful,
and a disgusting thing has been done in Israel and in Jerusalem.
For Judah has made unholy the holy place of the LORD, which he loves,
and has married the daughter of a foreign god.
[12] The LORD will cut off the man who does this
from the tents of Jacob,
whoever he is, the one who is awake and the one who answers,
and the one who brings an offering to the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “Don’t we all have one father? Hasn’t one God created us?” An important sentence: We are all brothers and sisters, because we have the same Creator. That is why we should not cheat each other.
> Verse 11: Judah “has married the daughter of a foreign god.” That means: Men married women who served other gods, and they took over the worship of those gods. This is about faith, not about origin or skin color. The Bible also tells about women from other nations who came to the God of Israel and are highly honored, like Ruth, the woman from Moab and great-grandmother of David.
> Verse 12: A serious judgment. “The one who is awake and the one who answers” is an old saying. Its exact meaning is not certain. It probably means: everyone, with no exception.

## Faithfulness in marriage (Verses 13–16)

[13] “And this is another thing you do:
You cover the altar of the LORD with tears,
with weeping and with sighing,
because he no longer pays attention to the offering
and no longer accepts it gladly from your hand.
[14] And you say, ‘Why?’
Because the LORD has been a witness
between you and the wife of your youth.
You have been unfaithful to her,
even though she is your partner
and the wife of your covenant.
[15] Didn’t he make you one,
even though he still had the rest of the Spirit?
And why one?
He was looking for children who would belong to God.
So be careful in your spirit,
and let no one be unfaithful to the wife of his youth.
[16] Whoever hates and divorces,”
says the LORD, the God of Israel,
“covers his clothes with violence!”
says the LORD of Armies.
“So be careful in your spirit,
so that you are not unfaithful.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: The men weep at the altar because God no longer answers their prayers. God tells them why: You have been unfaithful to your wives. It seems that men left the wives they had been married to for many years, to marry younger women, maybe from rich foreign families (compare verse 11).
> At that time, women had hardly any rights. A woman who was sent away was often left without support and without protection. Here God takes the side of the women who were left.
> Marriage is a “covenant,” and God himself is the witness. The wife is “your partner,” that is, an equal partner.
> Verse 15: In Hebrew, this verse is one of the hardest in the whole Old Testament. The translations are very different from each other. It probably means: God has made husband and wife one in marriage.
> Verse 16: “Whoever hates and divorces … covers his clothes with violence!” That means: Whoever sends his wife away because he dislikes her does violence to her. This is about men who leave their wives without caring what happens to them.
> Important: This verse wants to protect the weak. It must never be used to keep someone in a marriage where he or she experiences violence. Violence in a marriage itself destroys the covenant. Help is available: For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Where is the God of justice? (Verse 17)

[17] You have made the LORD tired with your words.
And you say, ‘How have we made him tired?’
You do it when you say,
‘Everyone who does evil is good in the LORD’s eyes,
and he is pleased with them,’
or, ‘Where is the God of justice?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people have become bitter and mocking: “Evil people are doing well. It seems that God likes evil. Where is this God who is fair?”
> God is “tired” of this talk. The answer to the question “Where is the God of justice?” comes in the next chapter: He is coming!
