# Lamentations – Chapter 4
### How the gold has lost its shine!

---

## The children of Zion (Verses 1–10)

[1] How the gold has lost its shine!
The purest gold has changed!
The stones of the holy place are thrown out
at the corner of every street.
[2] The precious sons of Zion
were worth as much as fine gold.
But now people think of them as clay jars,
made by the hands of a potter!
[3] Even the jackals give their milk.
They nurse their young.
But the daughter of my people has become cruel,
like the ostriches in the wilderness.
[4] The tongue of the nursing baby
sticks to the roof of his mouth because he is so thirsty.
The young children ask for bread,
and no one breaks any for them.
[5] Those who used to eat fine food
are lost and alone in the streets.
Those who grew up wearing purple
now lie on piles of garbage.
Purple cloth was very expensive. Only rich people wore it.
[6] For the guilt of the daughter of my people
is greater than the sin of Sodom.
Sodom was destroyed in a moment,
and no hands were raised against her.
[7] Her nobles were purer than snow.
They were whiter than milk.
Their bodies were redder than rubies.
They looked like polished sapphire.
[8] Now they look blacker than coal.
No one recognizes them in the streets.
Their skin sticks to their bones.
It is dried out.
It has become like wood.
[9] Those who are killed by the sword are better off
than those who are killed by hunger.
For the hungry waste away, as if they had been stabbed,
because there is no food from the fields.
[10] Women who used to be so loving
have boiled their own children with their own hands.
The children became their food
when my people were destroyed.

> **What does this mean?**
> The fourth poem begins again with “How …!” (in Hebrew: “Ekhah”).
> Verses 1–2: The gold has lost its shine. The people who were once as precious as gold are treated like cheap clay jars.
> Verses 3–4: Even wild animals nurse their young. But in the famine, mothers have nothing left to give their children. The children ask for bread, and no one has any. In those days the ostrich was thought to be a bird that does not take care of its eggs (compare Job 39:14–16).
> Verses 5–8: The rich, who used to wear purple and eat fine food, are now thin and black from hunger. Hunger makes all people equal.
> Verse 9: A terrible sentence: Someone who dies by the sword is better off than someone who slowly starves to death.
> Verse 10: Again the horror is described: Desperate mothers ate their own children during the famine (compare 2:20). The Bible does not keep quiet about this. It is an accusation against war and the siege that bring people into such a situation.
> Children still suffer in wars and famines today. These verses call us to look and to help.

## The guilt of the prophets and priests (Verses 11–16)

[11] The LORD has finished pouring out his anger.
He has poured out his burning anger.
He has lit a fire in Zion,
and it has burned up her foundations.
[12] The kings of the earth did not believe it,
and neither did any of the people living in the world,
that the enemy and the foe
would come in through the gates of Jerusalem.
[13] It happened because of the sins of her prophets
and the guilt of her priests.
They spilled the blood of righteous people
in the middle of the city.
[14] They wander like blind men in the streets.
They are so stained with blood
that no one can touch their clothes.
[15] “Go away!” people shouted at them. “Unclean!
Go away! Go away! Do not touch anything!”
When they ran away and wandered around,
people among the nations said,
“They cannot live here anymore.”
[16] The LORD’s anger has scattered them.
He will not care for them anymore.
People did not respect the priests.
They did not show kindness to the elders.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: No one had thought that Jerusalem could ever fall. People believed that no one could take the city.
> Verse 13: An important reason for the destruction: Of all people, the religious leaders, the prophets and priests, killed innocent people. When those who are supposed to speak for God do wrong, it is especially bad.
> Verses 14–15: Now they themselves are like people with leprosy. Everyone shouts: “Go away! Unclean!” No one wants them.

## Our hope was in vain (Verses 17–22)

[17] Our eyes are still worn out
from looking for help that never comes.
From our watchtower we have watched
for a nation that could not save us.
[18] They hunted our steps,
so that we could not walk in our own streets.
Our end is near.
Our days are over,
for our end has come.
[19] Those who chased us were faster
than the eagles in the sky.
They chased us over the mountains.
They waited to attack us in the wilderness.
[20] The breath of our life, the LORD’s anointed king,
was caught in their pits.
He is the one we talked about.
We said, “Under his shadow we will live among the nations.”
[21] Be happy and glad, daughter of Edom,
you who live in the land of Uz.
The cup will be passed to you too.
You will get drunk and will make yourself naked.
[22] The punishment for your guilt is finished, daughter of Zion.
He will not take you away as a prisoner anymore.
But he will punish your guilt, daughter of Edom.
He will uncover your sins.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Jerusalem had hoped for help from Egypt, but in vain.
> Verse 20: “The LORD’s anointed king” is King Zedekiah. The people had hoped to live under his protection, but he was captured (Jeremiah 39:5).
> Verse 21: Edom was glad when Jerusalem fell. But Edom will soon have to drink the “cup” too.
> Verse 22: And then the first ray of light in a long time: “The punishment for your guilt is finished, daughter of Zion.” The worst is over. God will not take you away again. This reminds us of Isaiah 40:2. There, God says that people should call out to Jerusalem that her guilt is forgiven.
