# Lamentations – Chapter 2
### God has become like an enemy

---

## The anger of the Lord (Verses 1–10)

[1] How the Lord has covered the daughter of Zion
with a cloud in his anger!
He has thrown the beauty of Israel down from heaven to the earth.
On the day of his anger
he has not remembered his footstool.
[2] The Lord has swallowed up all the homes of Jacob without pity.
In his anger he has torn down
the strong fortresses of the daughter of Judah.
He has brought them down to the ground.
He has made the kingdom and its princes unholy.
[3] In burning anger he has cut off all the strength of Israel.
The Hebrew word for this means “horn.” A horn is a picture of strength.
He has pulled back his right hand when the enemy came.
He has burned up Jacob like a flaming fire
that eats up everything around it.
[4] He has bent his bow like an enemy.
He has stood with his right hand raised like an enemy.
He has killed all who were pleasant to look at.
In the tent of the daughter of Zion
he has poured out his anger like fire.
[5] The Lord has become like an enemy.
He has swallowed up Israel.
He has swallowed up all her palaces.
He has destroyed his strong fortresses.
He has made the mourning and weeping great
in the daughter of Judah.
[6] He has violently torn down his tent,
as if it were only a garden.
He has destroyed the place where people met with him.
The LORD has made Zion forget
the holy festivals and the Sabbath.
In his burning anger
he has rejected the king and the priest.
[7] The Lord has thrown away his altar.
He hates his holy place.
He has handed over the walls of her palaces to the enemy.
They have made noise in the LORD’s house,
as on the day of a holy festival.
[8] The LORD has decided
to destroy the wall of the daughter of Zion.
He has stretched out the measuring line.
He has not pulled back his hand from destroying.
He has made the outer wall and the city wall mourn.
They grow weak together.
[9] Her gates have sunk into the ground.
He has destroyed and broken the bars of her gates.
Her king and her princes are among the nations,
where there is no law.
Yes, her prophets find no vision from the LORD.
[10] The elders of the daughter of Zion sit on the ground.
They are silent.
They have thrown dust on their heads.
They have put on sackcloth.
Sackcloth is rough cloth. People wore it when they were mourning.
The young women of Jerusalem
bow their heads down to the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> This second poem speaks even more harshly: God himself “has become like an enemy” (verse 5). The poet dares to say it: It feels as if God himself has destroyed Jerusalem.
> Verse 1: The “footstool” is the temple or the ark of the covenant. God did not remember even that.
> Verses 6–7: The temple is destroyed. Festivals and the Sabbath are no longer celebrated. Enemies make noise in the temple as if at a festival.
> Verse 9: There is no more law and no vision from the prophets. God seems to be silent.
> Verse 10: The elders sit silently on the ground. Sometimes silence is the only answer to great suffering (compare Job 2:13).
> These words are honest and shocking. The Bible allows people to speak about God like this when they have lived through something terrible. God can bear even such a lament.

## The children grow faint (Verses 11–17)

[11] My eyes are worn out from crying.
My heart is troubled.
My insides are poured out on the ground
because my people are destroyed,
because the young children and the babies faint
in the streets of the city.
[12] They ask their mothers,
“Where is grain and wine?”
They faint like wounded people
in the streets of the city.
Their life slips away
in their mothers’ arms.
[13] What can I say to you?
What can I compare you with, daughter of Jerusalem?
What can I compare to you, so that I can comfort you,
virgin daughter of Zion?
For your wound is as big as the sea.
Who can heal you?
[14] Your prophets have seen false and foolish visions for you.
They have not shown you your sin,
so that your fate could have been turned around.
Instead, they have seen false messages for you,
messages that led you away into exile.
[15] All who pass by clap their hands at you.
They hiss and shake their heads at the daughter of Jerusalem.
They say,
“Is this the city that people called
‘The perfection of beauty, the joy of the whole earth’?”
[16] All your enemies have opened their mouths wide against you.
They hiss and grind their teeth.
They say, “We have swallowed her up.
Yes, this is the day we were waiting for.
We have found it. We have seen it.”
[17] The LORD has done what he planned.
He has kept the word that he commanded in the days long ago.
He has torn down and has not had pity.
He has let the enemy cheer over you.
He has made your enemies strong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: The worst thing for the poet is the suffering of the children. They ask their mothers for bread and die in their arms. It is a heartbreaking picture. Sadly, it is still real today in every war and every famine.
> Verse 13: “Your wound is as big as the sea. Who can heal you?” There are no words to comfort such great suffering.
> Verse 14: The false prophets did not show the people their sin. Whoever keeps the truth quiet does not help but does harm.
> Verses 15–16: The enemies mock the city that was once so beautiful.
> Verse 17: The poet sees: God has done what he had announced through the prophets.

## Pour out your heart like water (Verses 18–22)

[18] Their heart cried out to the Lord.
Wall of the daughter of Zion,
let tears run down like a river day and night!
Give yourself no rest.
Do not let your eyes rest.
[19] Get up, cry out in the night,
when the night watches begin!
Pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord!
Lift up your hands to him
for the life of your young children,
who faint from hunger at the corner of every street.
[20] “Look, LORD, and see
who it is that you have done this to!
Should women eat their own children,
the children they held and bounced on their knees?
Should the priest and the prophet be killed
in the holy place of the Lord?
[21] “The young and the old lie on the ground in the streets.
My young women and my young men have been killed by the sword.
You have killed them on the day of your anger.
You have slaughtered them and have not had pity.
[22] “You have called my terrors from every side,
as people are called to the day of a holy festival.
On the day of the LORD’s anger
no one escaped, and no one was left.
My enemy has destroyed
the children I cared for and raised.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19 is a famous verse: “Pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord!” In prayer you may bring everything to God: pain, fear, anger, and despair. You should pray especially for the children.
> Verse 20: A terrible question to God: Look at what is happening! In the famine during the siege, mothers even eat their children. The Bible does not keep quiet about this horror. It is an accusation against war and its terrors. Children must never be harmed.
> Verses 21–22: The poem ends without comfort. Sometimes there is no quick comfort in grief. The Bible lets that stand.
