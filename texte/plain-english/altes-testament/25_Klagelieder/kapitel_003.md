# Lamentations – Chapter 3
### His mercies are new every morning

---

## I am the man who has seen suffering (Verses 1–20)

[1] I am the man who has seen suffering
under the rod of his anger.
[2] He has led me and made me walk in darkness,
and not in light.
[3] Yes, he turns his hand against me
again and again, all day long.
[4] He has made my flesh and my skin old.
He has broken my bones.
[5] He has built walls against me.
He has surrounded me with bitterness and hardship.
[6] He has made me live in dark places,
like people who have been dead for a long time.
[7] He has built a wall around me, so that I cannot get out.
He has made my chain heavy.
[8] Yes, when I cry out and call for help,
he shuts out my prayer.
[9] He has blocked my ways with a wall of cut stone.
He has made my paths crooked.
[10] To me he is like a bear lying in wait,
like a lion in hiding.
[11] He has turned me off my path and torn me to pieces.
He has left me alone and ruined.
[12] He has bent his bow
and made me the target for his arrow.
[13] He has shot the arrows from his quiver
deep into my body.
A quiver is a bag for carrying arrows.
[14] All my people laugh at me.
They sing songs about me all day long.
[15] He has filled me with bitterness.
He has filled me up with wormwood.
Wormwood is a very bitter plant.
[16] He has broken my teeth on gravel.
He has covered me with ashes.
[17] You have taken my soul far away from peace.
I have forgotten what it is like to do well.
[18] I said, “My strength is gone,
and so is my hope in the LORD.”
[19] Remember my suffering and my misery,
the wormwood and the bitterness.
[20] My soul still remembers them,
and it is bowed down inside me.

> **What does this mean?**
> The third poem is the heart of the whole book. It is built differently from the others: Every three verses begin with the same Hebrew letter. That is why it has 66 verses.
> Here a single person speaks: “I am the man who has seen suffering.” He describes his suffering with many pictures: darkness, prison, chains, a bear lying in wait, arrows.
> Verse 8: The worst thing is this: He prays, but God does not seem to hear.
> Verse 18: He is at the end of his strength: “My strength is gone, and so is my hope in the LORD.”
> Many people who go through a serious illness, depression, or a great loss find themselves in these words. It is good to know: Feelings like these also have a place in the Bible. If you feel this way, you may look for help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## New every morning (Verses 21–33)

[21] But this is what I call back to my mind,
and that is why I have hope:
[22] It is because of the LORD’s loving kindness
that we are not destroyed,
because his mercies do not fail.
[23] They are new every morning.
Great is your faithfulness.
[24] “The LORD is my share,” says my soul.
“That is why I will hope in him.”
[25] The LORD is good to those who wait for him,
to the soul who looks for him.
[26] It is good for a man to hope
and to wait quietly for the LORD to save.
[27] It is good for a man
to carry the yoke while he is young.
[28] Let him sit alone and be silent,
because the LORD has put it on him.
[29] Let him put his mouth in the dust.
Maybe there is still hope.
[30] Let him turn his cheek to the one who hits him.
Let him be filled with insults.
[31] For the Lord will not reject anyone forever.
[32] Even if he brings grief,
he will still have mercy,
because his loving kindness is so great.
[33] For he does not like to make people suffer.
He does not like to bring them grief.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–23: In the middle of the deepest despair, everything turns around. The poet remembers something that gives him hope: God’s loving kindness does not end. “His mercies do not fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.” These verses are among the best-known in the Bible. They stand exactly in the middle of the book, like a light in the middle of the darkness. Every new morning is a sign of God’s faithfulness.
> Verse 24: “The LORD is my share.” Even when everything is lost, God remains.
> Verse 26: Being able to hope quietly and wait is a kind of strength.
> Verses 27–30: These verses describe how someone bears suffering patiently. They are not a command to accept violence or abuse. If you are being hit or mistreated, you may and should get help: In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 31–33: A wonderful comfort: God does not reject anyone forever. And God does not like to bring suffering. His heart is full of mercy.

## Let us turn back (Verses 34–47)

[34] When people crush all the prisoners of the earth under their feet,
[35] when they take away a person’s rights
before the face of the Most High,
[36] when they twist the case of a person in court,
the Lord does not approve.
[37] Who can speak and have it happen,
if the Lord has not commanded it?
[38] Do not both bad things and good things
come from the mouth of the Most High?
[39] Why should a living person complain,
a man who is punished for his sins?
[40] Let us look closely at our ways and test them,
and let us turn back to the LORD.
[41] Let us lift up our hearts and our hands
to God in heaven.
[42] “We have sinned and have rebelled.
You have not forgiven.
[43] “You have covered us with anger and chased us.
You have killed. You have not had pity.
[44] You have covered yourself with a cloud,
so that no prayer can get through.
[45] You have made us like dirt and garbage
among the peoples.
[46] “All our enemies have opened their mouths wide against us.
[47] Terror and the pit have come on us,
ruin and destruction.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 34–36: The Lord does not approve when prisoners are mistreated and people are denied their rights. God is on the side of the oppressed.
> Verses 37–38: In the end, everything is in God’s hand, even the hard things. This is an attempt to understand what cannot be understood. It does not mean that God enjoys suffering (compare verse 33).
> Verses 40–41: Instead of only lamenting, the poet invites us: Let us test our lives and turn back to God. Lift up your hearts and hands to God!
> Verses 42–47: An honest prayer together: We have sinned. And it feels as if you have covered yourself with a cloud that no prayer can get through.

## You heard my voice (Verses 48–66)

[48] My eye runs with streams of tears,
because my people are destroyed.
[49] My eye pours out tears and does not stop,
without any break,
[50] until the LORD looks down
and sees from heaven.
[51] What my eye sees hurts my soul
because of all the daughters of my city.
[52] They have hunted me without stopping like a bird,
those who are my enemies for no reason.
[53] They have tried to end my life in the dungeon.
They have thrown a stone on me.
[54] Water flowed over my head.
I said, “I am lost.”
[55] I called on your name, LORD,
out of the lowest dungeon.
[56] You heard my voice:
“Do not close your ear to my sighing and my cry.”
[57] You came near on the day I called on you.
You said, “Do not be afraid.”
[58] Lord, you have stood up for me in my case.
You have saved my life.
[59] LORD, you have seen the wrong done to me.
Judge my case.
[60] You have seen all their revenge
and all their plans against me.
[61] You have heard their insults, LORD,
and all their plans against me.
[62] You have heard what the people who rise up against me say,
and how they plot against me all day long.
[63] You see them when they sit down and when they get up.
They sing songs about me.
[64] You will pay them back, LORD,
for what their hands have done.
[65] You will make their hearts hard.
Your curse will be on them.
[66] You will chase them in anger
and destroy them from under the LORD’s heavens.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 48–51: The poet weeps without a break until God looks down from heaven.
> Verses 53–55: These words remind us of Jeremiah, who was thrown into a cistern (Jeremiah 38:6). A cistern is a deep pit for collecting water. He called to God “out of the lowest dungeon” (3:55).
> Verses 56–58: And God heard! “You came near on the day I called on you. You said, ‘Do not be afraid.’” That is what many people have experienced: In the deepest trouble, God is near.
> Verses 59–66: The poet asks God to give him justice and to punish those who hunt him. He brings his anger to God and leaves the judgment to God, instead of taking revenge himself. Later, Jesus shows an even greater way: to pray for your enemies (Luke 23:34).
