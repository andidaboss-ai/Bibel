# 1 Samuel – Chapter 31
### The death of Saul and Jonathan

---

## The battle on Gilboa (Verses 1–6)

[1] The Philistines fought against Israel.
The men of Israel fled from the Philistines
and fell dead on Mount Gilboa.
[2] The Philistines caught up with Saul and his sons.
And the Philistines killed Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua, the sons of Saul.
[3] The battle went hard against Saul.
The archers caught up with him,
and he was badly wounded by the archers.
[4] Then Saul said to his armor bearer,
“Draw your sword and run me through with it,
so that these uncircumcised men don’t come
and run me through and make sport of me!”
But his armor bearer would not do it, for he was terrified.
So Saul took the sword and fell on it.
[5] When his armor bearer saw that Saul was dead,
he also fell on his sword and died with him.
[6] So Saul died, and his three sons, his armor bearer,
and all his men, together on that same day.

> **What does this mean?**
> What Samuel had announced in Endor happens: Israel is defeated, and Saul dies with his sons.
> Jonathan dies too, David’s faithful friend. He was a good man, but he stays at his father’s side to the end. He does not die for any guilt of his own.
> Saul is badly wounded. He is afraid that the Philistines will catch him alive, torture him, and mock him, as they had done with Samson (Judges 16). So he asks his armor bearer to kill him. When he refuses, Saul takes his own life.
> The Bible tells this without comment, honestly and sadly. It does not condemn Saul here, and it does not praise what he did either. It is the end of a man who had lost God and knew only fear.
> In 2 Samuel 1 an Amalekite tells a different version of Saul’s death. We will read there what is true about it.

## The Philistines dishonor Saul’s body (Verses 7–10)

[7] The men of Israel on the other side of the valley and beyond the Jordan
saw that the men of Israel had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead.
So they left their towns and fled.
And the Philistines came and lived in them.
[8] The next day the Philistines came to rob the dead.
They found Saul and his three sons fallen on Mount Gilboa.
[9] They cut off his head
and took off his armor.
And they sent messengers all around the land of the Philistines
to tell the news in the houses of their idols and to the people.
[10] They put his armor in the house of the Ashtaroth.
And they fastened his body to the wall of Beth Shan.

> **What does this mean?**
> The defeat is complete. The Philistines conquer many towns in the north of Israel. The land is now weaker than at the beginning of Saul’s rule.
> The Philistines cut off Saul’s head, just as David had once cut off Goliath’s head.
> They celebrate the victory in the temples of their gods. For them it is a victory of their gods over the God of Israel.
> Beth Shan was a town in the Jordan Valley. To hang the body on the wall was a great shame. The dead were not even supposed to get a grave.

## The faithfulness of the men of Jabesh (Verses 11–13)

[11] When the people of Jabesh Gilead heard
what the Philistines had done to Saul,
[12] all the brave men got up.
They walked all night
and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth Shan.
They came to Jabesh and burned them there.
[13] They took their bones
and buried them under the tamarisk tree in Jabesh.
And they fasted for seven days.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book ends with a sign of faithfulness. The men of Jabesh remember: Saul had rescued them when Nahash wanted to gouge out their eyes. That was Saul’s first great deed as king (Chapter 11).
> Now, many years later, they risk their lives. They march all night, take the dead down from the wall, and give them an honorable burial.
> Usually people in Israel did not burn the dead. Maybe they did it because the bodies had already been dishonored. But they buried the bones.
> Fasting for seven days was a sign of great mourning.
> So the story of Saul does not end only with shame, but also with thankfulness. The good he once did was not forgotten.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of 1 Samuel?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of 1 Samuel tells how Israel goes from the time of the judges to the time of the kings. Three people are at the center:
> 1. Samuel (Chapters 1–7): He is the child Hannah prayed for. As a boy he hears God’s voice: “Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening.” He becomes a prophet and the last judge. He leads the people to turn back to God. Under him God helps Israel: “Thus far the LORD has helped us.”
> 2. Saul (Chapters 8–15): The people want a king “like all the nations.” God gives them Saul, tall and handsome. He starts out humble and brave. But he has no patience to wait for God, and he obeys only halfway. Samuel tells him: “To obey is better than sacrifice.” God rejects Saul as king.
> 3. David (Chapters 16–31): God chooses Jesse’s youngest son, a shepherd: “Man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.” David defeats Goliath in the name of the LORD. Jonathan becomes his faithful friend. Saul becomes jealous and hunts David for years. Twice David could kill Saul, but he does not do it. He waits until God himself acts.
> The book also honestly shows the dark sides: Eli’s wicked sons, Saul’s hatred and his violence against the priests at Nob, but also David’s lies and his cruel raids. The Bible does not make anything look better than it was. It is not a book about heroes, but an honest book about people and God.
> Important messages of the book:
> God hears prayers, like the prayer of Hannah.
> God looks at the heart, not at the outside.
> God wants obedience and trust, not only devout rituals.
> True friendship is faithful and free of envy, as with Jonathan.
> Revenge belongs to God. David spares his enemy and leaves the judgment to God.
> In the end Saul is dead. The way is open for David. The book of 2 Samuel tells how David becomes king.
