# 1 Samuel – Chapter 10
### Saul is anointed and chosen as king

---

## Samuel anoints Saul (Verses 1–8)

[1] Then Samuel took the little bottle of oil
and poured it on his head.
Then he kissed him and said,
“Hasn’t the LORD anointed you to be prince over his inheritance?
[2] When you leave me today,
you will find two men by Rachel’s tomb,
on the border of Benjamin at Zelzah.
They will tell you,
‘The donkeys you went to look for have been found.
And look, your father has stopped worrying about the donkeys
and is worried about you.
He is saying, “What shall I do for my son?”’

[3] Then you will go on from there
and come to the oak of Tabor.
Three men will meet you there
who are going up to God at Bethel.
One is carrying three young goats,
one is carrying three loaves of bread,
and one is carrying a container of wine.
[4] They will greet you
and give you two loaves of bread.
You shall take them from their hand.

[5] After that you will come to the hill of God,
where the Philistine outpost is.
And this will happen:
When you come there to the city,
you will meet a group of prophets
coming down from the high place.
In front of them are a lute, a tambourine, a flute, and a harp,
and they will be prophesying.
[6] Then the Spirit of the LORD will come on you with power.
Then you will prophesy with them
and will be turned into a different man.
[7] When these signs happen to you,
then do what the moment calls for.
For God is with you.
[8] Go down ahead of me to Gilgal.
And look, I will come down to you
to offer burnt offerings and to sacrifice peace offerings.
You shall wait seven days until I come to you
and show you what you are to do.”

> **What does this mean?**
> To “anoint” means: to pour oil on someone’s head. This was a sign that God chooses a person for a special task. Priests were anointed, later kings, and sometimes prophets. The Hebrew word for “anointed one” is “mashiach,” Messiah.
> The anointing happens in secret. Only Samuel and Saul know about it.
> “His inheritance” means the people of Israel. It belongs to God. Saul is to lead it for God, not for himself.
> Samuel gives Saul three signs. When they happen, Saul knows: All this really comes from God.
> The old Greek translation has a longer text in verse 1: Samuel tells Saul clearly that he is to save the people from the hand of their enemies, and that the signs will confirm this.
> “The hill of God” was probably Gibeah, Saul’s home town. The Philistines had a military outpost there. This shows how much the Philistines ruled the land.
> The prophets make music and speak about God with great excitement. This was a special kind of worship, in which people were completely taken hold of by the Spirit of God.
> Verse 8 is important for later: Saul is to wait seven days for Samuel in Gilgal. In Chapter 13 this becomes the great test for Saul.

## The signs happen (Verses 9–13)

[9] And this is what happened:
When he turned to leave Samuel,
God gave him another heart.
And all these signs happened on that day.
[10] When they came there to the hill,
a group of prophets came to meet him.
The Spirit of God came on him with power,
and he prophesied among them.
[11] All who had known him before saw
that he was prophesying with the prophets.
Then the people said to one another,
“What has happened to the son of Kish?
Is Saul also among the prophets?”
[12] One man from there answered,
“And who is their father?”
So it became a saying:
“Is Saul also among the prophets?”
[13] When he had finished prophesying,
he came to the high place.

> **What does this mean?**
> “God gave him another heart”: Saul is changed on the inside. God prepares him for his task.
> The people are amazed: Saul, the son of a rich farmer, suddenly speaks like a prophet? That does not fit him.
> “Who is their father?” is a difficult question. Maybe it means: You do not become a prophet because of your family. The Spirit comes from God, not from your father. So Saul can be a prophet too.
> “Is Saul also among the prophets?” became a saying. People said it when someone did something completely unexpected. Chapter 19:24 tells how the saying is used once more.

## Saul keeps quiet about the kingdom (Verses 14–16)

[14] Saul’s uncle said to him and to his servant,
“Where did you go?”
He said,
“To look for the donkeys.
When we saw that they could not be found, we went to Samuel.”
[15] Saul’s uncle said,
“Please tell me what Samuel said to you.”
[16] Saul said to his uncle,
“He told us plainly that the donkeys had been found.”
But about the matter of the kingdom, which Samuel had spoken about,
he told him nothing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul keeps the great secret to himself. Is that humility? Or fear? Or wisdom, because the Philistines are still in the land? The text does not say.

## Saul is chosen by lot (Verses 17–24)

[17] Samuel called the people together to the LORD at Mizpah.
[18] He said to the people of Israel,
“This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘I brought Israel up out of Egypt.
I saved you from the hand of the Egyptians
and from the hand of all the kingdoms that oppressed you.’
[19] But today you have rejected your God,
who himself saves you from all your troubles and your distress.
And you have said to him,
‘No! Set a king over us!’
So now stand before the LORD
by your tribes and by your clans.”
[20] So Samuel had all the tribes of Israel come forward,
and the tribe of Benjamin was chosen.
[21] He had the tribe of Benjamin come forward by its families,
and the family of the Matrites was chosen.
Then Saul the son of Kish was chosen.
But when they looked for him, he could not be found.
[22] So they asked the LORD again,
“Has the man come here yet?”
The LORD answered,
“Look, he has hidden himself among the baggage.”
[23] They ran and brought him from there.
When he stood among the people,
he was taller than any of the people from his shoulders up.
[24] Samuel said to all the people,
“Do you see the one the LORD has chosen?
There is no one like him among all the people.”
Then all the people shouted,
“Long live the king!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Samuel reminds the people once more: God has always saved you. And still you want a king. He says it clearly before he gives them what they wish for.
> Now what Samuel had already done in secret is shown in public. By lot the choice falls on Saul. So all the people see: God himself chose him, not Samuel.
> Casting lots was a way of finding out God’s will. “Chosen” here means: The lot fell on him. This is also how the guilty man, Achan, was found back then (Joshua 7).
> But then comes the surprise: The new king has hidden himself among the baggage! Maybe out of humility, maybe out of fear. Saul has already been anointed and has seen the signs. Still he hides. This shows an unsureness that will stay with him all his life.
> “Long live the king!” The people cheer. They see a tall, handsome man. This is exactly the kind of king they wanted.

## The rules of the kingdom (Verses 25–27)

[25] Then Samuel explained to the people the rules of the kingdom.
He wrote them in a book and laid it before the LORD.
Then Samuel sent all the people away, each one to his house.
[26] Saul also went to his house in Gibeah.
With him went the band of men whose hearts God had touched.
[27] But some worthless fellows said,
“How can this man save us?”
They looked down on him and brought him no gift.
But he kept quiet about it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samuel writes down the rules for the king. This is to make it clear: The king, too, is under God’s law. He cannot do whatever he wants. This is a very early form of what today we call a constitution.
> Not everyone is excited. Some look down on Saul. Saul reacts wisely: He keeps quiet and does not let himself be provoked.
> In an old manuscript from the Dead Sea there is an extra section after verse 27: Nahash, the king of the Ammonites, had already badly oppressed the tribes of Gad and Reuben and had gouged out the right eye of many men. The Jewish historian Josephus also knows this story. The WEB does not have this section.
