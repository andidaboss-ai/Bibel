# 1 Samuel – Chapter 1
### Hannah prays for a child

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of 1 Samuel?

The books of Samuel tell how Israel becomes a kingdom. At the beginning, judges and priests are still ruling. At the end, David is king.
Originally, 1 Samuel and 2 Samuel were one single book. Only later was it divided into two parts.
The book of 1 Samuel tells about three great people:
Samuel is the last judge and a great prophet. He anoints the first two kings.
Saul is the first king of Israel. He starts well, but he does not obey God and fails.
David is a young shepherd whom God chooses. He defeats Goliath. Saul chases him for a long time, but in the end David becomes king.
The book is full of exciting and very human stories: friendship, jealousy, war, prayer, and betrayal. It shows honestly the strengths and weaknesses of people.
An important question in the book is: What makes a good leader? The answer is in a famous sentence: “Man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7).
But the book does not begin with a king. It begins with a woman who weeps and prays: Hannah.

## Elkanah and his two wives (Verses 1–8)

[1] There was a man from Ramathaim Zophim in the hill country of Ephraim.
His name was Elkanah.
He was the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephraimite.
[2] He had two wives.
The name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other was Peninnah.
Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.
[3] Year after year this man went up from his city
to worship the LORD of Armies in Shiloh and to offer sacrifices to him.
The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of the LORD there.
[4] When the day came for Elkanah to offer his sacrifice,
he gave portions of the sacrifice to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.
[5] But he gave Hannah a double portion,
because he loved Hannah.
But the LORD had closed her womb.
[6] Her rival hurt her badly to upset her,
because the LORD had closed her womb.
[7] This happened year after year:
Whenever she went up to the house of the LORD, her rival hurt her.
So she wept and did not eat.
[8] Her husband Elkanah said to her,
“Hannah, why are you weeping?
Why don’t you eat?
Why is your heart so sad?
Am I not better to you than ten sons?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ramathaim Zophim is called “Ramah” for short in verse 19. It was in the hill country north of Jerusalem.
> Elkanah has two wives. This was allowed at that time, but the Bible often shows how much quarreling and pain it brought, as with Sarah and Hagar or with Rachel and Leah.
> “The LORD of Armies” is in Hebrew “YHWH Tsebaoth.” This name for God appears here for the first time in the Bible. It means: God is the Lord over all powers in heaven and on earth. The WEB, our English source text, says “Yahweh of Armies.”
> At that time, the holy tent with the ark of the covenant stood in Shiloh. People went there once a year for the great feast.
> Being childless was a great sorrow and a disgrace at that time. Many people thought God had punished the woman. Peninnah takes advantage of this and hurts Hannah, especially at the feasts.
> Elkanah loves Hannah. But he does not fully understand her pain. He means well, but his question “Am I not better to you than ten sons?” does not comfort her.

## Hannah’s prayer (Verses 9–18)

[9] After they had eaten and drunk in Shiloh, Hannah got up.
Eli the priest was sitting on his seat by the doorpost of the temple of the LORD.
[10] She was deeply bitter in her soul.
She prayed to the LORD and wept bitterly.
[11] She made a vow and said,
“LORD of Armies,
if you will really look at the misery of your servant
and remember me and not forget your servant,
but give your servant a son,
then I will give him to the LORD for all the days of his life.
And no razor shall come on his head.”

[12] While she kept on praying in front of the LORD,
Eli watched her mouth.
[13] For Hannah was speaking in her heart.
Only her lips moved, but her voice could not be heard.
So Eli thought she was drunk.
[14] Eli said to her,
“How long will you stay drunk?
Get rid of your wine!”
[15] Hannah answered,
“No, my lord.
I am a woman with a troubled spirit.
I have not been drinking wine or strong drink.
But I have poured out my soul before the LORD.
[16] Don’t think of your servant as a wicked woman.
For I have been speaking out of my great sorrow and my hurt.”
[17] Then Eli answered,
“Go in peace.
May the God of Israel give you what you have asked of him.”
[18] She said,
“Let your servant find favor in your eyes.”
Then the woman went her way and ate.
And her face was no longer sad.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “temple” in Shiloh was not yet a big building like the later temple in Jerusalem, but probably a solid holy place with doors (1 Samuel 3:15).
> Hannah takes her pain straight to God. She promises: If you give me a son, I will give him back to you. He is to serve God his whole life. “No razor” means: He is to be a Nazirite, like Samson (Judges 13:5).
> At that time, people usually prayed out loud. Hannah prays quietly, only in her heart. Eli does not understand this and thinks she is drunk. The priest who should have helped her accuses her.
> But Hannah stays polite and explains herself. “I have poured out my soul before the LORD” is a beautiful picture for honest prayer: You tell God everything that is in your heart.
> After this, Hannah is changed. She does not yet know whether she will have a child. But she has handed her burden over to God. She can eat again.

## Samuel is born (Verses 19–20)

[19] They got up early in the morning and worshiped the LORD.
Then they went back home to Ramah.
Elkanah knew his wife Hannah,
and the LORD remembered her.
[20] When the time had come, Hannah became pregnant and had a son.
She named him Samuel.
For she said, “I have asked the LORD for him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, “to know” is a word for the love between a man and a woman.
> “The LORD remembered her”: God did not forget Hannah. That is what she had asked for in her prayer (verse 11).
> In Hebrew, the name “Samuel” sounds a bit like the word for “asked for” (sha’al). The name itself probably means “name of God” or “God has heard.” Every time Hannah calls her son, she is reminded: God has answered my prayer.

## Hannah brings Samuel to Shiloh (Verses 21–28)

[21] The man Elkanah and his whole family went up
to offer the yearly sacrifice to the LORD and to keep his vow.
[22] But Hannah did not go up.
For she said to her husband,
“Not until the child is weaned.
Then I will bring him, so that he may appear before the LORD and stay there forever.”
[23] Her husband Elkanah said to her,
“Do what seems good to you.
Stay until you have weaned him.
Only may the LORD make his word come true.”
So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him.

[24] When she had weaned him, she took him up with her,
with three bulls, about 6 gallons of flour, and a container of wine.
She brought him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh.
The child was still young.
[25] They killed the bull
and brought the child to Eli.
[26] She said,
“Oh, my lord, as surely as you live, my lord:
I am the woman who stood here beside you, praying to the LORD.
[27] I prayed for this child.
And the LORD has given me what I asked of him.
[28] So I also give him to the LORD.
As long as he lives, he is given to the LORD.”
And he worshiped the LORD there.

> **What does this mean?**
> To wean a child means: to stop feeding it at the breast.
> At that time, a child was often breastfed for two to three years. Only then does Hannah bring Samuel to Shiloh. This is a great sacrifice for a mother who has waited so long for a child. But she keeps her promise.
> The Bible says “an ephah of flour.” An ephah is about 6 gallons.
> Verse 24 says “three bulls,” verse 25 says “the bull.” An old manuscript from the Dead Sea and the old Greek translation have in verse 24: “a three-year-old bull.” This fits verse 25 better.
> In verses 27 and 28, Hannah plays with the Hebrew word “sha’al”: I “asked” for him, so I “give him back” (literally: “I lend him”) to the LORD.
> “He worshiped”: This probably means Elkanah or little Samuel. Some manuscripts have “they worshiped.”
