# 1 Samuel – Chapter 6
### The ark comes back

---

## The Philistines ask their priests (Verses 1–6)

[1] The ark of the LORD was in the land of the Philistines for seven months.
[2] The Philistines called for the priests and the fortune-tellers and said,
“What shall we do with the ark of the LORD?
Show us how we should send it back to its place.”
[3] They said,
“If you send away the ark of the God of Israel,
don’t send it away empty.
You must surely give him back a guilt offering.
Then you will be healed.
And you will find out why his hand has not turned away from you.”
[4] Then they asked,
“What should the guilt offering be that we give back to him?”
They said,
“Five golden tumors and five golden mice,
for the number of the lords of the Philistines.
For one plague was on all of you and on your lords.
[5] So you shall make images of your tumors
and images of your mice that are ruining the land.
And you shall give glory to the God of Israel.
Maybe then he will take his hand off you,
off your gods and off your land.
[6] Why do you want to harden your hearts,
as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts?
When he had done wonders among them,
didn’t they let the people go, and they left?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests of the Philistines advise: Give the God of Israel a gift to make up for what you did. This is what people did at that time when they had offended a god.
> Golden images of the disease and of the mice: People believed that if you make an image of the evil and send it away, the evil itself goes away too. This is not a custom of Israel, but a custom of the Philistines. Here God accepts their way of wanting to honor him.
> The priests of the Philistines are wiser than their people. They say: Learn from the story of Pharaoh. Whoever hardens himself against the God of Israel loses in the end.
> “Give glory to the God of Israel”: Of all people, foreign priests say this. The word for “glory” is again “kavod,” the same word as in Chapter 4:21. The glory was not lost. Now even the enemies have to admit it.

## The test with the cows (Verses 7–12)

[7] “Now take and get ready a new cart
and two milk cows that have never worn a yoke.
Hitch the cows to the cart,
and take their calves away from them and bring them home.
[8] Take the ark of the LORD and put it on the cart.
And put the gold objects that you are giving back to him as a guilt offering
in a box beside it.
Then send it away and let it go.
[9] And watch:
If it goes up the road to its own territory, to Beth Shemesh,
then he has done this great evil to us.
But if not, then we will know that it was not his hand that struck us.
It was just chance that happened to us.”

[10] The men did so.
They took two milk cows and hitched them to the cart.
They shut up their calves at home.
[11] They put the ark of the LORD on the cart,
and the box with the golden mice and the images of their tumors.
[12] The cows went straight along the road to Beth Shemesh.
They walked along the highway, mooing as they went.
They did not turn to the right or to the left.
And the lords of the Philistines followed them as far as the border of Beth Shemesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> A yoke is a wooden bar that is put over the necks of animals so that they can pull a cart or a plow.
> This is a clever test. Cows that have never pulled a cart do not usually walk straight ahead. And cows whose calves have just been taken away want to go back to their calves. If they still pull the cart into the land of Israel, then God must be guiding them.
> The cows are mooing because they are calling for their calves. But they keep going anyway, straight ahead, without turning off. God is guiding them.
> Beth Shemesh was a city of Israel at the border of the land of the Philistines. The name means “house of the sun.” It was a city of the Levites (Joshua 21:16).

## Joy in Beth Shemesh (Verses 13–18)

[13] The people of Beth Shemesh were harvesting their wheat in the valley.
They looked up and saw the ark,
and they were happy to see it.
[14] The cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh
and stopped there, where there was a large stone.
They split up the wood of the cart
and offered the cows to the LORD as a burnt offering.
[15] The Levites took down the ark of the LORD
and the box that was with it, in which the gold objects were.
They put them on the large stone.
And on that day the men of Beth Shemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrifices to the LORD.
[16] When the five lords of the Philistines had seen it,
they went back to Ekron the same day.

[17] These are the golden tumors
that the Philistines gave back to the LORD as a guilt offering:
one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Ashkelon, one for Gath, one for Ekron.
[18] And the golden mice,
for the number of all the cities of the Philistines that belonged to the five lords,
both the walled cities and the villages in the country,
as far as the large stone on which they set down the ark of the LORD.
That stone is still in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wheat harvest was in early summer. In the middle of their work, the people see the ark coming. What joy!
> Joshua of Beth Shemesh is not the famous Joshua, who came after Moses. He is a farmer with the same name.
> The Levites were the right people to carry the ark (Numbers 4:15). The large stone serves as an altar.
> The five cities of the Philistines are named: Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron. Together these five cities made up the land of the Philistines.
> In verse 4 there were five golden mice. In verse 18 it says: as many mice as all the cities and villages. Maybe there were more in addition to the five mice. The Bible does not explain it in more detail.

## The disaster in Beth Shemesh (Verses 19–21)

[19] He struck some of the men of Beth Shemesh,
because they had looked into the ark of the LORD.
He struck 50,070 of the men.
Then the people mourned, because the LORD had struck the people with a great slaughter.
[20] The men of Beth Shemesh said,
“Who is able to stand before the LORD, this holy God?
To whom shall he go up from us?”
[21] They sent messengers to the people of Kiriath Jearim, saying,
“The Philistines have brought back the ark of the LORD.
Come down and take it up to your town.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The joy ends with terror. The men of Beth Shemesh look into the ark. This was strictly forbidden. Not even the Levites were allowed to look at the holy things, or they would die (Numbers 4:20).
> The number in verse 19 is difficult. In the Hebrew text there are literally two numbers one after the other: 70 men and 50,000 men. Such a way of giving a number is very unusual in Hebrew. Beth Shemesh was a small town. 50,000 men would not have lived there. The Jewish historian Josephus and some Hebrew manuscripts give only 70 men. Many Bible teachers think this is the original number. The WEB has “fifty thousand seventy.”
> The old Greek translation tells verse 19 differently: The sons of a man named Jeconiah did not rejoice with the others when they saw the ark. That is why they were struck.
> This story is hard to understand. It wants to show: God is holy. You must not deal with him out of curiosity or without respect. He is not an object that you examine.
> The question “Who is able to stand before the LORD, this holy God?” is an important question of the whole Bible. The Bible’s answer is: No one by his own strength. Only because God himself is gracious can people come near him.
> Kiriath Jearim was about 9 miles west of Jerusalem. The story continues in the next chapter.
