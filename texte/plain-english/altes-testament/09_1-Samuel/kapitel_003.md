# 1 Samuel – Chapter 3
### God calls Samuel

---

## A voice in the night (Verses 1–10)

[1] The boy Samuel served the LORD under Eli.
The word of the LORD was rare in those days.
There were not many visions at that time.
[2] At that time Eli was lying down in his place.
His eyes had grown weak, so that he could no longer see.
[3] The lamp of God had not yet gone out.
And Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was.
[4] Then the LORD called Samuel.
He said, “Here I am.”
[5] He ran to Eli and said,
“Here I am. You called me.”
He said, “I didn’t call. Lie down again.”
So he went and lay down.
[6] The LORD called once more, “Samuel!”
Samuel got up, went to Eli, and said,
“Here I am. You called me.”
He answered, “I didn’t call, my son. Lie down again.”
[7] Samuel did not yet know the LORD,
and the word of the LORD had not yet been shown to him.
[8] The LORD called Samuel again, for the third time.
He got up, went to Eli, and said,
“Here I am. You called me.”
Then Eli understood that the LORD was calling the boy.
[9] So Eli said to Samuel,
“Go, lie down.
And if he calls you, say,
‘Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening.’”
So Samuel went and lay down in his place.
[10] The LORD came and stood there and called as at the other times,
“Samuel! Samuel!”
Then Samuel said,
“Speak, for your servant is listening.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The word of the LORD was rare”: At this time, God hardly spoke to people. The priests were corrupt, and Eli was old and almost blind. Eli’s weak eyes are like a picture: Israel no longer sees clearly in spiritual things either.
> “The lamp of God had not yet gone out”: In the holy place, a lampstand burned all night until the morning (Exodus 27:20–21). So it was shortly before sunrise. You can also read it as a picture: The light of God in Israel had almost gone out, but not quite.
> Samuel does not yet know God’s voice. He thinks it is Eli. Three times he runs to him. Only then does Eli understand what is happening.
> Here Eli is a good teacher: He shows the boy how to listen to God. The sentence “Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening” is still a prayer today for many people who want to know what God wants from them.
> Interesting: The fourth time, Samuel only says “Speak,” without the word “LORD.” Maybe he does not yet dare to say God’s name.

## A hard message (Verses 11–14)

[11] The LORD said to Samuel,
“I am going to do something in Israel
that will make both ears of everyone who hears it ring.
[12] On that day I will carry out against Eli everything
I have said about his family, from beginning to end.
[13] For I have told him
that I will judge his family forever because of the sin he knew about.
For his sons brought a curse on themselves,
and he did not stop them.
[14] So I have sworn to the family of Eli:
The sin of Eli’s family will never be taken away,
not by sacrifice and not by offering.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The first message that young Samuel receives from God is a hard message. And it is about Eli, of all people, his teacher, who is like a father to him.
> “Ears will ring” means: It will be so terrible that people will be shocked when they hear about it.
> God says why Eli is guilty: He knew what his sons were doing, and he did not stop them. Whoever has responsibility and lets wrong happen shares in the guilt.
> “His sons brought a curse on themselves”: This is what the Hebrew text says. The old Greek translation and many Bible teachers read: “His sons cursed God.” Jewish scribes sometimes changed such places, so that it would not be written that someone cursed God.
> The sons misused the sacrifices. That is why no sacrifice can help them anymore.

## Samuel tells Eli the truth (Verses 15–18)

[15] Samuel lay there until the morning.
Then he opened the doors of the house of the LORD.
Samuel was afraid to tell Eli about the vision.
[16] Then Eli called Samuel and said,
“Samuel, my son!”
He said, “Here I am.”
[17] He said,
“What did he say to you?
Please don’t hide it from me.
May God punish you, and even more,
if you hide from me anything of all that he said to you.”
[18] So Samuel told him everything
and hid nothing from him.
He said,
“It is the LORD.
Let him do what seems good to him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the morning Samuel does his usual work: He opens the doors. But he is afraid. How can he tell Eli something like this?
> Eli insists on hearing the truth. And Samuel is honest, even though it is hard. This is the first task of a prophet: to pass on God’s word without leaving anything out.
> Eli accepts the judgment: “It is the LORD.” There is humility in this. But you can also ask: Why doesn’t he change anything? He could still have acted now.

## Samuel becomes a prophet (Verses 19–21)

[19] Samuel grew up, and the LORD was with him.
He let none of his words fall to the ground.
[20] All Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, knew
that Samuel was confirmed as a prophet of the LORD.
[21] And the LORD appeared again in Shiloh.
For the LORD showed himself to Samuel in Shiloh through the word of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> “He let none of his words fall to the ground”: Everything that Samuel said in God’s name came true. This is how people recognized a true prophet (Deuteronomy 18:22).
> “From Dan to Beersheba” means: in the whole land, from the north to the south.
> At the beginning of the chapter, the word of the LORD was rare. At the end, God speaks again. Through a boy who has learned to listen, a new time begins for Israel.
