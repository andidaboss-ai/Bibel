# 1 Samuel – Chapter 30
### David gets everything back

---

## Ziklag is burned (Verses 1–6)

[1] When David and his men came to Ziklag on the third day,
the Amalekites had made a raid on the South and on Ziklag.
They had struck Ziklag and burned it with fire.
[2] And they had taken the women captive and everyone who was in it, young and old.
They had not killed anyone,
but had carried them off and gone their way.
[3] When David and his men came to the town,
it was burned with fire.
And their wives, their sons, and their daughters had been taken captive.
[4] Then David and the people who were with him cried out loud and wept
until they had no more strength to weep.
[5] David’s two wives had also been taken captive,
Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail, the widow of Nabal of Carmel.
[6] David was in great distress.
For the people talked about stoning him,
because all the people were bitter, each one because of his sons and daughters.
But David strengthened himself in the LORD his God.

> **What does this mean?**
> David was away for three days. During this time the Amalekites raided his town. This is bitter irony: David himself had raided the Amalekites (Chapter 27:8). Now they take revenge.
> But unlike David, the Amalekites did not kill anyone. They took the people away as slaves.
> David’s men weep until they cannot weep anymore. Then their sorrow turns into rage. They want to stone David. This is the lowest point in David’s life so far.
> And exactly here comes the important sentence: “But David strengthened himself in the LORD his God.” When everyone is against him and everything seems lost, David turns to God. He does not find his strength in himself, but in God.

---

## David asks God and chases the raiders (Verses 7–10)

[7] David said to Abiathar the priest, the son of Ahimelech,
“Please bring the ephod here to me.”
And Abiathar brought the ephod to David.
[8] David asked the LORD,
“If I chase after this band of raiders, will I catch up with them?”
He answered him,
“Chase them.
For you will surely catch up with them
and will surely rescue everything.”
[9] So David went, he and the 600 men who were with him.
They came to the brook Besor,
where those who were left behind stayed.
[10] But David kept chasing, he and 400 men.
For 200 men stayed behind,
because they were too worn out to cross the brook Besor.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 27 David had not asked before he went to the Philistines. Now he asks God again. He is back on the right path.
> God does not only answer with yes, but with a promise: You will get everything back.
> The men are worn out. They had been on the road for three days from Aphek to Ziklag, and now they go on chasing right away. Of them, 200 cannot go any farther.

---

## The Egyptian (Verses 11–15)

[11] They found an Egyptian in the field
and brought him to David.
They gave him bread, and he ate.
And they gave him water to drink.
[12] They gave him a piece of a fig cake and two clusters of raisins.
When he had eaten, his strength came back.
For he had not eaten any bread or drunk any water for three days and three nights.
[13] David asked him,
“To whom do you belong? And where are you from?”
He said,
“I am a young man from Egypt, the servant of an Amalekite.
My master left me behind because I got sick three days ago.
[14] We made a raid on the South of the Cherethites,
on the land that belongs to Judah,
and on the South of Caleb.
And we burned Ziklag with fire.”
[15] David said to him,
“Will you take me down to this band of raiders?”
He said,
“Swear to me by God that you will not kill me
and not hand me over to my master.
Then I will take you down to this band of raiders.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A sick slave is simply left behind by his master in the wilderness to die. David’s men find him and give him food and drink, even though they are in a hurry.
> This kindness is rewarded: The Egyptian leads them to the raiders. In this way the story shows: Whoever helps someone who is weak is often helped himself.
> The “Cherethites” were a group related to the Philistines who lived in the south. Later Cherethites were part of David’s bodyguard (2 Samuel 8:18).

---

## The victory over the Amalekites (Verses 16–20)

[16] When he had brought him down,
they were spread out over the whole area.
They were eating, drinking, and dancing
because of all the great plunder they had taken from the land of the Philistines and from the land of Judah.
[17] David struck them down from twilight until the evening of the next day.
Not one of them escaped,
except 400 young men who got on camels and fled.
[18] David rescued everything that the Amalekites had taken.
And David also rescued his two wives.
[19] Nothing of theirs was missing, young or old,
sons or daughters, or any of the plunder,
nothing of all that had been taken from them.
David brought everything back.
[20] David also took all the flocks and herds.
They drove them in front of the other livestock and said,
“This is David’s plunder.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Amalekites feel safe and celebrate. That is how they are taken by surprise.
> God’s promise comes true exactly: Nothing was missing. All the women and children come back. David even gets more than he had lost.

---

## Equal rights for all (Verses 21–25)

[21] David came to the 200 men
who had been too worn out to follow David
and who had been left at the brook Besor.
They went out to meet David and the people with him.
When David came near the people, he greeted them.
[22] Then all the wicked and worthless men among those who had gone with David said,
“Because they did not go with us,
we will not give them any of the plunder we have rescued.
Each man may only take his wife and his children
and leave with them.”
[23] Then David said,
“Don’t do that, my brothers,
with what the LORD has given us.
He has kept us safe
and given into our hand the band of raiders that came against us.
[24] Who will listen to you in this matter?
For the share of the one who goes down into the battle
shall be the same as the share of the one who stays with the baggage.
They shall share alike.”
[25] And so it was from that day on:
He made it a law and a rule for Israel to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Some men want to send away the worn-out men with nothing. But David says: No. The victory comes from God, not from us. So the plunder belongs to everyone.
> David makes it a law: Whoever fights and whoever stays behind to guard the baggage gets the same amount. The weak belong too.
> This is a principle of justice and of standing together: Everyone contributes what he can, and everyone shares. No one is to be treated unfairly because he is not as strong.

---

## Gifts to the elders of Judah (Verses 26–31)

[26] When David came to Ziklag,
he sent some of the plunder to the elders of Judah, his friends, and said,
“Here is a gift for you from the plunder of the LORD’s enemies.”
[27] He sent it to those in Bethel,
to those in Ramoth of the South,
to those in Jattir,
[28] to those in Aroer,
to those in Siphmoth,
to those in Eshtemoa,
[29] to those in Racal,
to those in the towns of the Jerahmeelites,
to those in the towns of the Kenites,
[30] to those in Hormah,
to those in Borashan,
to those in Athach,
[31] to those in Hebron,
and to all the places where David himself and his men used to stay.

> **What does this mean?**
> David shares his plunder with the towns in Judah that had helped him while he was on the run. That is thankfulness. And it is also wise: In this way he wins the hearts of the elders of Judah.
> “Bethel” here is probably a town in the south, not the well-known Bethel north of Jerusalem. Some manuscripts of the old Greek translation have “Beth Zur” here.
> The last town in the list is Hebron. There David will soon be anointed king over Judah (2 Samuel 2:4).
