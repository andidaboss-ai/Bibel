# 1 Samuel – Chapter 2
### Hannah’s song and Eli’s wicked sons

---

## Hannah’s song of praise (Verses 1–10)

[1] Hannah prayed and said,
“My heart rejoices in the LORD!
My horn is exalted by the LORD.
My mouth laughs at my enemies,
because I rejoice in your salvation.
[2] No one is as holy as the LORD.
For there is no one besides you.
And there is no rock like our God.

[3] Don’t keep talking so proudly.
Don’t let arrogant words come out of your mouth.
For the LORD is a God who knows everything.
By him all actions are weighed.

[4] The bows of the mighty men are broken.
Those who stumbled are given strength.
[5] Those who were full have hired themselves out for bread.
Those who were hungry are satisfied.
Yes, the barren woman has had seven children.
And she who has many children wastes away.

[6] The LORD kills and makes alive.
He brings down to Sheol and brings up again.
[7] The LORD makes poor and makes rich.
He brings low, and he also lifts up.
[8] He raises up the poor from the dust.
He lifts up the needy from the garbage heap,
to make them sit with princes
and give them a throne of honor.
For the pillars of the earth belong to the LORD.
He has set the world on them.
[9] He will guard the feet of his holy ones.
But the wicked will be silenced in darkness.
For no one wins by his own strength.
[10] Those who fight against the LORD will be broken to pieces.
He will thunder against them in the sky.
The LORD will judge the ends of the earth.
He will give strength to his king
and exalt the horn of his anointed one.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hannah sings a song of thanks. But it is much more than a personal thank-you for a child. It is a song about God, who turns the world upside down: The strong become weak, the weak become strong. Those who were full go hungry, and the hungry are satisfied. The poor sit with princes.
> The “horn” is a picture for strength and dignity, like the horn of a strong bull. “My horn is exalted” means: I am no longer put to shame, I can hold my head up again.
> “Rock” is a picture for God: He is firm and reliable, a safe place to hold on to.
> “Sheol” is the Hebrew word for the world of the dead. Hannah says: God has power over life and death.
> “The barren woman has had seven children”: Seven is a number for fullness. Later Hannah has six children in all (Verse 21).
> At the end, Hannah speaks of a “king” and an “anointed one.” But Israel does not even have a king yet. The song looks ahead to the time of the kings, to David. The Hebrew word for “anointed one” is “Mashiach,” Messiah. Christians see in this a pointer to Jesus, the “Christ,” which is the Greek word for “anointed one.”
> In the New Testament, Mary, the mother of Jesus, sings a song that is very much like this song (Luke 1:46–55).

## Eli’s wicked sons (Verses 11–17)

[11] Elkanah went home to Ramah.
And the boy served the LORD under Eli the priest.
[12] But the sons of Eli were wicked men.
They did not know the LORD.
[13] This was the custom of the priests with the people:
When anyone offered a sacrifice, the priest’s servant came
while the meat was boiling,
with a fork with three prongs in his hand.
[14] He stabbed it into the pan, or kettle, or cauldron, or pot.
Everything the fork brought up, the priest took for himself.
They did this to all the Israelites who came there to Shiloh.
[15] Even before the fat was burned,
the priest’s servant came and said to the man who was offering the sacrifice,
“Give meat to roast for the priest.
For he will not accept boiled meat from you, only raw.”
[16] If the man said to him,
“Let the fat be burned first, and then take as much as you want,”
then he would say,
“No, you must give it to me now.
If not, I will take it by force.”
[17] The sin of the young men was very great in front of the LORD.
For the men treated the LORD’s offering with contempt.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the beautiful story of Hannah comes a hard contrast: The priests in Shiloh are wicked.
> “They did not know the LORD”: They were priests, they worked in the holy place every day, but they had no real relationship with God.
> The law said exactly which part of a sacrifice the priests were to get (Leviticus 7:31–34). And the fat belonged first to God. It had to be burned on the altar (Leviticus 3:16). But Eli’s sons take whatever they want, even before God gets his part, and even by force.
> They misuse their holy office to make themselves rich. And they drive away the people who want to honor God. This is especially bad.

## Samuel grows up (Verses 18–21)

[18] But Samuel served in front of the LORD, while still a child,
dressed in a linen ephod.
[19] His mother also made him a little robe.
She brought it to him year after year,
when she came up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.
[20] Eli blessed Elkanah and his wife and said,
“May the LORD give you children from this woman,
in place of the one she asked for and gave to the LORD.”
Then they went to their home.
[21] The LORD visited Hannah,
and she became pregnant and had three sons and two daughters.
And the boy Samuel grew up in front of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> An “ephod” here was a simple priest’s garment made of linen. Little Samuel already wears the clothes of a servant in the holy place.
> Every year Hannah brings him a new, slightly bigger robe. In it is all the love of a mother who can see her child only once a year.
> Eli wishes Hannah more children, because she has given her request, the son she asked for, to the LORD.
> God blesses Hannah richly: She has five more children. Whoever gives to God does not become poorer.
> The storyteller keeps switching back and forth: Eli’s wicked sons, then good Samuel. In this way, the difference is easy to see.

## Eli warns his sons (Verses 22–26)

[22] Eli was very old.
He heard about everything his sons were doing to all Israel,
and that they slept with the women
who served at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.
[23] He said to them,
“Why do you do such things?
For I hear from all these people about your evil deeds.
[24] No, my sons.
What I hear is not a good report.
You are making the LORD’s people disobey.
[25] If one person sins against another, God will judge him.
But if a person sins against the LORD, who will speak up for him?”
But they did not listen to their father’s voice.
For the LORD intended to kill them.
[26] The boy Samuel kept on growing
and gained favor with the LORD and also with people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Eli’s sons also abuse the women who serve at the holy place. They take advantage of their power as priests. This is a serious crime.
> An old manuscript from the Dead Sea and the old Greek translation do not have the sentence about the women. But it is in the Hebrew text, which the WEB follows.
> Eli warns his sons. But he does nothing more. He is the high priest. He could have removed them from office, and he should have. He does not do it.
> Verse 25 is hard: “The LORD intended to kill them.” This does not mean that God forced them to do evil. They had decided against God for a long time. Now the time was up, and the judgment was fixed. The Bible often shows: Whoever keeps hardening himself against God may at some point no longer be able to turn back.
> Verse 26 sounds almost exactly like a sentence about Jesus as a child (Luke 2:52).

## A prophet announces judgment (Verses 27–36)

[27] A man of God came to Eli and said to him,
“This is what the LORD says:
‘Didn’t I clearly show myself to your father’s family
when they were slaves in Egypt in Pharaoh’s house?
[28] Didn’t I choose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest,
to go up to my altar, to burn incense,
and to wear an ephod in front of me?
Didn’t I give your father’s family all the offerings of the children of Israel made by fire?
[29] Why do you kick at my sacrifice and my offering,
which I commanded in my dwelling place?
And why do you honor your sons more than me,
so that you make yourselves fat with the best of all the offerings of my people Israel?’

[30] So the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘I did say that your family and your father’s family would walk in front of me forever.’
But now the LORD says:
‘Far be it from me!
For those who honor me I will honor.
And those who despise me will be cursed.
[31] The days are coming when I will cut off your strength
and the strength of your father’s family,
so that there will be no old man in your family.
[32] You will see trouble in my dwelling place,
in spite of all the good things I will give Israel.
There will never be an old man in your family.
[33] The man of yours whom I do not cut off from my altar
will make your eyes weak with weeping and fill your heart with grief.
And all the descendants of your family will die in the prime of life.
[34] And this will be the sign for you, which will come on your two sons,
on Hophni and Phinehas:
They will both die on the same day.
[35] I will raise up a faithful priest for myself,
who will do what is in my heart and in my mind.
I will build him a lasting family.
He will walk in front of my anointed one forever.
[36] And this is what will happen:
Everyone who is left in your family
will come and bow down to him for a piece of silver and a loaf of bread.
And he will say,
“Please give me a job with the priests,
so that I may have a bite of bread to eat.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> A prophet without a name comes to Eli. He reminds him: God made your family, the family of Aaron, priests out of grace.
> “Why do you honor your sons more than me?” Eli did not stop his sons, because they were his sons. And he probably ate some of the meat himself. The text says: “you make yourselves fat.”
> Verse 30 is an important sentence: “Those who honor me I will honor.” God’s promises are not a free pass. Whoever despises God cannot rely on where he comes from.
> What is announced here comes true step by step later: Hophni and Phinehas die on the same day (Chapter 4). Later almost all the priests from Eli’s family are killed in the city of Nob (Chapter 22). Only one, Abiathar, survives. Later King Solomon removes him from office (1 Kings 2:27).
> The “faithful priest” is probably first Samuel and later Zadok, who becomes high priest under Solomon. Christians also see in this a pointer to Jesus, the faithful high priest (Hebrews 2:17).
