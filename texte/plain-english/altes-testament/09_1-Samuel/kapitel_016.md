# 1 Samuel – Chapter 16
### Samuel anoints David

---

## Samuel goes to Bethlehem (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD said to Samuel,
“How long will you mourn for Saul?
I have rejected him from being king over Israel.
Fill your horn with oil and go.
I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem.
For I have chosen a king for myself among his sons.”
[2] Samuel said,
“How can I go?
If Saul hears about it, he will kill me.”
The LORD said,
“Take a heifer with you and say,
‘I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.’
[3] Invite Jesse to the sacrifice.
Then I will show you what you are to do.
You shall anoint for me the one I name to you.”
[4] Samuel did what the LORD had said and came to Bethlehem.
The elders of the town came to meet him trembling and said,
“Do you come in peace?”
[5] He said,
“In peace.
I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.
Make yourselves holy and come with me to the sacrifice.”
He made Jesse and his sons holy
and invited them to the sacrifice.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samuel is still mourning for Saul. But God says: It is time to look ahead. I already have a new king.
> Jesse is the grandson of Ruth and Boaz (Ruth 4:17). Here the story connects to the book of Ruth.
> Samuel is afraid of Saul. By now Saul is so dangerous that even the prophet fears for his life. God gives him a way: He is really to offer a sacrifice. That is not a lie, but he does not say everything.
> A heifer is a young cow.
> The elders of Bethlehem tremble. When the great prophet comes without warning, it can mean judgment. “Make yourselves holy” means: Get ready through washing and prayer to meet God.

---

## God looks at the heart (Verses 6–13)

[6] When they came, he saw Eliab and said,
“Surely the LORD’s anointed is standing here before him.”
[7] But the LORD said to Samuel,
“Don’t look at his appearance or at how tall he is,
for I have rejected him.
I don’t see as man sees.
Man looks at the outward appearance,
but the LORD looks at the heart.”
[8] Then Jesse called Abinadab
and had him walk past Samuel.
He said, “The LORD has not chosen this one either.”
[9] Then Jesse had Shammah walk past.
He said, “The LORD has not chosen this one either.”
[10] So Jesse had seven of his sons walk past Samuel.
Samuel said to Jesse,
“The LORD has not chosen these.”
[11] Samuel said to Jesse,
“Are these all your children?”
He said,
“There is still the youngest.
Look, he is taking care of the sheep.”
Samuel said to Jesse,
“Send for him and have him brought here.
For we will not sit down to eat until he comes here.”
[12] He sent for him and had him brought in.
He was ruddy, with a handsome face and a good appearance.
The LORD said,
“Get up! Anoint him, for he is the one.”
[13] Then Samuel took the horn of oil
and anointed him in the middle of his brothers.
And the Spirit of the LORD came on David with power
from that day on.
Then Samuel got up and went to Ramah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliab is the oldest son, tall and impressive, like Saul. Even Samuel thinks: It must be him. But God says one of the most important sentences in the Bible: “Man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.”
> This means: God does not judge people by their looks, their height, their wealth, or their position. He sees what a person is like inside.
> The youngest son was not even invited. He has to take care of the sheep. No one thought of him. But God chooses exactly him. God often chooses the small and the overlooked.
> “Ruddy” may mean reddish hair or a healthy, fresh skin color. In Hebrew, “with a handsome face” literally means “with beautiful eyes.”
> Only now, at the end, is his name given: David. The name probably means “beloved.”
> With Saul, the Spirit of God came only at certain moments. With David he stays “from that day on.”

## An evil spirit troubles Saul (Verses 14–23)

[14] The Spirit of the LORD left Saul,
and an evil spirit from the LORD troubled him.
[15] Saul’s servants said to him,
“Look, an evil spirit from God is troubling you.
[16] Let our lord command your servants who stand before you
to look for a man who can play the harp well.
Then when the evil spirit from God comes on you,
he will play with his hand.
And you will feel better.”
[17] Saul said to his servants,
“Find me a man who can play well
and bring him to me.”
[18] Then one of the young men answered,
“Look, I have seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem
who plays well.
He is a brave man, a warrior,
wise in speech, and handsome.
And the LORD is with him.”
[19] So Saul sent messengers to Jesse and said,
“Send me your son David, who is with the sheep.”
[20] Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread,
a container of wine, and a young goat,
and sent them to Saul with his son David.
[21] David came to Saul and entered his service.
Saul loved him very much,
and David became his armor bearer.
[22] Saul sent word to Jesse:
“Please let David stay in my service,
for he has found favor in my eyes.”
[23] Whenever the spirit from God came on Saul,
David took the harp and played with his hand.
Then Saul was refreshed and felt better,
and the evil spirit left him.

> **What does this mean?**
> “An evil spirit from the LORD” is a difficult expression. The people at that time believed: Everything, even the hard things, is in the end under God’s power. God allows Saul to be troubled, because Saul has turned away from him. It does not mean that God enjoys evil.
> Today we might say that Saul had severe depression, anxiety, or delusions. He is suspicious, moody, and has fits of rage. The Bible describes this honestly.
> Music helps Saul. People still know this today: Music can calm and comfort the soul. Today music therapy is used in medicine.
> The “harp” was actually a lyre, a small string instrument that people held in their arms.
> Here we see how God guides the paths: The future king comes to the court of the present king. At first Saul loves David. He does not know that Samuel has anointed him.
> David is not only a shepherd, but also a musician and poet. Later many psalms were said to be by him.
