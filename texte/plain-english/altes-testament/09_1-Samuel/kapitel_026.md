# 1 Samuel – Chapter 26
### David spares Saul a second time

---

## Spear and water jar (Verses 1–12)

[1] The Ziphites came to Saul at Gibeah and said,
“Isn’t David hiding on the hill of Hachilah, facing the desert?”
[2] So Saul got up and went down to the wilderness of Ziph,
with 3,000 chosen men of Israel,
to look for David in the wilderness of Ziph.
[3] Saul camped on the hill of Hachilah, facing the desert, by the road.
But David stayed in the wilderness.
He saw that Saul came after him into the wilderness.
[4] So David sent out spies
and found out that Saul had really come.
[5] Then David got up and came to the place where Saul had camped.
David saw the place where Saul lay,
with Abner the son of Ner, the commander of his army.
Saul lay in the middle of the camp with the wagons,
and the people were camped all around him.
[6] Then David said to Ahimelech the Hittite
and to Abishai the son of Zeruiah, the brother of Joab,
“Who will go down with me to Saul in the camp?”
Abishai said, “I will go down with you.”
[7] So David and Abishai came to the people by night.
And there was Saul, lying asleep in the middle of the camp with the wagons,
with his spear stuck in the ground at his head.
And Abner and the people were lying all around him.
[8] Then Abishai said to David,
“God has given your enemy into your hand today.
Now please let me pin him to the ground with the spear, with one single stroke.
I won’t need a second.”
[9] David said to Abishai,
“Don’t kill him.
For who can stretch out his hand against the LORD’s anointed
and be guiltless?”
[10] David said,
“As the LORD lives:
The LORD will strike him.
Or his day will come and he will die.
Or he will go down into battle and die there.
[11] The LORD forbid
that I should stretch out my hand against the LORD’s anointed.
But now please take the spear that is at his head
and the jar of water, and let’s go.”
[12] So David took the spear and the jar of water from Saul’s head,
and they went away.
No one saw it, no one knew it, and no one woke up.
For they were all asleep,
because a deep sleep from the LORD had fallen on them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Once again the Ziphites betray David (as in Chapter 23:19). Once again Saul hunts him with 3,000 men, even though in Chapter 24 he had promised to stop.
> This time David himself goes into the enemy’s camp, in the middle of the night. Abishai is David’s nephew. His mother Zeruiah was David’s sister (1 Chronicles 2:16). Abishai and his brother Joab will later be David’s most important army commanders.
> Abishai says the same thing as David’s men in the cave: God has given him to you! Let me kill him. And David answers again: No. God will act himself, when and how he wants.
> The spear was Saul’s sign of power. With this spear Saul had tried several times to kill David. Now David takes it away without using it.
> “A deep sleep from the LORD”: God himself protects David on this dangerous walk.

---

## David calls to Abner (Verses 13–16)

[13] Then David went over to the other side
and stood far away on the top of the mountain,
so that there was a great distance between them.
[14] David called to the people and to Abner the son of Ner,
“Won’t you answer, Abner?”
Then Abner answered,
“Who are you, calling to the king?”
[15] David said to Abner,
“Aren’t you a man?
And who is like you in Israel?
Why then have you not guarded your lord the king?
For one of the people came in to kill your lord the king.
[16] What you have done is not good.
As the LORD lives,
you deserve to die,
because you have not guarded your lord, the LORD’s anointed.
And now see where the king’s spear is,
and the jar of water that was at his head.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David mocks Abner, the great army commander: You are the king’s bodyguard, and you were asleep! If I had been a murderer, the king would be dead now.
> This is proof: David could have killed Saul. He did not do it.

---

## David’s last request to Saul (Verses 17–25)

[17] Saul recognized David’s voice and said,
“Is this your voice, my son David?”
David said,
“It is my voice, my lord the king.”
[18] He said,
“Why is my lord chasing his servant?
For what have I done?
What evil is in my hand?
[19] So now please let my lord the king hear the words of his servant:
If it is the LORD who has stirred you up against me,
then may he accept an offering.
But if it is people,
then may they be cursed before the LORD.
For they have driven me out today,
so that I no longer have a share in the LORD’s inheritance.
They say, ‘Go, serve other gods!’
[20] So now don’t let my blood fall to the ground far away from the presence of the LORD.
For the king of Israel has come out to look for a flea,
as someone hunts a partridge in the mountains.”

[21] Then Saul said,
“I have sinned.
Come back, my son David.
For I will not harm you anymore,
because my life was precious in your eyes today.
Look, I have played the fool and have been very wrong.”
[22] David answered,
“Here is the spear, O king!
Let one of the young men come over and get it.
[23] The LORD will reward everyone for his righteousness and his faithfulness.
For the LORD gave you into my hand today,
but I would not stretch out my hand against the LORD’s anointed.
[24] And look:
As your life was valued in my eyes today,
so may my life be valued in the eyes of the LORD.
And may he rescue me from all trouble.”
[25] Then Saul said to David,
“You are blessed, my son David.
You will do great things and will surely prevail.”
Then David went his way,
and Saul went back to his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> David says: If God has stirred you up against me, then let us bring an offering and make peace with God. But if people are stirring you up, then they are cursed.
> David suffers because he is being driven out of Israel. At that time people thought: Whoever lives in a foreign land is far from God’s presence and has to serve foreign gods there. David does not want to die far from God.
> “A partridge in the mountains”: A partridge is a bird that would rather run than fly. People hunt it until it is worn out. That is how David feels.
> Saul says again: “I have sinned” and “I have played the fool.” But David no longer believes him. He gives back the spear, but he does not go with Saul.
> This is the last time Saul and David meet. Saul’s last words to David are a blessing: “You will do great things and will surely prevail.”
