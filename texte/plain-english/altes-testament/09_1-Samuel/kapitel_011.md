# 1 Samuel – Chapter 11
### Saul rescues the city of Jabesh

---

## The Ammonite’s threat (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then Nahash the Ammonite came up
and surrounded Jabesh Gilead.
All the men of Jabesh said to Nahash,
“Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you.”
[2] Nahash the Ammonite said to them,
“I will make a covenant with you on this condition:
that the right eye of every one of you is gouged out.
That is how I will bring shame on all Israel.”
[3] The elders of Jabesh said to him,
“Give us seven days.
Then we will send messengers through the whole territory of Israel.
If there is no one to save us then,
we will come out to you.”
[4] Then the messengers came to Gibeah, Saul’s town,
and spoke these words in the hearing of the people.
Then all the people cried out loud and wept.
[5] Just then Saul was coming in from the field behind the oxen.
Saul said,
“What is wrong with the people? Why are they weeping?”
They told him the words of the men of Jabesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ammonites lived east of the Jordan. Jabesh Gilead was an Israelite city there, near the border.
> Nahash does not only want to defeat the men of Jabesh, but to humiliate them cruelly. In battle a man held his shield in front of his left eye. Without his right eye he could not see anything anymore. He was useless as a soldier. And the injury was meant to be a shame for all Israel.
> Nahash is so sure of himself that he even gives them seven days to look for help. He does not believe that Israel will help them.
> Why do the men of Jabesh send messengers to Gibeah of all places? Maybe because of an old connection: At the end of the book of Judges the men of Benjamin got wives from Jabesh (Judges 21:12–14). Many people in Gibeah may have had ancestors from Jabesh.
> Saul has been chosen king, but he still works as a farmer in the field. There is no palace and no army yet.

---

## Saul calls Israel together (Verses 6–11)

[6] The Spirit of God came on Saul with power when he heard these words,
and he became very angry.
[7] He took a pair of oxen,
cut them into pieces,
and sent the pieces by messengers through the whole territory of Israel with the message:
“Whoever doesn’t come out after Saul and after Samuel,
this is what will be done to his oxen.”
Then the fear of the LORD fell on the people,
and they came out as one man.
[8] He counted them at Bezek.
The children of Israel were 300,000 men,
and the men of Judah 30,000.
[9] They said to the messengers who had come,
“Tell the men of Jabesh Gilead:
‘Tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, you will be rescued.’”
The messengers came and told the men of Jabesh,
and they were glad.
[10] So the men of Jabesh said to the Ammonites,
“Tomorrow we will come out to you.
Then you can do with us whatever seems good to you.”
[11] The next day Saul divided the people into three groups.
In the morning watch they broke into the middle of the camp
and struck down the Ammonites until the heat of the day.
Those who were left were scattered,
so that not even two of them were left together.

> **What does this mean?**
> Once again the Spirit of God comes on Saul, as with the judges Othniel, Gideon, Jephthah, and Samson. Now Saul shows that he can be a rescuer.
> His anger is a just anger: He cannot stand by and watch people being tortured.
> Saul cuts oxen into pieces and sends the pieces through Israel. This strongly reminds us of Judges 19:29. Back then a man in Gibeah had cut up a woman’s body. Now a man from Gibeah takes oxen. He does not threaten the people, but their oxen.
> Saul also names Samuel. He does not act alone, but together with the prophet.
> The numbers are very large. Some Bible teachers think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” here rather means “unit” or “troop.” The old Greek translation has 600,000 and 70,000.
> The “morning watch” was the last watch of the night, shortly before sunrise. An attack at this time was a surprise.
> The men of Jabesh chose the words of their promise “Tomorrow we will come out to you” on purpose, so that it could mean two things. They came out, but not to surrender.

## No revenge on the day of rescue (Verses 12–15)

[12] The people said to Samuel,
“Who was it who said, ‘Shall Saul rule over us?’
Bring those men here, so that we can put them to death!”
[13] Saul said,
“No one shall be put to death today.
For today the LORD has rescued Israel.”
[14] Then Samuel said to the people,
“Come! Let’s go to Gilgal
and renew the kingdom there.”
[15] So all the people went to Gilgal.
And there they made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal.
There they offered peace offerings before the LORD.
And there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the victory some people want revenge on those who had looked down on Saul (Chapter 10:27). But Saul says: No. Today is a day of joy, because God has rescued us. This is a great moment for Saul. He is merciful and gives the glory to God, not to himself.
> This scene shows: Saul had good qualities. At the beginning he was humble, brave, and generous. That makes it all the sadder how he changes later.
> Gilgal was an important place. There Israel had its first camp in the land after crossing the Jordan (Joshua 4:19).
> The men of Jabesh never forgot that Saul rescued them. Many years later, when Saul was dead, they risked their lives to take his body back from the Philistines and gave him an honorable burial (Chapter 31:11–13).
