# 1 Samuel – Chapter 25
### David, Nabal, and wise Abigail

---

## Samuel dies (Verse 1)

[1] Samuel died.
All Israel gathered and mourned for him.
They buried him at his house in Ramah.
Then David got up and went down to the wilderness of Paran.

> **What does this mean?**
> The great prophet Samuel dies. He was the last judge and anointed the first two kings. All Israel mourns for him.
> With Samuel’s death David has lost an important supporter.
> The wilderness of Paran is far to the south. The old Greek translation has “the wilderness of Maon” here. That fits better with the rest of the story.

---

## Rich Nabal (Verses 2–13)

[2] There was a man in Maon whose property was in Carmel.
The man was very rich.
He had 3,000 sheep and 1,000 goats.
And he was shearing his sheep in Carmel.
[3] The name of the man was Nabal,
and the name of his wife was Abigail.
The woman was wise and had a beautiful face.
But the man was rude and evil in what he did.
He was from the family of Caleb.
[4] In the wilderness David heard that Nabal was shearing his sheep.
[5] So David sent 10 young men.
David said to the young men,
“Go up to Carmel, go to Nabal,
and greet him in my name.
[6] Say to him,
‘Long life to you!
Peace to you! Peace to your house!
Peace to all that you have!
[7] I have heard that you have shearers.
Your shepherds have been with us,
and we did not harm them.
Nothing of theirs was missing the whole time they were in Carmel.
[8] Ask your young men, and they will tell you.
So let these young men find favor in your eyes,
for we have come on a good day.
Please give your servants and your son David
whatever you have on hand.’”
[9] When David’s young men arrived,
they told Nabal all these words in David’s name and waited.
[10] Nabal answered David’s servants,
“Who is David?
Who is the son of Jesse?
These days there are many servants who run away from their masters.
[11] Shall I take my bread and my water
and my meat that I have killed for my shearers,
and give it to men when I don’t know where they come from?”
[12] So David’s young men turned around and went back.
They came and told him all these words.
[13] David said to his men,
“Every man put on his sword!”
And every man put on his sword.
David also put on his sword.
About 400 men went up after David,
and 200 stayed with the baggage.

> **What does this mean?**
> Maon and Carmel are towns in Judah, south of Hebron. This Carmel is not Mount Carmel in the north.
> The name “Nabal” means “fool” in Hebrew. His wife Abigail is the exact opposite: wise and beautiful.
> Shearing the sheep, that is, cutting off their wool, was a big celebration, like a harvest festival. At that time people usually were generous and shared.
> David and his men had protected Nabal’s shepherds in the wilderness, from robbers and wild animals. Now David politely asks for a share. At that time this was not rude, but normal.
> Nabal insults David: “Who is David?” He calls him a runaway servant. And he keeps saying “my”: my bread, my water, my meat. He thinks only of himself.
> David becomes very angry. He wants revenge. This is a very different David from the one in Chapter 24, who spared Saul.

---

## A servant warns Abigail (Verses 14–22)

[14] But one of the young men told Abigail, Nabal’s wife,
“Look, David sent messengers from the wilderness to greet our master.
And he insulted them.
[15] But the men were very good to us.
Nothing happened to us,
and we did not miss anything as long as we went around with them,
when we were in the fields.
[16] They were a wall around us, by night and by day,
the whole time we were with them taking care of the sheep.
[17] So now think about it and see what you will do.
For disaster has been decided against our master and against his whole house.
And he is such a worthless fellow that no one can talk to him.”

[18] Then Abigail hurried.
She took 200 loaves of bread, 2 containers of wine,
5 sheep ready to cook,
about 10 gallons of roasted grain,
100 clusters of raisins, and 200 cakes of figs,
and loaded them on donkeys.
[19] She said to her young men,
“Go on ahead of me.
Look, I am coming after you.”
But she did not tell her husband Nabal.
[20] As she was riding on her donkey
and coming down under cover of the mountain,
David and his men were coming down toward her,
and she met them.
[21] Now David had said,
“It was for nothing that I guarded everything this fellow has in the wilderness,
so that nothing of all that belonged to him was missing.
He has paid me back evil for good.
[22] May God do so to the enemies of David, and even more,
if by morning light I leave alive of all that belongs to him
even one who urinates on a wall.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The servant confirms: David and his men were like a protective wall. The servant knows that no one can talk to Nabal. That is why he goes to Abigail.
> Abigail acts right away and wisely. She prepares a big gift. The Bible says “five seahs.” A seah is about 2 gallons.
> David swears revenge. “One who urinates on a wall” is a crude expression for “man” or “boy.” David wants to kill all the men in Nabal’s house.
> “May God do so to the enemies of David”: That is what the Hebrew text says. Usually this kind of oath says “May God do so to me.” The old Greek translation has “May God do so to David.” Maybe the scribes changed the sentence so that the curse would not fall on David, because David did not carry out his oath.

## Abigail’s wise speech (Verses 23–31)

[23] When Abigail saw David,
she quickly got off her donkey,
fell on her face before David,
and bowed to the ground.
[24] She fell at his feet and said,
“On me, my lord, on me be the blame!
Please let your servant speak to you.
Hear the words of your servant.
[25] Please don’t let my lord pay attention to this worthless fellow, Nabal.
For as his name is, so is he.
Nabal is his name, and foolishness is with him.
But I, your servant, did not see my lord’s young men
whom you sent.
[26] And now, my lord, as the LORD lives and as you live:
The LORD has kept you from bringing blood guilt on yourself
and from taking revenge with your own hand.
So now let your enemies and all who want to harm my lord be like Nabal.
[27] And let this gift that your servant has brought to my lord
be given to the young men who follow my lord.
[28] Please forgive the wrong of your servant.
For the LORD will certainly make my lord a lasting house,
because my lord fights the LORD’s battles.
And no evil will be found in you all your life.
[29] Even if people rise up to chase you and to take your life,
the life of my lord will be bound in the bundle of life
with the LORD your God.
But the lives of your enemies he will sling away
as from the pocket of a sling.
[30] And this is what will happen:
When the LORD has done for my lord all the good he has promised you
and has made you prince over Israel,
[31] then this will not be a grief to you or a burden on your heart,
that you shed blood without cause
or that my lord took revenge himself.
And when the LORD has been good to my lord,
then remember your servant.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Abigail’s speech is one of the longest speeches by a woman in the Bible. And it is a masterpiece.
> She takes the blame on herself, even though she is innocent. In this way she makes it easy for David to give in.
> She reminds David of what God has promised him: You will be king. A king should not start out as someone who shed blood out of revenge. That would weigh on you later.
> She is really saying: You did not kill Saul to help yourself. Don’t do it with Nabal now either. Leave revenge to God.
> “The bundle of life” is a beautiful picture: God keeps your life safe, the way you tie something precious into a bundle. To this day many Jewish gravestones have five Hebrew letters that stand for the sentence: “May his soul be bound in the bundle of life.”
> The picture of the sling reminds us of David and Goliath.
> Abigail is a prophet without being called one. She tells David’s future.

## David thanks God for Abigail (Verses 32–35)

[32] David said to Abigail,
“Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
who sent you to meet me today!
[33] Blessed is your good sense,
and blessed are you,
who have kept me today from bringing blood guilt on myself
and from taking revenge with my own hand.
[34] For truly, as the LORD, the God of Israel, lives,
who has kept me from harming you:
If you had not hurried and come to meet me,
then by morning light not even one who urinates on a wall
would have been left to Nabal.”
[35] So David accepted from her hand what she had brought him.
Then he said to her,
“Go up to your house in peace.
Look, I have listened to your voice and granted your request.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David listens to a woman who disagrees with him. That is a sign of greatness. Saul never listened to advice. David can let himself be corrected.
> He realizes: God sent Abigail. Through her God kept me from a great sin.
> This is an important lesson: Sometimes God sends us people who stop us when we want to do something wrong in our anger.

## Nabal’s death (Verses 36–38)

[36] Abigail came to Nabal.
He was holding a feast in his house, like the feast of a king.
Nabal’s heart was merry, for he was very drunk.
So she told him nothing until morning light.
[37] In the morning, when the wine had gone out of Nabal,
his wife told him these things.
Then his heart died within him, and he became like a stone.
[38] About 10 days later the LORD struck Nabal, so that he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nabal celebrates “like the feast of a king.” He acts as if he were the king, while the true future king lives in the wilderness.
> When he is sober in the morning and hears how close he came to death, he is so shocked that “his heart died within him.” Maybe it was a stroke or a heart attack.
> After 10 days he dies. The Bible says: The LORD struck him. David did not have to take revenge. God judged him himself.

## David marries Abigail (Verses 39–44)

[39] When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said,
“Blessed be the LORD,
who has pleaded my cause against the insult from Nabal
and has kept his servant from evil.
The LORD has turned Nabal’s evil back on his own head.”
Then David sent messengers to speak with Abigail
about taking her as his wife.
[40] When David’s servants came to Abigail at Carmel,
they spoke to her and said,
“David has sent us to you
to take you to him as his wife.”
[41] She got up, bowed with her face to the ground, and said,
“Look, your servant is ready to be a servant who washes the feet of my lord’s servants.”
[42] Abigail quickly got up
and rode on a donkey, with her five maids following her.
She went after David’s messengers
and became his wife.
[43] David had also taken Ahinoam of Jezreel.
And they both became his wives.
[44] But Saul had given his daughter Michal, David’s wife,
to Palti the son of Laish, who was from Gallim.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is thankful that God kept him from taking revenge himself.
> David marries Abigail. At that time it was often a matter of life and death for a widow without a son to have a husband again. Abigail says yes. She joins herself to the man she knows will be king.
> Now David has several wives: Ahinoam and Abigail. Saul has given David’s first wife Michal to another man, probably to hurt David and to take away his claim to the royal family.
> At that time it was common for kings to have several wives. But the law contains a warning: A king should not have many wives, so that his heart does not turn away (Deuteronomy 17:17). David’s many marriages will later lead to great misery in his family.
