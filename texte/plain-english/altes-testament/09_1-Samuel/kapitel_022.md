# 1 Samuel – Chapter 22
### The cave of Adullam and the massacre at Nob

---

## David’s people (Verses 1–5)

[1] So David left there and escaped to the cave of Adullam.
When his brothers and his father’s whole family heard about it,
they went down there to him.
[2] Everyone who was in trouble,
everyone who was in debt,
and everyone who was bitter gathered around him.
And he became their leader.
There were about 400 men with him.
[3] From there David went to Mizpeh in Moab.
He said to the king of Moab,
“Please let my father and my mother stay with you
until I know what God will do for me.”
[4] He brought them to the king of Moab.
And they stayed with him the whole time that David was in the stronghold.
[5] The prophet Gad said to David,
“Don’t stay in the stronghold.
Leave and go into the land of Judah.”
So David left and came into the forest of Hereth.

> **What does this mean?**
> The cave of Adullam was in the hill country of Judah, about 15 miles southwest of Bethlehem. There are many caves there.
> David’s family has to flee too. Saul could take revenge on them.
> No heroes or nobles come to David, but people on the edge: the poor, people in debt, the unhappy. From them David makes a troop. Here Christians remember Jesus, to whom outcasts and despised people came too.
> David brings his parents to Moab. That is no accident: David’s great-grandmother Ruth was a Moabite (Ruth 4:17).
> Gad is a prophet who goes with David from now on. David too listens to the word of God through a prophet. That makes him different from Saul.
> According to their titles, Psalm 57 and Psalm 142 are prayers of David “in the cave.”

---

## Saul’s suspicion (Verses 6–10)

[6] Saul heard that David and the men with him had been found.
Saul was sitting in Gibeah, under the tamarisk tree in Ramah,
with his spear in his hand,
and all his servants were standing around him.
[7] Saul said to his servants who were standing around him,
“Listen, you Benjamites!
Will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards?
Will he make you all captains of thousands and captains of hundreds?
[8] Is that why all of you have plotted against me?
No one tells me when my son makes a covenant with the son of Jesse.
None of you is sorry for me
or tells me that my son has stirred up my servant against me
to lie in wait for me, as he is doing today.”
[9] Then Doeg the Edomite, who was standing with Saul’s servants, answered,
“I saw the son of Jesse coming to Nob,
to Ahimelech the son of Ahitub.
[10] He asked the LORD for him,
gave him food,
and gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A tamarisk is a tree with small leaves that grows in dry places.
> Saul sits under a tree with his spear in his hand. He is suspicious of everyone. He believes that everyone has plotted against him, even his own son.
> He appeals to his own people from the tribe of Benjamin: David is from Judah. He will give you nothing. Only I can reward you. In this way Saul divides the people by tribes.
> He feels sorry for himself: “None of you is sorry for me.” Saul sees himself as the victim, even though he is the one who is persecuting.
> Doeg uses the chance to make the king like him. He tells only half the truth: He does not say that Ahimelech knew nothing about David’s escape.

---

## Saul has the priests killed (Verses 11–19)

[11] Then the king sent for Ahimelech the priest, the son of Ahitub,
and his father’s whole family, the priests who were in Nob.
And they all came to the king.
[12] Saul said, “Listen now, you son of Ahitub!”
He answered, “Here I am, my lord.”
[13] Saul said to him,
“Why have you plotted against me, you and the son of Jesse?
You gave him bread and a sword
and asked God for him,
so that he could rise up against me and lie in wait for me, as he is doing today.”
[14] Then Ahimelech answered the king,
“Who among all your servants is as faithful as David?
He is the king’s son-in-law,
the captain of your bodyguard,
and honored in your house.
[15] Is today the first time I have asked God for him?
Far be it from me!
Don’t let the king blame his servant or my father’s whole family for anything.
For your servant knew nothing about all this, little or much.”
[16] The king said,
“You shall surely die, Ahimelech, you and your father’s whole family.”
[17] The king said to the guards who were standing around him,
“Turn and kill the priests of the LORD.
For their hand also is with David,
and they knew that he was fleeing and did not tell me.”
But the king’s servants would not raise their hand
to attack the priests of the LORD.
[18] Then the king said to Doeg,
“You turn and attack the priests!”
So Doeg the Edomite turned and attacked the priests.
That day he killed 85 men who wore a linen ephod.
[19] And he struck Nob, the town of the priests, with the sword:
men and women, children and nursing babies,
cattle, donkeys, and sheep, with the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahimelech defends himself honestly and bravely: David was your most faithful servant! I have done nothing evil. He is completely innocent.
> But Saul does not want to listen. He sentences the whole family of priests to death.
> Saul’s soldiers refuse. They do not want to kill priests of the LORD. This is brave disobedience. There are orders that must not be followed. Whoever is told to kill innocent people must refuse.
> Doeg, a foreigner with no respect for God, carries out the cruel order. He kills 85 priests. The old Greek translation has 305.
> Then Saul destroys the whole town of Nob, with women, children, and animals. This is shocking: Against the Amalekites Saul had not fully carried out God’s command (Chapter 15). But against God’s own priests he carries out a complete destruction out of hatred. That is how far Saul has fallen.
> Here the dark announcement about the house of Eli also comes true (1 Samuel 2:31–33).

## Abiathar escapes (Verses 20–23)

[20] One of the sons of Ahimelech the son of Ahitub escaped.
His name was Abiathar.
He fled to David.
[21] Abiathar told David that Saul had killed the priests of the LORD.
[22] David said to Abiathar,
“I knew on that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there,
that he would surely tell Saul.
I am responsible for the death of all the people of your father’s family.
[23] Stay with me.
Don’t be afraid.
For whoever wants to kill me also wants to kill you.
You will be safe with me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David takes responsibility. He says: It is my fault. I lied to the priest, and I saw Doeg. That makes him different from Saul, who always blames others.
> David takes Abiathar under his protection. From now on David has a priest with him. Abiathar brings the ephod with him, with which people can ask God for answers (1 Samuel 23:6).
> Saul has killed the priests, and with them the possibility of asking God. But David now has a priest and a prophet with him.
