# 1 Samuel – Chapter 24
### David spares Saul in the cave

---

## In the cave (Verses 1–7)

[1] When Saul came back from chasing the Philistines,
he was told,
“Look, David is in the wilderness of En Gedi.”
[2] Then Saul took 3,000 chosen men from all Israel
and went to look for David and his men on the rocks of the wild goats.
[3] He came to the sheep pens by the road, where there was a cave.
And Saul went in to relieve himself.
David and his men were sitting far back in the cave.
[4] David’s men said to him,
“Look, this is the day the LORD told you about:
‘Look, I will give your enemy into your hand,
and you can do to him whatever seems good to you.’”
Then David got up
and secretly cut off the edge of Saul’s robe.
[5] Afterward David’s heart troubled him,
because he had cut off the edge of Saul’s robe.
[6] He said to his men,
“The LORD forbid that I should do this to my lord,
the LORD’s anointed,
to stretch out my hand against him.
For he is the LORD’s anointed.”
[7] So David held his men back with these words
and did not let them attack Saul.
Saul got up, left the cave, and went on his way.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles the numbering in this chapter is shifted by one verse. Verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 23.
> Saul is hunting David with 3,000 elite soldiers. And then he goes into the very cave where David is hiding. He is alone, defenseless, with his back to them.
> David’s men say: This is your chance! God has given him into your hand! A word from God like the one they quote does not appear anywhere earlier. Maybe they are simply explaining the situation this way.
> David sneaks up to Saul. But he does not kill him. He only cuts off an edge of his robe. And even that makes him feel sorry afterward. His conscience troubles him.
> Why? The edge of the robe was a sign of dignity and power. Let us remember: Saul once tore off the edge of Samuel’s robe, and Samuel said that the kingdom would be torn from him in the same way (Chapter 15:27–28). David does not want to take the kingdom away from Saul himself. He wants to wait until God gives it to him.
> “The LORD’s anointed”: Saul is still appointed by God. David respects that, even though Saul is persecuting him. He leaves the judgment to God.

---

## David speaks to Saul (Verses 8–15)

[8] After that David also got up,
went out of the cave,
and called after Saul,
“My lord the king!”
When Saul looked behind him,
David bowed with his face to the ground and showed him respect.
[9] David said to Saul,
“Why do you listen to people who say,
‘Look, David wants to harm you’?
[10] Look, today your eyes have seen
how the LORD gave you into my hand today in the cave.
Some urged me to kill you.
But I spared you.
I said,
‘I will not stretch out my hand against my lord,
for he is the LORD’s anointed.’
[11] And look, my father,
see the edge of your robe in my hand.
I cut off the edge of your robe
and did not kill you.
From this you can know and see
that there is no evil or rebellion in my hand.
I have not sinned against you,
even though you are hunting me to take my life.
[12] May the LORD judge between me and you,
and may the LORD avenge me on you.
But my hand will not be on you.
[13] As the old saying goes,
‘Out of the wicked comes wickedness.’
But my hand will not be on you.
[14] Whom has the king of Israel come out against?
Whom are you chasing?
A dead dog? A flea?
[15] So may the LORD be judge
and decide between me and you.
May he see and plead my cause
and rescue me from your hand.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David shows great courage. He goes out and shows himself to the man who wants to kill him. He bows before him and calls him “my father.”
> He holds out the edge of the robe to him: Look, I could have killed you. I did not do it. I am not your enemy.
> David says it again and again: “My hand will not be on you.” He leaves the judgment to God. That is the heart of this story: Do not take revenge yourself, but let God judge. Paul later writes that we should not take revenge ourselves, but overcome evil with good (Romans 12:19–21).
> “A dead dog? A flea?”: David makes himself small. Why does the mighty king chase after such an unimportant person? That is ridiculous.

## Saul weeps (Verses 16–22)

[16] When David had finished saying these words to Saul,
Saul said,
“Is that your voice, my son David?”
And Saul cried out loud and wept.
[17] He said to David,
“You are more righteous than I.
For you have done good to me,
but I have done evil to you.
[18] Today you have shown how well you have treated me.
For the LORD had given me into your hand,
and you did not kill me.
[19] For if a man finds his enemy,
will he let him go away unharmed?
So may the LORD reward you with good for what you have done for me today.
[20] And now, look, I know that you will surely be king
and that the kingdom of Israel will be made firm in your hand.
[21] So now swear to me by the LORD
that you will not wipe out my descendants after me
and that you will not destroy my name from my father’s family.”
[22] David swore it to Saul.
Saul went home.
But David and his men went up to the stronghold.

> **What does this mean?**
> For a moment Saul’s hard heart opens up. He weeps. He calls David “my son.” And he tells the truth: “You are more righteous than I.”
> For the first time Saul says it openly: I know that you will be king. He asks for the same thing as Jonathan: Spare my family. David swears it.
> But David stays careful. He does not go home with Saul, but back to the stronghold. He knows: Saul’s mood can change quickly again. And that is exactly what will happen (Chapter 26).
> This story is a great lesson: You can answer evil with good. Love for your enemy can even touch the heart of a persecutor, at least for a moment.
