# 1 Samuel – Chapter 17
### David and Goliath

---

## The giant Goliath (Verses 1–11)

[1] The Philistines gathered their armies for battle.
They gathered at Socoh, which belongs to Judah,
and camped between Socoh and Azekah, at Ephesdammim.
[2] Saul and the men of Israel gathered,
camped in the valley of Elah,
and lined up for battle against the Philistines.
[3] The Philistines stood on the mountain on one side,
and Israel stood on the mountain on the other side.
And the valley was between them.

[4] Then a champion came out of the Philistine camp.
His name was Goliath of Gath.
He was about nine and a half feet tall.
[5] He had a bronze helmet on his head
and wore a coat of armor made of scales.
The coat weighed about 125 pounds of bronze.
[6] He had bronze armor on his shins
and a bronze javelin between his shoulders.
[7] The shaft of his spear was like a weaver’s beam.
And the head of his spear weighed about 15 pounds of iron.
His shield bearer went in front of him.
[8] He stood and shouted to the ranks of Israel,
“Why have you come out to line up for battle?
Am I not a Philistine, and you servants of Saul?
Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me.
[9] If he is able to fight with me and kill me,
then we will be your servants.
But if I defeat him and kill him,
then you will be our servants and serve us.”
[10] The Philistine said,
“I defy the ranks of Israel today!
Give me a man, so that we can fight each other!”
[11] When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine,
they were shocked and very afraid.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Elah” means “terebinth,” which is a kind of tree. The valley of Elah is about 15 miles southwest of Jerusalem. You can still visit it today.
> The Bible says: “six cubits and a span.” A cubit is about 18 inches, a span about 9 inches. That is about nine and a half feet. An old manuscript from the Dead Sea, the old Greek translation, and Josephus have “four cubits and a span,” which is about six and a half feet. Even that was a giant at that time, because most men were much shorter.
> The coat of armor weighed 5,000 shekels, the spearhead 600 shekels. A shekel is a little under half an ounce.
> A “weaver’s beam” was a thick wooden bar on a loom, a machine for weaving cloth.
> Goliath suggests a duel: One man against one man decides the whole war. This was sometimes done at that time.
> And Saul? He is the tallest man in Israel (Chapter 9:2). Really, he should have fought against Goliath. But he too is afraid.

## David comes to the camp (Verses 12–24)

[12] David was the son of that Ephrathite of Bethlehem in Judah whose name was Jesse.
He had eight sons.
In the days of Saul the man was already very old.
[13] The three oldest sons of Jesse had followed Saul to the war.
The names of his three sons who had gone to the war were:
Eliab the firstborn,
Abinadab the second,
and Shammah the third.
[14] David was the youngest.
And the three oldest had followed Saul.
[15] But David went back and forth from Saul
to take care of his father’s sheep at Bethlehem.
[16] The Philistine came forward morning and evening
and took his stand, for forty days.

[17] Jesse said to his son David,
“Take for your brothers about 6 gallons of this roasted grain
and these ten loaves of bread,
and bring them quickly to the camp to your brothers.
[18] And bring these ten cheeses to the commander of their thousand.
See how your brothers are doing,
and bring back news from them.”
[19] Saul and they and all the men of Israel were in the valley of Elah,
fighting against the Philistines.

[20] David got up early in the morning,
left the sheep with someone to watch them,
took the food, and went, as Jesse had commanded him.
He came to the camp with the wagons
just as the army was going out to the battle line, shouting the war cry.
[21] Israel and the Philistines lined up, army against army.
[22] David left his things with the man who guarded the baggage,
ran to the battle line,
came, and greeted his brothers.
[23] While he was talking with them,
the champion came up,
the Philistine of Gath, named Goliath,
out of the ranks of the Philistines.
He said the same words, and David heard them.
[24] When all the men of Israel saw the man,
they fled from him and were terrified.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here David is introduced once more, as if we had not heard of him yet. This is probably because different stories about David were put together here. The old Greek translation has a shorter text: Verses 12–31 and 55–58 are missing there.
> Here Jesse has eight sons. In 1 Chronicles 2:13–15 only seven are named, and there David is the seventh. Maybe one son died young.
> For forty days Goliath mocks Israel, and no one dares to fight. In the Bible, forty is often a number for a time of testing.
> The Bible says “an ephah.” An ephah is about 6 gallons.
> David does not come as a fighter, but as a messenger with food. He is the little brother who brings bread and cheese to his big brothers.

## David wants to fight (Verses 25–30)

[25] The men of Israel said,
“Have you seen this man who comes up?
He comes up to defy Israel.
The king will give great riches to the man who kills him.
He will give him his daughter
and free his father’s family in Israel from taxes.”
[26] David said to the men who were standing near him,
“What will be done for the man who kills this Philistine
and takes away the shame from Israel?
For who is this uncircumcised Philistine,
that he should defy the armies of the living God?”
[27] The people answered him in the same way and said,
“This is what will be done for the man who kills him.”
[28] Eliab, his oldest brother, heard him talking with the men.
Then Eliab became angry with David and said,
“Why have you come down here?
And with whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness?
I know your pride and the evil of your heart.
For you have only come down to watch the battle.”
[29] David said,
“What have I done now?
Is there not a cause?”
[30] He turned away from him to someone else
and said the same thing again.
And the people answered him again as before.

> **What does this mean?**
> The soldiers only talk about the reward. But David talks about God: Goliath is not only mocking Israel, but “the living God.” For David, that is the real outrage.
> “Uncircumcised” here means: He does not belong to the covenant with God.
> Eliab is furious. Maybe he is jealous because Samuel anointed David and not him. He accuses David of pride. But really Eliab is the one who does not dare to fight.
> “Is there not a cause?”: The Hebrew can also mean: “It was only a word” or “only a question.”

## David before Saul (Verses 31–40)

[31] When the words that David had spoken were heard,
they were told to Saul.
And he sent for him.
[32] David said to Saul,
“Let no one lose heart because of him.
Your servant will go and fight with this Philistine.”
[33] Saul said to David,
“You are not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him.
For you are only a young man,
and he has been a warrior since his youth.”
[34] David said to Saul,
“Your servant used to take care of his father’s sheep.
When a lion or a bear came
and took a lamb out of the flock,
[35] I went after it, struck it,
and rescued the lamb from its mouth.
When it rose up against me,
I grabbed it by its beard, struck it, and killed it.
[36] Your servant has struck down both the lion and the bear.
And this uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them,
because he has defied the armies of the living God.”
[37] David said,
“The LORD, who rescued me from the paw of the lion and from the paw of the bear,
will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine.”
Saul said to David,
“Go! The LORD will be with you.”

[38] Saul dressed David in his own clothes.
He put a bronze helmet on his head
and dressed him in a coat of armor.
[39] David strapped his sword over his clothes
and tried to walk, for he had never tried it before.
David said to Saul,
“I can’t walk in these,
for I have never tried them.”
So David took them off again.
[40] He took his staff in his hand,
chose five smooth stones from the stream,
and put them in his shepherd’s bag.
His sling was in his hand.
And he went toward the Philistine.

> **What does this mean?**
> David tells about his work as a shepherd: He defended the sheep against lions and bears. At that time this was really dangerous in Israel.
> But David does not brag about his strength. He says: The LORD rescued me. He will rescue me again. His courage comes from trusting God.
> Saul wants to give David his own armor. But it does not suit David. He cannot move in it. David does not fight with someone else’s weapons, but with what he knows: staff, stones, and sling.
> A sling was a leather strap with a small pouch. You put a stone in it, swung it around in a circle, and let go of one end. A trained slinger could hit very exactly and with great force. It was a real weapon (Judges 20:16).

## “I come to you in the name of the LORD” (Verses 41–47)

[41] The Philistine came closer and closer to David,
and the man who carried the shield went in front of him.
[42] When the Philistine looked around and saw David, he looked down on him.
For he was only a young man, ruddy and good-looking.
[43] The Philistine said to David,
“Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?”
And the Philistine cursed David by his gods.
[44] The Philistine said to David,
“Come here to me,
and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and to the animals of the field.”
[45] Then David said to the Philistine,
“You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin.
But I come to you in the name of the LORD of Armies,
the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied.
[46] Today the LORD will give you into my hand.
I will strike you down and cut off your head.
And today I will give the dead bodies of the Philistine army
to the birds of the sky and to the wild animals of the earth.
Then all the earth will know that there is a God in Israel.
[47] And all this crowd will know
that the LORD does not save with sword and spear.
For the battle is the LORD’s,
and he will give you into our hand.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Goliath feels insulted: A young shepherd with a stick? He threatens David cruelly.
> David’s answer is the heart of this story. It is not about strength against weakness. It is about trusting in weapons against trusting in God: “You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin. But I come to you in the name of the LORD of Armies.”
> David is not fighting for his own fame. He says: The whole world is to know that there is a God. And Israel is to learn: God does not save by weapons.
> “The battle is the LORD’s”: This thought is found often in the Bible. People should do their best, but God gives the victory.

## The stone hits (Verses 48–54)

[48] When the Philistine got up
and came closer to meet David,
David hurried and ran toward the battle line to meet the Philistine.
[49] David put his hand into his bag,
took out a stone,
slung it,
and struck the Philistine on his forehead.
The stone sank into his forehead,
and he fell on his face to the ground.
[50] So David defeated the Philistine with a sling and a stone.
He struck the Philistine and killed him,
even though David had no sword in his hand.
[51] Then David ran,
stood over the Philistine,
took his sword, drew it out of its sheath,
killed him, and cut off his head with it.
When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they fled.
[52] The men of Israel and Judah got up,
shouted the war cry,
and chased the Philistines as far as Gai and to the gates of Ekron.
The wounded Philistines fell along the road to Shaaraim,
as far as Gath and Ekron.
[53] The people of Israel came back from chasing the Philistines
and plundered their camp.
[54] David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem.
But he put his weapons in his tent.

> **What does this mean?**
> A single stone decides the fight. Goliath had heavy armor, but his forehead was uncovered.
> David had no sword. He uses Goliath’s own sword to cut off his head. Later this sword will play a part once more (Chapter 21:9).
> This story has become famous all over the world. To this day people say “David against Goliath” when someone small goes up against an opponent who seems far too strong.
> Here the Bible tells about a war with a lot of violence. The message is not: Violence is good. The message is: Whoever trusts God does not need to be afraid of enemies who seem far too strong.
> At that time Jerusalem still belonged to the Jebusites. David conquers the city only later (2 Samuel 5). Maybe he brought the head there later, or the storyteller is already naming the later capital.
> The Hebrew text of 2 Samuel 21:19 says that a man named Elhanan killed Goliath of Gath. The WEB has “the brother of Goliath the Gittite” there. 1 Chronicles 20:5 says that Elhanan killed a brother of Goliath. Bible teachers explain these passages in different ways.

## “Whose son is this young man?” (Verses 55–58)

[55] When Saul saw David going out against the Philistine,
he said to Abner, the commander of the army,
“Abner, whose son is this young man?”
Abner said,
“As surely as you live, O king, I don’t know.”
[56] The king said,
“Find out whose son this young man is!”
[57] When David came back from killing the Philistine,
Abner took him and brought him before Saul,
with the head of the Philistine in his hand.
[58] Saul said to him,
“Whose son are you, young man?”
David answered,
“I am the son of your servant Jesse of Bethlehem.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is surprising: In Chapter 16 David was already Saul’s harp player and armor bearer. Why does Saul not know him now?
> Some Bible teachers say: Here two different stories about how David came to the court have been put together. The old Greek translation does not have these verses. Others say: Saul is not asking about David himself, but about his family, because he had promised the winner his daughter and freedom from taxes for his family (verse 25). Or Saul was so confused because of his illness that he did not recognize David.
> We leave the text as it is and say honestly: Here there is a tension that cannot be fully resolved.
