# 1 Samuel – Chapter 21
### David in Nob and in Gath

---

## The holy bread (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then David came to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest.
Ahimelech came trembling to meet David and said to him,
“Why are you alone, and no one is with you?”
[2] David said to Ahimelech the priest,
“The king has given me a task and said to me,
‘Let no one know anything about the matter I am sending you on,
or about what I have commanded you.’
I have told the young men to meet me at a certain place.
[3] Now what do you have on hand?
Please give me five loaves of bread,
or whatever is there.”
[4] The priest answered David,
“I have no ordinary bread on hand,
but there is holy bread,
if only the young men have kept away from women.”
[5] David answered the priest,
“Truly, women have been kept away from us as usual these three days.
When I set out, the bodies of the young men were holy,
even though it was only an ordinary journey.
How much more will they be holy today?”
[6] So the priest gave him holy bread.
For there was no bread there except the show bread
that had been taken from before the LORD,
to be replaced with warm bread on the day it was taken away.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles this chapter is shifted by one verse. Verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 16.
> Nob was a town of the priests, near Jerusalem. After Shiloh was destroyed, the priests from Eli’s family lived there. Ahimelech was a great-grandson of Eli.
> Ahimelech trembles. Why does Saul’s famous army commander come all alone? That is strange.
> David lies to the priest. He says he is on a mission for the king. This lie will have terrible results (Chapter 22).
> The “show bread” was 12 loaves of bread that were set out fresh before God in the sanctuary every week. Only the priests were allowed to eat the old loaves (Leviticus 24:5–9).
> The priest makes an exception because David and his men are in need. He only asks whether they are clean. At that time the rule was: Whoever had recently slept with a woman was unclean for holy things for a while (Exodus 19:15). The Hebrew literally says “vessels.” This probably means their bodies or their equipment.
> Later Jesus referred to this story: A person’s need is more important than a religious rule (Mark 2:25–27).

## Doeg the Edomite (Verse 7)

[7] Now one of Saul’s servants was there that day,
held back before the LORD.
His name was Doeg the Edomite,
the chief of Saul’s herdsmen.

> **What does this mean?**
> This short sentence is like a dark cloud. A servant of Saul sees everything that happens here. Later he will betray it (Chapter 22:9).
> “Held back before the LORD”: Maybe he had to stay at the sanctuary for a while because of a vow or a cleansing.
> According to its title, Psalm 52 is a prayer of David about Doeg.

## Goliath’s sword (Verses 8–9)

[8] David said to Ahimelech,
“Don’t you have a spear or a sword here on hand?
For I have not brought my sword or my weapons with me,
because the king’s business was urgent.”
[9] The priest said,
“Look, the sword of Goliath the Philistine,
whom you killed in the valley of Elah,
is here, wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod.
If you want to take it, take it,
for there is no other here except this one.”
David said,
“There is none like it. Give it to me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Goliath’s sword had been kept in the sanctuary as a sign of victory. Now David gets it back.
> It is a sign of remembering: Back then God helped David against the giant. He will help him now too.

## David pretends to be insane (Verses 10–15)

[10] David got up and fled from Saul that day.
He went to Achish, the king of Gath.
[11] The servants of Achish said to him,
“Isn’t this David, the king of the land?
Didn’t they sing about him in their dances:
‘Saul has killed his thousands,
and David his ten thousands’?”
[12] David took these words to heart
and was very afraid of Achish, the king of Gath.
[13] He changed his behavior in front of them
and pretended to be insane while they held him.
He scribbled on the doors of the gate
and let his spit run down his beard.
[14] Then Achish said to his servants,
“Look, you can see the man is insane.
Why have you brought him to me?
[15] Do I lack madmen,
that you have brought this fellow to act like a madman in front of me?
Should this fellow come into my house?”

> **What does this mean?**
> David is so desperate that he flees to the Philistines, of all people. And to Gath of all places, the hometown of Goliath. And he has Goliath’s sword with him!
> The Philistines recognize him right away. They even call him “the king of the land.” They know more than Saul suspects.
> David becomes afraid and saves himself with a trick: He acts crazy. At that time people were afraid to harm someone who was insane.
> Achish makes fun of it: I already have enough madmen around me.
> The great hero who defeated Goliath now has to drool and scribble to survive. That is how low David has fallen. But God does not let him fall.
> According to its title, Psalm 34 is a song of David from this time. It says that the LORD is near to those who have a broken heart (Psalm 34:18; in some Bibles 34:19). In the title of the psalm the king is called “Abimelech.” This was probably a title of the Philistine kings.
