# 1 Samuel – Chapter 13
### Saul does not wait

---

## Saul and Jonathan against the Philistines (Verses 1–7)

[1] Saul was 30 years old when he became king,
and he ruled over Israel for 42 years.
[2] Saul chose 3,000 men of Israel for himself.
Of them, 2,000 were with Saul in Michmash and in the hill country of Bethel,
and 1,000 were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin.
He sent the rest of the people home, each one to his own tent.
[3] Jonathan struck down the Philistine outpost that was in Geba.
And the Philistines heard about it.
Saul had the trumpet blown throughout the whole land and said,
“Let the Hebrews hear!”
[4] All Israel heard:
“Saul has struck down the Philistine outpost.”
And they also heard that Israel had become hated by the Philistines.
The people were called together to follow Saul to Gilgal.

[5] The Philistines gathered to fight against Israel:
30,000 chariots, 6,000 horsemen,
and soldiers as many as the sand on the seashore.
They came up and camped in Michmash, east of Beth Aven.
[6] The men of Israel saw that they were in trouble,
for the people were hard pressed.
So the people hid in caves, in bushes, in rocks, in tombs, and in pits.
[7] Some of the Hebrews even went over the Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead.
But Saul was still in Gilgal,
and all the people followed him trembling.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 is damaged in the Hebrew text. According to the Hebrew text, Saul was one year old when he became king. And he ruled for two years. Numbers are obviously missing. The WEB, our English source text, has put in the numbers 30 and 42, like some other translations. The New Testament says that Saul ruled for 40 years (Acts 13:21). We do not know for sure how old he really was and exactly how long he ruled.
> Jonathan is Saul’s son. He is named here for the first time. He is brave and attacks a Philistine outpost. But Saul has it announced: “Saul has struck down.” He takes the honor for himself.
> Now the Philistines come with a huge army. The number 30,000 chariots is very high. Some old translations have 3,000. That fits better with the 6,000 horsemen.
> The Israelites panic. They hide everywhere or flee over the Jordan.

---

## Saul offers the sacrifice himself (Verses 8–14)

[8] He waited seven days, until the time that Samuel had set.
But Samuel did not come to Gilgal,
and the people were running away from him.
[9] So Saul said,
“Bring the burnt offering and the peace offerings here to me.”
And he offered the burnt offering.
[10] And this is what happened:
Just as he finished offering the burnt offering,
Samuel came.
Saul went out to meet him and greet him.
[11] Samuel said, “What have you done?”
Saul said,
“I saw that the people were running away from me,
and you did not come at the set time,
and the Philistines had gathered at Michmash.
[12] So I thought,
‘Now the Philistines will come down against me at Gilgal,
and I have not yet asked the LORD for his favor.’
So I forced myself and offered the burnt offering.”

[13] Samuel said to Saul,
“You have acted foolishly.
You have not kept the commandment of the LORD your God
that he commanded you.
For now the LORD would have made your kingdom over Israel firm forever.
[14] But now your kingdom will not last.
The LORD has looked for a man after his own heart.
And the LORD has appointed him to be prince over his people,
because you have not kept what the LORD commanded you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Samuel had told Saul: Wait seven days in Gilgal until I come (1 Samuel 10:8). Saul waits. But on the seventh day Samuel is still not there. The soldiers run away. The Philistines come closer. Saul gets nervous and acts on his own.
> We can understand Saul. His situation really was difficult. Why is the judgment so harsh?
> Saul did not wait until the end. He did not trust God’s instruction. He wanted to force God’s help with a sacrifice instead of waiting for God’s word through the prophet. The king was supposed to be under God’s word. But when things get tight, Saul puts himself above it.
> And Saul does not admit that he did something wrong. He blames others: the people, who ran away, and Samuel, who came too late.
> “A man after his own heart”: This means David. He is named only in Chapter 16. In the New Testament this sentence about David is quoted (Acts 13:22). This does not mean that David made no mistakes. It means: He was a man whose heart turned back to God again and again.

## Without weapons against the Philistines (Verses 15–23)

[15] Samuel got up and went from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin.
Saul counted the people who were with him, about 600 men.
[16] Saul and his son Jonathan and the people who were with them
stayed in Geba of Benjamin.
But the Philistines camped in Michmash.
[17] The raiders came out of the Philistine camp in three groups.
One group turned onto the road to Ophrah, to the land of Shual.
[18] Another group turned onto the road to Beth Horon.
And another group turned onto the road to the border
that looks down on the valley of Zeboim, toward the wilderness.

[19] There was no blacksmith in the whole land of Israel.
For the Philistines said,
“Otherwise the Hebrews will make themselves swords or spears.”
[20] So all the Israelites went down to the Philistines,
each one to have his plowshare, his hoe, his ax, and his sickle sharpened.
[21] The price was one payim each for sharpening hoes, plowshares, pitchforks, axes, and goads.
[22] So on the day of battle this is what happened:
Not one sword or spear was found in the hand of any of the people
who were with Saul and Jonathan.
Only Saul and his son Jonathan had them.
[23] The Philistine outpost went out to the pass of Michmash.

> **What does this mean?**
> Of the many soldiers, only 600 are left. The Philistines send out groups that rob the land.
> The Philistines have full control over iron. They do not let the Israelites have any blacksmiths, so that they cannot make weapons. Whoever wants to have a tool sharpened has to go to the Philistines and pay.
> A “payim” was a small weight. Archaeologists have found small stone weights with the word “pim” on them. A pim was about two-thirds of a shekel, around a quarter of an ounce of silver. Before these finds, no one knew what this word meant.
> Verse 21 is hard to understand in Hebrew. “Goads” were sticks with a point that people used to drive cattle.
> Israel’s situation is hopeless: few soldiers, no weapons, a huge army against them. If rescue comes now, it can only come from God.
