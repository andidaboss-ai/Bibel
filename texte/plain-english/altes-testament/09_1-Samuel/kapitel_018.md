# 1 Samuel – Chapter 18
### Jonathan’s friendship and Saul’s jealousy

---

## Jonathan and David make a covenant (Verses 1–5)

[1] When he had finished speaking with Saul,
the soul of Jonathan was bound to the soul of David.
And Jonathan loved him as his own soul.
[2] Saul took him into his service that day
and did not let him go back home to his father’s house anymore.
[3] Then Jonathan and David made a covenant,
because he loved him as his own soul.
[4] Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing
and gave it to David, with his clothes,
even his sword, his bow, and his belt.
[5] David went out wherever Saul sent him,
and he acted wisely.
Saul put him in charge of the soldiers.
This pleased all the people and also Saul’s servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonathan is the king’s son and really the next in line to be king. David is a shepherd from Bethlehem. Still they become best friends. Their friendship is one of the best-known friendships in the literature of the world.
> “He loved him as his own soul” means: as himself. Jonathan is ready to do anything for David.
> The “covenant” is a solemn promise of loyalty, made before God.
> Jonathan gives David his robe and his weapons. This is more than a gift. It is a sign: Jonathan accepts that one day David will be king, not he himself (compare 1 Samuel 23:17). Jonathan is free of envy. That is very rare.
> Some readers today ask whether the love between Jonathan and David was more than friendship. The text speaks of covenant, loyalty, and a deep bond. At that time the word “love” was also used for faithfulness and loyalty between allies. Most Jewish and Christian Bible teachers see here the model of a deep friendship.

---

## The women’s song (Verses 6–9)

[6] When they came home, when David returned from killing the Philistine,
the women came out of all the cities of Israel,
singing and dancing,
to meet King Saul,
with tambourines, with joy, and with musical instruments.
[7] As they played, the women sang to one another:
“Saul has killed his thousands,
and David his ten thousands.”
[8] Saul became very angry,
and this song displeased him.
He said,
“They have given David ten thousands,
and they have only given me thousands.
What more can he have but the kingdom?”
[9] From that day on Saul kept a suspicious eye on David.

> **What does this mean?**
> The women celebrate the victory with a song. In the poetry of the Bible people often say the same thing twice, and the second half goes further than the first. The women probably did not want to insult Saul. But Saul only hears: David is better than me.
> Now Saul’s jealousy begins. It will torment him for the rest of his life. Envy can eat a person up from the inside.
> “What more can he have but the kingdom?” Saul suspects that David will one day be king. And that is exactly God’s plan.

## Saul throws the spear (Verses 10–16)

[10] The next day an evil spirit from God came on Saul with power,
and he prophesied in the middle of the house.
David was playing with his hand, as he did every day.
Saul had his spear in his hand.
[11] And Saul threw the spear,
for he said, “I will pin David to the wall!”
But David got out of his way twice.
[12] Saul was afraid of David,
because the LORD was with him
but had left Saul.
[13] So Saul sent him away from him
and made him commander over a thousand.
And he went out and came in before the people.
[14] David acted wisely in all his ways,
and the LORD was with him.
[15] When Saul saw that he acted very wisely,
he was afraid of him.
[16] But all Israel and Judah loved David,
because he went out and came in before them.

> **What does this mean?**
> “He prophesied”: It is the same word as with the excited prophets in Chapter 10. But here it is an eerie, wild state.
> While David makes music to calm Saul, Saul tries to kill him. The man who helps him becomes his enemy.
> Saul is afraid of David because God is with him. Saul senses: God has left him and is with David.
> Saul sends David to war, maybe hoping that he will be killed there. But the opposite happens: David becomes more and more popular.
> “He went out and came in” means: He led the soldiers into battle and back again. He was a leader whom people saw and knew.

## Merab and Michal (Verses 17–27)

[17] Saul said to David,
“Here is my older daughter Merab.
I will give her to you as your wife.
Only be brave for me and fight the LORD’s battles.”
For Saul said to himself,
“I will not raise my hand against him,
but let the hand of the Philistines be against him.”
[18] David said to Saul,
“Who am I, and what is my life
or my father’s family in Israel,
that I should become the king’s son-in-law?”
[19] But when the time came for Merab, Saul’s daughter, to be given to David,
she was given as wife to Adriel the Meholathite.

[20] Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David.
They told Saul, and this pleased him.
[21] Saul said,
“I will give her to him,
so that she may be a trap for him
and the hand of the Philistines may be against him.”
So Saul said to David a second time,
“Today you shall become my son-in-law.”
[22] Saul commanded his servants,
“Talk with David secretly and say,
‘Look, the king is pleased with you,
and all his servants love you.
So now become the king’s son-in-law.’”
[23] Saul’s servants spoke these words in David’s hearing.
David said,
“Does it seem to you a small thing to become the king’s son-in-law,
since I am a poor man and not well known?”
[24] Saul’s servants told him,
“This is what David said.”
[25] Saul said,
“Tell David this:
‘The king wants no bride price
except 100 foreskins of Philistines,
to take revenge on the king’s enemies.’”
For Saul planned to have David fall by the hand of the Philistines.
[26] When his servants told David these words,
David was glad to become the king’s son-in-law.
Before the time was up,
[27] David got up and went, he and his men,
and killed 200 Philistine men.
David brought their foreskins,
and they gave the full number to the king,
so that he could become the king’s son-in-law.
Then Saul gave him his daughter Michal as wife.

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul had promised to give his daughter to the man who defeated Goliath (1 Samuel 17:25). But he does not keep his promise. He adds new conditions, hoping that David will die in battle.
> Merab is given to another man. Once again women are treated like objects that men use for politics.
> It is very rare that the Bible says clearly that a woman loves a man. With Michal it says so twice (verses 20 and 28). But her father uses her love as a trap.
> David is humble: I am poor, I cannot pay a bride price for a king’s daughter. At that time the bridegroom had to pay a bride price to the bride’s family.
> Saul asks for a cruel bride price: foreskins of killed Philistines. The foreskin is the skin that is removed at circumcision. This was proof that someone had really killed Philistines, because the Philistines were not circumcised. It is a custom from a brutal time of war. The Bible tells it, but it does not praise it.
> David even brings twice as many. The old Greek translation has 100 in verse 27. In 2 Samuel 3:14 David also speaks of 100.

## Saul becomes David’s enemy (Verses 28–30)

[28] Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David.
And Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved him.
[29] Then Saul was even more afraid of David.
And Saul was David’s enemy from then on.
[30] Then the princes of the Philistines went out to battle.
And every time they went out,
David acted more wisely than all of Saul’s servants,
so that his name was highly respected.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything Saul plans against David only makes David stronger. The spear misses him. The war makes him famous. The wedding makes him part of the king’s family. Even Saul’s own children, Jonathan and Michal, love David.
> Saul realizes: God is with David. But instead of being glad about it or turning back to God, he becomes David’s enemy. In this way he is really fighting against God himself.
> This chapter shows the difference between two people: Jonathan has every reason to be jealous, but he loves David. Saul has every reason to be thankful, but he hates David.
