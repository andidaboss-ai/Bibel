# 1 Samuel – Chapter 27
### David among the Philistines

---

## David flees to Gath (Verses 1–4)

[1] David said in his heart,
“One day I will surely die by the hand of Saul.
There is nothing better for me than to escape into the land of the Philistines.
Then Saul will give up looking for me anywhere in the territory of Israel.
So I will escape from his hand.”
[2] So David got up and went over,
he and the 600 men who were with him,
to Achish the son of Maoch, the king of Gath.
[3] David lived with Achish at Gath,
he and his men, each with his family,
and David with his two wives,
Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail of Carmel, Nabal’s widow.
[4] Saul was told that David had fled to Gath.
So he stopped looking for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> David loses heart. He has spared Saul twice, but Saul keeps hunting him. He thinks: Sooner or later he will catch me.
> It is striking: This time David does not ask God before he acts. He “said in his heart,” which means he decides alone. And he goes to Israel’s enemies, of all people.
> The first time, David came to Gath alone and had to pretend to be insane (Chapter 21). Now he comes with 600 warriors. For Achish he is a valuable ally against Saul.
> David’s plan works: Saul stops hunting him.

---

## The town of Ziklag (Verses 5–7)

[5] David said to Achish,
“If I have found favor in your eyes,
let them give me a place in one of the towns in the country,
so that I can live there.
For why should your servant live with you in the royal city?”
[6] So that day Achish gave him Ziklag.
That is why Ziklag belongs to the kings of Judah to this day.
[7] The time that David lived in the land of the Philistines
was a year and four months.

> **What does this mean?**
> David does not want to live in the capital, under the eyes of the king. In a town of his own he can act more freely.
> Ziklag was in the south, on the border between the land of the Philistines and Judah.
> “To this day”: When the book was written, Ziklag belonged to the kingdom of Judah. The town was a gift from the Philistines to David.

---

## David’s double game (Verses 8–12)

[8] David and his men went up
and raided the Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites.
For these peoples had lived in the land from long ago,
toward Shur and as far as the land of Egypt.
[9] David struck the land
and left no man or woman alive.
He took the sheep, the cattle, the donkeys, the camels, and the clothing.
Then he came back to Achish.
[10] Achish asked,
“Whom did you raid today?”
David said,
“The south of Judah,
the south of the Jerahmeelites,
and the south of the Kenites.”
[11] David left neither man nor woman alive to bring to Gath.
For he said,
“Otherwise they will tell about us and say,
‘This is what David did.
And this has been his way the whole time
he has lived in the land of the Philistines.’”
[12] Achish believed David and said,
“He has made his people Israel hate him completely.
So he will be my servant forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This passage shows a dark side of David. He raids peoples in the south who were enemies of Israel, like the Amalekites. And he leaves no one alive, not even women.
> Then he lies to Achish: He says he raided places in Judah. And so that no one can tell the truth, he kills all the witnesses.
> The Bible tells this without praise. It does not hide that even David, the man after God’s own heart, could act cruelly and dishonestly. David is a great hero of faith, but not a person without faults.
> We read this text as an honest report about a violent time, not as an example. No one may appeal to it to justify violence or lies.
> Achish falls for the lie. He thinks: Now Israel hates him, he can never go back. He belongs to me. But in his heart David stays with his people.
