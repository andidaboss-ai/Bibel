# 1 Samuel – Chapter 7
### The stone of help

---

## The ark in Kiriath Jearim (Verses 1–2)

[1] The men of Kiriath Jearim came and took the ark of the LORD.
They brought it into the house of Abinadab on the hill.
And they dedicated his son Eleazar to guard the ark of the LORD.
[2] From the day the ark stayed in Kiriath Jearim, a long time went by.
It was 20 years.
And the whole house of Israel mourned and longed for the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The ark does not go back to Shiloh. Shiloh had probably been destroyed by the Philistines (Jeremiah 7:12). The ark stays in a private house for many years. Only King David later brings it to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6).
> “Longed for the LORD” means: The people miss God. They long for him. This is the beginning of turning back.

---

## Samuel calls the people to turn back (Verses 3–6)

[3] Samuel spoke to the whole house of Israel:
“If you are returning to the LORD with all your heart,
then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you.
Turn your hearts to the LORD and serve him only.
Then he will save you from the hand of the Philistines.”
[4] So the people of Israel put away the Baals and the Ashtaroth
and served the LORD only.
[5] Samuel said,
“Gather all Israel at Mizpah.
Then I will pray to the LORD for you.”
[6] They gathered at Mizpah.
They drew water and poured it out in front of the LORD.
They fasted on that day and said there,
“We have sinned against the LORD.”
And Samuel judged the people of Israel at Mizpah.

> **What does this mean?**
> For 20 years we hear nothing about Samuel. Now he steps forward and says clearly: Real turning back does not only mean mourning. Real turning back means: putting away everything that stands between you and God.
> Baal was a god of rain and fertility. Ashtoreth was a goddess of love and war. The plural forms “Baals” and “Ashtaroth” mean their many images and places of worship.
> Pouring out water is a sign that does not appear like this anywhere else in the Bible. Maybe it means: We pour out our hearts before God, as Hannah did (1 Samuel 1:15). Or: Our life is before you like water poured out.
> The people say honestly: “We have sinned.” That is the most important sentence when people turn back.

## God thunders against the Philistines (Verses 7–11)

[7] When the Philistines heard that the people of Israel had gathered at Mizpah,
the lords of the Philistines went up against Israel.
When the people of Israel heard it, they were afraid of the Philistines.
[8] The people of Israel said to Samuel,
“Don’t stop crying out to the LORD our God for us,
so that he will save us from the hand of the Philistines.”
[9] Samuel took a young lamb that was still nursing
and offered it to the LORD as a whole burnt offering.
Samuel cried out to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD answered him.
[10] While Samuel was offering the burnt offering,
the Philistines came near to fight against Israel.
But on that day the LORD thundered with a mighty thunder against the Philistines
and threw them into confusion.
And they were beaten in front of Israel.
[11] The men of Israel went out from Mizpah,
chased the Philistines,
and struck them down as far as below Beth Kar.

> **What does this mean?**
> This time everything is different from Chapter 4. Then Israel brought the ark like a lucky charm. Now it prays. Then it relied on a thing. Now it relies on God.
> The people are afraid, but they do not run away. They ask Samuel to pray for them.
> God answers with thunder. In her song Hannah had said: “He will thunder against them in the sky” (1 Samuel 2:10). This comes true here.
> Today no one knows exactly where Beth Kar was.

## Ebenezer: Until now the LORD has helped (Verses 12–14)

[12] Then Samuel took a stone
and set it up between Mizpah and Shen.
He named it Ebenezer and said,
“Until now the LORD has helped us.”
[13] So the Philistines were brought down.
They did not come into the territory of Israel anymore.
The hand of the LORD was against the Philistines as long as Samuel lived.
[14] The cities that the Philistines had taken from Israel
were given back to Israel, from Ekron to Gath.
Israel freed its territory from the hand of the Philistines.
And there was peace between Israel and the Amorites.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Ebenezer” means “stone of help.” It was at a place with this very name that Israel had lost the ark in Chapter 4. Now the name gets a new meaning: God has helped.
> In Hebrew, “until now” can be understood in two ways: Up to this place God has helped us. And: Up to this time God has helped us. Both are true. The stone is meant to remind them: God has not left us until today. So we can trust him for tomorrow too.
> To this day, many Christians say “Ebenezer” or “Until now the LORD has helped us” when they look back on their lives. A well-known English hymn says: “Here I raise my Ebenezer.”
> “Amorites” here stands for the peoples of Canaan who still lived in the land.

## Samuel as judge (Verses 15–17)

[15] Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life.
[16] Year after year he went around in a circle to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah.
And in all these places he judged cases for Israel.
[17] Then he went back to Ramah, because his home was there.
And there he judged cases for Israel.
And he built an altar to the LORD there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samuel is like a judge who travels through the land. He comes to the people, so that they do not have to go far to get justice.
> Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah were all in the territory of the tribes of Benjamin and Ephraim, not far from each other. Ramah was his home town.
> Samuel is the last and maybe the best of the judges. He leads the people not mainly with the sword, but with prayer and with God’s word.
