# 1 Samuel – Chapter 29
### The Philistines send David back

---

## The lords do not trust David (Verses 1–5)

[1] The Philistines gathered all their armies at Aphek.
And the Israelites camped by the spring that is in Jezreel.
[2] The lords of the Philistines marched past by hundreds and by thousands.
And David and his men marched past at the back with Achish.
[3] Then the commanders of the Philistines said,
“What are these Hebrews doing here?”
Achish said to the commanders of the Philistines,
“Isn’t this David, the servant of Saul the king of Israel?
He has been with me for days, even years now.
I have found no fault in him
from the day he came over to me until today.”
[4] But the commanders of the Philistines were angry with him.
The commanders of the Philistines said to him,
“Send the man back.
Let him go back to the place you gave him.
Don’t let him go down with us into battle,
so that he does not turn against us in the battle.
For how could this fellow win back the favor of his lord any better
than with the heads of these men?
[5] Isn’t this David, about whom they sang in their dances:
‘Saul has killed his thousands,
and David his ten thousands’?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the storyteller jumps back a little: The Philistines first gather at Aphek, before they march north to Shunem (1 Samuel 28:4).
> The other lords of the Philistines are more suspicious than Achish. They say: In battle David could change sides and attack us from behind. In this way he could make peace with Saul again.
> Once again the famous song is quoted. David’s fame follows him even among his enemies.

---

## Achish sends David away (Verses 6–11)

[6] Then Achish called David and said to him,
“As the LORD lives:
You have been honest,
and I am pleased with your going out and coming in with me in the army.
For I have found no evil in you
from the day you came to me until today.
But the lords do not like you.
[7] So now turn back and go in peace,
so that you do nothing that displeases the lords of the Philistines.”
[8] David said to Achish,
“But what have I done?
What have you found in your servant from the time I came to you until today,
that I may not go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?”
[9] Achish answered David,
“I know that you are good in my eyes, like an angel of God.
But the commanders of the Philistines have said,
‘He shall not go up with us into battle.’
[10] So get up early in the morning, with the servants of your lord who came with you.
As soon as you are up early in the morning and it is light, leave.”
[11] So David got up early, he and his men,
to leave in the morning and go back to the land of the Philistines.
And the Philistines went up to Jezreel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Achish, a Philistine, swears by the LORD, the God of Israel. Maybe he wants to convince David especially well in this way.
> David acts as if he were disappointed. “Against the enemies of my lord the king” again has two meanings: Does he mean Achish as his lord, or Saul? We do not find out what David really would have done.
> In this way God has freed David from an impossible situation. He did not have to fight against his own people. And he has no part in it when Saul and Jonathan die.
