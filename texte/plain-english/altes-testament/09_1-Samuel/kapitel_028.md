# 1 Samuel – Chapter 28
### Saul and the medium at Endor

---

## David in a difficult position (Verses 1–2)

[1] In those days the Philistines gathered their armies for war
to fight against Israel.
Achish said to David,
“You must know for sure that you will go out with me in the army,
you and your men.”
[2] David said to Achish,
“Then you will know what your servant can do.”
Achish said to David,
“Then I will make you my bodyguard forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now David’s double game comes back on him. Achish demands that David fight with the Philistines against Israel, against his own people.
> David’s answer is meant to have two meanings: “You will know what your servant can do.” Achish understands: He will fight well for me. But David leaves open on which side.
> Chapter 29 tells what happens next.

---

## God no longer answers Saul (Verses 3–7)

[3] Samuel had died,
and all Israel had mourned for him and buried him in Ramah, his own town.
And Saul had driven out of the land the people who talked with spirits of the dead and the fortune-tellers.
[4] The Philistines gathered, came, and camped at Shunem.
And Saul gathered all Israel, and they camped on Gilboa.
[5] When Saul saw the army of the Philistines, he was afraid,
and his heart trembled greatly.
[6] Saul asked the LORD.
But the LORD did not answer him,
not by dreams, not by Urim, and not by prophets.
[7] Then Saul said to his servants,
“Find me a woman who has a familiar spirit.
Then I will go to her and ask her.”
His servants said to him,
“Look, there is a woman at Endor who has a familiar spirit.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The storyteller reminds us: Samuel is dead. And Saul himself had driven out of the land the people who called up the dead, as God’s law required (Leviticus 19:31; Deuteronomy 18:10–12).
> The Philistines camp at Shunem, in the fertile Jezreel Valley in the north. Saul camps across from them on the mountains of Gilboa.
> Saul becomes afraid, very afraid. He asks God, but God is silent. No dreams, no answer through the holy lots (Urim), no prophets. Saul had had the priests killed (Chapter 22) and had not listened to the prophets.
> In his despair Saul does exactly what he himself had forbidden. He looks for a woman who calls up the dead. This shows how far he has fallen.
> “A woman who has a familiar spirit” means: a woman who claims that she can talk with the dead. Such a woman is called a medium.

---

## Saul goes to Endor in disguise (Verses 8–14)

[8] Saul disguised himself and put on other clothes.
He went, he and two men with him,
and they came to the woman by night.
He said,
“Please ask the spirit for me
and bring up for me the one I name to you.”
[9] The woman said to him,
“Look, you know what Saul has done,
how he has cut off from the land the people who talk with spirits and the fortune-tellers.
Why are you setting a trap for my life, to get me killed?”
[10] Saul swore to her by the LORD and said,
“As the LORD lives,
no punishment will come to you for this.”
[11] Then the woman said,
“Whom shall I bring up for you?”
He said,
“Bring up Samuel for me.”
[12] When the woman saw Samuel, she screamed loudly.
And the woman said to Saul,
“Why have you deceived me?
You are Saul!”
[13] The king said to her,
“Don’t be afraid!
What do you see?”
The woman said to Saul,
“I see a god coming up out of the earth.”
[14] He said to her,
“What does he look like?”
She said,
“An old man is coming up.
He is wrapped in a robe.”
Then Saul knew that it was Samuel.
He bowed with his face to the ground and showed him respect.

> **What does this mean?**
> Endor was north of the Philistine camp. In the night Saul has to go close past the enemy camp. He disguises himself so that no one will recognize him.
> It is bitter: Saul swears by the LORD in order to do something the LORD has forbidden.
> The woman screams when Samuel appears. Maybe she herself did not expect that someone would really come. And suddenly she recognizes Saul.
> “A god” here means: a ghostly being, more than human.
> The robe was Samuel’s mark. Let us remember: Saul once tore Samuel’s robe (1 Samuel 15:27).
> Was it really Samuel? Jews and Christians have thought about this a lot. Many say: Yes, in this one case God himself sent Samuel to tell Saul his judgment. That is why the woman is so frightened. Others say: It was a deception. The Bible tells it as if it was Samuel. The book of Sirach says that Samuel still prophesied even after his death (Sirach 46:20).
> What is clear: The Bible forbids calling up the dead (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). This story is not a permission, but shows where despair without God leads. But the Bible also gives no one the right to accuse or hunt down people as “witches.”

## Samuel’s last message (Verses 15–19)

[15] Samuel said to Saul,
“Why have you disturbed me by bringing me up?”
Saul answered,
“I am in great distress.
For the Philistines are making war against me,
and God has left me
and no longer answers me, either by prophets or by dreams.
That is why I have called you,
so that you can tell me what I should do.”
[16] Samuel said,
“Why then do you ask me,
since the LORD has left you and has become your enemy?
[17] The LORD has done to you just as he said through me.
The LORD has torn the kingdom out of your hand
and given it to your neighbor, David.
[18] Because you did not obey the voice of the LORD
and did not carry out his fierce anger against Amalek,
the LORD has done this to you today.
[19] And the LORD will also give Israel with you into the hand of the Philistines.
Tomorrow you and your sons will be with me.
The LORD will also give the army of Israel into the hand of the Philistines.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul hopes for advice. But Samuel says nothing new. He only repeats what he had already said while he was alive (Chapter 15). Whoever does not listen to God while he is alive will not find a different answer among the dead either.
> For the first time it is said clearly who the “neighbor” is to whom God gives the kingdom: David.
> “Tomorrow you and your sons will be with me” means: Tomorrow you will be dead, you and your sons. At that time people imagined that all the dead are in one place, the place of the dead.
> It is a shattering message. On the evening before the battle Saul learns that he will die.

## The woman takes care of Saul (Verses 20–25)

[20] Then Saul immediately fell full length on the ground.
He was terrified because of Samuel’s words.
And he had no strength left,
because he had not eaten any bread all day and all night.
[21] The woman came to Saul and saw that he was very frightened.
She said to him,
“Look, your servant has listened to your voice.
I have risked my life
and have listened to the words you spoke to me.
[22] So now please listen to the voice of your servant too.
Let me set a piece of bread in front of you.
Eat, so that you have strength when you go on your way.”
[23] But he refused and said,
“I will not eat.”
But his servants and the woman too urged him.
So he listened to their voice.
He got up from the ground and sat on the bed.
[24] The woman had a fattened calf in the house.
She hurried and killed it.
She took flour, kneaded it, and baked bread without yeast from it.
[25] She brought it to Saul and his servants, and they ate.
Then they got up and went away that same night.

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul collapses. The great king lies on the ground, weak from hunger and fear.
> The woman, whom he really should have punished, feels sorry for him. She kills her only fattened calf, a great sacrifice for a poor woman. She gives him one last meal.
> This is a surprisingly human moment. Of all people, this woman shows Saul kindness on his last night.
> Then they go out into the night. The night fits Saul’s situation: He is going into the darkness, toward his death.
