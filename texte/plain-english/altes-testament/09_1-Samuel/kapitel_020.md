# 1 Samuel – Chapter 20
### Jonathan and the arrows

---

## “Only a step between me and death” (Verses 1–11)

[1] David fled from Naioth in Ramah.
He came and said to Jonathan,
“What have I done?
What is my guilt?
What is my sin against your father, that he is trying to kill me?”
[2] He said to him,
“Far from it! You will not die.
Look, my father does nothing, big or small,
without telling me.
Why would my father hide this from me?
It is not so.”
[3] David also swore and said,
“Your father knows very well that I have found favor in your eyes.
And he says to himself,
‘Don’t let Jonathan know this, or he will be upset.’
But truly, as the LORD lives and as you live,
there is only a step between me and death.”
[4] Then Jonathan said to David,
“Whatever you want, I will do it for you.”
[5] David said to Jonathan,
“Look, tomorrow is the new moon.
I am supposed to sit and eat with the king.
But let me go.
Then I will hide in the field until the evening of the third day.
[6] If your father misses me,
then say,
‘David begged me to let him hurry to Bethlehem, his town.
For the yearly sacrifice for the whole family is there.’
[7] If he says, ‘Good,’
then your servant is safe.
But if he gets angry,
then you will know that he has decided to do evil.
[8] So show kindness to your servant.
For you have brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD with you.
But if I am guilty, then kill me yourself.
Why should you bring me to your father?”
[9] Jonathan said,
“Far be it from you!
If I knew for sure that my father had decided to harm you,
wouldn’t I tell you?”
[10] Then David said to Jonathan,
“Who will tell me if your father gives you a harsh answer?”
[11] Jonathan said to David,
“Come! Let’s go out into the field.”
And the two of them went out into the field.

> **What does this mean?**
> David does not understand why Saul hates him. He asks: What have I done wrong? Many people who are persecuted for no reason ask this question.
> Jonathan does not want to believe that his father is really so evil. It is hard to think such a thing about your own father.
> “Only a step between me and death”: That is how David feels. Any day could be the end.
> At the “new moon,” the beginning of each month, there was a festival with a special meal (Numbers 10:10; 28:11). As the king’s son-in-law, David was supposed to be there.
> David suggests a test: How will Saul react when David is missing?
> “Covenant of the LORD”: The friendship between David and Jonathan is a covenant before God (1 Samuel 18:3). That is a holy promise.

## Jonathan’s covenant with David’s house (Verses 12–17)

[12] Jonathan said to David,
“By the LORD, the God of Israel:
When I have found out what my father thinks, about this time tomorrow or the day after,
if things look good for David,
won’t I send word to you and tell you?
[13] May the LORD do so to Jonathan and even more,
if my father wants to harm you
and I don’t tell you and send you away,
so that you can go in peace.
May the LORD be with you as he has been with my father.
[14] And not only while I am still alive
shall you show me the kindness of the LORD, so that I do not die.
[15] But you shall also never take your kindness away from my house,
not even when the LORD has cut off every one of David’s enemies
from the face of the earth.”
[16] So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David and said,
“The LORD will hold David’s enemies responsible.”
[17] Jonathan had David swear again,
because of his love for him.
For he loved him as he loved his own soul.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonathan says: “May the LORD be with you as he has been with my father.” Jonathan knows that David will be the next king, not he himself. And he wishes it for him.
> At that time new kings often killed the whole family of the old king, so that no one could fight them for the throne. That is why Jonathan asks: When you are king, spare my family.
> David kept this promise. Many years later he takes Jonathan’s lame son Mephibosheth to eat at his table (2 Samuel 9).
> Once again we find the word “kindness” (chesed), which we know from the book of Ruth. It is faithfulness that goes beyond this life.

## The sign with the arrows (Verses 18–23)

[18] Then Jonathan said to him,
“Tomorrow is the new moon.
You will be missed, because your seat will be empty.
[19] When you have waited three days,
go down quickly to the place where you hid when this matter began,
and stay by the stone Ezel.
[20] I will shoot three arrows to the side of it,
as if I were shooting at a target.
[21] And look, then I will send the boy and say,
‘Go, find the arrows!’
If I say to the boy,
‘Look, the arrows are on this side of you, take them,’
then come.
For then you are safe, and there is no danger, as the LORD lives.
[22] But if I say to the boy,
‘Look, the arrows are beyond you,’
then go your way.
For the LORD has sent you away.
[23] And as for the matter we have talked about, you and I:
Look, the LORD is between you and me forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonathan thinks up a secret sign. No one is to notice that he is warning David. A boy who fetches arrows does not attract attention at all.
> “The LORD is between you and me” means: God is the witness of our promise. He watches over it.

## Saul’s anger at the feast (Verses 24–34)

[24] So David hid in the field.
When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat.
[25] The king sat on his seat as usual,
on the seat by the wall.
Jonathan stood up, and Abner sat at Saul’s side.
But David’s place was empty.
[26] Still, Saul said nothing that day.
For he thought,
“Something has happened to him. He is not clean.
Surely he is not clean.”
[27] On the day after the new moon, the second day, David’s place was empty again.
Saul said to his son Jonathan,
“Why hasn’t the son of Jesse come to eat, either yesterday or today?”
[28] Jonathan answered Saul,
“David begged me for permission to go to Bethlehem.
[29] He said,
‘Please let me go,
for our family has a sacrifice in the town.
My brother has ordered me to be there.
Now, if I have found favor in your eyes,
please let me go away and see my brothers.’
That is why he has not come to the king’s table.”

[30] Then Saul became very angry with Jonathan and said to him,
“You son of a twisted, rebellious woman!
Don’t I know that you have chosen the son of Jesse,
to your own shame and to the shame of your mother’s nakedness?
[31] For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth,
you will not be secure, neither you nor your kingdom.
So now send for him and bring him to me,
for he must surely die!”
[32] Jonathan answered his father Saul and said to him,
“Why should he be put to death?
What has he done?”
[33] Then Saul threw his spear at him to strike him.
From this Jonathan knew that his father was determined to kill David.
[34] So Jonathan got up from the table in fierce anger
and ate no food on the second day of the month.
For he was grieved for David,
because his father had treated him so shamefully.

> **What does this mean?**
> On the first day Saul thinks: Maybe David is unclean, for example because he touched something dead. Then a person was not allowed to take part in the holy meal (Leviticus 7:20–21).
> On the second day Saul explodes. He insults Jonathan and his own wife. “To the shame of your mother’s nakedness” is a crude insult.
> Saul gives away his real fear: If David lives, you will not become king. For Saul it is about power for his family. For Jonathan it is about justice and faithfulness.
> Jonathan asks: “What has he done?” That is the right question. And Saul answers with the spear. He even attacks his own son.
> Jonathan is angry and sad, not because of himself, but because of David.

## The farewell (Verses 35–42)

[35] In the morning Jonathan went out into the field
at the time he had agreed on with David,
and a little boy was with him.
[36] He said to his boy,
“Run, find the arrows that I shoot.”
As the boy ran, he shot an arrow beyond him.
[37] When the boy came to the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot,
Jonathan called after the boy,
“Isn’t the arrow beyond you?”
[38] Jonathan called after the boy,
“Quick! Hurry! Don’t stop!”
Jonathan’s boy picked up the arrows and came back to his master.
[39] But the boy knew nothing.
Only Jonathan and David knew about the matter.
[40] Jonathan gave his weapons to his boy
and said to him,
“Go, carry them to the town.”
[41] As soon as the boy was gone,
David got up from the south side.
He fell on his face to the ground
and bowed three times.
They kissed each other and wept together,
and David wept the most.
[42] Jonathan said to David,
“Go in peace.
For we have both sworn in the name of the LORD and said,
‘The LORD is between me and you,
and between my descendants and your descendants forever.’”
He got up and left.
And Jonathan went into the town.

> **What does this mean?**
> The arrow flies beyond the boy. That means: Go away, you are in danger. Jonathan calls to the boy, “Quick! Hurry!” but really he is speaking to David.
> Really Jonathan would not have needed the sign. But the two want to see each other at least one more time.
> They kiss and weep. At that time it was normal for men to show their friendship and their pain at saying goodbye in this way.
> This is one of the saddest farewells in the Bible. The two see each other only one more time (1 Samuel 23:16–18).
> In the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles the numbering is different here: The last sentence (“He got up and left. And Jonathan went into the town.”) is Chapter 21, verse 1 there.
