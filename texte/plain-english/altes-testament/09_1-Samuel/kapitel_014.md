# 1 Samuel – Chapter 14
### Jonathan’s brave deed and Saul’s oath

---

## Jonathan and his armor bearer (Verses 1–15)

[1] One day Jonathan the son of Saul said to the young man who carried his armor,
“Come! Let’s go over to the Philistine outpost on the other side.”
But he did not tell his father.
[2] Saul was staying at the edge of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree that is in Migron.
And the people who were with him were about 600 men.
[3] Ahijah was also there, the son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod,
the son of Phinehas, the son of Eli, the priest of the LORD in Shiloh.
He was wearing an ephod.
The people did not know that Jonathan had gone.
[4] Between the passes by which Jonathan wanted to go over to the Philistine outpost,
there was a rocky cliff on one side and a rocky cliff on the other side.
The name of the one was Bozez, and the name of the other was Seneh.
[5] One cliff rose up on the north, facing Michmash,
and the other on the south, facing Geba.

[6] Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor,
“Come! Let’s go over to the outpost of these uncircumcised men.
Maybe the LORD will act for us.
For nothing can stop the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.”
[7] His armor bearer said to him,
“Do all that is in your heart.
Go, and look, I am with you, whatever you decide.”
[8] Then Jonathan said,
“Look, we will cross over to the men and let them see us.
[9] If they say to us,
‘Wait until we come to you!’
then we will stay where we are and not go up to them.
[10] But if they say,
‘Come up to us!’
then we will go up.
For then the LORD has given them into our hand.
This will be the sign for us.”

[11] The two of them let the Philistine outpost see them.
The Philistines said,
“Look, the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they have been hiding!”
[12] The men of the outpost called to Jonathan and his armor bearer,
“Come up to us, and we will show you something!”
Jonathan said to his armor bearer,
“Climb up after me,
for the LORD has given them into the hand of Israel.”
[13] Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, and his armor bearer after him.
The Philistines fell in front of Jonathan,
and his armor bearer killed them behind him.
[14] In that first attack, Jonathan and his armor bearer
killed about twenty men,
in an area of about half a furrow’s length in a field.
[15] Then there was terror in the camp, in the field, and among all the people.
The outpost and the raiders also trembled.
And the earth shook.
So there was a very great terror.

> **What does this mean?**
> While Saul sits under a tree doing nothing, his son Jonathan acts. He trusts in God. His sentence is famous: “Nothing can stop the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.” For God, numbers do not matter.
> Ahijah is a great-grandson of Eli. So Eli’s family is still serving as priests. Here the “ephod” is an object that people used to ask God for answers.
> Jonathan asks God for a sign. He does not go carelessly, but wants to know whether God is with him.
> The rocks had names: “Bozez” may mean “the shining one,” “Seneh” means “the thorny one.” You can still see this narrow pass in the land today.
> The Philistines make fun of the Israelites: They come out of their holes like mice. But then everything happens very quickly. God sends an earthquake, and panic breaks out.
> In Hebrew, “a very great terror” literally says: “a terror of God.” This means: a terror that God sent, or also: an enormous terror.

## The Philistines flee (Verses 16–23)

[16] Saul’s watchmen in Gibeah of Benjamin looked over.
And they saw the crowd melting away and running here and there.
[17] Then Saul said to the people who were with him,
“Count now, and see who is missing from us.”
When they had counted, Jonathan and his armor bearer were not there.
[18] Saul said to Ahijah,
“Bring the ark of God here.”
For the ark of God was with the people of Israel at that time.
[19] While Saul was still talking to the priest,
the noise in the Philistine camp kept getting louder.
So Saul said to the priest,
“Withdraw your hand!”
[20] Saul and all the people who were with him gathered and went into the battle.
And they saw that every man was striking the other with his sword,
in very great confusion.
[21] The Hebrews who had been with the Philistines before
and had gone up with them into the camp from all around
also changed sides and joined the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan.
[22] All the men of Israel who had hidden in the hill country of Ephraim
also heard that the Philistines were fleeing.
And they too chased after them in the battle.
[23] So the LORD saved Israel that day.
And the battle moved on beyond Beth Aven.

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul wants to ask God. But when the situation becomes urgent, he says to the priest: “Withdraw your hand!” That means: Stop, I have no more time. Once again Saul has no patience to wait for God’s answer.
> In verse 18 the old Greek translation has “ephod” instead of “ark.” That fits better, because people asked God with the ephod, and the ark was in Kiriath Jearim (Chapter 7:1–2).
> The Philistines panic so much that they kill each other. This is like the story of Gideon (Judges 7:22).
> “So the LORD saved Israel”: Not Saul, not even Jonathan, but God. That is the main point.

## Saul’s rash oath (Verses 24–30)

[24] The men of Israel were very worn out that day.
For Saul had made the people take an oath and had said,
“Cursed is the man who eats any food before evening,
until I am avenged of my enemies.”
So none of the people ate anything.
[25] All the people came into the forest.
There was honey on the ground.
[26] When the people came into the forest,
the honey was dripping.
But no one put his hand to his mouth,
because the people were afraid of the oath.
[27] But Jonathan had not heard his father make the people take the oath.
So he stretched out the end of the stick that was in his hand,
dipped it into the honeycomb,
and put his hand to his mouth.
And his eyes brightened.
[28] Then one of the people said,
“Your father strictly made the people take an oath and said,
‘Cursed is the man who eats food today.’”
And the people were worn out.
[29] Then Jonathan said,
“My father has brought trouble on the land.
Look how my eyes have brightened
because I tasted a little of this honey.
[30] How much better it would have been if the people had eaten freely today from the plunder of their enemies
that they found!
For now the defeat of the Philistines has not been great.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul wants to look especially devoted to God and forbids all soldiers to eat. But this is not sensible. Whoever has to fight needs strength. And he says: “until I am avenged of my enemies.” It is about himself.
> Jonathan knew nothing about the oath. He eats a little honey and gets new strength. “His eyes brightened” means: He is awake and fresh again.
> Jonathan says openly: My father has harmed the people. He is right.
> Once again there is a rash oath, as with Jephthah (Judges 11) and with the oath against Benjamin (Judges 21). The Bible warns again and again: Think carefully before you swear something.

## The people eat meat with the blood (Verses 31–35)

[31] That day they struck down the Philistines from Michmash to Aijalon.
The people were very worn out.
[32] So the people rushed at the plunder.
They took sheep, cattle, and calves,
killed them on the ground,
and the people ate them with the blood.
[33] Then someone told Saul,
“Look, the people are sinning against the LORD
by eating the meat with the blood.”
He said,
“You have been unfaithful.
Roll a large stone over to me today!”
[34] Saul said,
“Spread out among the people and tell them,
‘Each of you bring his ox and each his sheep to me,
and kill it here and eat.
And don’t sin against the LORD by eating the meat with the blood.’”
So that night all the people each brought his ox with him
and killed it there.
[35] Saul built an altar to the LORD.
This was the first altar that he built to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The results of Saul’s oath: In the evening the soldiers are so starving that they kill the animals and eat them right away, without letting the blood drain out. This was strictly forbidden. The blood stands for life and belongs to God (Leviticus 17:10–14; Genesis 9:4).
> Saul’s oath, which was meant to be devoted to God, drove the people into sin.
> Saul makes sure that the animals are killed on a large stone, so that the blood can drain out. Here he acts rightly.
> The sentence “This was the first altar that he built” sounds almost like a quiet criticism: Saul has been king for a while, but only now does he build an altar to God for the first time.

## Jonathan is to die (Verses 36–46)

[36] Saul said,
“Let’s go down after the Philistines by night
and plunder them until the morning light.
Let’s not leave a single man of them.”
They said, “Do whatever seems good to you.”
Then the priest said,
“Let’s come near to God here.”
[37] Saul asked God,
“Shall I go down after the Philistines?
Will you give them into the hand of Israel?”
But he did not answer him that day.
[38] Saul said,
“Come here, all you leaders of the people,
and find out and see who has committed this sin today.
[39] For as the LORD lives, who saves Israel:
even if it is my son Jonathan, he must surely die.”
But not one man among all the people answered him.
[40] Then he said to all Israel,
“You stand on one side,
and I and my son Jonathan will stand on the other side.”
The people said to Saul, “Do what seems good to you.”
[41] So Saul said to the LORD, the God of Israel,
“Show what is right.”
Jonathan and Saul were chosen, and the people went free.
[42] Saul said,
“Cast lots between me and my son Jonathan.”
And Jonathan was chosen.
[43] Then Saul said to Jonathan,
“Tell me what you have done!”
Jonathan told him and said,
“I did taste a little honey with the end of the stick
that was in my hand.
And look, I must die.”
[44] Saul said,
“May God do so to me and even more:
You must surely die, Jonathan.”
[45] But the people said to Saul,
“Shall Jonathan die, who has brought about this great rescue in Israel?
Far from it!
As the LORD lives,
not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground.
For he has worked with God today.”
So the people rescued Jonathan, and he did not die.
[46] Then Saul stopped chasing the Philistines.
And the Philistines went back to their own place.

> **What does this mean?**
> God does not answer Saul. Saul thinks: Someone has sinned. And right away he swears another hasty oath: Whoever it is must die, even my son.
> The lot falls on Jonathan. He honestly admits what he has done. After all, he knew nothing about the oath.
> In verse 41 the old Greek translation has a longer text. There Saul asks God to answer with “Urim” or “Thummim.” These were holy lots with which the priest asked God for answers (Exodus 28:30).
> Saul wants to kill his own son, just so that he does not lose face. He holds on to his oath, even though the oath was wrong.
> The people take Jonathan’s side. They say: God was with Jonathan, not against him. Here the people have more sense than the king. Jewish Bible teachers say about this: A wrong oath must not lead to the death of an innocent person.
> Because of all the confusion, the Philistines are able to escape. Saul’s oath made the victory smaller.

## Saul’s wars and his family (Verses 47–52)

[47] When Saul had taken over the kingdom of Israel,
he fought against all his enemies on every side:
against Moab, against the Ammonites, against Edom,
against the kings of Zobah, and against the Philistines.
Wherever he turned, he defeated them.
[48] He acted bravely and struck down the Amalekites.
And he rescued Israel from the hands of those who robbed them.
[49] The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua.
And the names of his two daughters were:
the older one was named Merab, and the younger one was named Michal.
[50] The name of Saul’s wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahimaaz.
The name of the commander of his army was Abner, the son of Ner, Saul’s uncle.
[51] Kish was the father of Saul,
and Ner, the father of Abner, was the son of Abiel.
[52] There was hard war against the Philistines as long as Saul lived.
And whenever Saul saw a strong man or a brave man,
he took him into his service.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is a short summary of Saul’s rule. Saul was a successful military leader. He protected Israel against many enemies. We have to give him credit for that.
> Zobah was a kingdom of the Arameans in the north, in what is Syria today.
> Ishvi may be the same person as Ish-bosheth, who later becomes king for a short time after Saul (2 Samuel 2:8). Merab and Michal will become important in the story of David.
> Abner was Saul’s cousin and his top general. He too plays a big role later.
