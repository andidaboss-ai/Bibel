# 1 Samuel – Chapter 5
### The ark among the Philistines

---

## Dagon falls over (Verses 1–5)

[1] The Philistines had captured the ark of God.
They brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.
[2] The Philistines took the ark of God,
brought it into the house of Dagon,
and set it up next to Dagon.
[3] When the people of Ashdod got up early the next morning,
Dagon had fallen on his face to the ground in front of the ark of the LORD.
They took Dagon and put him back in his place.
[4] When they got up early the following morning,
Dagon had again fallen on his face to the ground in front of the ark of the LORD.
Dagon’s head and both his hands were broken off and lay on the threshold.
Only Dagon’s body was still in one piece.
[5] That is why the priests of Dagon and all who go into Dagon’s house
in Ashdod do not step on the threshold of Dagon, to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ashdod was one of the five great cities of the Philistines. Dagon was their most important god. We already heard about him in the story of Samson (Judges 16:23).
> The threshold is the stone or board at the bottom of a doorway.
> The Philistines set the ark next to Dagon, like the spoils of war. They want to show: Our god has defeated the God of Israel.
> But in the morning Dagon lies on his face in front of the ark, as if he were bowing down before the LORD. The people have to set their god up again. A god who needs help to get up is not a god.
> On the second morning, the head and hands are broken off. The head stands for understanding, the hands for power. Dagon has neither. This is also a bit of mockery of idols.
> Israel had thought: God has been defeated. But God fights alone, without Israel, right in the temple of the enemies.

## The plague in Ashdod (Verses 6–7)

[6] But the hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod.
He destroyed them and struck them with tumors,
Ashdod and its territory.
[7] When the men of Ashdod saw what was happening, they said,
“The ark of the God of Israel must not stay with us.
For his hand is hard on us and on our god Dagon.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Tumors” were probably sores or swellings. Many Bible teachers think of a disease, maybe the plague. With the plague, people get big swellings, and it is spread by mice and rats. This fits the golden mice in Chapter 6.
> The old Greek translation also tells here that mice overran the land.
> “The hand of the LORD” is a picture for God’s power. The people of Ashdod realize: This is not chance.

## The ark moves from city to city (Verses 8–12)

[8] So they sent messengers and gathered all the lords of the Philistines.
They said,
“What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?”
They answered,
“Let the ark of the God of Israel be taken over to Gath.”
And they took the ark of the God of Israel there.
[9] After they had taken it there,
the hand of the LORD was against the city, with a very great panic.
He struck the men of the city, young and old,
so that tumors broke out on them.
[10] So they sent the ark of God to Ekron.
When the ark of God came to Ekron, the people of Ekron cried out,
“They have brought the ark of the God of Israel here to us,
to kill us and our people!”
[11] So they sent messengers and gathered all the lords of the Philistines.
They said,
“Send the ark of the God of Israel away.
Let it go back to its own place,
so that it will not kill us and our people.”
For there was a deadly panic in the whole city.
The hand of God was very heavy on it.
[12] The men who did not die were struck with the tumors.
And the cry of the city went up to heaven.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gath and Ekron were other cities of the Philistines. No city wants to have the ark. Wherever it goes, the disease breaks out.
> This is almost like a repeat of the plagues in Egypt. The Philistines had reminded themselves of them (Chapter 4:8). Now they experience it themselves.
> The ark, which the Israelites had lost like spoils of war, becomes a burden for the Philistines that no one can bear. God does not need an army to defend himself.
