# 1 Samuel – Chapter 23
### On the run in the wilderness

---

## David rescues Keilah (Verses 1–6)

[1] David was told,
“Look, the Philistines are fighting against Keilah
and are robbing the threshing floors.”
[2] So David asked the LORD,
“Shall I go and strike down these Philistines?”
The LORD said to David,
“Go, strike down the Philistines, and save Keilah.”
[3] David’s men said to him,
“Look, we are afraid here in Judah already.
How much more if we go to Keilah against the armies of the Philistines!”
[4] Then David asked the LORD once more.
And the LORD answered him,
“Get up, go down to Keilah.
For I will give the Philistines into your hand.”
[5] David and his men went to Keilah
and fought against the Philistines.
They drove away their livestock
and defeated them badly.
So David saved the people of Keilah.
[6] When Abiathar the son of Ahimelech fled to David at Keilah,
he came down with an ephod in his hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Keilah was a town in Judah, near the border with the Philistines. The “threshing floors” were the places where the harvested grain was beaten to separate the grain from the stalks. The Philistines are stealing the harvest, the food for a whole year.
> David himself is on the run. Still he helps others. Really, that would have been the king’s job. But Saul is busy hunting David.
> David asks God before he acts. And when his men are afraid, he asks once more. This is very different from Saul, who often did not wait for God.
> With the ephod the priest could ask God and get an answer of yes or no.

---

## The ungratefulness of Keilah (Verses 7–14)

[7] Saul was told that David had come to Keilah.
Saul said,
“God has given him into my hand.
For he has shut himself in
by going into a town with gates and bars.”
[8] Saul called all the people together for war,
to go down to Keilah and surround David and his men.
[9] David found out that Saul was planning evil against him.
He said to Abiathar the priest,
“Bring the ephod here.”
[10] Then David said,
“LORD, God of Israel,
your servant has surely heard
that Saul wants to come to Keilah
to destroy the town because of me.
[11] Will the men of Keilah hand me over to him?
Will Saul come down, as your servant has heard?
LORD, God of Israel, please tell your servant.”
The LORD said,
“He will come down.”
[12] Then David said,
“Will the men of Keilah hand me and my men over to Saul?”
The LORD said,
“They will hand you over.”
[13] So David and his men got up, about 600 men,
left Keilah,
and went wherever they could go.
Saul was told that David had escaped from Keilah.
So he gave up going there.
[14] David stayed in the wilderness, in the strongholds,
and remained in the hill country in the wilderness of Ziph.
Saul looked for him every day,
but God did not give him into his hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Saul thinks: God has handed David over to me! He uses God’s name for his evil plans. But God is not on his side.
> The people of Keilah would betray David, even though he has just saved them. They would do it out of fear of Saul. They had seen what happened to Nob.
> David does not want a whole town to suffer because of him. So he leaves.
> “God did not give him into his hand”: That is the key sentence. Saul searches every day, with a whole army. But he does not find David. God protects him.
> The wilderness of Ziph is southeast of Hebron, a dry, rocky area at the edge of the wilderness of Judah.

---

## Jonathan strengthens David (Verses 15–18)

[15] David saw that Saul had come out to take his life.
David was in the wilderness of Ziph, in the woods.
[16] Then Jonathan, Saul’s son, got up
and went to David in the woods.
He strengthened his hand in God.
[17] He said to him,
“Don’t be afraid.
For the hand of my father Saul will not find you.
And you will be king over Israel,
and I will be second to you.
And my father Saul knows this too.”
[18] The two of them made a covenant before the LORD.
Then David stayed in the woods,
and Jonathan went home.

> **What does this mean?**
> “In the woods”: The Hebrew word is probably a place name: “Horesh.” That is why many other Bibles write “at Horesh.”
> Jonathan comes to David secretly, even though it is dangerous for him. He does exactly what a true friend does: He gives David courage, not by his own strength, but “in God.” He reminds David of God’s promise.
> Jonathan says openly: You will be king, not I. And I gladly want to be second. That is true greatness.
> This is the last time the two friends see each other. Jonathan will die in battle together with Saul (Chapter 31). His wish to be second to David does not come true.

## The Ziphites betray David (Verses 19–29)

[19] Then the Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah and said,
“Isn’t David hiding among us in the strongholds in the woods,
on the hill of Hachilah, which is south of the desert?
[20] So now, O king, come down,
just as you wish.
And our part will be to hand him over to the king.”
[21] Saul said,
“You are blessed by the LORD,
for you have had compassion on me.
[22] Please go and make even more sure.
Find out and see the place where he stays,
and who has seen him there.
For I have been told that he is very cunning.
[23] Look and find out all the hiding places where he hides.
Then come back to me with sure news,
and I will go with you.
And if he is in the land,
I will search him out among all the thousands of Judah.”
[24] They got up and went to Ziph ahead of Saul.
But David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon,
in the Arabah, south of the desert.
[25] Saul and his men went to look for him.
When David was told,
he went down to the rock and stayed in the wilderness of Maon.
When Saul heard that, he chased David into the wilderness of Maon.
[26] Saul went on this side of the mountain,
and David and his men on the other side of the mountain.
David hurried to get away from Saul out of fear.
For Saul and his men were surrounding David and his men to capture them.
[27] But a messenger came to Saul and said,
“Hurry and come,
for the Philistines have attacked the land!”
[28] So Saul turned back from chasing David
and went against the Philistines.
That is why that place was called Sela Hammahlekoth.
[29] David went up from there
and lived in the strongholds of En Gedi.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ziphites belonged to the tribe of Judah, like David himself. Still they betray him. According to its title, Psalm 54 is a prayer of David from the time when the Ziphites betrayed him.
> Saul blesses the traitors in the name of the LORD. And again he says: “You have had compassion on me.” He still sees himself as the victim.
> At the mountain it gets very tight. Only a mountain separates Saul from David. He has almost surrounded him. And at exactly this moment a messenger comes: The Philistines are attacking! Saul has to turn back.
> Was that an accident? Not for the storyteller. God rescues David at the last moment, even through Israel’s enemies.
> “Sela Hammahlekoth” probably means “rock of separation,” because Saul and David separated here.
> En Gedi is an oasis by the Dead Sea, with a spring and caves in the rocks. Wild mountain goats still live there today.
> In the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles, verse 29 is already Chapter 24, verse 1.
