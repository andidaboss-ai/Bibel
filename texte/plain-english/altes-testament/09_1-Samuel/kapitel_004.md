# 1 Samuel – Chapter 4
### The ark of the covenant is lost

---

## Israel loses the first battle (Verses 1–4)

[1] The word of Samuel came to all Israel.
Israel went out to battle against the Philistines
and camped near Ebenezer.
The Philistines camped at Aphek.
[2] The Philistines lined up for battle against Israel.
When the battle began, Israel was beaten by the Philistines.
They killed about 4,000 men of the army on the battlefield.
[3] When the people came back to the camp, the elders of Israel said,
“Why has the LORD let us be beaten by the Philistines today?
Let’s bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD here from Shiloh.
Then it will come among us
and save us from the hand of our enemies.”
[4] So the people sent messengers to Shiloh.
From there they brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD of Armies,
who sits enthroned above the cherubim.
And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Aphek was in the coastal plain, at the border of the land of the Philistines. Ebenezer was nearby. The name “Ebenezer” means “stone of help.” How the place got this name is told only in Chapter 7.
> The ark of the covenant was a wooden chest covered with gold. Inside it were the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments. On top of the lid were two cherubim, angel figures with wings. The ark was the sign that God is right among his people. People said: God sits enthroned above the cherubim, like a king on his throne.
> The elders ask: Why has the LORD let us be beaten? That is a good question. But they do not wait for an answer. They do not ask God or Samuel. They think: If we have the ark with us, God has to help us.
> In this way they treat the ark like a lucky charm, like a magic object. They want to use God, but not listen to him. And of all people, the wicked priests Hophni and Phinehas go along with the ark.

---

## The Philistines become afraid (Verses 5–9)

[5] When the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp,
all Israel shouted so loudly that the earth shook.
[6] When the Philistines heard the noise of the shouting, they said,
“What does the noise of this great shouting in the camp of the Hebrews mean?”
They found out that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp.
[7] The Philistines became afraid.
For they said, “God has come into the camp.”
They said,
“How terrible for us!
For nothing like this has ever happened before.
[8] How terrible for us!
Who will save us from the hand of these mighty gods?
These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with all kinds of plagues in the wilderness.
[9] Be strong and act like men, you Philistines,
so that you will not become slaves of the Hebrews, as they have been your slaves.
Be men and fight!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hebrews” was a name that other peoples used for the Israelites.
> The Philistines know the story of the exodus from Egypt. But they think that Israel has many gods, just as they do. And they mix up the story a little: The plagues happened in Egypt, not in the wilderness.
> Instead of giving up, the Philistines encourage each other. Their fear makes them determined.

---

## The great defeat (Verses 10–11)

[10] The Philistines fought, and Israel was beaten.
Each man fled to his tent.
It was a very great slaughter.
For 30,000 foot soldiers of Israel fell.
[11] The ark of God was captured.
And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were killed.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a disaster. The ark, the holiest thing in Israel, falls into the hands of the enemies.
> The message is clear: God cannot be forced. The ark is not a magic tool. Without obedience and without real trust, even the holiest object does not help.
> Hophni and Phinehas die on the same day, just as the prophet had announced (Chapter 2:34).
> Later the Psalms and the prophets remember this day: God left his dwelling place in Shiloh (Psalm 78:60–61; Jeremiah 7:12).

---

## Eli’s death (Verses 12–18)

[12] A man from Benjamin ran away from the army
and came to Shiloh the same day.
His clothes were torn, and he had dirt on his head.
[13] When he arrived, Eli was sitting on his seat by the road, watching.
For his heart was trembling for the ark of God.
When the man came into the city and told about it,
the whole city cried out.
[14] When Eli heard the noise of the crying, he said,
“What does the noise of this uproar mean?”
The man hurried over, came, and told Eli.
[15] Eli was 98 years old.
His eyes were fixed, so that he could not see.
[16] The man said to Eli,
“I am the one who came from the army.
I fled from the army today.”
He said, “How did it go, my son?”
[17] The one who brought the news answered,
“Israel has fled from the Philistines.
There has also been a great slaughter among the people.
Your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead too.
And the ark of God has been captured.”
[18] When he mentioned the ark of God,
Eli fell backward off his seat, by the side of the gate.
His neck broke, and he died.
For he was an old man and heavy.
He had judged Israel for 40 years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Torn clothes and dirt on the head were signs of mourning at that time. Everyone saw right away: This man is bringing bad news.
> Eli is more worried about the ark than about his sons. When he hears that his sons are dead, he is still alive. Only when he hears that the ark is lost does he fall over. This shows: With all his faults, Eli loved God and his holy place.
> “He was heavy”: The Hebrew word for “heavy” is related to the word for “glory” or “honor” (“kavod”). In Chapter 2:29 God had said: “you make yourselves fat with the best of all the offerings.” And verses 21 and 22 play with the same word: The “glory” is gone.
> The old Greek translation says that Eli judged Israel for 20 years.

---

## Ichabod: “Where is the glory?” (Verses 19–22)

[19] His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and about to give birth.
When she heard the news that the ark of God had been captured
and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead,
she bent over and gave birth.
For her labor pains came on her suddenly.
[20] As she was dying, the women standing beside her said to her,
“Don’t be afraid, for you have given birth to a son.”
But she did not answer or pay attention to it.
[21] She named the child Ichabod and said,
“The glory has departed from Israel!”
Because the ark of God had been captured,
and because of her father-in-law and her husband.
[22] She said,
“The glory has departed from Israel,
for the ark of God has been captured.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Again a sad story about a woman who suffers because of what the men have done. The news is so terrible that the birth starts too early. The woman dies.
> The women want to comfort her: You have a son! At that time, that was the greatest joy. But she cannot be happy.
> The name “Ichabod” means: “Where is the glory?” or “No glory.” God’s “glory” was his presence. With the ark, she thinks, God himself has left Israel.
> This woman understands more than the elders: The worst thing is not the lost battle. The worst thing is when God is no longer with his people.
> But the story goes on. The next chapters show: God is not defeated. He can help himself even in the land of the enemies.
