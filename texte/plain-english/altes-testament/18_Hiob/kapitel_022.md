# Job – Chapter 22
### Eliphaz’s third speech: an open accusation

---

## Does God gain anything if you are righteous? (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
[2] “Can a person be useful to God?
No, a wise person is only useful to himself.
[3] Does it give the Almighty any joy that you are righteous?
Does he gain anything if you make your ways perfect?
[4] Is it because you fear him that he corrects you?
Is that why he takes you to court?

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz says: God is so great that he needs nothing from us. Whether you are good or bad changes nothing for God.
> Verse 4 is ironic: God surely does not punish you because you are devout! So there must be another reason.
> The irony for the reader: That is exactly what happened! God praised Job before Satan precisely because Job was devout (Job 1:8). And that is why Job was tested. Eliphaz has no idea how right he is without meaning to be.
> And: God does take joy in a righteous person. The whole Bible shows this.

---

## The concrete accusations (Verses 5–11)

[5] Isn’t your evil great?
Your guilt has no end.
[6] For you took things from your brothers as security for a debt, for no reason.
You took the clothes off people and left them naked.
[7] You did not give water to the tired to drink.
You held back bread from the hungry.
[8] But the powerful man owned the land,
and the honored man lived in it.
[9] You sent widows away with empty hands.
The arms of the orphans were broken.
[10] That is why traps are all around you,
and sudden fear troubles you,
[11] or darkness, so that you cannot see,
and floods of water cover you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Eliphaz gets concrete. He accuses Job of serious sins: Job supposedly took advantage of poor people, gave no bread to the hungry and left widows and orphans alone.
> But Eliphaz has no proof. He makes up these accusations because his theory needs them. He thinks: Job suffers badly, so he must have sinned badly. And since a rich man like Job could most easily sin against the poor, Eliphaz accuses him of that.
> That is slander. In Chapters 29 and 31, Job will show that he did exactly the opposite: He was a father to the poor and helped widows and orphans.
> Here you can see where a false theory can lead: People accuse the innocent so that the theory will be right.

---

## God sees everything (Verses 12–20)

[12] “Isn’t God high up in heaven?
Look how high the stars are!
[13] And you say, ‘What does God know?
Can he judge through the thick darkness?
[14] Thick clouds cover him, so that he does not see.
He walks on the dome of the sky.’
[15] Will you keep to the old path
that evil people walked on?
[16] They were snatched away before their time.
Their foundation was washed away like a river.
[17] They said to God, ‘Go away from us!’
and, ‘What can the Almighty do for us?’
[18] Yet he filled their houses with good things.
But the plans of the wicked are far from me.
[19] The righteous see it and are glad.
The innocent make fun of them.
[20] They say, ‘Surely those who rose up against us are destroyed.
The fire has burned up what was left of them.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz puts words into Job’s mouth that Job never said: What does God know? He doesn’t see anything. On the contrary, Job always said that God sees everything.
> “The old path that evil people walked on”: Maybe Eliphaz is thinking of the people at the time of the Flood, who were washed away by floods of water.
> Verses 17–18 repeat Job’s words from Job 21:14–16 almost word for word. Eliphaz turns them against Job.

---

## Turn back to the Almighty! (Verses 21–30)

[21] “Get to know him now, and be at peace.
That way good things will come to you.
[22] Please accept teaching from his mouth.
Keep his words in your heart.
[23] If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up again,
if you put wrongdoing far away from your tents.
[24] Throw your treasure into the dust,
the gold of Ophir among the stones of the streams.
[25] Then the Almighty will be your treasure,
and precious silver to you.
[26] For then you will find joy in the Almighty,
and you will lift up your face to God.
[27] You will pray to him, and he will hear you.
You will keep your promises to him.
[28] You will also decide on something, and it will happen for you.
Light will shine on your ways.
[29] When people are brought down, you will say, ‘Be lifted up.’
He will save the humble person.
[30] He will even rescue the one who is not innocent.
Yes, that person will be rescued because your hands are clean.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, Eliphaz becomes friendly again. He invites Job to turn back. Many of these words are beautiful: “Then the Almighty will be your treasure.” “You will pray to him, and he will hear you.”
> God himself is the greatest treasure. That is a true and deep insight.
> Ophir was a land that was famous for its fine gold.
> Verse 30 is amazing: Eliphaz says that even guilty people can be rescued when a righteous person prays for them. That is exactly what will happen at the end, but the other way around: Job will pray for Eliphaz, and God will forgive Eliphaz for Job’s sake (Job 42:8). Without knowing it, Eliphaz is predicting his own future here.
