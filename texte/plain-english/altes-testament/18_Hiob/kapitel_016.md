# Job – Chapter 16
### “My witness is in heaven”

---

## You are miserable comforters (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then Job answered,
[2] “I have heard many things like this.
You are all miserable comforters!
[3] Will your empty words ever end?
What is bothering you, that you keep answering?
[4] I could also speak the way you do,
if you were in my place.
I could string words together against you
and shake my head at you.
[5] But I would make you strong with my words.
The comfort of my lips would ease your pain.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Miserable comforters”: This expression has become a saying. It describes people who want to comfort, but make everything worse with their words.
> Job says: If you were in my place, I could also talk the way you do. But I would not do it. I would comfort you and make you strong.
> This is an important thought: How would I feel if I were in the place of the one who is suffering? This question helps us to comfort in the right way (compare Matthew 7:12).

---

## God has attacked me (Verses 6–17)

[6] “If I speak, my pain does not get better.
If I keep quiet, does any of it go away?
[7] But now, God, you have truly worn me out.
You have destroyed my whole family.
[8] You have made me shrivel up. That is a witness against me.
My thin body rises up against me.
It testifies against me to my face.
[9] He has torn me apart in his anger and hunted me down.
He has ground his teeth at me.
My enemy looks at me with sharp eyes.
[10] People have opened their mouths wide against me.
They have slapped my cheek to shame me.
They gang up together against me.
[11] God hands me over to the ungodly.
He throws me into the hands of the wicked.
[12] I was living in peace, and he broke me apart.
Yes, he has grabbed me by the neck and smashed me to pieces.
He has also set me up as his target.
[13] His archers surround me.
He splits my kidneys open and does not spare me.
He pours out my bile on the ground.
[14] He breaks through me, again and again, like a wall.
He charges at me like a warrior.
[15] I have sewn rough sackcloth over my skin.
I have pushed my horn into the dust.
[16] My face is red from crying.
Deep darkness lies on my eyelids,
[17] even though there is no violence in my hands,
and my prayer is pure.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job describes his suffering in strong pictures: God is like a wild animal that tears him apart. Like a warrior who attacks him. Like an archer who uses him as a target. Like an army that breaks through a city wall.
> People also mock him and slap him on the cheek.
> “Pushed my horn into the dust”: The horn is a picture for strength and honor. Job has lost all his dignity.
> Verse 17 is the heart of it: Job suffers “even though there is no violence in my hands, and my prayer is pure.” He insists: I am innocent.
> Some words in verse 10 remind Christians of the suffering of Jesus, who was also mocked and struck, even though he was innocent (Matthew 26:67).

---

## My witness is in heaven (Verses 18–22)

[18] “Earth, do not cover my blood!
Let my cry find no place to rest!
[19] Even now, look, my witness is in heaven.
The one who speaks up for me is on high.
[20] My friends mock me.
My eyes pour out tears to God.
[21] I want him to defend a man’s case before God,
as a person does for his neighbor!
[22] For in only a few years
I will go on the road from which I will not come back.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Earth, do not cover my blood”: Innocent blood that has been shed cries out to heaven (compare Abel in Genesis 4:10). Job wants his cry not to fall silent, even if he dies.
> Verse 19 is a great statement of faith: “Even now, look, my witness is in heaven.” In the middle of the deepest despair, Job believes: There is someone in heaven who stands up for me. In Job 9:33 he had wished for a referee. Now he says: There is one!
> Who is this witness? Maybe God himself. Then Job would be appealing to God against God: to the God who is just, against the God who seems to torment him.
> Verse 21: He is to defend “a man’s case before God,” like a lawyer.
> Christians see here a hint of Jesus, who “speaks up for us” before God (Romans 8:34; Hebrews 7:25; 1 John 2:1).
