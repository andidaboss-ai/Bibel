# Job – Chapter 11
### Zophar: “God asks less of you than you deserve”

---

## Zophar attacks Job (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then Zophar the Naamathite answered,
[2] “Shouldn’t all these words get an answer?
Should a man who talks so much be proved right?
[3] Should your bragging make people keep quiet?
When you mock, will no one put you to shame?
[4] For you say, ‘My teaching is pure.
I am clean in your eyes.’
[5] Oh, if only God would speak,
and open his lips against you,
[6] and show you the secrets of wisdom!
For true wisdom has two sides.
So know this: God asks less of you
than your guilt deserves.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zophar is the harshest of the three friends. He calls Job a big talker and a bragger.
> He wishes that God would speak, to put Job in his place. The irony: At the end God really does speak. But he does not correct Job. He corrects the friends (Job 42:7)!
> Verse 6 is cruel: “So know this: God asks less of you than your guilt deserves.” Zophar tells a man who has lost all his children and his health: Really you deserved even more.
> Zophar does not know Job’s life at all. He judges without knowing.

---

## God’s wisdom cannot be fathomed (Verses 7–12)

[7] “Can you find out the deep secrets of God?
Can you discover the limits of the Almighty?
[8] They are as high as heaven. What can you do?
They are deeper than Sheol (the world of the dead). What can you know?
[9] They are longer than the earth
and wider than the sea.
[10] If he passes by, or locks someone up, or calls a court together,
who can stop him?
[11] For he knows who is false.
He sees wrongdoing too, even when he does not seem to pay attention to it.
[12] An empty-headed man will become wise
when a human being is born as a wild donkey’s colt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zophar says something true: God’s wisdom is endlessly great. No human being can fully understand it (compare Romans 11:33).
> But he uses this truth against Job. If God’s ways are so far beyond understanding, then Zophar could not understand them either. And yet he acts as if he knew exactly why Job is suffering.
> Verse 12 is a mocking proverb: A fool will become wise just as soon as a human being is born as a wild donkey’s colt. So never. Zophar means Job.

---

## Turn back, and everything will be fine (Verses 13–20)

[13] “If you set your heart right
and stretch out your hands to him,
[14] if there is wrongdoing in your hand, put it far away.
Do not let injustice live in your tents.
[15] Then surely you will lift up your face without any stain.
Yes, you will stand firm and not be afraid.
[16] For you will forget your misery.
You will remember it like water that has flowed past.
[17] Your life will be brighter than noon.
Even if there is darkness, it will be like the morning.
[18] You will feel safe, because there is hope.
Yes, you will look around and rest in safety.
[19] You will lie down, and no one will frighten you.
Yes, many will try to win your favor.
[20] But the eyes of the wicked will fail.
They will have no place to escape to.
Their only hope will be to breathe their last.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Like Eliphaz and Bildad, Zophar also ends with a beautiful promise: If you turn back, everything will be fine.
> That sounds good. And much of it is true. God can turn misery into joy.
> But it takes for granted that Job has sinned. That is the problem. The friends say: Admit that you are guilty, then everything will be fine. But Job is not guilty. He cannot admit something that is not true.
> The last verse is a warning: If you do not turn back, you are like the wicked. Their only hope is death.
