# Job – Chapter 24
### The suffering of the poor

---

## Why does God not step in? (Verses 1–4)

[1] “Why does the Almighty not set times for judgment?
Why do those who know him not see his days?
[2] There are people who move the boundary stones.
They take flocks by force and let them graze as their own.
[3] They drive away the donkey of the orphans.
They take the widow’s ox as security for a debt.
[4] They push the needy off the road.
The poor of the land all have to hide.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job asks: Why does God not hold a day of judgment? Why do the faithful not see God step in?
> Then he describes what happens in the world: The strong take advantage of the weak.
> “Move the boundary stones” means: stealing land by secretly moving the border. The law strictly forbade this (Deuteronomy 19:14; 27:17).
> The orphan’s donkey and the widow’s ox were often all they owned. Without them they could not survive.

---

## How the poor live (Verses 5–12)

[5] Look, like wild donkeys in the desert,
they go out to their work.
They search hard for food.
The wilderness gives them bread for their children.
[6] They cut their food in the field.
They gather what is left in the vineyard of the wicked.
[7] They lie naked all night without clothes.
They have no cover in the cold.
[8] They are wet from the rain in the mountains.
They hug the rocks because they have no shelter.
[9] There are people who tear the orphan from the mother’s breast.
They take things from the poor as security for a debt,
[10] so that the poor go around naked without clothes.
They are hungry while they carry the bundles of grain.
[11] They make olive oil inside the walls of these men.
They tread the grapes in the winepresses, but they suffer thirst.
[12] In the crowded city, people groan.
The wounded cry out.
Yet God pays no attention to the wrong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job describes the misery of the poor very exactly and with much compassion:
> – They search the desert for food like wild animals.
> – They have no clothes and are cold at night.
> – They look for shelter under rocks.
> – Their children are taken from them as security for debts.
> – They are hungry while they carry the grain of the rich. They are thirsty while they press the wine.
> This is one of the most powerful descriptions of poverty in the Bible. Sadly, such conditions still exist today.
> Verse 12 is a bitter accusation: “Yet God pays no attention to the wrong.” Job does not understand why God does not step in.
> Job does not only see his own suffering. He sees the suffering of many people. His pain has made him compassionate.

---

## The people who hate the light (Verses 13–17)

[13] “These are the ones who rebel against the light.
They do not know its ways.
They do not stay on its paths.
[14] The murderer gets up when it gets light.
He kills the poor and the needy.
At night he is like a thief.
[15] The eye of the man who cheats on his wife also waits for the evening.
He says, ‘No eye will see me.’
He covers his face.
[16] In the dark they break into houses.
In the daytime they lock themselves in.
They do not know the light.
[17] For all of them, the morning is like thick darkness,
because they know the terrors of thick darkness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job describes criminals who work in the dark: murderers, men who cheat on their wives, burglars. They love the night because no one sees them.
> “For all of them, the morning is like thick darkness”: The light is their enemy, because it shows what they do.
> Jesus says something similar: “For everyone who does evil hates the light and doesn’t come to the light, so that his works won’t be exposed” (John 3:20).

---

## What happens to the wicked (Verses 18–25)

[18] “They are foam on the surface of the water.
Their share of the land is cursed.
They no longer turn onto the road to the vineyards.
[19] Drought and heat dry up the water from the snow.
In the same way, Sheol, the world of the dead, takes those who have sinned.
[20] The mother’s womb will forget him.
The worm will feed on him with pleasure.
No one will remember him anymore.
Wrongdoing will be broken like a tree.
[21] He robs the woman who cannot have children.
He does no kindness to the widow.
[22] Yet God keeps the mighty alive by his power.
He gets up again, even when he is not sure he will live.
[23] God gives them safety, and they rely on it.
His eyes are on their ways.
[24] They are raised up high, but in a little while they are gone.
Yes, they are brought low.
They are taken away like everyone else.
They are cut off like the tops of the heads of grain.
[25] If it is not so, who will prove that I am lying?
Who will show that my words are worth nothing?”

> **What does this mean?**
> This section is very hard to understand in Hebrew. The translations differ greatly.
> Some verses (18–20) sound like the speeches of the friends: The wicked quickly pass away. Maybe Job is quoting his friends’ opinion here. Or he is saying: Yes, in the end the wicked die too, but often only after a long, good life (verses 22–24).
> Verses 22–23: God even seems to protect the mighty and give them safety.
> Verse 24: In the end they die “like everyone else.” Death makes no difference. But Job does not see any special punishment for them.
> Verse 25 is a challenge: If I am not right, then prove it to me!
> Some scholars think that the order of the speeches in Chapters 24–27 got mixed up when the text was copied. For in the third round, Bildad’s speech is very short, and Zophar does not speak at all anymore. This rendering follows the text as it has been handed down.
