# Job – Chapter 20
### Zophar’s second speech: The short happiness of the wicked

---

## The joy of the wicked is short (Verses 1–11)

[1] Then Zophar the Naamathite answered,
[2] “That is why my thoughts give me an answer,
because I am so stirred up inside.
[3] I have heard a warning that puts me to shame.
But my understanding gives me an answer.
[4] Don’t you know this from long ago,
ever since human beings were put on the earth:
[5] The cheering of the wicked is short.
The joy of the godless lasts only a moment.
[6] His pride may rise up to the heavens,
and his head may reach the clouds.
[7] Yet he will be gone forever, like his own dung.
Those who have seen him will say, ‘Where is he?’
[8] He will fly away like a dream and not be found.
Yes, he will be chased away like a vision in the night.
[9] The eye that saw him will not see him again.
His place will not see him anymore.
[10] His children will have to beg the poor for help.
His hands will have to give back his wealth.
[11] His bones are full of the strength of youth,
but it will lie down with him in the dust.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zophar is offended. Job had warned the friends (Job 19:29). Zophar feels put to shame and answers in an agitated way.
> He paid no attention at all to Job’s great confession, “I know that my Redeemer lives.” He just keeps talking about his theory.
> His teaching: The wicked are happy only for a short time. Even if they rise up to the heavens, they soon fall.
> The crude picture “like his own dung” shows how much Zophar looks down on the wicked.
> Verse 10: The children of the wicked even have to ask the poor for help. And he has to give back everything he stole.

---

## Evil tastes sweet, but turns into poison (Verses 12–19)

[12] “Evil may taste sweet in his mouth.
He may hide it under his tongue.
[13] He may hold on to it and not let it go,
but keep it in his mouth.
[14] Yet his food turns sour in his stomach.
It becomes cobra venom inside him.
[15] He has swallowed riches, and he will vomit them up again.
God will throw them out of his belly.
[16] He will suck cobra venom.
The viper’s tongue will kill him.
[17] He will not enjoy the rivers,
the flowing streams of honey and butter.
[18] He will have to give back what he worked for.
He will not be able to swallow it.
He will not enjoy the wealth he has gained.
[19] For he has oppressed the poor and left them helpless.
He has taken a house by force,
and he will not build it up.

> **What does this mean?**
> A strong picture: At first evil tastes sweet, like a candy you keep in your mouth. But in the stomach it becomes poison.
> This is an important truth: Doing wrong can feel good at first. But it destroys the one who does it.
> Verse 19 names the sin: He has oppressed the poor and taken their houses away from them.
> “Streams of honey and butter”: a picture of wealth and plenty.
> Is Zophar accusing Job of oppressing the poor? He does not say it directly. But in Chapter 22, Eliphaz will claim exactly this. And in Chapters 29 and 31, Job will show that he always helped the poor.

---

## God’s anger strikes him (Verses 20–29)

[20] “Because he never knew peace inside himself,
he will not save anything he enjoys.
[21] There was nothing left that he did not gobble up.
That is why his wealth will not last.
[22] When he has more than enough, trouble will catch up with him.
Everyone who is in misery will raise a hand against him.
[23] Just when he is about to fill his belly,
God will throw his burning anger at him.
It will rain down on him while he is eating.
[24] He will run away from the iron weapon,
but the bronze arrow will pierce him.
[25] He pulls it out, and it comes out of his body.
Yes, the shining point comes out of his liver.
Terrors come over him.
[26] Total darkness is waiting for his treasures.
A fire that no one fanned will burn him up.
It will burn up whatever is left in his tent.
[27] The heavens will reveal his guilt.
The earth will rise up against him.
[28] The wealth of his house will be carried off.
It will flow away on the day of God’s anger.
[29] This is what God gives to a wicked man.
This is the inheritance God has set for him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Zophar describes God’s punishment of the wicked in harsh, vivid pictures: God’s anger rains down on him, arrows pierce him, fire burns him up.
> “A fire that no one fanned”: A fire from God, maybe lightning. This reminds us of Job 1:16, when “the fire of God” burned up Job’s sheep. Zophar is aiming at Job again.
> Verse 27: Heaven and earth come forward as witnesses against him. Job had said, “Even now, look, my witness is in heaven” (Job 16:19). Zophar answers: Heaven will be a witness against you!
> This is Zophar’s last speech. In the third round, he will not speak anymore. Maybe he ran out of things to say.
