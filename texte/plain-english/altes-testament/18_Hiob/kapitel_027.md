# Job – Chapter 27
### “I hold on to my righteousness”

---

## Job’s oath (Verses 1–6)

[1] Job went on with his speech and said:
[2] “As surely as God lives, who has taken away my right,
the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter
[3] (for as long as there is still life in me,
and the breath of God is in my nose),
[4] my lips will surely not speak what is wrong.
My tongue will not say anything false.
[5] I would never say that you are right!
Until I die, I will not give up my integrity.
[6] I hold on to my righteousness and will not let it go.
My heart will not blame me as long as I live.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job swears by God, the God who has taken away his right. That is a contradiction: He accuses God and at the same time swears by him. Job holds on to God, even though he does not understand him.
> “The breath of God is in my nose”: God breathed the breath of life into human beings (Genesis 2:7).
> Verses 5–6: Job will not lie just to satisfy his friends. He will not admit he is guilty when he is not. “My heart will not blame me as long as I live.” He has a clear conscience.
> “Integrity” is the same word God used in heaven: “He still holds on to his integrity” (Job 2:3). And that is exactly what Satan wanted to destroy. He did not succeed.

---

## What hope does the godless person have? (Verses 7–12)

[7] “Let my enemy be like the wicked.
Let the one who rises up against me be like the unrighteous.
[8] For what hope does the godless person have
when he is cut off,
when God takes away his life?
[9] Will God hear his cry
when trouble comes on him?
[10] Will he find joy in the Almighty?
Will he call on God at all times?
[11] I will teach you about the hand of God.
I will not hide what the Almighty has in mind.
[12] Look, all of you have seen it yourselves.
So why have you become so completely foolish?

> **What does this mean?**
> Job clearly sets himself apart from the godless person: The godless person has no hope. He cannot find joy in God. He does not call on God.
> Job, on the other hand, calls on God all the time, even when he complains. That very fact shows that he is not godless.
> Verse 9: Does God hear the cry of the godless? Job knows: God hears his own cry too, even though he has not yet received an answer.

---

## The end of the wicked (Verses 13–23)

[13] “This is what God gives a wicked person as his share.
This is what the cruel receive from the Almighty as their inheritance:
[14] If his children become many, it is for the sword.
His descendants will not have enough bread to eat.
[15] Those of his family who are left will be buried by death.
His widows will not mourn.
[16] He may pile up silver like dust
and get clothes ready like heaps of clay.
[17] He may get them ready, but the righteous will wear them.
The innocent will divide up the silver.
[18] He builds his house like a moth,
like a hut that a watchman makes.
[19] He lies down rich, but he will not do it again.
He opens his eyes, and he is gone.
[20] Terrors overtake him like a flood.
A storm carries him off in the night.
[21] The east wind carries him away, and he is gone.
It sweeps him away from his place.
[22] For it hurls itself at him without mercy,
while he tries to flee from its power.
[23] People will clap their hands at him.
They will hiss him out of his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses sound surprisingly like the speeches of the friends: The wicked person loses everything. His children die. His wealth goes to the righteous.
> How does this fit with Job, who has just said that the wicked often do well (Chapter 21)?
> There are different explanations:
> – Maybe Job is saying: I also know that in the end God will judge the wicked. I do not deny that. But that does not mean that everyone who suffers is wicked.
> – Maybe Job is mockingly quoting what the friends always say.
> – Many scholars think that these verses originally belonged to Zophar’s third speech, which is otherwise completely missing. The order may have gotten mixed up when the text was copied.
> This rendering follows the text as it has been handed down. Here the text is given to Job.
> “Buried by death”: The Hebrew word for “death” can also mean “plague” here.
> “A hut that a watchman makes”: a simple hut made of branches in the vineyard. It soon falls apart again.
> “People will clap their hands … they will hiss”: signs of mockery and contempt.
