# Job – Chapter 36
### Elihu’s fourth speech: God teaches through suffering

---

## I speak for God (Verses 1–4)

[1] Elihu went on and said,
[2] “Be patient with me a little longer, and I will show you.
For I still have something to say for God.
[3] I will bring my knowledge from far away.
I will show that my Maker is righteous.
[4] For truly, my words are not false.
One who is perfect in knowledge is with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu’s fourth and last speech begins. He wants to speak “for God” and show that God is just.
> Verse 4: “One who is perfect in knowledge”: Some understand this as Elihu praising himself. Others think he is already speaking about God here (the same phrase is used for God in Job 37:16). Both are possible.

---

## God despises no one (Verses 5–12)

[5] “Look, God is mighty, and he despises no one.
He is mighty in the strength of his understanding.
[6] He does not keep the wicked alive,
but he gives justice to those who suffer.
[7] He does not take his eyes off the righteous.
He seats them with kings on the throne forever,
and they are raised up high.
[8] If they are bound in chains
and caught in the ropes of misery,
[9] then he shows them what they have done.
He shows them their wrongdoings, that they have acted proudly.
[10] He also opens their ears to his teaching.
He commands them to turn back from wrongdoing.
[11] If they listen and serve him,
they will spend their days in good times,
and their years in pleasure.
[12] But if they do not listen, they will die by the sword.
They will die without knowledge.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is mighty, but he despises no one. His power goes together with understanding and care.
> Elihu explains his main idea once more: When righteous people get into suffering (“bound in chains”), God wants to show them something. Maybe there is pride in their lives that they do not see. God opens their ears.
> Whoever listens finds happiness again. Whoever does not listen is destroyed.
> The Bible knows this idea in other places too (compare Hebrews 12:5–11). But it does not explain every kind of suffering. According to Chapters 1–2, Job’s suffering had a different reason.

---

## God rescues through suffering (Verses 13–16)

[13] “But those who are godless in their hearts store up anger.
They do not cry for help when he binds them.
[14] They die while they are young.
Their life ends among the unclean.
[15] He rescues those who suffer through their suffering.
He opens their ears through their trouble.
[16] Yes, he would have drawn you out of distress
into a wide place, where nothing holds you in.
What was set on your table would be full of rich food.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Among the unclean”: The Hebrew has a word for men who served in the temples of foreign gods. It means a shameful end.
> Verse 15 is Elihu’s key sentence: God rescues the one who suffers “through” the suffering. Suffering can be a way in which God speaks to a person.
> Verse 16: God wanted to lead Job out of the tight place into a wide open space. “What was set on your table would be full of rich food.” That means: rich, good food, a picture of blessing (compare Psalm 23:5).

---

## A warning to Job (Verses 17–21)

[17] “But you are full of judgment on the wicked.
Judgment and justice have taken hold of you.
[18] Do not let riches lure you into anger.
Do not let a large bribe turn you aside.
[19] Would your wealth keep you going in distress,
or all the might of your strength?
[20] Do not long for the night,
when peoples are taken away from their place.
[21] Be careful! Do not turn to wrongdoing,
for you have chosen this rather than suffering.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are very hard to understand in Hebrew. The translations differ greatly from each other.
> It probably means: Job, you are completely caught up in arguing about justice and judgment. Be careful that this does not make you bitter.
> Verse 20: “Do not long for the night”: Job had often wished he were dead (Chapter 3). Elihu warns him against this.
> If you yourself see no meaning in life right now: Talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Who is a teacher like God? (Verses 22–26)

[22] “Look, God is lifted high in his power.
Who is a teacher like him?
[23] Who has told him which way to go?
Who can say to him, ‘You have done wrong’?
[24] Remember to praise his work,
which people have sung about.
[25] All people have looked at it.
People see it from far away.
[26] Look, God is great, and we do not know him.
The number of his years cannot be found out.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Elihu changes his tone. He no longer argues. He praises God.
> God is the best teacher. No one can tell him what to do.
> Instead of accusing God, Job should praise God’s works, as people have always done in songs.
> Verse 26: “God is great, and we do not know him.” Our minds are not big enough to understand God fully. He is eternal.

---

## God in the rain and the thunderstorm (Verses 27–33)

[27] “For he draws up the drops of water,
which fall as rain from his mist.
[28] The clouds pour it down.
It falls on people in plenty.
[29] Can anyone really understand how the clouds spread out,
and the thunder from his tent?
[30] Look, he spreads his light around him.
He covers the bottom of the sea.
[31] For by these he judges the peoples.
He gives food in plenty.
[32] He covers his hands with the lightning.
He commands it to strike its target.
[33] Its crashing tells about him.
The livestock also tell that a storm is coming.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu describes the water cycle: God draws the water up as mist, and it falls down again as rain. People were amazed by this even back then.
> The clouds are like God’s “tent,” and the thunder is his sound. God holds lightning in his hands.
> Verse 31: With the weather God can punish (storms, drought) or bless (rain, a good harvest).
> Verse 33: Even the livestock sense when a thunderstorm is coming.
> It seems as if a thunderstorm is coming up while Elihu is speaking. This prepares for Chapter 38: There God answers “out of the whirlwind.”
