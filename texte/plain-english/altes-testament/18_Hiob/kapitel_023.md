# Job – Chapter 23
### “Oh, if only I knew where I could find him!”

---

## I am looking for God (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then Job answered:
[2] “Even today my complaint is rebellious.
His hand is heavy on me, even though I groan.
[3] Oh, if only I knew where I could find him!
If only I could come to his throne!
[4] I would lay out my case before him.
I would fill my mouth with arguments.
[5] I would learn the words he would answer me.
I would understand what he would tell me.
[6] Would he fight with me with all his great power?
No, he would listen to me.
[7] There an honest person could argue his case with him.
So I would be set free from my judge forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job no longer answers Eliphaz’s accusations. He has had enough of his friends. He turns to God again.
> Verse 3 is one of the most moving sentences in the book: “Oh, if only I knew where I could find him!” Job longs for God. He does not want to get rid of God. He wants to find him.
> Verse 6 shows Job’s deep trust: God would not strike me down with his power. He would listen to me. Job believes in a just God, even though he does not understand him right now.

---

## I cannot find him – but he knows my way (Verses 8–12)

[8] “If I go east, he is not there.
If I go west, I cannot find him.
[9] He works in the north, but I cannot see him.
He turns to the south, but I cannot catch sight of him.
[10] But he knows the way I take.
When he has tested me, I will come out like gold.
[11] My feet have stayed close to his steps.
I have kept to his way and have not turned aside.
[12] I have not turned back from the commands of his lips.
I have treasured the words of his mouth more than the food I need.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job looks for God in every direction and does not find him. Many people know this feeling: God seems to be absent.
> But then comes verse 10, a wonderful sentence of trust: “But he knows the way I take. When he has tested me, I will come out like gold.”
> Gold is made pure in fire. Job senses: Maybe my suffering is a test. And in the end I will not be destroyed but made pure. The reader knows from Chapters 1–2 that it really is a test.
> Even when I cannot find God, he finds me. He knows my way (compare Psalm 139; 1 Peter 1:7).
> Verse 12: God’s words were more important to Job than food. Jesus says something similar: “People do not live by bread alone. They live by every word that comes out of God’s mouth” (Matthew 4:4).

---

## God does what he wants (Verses 13–17)

[13] “But he is the only one, and who can change his mind?
Whatever he desires, he does.
[14] For he carries out what he has planned for me.
He has many more plans like this.
[15] That is why I am terrified in front of him.
When I think about it, I am afraid of him.
[16] For God has made my heart weak.
The Almighty has terrified me,
[17] because I was not destroyed before the darkness came,
and he did not hide the thick darkness from my face.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job swings back and forth again: A moment ago he was full of trust. Now he is afraid. God is so powerful that no one can stop him.
> That is honest. Faith is not always equally strong. Trust and fear take turns.
> Verse 17 is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: God has led me into darkness and did not keep it away from me.
