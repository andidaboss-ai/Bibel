# Job – Chapter 18
### Bildad’s second speech: The end of the wicked

---

## Stop hunting for words (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then Bildad the Shuhite answered,
[2] “How long will you keep hunting for words?
Think about it, and then we will talk.
[3] Why are we treated like animals?
Why are we unclean in your eyes?
[4] You tear yourself apart in your anger.
Should the earth be abandoned because of you?
Should the rock be moved from its place?

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad is offended. Job had said that there was no wise man among them. Bildad asks: Do you think we are stupid animals?
> Verse 4 is mocking: Do you think the whole order of the world will change because of you? The rule is: Whoever suffers is guilty. This rule is as firm as a rock. It will not be overturned because of you.

---

## The traps of the wicked (Verses 5–10)

[5] “Yes, the light of the wicked will be put out.
The spark of his fire will not shine.
[6] The light in his tent will grow dark.
His lamp above him will go out.
[7] His strong steps will become short.
His own plans will bring him down.
[8] For his own feet lead him into a net.
He walks right into its mesh.
[9] A snare grabs him by the heel.
A trap holds him tight.
[10] A rope is hidden for him in the ground.
A trap is set for him on the path.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad describes what happens to the wicked. His light goes out, and he falls into traps.
> Six different words for traps and nets: The wicked is surrounded on all sides by dangers that he has set up himself.
> This is well-known wisdom: Whoever plans evil often falls into it himself (compare Psalm 7:16; Proverbs 26:27). But Bildad applies it to Job again.

---

## The king of terrors (Verses 11–21)

[11] “Terrors will frighten him on every side.
They will follow at his heels.
[12] His strength will starve.
Disaster stands ready at his side.
[13] The parts of his body will be eaten away.
The firstborn of death will eat up his limbs.
[14] He will be torn out of the safety of his tent.
He will be brought to the king of terrors.
[15] Things that are not his will live in his tent.
Sulfur will be scattered over his home.
[16] His roots will dry up below.
His branch will be cut off above.
[17] The memory of him will disappear from the earth.
No one will speak his name in the street.
[18] He will be driven out of the light into the darkness,
and chased out of the world.
[19] He will have no son or grandson among his people.
No one will be left where he lived.
[20] Those who come after him will be shocked at what happened to him,
just as those who came before were filled with fear.
[21] Surely this is what happens to the homes of the unrighteous.
This is the place of the one who does not know God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad’s speech is cruel when you think of Job:
> – “The parts of his body will be eaten away”: That is exactly what Job is going through with his illness.
> – “He will have no son or grandson”: Job has lost all his children.
> – “Sulfur will be scattered over his home”: Like with Job, when “the fire of God fell from the sky” (Job 1:16).
> Bildad does not say it directly, but every sentence is aimed at Job. At the end he concludes: This is what happens to “the one who does not know God.” That is a monstrous accusation against a man whom God himself called blameless.
> “The firstborn of death” is probably a deadly disease. “The king of terrors” is death itself.
> This speech shows how dangerous it is to apply a right teaching in the wrong way. Bildad talks about God and at the same time deeply hurts a human being.
