# Job – Chapter 8
### Bildad: “God does not twist justice”

---

## Your children must have sinned (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then Bildad the Shuhite answered,
[2] “How long will you keep saying these things?
Will the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?
[3] Does God twist justice?
Does the Almighty twist what is right?
[4] If your children sinned against him,
he handed them over to the power of their wrongdoing.
[5] If you will seek God with all your heart
and plead with the Almighty,
[6] if you are pure and honest,
then surely he will now wake up for you.
He will give you back the home you deserve as a righteous man, and make it prosper.
[7] Even if your beginning was small,
your future will be very great.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad is less polite than Eliphaz. He calls Job’s words “a mighty wind,” that is, empty talk.
> His question in verse 3 is right: God does not twist justice. But his conclusion is cruel:
> Verse 4: “If your children sinned against him, he handed them over to the power of their wrongdoing.” That means: God punished them. Bildad tells a father who has just lost all his children: It is probably their own fault that they died. This is one of the harshest statements in the whole book.
> No one may say something like this to a grieving person. Bildad thinks he is defending God. But he deeply hurts a human being.
> Verse 7 is ironic: At the end of the book Job will really receive more than before (Job 42:12). But not because Bildad was right.

---

## Ask the generations of the past (Verses 8–10)

[8] “Please ask the generations of the past.
Find out what their ancestors learned.
[9] (For we were only born yesterday, and we know nothing.
Our days on earth are a shadow.)
[10] Won’t they teach you and tell you?
Won’t they speak words from their hearts?

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz pointed to a vision. Bildad points to tradition, to the wisdom of the ancestors.
> Tradition is valuable. But Bildad uses it so that he does not have to think for himself. He does not listen to what Job really experiences.

---

## The plant without water (Verses 11–19)

[11] “Can papyrus grow without a swamp?
Can reeds grow without water?
[12] While it is still green and not yet cut,
it dries up faster than any other plant.
[13] That is how it goes for all who forget God.
The hope of the godless will die.
[14] What he relies on breaks apart.
What he trusts is a spider’s web.
[15] He leans on his house, but it will not stand.
He holds on to it, but it will not last.
[16] He is green in the sun.
His shoots spread out over his garden.
[17] His roots wrap around the pile of rocks.
He looks at the place of stones.
[18] But if he is torn out of his place,
then the place will deny him and say, ‘I have never seen you.’
[19] That is the joy of his way!
Others will spring up out of the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad uses pictures from nature. Papyrus and reeds need water. Without water they dry up quickly. It is the same with people who forget God.
> “What he trusts is a spider’s web”: It looks strong, but it cannot hold anything.
> The second plant (verses 16–19) grows strongly, even on stones. But when it is torn out, it is as if it had never been there. The place itself “denies” it.
> Bildad means: That is what happens to the godless. And without saying it out loud: Maybe to you too, Job.

---

## God does not reject a blameless man (Verses 20–22)

[20] “God will not reject a blameless man.
He will not support evildoers.
[21] He will yet fill your mouth with laughter,
and your lips with shouts of joy.
[22] Those who hate you will be clothed with shame.
The tent of the wicked will be gone.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad ends with a hope: If you are blameless, God will make you glad again.
> The irony: God himself called Job “blameless” (Job 1:8). But by Bildad’s logic, that cannot be true, because Job is suffering.
> The friends have a ready-made theory. And because reality does not fit their theory, Job must be to blame.
