# Job – Chapter 12
### “You are the people, and wisdom will die with you!”

---

## Job mocks them (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then Job answered,
[2] “No doubt you are the people that matter,
and wisdom will die with you!
[3] But I have understanding as well as you.
I am not less than you.
Yes, who doesn’t know such things?
[4] I have become a joke to my neighbor,
I, who called on God, and he answered me.
The righteous, blameless man has become a joke.
[5] A person who lives at ease looks down on misfortune.
It is waiting for those whose feet slip.
[6] The tents of robbers do well.
Those who provoke God are safe,
those who carry their god in their hands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job answers with biting mockery: “You are the only wise people! When you die, there will be no more wisdom in the world!”
> He says: What you are telling me, I know too. These are not new insights.
> Verse 5 is a wise observation: A person who lives at ease easily looks down on those who are doing badly. People who have never suffered often do not understand those who suffer.
> Verse 6: In reality, robbers often do well. The friends’ simple formula is not true.
> “Those who carry their god in their hands”: Maybe this means idols. Or: Their god is their own hand, their violence.

---

## Ask the animals! (Verses 7–12)

[7] “But ask the animals, and they will teach you.
Ask the birds of the sky, and they will tell you.
[8] Or speak to the earth, and it will teach you.
The fish of the sea will explain it to you.
[9] Which of all these does not know
that the hand of the LORD has done this?
[10] In his hand is the life of every living thing,
and the breath of all people.
[11] Doesn’t the ear test words,
just as the tongue tastes food?
[12] Old people have wisdom.
A long life brings understanding.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job says: Every animal knows that God is mighty! You don’t need clever friends for that.
> Verse 9 is remarkable: It is the only place in the speeches (Chapters 3–37) where God’s name “the LORD” (Yahweh) appears. Otherwise Job and his friends say “God” or “the Almighty.”
> Verse 10 is a beautiful sentence: In God’s hand is the life of all living things and the breath of all people.
> Verse 11: Just as you test food with your tongue, you should test words with your ear. Not everything that is said is true.
> Verse 12 may be meant ironically: “Old people have wisdom,” people say. But Job’s old friends are showing no wisdom at all.

---

## God’s power over everyone (Verses 13–25)

[13] “God has wisdom and strength.
He has good advice and understanding.
[14] When he tears something down, it cannot be built again.
When he locks a man up, no one can set him free.
[15] When he holds back the water, it dries up.
When he lets it go, it floods the earth.
[16] He has strength and wisdom.
Both the one who is fooled and the one who fools others belong to him.
[17] He leads counselors away stripped bare.
He makes judges into fools.
[18] He loosens the bonds of kings.
He ties a belt around their waists.
[19] He leads priests away stripped bare.
He overthrows the mighty.
[20] He takes away the speech of those who are trusted.
He takes away the good sense of the elders.
[21] He pours shame on princes.
He loosens the belt of the strong.
[22] He uncovers deep things hidden in the darkness.
He brings the shadow of death into the light.
[23] He makes nations grow, and he destroys them.
He makes nations spread out, and he leads them away as captives.
[24] He takes away the understanding of the leaders of the peoples of the earth.
He makes them wander in a wilderness where there is no path.
[25] They feel their way in the dark without light.
He makes them stagger like a drunk.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job shows that he knows God’s power better than his friends do. But he sees it differently: God’s power cannot always be explained. God overthrows kings, judges, priests, and whole nations. No one is safe.
> “He loosens the bonds of kings. He ties a belt around their waists”: Kings are taken prisoner and must wear a belt like slaves.
> Verse 16: “Both the one who is fooled and the one who fools others belong to him.” God stands above everything, even above good and evil among people.
> With this, Job says: You cannot squeeze God’s actions into your simple formula. God is greater than your theories.
> Remarkable: These words about kings who are overthrown and mighty people who are brought low remind us of Hannah’s song of praise (1 Samuel 2:6–8) and Mary’s song of praise (Luke 1:52).
