# Job – Chapter 3
### Job curses the day he was born

---

## Job breaks his silence (Verses 1–10)

[1] After this, Job opened his mouth
and cursed the day he was born.
[2] Job said,
[3] “Let the day I was born be wiped out,
and the night that said, ‘A boy has been conceived!’
[4] Let that day be darkness.
Let God above not look for it.
Let no light shine on it.
[5] Let darkness and the shadow of death claim it for themselves.
Let a cloud settle over it.
Let everything that makes the day black terrify it.
[6] Let thick darkness grab that night.
Let it not be happy among the days of the year.
Let it not be counted among the months.
[7] Let that night be barren.
Let no joyful shout come into it.
[8] Let those who curse days curse it,
those who are ready to wake up Leviathan.
[9] Let the stars of its dawn be dark.
Let it hope for light but get none.
Let it not see the eyelids of the morning.
[10] For it did not shut the doors of my mother’s womb.
It did not hide trouble from my eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> After seven days of silence, it all bursts out of Job. Now the part in the form of poems begins.
> Job does not curse God. He curses the day he was born. He wishes he had never been born. The prophet Jeremiah lamented in a similar way (Jeremiah 20:14–18).
> On the first day of creation, God had said, “Let there be light” (Genesis 1:3). Job says the opposite: “Let that day be darkness” (3:4). He wishes that creation would be undone for him.
> “Leviathan” is a sea monster from old stories. It stands for chaos. Some people believed that sorcerers could wake it up to curse a day.
> “The eyelids of the morning” is a beautiful picture for the first rays of the sun.

---

## Why didn’t I die? (Verses 11–19)

[11] “Why didn’t I die at birth?
Why didn’t I breathe my last when my mother gave birth to me?
[12] Why were there knees to receive me?
Or why were there breasts for me to nurse?
[13] For then I would be lying down and quiet now.
I would be asleep. Then I would be at rest,
[14] with kings and counselors of the earth,
who built up ruins for themselves,
[15] or with princes who had gold,
who filled their houses with silver.
[16] Or I would not exist, like a baby born dead and hidden away,
like babies who never saw the light.
[17] There the wicked stop causing trouble.
There the tired are at rest.
[18] There the prisoners are at peace together.
They do not hear the voice of the slave driver.
[19] The small and the great are there.
The servant is free from his master.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job asks again and again: Why? Why did I have to live?
> “Knees to receive me”: At a birth, the father or the grandparents took the child on their knees. That was a sign that they accepted the child.
> Job imagines death as a place of rest. There everyone is equal: kings and servants, rich and poor. There is no more pain there.
> We must understand these words as the cry of a person in unbearable pain. The Bible takes this cry seriously. It does not cross it out.

---

## Why does God give life to those who suffer? (Verses 20–26)

[20] “Why is light given to someone in misery,
and life to those who are bitter in their soul,
[21] who long for death, but it does not come,
who dig for it more than for hidden treasure,
[22] who would be very happy and full of joy
if they could find the grave?
[23] Why is light given to a man whose way is hidden,
a man whom God has fenced in?
[24] For my sighing comes before I eat.
My groaning pours out like water.
[25] For the thing I fear comes on me.
What I am afraid of happens to me.
[26] I have no peace. I have no quiet.
I have no rest.
Only trouble comes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Job no longer asks only about himself. He asks about all who suffer: Why does God let people live who suffer so much?
> Verse 23 is bitter: Satan had said that God had put a protecting fence around Job (Job 1:10). Now Job feels fenced in by God, as if he were in a prison. The same fence, but felt in a completely different way.
> “My sighing comes before I eat”: He cannot even eat without groaning.
> Important: Job wishes he were dead, but he does not take his own life. He brings his pain to God, even when he laments and cries out. He keeps talking with God. The book shows: You may tell God everything, even your darkest thoughts.
