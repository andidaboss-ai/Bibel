# Job – Chapter 28
### The song of wisdom

---

## People find treasures deep down (Verses 1–11)

[1] “Surely there is a mine for silver,
and a place for gold that people make pure.
[2] Iron is taken out of the earth.
Copper is melted out of the rock.
[3] People put an end to darkness.
They search to the farthest limit
for the stones in the dark and thick darkness.
[4] They open a shaft far away from where people live.
Feet that walk by forget them.
They hang far away from other people.
They swing back and forth.
[5] Bread comes out of the earth,
but underneath, the earth is turned up as if by fire.
[6] Sapphires come from its rocks.
It has dust of gold.
[7] No bird of prey knows that path.
The falcon’s eye has not seen it.
[8] The proud wild animals have not walked on it.
The fierce lion has not passed by there.
[9] A person puts his hand on the hard rock.
He turns the mountains over from their roots.
[10] He cuts tunnels through the rocks.
His eye sees every precious thing.
[11] He dams up the streams so that they do not trickle.
He brings what is hidden out into the light.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is a poem about wisdom. It interrupts the argument between Job and his friends. It is calmer, like a pause for breath. Many scholars think that it does not belong directly to Job’s speech, but is a separate song that was put in here.
> First the poem describes mining. Even in ancient times, people dug deep shafts into the earth to find silver, gold, iron and copper. They hung on ropes in the shafts.
> Human beings are amazingly clever and brave. They get to places that no bird and no lion has ever seen. They find the most hidden treasures.

---

## But where is wisdom? (Verses 12–22)

[12] “But where can wisdom be found?
Where is the place of understanding?
[13] People do not know what it is worth.
It is not found in the land of the living.
[14] The deep waters say, ‘It is not in me.’
The sea says, ‘It is not with me.’
[15] You cannot get it for gold.
Silver cannot be weighed out as its price.
[16] It cannot be valued with the gold of Ophir,
with precious onyx or sapphire.
[17] Gold and glass cannot equal it.
It cannot be traded for jewels of fine gold.
[18] Coral and crystal are not even worth mentioning.
Yes, the price of wisdom is higher than rubies.
[19] The topaz of Ethiopia cannot equal it.
It cannot be valued with pure gold.
[20] So where does wisdom come from?
Where is the place of understanding?
[21] For it is hidden from the eyes of all living things.
It is kept hidden from the birds of the sky.
[22] Destruction and Death say,
‘We have heard a rumor of it with our ears.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the big question: People find gold deep in the earth. But where do they find wisdom?
> The answer: nowhere on earth. Not in the deep, not in the sea. And no one can buy it, not with gold, not with precious stones.
> Wisdom is worth more than all the treasures in the world (compare Proverbs 3:13–15; 8:11).
> Human beings are very gifted at technology. But they cannot dig up the deepest truth about life.
> “Destruction and Death”: Even they have only heard a rumor of wisdom.
> The WEB says “topaz of Ethiopia.” This means the land of Cush.

---

## God knows the way to wisdom (Verses 23–28)

[23] “God understands the way to it.
He knows its place.
[24] For he looks to the ends of the earth.
He sees everything under the whole sky.
[25] He sets the force of the wind.
Yes, he measures out the waters by measure.
[26] When he made a law for the rain
and a path for the lightning and thunder,
[27] then he saw wisdom and made it known.
He set it in place, yes, and searched it out.
[28] And to human beings he said,
‘Look, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom.
To turn away from evil is understanding.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only God knows where wisdom is. He already saw it when he created the world, when he put wind, water, rain and lightning in order.
> Verse 28 is the high point of the chapter and maybe of the whole book: “The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom. To turn away from evil is understanding.”
> “Fear of the Lord” does not mean being scared of God. It means awe, respect and trust: taking God seriously. That is the beginning of all wisdom (Proverbs 1:7; 9:10; Psalm 111:10).
> What is special: This is exactly how Job was described at the beginning: He “feared God and turned away from evil” (Job 1:1). Job already has this wisdom. He does not understand why he suffers. But he lives the wisdom that matters.
> People cannot understand everything. But they can trust God and do what is good.
