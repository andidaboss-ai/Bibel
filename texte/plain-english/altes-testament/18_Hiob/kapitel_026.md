# Job – Chapter 26
### “These are only the edges of his ways”

---

## How well you have helped! (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then Job answered:
[2] “How well you have helped the one who has no power!
How well you have saved the arm that has no strength!
[3] How well you have advised the one who has no wisdom!
How much sound knowledge you have shared!
[4] To whom have you spoken these words?
Whose spirit came out of you?

> **What does this mean?**
> Job answers with biting mockery: How wonderfully you have helped me! How wise your advice was!
> Verse 4: “Whose spirit came out of you?” Job asks: Where did you get this from? It is surely not from God. You only repeat what others have said.

---

## God’s greatness is beyond understanding (Verses 5–14)

[5] “The spirits of the dead tremble,
those beneath the waters and all who live there.
[6] Sheol lies naked before God.
Abaddon has no cover.
[7] He stretches out the north over empty space.
He hangs the earth on nothing.
[8] He wraps up the waters in his thick clouds,
and the clouds do not burst under their weight.
[9] He covers the sight of his throne
and spreads his cloud over it.
[10] He has drawn a boundary on the surface of the waters,
out to where light and darkness meet.
[11] The pillars of heaven tremble.
They are shocked when he speaks sharply.
[12] He stirs up the sea with his power.
With his understanding he strikes down Rahab.
[13] By his Spirit the heavens are made beautiful.
His hand has pierced the fleeing serpent.
[14] Look, these are only the edges of his ways!
How quiet is the whisper we hear of him!
But the thunder of his power – who can understand it?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Job shows that he knows God’s greatness much better than Bildad. His description is magnificent:
> – Even Sheol, the world of the dead, is open before God. Nothing is hidden from him.
> – “He hangs the earth on nothing”: an amazing sentence. Many ancient peoples believed that the earth rested on pillars or on the back of an animal. Job says: The earth hangs freely in space.
> – God wraps up water in the clouds, and they do not burst.
> – The horizon is the boundary between light and darkness.
> “Abaddon” is another Hebrew name for the world of the dead (compare Revelation 9:11).
> “Rahab” and “the fleeing serpent” are pictures from ancient stories for chaos. God defeats all the powers of chaos.
> Verse 14 is one of the most beautiful verses of the book: Everything we see of God is “only the edges of his ways.” We hear only a quiet whisper of him. His full power, the “thunder,” we cannot understand at all.
> That is true humility: We know only a little about God. So no one should act as if they had fully understood him, as the friends do.
