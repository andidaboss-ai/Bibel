# Job – Chapter 15
### Eliphaz’s second speech: “Your own mouth condemns you”

---

## You speak against God (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
[2] “Should a wise man answer with empty knowledge
and fill his belly with the east wind?
[3] Should he argue with useless talk,
or with speeches that do no good?
[4] Yes, you are doing away with the fear of God.
You are standing in the way of devotion to God.
[5] For your guilt teaches your mouth what to say.
You choose the language of the crafty.
[6] Your own mouth condemns you, not I.
Yes, your own lips speak against you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the second round of speeches begins. Eliphaz is no longer as polite as the first time.
> “The east wind” is the hot desert wind that brings only heat and dust. Eliphaz means: Your words are hot air.
> He accuses Job: With your complaints you are destroying faith. Because of you, others could lose their respect for God.
> Verses 5–6: Eliphaz says: Your words prove that you are guilty. Only a guilty person talks like that.

---

## Do you think you are wiser than everyone? (Verses 7–16)

[7] “Were you the first human being ever born?
Were you brought into being before the hills?
[8] Have you listened in on God’s secret council?
Do you keep wisdom all to yourself?
[9] What do you know that we don’t know?
What do you understand that we don’t understand too?
[10] We have gray-haired men among us, and very old men,
much older than your father.
[11] Are God’s comforts too small for you,
even the words that treat you gently?
[12] Why does your heart carry you away?
Why do your eyes flash,
[13] so that you turn your spirit against God
and let such words come out of your mouth?
[14] What is a human being, that he could be clean?
What is someone born of a woman, that he could be righteous?
[15] God does not even trust his holy ones.
Even the heavens are not clean in his eyes.
[16] How much less someone disgusting and corrupt,
a man who drinks wrongdoing like water!

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz mocks: Are you the first human being who ever lived? Were you there when God made the world? Do you think you are wiser than everyone?
> The irony: Later God will ask Job similar questions (Job 38:4). But not to condemn him.
> Verse 10: Eliphaz points to the wisdom of the old.
> Verse 11: Eliphaz thinks his own words are “God’s comforts” and gentle words. He does not notice how much he hurts Job.
> Verses 14–16 repeat the thought from Job 4:17–19.
> But now it is sharper: Verse 16 speaks of a man “who drinks wrongdoing like water.” Eliphaz probably means Job too.

---

## What happens to the wicked (Verses 17–35)

[17] “I will show you. Listen to me.
I will tell you what I have seen.
[18] Wise men have told it, as they learned it from their ancestors.
They have not hidden it.
[19] The land was given to those ancestors alone.
No foreigner moved among them.
[20] The wicked man twists in pain all his days,
all the years that are stored up for the oppressor.
[21] Terrifying sounds are in his ears.
When things are going well, the destroyer will come on him.
[22] He does not believe that he will come back out of the darkness.
The sword is waiting for him.
[23] He wanders around looking for bread and says, ‘Where is it?’
He knows that the day of darkness is close at hand.
[24] Distress and anguish frighten him.
They overpower him, like a king ready for battle.
[25] For he has stretched out his hand against God.
He has acted proudly against the Almighty.
[26] He charges at him stubbornly,
with his thick, heavy shields.
[27] For he has covered his face with his fat
and put on fat around his hips.
[28] He has lived in ruined cities,
in houses where no one lived,
houses that were about to become heaps of rubble.
[29] He will not stay rich.
His wealth will not last.
Their possessions will not spread over the earth.
[30] He will not escape the darkness.
The flame will dry up his branches.
He will be carried away by the breath of God’s mouth.
[31] Let him not trust in what is empty and so fool himself,
for emptiness will be his reward.
[32] It will happen before his time.
His branch will not be green.
[33] He will shake off his unripe grapes like a vine.
He will drop his blossoms like an olive tree.
[34] For the company of the godless will be barren.
Fire will burn up the tents of those who take bribes.
[35] They become pregnant with trouble and give birth to wrongdoing.
Their hearts prepare deceit.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz describes in detail what happens to the wicked: He lives in constant fear, loses everything, and dies early.
> He does not say it directly, but everyone understands: This is a picture of Job. Job is afraid, and he has lost everything. So he must be a wicked man.
> In the Bible, “fat” was often a picture for wealth and self-satisfaction.
> Some of this is true: People who do evil often live in fear. But Eliphaz makes it into a rule that is supposed to be true always. And he applies it to an innocent man.
> Verse 35 is a strong picture: The wicked “become pregnant with trouble.” Evil grows inside them like a child until it is born.
