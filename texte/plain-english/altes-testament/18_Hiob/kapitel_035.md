# Job – Chapter 35
### Elihu’s third speech: God is far above us

---

## What does our behavior do for God? (Verses 1–8)

[1] Elihu went on and said,
[2] “Do you think this is your right?
Do you say, ‘My righteousness is greater than God’s’?
[3] For you ask, ‘What good will it do you?
What do I gain, more than if I had sinned?’
[4] I will answer you,
and your friends with you.
[5] Look up at the skies and see!
Look at the clouds, which are higher than you!
[6] If you have sinned, what harm do you do to him?
If your wrongdoings are many, what do you do to him?
[7] If you are righteous, what do you give him?
What does he receive from your hand?
[8] Your evil may hurt a person like yourself.
Your righteousness may help another human being.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job had asked: What good does it do me not to sin? (compare Job 7:20; 10:14–15). Elihu answers by looking up: The sky is so high above you. God is much higher still.
> God does not depend on us. Our sin cannot harm him. Our righteousness does not make him richer.
> But verse 8 is important: What we do affects other people. Evil hurts people. Good helps them. That is why it matters how we live.

---

## Why God sometimes does not answer (Verses 9–16)

[9] “People cry out because there is so much oppression.
They cry for help because of the power of the mighty.
[10] But no one says, ‘Where is God my Maker,
who gives songs in the night,
[11] who teaches us more than the animals of the earth,
and makes us wiser than the birds of the sky?’
[12] There they cry out, but no one answers,
because of the pride of evil people.
[13] Surely God will not listen to an empty cry.
The Almighty will not pay attention to it.
[14] How much less when you say that you do not see him!
Your case is before him, and you are waiting for him!
[15] But now, because he has not punished in his anger,
and he does not pay much attention to arrogance,
[16] Job opens his mouth with empty talk.
He piles up words without knowledge.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu says: Many people cry out in their need. But they cry out only because of their pain, not for God himself. They do not ask: “Where is God my Maker?”
> “Who gives songs in the night” is a beautiful picture: Even in the middle of dark times, God can give comfort and even joy (compare Psalm 42:8; Acts 16:25).
> Verse 14 can also be read as a comfort: “Your case is before him, and you are waiting for him!” God has not forgotten Job’s case.
> Verses 15–16: Elihu thinks that God has not yet punished Job in anger. That is why Job talks so much. Here too Elihu is very strict. Many people in need did not just cry out with “empty talk.” They really searched for God, just as Job did.
