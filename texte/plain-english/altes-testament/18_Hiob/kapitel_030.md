# Job – Chapter 30
### “But now …”

---

## Now the despised make fun of me (Verses 1–8)

[1] “But now those who are younger than I am laugh at me.
I would not have thought their fathers good enough
to put with the dogs that guard my sheep.
[2] What use would the strength of their hands be to me?
They are men whose strength is gone.
[3] They are thin from need and hunger.
They gnaw on the dry ground,
in the gloom of waste and ruin.
[4] They pick salty plants among the bushes.
The roots of the broom bush are their food.
[5] They are driven out from among people.
People shout after them as if after a thief,
[6] so that they have to live in frightening valleys,
in holes in the ground and in the rocks.
[7] They bray like donkeys among the bushes.
They huddle together under the thorny weeds.
[8] They are children of fools, yes, children of wicked men.
They were whipped out of the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> “But now”: With these words the contrast to Chapter 29 begins. Job used to be honored. Now he is mocked.
> And by the poorest of the poor: by outcasts who live in caves and eat roots.
> These verses are hard to read, because Job speaks very scornfully about these people. We need to remember: Job is bitter and hurt. And now the very people who themselves live on the edge are kicking him.
> In Chapter 24, Job spoke about the poor with great compassion. Here we hear the pain of a man who has been humiliated. The Bible shows Job honestly, with all his feelings, even the ugly ones.
> “Children of wicked men”: The Hebrew literally says “children without a name.” That means people without respect and without a family that counts for anything.

---

## I have become a mocking song (Verses 9–15)

[9] “And now I have become their mocking song.
Yes, I have become a saying people use against me.
[10] They hate me. They keep away from me.
They do not hold back from spitting in my face.
[11] For he has loosened his bowstring and brought me low.
So they have thrown off all self-control in front of me.
[12] On my right side the mob rises up.
They push my feet away.
They build up their roads of destruction against me.
[13] They break up my path.
They push for my downfall, and no one has to help them.
[14] They come in as through a wide gap in a wall.
They roll in among the ruins.
[15] Terrors have turned against me.
They chase away my honor like the wind.
My well-being has passed by like a cloud.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job is mocked, spat on and attacked. He feels like a city whose wall has been broken through, so that the enemies come pouring in.
> “He has loosened his bowstring”: God has unstrung Job’s bow. That means he has taken away Job’s strength. When God brought Job low, people lost all respect for him.
> Something similar happened to Jesus: He was mocked and spat on (Mark 14:65; 15:19).

---

## God does not answer me (Verses 16–23)

[16] “And now my soul is poured out inside me.
Days of misery have taken hold of me.
[17] At night my bones are pierced inside me.
The gnawing pains never rest.
[18] My clothing is twisted out of shape by great force.
It is tight around me like the collar of my shirt.
[19] He has thrown me into the mud.
I have become like dust and ashes.
[20] I cry out to you, and you do not answer me.
I stand up, and you just stare at me.
[21] You have become cruel to me.
With the strength of your hand you hunt me down.
[22] You lift me up into the wind and let it drive me away.
You make me melt away in the storm.
[23] For I know that you will bring me to death,
to the house that is set for all the living.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job describes his physical pain: At night it bores into his bones. His clothes stick to his sick body.
> “Dust and ashes”: Job feels very small and worthless. At the end of the book he will use the same words, but in a completely different sense (Job 42:6).
> Verse 20 is the deepest pain: “I cry out to you, and you do not answer me.” God’s silence is worse than any illness. Many people know this feeling (compare Psalm 22:2; in some Bibles 22:3).
> Verse 21: Job even calls God “cruel.” Those are hard words. But he says them to God, not about God. He keeps talking with God.

---

## I hoped for good, but evil came (Verses 24–31)

[24] “But doesn’t someone stretch out his hand when he falls?
Doesn’t he cry for help in his disaster?
[25] Didn’t I weep for the one who was in trouble?
Wasn’t my soul sad for the needy?
[26] When I hoped for good, evil came.
When I waited for light, darkness came.
[27] My insides are churning and cannot rest.
Days of misery have come on me.
[28] I go around mourning, without the sun.
I stand up in the assembly and cry for help.
[29] I have become a brother to jackals
and a companion to ostriches.
[30] My skin turns black and peels off me.
My bones burn with fever.
[31] That is why my harp has turned to mourning,
and my flute to the sound of those who weep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: Job himself suffered along with others. He wept when others were having a hard time. Why does no one feel sorry for him now?
> Verse 26 describes a bitter experience: “When I hoped for good, evil came. When I waited for light, darkness came.”
> Jackals and ostriches live in the desert and make mournful sounds at night. Job feels like them: alone in the desert, crying out.
> Verse 31 is a sad picture: The instruments that used to play happy music now play only songs of mourning.
