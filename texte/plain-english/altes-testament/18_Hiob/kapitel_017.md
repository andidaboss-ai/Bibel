# Job – Chapter 17
### “Where then is my hope?”

---

## You be my guarantee! (Verses 1–5)

[1] “My spirit is used up.
My days are burned out.
The grave is ready for me.
[2] There are mockers all around me.
My eyes have to look at their insults.
[3] “Now give a pledge for me. Be my guarantee before yourself.
Who else will shake hands on it with me?
[4] For you have closed their hearts to understanding.
That is why you will not let them win.
[5] Whoever turns in his friends to get a share of the loot,
even the eyes of his children will fail.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job feels that death is near.
> Verse 3 is amazing: Job asks God himself to stand guarantee for him, before God! He has no one else to stand up for him. His friends do not help him. So he turns to God himself.
> “Shake hands on it” was a gesture to seal a guarantee.
> Verse 5 is probably a proverb: Whoever betrays his friends to gain something for himself will be punished. Job probably means his friends.

---

## A joke to everyone (Verses 6–10)

[6] “But he has made me a byword among the people,
a name they use to mock.
They spit in my face.
[7] My eyes have grown dim with grief.
My whole body is like a shadow.
[8] Honest people will be shocked by this.
The innocent will rise up against the godless.
[9] Yet the righteous will hold on to his way.
Whoever has clean hands will grow stronger and stronger.
[10] But as for all of you, come back again!
I will not find a wise man among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job has become so thin that he is only a shadow of himself. People spit in his face.
> Verse 9 is a ray of light: “Yet the righteous will hold on to his way. Whoever has clean hands will grow stronger and stronger.” This is a confession that applies to Job himself too. He does not give up.
> Verse 10: Job challenges his friends: Come on, keep talking, but you have no wisdom.

---

## Where is my hope? (Verses 11–16)

[11] “My days are over.
My plans are torn apart,
and so are the wishes of my heart.
[12] They turn night into day.
‘The light is near,’ they say, when it is dark.
[13] If I look to Sheol (the world of the dead) as my home,
if I have made my bed in the darkness,
[14] if I have said to decay, ‘You are my father,’
and to the worm, ‘My mother’ and ‘My sister,’
[15] where then is my hope?
And my hope – who will see it?
[16] Will it go down with me to the gates of Sheol?
Will we go down into the dust together?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The friends want to talk Job into believing that everything will soon be fine. “The light is near!” But Job sees only darkness. False optimism does not help people who are suffering.
> Verses 13–14 are very dark: The grave is his home, decay is his father, the worm is his mother and sister. He has no other family anymore.
> Verse 15: “Where then is my hope?” That is the question of the whole book. Will his hope go into the grave with him?
> The chapter ends without an answer. But that is exactly what is honest about it. Sometimes we have no answer. And still Job asks the question, and he asks it to God.
