# Job – Chapter 10
### “Your hands made me”

---

## Why do you fight against me? (Verses 1–7)

[1] “I am sick of my life.
I will let my complaint pour out freely.
I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.
[2] I will say to God,
‘Do not condemn me!
Show me why you are fighting against me.
[3] Do you think it is good to oppress me,
to reject the work of your own hands,
and to smile on the plans of the wicked?
[4] Do you have human eyes?
Do you see things the way a human being sees them?
[5] Are your days like the days of a mortal,
or your years like the years of a man,
[6] that you search for my guilt
and hunt for my sin?
[7] You know that I am not wicked.
And there is no one who can rescue anyone from your hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job wants to bring his complaint before God. He would at least like to know why he is suffering.
> “The work of your own hands”: Job reminds God that God himself created him. How can God destroy his own work?
> Verses 4–6: Job asks: Are you like a human being who looks for mistakes because he does not know everything? You know everything! You know that I am not wicked!
> This is great trust in the middle of a lament: God knows that I am innocent.

---

## You made me with such love (Verses 8–12)

[8] “‘Your hands shaped me and formed me completely,
and yet you are destroying me.
[9] Please remember that you shaped me like clay.
Will you turn me back into dust?
[10] Didn’t you pour me out like milk
and let me thicken like cheese?
[11] You clothed me with skin and flesh.
You wove me together with bones and sinews.
[12] You gave me life and loving kindness.
Your care has kept my spirit alive.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a wonderful picture of how God forms a human being in the mother’s womb. Like a potter, he shapes the clay. Like a weaver, he weaves bones and sinews together (compare Psalm 139:13–16).
> “Pour me out like milk and let me thicken like cheese”: Back then, people imagined that this is how a child comes into being in the womb.
> Job knows: God made me with love. He gave me life and kindness. That is exactly why he does not understand why God is destroying him now.
> These verses show: Every human being is lovingly formed by God.

---

## You hid it in your heart (Verses 13–17)

[13] “‘Yet you hid these things in your heart.
I know that this is what you had in mind:
[14] If I sin, you watch me closely.
You will not declare me free from my guilt.
[15] If I am wicked, how terrible for me!
And if I am in the right, I still cannot lift up my head.
I am full of shame, and I see my misery.
[16] If I hold my head high, you hunt me like a lion.
Again you show your mighty power against me.
[17] You keep bringing new witnesses against me.
Your anger against me keeps growing.
Wave after wave of attack comes against me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job suspects: From the beginning God had in mind to torment him. This is a terrible thought that comes from his pain.
> Whatever he does, he feels lost: If he sins, he is punished. If he is in the right, he is still put to shame.
> “New witnesses”: His illnesses and losses are like witnesses in a trial, who speak against him.

---

## Why did you let me be born? (Verses 18–22)

[18] “‘Why then did you bring me out of the womb?
I wish I had died, and no eye had seen me!
[19] Then I would be as if I had never been.
I would have been carried from the womb straight to the grave.
[20] Aren’t my days few? Stop!
Leave me alone, so that I can find a little comfort,
[21] before I go to the place from which I will not come back,
to the land of darkness and of the shadow of death,
[22] the land as dark as midnight,
of the shadow of death, without any order,
where even the light is like midnight.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Job returns to his lament from Chapter 3: Why did I have to be born?
> He asks God for only one thing: Leave me alone a little, so that I can find some comfort before I die.
> He describes the world of the dead as a dark land without order. Even the light there is dark.
