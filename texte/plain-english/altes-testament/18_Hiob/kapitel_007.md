# Job – Chapter 7
### “What is a human being?”

---

## Life is a breath (Verses 1–10)

[1] “Isn’t a human being forced to do hard labor on earth?
Aren’t his days like the days of a hired worker?
[2] Like a slave who longs for the shade,
like a hired worker who waits for his pay,
[3] so I have been given months of misery.
Exhausting nights have been handed to me.
[4] When I lie down, I say,
‘When can I get up? When will the night be over?’
I toss and turn until the dawn.
[5] My body is covered with worms and clumps of dirt.
My skin closes up and then breaks open again.
[6] My days go by faster than a weaver’s shuttle.
They come to an end without hope.
[7] Oh, remember that my life is only a breath.
My eyes will never again see anything good.
[8] The eyes of those who see me now will see me no more.
Your eyes will look for me, but I will be gone.
[9] As a cloud fades and disappears,
so whoever goes down to Sheol, the world of the dead, will not come up again.
[10] He will never come back to his house.
His home will not know him anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job no longer speaks to his friends now, but to God. “Oh, remember …”
> He describes his misery: He cannot sleep. His skin is full of wounds that do not heal. Time passes quickly, but without hope.
> A weaver’s “shuttle” flies quickly back and forth during weaving. That is how fast the days go by.
> In Hebrew, the world of the dead is called “Sheol.” People imagined it as a dark place under the earth, where the dead are. Job sees no hope of coming back from there. Only later in the Bible does the hope of a resurrection grow.
> Verse 8 is bitter: God will look for Job, but Job will no longer be there.

## Why do you guard me? (Verses 11–16)

[11] “So I will not keep quiet.
I will speak in the anguish of my spirit.
I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.
[12] Am I the sea, or a sea monster,
that you put a guard over me?
[13] When I say, ‘My bed will comfort me,
my couch will ease my complaint,’
[14] then you frighten me with dreams
and terrify me with visions.
[15] So I would rather be strangled.
I would rather die than live on in these bones.
[16] I hate my life. I don’t want to live forever.
Leave me alone, for my days are only a breath.

> **What does this mean?**
> “I will not keep quiet”: Job decides to lament honestly. He does not swallow his pain.
> In old stories, the sea and the sea monster were pictures for the chaos that God had to tame. Job asks: Am I so dangerous that you guard me so strictly?
> He does not find rest even in his sleep. Nightmares torment him.
> Verses 15–16 are very dark words. Job does not want to live anymore. But he says this to God. He keeps talking with him. This is important: Even in the deepest despair, you can talk with God.

> If you yourself feel this way and don’t want to live anymore: You are not alone. Please talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## What is a human being? (Verses 17–21)

[17] “What is a human being, that you make so much of him,
that you pay so much attention to him,
[18] that you check on him every morning
and test him every moment?
[19] How long will you keep staring at me?
Won’t you leave me alone
even long enough to swallow my spit?
[20] If I have sinned, what harm does that do to you,
you who watch over people?
Why have you made me your target,
so that I have become a burden to myself?
[21] Why don’t you forgive my wrongdoing
and take away my guilt?
For soon I will lie down in the dust.
You will search hard for me, but I will be gone.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17 reminds us of Psalm 8:4: “What is a human being, that you think of him?” In the psalm, this is praise full of wonder: God cares about small human beings! Job turns it around: Why don’t you leave me alone? Your attention is torture for me.
> “Long enough to swallow my spit”: God does not leave him alone for even a moment.
> Job does not say that he has sinned. He says: Even if I had sinned, why don’t you simply forgive me?
> The last sentence is amazing: “You will search hard for me.” Deep in his heart, Job knows that God will miss him. He still believes that God loves him, even if he cannot feel it right now.
