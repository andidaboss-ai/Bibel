# Job – Chapter 38
### God answers out of the whirlwind

---

## God speaks (Verses 1–3)

[1] Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind.
He said,
[2] “Who is this who makes my plan dark
with words without knowledge?
[3] Get ready like a man!
I will ask you questions, and you will answer me!

> **What does this mean?**
> At last God answers! Job has been waiting for this through the whole book.
> Important: Here God’s name “the LORD” appears again (in Hebrew: YHWH). In the speeches before, God was mostly called only “God” or “the Almighty.” Now Job meets the God of Israel in person.
> God comes “out of the whirlwind.” This is a reminder of other meetings with God: at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19) or with Elijah (1 Kings 19).
> God accuses Job of having spoken “without knowledge.” But: God does not say that Job has sinned. He does not repeat the friends’ accusations.
> “Get ready like a man”: Word for word, the Hebrew says: Tie up your clothing around your waist like a man. In those days, people tied up their long robes before they worked or fought. It means: Get ready! Job wanted to ask God questions. Now God asks Job questions.

---

## Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? (Verses 4–7)

[4] “Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?
Tell me, if you have understanding!
[5] Who decided how big it would be? Surely you know!
Who stretched the measuring line over it?
[6] What were its foundations set on?
Who laid its cornerstone,
[7] when the morning stars sang together,
and all the sons of God shouted for joy?

> **What does this mean?**
> God describes the creation of the world like building a house: deciding the size, stretching the measuring line, laying the foundations, setting the cornerstone.
> And Job was not there. He does not know the plan by which the world was built.
> Verse 7: a beautiful picture. When the world was finished, the stars and the angels (“sons of God,” compare Job 1:6) rejoiced. Creation was a reason for joy.

---

## The sea has its boundary (Verses 8–11)

[8] “Or who shut up the sea behind doors,
when it burst out of the womb,
[9] when I made the clouds its clothing,
and wrapped it in thick darkness like a baby’s cloth,
[10] when I set my boundary for it,
and put bars and doors in place,
[11] and said, ‘You may come this far, but no farther.
Your proud waves must stop here’?

> **What does this mean?**
> For people in those days, the sea was dangerous and frightening. It stood for chaos and threat.
> But God describes the sea like a newborn baby: It comes out of the womb, and God wraps it in clouds as in baby cloths.
> God is stronger than the sea. He has set it a boundary: “You may come this far, but no farther.” Chaos does not go on without limits. God has it under control.

---

## Have you given orders to the morning? (Verses 12–15)

[12] “Have you ever in your life given orders to the morning?
Have you shown the dawn its place,
[13] so that it takes hold of the ends of the earth,
and shakes the wicked out of it?
[14] The earth changes like clay under a seal.
It stands out like a piece of clothing.
[15] The wicked are kept from their light.
The arm raised to strike is broken.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every morning the sun rises. Who ordered that? Not Job.
> The morning light grabs the earth like a blanket by its corners and shakes the criminals out. For many crimes happen at night (compare Job 24:13–17).
> Verse 14: When the light comes, things get their shape and color again, just as soft clay gets a picture from a seal stamp.
> This is an answer to Job’s question about justice: God has already built it into the order of the world that evil is limited.

---

## Do you know the depths and the light? (Verses 16–21)

[16] “Have you gone down to the springs of the sea?
Have you walked through the hidden places of the deep?
[17] Have the gates of death been shown to you?
Have you seen the gates of the shadow of death?
[18] Have you understood how wide the earth is?
Tell me, if you know all this!
[19] “What is the way to the home of light?
And darkness, where does it live,
[20] so that you could take it to its border,
and know the paths to its house?
[21] Surely you know, for you were born back then,
and you have lived so many days!

> **What does this mean?**
> God asks about the deepest depths of the sea, about the world of the dead and about the size of the earth. Job knows nothing about any of it.
> Verse 21 is meant ironically: “Surely you know, for you were born back then, and you have lived so many days!” Of course Job was not there at creation.
> God is not angry or hurtful, but he makes it clear: Job sees only a small part of the whole.

---

## Snow, hail, rain and ice (Verses 22–30)

[22] “Have you gone into the storerooms of the snow?
Have you seen the storerooms of the hail,
[23] which I have kept for the time of trouble,
for the day of battle and war?
[24] By what way is the lightning spread out?
How is the east wind scattered over the earth?
[25] Who has cut a channel for the floodwaters,
or a path for the thunderstorm,
[26] to make it rain on a land where no one lives,
on the wilderness, where there are no people,
[27] to water the empty, ruined ground,
and to make the tender grass grow?
[28] Does the rain have a father?
Who is the father of the drops of dew?
[29] Whose womb did the ice come out of?
Who gave birth to the gray frost of the sky?
[30] The waters become hard like stone,
when the surface of the deep is frozen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: “Lightning”: The Hebrew word literally means “light.” That is why many Bibles translate “the light” here.
> Verses 26–27 are especially beautiful: God makes it rain even where no one lives. He takes care of the desert and the little grass, even when no human being gets anything out of it.
> That means: The world does not turn only around human beings. God takes joy in all of his creation. This view is new for Job.
> Verses 28–29: Rain, dew, ice and frost have no human parents. God alone brings them about.

---

## The stars (Verses 31–33)

[31] “Can you tie together the cluster of the Pleiades?
Can you loosen the ropes of Orion?
[32] Can you lead out the constellations in their season?
Can you guide the Bear with her cubs?
[33] Do you know the laws of the heavens?
Can you set up their rule over the earth?

> **What does this mean?**
> The Pleiades (the Seven Sisters), Orion and the Great Bear (the Big Dipper) are well-known groups of stars. You can still see them in the night sky today.
> God has given them their paths. They follow fixed “laws of the heavens.” No human being can move them.
> Job himself had already spoken about these constellations (Job 9:9). Now God asks him: Can you guide them?

---

## Clouds, lightning and wisdom (Verses 34–38)

[34] “Can you lift up your voice to the clouds,
so that plenty of water covers you?
[35] Can you send out lightning, so that it goes?
Does it report to you, ‘Here we are’?
[36] Who has put wisdom deep inside people?
Who has given understanding to the mind?
[37] Who is wise enough to count the clouds?
Who can tip over the water jars of the sky,
[38] when the dust runs together into a mass,
and the clods of earth stick together?

> **What does this mean?**
> Can Job order rain? Do the lightning bolts obey him like soldiers who report: “Here we are!”? No.
> Verse 36: Human wisdom also comes from God. What Job knows, he has received from God.
> Verses 37–38: The clouds are like jars from which God pours water. After that, the dry dust turns into moist clods of earth.

---

## Food for lions and ravens (Verses 39–41)

[39] “Can you hunt prey for the lioness?
Can you satisfy the hunger of the young lions,
[40] when they crouch in their dens,
and lie in wait in the thick bushes?
[41] Who provides food for the raven,
when its young ones cry to God,
and wander around because they have no food?

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God moves from lifeless nature to the animals. This goes on in Chapter 39.
> God takes care of the wild animals: the strong lions as well as the young ravens that cry for food.
> Jesus later takes this up: “Consider the ravens … And God feeds them” (Luke 12:24). If God even takes care of the ravens, he takes care of you too.
