# Job – Chapter 37
### Elihu: God’s greatness in the thunderstorm

---

## Hear God’s voice in the thunder (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Yes, my heart trembles at this.
It jumps out of its place.
[2] Listen, oh, listen to the roar of his voice,
the sound that comes out of his mouth!
[3] He sends it out under the whole sky.
He sends his lightning to the ends of the earth.
[4] After it, a voice roars.
He thunders with his majestic voice.
He holds nothing back when his voice is heard.
[5] God thunders wonderfully with his voice.
He does great things that we cannot understand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu keeps on speaking. A thunderstorm is coming up, and Elihu is deeply impressed. His heart trembles.
> In the thunder he hears God’s voice. This is an old picture in the Bible (compare Psalm 29, which says that the voice of the LORD is over the waters).
> Verse 5: “He does great things that we cannot understand.” That is the heart of Elihu’s conclusion: God is greater than our minds.

---

## Snow, rain, ice (Verses 6–13)

[6] “For he says to the snow, ‘Fall on the earth!’
He says the same to the rain shower,
and to the showers of his heavy rain.
[7] He seals up the hand of every person,
so that all the people he has made may know it.
[8] Then the animals take cover.
They stay in their dens.
[9] The storm comes out of its room,
and cold comes out of the north.
[10] By the breath of God, ice is made.
The wide waters freeze over.
[11] Yes, he loads the thick cloud with moisture.
He spreads out the cloud of his lightning.
[12] It turns around and around as he guides it,
so that the clouds do whatever he commands them
on the face of the inhabited world.
[13] He makes it come,
whether to correct people, or for his land, or out of loving kindness.

> **What does this mean?**
> God commands the weather, and it obeys him.
> Verse 7: “He seals up the hand of every person”: In snow and heavy rain, people cannot work in the fields. Their hands rest. Then they notice that God is above them.
> Verse 13: God sends the weather for different purposes: to warn, to water the land, or simply out of love and goodness.
> The Hebrew word for loving kindness is “chesed.” It means God’s faithful, loving care.

---

## Job, stand still and be amazed (Verses 14–20)

[14] “Listen to this, Job!
Stand still and think about God’s wonderful works!
[15] Do you know how God controls them,
and makes the lightning of his cloud flash?
[16] Do you know how the clouds float,
the wonderful works of the one who is perfect in knowledge?
[17] Your clothes are hot
when the earth is still because of the south wind.
[18] Can you, together with him, spread out the sky,
which is as hard as a mirror made of cast metal?
[19] Teach us what we should say to him,
for we cannot set out our case because of the darkness.
[20] Should he be told that I want to speak?
Or would anyone wish to be swallowed up?

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu calls on Job: Stop arguing. Stand still and be amazed.
> He asks Job questions: Do you know how the clouds float? Can you spread out the sky? These are questions that God himself will soon ask in Chapter 38.
> Verse 17: The hot south wind (from the desert) makes everything still and muggy. People can only sweat. They cannot change the weather.
> Verse 18: In those days, people pictured the sky as a solid, shining dome. Mirrors back then were made of polished metal, not glass.
> Verses 19–20: Elihu means: We human beings are groping in the dark. Who would want to argue with God then? Whoever tries such a thing will be “swallowed up.”

---

## Golden splendor from the north (Verses 21–24)

[21] “Right now people cannot see the light that is bright in the skies.
But the wind passes by and clears the clouds away.
[22] Golden splendor comes out of the north.
God has awesome majesty.
[23] We cannot reach the Almighty.
He is lifted high in power.
In justice and great righteousness he will not oppress anyone.
[24] That is why people have deep respect for him.
He pays no attention to anyone who is wise in his own heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> After the storm, the sun comes out again. The wind has blown the clouds away.
> “Golden splendor comes out of the north”: In the ancient Near East, people often pictured God’s home in the north. God’s glory shines brightly like gold.
> Verse 23 sums up Elihu’s message: God is so great that no one can reach him. But he is just and oppresses no one.
> Verse 24: Whoever thinks he is wise does not impress God. Deep respect for God is the beginning of wisdom (compare Job 28:28).
> These are Elihu’s last words. From now on, God himself speaks.
