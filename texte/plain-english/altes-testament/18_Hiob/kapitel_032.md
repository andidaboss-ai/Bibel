# Job – Chapter 32
### A young man speaks up: Elihu

---

## Elihu is angry (Verses 1–5)

[1] So these three men stopped answering Job,
because he was righteous in his own eyes.
[2] Then Elihu became very angry.
He was the son of Barachel the Buzite,
from the family of Ram.
He was angry with Job,
because Job made himself out to be right rather than God.
[3] He was also angry with Job’s three friends,
because they had found no answer,
and yet they had condemned Job.
[4] Elihu had waited before speaking to Job,
because they were older than he was.
[5] When Elihu saw that these three men had no answer left,
he became very angry.

> **What does this mean?**
> Surprisingly, a new speaker appears: Elihu. He was never mentioned before. He is younger than the others and has been listening the whole time.
> Elihu is angry with both sides:
> – with Job, because Job made himself out to be more righteous than God,
> – with the friends, because they condemned Job without having a convincing answer.
> Elihu is the only speaker in the book with a Hebrew name. It means: “He is my God.”
> Elihu speaks from Chapter 32 to Chapter 37. After that, God himself answers. Interesting: At the end of the book, God neither praises nor blames Elihu. Scholars do not agree on how his speeches should be understood.

---

## Wisdom does not come only with age (Verses 6–10)

[6] Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered:
“I am young, and you are very old.
That is why I held back.
I did not dare to tell you what I think.
[7] I said, ‘Age should speak.
Many years should teach wisdom.’
[8] But there is a spirit in people,
and the breath of the Almighty gives them understanding.
[9] It is not the great who are wise.
It is not the old who understand what is right.
[10] That is why I said, ‘Listen to me!
I too will tell you what I think.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu is polite: He waited because the others are older. That was the custom in those days.
> But then he says something important: Wisdom does not come automatically with age. It comes from the Spirit of God. A young person can also be wise when God’s Spirit gives him understanding (compare 1 Timothy 4:12).
> This is a criticism of the friends, who always appealed to age and tradition (Job 8:8; 15:10).

---

## You did not convince Job (Verses 11–17)

[11] “Look, I waited for your words.
I listened to your reasons,
while you were searching for what to say.
[12] Yes, I paid close attention to you.
But none of you proved Job wrong.
None of you answered his words.
[13] Be careful not to say, ‘We have found wisdom.
God may prove him wrong, not a human being.’
[14] For he has not aimed his words at me.
And I will not answer him with your speeches.
[15] They are shocked. They do not answer anymore.
They have nothing left to say.
[16] Should I wait because they do not speak,
because they just stand there and answer no more?
[17] I too will give my answer.
I too will tell you what I think.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu listened carefully. His verdict: The friends did not convince Job. They gave no answer to his questions.
> Verse 13: Elihu warns the friends not to make excuses with: “God may prove him wrong, not a human being.”
> Verse 14: Elihu wants to bring new arguments, not repeat the old ones.

---

## I am full of words (Verses 18–22)

[18] “For I am full of words.
The spirit inside me pushes me.
[19] Look, my insides are like wine that has no opening.
Like new wineskins, they are about to burst.
[20] I will speak, so that I may feel better.
I will open my lips and answer.
[21] Please, let me not take sides with anyone.
I will not flatter anyone.
[22] For I do not know how to flatter.
If I did, my Maker would soon take me away.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu cannot stay silent any longer. He is like a wineskin in which the fermenting wine builds up so much pressure that it is about to burst.
> Some readers find Elihu a bit arrogant: He talks for a very long time about the fact that he is now going to talk. Others see in him a young man with real zeal for God.
> Verses 21–22: Elihu wants to be honest and fair to all. He does not want to flatter anyone, neither Job nor the friends.
