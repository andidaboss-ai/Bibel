# Job – Chapter 5
### Eliphaz: “Turn to God”

---

## The fool and his trouble (Verses 1–7)

[1] “Call out if you want! Is there anyone who will answer you?
To which of the holy ones will you turn?
[2] For anger kills the foolish man,
and jealousy kills the simple one.
[3] I have seen the fool putting down roots.
But suddenly I cursed his home.
[4] His children are far from safety.
They are crushed in the town gate.
There is no one to rescue them.
[5] The hungry eat up his harvest.
They take it even from among the thorns.
The trap opens wide to catch their wealth.
[6] For trouble does not come out of the dust.
Hardship does not grow out of the ground.
[7] But a human being is born to hardship,
as surely as sparks fly upward.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The holy ones” are angels. Eliphaz means: No angel will help you if you rebel against God.
> Eliphaz warns Job: Whoever gets angry and furious harms himself.
> Verse 4 is cruel when you think of Job: Eliphaz speaks of a fool whose children die. Job’s children have just died! Eliphaz does not notice how much he hurts Job.
> “The town gate” was the place where court was held. Without anyone to protect them, the children were treated unfairly there.
> Verse 7 is a well-known sentence: “A human being is born to hardship, as surely as sparks fly upward.” Suffering is part of being human. It is as natural as sparks rising.

---

## If I were you, I would seek God (Verses 8–16)

[8] “But as for me, I would seek God.
I would put my case in God’s hands.
[9] He does great things that no one can understand,
wonders that cannot be counted.
[10] He gives rain to the earth
and sends water on the fields.
[11] So he sets the lowly up high,
and those who mourn are lifted up to safety.
[12] He spoils the plans of the crafty,
so that their hands cannot carry out what they planned.
[13] He catches the wise in their own craftiness.
The plans of the cunning are swept away.
[14] They run into darkness in the daytime.
At noon they feel their way as if it were night.
[15] But he saves the needy from the sword of their mouth,
and from the hand of the mighty.
[16] So the poor have hope,
and injustice shuts its mouth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz says many true things about God: He is great, he does wonders, he helps the lowly and the poor.
> Paul quotes verse 13 in the New Testament: “He has caught the wise in their own tricks” (1 Corinthians 3:19). So the words are not wrong.
> The problem is not what Eliphaz says, but how he applies it to Job. He gives wise advice without really listening.
> “If I were you, I would …”: People often say this to those who suffer. But no one is ever really in someone else’s place.

---

## Happy is the one whom God corrects (Verses 17–27)

[17] “Happy is the person whom God corrects.
So do not reject the discipline of the Almighty!
[18] For he wounds, and he bandages.
He injures, and his hands heal.
[19] He will rescue you in six troubles.
Yes, in seven troubles no harm will touch you.
[20] In famine he will save you from death,
and in war from the power of the sword.
[21] You will be hidden from the whip of the tongue.
You will not be afraid of destruction when it comes.
[22] You will laugh at destruction and famine.
You will not be afraid of the wild animals.
[23] For you will be in league with the stones of the field.
The wild animals will be at peace with you.
[24] You will know that your tent is safe.
You will check your sheep pen and find nothing missing.
[25] You will also know that you will have many children,
and your descendants will be like the grass of the earth.
[26] You will come to your grave at a ripe old age,
like a bundle of grain that is brought in at harvest time.
[27] We have looked into this. It is true.
Listen to it, and take it to heart for your own good.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz says: Your suffering is a discipline from God. If you accept it, everything will be good again. God will bless you.
> This is also said in other places in the Bible (Proverbs 3:11–12; Hebrews 12:5–11). Suffering can make a person grow.
> But for Job this is no comfort. God has said that Job suffers “without any reason” (Job 2:3). It is not discipline.
> Verse 25 is especially painful: Eliphaz promises Job many children. But Job has just lost all his children. New children cannot replace the ones he lost.
> Verse 27 shows Eliphaz’s attitude: “We have looked into this. It is true.” He is completely sure. He leaves no room for questions. That is the problem with people who have an answer for everything.
