# Job – Chapter 31
### Job’s oath of innocence

---

## A covenant with my eyes (Verses 1–4)

[1] “I made a covenant with my eyes.
So how could I look at a young woman with desire?
[2] For what would my share from God above be then?
What would I inherit from the Almighty on high?
[3] Isn’t it disaster for the unrighteous,
and ruin for those who do wrong?
[4] Doesn’t he see my ways
and count all my steps?

> **What does this mean?**
> In this chapter, Job takes a solemn oath. He goes through many possible sins and says each time: If I have done this, let a curse strike me. In those days, this was how people declared their innocence in court.
> Job does not begin with deeds, but with thoughts. “A covenant with my eyes”: A covenant is a binding agreement. Job has committed himself not even to look at a woman with desire.
> This shows how seriously Job takes God’s will. Later Jesus says something similar: Even a look of desire is adultery in the heart (Matthew 5:28).
> Verse 4: Job knows that God sees all his ways. So he lives honestly, even when no one is watching.

---

## Honesty (Verses 5–8)

[5] “If I have gone along with lies,
if my feet have hurried to cheat
[6] (let God weigh me on honest scales,
so that he may know my integrity),
[7] if my steps have turned off the way,
if my heart has followed my eyes,
if any stain has stuck to my hands,
[8] then let me sow, and let someone else eat.
Yes, let the crops of my field be pulled up by the roots.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job asks: Let God weigh me on honest scales. Then he will see that I am honest.
> “My heart has followed my eyes”: if I wanted to have everything I saw.
> The punishment Job wishes on himself: What he has sown, someone else should harvest.

---

## Faithful in marriage (Verses 9–12)

[9] “If my heart has been lured by a woman,
and I have lain in wait at my neighbor’s door,
[10] then let my wife grind grain for another man,
and let other men sleep with her.
[11] For that would be a terrible crime.
Yes, it would be a sin that the judges must punish.
[12] For it is a fire that burns everything up to destruction.
It would pull up by the roots all that I have gained.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job was faithful to his wife. He never seduced another man’s wife.
> Verse 10 is hard for today’s readers: As a punishment, Job wishes that his wife would have to serve another man. In the ancient world, this was seen as the worst shame for a man. Today we see: The wife would be punished for her husband’s guilt. That is unjust. Here the Bible reports Job’s words and the way people thought in his time. It does not make a law out of it.
> Verse 12: Adultery is like a fire that destroys everything: family, trust and property (compare Proverbs 6:27–29).

---

## Fairness to male and female servants (Verses 13–15)

[13] “Suppose I had not respected the rights of my male servant or my female servant
when they had a complaint against me.
[14] What would I do then when God rises up?
When he comes to look into it, what would I answer him?
[15] Didn’t the one who made me in the womb make him too?
Didn’t the same God form us both in the womb?

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an amazing section for that time. Job treated his male and female servants as people of equal worth. When they complained to him, he listened to them.
> His reason in verse 15 is wonderful: The same God made me and my servant in the womb. Before God we are equal.
> Here we see an idea that later became very important: All people have the same dignity, because God created them all (compare Malachi 2:10; Galatians 3:28).

---

## Caring for the poor (Verses 16–23)

[16] “If I have kept back from the poor what they wished for,
or have let the widow’s eyes grow tired from waiting,
[17] or if I have eaten my bit of food alone,
and the orphan has not eaten any of it
[18] (no, from my youth the orphan grew up with me as with a father,
and from my mother’s womb I have guided the widow);
[19] if I have seen anyone dying because he had no clothes,
or a needy person with no cover;
[20] if his heart has not blessed me,
if he has not been warmed with the wool of my sheep;
[21] if I have raised my hand against the orphan,
because I saw that I had support at the city gate;
[22] then let my shoulder fall from my shoulder blade.
Let my arm be broken off at the bone.
[23] For disaster from God terrifies me.
Because of his majesty I can do nothing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job shared his food with orphans. He gave clothes to the poor. He never used his power in court (“at the city gate”) against the weak.
> This is the answer to Eliphaz’s accusations in Chapter 22. Job did exactly the opposite.
> “Eaten my bit of food alone”: Job never ate without thinking of the hungry.
> The punishment he wishes on himself: The arm that would have been raised against the orphan should be broken.

---

## No trust in money and no other gods (Verses 24–28)

[24] “If I have made gold my hope,
and have said to fine gold, ‘You are what I trust in,’
[25] if I have been glad because my wealth was great,
and because my hand had gained so much,
[26] if I have looked at the sun when it was shining,
or at the moon moving along in its splendor,
[27] and my heart has been secretly lured,
so that my hand threw them a kiss from my mouth,
[28] then this too would be a sin that the judges must punish,
for I would have denied the God who is above.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job was very rich, but he did not put his trust in money. Money was not his god (compare Matthew 6:24).
> In those days, many peoples worshiped the sun and the moon. People threw them a kiss with their hand as a sign of worship. Job never did that. He worshiped only the one God.
> Greed for money and worshiping idols belong together: Both mean putting something other than God in first place.

---

## No joy at others’ misfortune, open doors (Verses 29–34)

[29] “If I have been glad when the one who hated me was destroyed,
or felt proud when evil came on him
[30] (no, I have not let my mouth sin
by calling down a curse on his life);
[31] if the men of my household have not said,
‘Who can find anyone who has not had his fill of Job’s meat?’
[32] (the foreigner did not have to spend the night in the street,
but I opened my doors to the traveler);
[33] if, like Adam, I have covered up my wrongdoings
by hiding my guilt in my heart,
[34] because I was afraid of the great crowd,
and the contempt of families terrified me,
so that I kept silent and did not go out of my door –

> **What does this mean?**
> Job was not glad when things went badly for his enemies. He did not even curse them. That goes very far. Later Jesus teaches: “Love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44).
> He was very welcoming to guests: Everyone got food at his house. No stranger had to sleep in the street.
> Verse 33: “Like Adam” (or: “as people do”): Adam hid after his sin (Genesis 3:8–10). Job did not hide anything. He admitted his mistakes, even when he was afraid of what people would think.

---

## Here is my signature! (Verses 35–37)

[35] “Oh, if only I had someone to hear me!
Look, here is my signature!
Let the Almighty answer me!
Let my accuser write down the charges against me!
[36] I would surely carry them on my shoulder.
I would tie them on like a crown.
[37] I would tell him the number of my steps.
I would come near to him like a prince.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point of Job’s speeches. He has taken his oath. Now he “signs” it, the way people sign a statement in court.
> And he challenges God: Answer me! Write down your charges!
> Job is so sure that he is innocent that he would wear the list of charges proudly, like a crown. He would come before God “like a prince,” not like a criminal.
> Now only God can answer. And God will answer, but differently from what Job expects (Chapter 38).

---

## The land does not accuse me (Verses 38–40)

[38] “If my land cries out against me,
and its furrows weep together,
[39] if I have eaten its crops without paying,
or have caused its owners to lose their lives,
[40] then let thorns grow instead of wheat,
and stinking weeds instead of barley.”
The words of Job are ended.

> **What does this mean?**
> Finally, one last point: Job did not get his land unjustly. He paid the workers and did not drive anyone away.
> “My land cries out”: Just as Abel’s blood cried out from the ground (Genesis 4:10), the soil would also cry out against injustice.
> “The words of Job are ended”: Job has said everything. He will only speak again after God has spoken (Job 40:3–5; 42:1–6).
