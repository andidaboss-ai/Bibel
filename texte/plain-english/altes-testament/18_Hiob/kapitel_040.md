# Job – Chapter 40
### Job falls silent – God’s second speech: behemoth

---

## Job puts his hand over his mouth (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD went on and answered Job:
[2] “Will the one who argues fight with the Almighty?
Whoever argues with God, let him answer!”
[3] Then Job answered the LORD:
[4] “Look, I am too small.
What can I answer you?
I put my hand over my mouth.
[5] I have spoken once, and I will not answer again.
Yes, I have spoken twice, but I will not say anything more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God calls on Job: You wanted to argue with me. Now answer!
> And Job, who has talked so much, falls silent. “I put my hand over my mouth” is a gesture. It means: I am silent. I have nothing more to say.
> Job does not say: I have sinned. He says: I am too small. He has seen how great God is.
> But this is not the end yet. God speaks a second time.

---

## Will you set aside my justice? (Verses 6–14)

[6] Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind:
[7] “Now get ready like a man!
I will ask you questions, and you will answer me.
[8] Will you even set aside my judgment?
Will you condemn me, so that you can be in the right?
[9] Do you have an arm like God’s?
Can you thunder with a voice like his?
[10] “Then dress yourself in greatness and dignity!
Clothe yourself with honor and majesty!
[11] Pour out your burning anger!
Look at everyone who is proud, and bring him down!
[12] Look at everyone who is proud, and humble him!
Crush the wicked where they stand!
[13] Hide them all together in the dust!
Tie up their faces in the hidden place!
[14] Then I too will admit to you
that your own right hand can save you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 is the heart of God’s second speech: Job, to show that you are in the right, you have called me unjust. That goes too far.
> God does not say that Job has sinned and is suffering because of it. But he rebukes Job for accusing God.
> Then God makes an offer, with irony: Go ahead, sit on my throne! You make the world just! Bring down all the proud and the wicked!
> Job had complained that God does not punish the wicked. God says: You try it. Ruling the world is harder than you think.

---

## Behemoth (Verses 15–24)

[15] “Look now at behemoth,
which I made just as I made you.
He eats grass like an ox.
[16] Look now, his strength is in his hips.
His power is in the muscles of his belly.
[17] He swings his tail like a cedar tree.
The sinews of his thighs are tightly woven together.
[18] His bones are like pipes of bronze.
His legs are like bars of iron.
[19] He is the first among the works of God.
The one who made him gives him his sword.
[20] Surely the mountains produce food for him,
where all the wild animals play.
[21] He lies under the lotus plants,
hidden in the reeds and the marsh.
[22] The lotus plants cover him with their shade.
The willows by the stream surround him.
[23] Look, if a river floods, he does not tremble.
He is calm, even if the Jordan rises up to his mouth.
[24] Can anyone catch him while he is watching?
Can anyone pierce his nose with a trap?

> **What does this mean?**
> “Behemoth” is a Hebrew word. It is the plural of “animal,” so something like “the animal of all animals,” “the giant beast.”
> Which animal is meant? Many think of the hippopotamus: It eats grass, lies in the marsh under plants and is not afraid of floods. Others think of the elephant. Still others see in it a mythical creature from the beginning, a picture of the wild, untamed forces of the world.
> Verse 15 is important: “which I made just as I made you.” Behemoth is a creature of God, just like Job. Even the strongest, wildest animal is in God’s hand.
> Verse 19: “The first among the works of God”: a masterpiece of God’s creation.
> A note on verse numbers: In some Bibles (following the Hebrew), Chapter 40 has 32 verses in all. Verses 25–32 there are Chapter 41:1–8 here.
