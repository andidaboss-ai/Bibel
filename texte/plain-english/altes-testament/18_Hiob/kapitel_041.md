# Job – Chapter 41
### Leviathan

---

## Can you catch Leviathan? (Verses 1–11)

[1] “Can you pull out Leviathan with a fishhook?
Can you press down his tongue with a cord?
[2] Can you put a rope through his nose?
Can you pierce his jaw with a hook?
[3] Will he beg you over and over for mercy?
Will he speak gentle words to you?
[4] Will he make a covenant with you,
so that you can take him as your servant forever?
[5] Will you play with him as with a pet bird?
Will you put him on a leash for your girls?
[6] Will traders bargain over him?
Will they divide him up among the merchants?
[7] Can you fill his skin with harpoons,
or his head with fishing spears?
[8] Lay your hand on him.
Remember the fight, and you will never do it again!
[9] Look, any hope of beating him is useless.
Won’t a person be knocked down just by the sight of him?
[10] No one is so fierce that he dares to stir him up.
So who is the one who can stand before me?
[11] Who has given me anything first, so that I would have to pay him back?
Everything under the heavens is mine.

> **What does this mean?**
> Leviathan is a huge sea monster. Many think of the crocodile (compare the armor of scales in verses 15–17). But in ancient stories of the Near East, Leviathan also stands for chaos, for evil and for the powers that threaten human beings (compare Job 3:8; Psalm 74:14; Isaiah 27:1).
> God asks with humor: Can you catch Leviathan on a hook like a fish? Will he beg you for mercy? Can you put him on a leash as a pet for your daughters? Of course not!
> Verse 8: Whoever attacks him once never does it again.
> Verses 10–11 are the conclusion: If no one can stand before Leviathan, who can stand before God, his Creator? God owes nobody anything. Everything belongs to him. Paul quotes this thought in Romans 11:35.
> A note on verse numbers: In some Bibles, verses 1–8 of this chapter are Job 40:25–32. After that, the verse numbers there are 8 lower (verse 9 here is 41:1 there).

## His body (Verses 12–17)

[12] “I will not keep quiet about his limbs,
or his mighty strength, or his fine build.
[13] Who can strip off his outer coat?
Who can get between his jaws?
[14] Who can open the doors of his face?
All around his teeth is terror.
[15] His strong scales are his pride.
They are shut tight together like a seal.
[16] They lie so close to one another
that no air can get between them.
[17] They are joined to one another.
They stick together, so that they cannot be pulled apart.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is proud of his creature. He describes Leviathan the way an artist describes his masterpiece.
> “His outer coat” is his skin, his armor. “The doors of his face” are his huge mouth.
> The scales lie so close together that nothing gets through, neither air nor any weapon.

## Fire and smoke (Verses 18–24)

[18] “When he sneezes, light flashes out.
His eyes are like the eyelids of the dawn.
[19] Burning torches come out of his mouth.
Sparks of fire leap out.
[20] Smoke comes out of his nostrils,
like a boiling pot over a fire of reeds.
[21] His breath sets coals on fire.
A flame comes out of his mouth.
[22] There is strength in his neck.
Terror dances in front of him.
[23] The folds of his flesh are joined together.
They are firm on him. They cannot be moved.
[24] His heart is as hard as a stone,
yes, as hard as the lower millstone.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the description becomes more and more powerful: fire from the mouth, smoke from the nose. This is poetic language that wants to show how frightening he is. It makes you think of a dragon.
> Verse 18: When a crocodile snorts in the sunlight, the water sparkles. Its eyes come up out of the water with a reddish glow, like the dawn.
> Verse 24: His heart is as hard as stone. He knows no fear and no pity.

---

## No weapon helps (Verses 25–34)

[25] “When he rises up, the mighty are afraid.
They back away from his thrashing.
[26] If someone attacks him with the sword, it does nothing.
Neither does the spear, the dart or the pointed shaft.
[27] He thinks of iron as straw,
and of bronze as rotten wood.
[28] An arrow cannot make him run away.
Stones from a sling are like the dry husks of grain to him.
[29] He thinks of clubs as straw.
He laughs at the rushing sound of the javelin.
[30] His underside is like sharp pieces of broken pottery.
He leaves a trail in the mud like a threshing sledge.
[31] He makes the deep boil like a pot.
He makes the sea like a pot of ointment.
[32] He leaves a shining path behind him.
You would think the deep had white hair.
[33] On earth there is nothing equal to him.
He was made without fear.
[34] He looks at everything that is high.
He is king over all the sons of pride.”

> **What does this mean?**
> All human weapons are useless against Leviathan: sword, spear, arrow, sling, club.
> Verses 31–32: When he swims through the sea, the water foams up. Behind him there is a white trail of foam, as if the sea had gotten white hair.
> Verse 34: Leviathan is “king over all the sons of pride.” That means: over all the proud. This is the key: In Job 40:11–12, God challenged Job to bring down the proud. Job cannot even defeat Leviathan. But God created him and holds him in his hand.
> The message of God’s two speeches: The world is full of wild and dangerous forces that people do not control. But God controls them. Job does not have to understand everything. He can trust God.
> God has not answered Job’s question “Why?” He has not told him anything about the conversation in heaven (Chapters 1–2). But God has shown himself to Job. And that is enough for Job, as the next chapter shows.
