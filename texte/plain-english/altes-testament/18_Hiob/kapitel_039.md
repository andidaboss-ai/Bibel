# Job – Chapter 39
### God and the wild animals

---

## Mountain goats and deer (Verses 1–4)

[1] “Do you know the time when the mountain goats give birth?
Do you watch when the doe has her fawns?
[2] Can you count the months until they are ready?
Do you know the time when they give birth?
[3] They bend down. They give birth to their young.
Their labor pains are over.
[4] Their young ones grow strong.
They grow up in the open field.
They go away and do not come back.

> **What does this mean?**
> God keeps asking Job questions, now about wild animals. They are animals that people have not tamed and do not control.
> The mountain goats live high up in the mountains. No human being is there when they have their young. But God sees it and takes care of it.
> The young grow up and become independent. Life goes on, completely without human beings.

---

## The wild donkey (Verses 5–8)

[5] “Who has set the wild donkey free?
Who has untied the ropes of the swift donkey?
[6] I have made the wilderness his home,
and the salty land the place where he lives.
[7] He laughs at the noise of the city.
He does not hear the shouting of a driver.
[8] The mountains are his pasture.
He searches for anything green.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wild donkey is free. He does not have to carry loads, and no driver shouts at him.
> God gave him freedom and the desert as his home. What is harsh for human beings is just right for the wild donkey.
> God has given every creature its own place.

---

## The wild ox (Verses 9–12)

[9] “Will the wild ox be willing to serve you?
Will he stay by your feeding trough?
[10] Can you keep the wild ox in the furrow with a harness?
Will he plow the valleys behind you?
[11] Will you trust him because his strength is great?
Will you leave your work to him?
[12] Will you rely on him
to bring home your grain,
and gather it from your threshing floor?

> **What does this mean?**
> The wild ox (the aurochs) was a huge, very strong kind of cattle. It has died out today. People could not tame it.
> God asks: Can you hitch the wild ox to your plow like a farm ox? No. He is too wild and too strong.
> People cannot control everything. There are forces in creation that only God holds in his hand.

---

## The ostrich (Verses 13–18)

[13] “The wings of the ostrich flap proudly,
but are they the feathers and plumage of love?
[14] For she leaves her eggs on the ground.
She lets them warm in the dust.
[15] She forgets that a foot may crush them,
or that a wild animal may trample them.
[16] She is harsh with her young, as if they were not hers.
She is not afraid that her work may be for nothing,
[17] because God has not given her wisdom.
He has not given her a share of understanding.
[18] But when she rises up to run,
she laughs at the horse and its rider.

> **What does this mean?**
> The ostrich is a strange bird: It has wings, but it cannot fly. It lays its eggs in the sand, and it looks as if it does not care about them.
> “Feathers of love”: In Hebrew this is a play on words. The word for stork means “the faithful one” or “the loving one.” The ostrich, on the other hand, seems not to care.
> But then: When the ostrich starts running, it is faster than a horse with its rider.
> God has also made strange creatures that seem “foolish.” And yet they have their own strength. God’s creation is full of surprises and humor.

---

## The war horse (Verses 19–25)

[19] “Have you given the horse his strength?
Have you clothed his neck with a flowing mane?
[20] Have you made him leap like a locust?
His proud snorting is terrifying.
[21] He paws the ground in the valley and enjoys his strength.
He charges out to meet the armed men.
[22] He laughs at fear and is not afraid.
He does not turn back from the sword.
[23] The quiver of arrows rattles against him,
along with the flashing spear and the javelin.
[24] He eats up the ground with fierce rage.
He cannot stand still at the sound of the trumpet.
[25] Whenever the trumpet sounds, he snorts, ‘Aha!’
He smells the battle from far away,
the thunder of the commanders, and the shouting.

> **What does this mean?**
> The horse in war is brave and strong. It is not afraid of weapons or noise.
> “He eats up the ground”: He gallops so fast that he seems to swallow up the road.
> “Aha!” (in Hebrew “He’ach!”): The horse neighs with impatience when it hears the signal to attack.
> This strength and this courage also come from God, not from human beings. The Bible does not praise war here. It is amazed at the animal God has made.

## Hawk and eagle (Verses 26–30)

[26] “Is it by your wisdom that the hawk soars,
and spreads her wings toward the south?
[27] Is it at your command that the eagle flies up high,
and builds his nest up high?
[28] He lives on the cliff and makes his home there,
on the tip of the cliff, in a safe stronghold.
[29] From there he looks out for prey.
His eyes see it from far away.
[30] His young ones also suck up blood.
Where the dead bodies are, there he is.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 26: In the fall, the hawk flies south. Who taught it that? Not Job. God has given the migrating birds their knowledge.
> The eagle builds his nest high up in the rocks. He sees his prey from a great distance.
> Verse 30 also shows the hard side of nature: Animals eat other animals. God does not keep quiet about this. That too belongs to his creation, which human beings do not fully understand.
> Jesus takes up this picture: “For wherever the dead body is, that is where the vultures gather” (Matthew 24:28).
