# Job – Chapter 6
### Job: “My friends are like a dried-up stream”

---

## My pain is heavier than sand (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then Job answered,
[2] “Oh, if only my pain could be weighed,
and all my misery put on the scales!
[3] For now it would be heavier than the sand of the seas.
That is why my words have been rash.
[4] For the arrows of the Almighty are stuck in me.
My spirit drinks their poison.
God’s terrors line up against me.
[5] Does a wild donkey bray when it has grass?
Does an ox bellow over its feed?
[6] Can food without flavor be eaten without salt?
Is there any taste in the white of an egg?
[7] I refuse to touch it.
It is like disgusting food to me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job answers Eliphaz. He says: If only you knew how heavy my pain is! Heavier than all the sand of the sea!
> He even apologizes a little: That is why I spoke so rashly (in Chapter 3).
> Job feels attacked by God, as if by poisoned arrows.
> Verse 5: An animal only cries out when it lacks something. Job means: I am not crying out for no reason!
> Verses 6–7: The words of Eliphaz taste to him like bland food without salt. They disgust him. What Eliphaz says does not help him at all.

---

## I can’t go on (Verses 8–13)

[8] “Oh, if only my request would be granted,
and God would give me what I long for:
[9] that it would please God to crush me,
that he would let his hand go and cut me off!
[10] Then I would still have this comfort.
Yes, I would jump for joy in pain that does not let up:
I have not denied the words of the Holy One.
[11] What strength do I have, that I should keep waiting?
What end is ahead of me, that I should be patient?
[12] Is my strength the strength of stones?
Is my body made of bronze?
[13] Isn’t it true that I have no help inside me,
and that wisdom has been driven away from me?

> **What does this mean?**
> Job wishes again that he were dead. But he asks God for it. He does not take his life into his own hands.
> His only comfort: “I have not denied the words of the Holy One.” He has stayed faithful to God, in spite of everything.
> Verses 11–12 are very honest: I have no strength left. I am not made of stone or metal. I am a human being, and I can’t go on.
> The Bible takes such feelings seriously. It does not condemn Job for them.

> If you yourself are thinking, “I have no strength left, I don’t want to live anymore”: You are not alone. Please talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Friends who let him down (Verses 14–23)

[14] “Someone who is about to collapse should receive kindness from his friend,
even if he gives up the fear of the Almighty.
[15] My brothers have been as unreliable as a stream,
like the bed of streams that dry up.
[16] They are dark because of the ice,
and the snow hides in them.
[17] In the dry season they disappear.
When it is hot, they vanish from their place.
[18] The caravans that travel along them turn aside.
They go up into the empty desert and die.
[19] The caravans of Tema looked for water.
The travelers of Sheba waited for it.
[20] They were let down because they had been sure.
They came there and were put to shame.
[21] Now you too have become nothing.
You see something terrible, and you are afraid.
[22] Did I ever say, ‘Give me something’?
Or, ‘Pay a gift for me out of your wealth’?
[23] Or, ‘Rescue me from the enemy’s hand’?
Or, ‘Buy me free from the hand of the oppressors’?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 is an important sentence: A true friend stands by you, even when you are in despair, even when you doubt God.
> Job compares his friends to a desert stream (a wadi). In winter, when no one needs water, it is full. In summer, when people badly need water, it is dried up. The caravans that hoped for water die of thirst.
> His friends are like that: In good times they were there. Now, when he really needs them, they fail him.
> “You see something terrible, and you are afraid”: Maybe the friends are afraid that such a disaster could hit them too. That is why they look for the guilt in Job. Then they themselves feel safe.
> Job has asked nothing of them, no money, no help. Only compassion.
> Tema and Sheba were places in Arabia from which trade caravans came.

## Show me my mistake! (Verses 24–30)

[24] “Teach me, and I will be quiet.
Help me understand where I went wrong.
[25] How powerful honest words are!
But what does your criticism prove?
[26] Do you want to criticize words,
when the speeches of a desperate person are like wind?
[27] Yes, you would even throw lots for an orphan,
and you would sell your friend.
[28] Now please look at me.
I will surely not lie to your face.
[29] Please turn back. Let there be no injustice.
Yes, turn back. My cause is right.
[30] Is there anything wrong on my tongue?
Can’t my sense of taste tell what is harmful?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Job challenges his friends: If I have sinned, then show me where! Say it plainly!
> Verse 26 is very human: The words of a desperate person are “like wind.” You should not weigh every word. A person in deep trouble sometimes says harsh things. A friend should understand that.
> “Look at me!”: Job asks his friends to really look at him, as a person, not as a case for their theory.
> This is an important lesson for everyone who stands by people who suffer: First listen, first look, before giving advice.
