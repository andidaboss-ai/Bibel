# Job – Chapter 2
### Job becomes sick – and his friends come

---

## The second test (Verses 1–6)

[1] Again, one day the sons of God came
to stand before the LORD.
Satan also came among them
to stand before the LORD.
[2] The LORD said to Satan,
“Where have you come from?”
Satan answered the LORD,
“From roaming back and forth on the earth,
and from walking up and down on it.”
[3] The LORD said to Satan,
“Have you noticed my servant Job?
There is no one like him on the earth.
He is a blameless and honest man.
He fears God and turns away from evil.
He still holds on to his integrity,
even though you stirred me up against him
to ruin him without any reason.”
Integrity means: being honest and faithful through and through.
[4] Satan answered the LORD,
“Skin for skin!
Yes, a man will give everything he has for his life.
[5] But now stretch out your hand
and touch his bones and his flesh.
Then he will turn away from you to your face!”
[6] The LORD said to Satan,
“All right. He is in your hand.
Only spare his life.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God himself says: Job suffers “without any reason.” This is the key to the whole book. Later Job’s friends will claim that Job must have sinned. But the reader knows better: Job is innocent.
> “Skin for skin” was probably a saying. It means: A person will give everything to save his own skin. As long as it does not hit Job himself, he will stay faithful to God.
> Again God sets a limit: “Only spare his life.”

---

## Job becomes sick (Verses 7–10)

[7] So Satan went away from the LORD.
He struck Job with painful sores,
from the soles of his feet to the top of his head.
[8] Job took a piece of broken pottery to scratch himself with.
And he sat among the ashes.
[9] Then his wife said to him,
“Are you still holding on to your integrity?
Turn away from God and die!”
[10] But he said to her,
“You are talking like a foolish woman.
Should we accept good things from God,
and not accept bad things?”
In all this, Job did not sin with his lips.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job becomes very sick. His whole body is covered with painful sores. He sits in the ashes, probably on the ash heap outside the town, where garbage was burned. That is where the outcasts sat.
> Job’s wife is often judged harshly. But we must remember: She too has lost all 10 of her children. She too suffers endlessly. Maybe she means: Make an end of it, then you will have no more pain. Her words come from deep despair.
> Job answers: We cannot accept only the good things from God. He keeps holding on to God.
> “He did not sin with his lips”: Jewish interpreters have noticed: Here it says “with his lips,” but in Job 1:22 it does not. Maybe doubt was already starting in his heart. From Chapter 3 on, he will speak it out loud.

## Three friends come (Verses 11–13)

[11] Job had three friends.
They heard about all the trouble that had come on him.
So they came, each one from his own home:
Eliphaz the Temanite,
Bildad the Shuhite,
and Zophar the Naamathite.
They agreed to meet
and come together to share his grief and to comfort him.
[12] They looked up from far away,
but they did not recognize him.
Then they cried out loud and wept.
Each of them tore his robe.
They threw dust into the air over their heads.
[13] Then they sat with him on the ground
for seven days and seven nights.
No one said a word to him,
because they saw that his pain was very great.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job’s friends are good friends. They come from far away. They agree to meet so that they can comfort him together.
> Job looks so changed that they do not recognize him.
> The best thing they do: They sit with him in silence for seven days. They say nothing. They are simply there. That is often the best comfort for someone who is grieving. In the Jewish tradition there is still the “Shiva” today: seven days of mourning, when friends visit the mourners.
> Sadly, later they start to talk. And then they make everything worse.
> Teman was in Edom. We do not know exactly where Bildad and Zophar came from.
