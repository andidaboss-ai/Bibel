# Job – Chapter 29
### Remembering the good old days

---

## When God was my friend (Verses 1–6)

[1] Job went on with his speech and said,
[2] “Oh, if only I were as I was in the months long ago,
as in the days when God watched over me,
[3] when his lamp shone over my head,
and by his light I walked through the darkness,
[4] as I was in the best years of my life,
when God’s friendship was over my tent,
[5] when the Almighty was still with me,
and my children were all around me,
[6] when my steps were bathed in butter,
and the rock poured out streams of olive oil for me!

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapters 29–31, Job gives his closing speech. He looks back on his former life (Chapter 29), describes his misery now (Chapter 30), and insists one last time that he is innocent (Chapter 31).
> What Job misses most is not his wealth but being close to God: “when God watched over me,” “when God’s friendship was over my tent.”
> And he misses his children: “when my children were all around me.” This short sentence is full of pain.
> “Steps bathed in butter” and “streams of olive oil”: pictures of plenty and wealth.

---

## Respected by everyone (Verses 7–11)

[7] “When I went out to the city gate,
when I set up my seat in the public square,
[8] the young men saw me and stepped back out of sight.
The old men stood up and stayed standing.
[9] The leaders stopped talking
and put their hands over their mouths.
[10] The voices of the nobles went quiet.
Their tongues stuck to the roof of their mouths.
[11] For when an ear heard me, it called me blessed.
When an eye saw me, it spoke well of me,

> **What does this mean?**
> The city gate was the place where the elders judged cases and talked about important matters. Job had a place of honor there.
> Everyone respected him: The young made room, the old stood up, and even the leaders went quiet when he came.

---

## A father to the poor (Verses 12–17)

[12] because I rescued the poor who cried for help,
and the orphan who had no one to help him.
[13] The blessing of the one who was about to die came on me.
I made the widow’s heart sing for joy.
[14] I put on righteousness, and it clothed me.
My justice was like a robe and a crown.
[15] I was eyes for the blind
and feet for the lame.
[16] I was a father to the needy.
I looked into the case of the stranger I did not know.
[17] I broke the jaws of the unrighteous
and pulled the prey out of his teeth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here you can see why Job was respected: not because of his wealth but because of his goodness.
> Eliphaz had accused him of leaving widows and orphans alone (Job 22:9). Here Job says the opposite: I rescued the poor, protected the orphan and made the widow’s heart glad.
> Verse 15 is a beautiful picture: “I was eyes for the blind and feet for the lame.” Job was there for others where they could not help themselves.
> Verse 16: He even stood up for strangers he did not know. He looked carefully into their case, so that they would get justice.
> Verse 17: He stood against the unrighteous and set their victims free.
> According to the Bible, this is how a righteous person should live (compare Isaiah 1:17; James 1:27).

---

## I thought it would always stay this way (Verses 18–20)

[18] “Then I said, ‘I will die in my own house.
My days will be as many as the grains of sand.
[19] My roots reach out to the water.
The dew lies all night on my branches.
[20] My honor stays fresh in me.
My bow stays new in my hand.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Job thought his happiness would last forever. He would grow old and die in peace in his own house.
> “In my own house”: The Hebrew literally says “by my nest.”
> He saw himself as a tree that stands by the water and always stays green (compare Psalm 1:3).
> Many people think like this when things go well for them. But life can suddenly change.

---

## A king who comforts those who mourn (Verses 21–25)

[21] “People listened to me and waited.
They kept quiet to hear my advice.
[22] After I had spoken, no one spoke again.
My words fell on them.
[23] They waited for me as people wait for the rain.
They opened their mouths as if to drink the spring rain.
[24] I smiled at them when they had lost their confidence.
They did not turn away from the light of my face.
[25] I chose their way for them and sat as their chief.
I lived like a king among his troops,
like someone who comforts those who mourn.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job’s advice was like rain on dry land. People waited for it.
> Verse 24 is touching: When others had lost heart, Job smiled at them and gave them courage.
> Verse 25: He was like a king, but a king “like someone who comforts those who mourn.”
> The bitter irony: Job himself used to comfort those who mourned. Now he needs comfort himself, and his friends cannot comfort him.
