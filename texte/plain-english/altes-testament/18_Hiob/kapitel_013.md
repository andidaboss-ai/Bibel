# Job – Chapter 13
### “You are doctors who are worth nothing”

---

## You defend God with lies (Verses 1–12)

[1] “Look, my eyes have seen all this.
My ears have heard it and understood it.
[2] What you know, I know too.
I am not less than you.
[3] “But I want to speak to the Almighty.
I want to argue my case with God.
[4] But you make up lies.
You are all doctors who are worth nothing.
[5] Oh, if only you would be completely silent!
Then you would be wise.
[6] Now listen to my argument.
Pay attention to the case my lips are making.
[7] Will you say what is wrong to defend God?
Will you tell lies for him?
[8] Will you take his side?
Will you argue God’s case for him?
[9] Would it go well for you if he examined you?
Or will you fool him, the way you fool a human being?
[10] He will surely correct you
if you secretly take sides.
[11] Won’t his majesty frighten you?
Won’t the terror of him fall on you?
[12] Your wise sayings are proverbs made of ashes.
Your defenses are defenses made of clay.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job no longer wants to talk with his friends. He wants to talk directly with God.
> He calls his friends people who “make up lies” and “doctors who are worth nothing.” They wanted to heal him, but their medicine makes everything worse.
> Verse 5 is famous: “Oh, if only you would be completely silent! Then you would be wise.” At the beginning, they had been silent for seven days (Job 2:13). That was the best thing they did.
> Verses 7–8 are sharp criticism: The friends want to defend God by saying untrue things about Job. Job says: God does not need lies to defend him. God wants the truth.
> At the end of the book, God will confirm exactly this (Job 42:7).
> “Proverbs made of ashes”: Their clever sentences are worthless. They fall apart when you touch them.

---

## I will defend my ways before God (Verses 13–19)

[13] “Be quiet! Leave me alone, so that I can speak.
Let whatever may happen to me come.
[14] Why should I take my flesh in my teeth
and put my life in my hand?
[15] He will kill me. I have no hope.
Even so, I will defend my ways before him.
[16] This too will be my rescue:
A godless person would not dare to come before him.
[17] Listen carefully to what I say.
Let my words fill your ears.
[18] Look, I have prepared my case.
I know that I am in the right.
[19] Who is there who will argue against me?
If someone could, I would be quiet and die.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job is ready to risk everything. He wants to come before God, even if it costs him his life.
> “Take my flesh in my teeth” and “put my life in my hand” are sayings. They mean: I am risking everything.
> Verse 15 is very well known, but it is hard to understand in Hebrew. The WEB, our English source text, follows the written Hebrew text: “He will kill me. I have no hope.” At this place, the Jewish tradition reads one word differently. Then it says: “Even if he kills me, I will hope in him.” That is how many Bibles have it. Both readings show: Job does not give up on God. He wants to face him.
> Verse 16: Job is sure that a godless person would not dare to come before God. That he dares to do it shows that he is innocent.

## Job speaks to God (Verses 20–28)

[20] “Only do not do two things to me.
Then I will not hide from your face:
[21] Take your hand far away from me,
and do not let your terror frighten me.
[22] Then call, and I will answer.
Or let me speak, and you answer me.
[23] How many wrongs and sins have I done?
Show me my wrongdoing and my sin!
[24] Why do you hide your face
and think of me as your enemy?
[25] Will you chase a leaf blown by the wind?
Will you hunt down dry straw?
[26] For you write bitter charges against me.
You make me pay for the sins of my youth.
[27] You also lock my feet in the stocks.
You watch every path I take.
You draw a line around the soles of my feet,
[28] though I am rotting away like something decayed,
like a piece of clothing eaten by moths.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Job speaks directly to God. He asks for only two things: Take the pain away, and do not let me be afraid of you. Then he will speak.
> Verse 23: Job wants to know what he did wrong. He is ready to see his guilt if God shows it to him.
> Verse 24 is the deepest pain: “Why do you hide your face?” The worst thing for Job is not the illness. It is that God is silent and seems far away (compare Psalm 13:1; 22:1).
> “A leaf blown by the wind”: Job feels so weak and small. Why does the almighty God fight against something so small?
> “The sins of my youth”: Maybe he is thinking of small mistakes from his younger years. Should they be the reason for so much suffering?
> “The stocks” were a wooden beam in which the feet of prisoners were locked.
