# Job – Chapter 21
### “Why do the wicked live?”

---

## Please listen to me! (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then Job answered,
[2] “Listen carefully to what I say.
Let that be the comfort you give me.
[3] Let me speak.
After I have spoken, go on mocking.
[4] As for me, is my complaint against a human being?
Why shouldn’t I be impatient?
[5] Look at me and be shocked.
Put your hand over your mouth.
[6] When I think about it, I am troubled.
My body shakes with horror.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2 is bitter: The only comfort you can give me is to listen to me.
> “Put your hand over your mouth”: a sign that you are speechless and keep quiet.
> Job says: My complaint is aimed at God, not at people. So I may be impatient.

---

## The wicked are doing well (Verses 7–16)

[7] “Why do the wicked go on living?
Why do they grow old and become very powerful?
[8] Their children are settled around them.
They see their descendants grow up before their eyes.
[9] Their houses are safe, with nothing to fear.
God’s rod does not strike them.
[10] Their bulls breed and never fail.
Their cows give birth and never lose a calf.
[11] They send out their little ones like a flock.
Their children dance.
[12] They sing to the tambourine and the harp.
They have fun at the sound of the flute.
[13] They spend their days in good times.
Then in an instant they go down to Sheol (the world of the dead).
[14] They say to God, ‘Go away from us!
We don’t want to know your ways.
[15] Who is the Almighty, that we should serve him?
What would we gain if we prayed to him?’
[16] Yet their good fortune is not in their own hands.
The plans of the wicked are far from me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Job attacks the friends’ teaching directly. They have said: Things go badly for the wicked. Job says: Just look at reality! Things often go very well for the wicked!
> They grow old, have many children, their houses are safe, their animals thrive, their children dance and sing. And they die peacefully, “in an instant,” without long suffering.
> And this even though they look down on God: “What would we gain if we prayed to him?”
> Other parts of the Bible ask this question too (Psalm 73; Jeremiah 12:1). Why do evil people do well?
> Verse 16: Job clearly separates himself from them. He does not want to live like the wicked, even if they are doing well.

---

## How often are the wicked really punished? (Verses 17–21)

[17] “How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?
How often does disaster come on them?
How often does God hand out pain in his anger?
[18] How often are they like straw in the wind,
like chaff that the storm carries away?
[19] You say, ‘God stores up a man’s guilt for his children.’
Let God pay back the man himself, so that he feels it!
[20] Let his own eyes see his ruin.
Let him drink the anger of the Almighty.
[21] For what does he care about his family after him,
when the number of his months has run out?

> **What does this mean?**
> Bildad had said, “Yes, the light of the wicked will be put out” (Job 18:5). Job asks: How often does that really happen? Rarely!
> The friends say: If the wicked man himself is not punished, then his children will be punished. Job thinks this is unfair: The one who did wrong should feel the results himself, not his children. After his death, he does not care anyway what happens to his children.
> This fits with what the prophet Ezekiel later says: Everyone is responsible for his own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).

---

## In death everyone is equal (Verses 22–26)

[22] “Can anyone teach God knowledge,
since he judges even those who are high up?
[23] One man dies in his full strength,
completely at ease and quiet.
[24] His buckets are full of milk.
The marrow of his bones is still moist.
[25] Another man dies with a bitter soul.
He never tasted anything good.
[26] They lie down together in the dust.
The worms cover them both.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job says: You cannot tell who is good and who is evil if you only look at their lives. One dies happy, the other unhappy. And in the end both lie in the grave.
> The Preacher in Ecclesiastes says something similar (Ecclesiastes 9:2–3).
> Verse 22: No one can teach God. But that is exactly what the friends are trying to do with their theory.

---

## Your comfort is lies (Verses 27–34)

[27] “Look, I know your thoughts,
the plans you have to do me wrong.
[28] For you say, ‘Where is the house of the prince now?
Where is the tent where the wicked lived?’
[29] Haven’t you asked people who travel?
Don’t you know what they report?
[30] They say that the evil man is kept for the day of disaster,
that such people are led out to the day of anger.
[31] Who will tell him to his face what he has done?
Who will pay him back for it?
[32] Yet he is carried to the grave,
and people keep watch over his tomb.
[33] The soil of the valley is sweet to him.
All people will follow after him,
just as countless people went before him.
[34] So how can you comfort me with nonsense?
Nothing is left of your answers but lies.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Job sees through his friends: When you ask, “Where is the house of the prince now?” you mean my destroyed house.
> He says: Just ask the travelers who have seen much of the world. They know: The evil man often even gets a beautiful funeral with an honored grave. Many follow in his funeral procession. No one holds his deeds against him while he is alive.
> Verse 30 is not completely clear in Hebrew. The English source understands it this way: The evil man is kept for the day of disaster. Then this would be the friends’ opinion, which Job repeats here. But many other Bibles translate it the way the context suggests: The evil man is spared on the day of disaster.
> Verse 34 is Job’s verdict on the whole second round: Your answers are lies. They do not fit reality.
> Job does not deny that God is just. But he refuses to accept an easy explanation that is not true. That is honest faith.
