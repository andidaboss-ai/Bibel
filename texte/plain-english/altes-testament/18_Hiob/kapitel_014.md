# Job – Chapter 14
### “If a man dies, will he live again?”

---

## A human life is short (Verses 1–6)

[1] “A human being, born of a woman,
lives only a few days, and they are full of trouble.
[2] He grows up like a flower, and then he is cut down.
He slips away like a shadow and does not last.
[3] Do you really keep your eyes open on someone like that?
Do you bring me into court with you?
[4] Who can bring something clean out of something unclean?
No one.
[5] His days are already set.
The number of his months is decided by you.
You have set limits for him that he cannot pass.
[6] So look away from him, so that he can rest,
until he finishes his day like a hired worker.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses about the shortness of life are very well known. They are often read at funerals.
> Life is like a flower: beautiful, but quickly wilted (compare Psalm 90:5–6; 103:15–16; Isaiah 40:6–8).
> Job asks God: If life is so short, then at least leave a person alone, so that he can live his short time in peace.
> Verse 4: No human being is completely pure. Job admits that he is not perfect. But he asks: Why is he of all people treated so harshly?

---

## Hope for a tree, but for a human being? (Verses 7–12)

[7] “For there is hope for a tree:
If it is cut down, it will sprout again.
Its new shoots will not stop growing.
[8] Its root may grow old in the earth,
and its stump may die in the ground.
[9] Yet at the mere smell of water it will bud
and put out branches like a young plant.
[10] But a human being dies and lies there.
Yes, he breathes his last. And where is he?
[11] As water disappears from a lake,
and a river runs low and dries up,
[12] so a human being lies down and does not rise.
Until the heavens are no more, people will not wake up.
They will not be woken from their sleep.

> **What does this mean?**
> A beautiful and sad picture: A tree that has been cut down can sprout again. Even the smell of water makes it grow again.
> But a human being? When he dies, he is gone. Like a dried-up river.
> “Until the heavens are no more”: Job probably means: never. But later parts of the Bible speak of exactly this: God creates a new heaven and raises the dead (Isaiah 65:17; Daniel 12:2; Revelation 21:1).
> Later, Isaiah uses the picture of a tree stump as a picture of hope: “A shoot will come out of the stump of Jesse” (Isaiah 11:1).

---

## A quiet hope (Verses 13–17)

[13] “Oh, if only you would hide me in Sheol (the world of the dead)
and keep me hidden until your anger is over!
If only you would set a time for me and then remember me!
[14] If a man dies, will he live again?
I would wait all the days of my hard service,
until my relief came.
[15] You would call, and I would answer you.
You would long for the work of your hands.
[16] But now you count my steps.
Don’t you keep watch over my sin?
[17] My wrongdoing is sealed up in a bag.
You tie up my guilt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a quiet hope shines through. Job imagines: Maybe God will hide him in the world of the dead, only for a while, until his anger is over. And then God will remember him.
> Verse 14 asks the big question: “If a man dies, will he live again?” Job does not know the answer. But he dares to ask the question.
> Verse 15 is very beautiful: “You would long for the work of your hands.” Deep inside, Job believes that God loves him and misses him.
> Christians see in this question a hint of the resurrection that Jesus speaks about: “I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me will live, even if he dies” (John 11:25).
> “Sealed up in a bag”: God collects Job’s guilt as if in a closed bag. Some also understand it this way: God closes up the guilt and does not look at it anymore.

---

## But the hope melts away (Verses 18–22)

[18] “But a mountain that falls crumbles away.
A rock is moved from its place.
[19] Water wears away stones.
Its floods wash away the soil of the earth.
In the same way you destroy a human being’s hope.
[20] You overpower him forever, and he passes away.
You change his face and send him away.
[21] His sons are honored, and he does not know it.
They are brought low, but he does not notice it.
[22] He feels only the pain of his own body.
His soul mourns only for himself.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The hope lasted only a short time. Now it grows dark again.
> As water slowly wears away stones, God slowly destroys a person’s hope.
> Verse 21 is especially painful for Job: A dead person no longer finds out how his children are doing. Job has no children anymore.
> Job swings back and forth between hope and despair. That is very human. Many people who are grieving know this back and forth.
