# Job – Chapter 34
### Elihu’s second speech: God does no wrong

---

## Test my words (Verses 1–4)

[1] Elihu went on and said,
[2] “Hear my words, you wise men!
Listen to me, you who have knowledge!
[3] For the ear tests words,
just as the mouth tastes food.
[4] Let us choose for ourselves what is right.
Let us find out together what is good.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Elihu speaks to the wise, that is, to the three friends and to everyone who is listening.
> Verse 3: Just as you taste with your mouth whether food is good, you should test with your ear whether words are true. Elihu invites them to test together what is right. Job himself had already used the same picture (Job 12:11).

---

## What Job has said (Verses 5–9)

[5] “For Job has said, ‘I am righteous,
but God has taken away my right.
[6] Even though I am right, I am treated as a liar.
My wound cannot be healed, even though I have not disobeyed.’
[7] What man is like Job?
He drinks mockery like water.
[8] He keeps company with those who do evil.
He walks with wicked men.
[9] For he has said, ‘It does a person no good
to find his joy in God.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu quotes Job (compare Job 27:2; 9:22).
> Then he becomes sharp: Job “drinks mockery like water.” That means: He speaks scornfully about God as easily as people drink water.
> Verse 8 is a harsh accusation: Job supposedly talks like the wicked. Elihu probably does not mean that Job spends time with criminals, but that his words sound like theirs.
> Verse 9: Job had said that the wicked often do well (Chapter 21). Elihu understands this to mean: Job thinks it does not pay to serve God. Here Elihu is too harsh: Job never stopped holding on to God.

---

## God does no wrong (Verses 10–15)

[10] “So listen to me, you men of understanding:
God would never do evil!
The Almighty would never do wrong!
[11] For he pays a person back for what he has done.
He lets everyone receive what his ways deserve.
[12] Yes, surely God does not do evil.
The Almighty does not twist justice.
[13] Who put him in charge of the earth?
Who gave him the whole world to rule?
[14] If he thought only of himself,
if he took his spirit and his breath back to himself,
[15] all living things would die together,
and people would turn back into dust.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is Elihu’s main idea: God cannot be unjust. That would go against who he is.
> Verse 13: No one handed the world over to God. It belongs to him because he created it. He does not owe anyone an explanation.
> Verses 14–15: All life depends on God’s breath. If God thought only of himself and took back his breath, all life would be dead at once (compare Psalm 104:29; Genesis 2:7; 3:19). The fact that we are alive shows that God takes care of what he has made.

---

## God does not take sides (Verses 16–20)

[16] “If you have understanding, hear this!
Listen to the sound of my words!
[17] Could someone who hates justice rule?
Will you condemn the one who is righteous and mighty,
[18] who says to a king, ‘You are worthless!’
or to nobles, ‘You are wicked!’?
[19] He does not favor princes.
He does not value the rich more than the poor,
for they are all the work of his hands.
[20] They die in a moment, even at midnight.
The people are shaken and pass away.
The mighty are taken away, and no human hand does it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu asks: Can someone who hates justice rule the world? No. God rules the world, so he must be just.
> God is not afraid of the powerful. He can tell even a king the truth. To him, rich and poor are the same, because he made them all (compare Job 31:15).
> Verse 20: Even the most powerful can suddenly die without any human laying a hand on them. God does not need an army.

---

## God sees everything (Verses 21–30)

[21] “For his eyes are on the ways of every person.
He sees every step he takes.
[22] There is no darkness and no deep gloom
where those who do evil can hide.
[23] For he does not need to look at a person any longer
before that person comes before God for judgment.
[24] He breaks the mighty into pieces in ways no one can figure out.
He puts others in their place.
[25] He knows what they do.
He overthrows them in the night, so that they are destroyed.
[26] He strikes them down for being wicked
where everyone can see it,
[27] because they turned away from following him.
They did not pay attention to any of his ways.
[28] So they caused the cry of the poor to come to him.
He heard the cry of those who suffer.
[29] When he keeps quiet, who can condemn him?
When he hides his face, who can see him?
He rules over a nation and over a single person alike,
[30] so that the godless person may not rule,
and there is no one to trap the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> God sees everything. No one can hide from him (compare Psalm 139:11–12).
> Verse 23: God does not need a long trial as people do. He already knows everything. Job had wished for a court date with God. Elihu says: That is not necessary.
> Verse 28: God hears the cry of the poor. When the powerful oppress the poor, God steps in (compare Exodus 3:7).
> Verse 29: Sometimes God is silent. It says: “When he hides his face, who can see him?” But even then he rules. He makes sure that the godless do not rule forever.

---

## Job speaks without understanding (Verses 31–37)

[31] “For has anyone ever said to God,
‘I am guilty, but I will not do wrong anymore.
[32] Teach me what I cannot see.
If I have done wrong, I will not do it again’?
[33] Should God pay you back the way you want, since you refuse what he does?
For you must choose, not I.
So tell us what you know.
[34] People of understanding will tell me,
yes, every wise man who hears me:
[35] ‘Job speaks without knowledge.
His words have no wisdom.’
[36] I wish that Job would be tested to the very end,
because he answers like wicked men.
[37] For he adds rebellion to his sin.
He claps his hands among us in mockery.
He piles up his words against God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 31–32 suggest the right way to speak with God: “Teach me what I cannot see.” That is a humble prayer. Elihu means: This is how Job should pray, instead of accusing God.
> Verse 33 is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: God does not follow your wishes, Job. If you know better, say so.
> Verses 36–37 are very harsh. Elihu wishes that Job would be “tested to the very end.” He says that Job adds rebellion to his sin. Here Elihu sounds almost like the three friends again.
> At the end of the book, God himself says that Job has “spoken of me what is right” (Job 42:7). So Elihu’s sharp verdict is not the last word.
