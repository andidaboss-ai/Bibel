# Job – Chapter 33
### Elihu: God speaks in many ways

---

## Listen to me, Job (Verses 1–7)

[1] “But now, Job, please hear what I say.
Listen to all my words.
[2] Look, I have opened my mouth.
My tongue is speaking in my mouth.
[3] My words come from an honest heart.
My lips will speak sincerely what they know.
[4] The Spirit of God has made me.
The breath of the Almighty gives me life.
[5] If you can, answer me!
Set your words in order before me and stand up!
[6] Look, before God I am just like you.
I too was formed out of clay.
[7] Look, you do not need to be afraid of me.
I will not press down heavily on you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu speaks to Job directly by name. The three friends never did that.
> He says: I am a human being like you, made of clay. You do not need to be afraid of me.
> This is an answer to Job’s wish: Job wanted someone to talk with who was not overpowering (Job 9:34; 13:21). Elihu says: I am someone like that. You can talk with me.

---

## What you have said (Verses 8–13)

[8] “Surely you have spoken where I could hear.
I have heard the sound of your words:
[9] ‘I am clean, without wrongdoing.
I am innocent. There is no guilt in me.
[10] Look, he looks for reasons to attack me.
He thinks of me as his enemy.
[11] He puts my feet in the stocks.
He watches all my paths.’
[12] “Look, I will answer you.
In this you are not right,
for God is greater than any human being.
[13] Why do you fight against him
because he does not explain anything he does?

> **What does this mean?**
> Elihu sums up what Job has said. He quotes Job quite closely (compare Job 13:24, 27).
> Elihu does not accuse Job of having sinned, as the friends did. He criticizes something else: Job has accused God of being his enemy. And he has complained that God does not answer.
> Elihu’s answer: God is greater than human beings. He does not have to justify himself to us.

---

## God speaks through dreams (Verses 14–18)

[14] “For God speaks once, yes, twice,
but people do not pay attention.
[15] In a dream, in a vision at night,
when deep sleep falls on people,
while they are sleeping in bed,
[16] then he opens people’s ears
and seals the warning he gives them,
[17] to turn a person away from what he planned to do,
and to keep pride away from him.
[18] He keeps his soul from the pit,
and his life from dying by the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> Job had complained that God does not answer. Elihu says: God does speak, just in a different way than you think.
> The first way: dreams and visions at night. God can warn people in a dream, so that they do not run into ruin.
> God’s goal is not punishment but rescue: He wants to keep people “from the pit,” that is, from death.

---

## God speaks through suffering (Verses 19–22)

[19] “A person is also corrected with pain in his bed,
with constant aching in his bones,
[20] so that he cannot stand to eat bread,
and he hates even his favorite food.
[21] His flesh wastes away until it can no longer be seen.
His bones, which could not be seen before, stick out.
[22] Yes, his soul comes near to the pit,
and his life to those who bring death.

> **What does this mean?**
> The second way God speaks: through illness and pain.
> This is a new idea. The friends said: Suffering is punishment for sin. Elihu says: Suffering can also be a warning or a way of teaching. Through it, God wants to bring a person back before it is too late.
> Many people have found that during a serious illness they thought about their lives and came closer to God. But we must not make this a rule that we hold up to every sick person.

---

## An angel who speaks up for him (Verses 23–30)

[23] “Suppose there is an angel at his side,
someone who speaks for him,
one out of 1,000,
to show the person what is right for him.
[24] Then God is gracious to him and says,
‘Rescue him from going down to the pit!
I have found a ransom.’
[25] His flesh becomes fresher than a child’s.
He goes back to the days of his youth.
[26] He prays to God, and God is pleased with him,
so that he sees God’s face with joy.
God gives him back his righteousness.
[27] He sings in front of people and says,
‘I have sinned and twisted what was right,
and it did me no good.
[28] He has saved my soul from going into the pit.
My life will see the light.’
[29] “Look, God does all these things with a person
twice, yes, three times,
[30] to bring back his soul from the pit,
so that the light of life may shine on him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Elihu comes back to Job’s wish for a judge between him and God, a witness and a redeemer (Job 9:33; 16:19; 19:25).
> He says: There may be an angel, someone “who speaks for him,” who stands up for the person. Then God says: “I have found a ransom.” The person is rescued.
> A “ransom” is a price that is paid to set someone free. Christians see here a hint of Jesus, who says that he came “to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many” (Mark 10:45).
> The result: The person gets well, prays to God, sees God’s face with joy and sings about being rescued. He honestly admits his guilt.
> Verses 29–30: God does this again and again, “twice, yes, three times.” He does not give up. He wants to bring the person back into the light.

---

## I want to declare you righteous (Verses 31–33)

[31] “Pay attention, Job, and listen to me.
Be quiet, and I will speak.
[32] If you have anything to say, answer me.
Speak, for I want to declare you righteous.
[33] If not, listen to me.
Be quiet, and I will teach you wisdom.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 32 is remarkable: “I want to declare you righteous.” Elihu does not want to condemn Job like the friends did. He wants to help him.
> Job does not answer. Maybe he is listening. Maybe he is exhausted. The Bible does not say.
