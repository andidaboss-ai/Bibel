# Job – Chapter 4
### Eliphaz: “Who ever died while he was innocent?”

---

## Eliphaz begins kindly (Verses 1–6)

[1] Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
[2] “If someone tries to talk with you, will it upset you?
But who can keep from speaking?
[3] You have taught many people.
You have made weak hands strong.
[4] Your words have helped up those who were stumbling.
You have made shaky knees firm.
[5] But now it has come to you, and you lose heart.
It touches you, and you are terrified.
[6] Isn’t your reverence for God your confidence?
Isn’t your honest way of life your hope?

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz is the oldest and probably the most respected of the three friends. He begins politely and carefully.
> He reminds Job how often Job himself has comforted other people. But then comes a jab: Now that it hits you yourself, you lose heart.
> Eliphaz means well: You love God, so you have reason to hope. But he does not understand how deep Job’s pain is.

---

## What Eliphaz teaches (Verses 7–11)

[7] “Remember this:
Who ever died while he was innocent?
Where were honest people ever wiped out?
[8] This is what I have seen:
Those who plow wrongdoing and sow trouble
harvest the same.
[9] They die by the breath of God.
They are destroyed by the blast of his anger.
[10] The roaring of the lion, the voice of the fierce lion,
and the teeth of the young lions are broken.
[11] The old lion dies because it has no prey.
The cubs of the lioness are scattered.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is the teaching that all three friends hold: Things go well for those who are good. Things go badly for those who are evil. “You harvest what you sow.”
> For Job, this means, without it being said out loud: If things are going so badly for you, you must have done something evil.
> There is some truth in this teaching. Wrongdoing often has bad results. But it is not always true. The reader knows from Chapters 1–2: Job is innocent. God himself said so.
> Later Jesus clearly rejected this simple formula (John 9:1–3; Luke 13:1–5).
> The lions are a picture of powerful, violent people. In the end they too are broken.

---

## A frightening vision in the night (Verses 12–21)

[12] “A message was brought to me in secret.
My ear caught a whisper of it.
[13] It came in troubled thoughts from visions in the night,
when deep sleep falls on people.
[14] Fear came over me, and trembling.
It made all my bones shake.
[15] Then a spirit passed in front of my face.
The hair on my body stood up.
[16] It stood still, but I could not make out what it looked like.
A shape was in front of my eyes.
There was silence. Then I heard a voice say,
[17] ‘Can a mortal human be more right than God?
Can a man be more pure than his Maker?
[18] He does not trust even his servants.
He blames his angels for mistakes.
[19] How much more those who live in houses of clay,
whose foundation is in the dust,
who are crushed like a moth!
[20] Between morning and evening they are destroyed.
They die forever, and no one pays attention.
[21] Isn’t their tent cord pulled up inside them?
They die, and they die without wisdom.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Eliphaz points to a mysterious appearance in the night. The voice said: No human being is truly right before God.
> That is true in itself. No human being is perfect (compare Romans 3:23). But Eliphaz uses this truth in the wrong way: He wants to say that Job deserves his suffering.
> “Houses of clay” is a picture for the human body. People are made from the earth (Genesis 2:7) and are as fragile as a moth.
> “The tent cord pulled up”: Life is like a tent. When the cord is pulled out, the tent falls down. Paul uses a similar picture (2 Corinthians 5:1).
> At the end of the book, God will say that Eliphaz and his friends did not speak rightly about him (Job 42:7).
