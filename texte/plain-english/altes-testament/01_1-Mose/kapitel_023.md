# Genesis – Chapter 23
### Sarah dies – Abraham buys a grave

---

## Abraham mourns for Sarah (Verses 1–2)

[1] Sarah lived to be 127 years old.
That was how long Sarah lived.
[2] Sarah died in Kiriath Arba – that is Hebron – in the land of Canaan.
Abraham came to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sarah is the only woman in the Bible whose age at death is given. This shows how important she is. She is the mother of the people of Israel.
> Abraham weeps for his wife. A man of faith may grieve and weep too. Faith and grief belong together.
> Mourning for the dead was a fixed custom back then: People grieved loudly and in public for someone who had died.

---

## Abraham asks for a burial place (Verses 3–9)

[3] Then Abraham got up and left his dead wife.
He spoke to the people of Heth.
He said,
[4] “I am a stranger and a foreigner living among you.
Give me a piece of land among you as my own, so that I can have a grave.
Then I can bury my dead there.”

[5] The people of Heth answered Abraham.
They said to him,
[6] “Listen to us, my lord!
You are a prince of God among us.
Bury your dead in the best of our tombs.
None of us will refuse you his tomb.
Bury your dead.”

[7] Then Abraham got up and bowed down to the people of the land, to the people of Heth.
[8] He said to them,
“If you agree that I may bury my dead,
then listen to me and speak for me to Ephron, the son of Zohar.
[9] Let him sell me the cave of Machpelah, which belongs to him.
It is at the end of his field.
Let him sell it to me for the full price, here in front of all of you.
Then it will be my own, as a burial place.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God has promised Abraham the whole land. But until now he does not own a single square foot of it. He lives there as a foreigner.
> The people of Heth, the Hittites, are a people who lived in the land at that time.
> They treat Abraham with great respect: “a prince of God.”
> But Abraham does not want the grave as a gift, and he does not just want to borrow it. He wants to buy it – as his own, for good.

---

## The bargaining (Verses 10–16)

[10] Ephron was sitting among the people of Heth.
Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham so that all the people of Heth could hear,
all who had come to the gate of his city.
He said,
[11] “No, my lord, listen to me!
I give you the field.
And I also give you the cave that is in it.
In front of my people, I give it to you.
Bury your dead.”

[12] Then Abraham bowed down before the people of the land.
[13] He spoke to Ephron so that the people of the land could hear:
“Please listen to me!
I will pay you the price of the field.
Take the money from me.
Then I will bury my dead there.”

[14] Ephron answered Abraham.
He said to him,
[15] “My lord, listen to me!
A piece of land worth 400 shekels of silver –
what is that between me and you?
Bury your dead.”

[16] Abraham agreed with Ephron.
He weighed out for Ephron the silver he had named in front of the people of Heth:
400 shekels of silver (about 10 pounds), by the weight the traders used.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is how people bargained in the Middle East back then: First the seller acts as if he wants to give everything away. Then he mentions a price “by the way.” And it is high.
> Back then, a shekel was not a coin. It was a weight, about 0.4 ounces. 400 shekels are about 10 pounds of silver. That was a lot of money.
> Abraham does not haggle. He pays the full price, in public and in front of witnesses. No one is ever to be able to say later that the grave does not belong to him.

---

## The first piece of the promised land (Verses 17–20)

[17] So the field of Ephron in Machpelah, near Mamre, became Abraham’s:
the field, the cave in it
and all the trees in the field, all around, as far as its borders.
[18] It became Abraham’s own in front of the people of Heth,
in front of all who had come to the gate of his city.

[19] After this, Abraham buried his wife Sarah in the cave of the field of Machpelah,
near Mamre – that is Hebron – in the land of Canaan.
[20] So the field and the cave in it were given over to Abraham by the people of Heth as a burial place of his own.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first piece of land that Abraham owns in the promised land is a grave. That sounds sad. But it is also a sign of hope: Abraham firmly believes that his descendants will live here one day.
> Later, Abraham himself, Isaac, Rebekah, Jacob and Leah will also be buried in this cave.
> To this day, the cave of Machpelah in Hebron is a holy place for Jews, Christians and Muslims.
