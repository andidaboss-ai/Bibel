# Genesis – Chapter 19
### The destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah

---

## Lot welcomes the guests (Verses 1–3)

[1] The two angels came to Sodom in the evening.
Lot was sitting in the gate of Sodom.
When Lot saw them, he got up to meet them.
He bowed down with his face to the ground
[2] and said,
“Please listen, my lords:
Please come into your servant’s house.
Stay overnight and wash your feet.
Then you can get up early and go on your way.”
They said,
“No, we will spend the night in the town square.”
[3] But Lot urged them strongly.
So they went with him and came into his house.
He made them a feast.
He baked bread without yeast, and they ate.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two of the three men from Chapter 18 come to Sodom. Now it is said clearly: They are angels, that is, messengers of God.
> Back then, the city gate was the most important place in a city. The respected men met there and settled legal cases. Lot sits there – so he has become a respected man in Sodom.
> Lot is hospitable like Abraham. He urges the strangers not to stay outside. He probably knows how dangerous the night in Sodom is for strangers.
> Bread without yeast does not have to rise. It is quick to bake.

---

## The men of Sodom (Verses 4–11)

[4] The guests had not yet lain down to sleep.
Then the men of the city surrounded the house – the men of Sodom, young and old,
all the people from every part of the city.
[5] They called to Lot and said to him,
“Where are the men who came to you tonight?
Bring them out to us!
We want to have sex with them.”

[6] Lot went out to them through the door.
He shut the door behind him.
[7] He said,
“Please, my brothers, do not do such an evil thing!
[8] Listen: I have two daughters who have never slept with a man.
Let me bring them out to you.
Do with them whatever you like.
Only do nothing to these men.
For they have come under the protection of my roof.”

[9] But they said, “Get out of the way!”
And they said,
“This one fellow came here as a foreigner, and now he wants to play the judge!
Now we will treat you worse than them!”
They pushed hard against Lot
and came close to breaking down the door.

[10] Then the men inside reached out their hands.
They pulled Lot into the house with them
and shut the door.
[11] They struck the people outside the door with blindness, young and old.
So they wore themselves out trying to find the door.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a very dark scene. The whole city wants to sexually abuse the strangers by force. This is the “cry” that has come to God: violence against the weak and against strangers, contempt for every rule of hospitality.
> Lot tries to protect his guests. But his offer to hand over his own daughters is horrible. The Bible does not praise this. It simply tells it as it was. It shows how much Sodom has already corrupted Lot too.
> Later the Bible says about the guilt of Sodom: They were proud and had more than enough food, but they did not help the poor and needy (Ezekiel 16:49). The story shows a city without any compassion.
> In the end, Lot does not save the guests – the guests save Lot.

---

## The warning (Verses 12–14)

[12] The men said to Lot,
“Do you have anyone else here?
Sons-in-law, sons, daughters or anyone else in the city?
Bring them out of this place!
[13] For we are going to destroy this place.
The cry against its people has become so great before the LORD
that the LORD has sent us to destroy it.”

[14] So Lot went out.
He spoke to his future sons-in-law, who were going to marry his daughters.
He said,
“Get up! Get out of this place!
For the LORD is going to destroy the city.”
But his sons-in-law thought he was joking.

> **What does this mean?**
> God gives Lot’s family a chance to be saved. But the future sons-in-law do not take the warning seriously. They laugh about it.
> Lot has lived in Sodom for so long that no one believes he could speak about God anymore.

## The escape from Sodom (Verses 15–23)

[15] When morning came, the angels urged Lot to hurry.
They said,
“Get up! Take your wife and your two daughters who are here.
Or else you will be swept away when the city is punished for its guilt.”
[16] But Lot hesitated.
So the men grabbed his hand,
and the hands of his wife and his two daughters.
For the LORD was merciful to him.
They brought him out and only let go of him outside the city.

[17] When they had brought them out, one of them said,
“Run for your life!
Do not look behind you!
Do not stop anywhere in the whole plain!
Escape to the mountains, or else you will be swept away!”

[18] Lot said to them,
“Oh no, my lord!
[19] Look: Your servant has found favor in your eyes.
You have been very kind to me and saved my life.
But I cannot escape to the mountains.
Disaster might catch up with me, and then I will die.
[20] Look, there is a city nearby.
I can escape there. It is a little one.
Please let me escape there – it is only a little one, isn’t it?
Then I will stay alive.”

[21] He said to him,
“All right, I will grant you this request too.
I will not destroy the city you are talking about.
[22] Hurry, escape there!
For I cannot do anything until you get there.”
That is why the city is called Zoar.

[23] The sun had risen over the earth when Lot came to Zoar.

> **What does this mean?**
> Lot hesitates. He clings to his belongings and his life in Sodom. The angels literally have to pull him out by the hand.
> The most important sentence: “For the LORD was merciful to him.” Lot is not saved because he is so good, but because God is merciful – and because Abraham prayed for him.
> Even now, Lot is still bargaining. And God even grants him this request.
> “Zoar” means “little.” After all, Lot kept saying: “It is a little one.”

## Fire from the sky (Verses 24–29)

[24] Then the LORD rained sulfur and fire on Sodom and Gomorrah.
It came from the LORD, out of the sky.
Sulfur is a yellow substance that burns with a terrible smell.
[25] He destroyed those cities and the whole plain,
all the people who lived in the cities
and everything that grew on the ground.

[26] But Lot’s wife, who was behind him, looked back.
And she became a pillar of salt.

[27] Abraham got up early in the morning.
He went to the place where he had stood before the LORD.
[28] He looked down toward Sodom and Gomorrah and toward the whole land of the plain.
Then he saw: Smoke was rising from the land, like the smoke from a furnace.

[29] This is what happened:
When God destroyed the cities of the plain, God remembered Abraham.
So he brought Lot out of the middle of the destruction,
when he destroyed the cities where Lot had lived.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sodom and Gomorrah were probably in the area south of the Dead Sea. To this day, there is a lot of salt, sulfur and tar there. Some researchers think there was an earthquake or a natural disaster. The Bible says: It was God’s judgment.
> Lot’s wife looks back, even though this was forbidden. In her heart, she still clings to Sodom. Later, Jesus reminds people: Remember Lot’s wife! (Luke 17:32). This means: Whoever is saved should not hold on to the old, evil life.
> Abraham stands at the same place where he prayed for Sodom. He only sees smoke. There were not even 10 righteous people.
> But verse 29 says: “God remembered Abraham.” Abraham’s prayer was not in vain. Lot was saved.

## Lot and his daughters (Verses 30–38)

[30] Lot went up from Zoar into the mountains and lived there with his two daughters.
For he was afraid to stay in Zoar.
He lived in a cave with his two daughters.

[31] The older daughter said to the younger one,
“Our father is old.
And there is no man left on the earth to sleep with us, as is the custom all over the earth.
[32] Come, let’s give our father wine to drink.
Then we will sleep with him.
That way we can keep our father’s family line alive.”

[33] That night they gave their father wine to drink.
Then the older one went in and slept with her father.
He did not notice when she lay down or when she got up.

[34] The next day, the older one said to the younger one,
“Look, last night I slept with my father.
Let’s give him wine to drink again tonight.
Then you go in and sleep with him.
That way we can keep our father’s family line alive.”
[35] That night too they gave their father wine to drink.
Then the younger one went and slept with him.
He did not notice when she lay down or when she got up.

[36] So both of Lot’s daughters became pregnant by their father.
[37] The older one had a son and named him Moab.
He is the father of the Moabites to this day.
[38] The younger one also had a son and named him Ben Ammi.
He is the father of the people of Ammon to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a sad ending for Lot. He chose the most beautiful land – and in the end he lost everything: his belongings, his wife, his good name. Now he lives in a cave.
> The daughters believe they are the last people far and wide. In Sodom, they learned that you take what you want. What they do is incest – sex between a father and his daughters. Later, the Bible clearly forbids this (Leviticus 18).
> The Bible tells this without approving of it. It shows plainly where the path has led.
> Moab and Ammon later became neighboring peoples of Israel, east of the Jordan. Still, they are not lost: Ruth, a woman from Moab, becomes an ancestor of King David and of Jesus (Ruth 4; Matthew 1:5).
