# Genesis – Chapter 10
### The nations of the earth

---

## The sons of Noah (Verse 1)

[1] This is the story of the families of the sons of Noah: Shem, Ham and Japheth.
After the flood, sons were born to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is a long list of names. It is called the “Table of Nations.”
> Many names are at the same time names of people, of peoples and of countries. In ancient times, people thought: Every people comes from one forefather and carries his name.
> Names that end in “-ites” or “-im” are names of peoples – just as today we say “the Italians” or “the Texans.”
> The list is arranged in three parts: Japheth, Ham and Shem.

---

## The descendants of Japheth (Verses 2–5)

[2] The sons of Japheth were:
Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras.
[3] The sons of Gomer were:
Ashkenaz, Riphath and Togarmah.
[4] The sons of Javan were:
Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim and Dodanim.
[5] From these, the peoples of the coasts and islands spread out.
They live in their lands, each people with its own language,
by their families and nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> The descendants of Japheth lived mostly in the north and west: Javan stands for the Greeks. Madai stands for the Medes in what is now Iran. The Kittim lived on the island of Cyprus. Tarshish was a port city far in the west, maybe in Spain.
> Already here, it says as a matter of course: Every people has its own language. Variety is part of the world.

---

## The descendants of Ham (Verses 6–20)

[6] The sons of Ham were:
Cush, Mizraim, Put and Canaan.
[7] The sons of Cush were:
Seba, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah and Sabteca.
The sons of Raamah were:
Sheba and Dedan.

[8] Cush became the father of Nimrod.
Nimrod was the first to become a mighty ruler on the earth.
[9] He was a mighty hunter before the LORD.
That is why people say, “Like Nimrod, a mighty hunter before the LORD.”
[10] His kingdom began with Babel, Erech, Accad and Calneh in the land of Shinar.
[11] From that land he went into Assyria.
There he built Nineveh, Rehoboth Ir, Calah,
[12] and Resen between Nineveh and the great city Calah.

[13] Mizraim became the father of the Ludim, Anamim, Lehabim, Naphtuhim,
[14] Pathrusim, Casluhim – the Philistines came from them – and the Caphtorim.

[15] Canaan became the father of Sidon, his firstborn, and of Heth,
[16] and of the Jebusites, the Amorites and the Girgashites,
[17] the Hivites, the Arkites and the Sinites,
[18] the Arvadites, the Zemarites and the Hamathites.
Later, the families of the Canaanites spread out.
[19] The land of the Canaanites reached from Sidon toward Gerar, as far as Gaza,
and toward Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim, as far as Lasha.

[20] These are the sons of Ham, by their families and languages,
in their lands and nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> The descendants of Ham lived mostly in the south: Cush is the land south of Egypt (what is now Sudan). Mizraim is the Hebrew name for Egypt. Put is probably Libya.
> Nimrod is the first great ruler. He founds Babel and Nineveh. Later in the Bible, both cities become great enemies of Israel. Where people gather a lot of power, it becomes dangerous.
> The peoples of Canaan lived in the land that God will later promise to Abraham. That is why they are listed here so carefully.
> Sodom and Gomorrah will play a sad part later, in Chapter 19.

---

## The descendants of Shem (Verses 21–31)

[21] Children were also born to Shem.
He was the older brother of Japheth and the forefather of all the descendants of Eber.
[22] The sons of Shem were:
Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud and Aram.
[23] The sons of Aram were:
Uz, Hul, Gether and Mash.
[24] Arpachshad became the father of Shelah.
And Shelah became the father of Eber.
[25] Two sons were born to Eber.
One was named Peleg, which means “division.”
For in his days the earth was divided.
His brother was named Joktan.
[26] Joktan became the father of Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah,
[27] Hadoram, Uzal, Diklah,
[28] Obal, Abimael, Sheba,
[29] Ophir, Havilah and Jobab.
All these were sons of Joktan.
[30] The land where they lived reached from Mesha toward Sephar, the mountain in the east.

[31] These are the sons of Shem, by their families and languages,
in their lands and nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “Semites” come from Shem, for example the Hebrews (who later became the people of Israel), the Arameans and the Assyrians (Asshur).
> The word “Hebrew” probably comes from the name “Eber.” Here the Bible is already preparing to tell where Abraham comes from.
> “In his days the earth was divided” – this points to the story in the next chapter: the tower of Babel.

---

## All the nations (Verse 32)

[32] These are the families of the sons of Noah, by their family lines, in their nations.
From them, the nations spread out over the earth after the flood.

> **What does this mean?**
> If you count all the names, you get about 70 peoples. In the Bible, the number 70 stands for “all” – the whole world.
> The message: All the peoples of the earth belong to **one** human family. God cares about all of them, not just one people.
> This is important for what comes next: When God later chooses Abraham, he does it so that through him **all** peoples will be blessed (Chapter 12).
