# Genesis – Chapter 48
### Jacob blesses Ephraim and Manasseh

---

## Joseph visits his sick father (Verses 1–7)

[1] Some time later, someone told Joseph,
“Look, your father is sick.”
So he took his two sons Manasseh and Ephraim with him.
[2] Someone told Jacob,
“Look, your son Joseph is coming to you.”
Then Israel gathered all his strength and sat up in bed.

[3] Jacob said to Joseph,
“God Almighty appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan and blessed me.
[4] He said to me,
‘Look, I will make you fruitful and give you many descendants.
I will make you into a community of peoples.
And I will give this land to your descendants after you, as their property forever.’

[5] Your two sons were born to you in the land of Egypt, before I came to you in Egypt.
Now they are mine.
Ephraim and Manasseh will be mine, just as Reuben and Simeon are.
[6] But the children you have after them will be yours.
They will be counted under the names of their brothers when the land is shared out.

[7] When I was coming back from Paddan, Rachel died on the way, while I was with her in the land of Canaan.
There was still some distance to go to Ephrath.
I buried her there, by the road to Ephrath.
Ephrath is also called Bethlehem.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Luz is the old name of Bethel, where Jacob saw the stairway to heaven.
> Jacob accepts Joseph’s sons as his own sons. That means: Joseph receives a double share of the inheritance. Later, two tribes of Israel come from him: Ephraim and Manasseh.
> The double share really belonged to the firstborn, that is, to Reuben. But Reuben lost this right (Genesis 35:22). Now Joseph receives it, the first son of Rachel.
> Jacob thinks of Rachel. Even at the end of his life, he still grieves for his beloved wife. Maybe he is also explaining to Joseph: Rachel should have had more children. That is why I am taking your sons as my own.

---

## The grandfather and the grandsons (Verses 8–12)

[8] Then Israel saw Joseph’s sons and asked,
“Who are these?”
[9] Joseph said to his father,
“They are my sons, whom God has given me here.”
Jacob said,
“Please bring them to me, and I will bless them.”

[10] Israel’s eyes had grown weak with age.
He could not see well.
Joseph brought them close to him.
And he kissed them and hugged them.
[11] Israel said to Joseph,
“I did not think I would ever see your face again.
And look: God has even let me see your children.”

[12] Then Joseph took them from between his father’s knees.
And he bowed down with his face to the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like his father Isaac, Jacob is almost blind in his old age. Do you remember? When Isaac was blind, Jacob tricked him (Chapter 27). Now Jacob himself is the blind father who gives the blessing.
> Jacob is overwhelmed with thankfulness: he thought Joseph was dead. Now he even holds Joseph’s children in his arms.
> Taking the children on one’s knees was a sign: I accept them as my own.

---

## The younger before the older (Verses 13–20)

[13] Then Joseph took them both.
He held Ephraim with his right hand, toward Israel’s left hand.
And he held Manasseh with his left hand, toward Israel’s right hand.
So he brought them close to him.
[14] But Israel stretched out his right hand and laid it on Ephraim’s head, even though he was the younger.
And he laid his left hand on Manasseh’s head.
He crossed his hands on purpose,
for Manasseh was the firstborn.

[15] He blessed Joseph and said,
“The God whom my ancestors Abraham and Isaac followed,
the God who has fed me all my life long to this day,
[16] the angel who has rescued me from all harm –
may he bless the boys.
May my name live on in them,
and the name of my ancestors Abraham and Isaac.
May they grow into a great crowd on the earth.”

[17] Joseph saw that his father laid his right hand on Ephraim’s head.
He did not like that.
He took hold of his father’s hand to move it from Ephraim’s head to Manasseh’s head.
[18] Joseph said to his father,
“Not like that, my father!
This one is the firstborn.
Put your right hand on his head.”

[19] But his father refused.
He said,
“I know, my son, I know.
He too will become a people.
He too will be great.
But his younger brother will be greater than he is.
And his descendants will become a great number of nations.”

[20] So he blessed them that day and said,
“The people of Israel will use your name to bless others.
They will say, ‘May God make you like Ephraim and Manasseh!’”
So he put Ephraim ahead of Manasseh.

> **What does this mean?**
> The right hand was seen as the more important one. It was meant for the firstborn.
> Once again, the same thing happens as so often in the book of Genesis: the younger is put before the older. Abel before Cain, Isaac before Ishmael, Jacob before Esau, Joseph before his older brothers, Perez before Zerah. God does not keep to human rules. He often chooses the ones who are overlooked at first.
> Jacob, who once got the blessing by a trick, now gives it very deliberately and openly. He knows what he is doing.
> Jacob says that God has “fed” him. In Hebrew, this is the word for what a shepherd does. Jacob himself was a shepherd all his life. Now he sees: God has watched over me like a shepherd. This picture comes back later in the famous Psalm 23: “The LORD is my shepherd” (Psalm 23:1).
> To this day, Jewish fathers bless their sons on Friday evening with the words: “May God make you like Ephraim and Manasseh.”

## A special gift for Joseph (Verses 21–22)

[21] Then Israel said to Joseph,
“Look, I am dying.
But God will be with you.
He will bring you back to the land of your ancestors.
[22] And I give you one portion of land more than your brothers.
I took it from the Amorite with my sword and with my bow.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is sure: God will keep his promise. The family will return to Canaan.
> In Hebrew, the word for “portion” here is “Shechem.” That is also the name of the city of Shechem. Probably this land is meant. Later, Joseph is in fact buried there (Joshua 24:32).
> The Bible tells nothing else about a battle of Jacob against the Amorites. Maybe Jacob is thinking of his sons’ attack on Shechem (Chapter 34) or of some other event.
