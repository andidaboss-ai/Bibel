# Genesis – Chapter 41
### Joseph explains Pharaoh’s dreams and becomes ruler over Egypt

---

## Pharaoh’s dreams (Verses 1–8)

[1] At the end of two full years, Pharaoh had a dream.
Look: He was standing by the river.
[2] And look: Seven cows came up out of the river.
They were sleek and fat, and they were eating in the marsh grass.
[3] And look: Seven other cows came up out of the river after them.
They were ugly and thin.
They stood beside the other cows on the bank of the river.
[4] The ugly and thin cows ate up the seven sleek and fat cows.
Then Pharaoh woke up.

[5] He fell asleep and dreamed a second time.
Look: Seven heads of grain grew on one stalk, healthy and good.
[6] And look: After them, seven heads of grain sprang up, thin and dried out by the east wind.
[7] The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven healthy and full heads.
Then Pharaoh woke up.
And look: It was a dream.

[8] In the morning, Pharaoh was troubled.
He sent for all the magicians and all the wise men of Egypt.
Pharaoh told them his dreams.
But no one could explain them to Pharaoh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph has to wait two more years after the cup bearer forgot him.
> The river is the Nile, the great river of Egypt. Without it, Egypt would be only desert. Every year it flooded its banks and made the land fertile. When that did not happen, hunger came.
> The east wind is a hot, dry desert wind. It makes the grain dry up.
> The magicians of Egypt were famous. But here they do not know what to do. Only those to whom God explains his message can understand it.

---

## The cup bearer remembers (Verses 9–13)

[9] Then the chief cup bearer said to Pharaoh,
“Today I must think of my mistakes.
[10] Pharaoh was angry with his servants.
He put me under guard in the house of the captain of the guard, me and the chief baker.
[11] We both had a dream in one night, he and I.
Each dream had its own meaning.
[12] There was a young man with us there, a Hebrew, a servant of the captain of the guard.
We told him our dreams.
He explained them to us.
He explained to each man his own dream.
[13] And it happened exactly as he explained it to us:
Pharaoh gave me back my job, and he hanged the baker.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At last the cup bearer remembers. Now, at exactly the right moment.
> If Joseph had been set free two years earlier, maybe he would simply have gone home. Then he could not have saved Egypt and his family from hunger. God’s timing is sometimes hard to understand – but it has a purpose.

---

## Joseph before Pharaoh (Verses 14–24)

[14] Then Pharaoh sent for Joseph.
They quickly brought him out of the dungeon.
Joseph shaved, changed his clothes and came in to Pharaoh.
[15] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“I have had a dream, and no one can explain it.
I have heard people say about you: When you hear a dream, you can explain it.”
[16] Joseph answered Pharaoh,
“It is not in me.
God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace.”

[17] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“In my dream – look: I was standing on the bank of the river.
[18] And look: Seven fat and sleek cows came up out of the river.
They were eating in the marsh grass.
[19] And look: Seven other cows came up after them,
weak, very ugly and thin.
I have never seen such ugly cows in all the land of Egypt.
[20] The thin and ugly cows ate up the first seven fat cows.
[21] And when they had eaten them up, no one could tell that they had eaten them.
They were still as ugly as at the beginning.
Then I woke up.

[22] Then I saw in my dream – look:
Seven heads of grain grew on one stalk, full and good.
[23] And look: After them, seven heads of grain sprang up,
dried up, thin and dried out by the east wind.
[24] The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven good heads of grain.
I told it to the magicians.
But no one could explain it to me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph shaves, because the Egyptians – unlike the Hebrews – were clean-shaven. He fits in, so that he can appear before the king.
> Joseph is now a grown-up man. Earlier he proudly told about his dreams. Now he says humbly: “It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer.”
> Pharaoh tells the dream once more and adds something: The thin cows stay thin, even though they have eaten the fat ones. That makes the message even clearer.

---

## Joseph explains the dreams (Verses 25–32)

[25] Joseph said to Pharaoh,
“Pharaoh’s dreams are one and the same.
God has told Pharaoh what he is about to do.
[26] The seven good cows are seven years.
And the seven good heads of grain are seven years.
It is one and the same dream.
[27] The seven thin and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years.
And the seven empty heads of grain, dried out by the east wind, are also seven years.
They will be seven years of famine.

[28] That is what I have said to Pharaoh:
God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do.
[29] Look: Seven years of great plenty are coming in all the land of Egypt.
[30] After them, seven years of famine will come.
Then all the plenty in the land of Egypt will be forgotten.
The famine will use up the land.
[31] No one will notice the plenty in the land anymore, because of the famine that follows.
For it will be very severe.
[32] Pharaoh had the dream twice.
That means: God has firmly decided it.
And God will soon make it happen.”
A famine is a time when there is not enough food.

> **What does this mean?**
> Five times in this conversation Joseph says “God.” In front of the most powerful king in the world, he speaks openly about his God.
> The dream comes twice – that means: It is completely certain. It was the same with Joseph’s own dreams (Chapter 37): They came twice too.

---

## Joseph’s advice (Verses 33–36)

[33] “So now let Pharaoh look for a man who is wise and understanding.
Let him put this man in charge of the land of Egypt.
[34] Let Pharaoh do this.
Let him appoint officials over the land.
In the seven years of plenty, let him collect a fifth of the harvest of the land of Egypt.
[35] Let them gather all the food of these good years that are coming.
Let them store the grain under Pharaoh’s control as food in the cities, and let them keep it safe.
[36] This food will be a supply for the land, for the seven years of famine that will come in the land of Egypt.
Then the land will not be ruined by the famine.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph does not only explain the dream. He also has a wise plan right away: In good times, prepare for bad times.
> A fifth is 20 percent of the harvest. That was a high tax, but people could manage it.
> God’s message is not a threat without a way out. It is a warning, so that people can act in time and save lives.

---

## Joseph becomes the second man in Egypt (Verses 37–46)

[37] The plan pleased Pharaoh and all his servants.
[38] Pharaoh said to his servants,
“Can we find anyone like this man, a man in whom is the Spirit of God?”
[39] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“God has shown you all of this.
So there is no one as wise and understanding as you.
[40] You will be in charge of my house.
All my people will follow your orders.
Only because of my throne will I be greater than you.”

[41] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“Look, I have put you in charge of all the land of Egypt.”
[42] Pharaoh took his signet ring off his hand and put it on Joseph’s hand.
A signet ring is a ring with a seal.
He dressed him in robes of fine linen.
And he put a gold chain around his neck.
[43] He let him ride in his second chariot.
And people shouted in front of him, “Bow the knee!”
So he put him in charge of all the land of Egypt.

[44] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“I am Pharaoh.
But without your permission, no one in all the land of Egypt will lift a hand or a foot.”
[45] Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-Paneah.
And he gave him Asenath as his wife.
She was the daughter of Potiphera, the priest of On.
And Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

[46] Joseph was 30 years old when he stood before Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.
Joseph went out from Pharaoh and traveled through all the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> In a single day: from prison to the second most powerful man in the world. That is how fast God can turn a life around.
> Even Pharaoh, who believed in many gods, recognizes: The Spirit of God is in this man.
> The signet ring was like the king’s signature. Whoever wore it could give orders in Pharaoh’s name.
> Once again clothing plays a part: The brothers took away the colorful robe. He lost his clothes at Potiphar’s house when he ran away. Now he gets royal robes.
> Zaphenath-Paneah is an Egyptian name. Its meaning is not quite certain. Maybe it means “God speaks, and he lives.”
> On is the city of Heliopolis, a center of the sun god, near today’s Cairo.
> It has been 13 years since Joseph was sold at the age of 17. 13 years of slavery and prison. Now he is 30.

---

## The seven good years (Verses 47–52)

[47] In the seven years of plenty, the land produced huge harvests.
[48] Joseph gathered all the food of the seven years in the land of Egypt.
He stored the food in the cities.
In each city he stored the food from the fields around that city.
[49] So Joseph stored up grain like the sand of the sea, very much,
until he stopped counting.
For it could not be counted.

[50] Before the years of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph.
Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, the priest of On, gave birth to them.
[51] Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh.
For he said,
“God has made me forget all my hardship and all my father’s house.”
[52] He named the second one Ephraim.
For he said,
“God has made me fruitful in the land of my suffering.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Like the sand of the sea” – that is how many descendants God had promised Abraham. Now there is that much grain.
> Manasseh sounds like “make forget.” Ephraim sounds like “doubly fruitful.”
> Joseph has accepted his new life. The pain over what his family did to him is healed. But he has not completely forgotten it – the next chapter shows that.
> Later, Manasseh and Ephraim become two separate tribes of Israel.

---

## The famine comes (Verses 53–57)

[53] The seven years of plenty in the land of Egypt came to an end.
[54] Then the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said.
There was famine in all the lands.
But in all the land of Egypt there was bread.

[55] Then all the land of Egypt became hungry too.
The people cried out to Pharaoh for bread.
Pharaoh said to all the Egyptians,
“Go to Joseph!
Do what he tells you.”
[56] The famine was over the whole earth.
Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians.
For the famine was severe in the land of Egypt.
[57] And all the countries came to Egypt, to Joseph, to buy grain.
For the famine was severe in the whole earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything happens as God showed. Because Joseph planned ahead, people have food – not only in Egypt, but in the whole region.
> “Do what he tells you” – here Christians remember the wedding at Cana. There Mary says about Jesus: Do whatever he tells you (John 2:5).
> The last verse prepares for what is coming: If all the countries come to Egypt, then Jacob’s family from Canaan will come too.
