# Genesis – Chapter 45
### “I am Joseph!”

---

## Joseph tells them who he is (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then Joseph could no longer control himself in front of all the people standing around him.
He called out,
“Send everyone out from me!”
So no one else was with him when Joseph told his brothers who he was.
[2] He wept loudly.
The Egyptians heard it.
The household of Pharaoh heard it too.

[3] Joseph said to his brothers,
“I am Joseph!
Is my father still alive?”
But his brothers could not answer him.
They were terrified in front of him.

[4] Joseph said to his brothers,
“Please come closer to me.”
And they came closer.
He said,
“I am Joseph, your brother,
whom you sold into Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah’s speech has touched Joseph deeply. He sees: my brothers are not the same anymore. Now he can no longer pretend.
> He sends all the Egyptians out. What comes now belongs to the family alone.
> “I am Joseph!” – three words that change everything. The brothers are frozen with fear. The powerful ruler they trembled before is their brother. The brother they sold.
> Joseph tells the truth quite openly: “whom you sold into Egypt.” He hides nothing. But he says it without blaming them.

---

## God turned it to good (Verses 5–8)

[5] Now do not be upset, and do not be angry with yourselves that you sold me here.
For God sent me ahead of you to save lives.
[6] The famine has been in the land for two years now.
And there are still five more years in which there will be no plowing and no harvest.
[7] God sent me ahead of you,
so that some of you would be left alive on the earth,
and to keep you alive by a great rescue.
[8] So it was not you who sent me here, but God.
He has made me a father to Pharaoh,
lord of his whole household
and ruler over the whole land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the heart of the whole story of Joseph. Joseph sees his life with new eyes: behind all the suffering, behind the pit, the slavery and the prison, God was following a plan.
> The brothers did evil. Joseph says that very clearly. But God made something good out of it: the rescue of many people.
> Joseph forgives his brothers. He does not want them to go on tormenting themselves.
> It says: God has made me a “father to Pharaoh.” That was a title for the king’s most important adviser.

---

## Bring my father! (Verses 9–15)

[9] Hurry and go up to my father and tell him,
‘This is what your son Joseph says:
“God has made me lord of all Egypt.
Come down to me. Do not wait.
[10] You will live in the land of Goshen.
Then you will be near me,
you and your children and your grandchildren,
your flocks, your herds and everything you have.
[11] I will take care of you there.
For there are still five years of famine to come.
Otherwise you would become poor, you and your household and everything you have.”’

[12] Look, you can see it with your own eyes, and so can my brother Benjamin:
it is really I who am speaking to you.
[13] Tell my father about all my glory in Egypt
and about everything you have seen.
Hurry and bring my father down here.”

[14] Then Joseph hugged his brother Benjamin around the neck and wept.
And Benjamin wept on his neck.
[15] He kissed all his brothers and wept as he held them.
After that, his brothers talked with him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Goshen is a fertile area in the northeast of Egypt, in the Nile Delta. There was good pasture there for the herds.
> Joseph wants to see his father again as quickly as possible.
> Joseph first hugs Benjamin, then all his brothers. He weeps with each one.
> The last sentence is very simple, but beautiful: “After that, his brothers talked with him.” Earlier, “they could not speak a friendly word to him” (Genesis 37:4). Now they talk with each other again. The family is reconciled.

---

## Pharaoh invites the family (Verses 16–20)

[16] The news reached Pharaoh’s household:
“Joseph’s brothers have come!”
This pleased Pharaoh and his officials very much.
[17] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“Tell your brothers,
‘Do this: load your animals and travel to the land of Canaan.
[18] Get your father and your households and come to me.
I will give you the best of the land of Egypt.
You will eat the richest food of the land.’
[19] You are also to give them this order:
‘Do this: take wagons from the land of Egypt
for your little children and for your wives.
Bring your father and come.
[20] And do not worry about your belongings.
For the best of all the land of Egypt is yours.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Pharaoh is generous. He knows: Joseph has saved Egypt. Now he wants to do something good for Joseph’s family.
> Back then, wagons were something special. There were hardly any in the land of Canaan. People traveled on foot or on donkeys.

---

## The brothers go home (Verses 21–28)

[21] The sons of Israel did this.
Joseph gave them wagons, as Pharaoh had ordered.
And he gave them food for the journey.
[22] He gave each of them a change of clothes.
But to Benjamin he gave 300 pieces of silver and five changes of clothes.
[23] He sent this to his father:
ten donkeys loaded with the best things of Egypt,
and ten female donkeys loaded with grain, bread and food for his father’s journey.
[24] Then he sent his brothers on their way, and they left.
He said to them,
“Do not quarrel on the way!”

[25] So they went up out of Egypt and came to their father Jacob in the land of Canaan.
[26] They told him,
“Joseph is still alive!
And he is ruler over the whole land of Egypt!”
But Jacob’s heart went numb,
for he did not believe them.

[27] Then they told him everything Joseph had said to them.
And he saw the wagons Joseph had sent to carry him.
Then the spirit of their father Jacob came back to life.
[28] Israel said,
“It is enough! My son Joseph is still alive!
I will go and see him before I die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph once received a special robe, and that caused jealousy. Now he gives clothes to all his brothers.
> Benjamin again gets more. But this time the brothers do not become jealous.
> “Do not quarrel on the way!” – maybe Joseph is winking a little. He knows his brothers. Maybe he also means: do not blame each other now for what happened back then.
> At first Jacob cannot believe it. He has mourned for Joseph for 22 years. Only when he sees the wagons does it become real.
> The brothers probably also have to tell their father the truth now: that Joseph was not eaten by a wild animal. The Bible does not say how this conversation went.
