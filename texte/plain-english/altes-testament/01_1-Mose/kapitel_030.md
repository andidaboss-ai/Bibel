# Genesis – Chapter 30
### Jacob’s children – and Jacob becomes rich

---

## Rachel and her servant Bilhah (Verses 1–8)

[1] Rachel saw that she did not give birth to any children for Jacob.
So she became jealous of her sister.
She said to Jacob,
“Give me children, or else I will die!”
[2] Then Jacob became very angry with Rachel.
He said,
“Am I in God’s place?
He is the one who has kept you from having children.”

[3] Rachel said,
“Look, here is my servant Bilhah.
Sleep with her.
She can give birth on my knees.
Then I too can have children through her.”
[4] So she gave him her servant Bilhah as a wife.
And Jacob slept with her.
[5] Bilhah became pregnant and gave birth to a son for Jacob.
[6] Rachel said,
“God has judged in my favor.
He has also heard my voice and has given me a son.”
That is why she named him Dan.

[7] Bilhah, Rachel’s servant, became pregnant again.
She gave birth to a second son for Jacob.
[8] Rachel said,
“I have wrestled with my sister in a mighty struggle.
And I have won.”
She named him Naphtali.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rachel is loved, but she has no children. Leah has children, but she is not loved. Both sisters suffer – and they are jealous of each other.
> Jacob’s answer is harsh, but true: Children are a gift from God. No person can “make” them.
> Rachel does the same thing as Sarah with Hagar: She gives her servant to her husband. “Give birth on my knees” was a custom: After the birth, the mistress took the child on her lap. That showed: This is my child.
> Dan means “He has judged in my favor.” Naphtali means “my struggle.”

---

## Leah and her servant Zilpah (Verses 9–13)

[9] Leah saw that she had stopped having children.
So she took her servant Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife.
[10] Zilpah, Leah’s servant, gave birth to a son for Jacob.
[11] Leah said,
“How lucky!”
She named him Gad.
[12] Zilpah, Leah’s servant, gave birth to a second son for Jacob.
[13] Leah said,
“How happy I am!
The women will call me happy.”
She named him Asher.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Leah copies her sister. It is a real contest between the sisters for children.
> Gad means “luck.” Asher means “happy.”

---

## The mandrakes (Verses 14–21)

[14] At the time of the wheat harvest, Reuben went out into the field.
There he found mandrakes.
He brought them to his mother Leah.
Then Rachel said to Leah,
“Please give me some of your son’s mandrakes.”
[15] Leah said to her,
“Isn’t it enough that you have taken my husband away from me?
Do you want to take my son’s mandrakes too?”
Rachel said,
“All right. Then Jacob will sleep with you tonight.
In return, I get your son’s mandrakes.”

[16] In the evening, Jacob came home from the field.
Leah went out to meet him and said,
“You must sleep with me tonight.
For I have paid for you with my son’s mandrakes.”
And he slept with her that night.

[17] God listened to Leah.
She became pregnant and gave birth to a fifth son for Jacob.
[18] Leah said,
“God has paid me back, because I gave my servant to my husband.”
She named him Issachar.

[19] Leah became pregnant again.
She gave birth to a sixth son for Jacob.
[20] Leah said,
“God has given me a good gift.
Now my husband will live with me,
because I have given birth to six sons for him.”
She named him Zebulun.

[21] After that, she gave birth to a daughter.
She named her Dinah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mandrakes are the fruits of a plant. Back then, people believed they helped a woman to become pregnant.
> The scene is sad: Two sisters trade their husband like goods. Leah has to “buy” her own husband so that he spends a night with her.
> But it is not the mandrakes that help. It is God: Rachel has the mandrakes – and Leah gets the children.
> Issachar sounds like “pay.” Zebulun sounds like “gift” and “live with.”
> Dinah is the only daughter of Jacob who is named. She becomes important in Chapter 34.

---

## At last: Joseph (Verses 22–24)

[22] Then God remembered Rachel.
God listened to her and let her have children.
[23] She became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
She said,
“God has taken away my shame.”
[24] She named him Joseph and said,
“May the LORD give me another son!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “God remembered Rachel” – just like “God remembered Noah.” After long years of waiting, God turns to her.
> Back then, having no children was seen as a shame. At last, Rachel is free of this burden.
> Joseph means “May he take away” and “May he add.” Rachel wishes for another son. This wish will come true – but it will cost her her life (Chapter 35).
> Later, Joseph becomes one of the most important people in the whole book of Genesis.

## Jacob wants to go home (Verses 25–34)

[25] After Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban,
“Let me go.
I want to go back to my own place and to my own country.
[26] Give me my wives and my children.
I have worked for you for them.
Let me go.
For you know how hard I have worked for you.”

[27] Laban said to him,
“If you are pleased with me, please stay here.
I have learned by signs that the LORD has blessed me because of you.”
[28] He said,
“Tell me what pay you want, and I will give it.”

[29] Jacob said to him,
“You know how I have worked for you,
and how your livestock have done in my care.
[30] You had only a little before I came.
Now it has grown into very much.
The LORD has blessed you wherever I went.
But when will I take care of my own family too?”

[31] Laban asked,
“What should I give you?”
Jacob said,
“You don’t need to give me anything.
If you will do this one thing for me, then I will again feed your flock and take care of it.
[32] Today I will go through your whole flock.
I will take out every speckled and spotted animal,
every black one among the sheep,
and the spotted and speckled ones among the goats.
That will be my pay.
[33] So later my honesty will speak for me,
when you come to check my pay.
Every goat with me that is not speckled and spotted,
and every sheep with me that is not black,
will count as stolen.”
[34] Laban said,
“Good! Let it be as you have said.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob has now worked 14 years for his wives. He wants to go home.
> Laban does not want to let him go. He has noticed: Since Jacob came, he keeps getting richer. God blesses him because of Jacob. “Learned by signs” means: Laban used fortune-telling.
> Jacob makes an offer that looks very good for Laban: Sheep were mostly white, goats mostly black or brown. There were only a few spotted animals. So Jacob asks only for the rare animals.

---

## Laban cheats – Jacob becomes rich anyway (Verses 35–43)

[35] But that same day, Laban took out the male goats that were striped and spotted,
all the female goats that were speckled and spotted, every one that had white on it,
and all the black ones among the sheep.
He gave them to his sons.
[36] He put a distance of three days’ journey between himself and Jacob.
And Jacob fed the rest of Laban’s flocks.

[37] Then Jacob took fresh sticks from poplar, almond and plane trees.
He peeled white stripes into them.
So the white wood inside the sticks could be seen.
[38] He set the peeled sticks in front of the flocks, in the water troughs
where the flocks came to drink.
The animals mated when they came to drink.
[39] So the flocks mated in front of the sticks.
And the flocks had young ones that were striped, speckled and spotted.

[40] Jacob separated the lambs.
He turned the faces of the flock toward the striped and all the black animals in Laban’s flock.
He kept his own herds apart.
He did not put them with Laban’s flock.
[41] Whenever the stronger animals of the flock mated,
Jacob laid the sticks in front of their eyes in the water troughs,
so that they would mate among the sticks.
[42] But when the animals were weak, he did not put the sticks there.
So the weaker animals were Laban’s, and the stronger ones were Jacob’s.

[43] So the man became very, very rich.
He had large flocks, female servants and male servants, camels and donkeys.

> **What does this mean?**
> Laban cheats right away: He takes away all the spotted animals before Jacob can get them. Then he takes them three days’ journey away.
> Jacob tries a trick: Back then, people believed that what an animal sees while mating affects how its young look. Today we know that this is not true. Color is passed on from the parents.
> In the next chapter, Jacob himself explains what really happened: God took care of it, as Jacob saw in a dream (Genesis 31:10–12). It was not the sticks that made Jacob rich. It was God.
> Jacob is clever and skillful. But he is still a man who works with tricks. Two tricksters meet here: Laban and Jacob.
