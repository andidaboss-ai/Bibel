# Genesis – Chapter 16
### Hagar and Ishmael

---

## Sarai’s plan (Verses 1–6)

[1] Sarai, Abram’s wife, had not given him any children.
She had a servant woman from Egypt.
Her name was Hagar.
[2] Sarai said to Abram,
“Listen: The LORD has kept me from having children.
Please sleep with my servant.
Maybe I can have children through her.”
And Abram listened to Sarai.

[3] So Sarai, Abram’s wife, took her Egyptian servant Hagar
and gave her to her husband Abram to be his wife.
That was after Abram had lived in the land of Canaan for ten years.
[4] Abram slept with Hagar, and she became pregnant.
When Hagar saw that she was pregnant, she looked down on her mistress.
Her mistress was the woman she served.

[5] Then Sarai said to Abram,
“The wrong that is being done to me is your fault!
I put my servant into your arms.
And now that she sees that she is pregnant, she looks down on me.
May the LORD judge between me and you!”

[6] But Abram said to Sarai,
“Look, your servant is in your hands.
Do with her whatever you think is right.”
Then Sarai treated her harshly.
And Hagar ran away from her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abram and Sarai have been waiting for ten years. God’s promise is not coming true. So Sarai takes matters into her own hands.
> Back then, it was common: If a woman could not have children, she could give her servant to her husband. The servant’s child then counted as her own child. For us today, that is hard to understand. Hagar is not asked. She is a slave.
> Their own plan only brings conflict: Hagar becomes proud, Sarai becomes jealous and harsh, and Abram avoids his responsibility.
> The Bible tells this honestly. It shows: When we try to force God’s promises with our own means, suffering often follows.

## The God who sees me (Verses 7–14)

[7] The angel of the LORD found Hagar by a spring of water in the wilderness,
by the spring on the road to Shur.
[8] He said,
“Hagar, Sarai’s servant, where have you come from? And where are you going?”
She answered,
“I am running away from my mistress Sarai.”

[9] The angel of the LORD said to her,
“Go back to your mistress and put yourself under her authority.”
[10] And the angel of the LORD said to her,
“I will make your descendants very many.
There will be so many that no one will be able to count them.”
[11] And the angel of the LORD said to her,
“Look, you are pregnant.
You will have a son.
You are to name him Ishmael.
For the LORD has heard your misery.
[12] He will be like a wild donkey among people.
His hand will be against everyone, and everyone’s hand against him.
He will live in conflict with all his brothers.”

[13] Then Hagar gave a name to the LORD who had spoken to her:
“You are a God who sees.”
For she said,
“Have I really stayed alive after seeing him?”
[14] That is why the well is called Beer Lahai Roi.
That means: “well of the Living One who sees me.”
It is between Kadesh and Bered.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hagar is a slave, a foreigner, pregnant and alone in the wilderness. No one cares about her. But God looks for her and finds her.
> The “angel of the LORD” is a messenger of God. Often God himself speaks through him. That is why Hagar says afterward: I have seen God.
> The angel calls her by her name. He asks about her story: Where have you come from? Where are you going?
> “Ishmael” means “God hears.” The child’s name is a reminder forever: God has heard Hagar’s misery.
> Back then, a “wild donkey” was not an insult. It means: free, strong, independent, not to be tamed – like someone who lives in the desert.
> Hagar is the first person in the Bible to give God a name: “You are a God who sees.” That is a great comfort for everyone who feels overlooked.

## Ishmael is born (Verses 15–16)

[15] Hagar had a son by Abram.
Abram named his son, whom Hagar gave birth to, Ishmael.
[16] Abram was 86 years old when Hagar gave birth to Ishmael for Abram.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abram gives the child exactly the name the angel said. So Hagar told him about the meeting, and Abram believes her.
> Many Arabs and Muslims see Ishmael as their forefather.
