# Genesis – Chapter 25
### Abraham dies – Esau and Jacob are born

---

## Abraham’s other children (Verses 1–6)

[1] Abraham took another wife. Her name was Keturah.
[2] She had these sons with him: Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak and Shuah.
[3] Jokshan became the father of Sheba and Dedan.
The sons of Dedan were Asshurim, Letushim and Leummim.
[4] The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida and Eldaah.
All these were the descendants of Keturah.

[5] Abraham gave everything he owned to Isaac.
[6] But to the sons of his concubines, Abraham gave gifts.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
While Abraham was still alive, he sent these sons away from his son Isaac.
He sent them east, to the land of the east.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham has many more children even after Sarah’s death. God’s promise to make him the “father of many nations” comes true in this way too.
> The Midianites come from Midian. They appear often later on, for example in the story of Moses.
> Isaac is the main heir. But the other sons are not forgotten: Abraham takes care of them with gifts. Then he sends them away, so that there will be no fight about the inheritance later.

---

## Abraham dies (Verses 7–11)

[7] Abraham lived to be 175 years old.
That is how long Abraham lived.
[8] Then he breathed his last and died at a good old age.
He was an old man and had lived a full life.
And he was gathered to his people.

[9] His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah.
It is in the field of Ephron, the son of Zohar the Hittite, near Mamre.
[10] That was the field that Abraham had bought from the people of Heth.
Abraham was buried there with his wife Sarah.

[11] After Abraham’s death, God blessed his son Isaac.
Isaac lived near the well Beer Lahai Roi.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Had lived a full life” – Abraham had a rich, complete life. He has lived enough and can go in peace, just as God had promised him: “You will be buried at a good old age” (Genesis 15:15).
> “To be gathered to his people” is an old way of saying: to die and to go to those who have already died. It sounds like hope: death is not simply the end.
> A beautiful detail: Isaac and Ishmael bury their father **together**. At their father’s grave, the brothers come together again.
> God’s blessing now passes on to Isaac.

---

## The family of Ishmael (Verses 12–18)

[12] This is the family history of Ishmael, Abraham’s son.
Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah’s servant, had given birth to him for Abraham.
[13] These are the names of the sons of Ishmael, in the order they were born:
The firstborn of Ishmael was Nebaioth, then came Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,
[14] Mishma, Dumah, Massa,
[15] Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish and Kedemah.
[16] These are the sons of Ishmael, and these are their names.
Their villages and their camps are named after them.
They are twelve princes, each with his own people.

[17] Ishmael lived to be 137 years old.
Then he breathed his last and died.
And he was gathered to his people.
[18] His descendants lived from Havilah to Shur.
Shur is near Egypt, on the way toward Assyria.
Ishmael lived opposite all his relatives.

> **What does this mean?**
> God keeps his promise to Hagar and Abraham: Ishmael becomes a great people. “He will become the father of twelve princes” (Genesis 17:20).
> Kedar and Nebaioth later were well-known Arab tribes in the desert.
> “Opposite all his relatives” reminds us of the angel’s words to Hagar (Genesis 16:12). Ishmael’s descendants live on their own, apart from the others.

## Two nations in one womb (Verses 19–26)

[19] This is the family history of Isaac, Abraham’s son.
Abraham became the father of Isaac.
[20] Isaac was 40 years old when he married Rebekah.
She was the daughter of Bethuel the Syrian from Paddan Aram.
And she was the sister of Laban the Syrian.

[21] Isaac prayed to the LORD for his wife,
because she could not have children.
The LORD answered his prayer.
And his wife Rebekah became pregnant.

[22] The children pushed against each other inside her.
She said,
“If it is like this, why am I even alive?”
And she went to ask the LORD.
[23] The LORD said to her,
“Two nations are in your womb.
Two peoples will come from your body and go separate ways.
One people will be stronger than the other.
And the older will serve the younger.”

[24] When the time came for her to give birth –
look: there were twins in her womb.
[25] The first one came out.
He was red all over and hairy, like a coat of fur.
They named him Esau.
[26] After that, his brother came out.
His hand was holding on to Esau’s heel.
He was named Jacob.
Isaac was 60 years old when she gave birth to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paddan Aram is the area around the city of Haran in Mesopotamia. Abraham’s relatives lived there.
> Rebekah, too, cannot have children at first – just like Sarah. Isaac and Rebekah wait 20 years. Isaac prays, and God hears.
> The pregnancy is hard. Rebekah brings her distress to God. The answer: the two children will become two nations. And against all the rules of that time, the younger will stand above the older.
> “Esau” sounds like “hairy.” Esau also has a second name: Edom, which means “red.”
> In Hebrew, “Jacob” sounds like “heel.” It can also mean “one who holds on” or “one who cheats.” That fits his story.

---

## Two very different brothers (Verses 27–28)

[27] The boys grew up.
Esau became a skillful hunter, a man who lived out in the open country.
Jacob was a quiet man who stayed in the tents.
[28] Isaac loved Esau, because he liked to eat the wild game Esau hunted.
But Rebekah loved Jacob.

> **What does this mean?**
> The twins are completely different: Esau is the wild, strong outdoors type. Jacob is the quiet, clever type who prefers to stay at home.
> A big problem: each parent has a favorite son. Later this will lead to much conflict and pain. Parents should not love their children differently.

---

## The lentil stew (Verses 29–34)

[29] One day Jacob was cooking stew.
Esau came in from the field.
He was starving.
[30] Esau said to Jacob,
“Please let me eat some of that red stew,
because I am starving.”
That is why he was called Edom.
Edom means “red.”

[31] Jacob said,
“First sell me your birthright.”
[32] Esau said,
“Look, I am about to die.
What good is the birthright to me?”
[33] Jacob said,
“Swear it to me first.”
So Esau swore it to him.
And so he sold his birthright to Jacob.

[34] Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew.
Esau ate and drank.
Then he got up and went on his way.
So Esau treated his birthright as worthless.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, the “birthright” belonged to the oldest son. He received a double share of the inheritance. After his father, he became the head of the family. In this family it meant even more: whoever had it carried on God’s promise to Abraham.
> Esau sells this great gift for a bowl of lentil soup. He thinks only of his hunger in the moment. He does not see what he is losing forever.
> But Jacob is not a good example either. He coldly takes advantage of his brother’s weakness.
> In the end, the Bible judges Esau: he treated his birthright as worthless. In the New Testament, he is named as a warning (Hebrews 12:16).
> In German there is a saying to this day: “to sell something for a dish of lentils.” It means: to give away something very valuable for almost nothing.
