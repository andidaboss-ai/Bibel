# Genesis – Chapter 50
### Jacob’s burial – Joseph forgives his brothers – Joseph dies

---

## Mourning for Jacob (Verses 1–3)

[1] Joseph threw himself on his father’s face.
He wept over him and kissed him.
[2] Then Joseph ordered his servants, the doctors, to embalm his father.
And the doctors embalmed Israel.
[3] It took 40 days,
for that is how long it takes to embalm.
The Egyptians mourned for Israel for 70 days.

> **What does this mean?**
> To embalm means: to treat a dead body with special ointments and oils so that it does not decay. This is how the Egyptians made mummies. It was necessary because Jacob was still to be carried a long way to Canaan.
> 70 days of mourning – almost as long as the Egyptians mourned for a Pharaoh. Jacob is greatly honored in Egypt.

---

## The great funeral procession to Canaan (Verses 4–14)

[4] When the days of mourning for him were over, Joseph spoke to the people at Pharaoh’s court.
He said,
“If I have found favor with you, please speak to Pharaoh for me.
Tell him,
[5] ‘My father made me swear an oath.
He said, “Look, I am dying.
Bury me in my grave that I dug for myself in the land of Canaan.”
So please let me go up and bury my father.
Then I will come back.’”
[6] Pharaoh said,
“Go up and bury your father, just as he made you swear.”

[7] So Joseph went up to bury his father.
With him went all of Pharaoh’s officials,
the leaders of his household
and all the leaders of the land of Egypt,
[8] and also Joseph’s whole household, his brothers and his father’s household.
Only their little children, their flocks and their herds stayed behind in the land of Goshen.
[9] Chariots and horsemen also went up with him.
It was a very large crowd.

[10] They came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan.
There they mourned with very great and bitter mourning.
Joseph mourned for his father for seven days.
[11] The people who lived in the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad.
They said,
“This is a time of deep mourning for the Egyptians.”
That is why the place was named Abel Mizraim.
It is beyond the Jordan.

[12] Jacob’s sons did for him just what he had told them.
[13] His sons carried him into the land of Canaan.
They buried him in the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre.
Abraham had bought it, together with the field, from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place of his own.

[14] After he had buried his father, Joseph went back to Egypt,
he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph keeps his promise to his father.
> Joseph has to ask Pharaoh for permission. He promises to come back. He is powerful, but he is also a servant of Pharaoh.
> A threshing floor is a hard, flat place where people beat the grain out of the plants.
> “Abel Mizraim” means “mourning of the Egyptians” or “meadow of the Egyptians.”
> A huge Egyptian funeral procession goes into the promised land. It is almost like a foretaste of the exodus from Egypt many years later.

---

## The brothers are afraid (Verses 15–18)

[15] Joseph’s brothers saw that their father was dead.
Then they said,
“Maybe Joseph will hate us now.
Maybe he will pay us back in full for all the evil we did to him.”
[16] So they sent a message to Joseph:
“Before he died, your father gave this order:
[17] ‘This is what you are to say to Joseph:
“Please forgive your brothers for the wrong they did and for their sin,
because they did evil to you.”’
So now, please forgive the wrong done by the servants of the God of your father.”
When they spoke to him, Joseph wept.

[18] Then his brothers came themselves.
They threw themselves down before him and said,
“Look, we are your slaves.”

> **What does this mean?**
> For 17 years, the brothers have lived in Egypt with Joseph. Still they are afraid. Maybe they thought: Joseph was only kind because of our father. Now that our father is dead, he will take revenge.
> They send a message that is supposed to be from Jacob. We do not know whether Jacob really said that. Maybe the brothers made it up because they were so afraid.
> Joseph weeps. It hurts him that his brothers still do not trust him.
> For the last time, the brothers bow down before Joseph. They offer themselves to him as slaves. Back then, they sold him as a slave.

---

## “You planned evil – God planned it for good” (Verses 19–21)

[19] But Joseph said to them,
“Do not be afraid.
Am I in the place of God?
[20] You planned evil against me.
But God planned it for good.
He did it to keep many people alive, as is happening today.
[21] So do not be afraid.
I will take care of you and your little children.”
So he comforted them and spoke kindly to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important sentences in the whole Bible: “You planned evil against me. But God planned it for good.”
> Joseph does not gloss over anything. What the brothers did was evil. But God is greater than the evil people do. He can make something good even out of great wrong.
> “Am I in the place of God?” means: it is not my task to punish you. Judgment belongs to God. And I forgive you.
> Joseph does not forgive only with words. He takes care of his brothers and their children. Real forgiveness shows itself in actions.
> The book of Genesis began with a family in which one brother killed the other (Cain and Abel). It ends with a family in which a brother forgives his brothers.

---

## Joseph’s last years and his death (Verses 22–26)

[22] Joseph stayed in Egypt, he and his father’s household.
Joseph lived to be 110 years old.
[23] Joseph saw Ephraim’s children to the third generation.
The children of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were also born on Joseph’s knees.

[24] Joseph said to his brothers,
“I am dying.
But God will surely come to help you.
He will bring you up out of this land
to the land he promised with an oath to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob.”
[25] Joseph made the people of Israel swear an oath.
He said,
“God will surely come to help you.
Then you must carry my bones up from here.”

[26] So Joseph died at the age of 110.
They embalmed him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Egypt, 110 years was thought to be the ideal, perfect age.
> “Born on Joseph’s knees” means: Joseph accepted them as his own children, just as Jacob did with Ephraim and Manasseh.
> Joseph lives in Egypt, but his heart belongs to the promised land. He firmly believes: one day God will lead his people out.
> Then his bones are to be taken along. Many years later, at the exodus from Egypt, Moses will do exactly that (Exodus 13:19). And Joseph will be buried in Shechem (Joshua 24:32).
> The book of Genesis ends with a coffin in Egypt. That sounds sad. But it also ends with a great hope: “God will surely come to help you.” The story is not over yet. It goes on in the book of Exodus.

## Looking back: the book of Genesis

The first book of the Bible tells a long story:
God creates a good world.
People do not trust God, and evil spreads.
But God does not give up.
He starts again with a single family: with Abraham and Sarah.
He promises them land, many descendants and blessing for all nations.
Through four generations – Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph – God keeps his promise,
even through mistakes, lies, quarrels and suffering.
In the end, a family has become a nation of 12 tribes: Israel.
