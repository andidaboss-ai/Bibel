# Genesis – Chapter 28
### Jacob’s dream of the stairway to heaven

---

## Isaac blesses Jacob and sends him away (Verses 1–5)

[1] Isaac called Jacob to him.
He blessed him and gave him a command.
He said to him,
“Do not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan.
[2] Get up and go to Paddan Aram,
to the house of Bethuel, your mother’s father.
Take a wife there from the daughters of Laban, your mother’s brother.
[3] May God Almighty bless you.
May he make you fruitful and give you many descendants,
so that you become a great community of peoples.
[4] May he give you the blessing of Abraham, to you and to your descendants with you.
Then you will own the land where you now live as a stranger.
God gave this land to Abraham.”

[5] So Isaac sent Jacob away.
Jacob went to Paddan Aram, to Laban.
Laban was the son of Bethuel the Syrian.
He was the brother of Rebekah, the mother of Jacob and Esau.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Isaac blesses Jacob once more – this time with open eyes. He knows whom he is blessing. And he clearly passes on to him the “blessing of Abraham”: land, descendants and God’s closeness.
> Paddan Aram is the area around the city of Haran, the old home of Abraham’s family. Jacob goes the same way as Abraham’s servant in Chapter 24 – only the other way around, and as someone running away.

---

## Esau marries one more wife (Verses 6–9)

[6] Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob.
He saw that Isaac had sent him away to Paddan Aram to take a wife from there.
And he saw that Isaac had given him a command when he blessed him.
Isaac had said, “Do not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan.”
[7] Esau also saw that Jacob had obeyed his father and his mother
and had gone to Paddan Aram.
[8] So Esau saw that his father Isaac did not like the daughters of Canaan.
[9] That is why Esau went to Ishmael.
He already had wives.
But in addition to them, he took Mahalath as his wife.
She was the daughter of Ishmael, Abraham’s son.
And she was the sister of Nebaioth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only now does Esau notice that his wives cause his parents grief. He tries to make it right and marries a woman from Abraham’s family.
> Esau wants to please his father. That is somehow touching. But he does not really understand what it is about. He simply adds one more wife.

---

## The stairway to heaven (Verses 10–15)

[10] Jacob left Beersheba and went toward Haran.
[11] He came to a certain place.
He stayed there for the night, because the sun had set.
He took one of the stones of that place and put it under his head.
Then he lay down in that place to sleep.

[12] He had a dream:
He saw a stairway that stood on the earth.
Its top reached to heaven.
Look! The angels of God were going up and coming down on it.

[13] Look! The LORD stood above it and said,
“I am the LORD, the God of your father Abraham, and the God of Isaac.
I will give the land you are lying on to you and to your descendants.
[14] Your descendants will be as many as the dust of the earth.
You will spread out to the west and to the east, to the north and to the south.
Through you and through your descendants, all the families of the earth will be blessed.
[15] Look! I am with you.
I will protect you wherever you go.
And I will bring you back into this land.
For I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is alone and on the run. He has cheated his brother and lost his family. He sleeps under the open sky, with a stone as a pillow. He is at the very bottom.
> And exactly there, God meets him. Not in the temple, not in a holy moment, but on the run, after a serious mistake.
> The stairway (often also called “Jacob’s ladder”) connects heaven and earth. The picture says: God is not far away. There is a connection between him and us.
> God does not blame Jacob. He gives him promises: “I am with you. I will protect you wherever you go. I will not leave you.”
> In the New Testament, Jesus refers to this story. He says that he himself is this connection between heaven and earth (John 1:51).

---

## Jacob wakes up (Verses 16–22)

[16] Then Jacob woke up from his sleep and said,
“Truly, the LORD is in this place, and I did not know it!”
[17] He was afraid and said,
“How awesome this place is!
This is none other than God’s house.
And this is the gate of heaven.”

[18] Jacob got up early in the morning.
He took the stone that he had put under his head
and set it up as a pillar.
A pillar is a tall stone that stands upright as a sign.
Then he poured oil on its top.
[19] He named that place Bethel.
But at first, the name of the city was Luz.

[20] Then Jacob made a vow.
A vow is a solemn promise to God.
He said,
“If God will be with me,
and if he will protect me on this way that I am going,
and if he will give me bread to eat and clothes to wear,
[21] so that I come back to my father’s house in peace,
and if the LORD will be my God,
[22] then this stone that I have set up as a pillar will be God’s house.
And of everything you give me, I will surely give a tenth to you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The LORD is in this place, and I did not know it!” That is a wonderful sentence. God is often closer than we notice – especially in places where we do not expect him at all.
> “Bethel” means “house of God.”
> With the oil, Jacob sets the stone apart for God. He makes it a reminder: God met me here.
> Jacob is still careful. He says: “**If** God will be with me …, **then** …” He is not as far along as Abraham yet. For now, he is only trying out faith. But God meets him where he is. God is patient with people whose faith is still small.
