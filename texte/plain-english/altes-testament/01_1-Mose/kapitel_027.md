# Genesis – Chapter 27
### Jacob gets the blessing by a trick

---

## Isaac wants to bless Esau (Verses 1–4)

[1] Isaac had grown old.
His eyes were so weak that he could no longer see.
He called Esau, his older son, and said to him,
“My son?”
Esau said to him, “Here I am.”
[2] Isaac said,
“Look, I am old.
I do not know the day I will die.
[3] So please take your weapons, your quiver and your bow.
Go out into the field and hunt some wild game for me.
[4] Make me a tasty meal, the kind I love.
Bring it to me so that I may eat.
Then I will bless you with all my heart before I die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, a father’s blessing was very important. People believed: what a father promises in a blessing before his death is valid. It cannot be taken back. It shapes the future.
> Isaac wants to give the blessing to Esau, his favorite son. But before the birth, God had said: “The older will serve the younger” (Genesis 25:23). Isaac does not keep to that.
> A quiver is a case for arrows.

---

## Rebekah’s plan (Verses 5–17)

[5] Rebekah heard Isaac speaking to his son Esau.
Esau went out into the field to hunt wild game and bring it home.
[6] Then Rebekah said to her son Jacob,
“Listen! I heard your father say to your brother Esau,
[7] ‘Bring me wild game and make me a tasty meal, so that I may eat.
Then I will bless you in the presence of the LORD before I die.’
[8] So now, my son, listen to me and do what I tell you.
[9] Go to the flock and get me two good young goats.
I will make a tasty meal from them for your father, the kind he loves.
[10] You will bring it to your father so that he may eat.
Then he will bless you before he dies.”

[11] Jacob said to his mother Rebekah,
“Look, my brother Esau is a hairy man,
and my skin is smooth.
[12] What if my father touches me?
Then he will see me as a cheat.
And I would bring a curse on myself, not a blessing.”
[13] His mother said to him,
“Let your curse fall on me, my son.
Just listen to me. Go and get the goats for me.”

[14] So Jacob went, got the goats and brought them to his mother.
His mother made a tasty meal, the kind his father loved.
[15] Then Rebekah took the best clothes of Esau, her older son.
She had them with her in the house.
She put them on Jacob, her younger son.
[16] She put the skins of the young goats on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck.
[17] Then she handed her son Jacob the tasty meal and the bread she had made.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rebekah knows God’s word that Jacob is to come first. But instead of trusting God, she takes matters into her own hands – with a trick.
> Jacob has no worries that it is wrong. He is only afraid of getting caught.
> The whole family is torn apart: each parent fights for their favorite child. No one talks openly with anyone else.

---

## Jacob before his father (Verses 18–29)

[18] Jacob went in to his father and said,
“My father?”
Isaac said,
“Here I am. Who are you, my son?”
[19] Jacob said to his father,
“I am Esau, your firstborn.
I have done what you asked me to do.
Please get up, sit down and eat some of my wild game.
Then you can bless me with all your heart.”

[20] Isaac said to his son,
“How did you find it so quickly, my son?”
Jacob said,
“Because the LORD your God gave me success.”
[21] Isaac said to Jacob,
“Please come closer, my son.
I want to touch you and find out whether you are really my son Esau or not.”
[22] Jacob went close to his father Isaac.
Isaac touched him and said,
“The voice is Jacob’s voice,
but the hands are the hands of Esau.”
[23] He did not recognize him,
because his hands were hairy, like the hands of his brother Esau.
So he blessed him.

[24] He asked,
“Are you really my son Esau?”
Jacob said,
“I am.”
[25] Isaac said,
“Bring it to me, and I will eat some of my son’s wild game.
Then I will bless you with all my heart.”
Jacob brought it to him, and he ate.
He also brought him wine, and he drank.

[26] Then his father Isaac said to him,
“Come here now and kiss me, my son.”
[27] Jacob came close and kissed him.
Isaac smelled the smell of his clothes.
He blessed him and said,
“Look, the smell of my son
is like the smell of a field that the LORD has blessed.
[28] May God give you the dew of the sky
and the rich soil of the earth,
plenty of grain and new wine.
[29] Let peoples serve you,
and let nations bow down to you.
Be lord over your brothers.
Let your mother’s sons bow down to you.
Cursed be everyone who curses you.
Blessed be everyone who blesses you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob lies right to the face of his blind father three times. He even uses God’s name for his lie: “Because the LORD your God gave me success.”
> Isaac is suspicious. He listens to the voice, he touches, he asks once more. But he lets himself be fooled by the smell and the skins.
> The blessing is a blessing for a fertile land and for power. And it repeats what God had said to Abraham: “Blessed be everyone who blesses you.”
> Dew was very important in the dry land. At night it makes the plants wet, even when it has not rained for a long time.

## Esau comes back (Verses 30–40)

[30] Isaac had just finished blessing Jacob.
Jacob had barely left his father Isaac.
Then his brother Esau came back from hunting.
[31] He also made a tasty meal and brought it to his father.
He said to his father,
“Let my father get up and eat some of his son’s wild game.
Then you can bless me with all your heart.”

[32] His father Isaac asked him,
“Who are you?”
He said,
“I am your son, your firstborn, Esau.”
[33] Isaac began to tremble violently.
He said,
“Then who was it that hunted wild game and brought it to me?
I ate all of it before you came.
And I have blessed him.
Yes, he will stay blessed.”

[34] When Esau heard his father’s words, he cried out loudly.
It was a very great and bitter cry.
He said to his father,
“Bless me too, my father!”
[35] Isaac said,
“Your brother came with a trick.
He has taken away your blessing.”

[36] Esau said,
“He is rightly named Jacob!
He has cheated me two times now.
He took away my birthright.
And look, now he has taken away my blessing.”
And he said,
“Have you not saved a blessing for me?”

[37] Isaac answered Esau,
“Look, I have made him your lord.
I have given him all his brothers as servants.
I have supplied him with grain and new wine.
So what can I still do for you, my son?”
[38] Esau said to his father,
“Do you have only one blessing, my father?
Bless me too, my father!”
And Esau began to weep loudly.

[39] His father Isaac answered him,
“Look, your home will have a share in the rich soil of the earth
and in the dew of the sky above.
[40] You will live by your sword,
and you will serve your brother.
But this will happen: when you break free,
you will shake his yoke off your neck.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaac trembles all over. He realizes: I wanted to act against God’s word, and God still guided it the way he had said. That is why he says: “Yes, he will stay blessed.”
> Esau’s cry and his tears are heartbreaking. The Bible shows his pain with much compassion. He is not just the villain.
> “Jacob” sounds like “heel-holder” or “cheat.” Esau says bitterly: the name fits!
> In Hebrew, verse 39 can be read in two ways: Esau’s home will have a share in the rich soil, or it will be far away from the rich soil. Our text follows the first reading. Many Bibles translate it the second way, because Esau’s descendants, the Edomites, later lived in a poor, mountainous land southeast of the Dead Sea.
> A “yoke” is a wooden beam used to hitch oxen to a plow. It is a picture of oppression. Isaac foretells: Edom will serve Israel, but one day it will break free. That is what happened later (2 Kings 8:20–22).

## Esau wants to kill Jacob (Verses 41–46)

[41] Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing his father had given him.
Esau said to himself,
“The days of mourning for my father are near.
Then I will kill my brother Jacob.”

[42] Someone told Rebekah what her older son Esau had said.
She sent for her younger son Jacob.
She said to him,
“Listen: your brother Esau is comforting himself with the plan to kill you.
[43] So now, my son, listen to me.
Get up and flee to my brother Laban in Haran.
[44] Stay with him for a while,
until your brother’s rage has calmed down –
[45] until your brother’s anger turns away from you
and he forgets what you have done to him.
Then I will send for you and bring you back from there.
Why should I lose you both in one day?”

[46] Rebekah said to Isaac,
“I am sick of my life because of the daughters of Heth.
If Jacob marries one of the daughters of Heth like these,
one of the daughters of this land,
what good will my life be to me?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The trick has terrible results. The quarreling brothers almost become a second Cain and Abel.
> “Why should I lose you both in one day?” – Rebekah is afraid: if Esau kills Jacob, she loses Jacob. And Esau would have to flee as a murderer or would be killed himself.
> Rebekah says that Jacob is to be away only “for a while.” In reality, he will stay away for 20 years. And Rebekah will never see her favorite son again. The Bible tells nothing more about her, except about her grave (Genesis 49:31).
> With verse 46, Rebekah cleverly gets Isaac to send Jacob away – without telling him about Esau’s plans to murder him.
> The whole story shows: God reaches his goal, even through people’s mistakes. But that does not make the trick good. Everyone in this family has to pay dearly for it.
