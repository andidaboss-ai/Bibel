# Genesis – Chapter 36
### Esau’s family – the people of Edom

---

## Esau’s wives and sons (Verses 1–5)

[1] This is the family history of Esau. Esau is Edom.
[2] Esau took his wives from the daughters of Canaan:
Adah, the daughter of Elon the Hittite,
and Oholibamah, the daughter of Anah and granddaughter of Zibeon the Hivite,
[3] and Basemath, Ishmael’s daughter and the sister of Nebaioth.
[4] Adah gave birth to Eliphaz for Esau.
Basemath gave birth to Reuel.
[5] Oholibamah gave birth to Jeush, Jalam and Korah.
These are the sons of Esau who were born to him in the land of Canaan.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible also tells about Esau and his descendants. Esau is not forgotten. A people comes from him too, just as Isaac had blessed him.
> The names of the wives do not quite match Chapters 26 and 28. There they are called Judith, Basemath and Mahalath. Probably some women had two names. That was common back then. Scholars also explain it this way: Different old traditions come together here.

---

## Esau moves away (Verses 6–8)

[6] Esau took his wives, his sons and his daughters,
and all the people of his household,
his livestock and all his animals,
and all the things he had gathered in the land of Canaan.
And he went into another land, away from his brother Jacob.
[7] For they owned too much to live together.
The land where they lived as foreigners could not feed them, because of their livestock.
[8] So Esau lived in the hill country of Seir.
Esau is Edom.

> **What does this mean?**
> It is like with Abraham and Lot (Chapter 13): too many possessions, too little room. The brothers part ways – this time in peace.
> The land of Canaan remains for Jacob. Esau moves to the hill country of Seir, southeast of the Dead Sea, in today’s Jordan.

---

## Esau’s sons and grandsons (Verses 9–14)

[9] This is the family history of Esau, the father of the Edomites in the hill country of Seir.
[10] These are the names of Esau’s sons:
Eliphaz, the son of Adah, Esau’s wife,
and Reuel, the son of Basemath, Esau’s wife.

[11] The sons of Eliphaz were:
Teman, Omar, Zepho, Gatam and Kenaz.
[12] Timna was a concubine of Eliphaz, Esau’s son.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
She gave birth to Amalek for Eliphaz.
These are the descendants of Adah, Esau’s wife.

[13] These are the sons of Reuel:
Nahath, Zerah, Shammah and Mizzah.
These were the descendants of Basemath, Esau’s wife.

[14] These were the sons of Oholibamah, Esau’s wife.
She was the daughter of Anah and granddaughter of Zibeon.
She gave birth to Jeush, Jalam and Korah for Esau.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amalek becomes important later. His descendants, the Amalekites, become great enemies of Israel (Exodus 17).
> Later, Teman became the name of an area in Edom. According to tradition, Eliphaz, one of Job’s friends, also lived there (Job 2:11).

---

## The chiefs of Esau (Verses 15–19)

[15] These are the chiefs among the sons of Esau.
A chief is the leader of a tribe.
The sons of Eliphaz, Esau’s firstborn, were:
chief Teman, chief Omar, chief Zepho, chief Kenaz,
[16] chief Korah, chief Gatam and chief Amalek.
These are the chiefs who came from Eliphaz in the land of Edom.
These are the sons of Adah.

[17] These are the sons of Reuel, Esau’s son:
chief Nahath, chief Zerah, chief Shammah and chief Mizzah.
These are the chiefs who came from Reuel in the land of Edom.
These are the sons of Basemath, Esau’s wife.

[18] These are the sons of Oholibamah, Esau’s wife:
chief Jeush, chief Jalam and chief Korah.
These are the chiefs who came from Oholibamah, the daughter of Anah, Esau’s wife.

[19] These are the sons of Esau (that is, Edom), and these are their chiefs.

> **What does this mean?**
> A “chief” is the head of a tribe or a large clan. In the beginning, Edom was not a kingdom, but a group of tribes with their own leaders.

---

## The first people of Seir (Verses 20–30)

[20] These are the sons of Seir the Horite, who already lived in the land:
Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon, Anah,
[21] Dishon, Ezer and Dishan.
These are the chiefs of the Horites, the descendants of Seir, in the land of Edom.

[22] The children of Lotan were Hori and Heman.
Lotan’s sister was Timna.
[23] These are the children of Shobal:
Alvan, Manahath, Ebal, Shepho and Onam.
[24] These are the children of Zibeon:
Aiah and Anah.
This is the Anah who found the hot springs in the wilderness,
while he was feeding the donkeys of his father Zibeon.
[25] These are the children of Anah:
Dishon and Oholibamah, the daughter of Anah.
[26] These are the children of Dishon:
Hemdan, Eshban, Ithran and Cheran.
[27] These are the children of Ezer:
Bilhan, Zaavan and Akan.
[28] These are the children of Dishan:
Uz and Aran.

[29] These are the chiefs of the Horites:
chief Lotan, chief Shobal, chief Zibeon, chief Anah,
[30] chief Dishon, chief Ezer and chief Dishan.
These are the chiefs of the Horites, by their chiefs, in the land of Seir.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before Esau came, the Horites lived in Seir. Esau’s family mixed with them: His wife Oholibamah and the concubine Timna come from this family.
> The little story of Anah and the hot springs shows: These lists are not dry lists. Behind every name there is a person with a story. To this day, there are hot springs in the area of Edom.

---

## The kings of Edom (Verses 31–39)

[31] These are the kings who ruled in the land of Edom,
before any king ruled over the people of Israel:
[32] Bela, the son of Beor, was king in Edom.
His city was named Dinhabah.
[33] Bela died.
Then Jobab became king in his place. He was the son of Zerah from Bozrah.
[34] Jobab died.
Then Husham became king in his place. He came from the land of the Temanites.
[35] Husham died.
Then Hadad became king in his place. He was the son of Bedad.
He had defeated Midian in the country of Moab.
His city was named Avith.
[36] Hadad died.
Then Samlah became king in his place. He came from Masrekah.
[37] Samlah died.
Then Shaul became king in his place. He came from Rehoboth by the river.
[38] Shaul died.
Then Baal Hanan became king in his place. He was the son of Achbor.
[39] Baal Hanan, the son of Achbor, died.
Then Hadar became king in his place.
His city was named Pau.
His wife was named Mehetabel. She was the daughter of Matred and the granddaughter of Mezahab.

> **What does this mean?**
> Edom already had kings when there were none in Israel yet. Israel’s first king, Saul, came much later.
> Notice: No king is the son of the king before him. Each comes from a different city. So there was no fixed royal family.
> God had promised Abraham that kings would come from him (Genesis 17:6). With Esau, this comes true early. With Jacob, it still takes a long time.

---

## The chiefs of Esau once more (Verses 40–43)

[40] These are the names of the chiefs who came from Esau, by their families and their places, by their names:
chief Timna, chief Alvah, chief Jetheth,
[41] chief Oholibamah, chief Elah, chief Pinon,
[42] chief Kenaz, chief Teman, chief Mibzar,
[43] chief Magdiel and chief Iram.
These are the chiefs of Edom, by the places where they lived in the land they owned.
This is Esau, the father of the Edomites.

> **What does this mean?**
> This second list names the chiefs by the places where they lived. Some names are probably names of areas or cities.
> With this chapter, the Bible says goodbye to Esau. From now on, it is about Jacob and his sons – above all about Joseph.
> The Edomites and the Israelites remain neighbors and brother peoples. Their history is often difficult. But the book of Deuteronomy says: Do not hate an Edomite, for he is your brother (Deuteronomy 23:7; in some Bibles 23:8).
