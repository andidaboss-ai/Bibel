# Genesis – Chapter 32
### Jacob wrestles with God and gets the name Israel

---

## God’s army (Verses 1–2)

[1] Jacob went on his way.
Then the angels of God met him.
[2] When Jacob saw them, he said,
“This is God’s army.”
He named that place Mahanaim.

> **What does this mean?**
> When Jacob ran away, he saw angels at Bethel (Chapter 28). Now, on the way back, he sees angels again. God has been with him the whole time – on the way there and on the way back.
> “Mahanaim” means “two camps”: Jacob’s camp and God’s camp. Jacob is not traveling alone.

---

## Jacob is afraid of Esau (Verses 3–8)

[3] Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to his brother Esau,
to the land of Seir, the country of Edom.
[4] He gave them this command:
“This is what you must say to my lord Esau:
‘This is what your servant Jacob says:
I have lived as a foreigner with Laban, and I have stayed there until now.
[5] I have cattle, donkeys, flocks, male servants and female servants.
I have sent messengers to tell this to my lord.
I hope that you will be kind to me.’”

[6] The messengers came back to Jacob and said,
“We came to your brother Esau.
He is coming to meet you.
And 400 men are with him.”

[7] Then Jacob was very afraid and very upset.
He divided the people who were with him into two groups.
He also divided the flocks, the herds and the camels.
[8] He said,
“If Esau comes to the one group and attacks it,
then the group that is left can escape.”

> **What does this mean?**
> 20 years have passed. Back then, Esau wanted to kill Jacob. Jacob does not know whether his brother has forgiven him.
> Jacob calls Esau “my lord” and himself “your servant.” In a way, he turns the stolen blessing around: The blessing said that Esau would serve Jacob. Now Jacob makes himself small.
> 400 men – that is a small army. Jacob fears the worst.
> His plan is typical of Jacob: clever and careful. He divides everything into two groups, so that at least half survive.

---

## Jacob prays (Verses 9–12)

[9] Then Jacob prayed:
“God of my father Abraham, and God of my father Isaac!
LORD, you said to me,
‘Go back to your country and to your relatives, and I will do good to you.’
[10] I am not worthy of even the smallest of all the kindness and faithfulness
that you have shown to me, your servant.
For I crossed this Jordan with only my walking stick.
And now I have become two groups.
[11] Please save me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau!
For I am afraid of him.
He might come and attack me, and the mothers together with the children.
[12] You said,
‘I will surely do good to you.
I will make your descendants as many as the sand of the sea,
which can’t be counted because there is so much of it.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is Jacob’s first real prayer in the Bible. And it is a beautiful prayer.
> Jacob has become humble: “I am not worthy.” He knows: Everything he has is a gift.
> He honestly tells God: “I am afraid of him.”
> And he reminds God of his own promises. You may do that in prayer: hold God to his word.

## Gifts for Esau (Verses 13–21)

[13] Jacob stayed there that night.
Then he took a gift for his brother Esau from the things he had with him:
[14] 200 female goats and 20 male goats,
200 female sheep and 20 rams,
[15] 30 camels that were nursing their young, together with their young,
40 cows and 10 bulls,
20 female donkeys and 10 young donkeys.

[16] He gave them to his servants, each herd by itself.
He said to his servants,
“Go on ahead of me.
Leave a space between one herd and the next.”
[17] He gave a command to the first servant:
“When my brother Esau meets you and asks you,
‘Whose servant are you? Where are you going? And whose animals are these in front of you?’
[18] Then you must say,
‘They belong to your servant Jacob.
They are a gift sent to my lord Esau.
And look, he himself is coming behind us.’”

[19] He gave the same command to the second servant and to the third,
and to all who followed the herds.
He said,
“This is how you must speak to Esau when you find him.
[20] And you must say,
‘Not only that, but look, your servant Jacob is coming behind us.’”
For Jacob thought,
“I will calm him with the gift that goes ahead of me.
After that, I will see his face.
Maybe he will accept me.”

[21] So the gift went on ahead of him.
But he himself stayed in the camp that night.

> **What does this mean?**
> All together these are 550 animals – a huge gift. Jacob wants to put his brother in a kind mood.
> The herds go one after the other, with space between them. So Esau is surprised again and again, and his anger can calm down step by step.
> Jacob prays – and at the same time he makes clever plans. Both belong together: trusting God and doing the right thing yourself.

## The fight at night (Verses 22–32)

[22] That same night, Jacob got up.
He took his 2 wives, his 2 female servants and his 11 sons.
He crossed the river Jabbok at a shallow place.
[23] He took them and sent them across the stream.
He also sent across everything he had.

[24] Jacob was left alone.
A man wrestled with him there until the day began to dawn.
[25] The man saw that he could not win against Jacob.
So he struck the socket of Jacob’s hip.
And Jacob’s hip was pulled out of joint while he wrestled with him.

[26] The man said,
“Let me go, for the day is dawning.”
Jacob said,
“I won’t let you go unless you bless me.”
[27] The man asked him,
“What is your name?”
He said,
“Jacob.”
[28] The man said,
“Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel.
For you have fought with God and with people, and you have won.”

[29] Jacob asked him,
“Please tell me your name.”
He said,
“Why do you ask what my name is?”
And he blessed him there.

[30] Jacob named the place Peniel.
For he said,
“I have seen God face to face,
and yet my life was spared.”

[31] The sun rose on him as he passed Peniel.
He was limping because of his hip.
[32] That is why the people of Israel do not eat the sinew of the hip, which lies on the hip socket, to this very day.
For the man struck Jacob’s hip socket at this sinew.
A sinew is a tough cord in the body.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most mysterious stories in the Bible. Jacob is alone at night, full of fear of the next day. Suddenly a stranger wrestles with him.
> In the end Jacob realizes: This “man” was God himself. Some interpreters say: It was an angel. Jacob himself says: “I have seen God face to face.”
> All his life Jacob has fought: with Esau even in their mother’s womb, with his father, with Laban. Now he fights with God.
> Jacob is hurt, but he does not give up. He holds on: “I won’t let you go unless you bless me.” Earlier he got the blessing by trickery. Now he honestly asks for it – from God himself.
> The question “What is your name?” is important. When his father asked him that, Jacob lied and said “Esau.” Now he tells the truth: “Jacob” – “cheater.” He admits who he is.
> Then he gets a new name: **Israel**. That means “God fights” or “He fought with God.” From now on, the whole people is named after him: Israel.
> “Peniel” (also Penuel) means “face of God.” People believed: Whoever sees God must die. Jacob is amazed that he is alive.
> Jacob comes out of the fight as the winner, but he limps. Whoever truly meets God does not stay the same. His strength no longer lies in his tricks, but in God’s blessing.
> To this day, many Jews do not eat this sinew (the sciatic nerve) of cattle or sheep. This reminds them of this fight.
