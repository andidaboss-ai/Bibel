# Genesis – Chapter 11
### The tower of Babel

---

## A city and a tower (Verses 1–4)

[1] The whole earth had one language and the same words.
[2] As people traveled east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar.
They settled there.
[3] They said to one another,
“Come, let’s make bricks and bake them hard in the fire!”
So they used bricks instead of stones,
and tar instead of mortar.
Mortar is what holds the stones of a wall together.
[4] Then they said,
“Come, let’s build ourselves a city and a tower.
Its top will reach to the sky.
Let’s make a name for ourselves.
Then we will not be scattered over the face of the whole earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> People have a new invention: baked bricks. With them, you can build high. Inventions in themselves are not bad.
> The problem is their goal: “Let’s make a name for ourselves.” They want to be famous and powerful. They want to reach the sky – all the way to God.
> And they want to stay together instead of spreading out over the earth. But God had said: “Fill the earth.”
> In ancient Babylon, there really were huge temple towers made of bricks, with levels like a staircase to the sky. They are called ziggurats.

---

## God confuses the language (Verses 5–9)

[5] Then the LORD came down.
He wanted to see the city and the tower that the people were building.
[6] The LORD said,
“Look: They are one people, and they all have one language.
And this is only the beginning of what they will do.
Now nothing they plan to do will be impossible for them.
[7] Come, let’s go down
and confuse their language there,
so that they do not understand one another’s speech.”

[8] So the LORD scattered them from there over the face of all the earth.
And they stopped building the city.
[9] That is why the city is called Babel.
For there the LORD confused the language of all the earth.
And from there the LORD scattered them over the face of all the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> There is a quiet joke in this: People want to build a tower up to the sky. But God first has to “come down” just to see it. That is how small the tower is from God’s point of view.
> God is not afraid of people. But he sees: When all people join together to be like God themselves, it becomes dangerous – most of all for themselves.
> In Hebrew, “Babel” sounds like “balal.” That means “to confuse, to mix up.”
> The many languages are a punishment. But they are also a new beginning: Now people do spread out over the earth, as God wanted.
> In the New Testament there is a counterpart: At Pentecost (Acts 2), people from many countries hear the good news about Jesus – each one in their own language. God brings people together again without taking away their variety.

## From Shem to Terah (Verses 10–26)

[10] This is the family history of Shem.
Shem was 100 years old when he had Arpachshad. That was 2 years after the flood.
[11] After Arpachshad was born, Shem lived another 500 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[12] Arpachshad was 35 years old when he had Shelah.
[13] After Shelah was born, Arpachshad lived another 403 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[14] Shelah was 30 years old when he had Eber.
[15] After Eber was born, Shelah lived another 403 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[16] Eber was 34 years old when he had Peleg.
[17] After Peleg was born, Eber lived another 430 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[18] Peleg was 30 years old when he had Reu.
[19] After Reu was born, Peleg lived another 209 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[20] Reu was 32 years old when he had Serug.
[21] After Serug was born, Reu lived another 207 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[22] Serug was 30 years old when he had Nahor.
[23] After Nahor was born, Serug lived another 200 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[24] Nahor was 29 years old when he had Terah.
[25] After Terah was born, Nahor lived another 119 years.
He had more sons and daughters.

[26] Terah was 70 years old.
Then he had Abram, Nahor and Haran.

> **What does this mean?**
> This list leads in 10 generations from Shem to Abram – just as the list in Chapter 5 led in 10 generations from Adam to Noah.
> People no longer live as long as before. Shem still lives to be 600 years old, Nahor only 148. The ancient time is coming to an end.
> After the confusion of Babel, the Bible turns its eyes to **one** family. With this family, God wants to begin a new path for all of humanity.

---

## The family of Terah (Verses 27–32)

[27] This is the family history of Terah.
Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor and Haran.
And Haran became the father of Lot.
[28] Haran died while his father Terah was still alive,
in the land where he was born: in Ur of the Chaldees.

[29] Abram and Nahor took wives.
Abram’s wife was named Sarai.
Nahor’s wife was named Milcah.
She was the daughter of Haran.
Haran was the father of Milcah and of Iscah.

[30] Sarai was barren.
Barren means: She could not have children.
She had no child.

[31] Terah took his son Abram,
his grandson Lot, the son of Haran,
and his daughter-in-law Sarai, the wife of his son Abram.
Together they left Ur of the Chaldees.
They wanted to go to the land of Canaan.
They came as far as Haran and settled there.

[32] Terah lived 205 years.
Then Terah died in Haran.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now we meet the main characters of the next chapters: Abram (later he is called Abraham), his wife Sarai (later Sarah), and Lot, Abram’s nephew.
> Ur was a large, rich city in the south of what is now Iraq. Haran is a city in the south of what is now Turkey. It has almost the same name as Abram’s brother Haran who died, but in Hebrew it is a different name.
> The most important sentence is very short: “She had no child.” In those days, that was a great sorrow. Without children, a family had no future.
> But right here, where humanly speaking there is no future, God begins something completely new. The next chapter tells about it.
