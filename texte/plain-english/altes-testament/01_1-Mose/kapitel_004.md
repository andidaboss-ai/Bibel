# Genesis – Chapter 4
### Cain and Abel

---

## Two brothers (Verses 1–2)

[1] The man slept with his wife Eve.
She became pregnant and gave birth to Cain.
She said, “I have gotten a man with the LORD’s help.”
[2] Then she gave birth again, to Cain’s brother Abel.
Abel became a shepherd who kept sheep.
Cain became a farmer who worked the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> Outside the garden, life goes on. Children are born.
> Eve knows: A child is a gift. God has helped.
> “Abel” means “breath” or “a puff of wind.” That is already a hint: His life will soon be over.

---

## The brothers’ offerings (Verses 3–7)

[3] After some time, Cain brought an offering to the LORD.
An offering is a gift for God.
Cain brought some of the fruit of the ground.
[4] Abel also brought an offering.
He brought some of the firstborn animals of his flock and some of their fat.
The LORD looked kindly at Abel and his offering.
[5] But he did not look kindly at Cain and his offering.
Then Cain became very angry.
His face fell, and he looked gloomy.

[6] The LORD said to Cain,
“Why are you angry?
Why has your face fallen?
[7] If you do what is good, will you not be able to lift up your face?
But if you do not do what is good, sin is crouching at the door.
It wants to have you.
But you are to rule over it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible does not say exactly why God accepts Abel’s offering and not Cain’s offering. There is a hint in the words: Abel brings “the firstborn” and “their fat,” that means, the best. For Cain, it only says “some.” Maybe what matters is the heart with which a person gives.
> “Sin” is what separates us from God and from other people.
> God speaks with Cain like a father with his son. He warns him: Your anger is like a wild animal in front of your door. Be careful! But you can control it.
> Cain has a choice. He is not helpless against sin.

---

## The first murder (Verses 8–12)

[8] Cain said to his brother Abel, “Let’s go out into the field.”
While they were in the field, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.

[9] Then the LORD asked Cain,
“Where is your brother Abel?”
Cain answered,
“I don’t know. Am I my brother’s keeper?”
A keeper is someone who watches over another.

[10] The LORD said,
“What have you done?
Listen: Your brother’s blood is crying out to me from the ground!
[11] So now you are cursed.
You are cut off from the ground,
which opened its mouth to take your brother’s blood from your hand.
[12] From now on, when you work the ground, it will not give you its strength.
You will be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth.”
A fugitive is someone who has to run away.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cain does not listen to God’s warning. Anger turns into murder. It can happen that fast.
> God asks again – just like with Adam: “Where …?” Again, God gives a chance to be honest.
> Cain lies and becomes rude: “Am I my brother’s keeper?” The whole Bible answers: **Yes.** We are responsible for one another.
> No wrong stays hidden from God. The suffering of a victim “cries out” to him. God hears it.
> “A fugitive and a wanderer” means: Cain will not feel at home anywhere anymore. He will wander around without rest.

---

## God protects Cain (Verses 13–16)

[13] Cain said to the LORD,
“My punishment is too great. I cannot bear it.
[14] Look: Today you are driving me away from the ground.
I will be hidden from your face.
I will be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth.
And whoever finds me will kill me.”

[15] The LORD said to him,
“No! Whoever kills Cain will be paid back seven times.”
And the LORD put a sign on Cain,
so that no one who found him would strike him down.

[16] Then Cain went away from the presence of the LORD.
He lived in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cain carries great guilt. He has to bear the consequences.
> But God does not drop him. He even protects the murderer from the revenge of others. So the “mark of Cain” is not a mark of shame. It is a sign of protection.
> God does not want violence to keep causing new violence. Even a guilty person is still a person whom God cares about.
> “Nod” means “flight” or “homelessness.”

---

## Cain’s family (Verses 17–24)

[17] Cain slept with his wife.
She became pregnant and gave birth to Enoch.
Cain built a city.
He named the city after his son: Enoch.
[18] Enoch had a son: Irad.
Irad became the father of Mehujael.
Mehujael became the father of Methushael.
And Methushael became the father of Lamech.

[19] Lamech took two wives.
The first one was named Adah, and the second one was named Zillah.
[20] Adah gave birth to Jabal.
He became the father of all who live in tents and keep livestock.
[21] His brother was named Jubal.
He became the father of all who play the harp and the pipe.
[22] Zillah also had a son: Tubal Cain.
He was a smith who made all kinds of cutting tools out of bronze and iron.
Tubal Cain’s sister was Naamah.

[23] Lamech said to his wives,
“Adah and Zillah, hear my voice!
You wives of Lamech, listen to what I say:
I have killed a man because he wounded me.
I have killed a young man because he bruised me.
[24] If Cain will be paid back seven times,
then Lamech seventy-seven times!”

> **What does this mean?**
> People become inventive: They build cities, move around with herds, make music and forge tools. This is how culture begins.
> But at the same time, violence grows. Lamech boasts: For a small wound, he killed a person.
> God wanted to protect Cain with the number seven. Lamech turns it into a threat: Seventy-seven!
> Many centuries later, Jesus turns this saying around. He says: Do not take revenge again and again. Instead, **forgive** again and again – seventy times seven times (Matthew 18:22).

---

## A new beginning (Verses 25–26)

[25] Adam slept with his wife again.
She gave birth to a son and named him Seth.
She said, “God has given me another child in place of Abel.
For Cain killed him.”
[26] Seth also had a son. He named him Enosh.
At that time, people began to call on the name of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> After all these terrible things, God gives a new beginning. In Hebrew, “Seth” sounds like “he has given.”
> “To call on the name of the LORD” means: to pray to God, to speak to him, to ask him for help.
> The chapter shows two paths: With Lamech, violence grows. With Seth and Enosh, prayer grows.
