# Genesis – Chapter 42
### Joseph’s brothers come to Egypt

---

## Jacob sends his sons (Verses 1–5)

[1] Jacob found out that there was grain in Egypt.
So Jacob said to his sons,
“Why do you just look at one another?”
[2] He said,
“Look, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt.
Go down there and buy grain for us from there.
Then we will live and not die.”

[3] So Joseph’s 10 brothers went down to buy grain from Egypt.
[4] But Jacob did not send Benjamin, Joseph’s brother, with his brothers.
For he said, “Something bad might happen to him.”
[5] So the sons of Israel came to buy grain, together with the others who came.
For there was famine in the land of Canaan.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers sit around and do not know what to do. Their father has to push them.
> Jacob leaves Benjamin at home. Benjamin is now his favorite son – the only son of Rachel he still has. He is afraid of losing him too. You can feel it: Jacob does not fully trust his other sons.

---

## The brothers bow down before Joseph (Verses 6–8)

[6] Joseph was the governor over the land.
He was the one who sold grain to all the people of the land.
Joseph’s brothers came.
They bowed down before him with their faces to the ground.

[7] Joseph saw his brothers and recognized them.
But he acted like a stranger to them.
He spoke harshly with them.
He asked them,
“Where do you come from?”
They said,
“From the land of Canaan, to buy food.”
[8] Joseph recognized his brothers.
But they did not recognize him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now what Joseph dreamed as a boy comes true: His brothers’ sheaves bow down before him (Chapter 37). They bow down to the ground – without knowing to whom.
> More than 20 years have passed. Joseph was 17 back then; now he is almost 40. He wears Egyptian clothes, is clean-shaven and speaks Egyptian. No wonder the brothers do not recognize him.

---

## “You are spies!” (Verses 9–17)

[9] Joseph remembered the dreams that he had dreamed about them.
He said to them,
“You are spies!
You have come to find out where the land is weak and unprotected.”

[10] They said to him,
“No, my lord!
Your servants have come to buy food.
[11] We are all sons of one man.
We are honest men.
Your servants are not spies.”
[12] He said to them,
“No!
You have come to find out where the land is weak and unprotected!”

[13] They said,
“We, your servants, are 12 brothers,
the sons of one man in the land of Canaan.
Look: The youngest is with our father today.
And one is no more.”

[14] Joseph said to them,
“It is as I told you: You are spies!
[15] This is how you will be tested:
As surely as Pharaoh lives, you will not leave this place
unless your youngest brother comes here.
[16] Send one of you.
Let him get your brother.
The rest of you will stay locked up.
That way your words will be tested, whether you are telling the truth.
If not, then as surely as Pharaoh lives, you are spies.”

[17] He put them all together in prison for three days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Why is Joseph so harsh? He wants to find out whether his brothers have changed. Are they still the same men who would sell a brother? How do they treat Benjamin, Rachel’s other son?
> “One is no more” – that is how the brothers talk about Joseph. They do not know that he is standing in front of them.
> Joseph locks them up in prison for three days. Now the brothers experience a little of what Joseph went through.

---

## The brothers see their guilt (Verses 18–24)

[18] On the third day, Joseph said to them,
“Do this, and you will live.
For I fear God.
[19] If you are honest men, then let one of your brothers stay locked up in your prison.
The rest of you, go and take grain home to your hungry families.
[20] But bring your youngest brother to me.
Then it will be proven that your words are true.
And you will not die.”
They agreed to do it.

[21] They said to one another,
“We really are guilty because of our brother.
We saw how afraid he was when he begged us.
But we would not listen.
That is why this trouble has come upon us.”
[22] Reuben answered them,
“Didn’t I tell you, ‘Don’t sin against the boy’?
But you would not listen.
Now we must pay for his blood.”

[23] They did not know that Joseph understood them.
For there was an interpreter between them.
[24] Joseph turned away from them and cried.
Then he came back to them and talked with them.
He took Simeon from among them and tied him up in front of their eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> “I fear God” – Joseph lets them know that he believes in God. The brothers probably think he means the Egyptian gods.
> After more than 20 years, the brothers talk openly about their guilt for the first time. They remember how Joseph cried for help from the pit. Their bad conscience has not let go of them all these years.
> Joseph hears everything. And he cries. He is not harsh out of revenge. It breaks his heart.
> An interpreter puts the words of one language into another.
> Why Simeon? Maybe because he was the oldest after Reuben – and Reuben had wanted to save Joseph.

---

## Money in the sacks (Verses 25–28)

[25] Then Joseph gave orders to fill their bags with grain.
Each man’s money was to be put back into his sack.
And they were to be given food for the journey.
So it was done for them.
[26] They loaded their grain onto their donkeys and left.

[27] At the place where they stopped for the night, one of them opened his sack to give his donkey food.
Then he saw his money.
Look: It was at the top of his sack.
[28] He said to his brothers,
“My money has been given back!
Look, it is in my sack!”
Their hearts sank.
Trembling, they turned to one another and said,
“What is this that God has done to us?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph secretly gives them the grain as a gift. But the brothers are not happy. They are afraid that people will now think they are thieves.
> For the first time, the brothers speak of God. They sense: God has something to do with what is happening. Their conscience has woken up.

---

## The report to their father (Verses 29–38)

[29] They came to their father Jacob in the land of Canaan.
They told him everything that had happened to them.
They said,
[30] “The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly with us.
He thought we were spies who wanted to explore the country.
[31] We said to him,
‘We are honest men. We are not spies.
[32] We are 12 brothers, sons of our father.
One is no more.
And the youngest is with our father today in the land of Canaan.’
[33] Then the man, the lord of the land, said to us,
‘This is how I will know that you are honest men:
Leave one of your brothers with me.
Take grain for your hungry families and go.
[34] Bring your youngest brother to me.
Then I will know that you are not spies, but honest men.
Then I will give your brother back to you.
And you may trade in the land.’”

[35] Then they emptied their sacks.
And look: Each man’s bag of money was in his sack.
When they and their father saw their bags of money, they were afraid.

[36] Their father Jacob said to them,
“You have taken my children from me!
Joseph is no more.
Simeon is no more.
And now you want to take Benjamin away.
All these things are against me!”

[37] Reuben said to his father,
“You may kill my two sons if I do not bring him back to you.
Put him in my care.
I will bring him back to you.”
[38] But Jacob said,
“My son will not go down with you.
For his brother is dead, and he is the only one left.
If something bad happens to him on the way you are going,
then you will bring my gray hairs down to Sheol (the world of the dead) in sorrow.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is bitter. He blames his sons – more than he knows. For in fact, they did take Joseph away from him.
> “He is the only one left” – for Jacob, only Rachel’s sons count. The other 10 sons have to listen to that. That hurts.
> Reuben’s offer is well meant, but not thought through: What good would it do Jacob to kill his own grandsons?
> “Sheol” is the world of the dead again, as in Chapter 37. “Bring my gray hairs down to Sheol” means: You will make me, an old man, die of grief.
