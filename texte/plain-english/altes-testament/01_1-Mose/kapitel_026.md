# Genesis – Chapter 26
### Isaac in Gerar – fighting over wells

---

## God renews his promise to Isaac (Verses 1–6)

[1] There was a famine in the land.
It was another famine, besides the first famine in the time of Abraham.
Isaac went to Abimelech, the king of the Philistines, in Gerar.

[2] The LORD appeared to him and said,
“Do not go down to Egypt.
Live in the land that I will tell you about.
[3] Live in this land, and I will be with you and bless you.
For I will give all these lands to you and to your descendants.
And I will keep the oath that I swore to your father Abraham.
[4] I will make your descendants as many as the stars in the sky.
I will give all these lands to your descendants.
And through your descendants all the nations of the earth will be blessed.
[5] For Abraham listened to my voice.
He kept what I required of him: my commandments, my rules and my laws.”

[6] So Isaac lived in Gerar.

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaac goes through the same thing as his father: a famine. But God tells him: Stay in the land. I am with you.
> God gives Isaac the same promises that he gave to Abraham: land, many descendants and blessing for all nations. The covenant goes on, from generation to generation.
> Isaac obeys and stays.

---

## Isaac repeats Abraham’s mistake (Verses 7–11)

[7] The men of the place asked him about his wife.
He said, “She is my sister.”
For he was afraid to say, “She is my wife.”
He thought,
“The men of this place might kill me to get Rebekah,
because she is very beautiful.”

[8] When he had been there for a long time,
Abimelech, the king of the Philistines, looked out of a window.
He saw Isaac caressing his wife Rebekah.
[9] Abimelech called Isaac and said,
“Look, she really is your wife!
Why did you say, ‘She is my sister’?”
Isaac said to him,
“Because I thought, ‘I might die because of her.’”

[10] Abimelech said,
“What have you done to us?
One of the people could easily have slept with your wife.
Then you would have brought guilt on us!”
[11] Abimelech gave an order to all the people:
“Anyone who touches this man or his wife will surely be put to death.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Like father, like son: Isaac makes exactly the same mistake as Abraham (Chapters 12 and 20). Weaknesses and fears are often passed on in families.
> The Abimelech here is probably not the same king as in Abraham’s time. “Abimelech” was probably a title of the kings of Gerar, like “Pharaoh” in Egypt.
> Once again, the foreign king acts more decently than the man of God. And once again, God protects the woman.

---

## Isaac becomes rich – and envied (Verses 12–17)

[12] Isaac planted seed in that land.
In the same year he harvested 100 times as much as he had planted.
The LORD blessed him.
[13] The man became rich.
He became richer and richer, until he was very rich.
[14] He owned flocks of sheep and goats, herds of cattle and a large household.
The Philistines became jealous of him.

[15] The servants of his father Abraham had dug wells in Abraham’s lifetime.
The Philistines had blocked all these wells and filled them with earth.
[16] Abimelech said to Isaac,
“Go away from us!
You have become much more powerful than we are.”

[17] So Isaac left that place.
He set up camp in the valley of Gerar and lived there.

> **What does this mean?**
> To harvest 100 times as much – that is a huge harvest, in the middle of a time of famine. That is God’s blessing.
> But success makes others jealous. The Philistines destroy the wells. In the desert, that is a terrible attack, because nobody can live without water.
> Isaac does not fight back with violence. He goes away.

---

## Fighting over the wells (Verses 18–22)

[18] Isaac dug out again the wells of water that had been dug in the time of his father Abraham.
For the Philistines had blocked them after Abraham’s death.
He gave them the same names that his father had given them.

[19] Isaac’s servants dug in the valley.
There they found a well of fresh, flowing water.
[20] But the herdsmen of Gerar argued with Isaac’s herdsmen.
They said, “The water is ours!”
So Isaac named the well Esek.
Esek means “quarrel.”
For they had quarreled with him.

[21] Then they dug another well.
They argued over that one too.
So he named it Sitnah.
Sitnah means “hostility.”

[22] He moved on from there and dug another well.
They did not argue over this one.
So he named it Rehoboth.
Rehoboth means “wide spaces.”
He said,
“Now the LORD has made room for us.
Now we will grow in the land.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaac is a peaceful man. Twice his well is taken away from him. He does not fight. Instead, he moves on and digs a new one.
> In the end, he finds a place without conflict: “Rehoboth” – wide spaces. He sees God’s gift in this.
> Whoever gives in does not necessarily lose. Sometimes it is exactly then that God opens up a new, wide space.

---

## God appears to Isaac in Beersheba (Verses 23–25)

[23] From there Isaac went up to Beersheba.
[24] That same night the LORD appeared to him and said,
“I am the God of your father Abraham.
Do not be afraid, for I am with you.
I will bless you and give you many descendants,
for the sake of my servant Abraham.”
[25] Isaac built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD.
He set up his tent there.
And there Isaac’s servants dug a well.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Do not be afraid, for I am with you” – this is one of the most frequent sentences in the Bible. God says it again and again to people who feel unsure.
> Isaac does what his father Abraham did: he builds an altar and prays. He has taken on his father’s faith.

---

## Peace with Abimelech (Verses 26–33)

[26] Then Abimelech came to him from Gerar.
With him came his friend Ahuzzath and Phicol, the commander of his army.
[27] Isaac said to them,
“Why have you come to me?
You hate me, and you sent me away from you.”

[28] They said,
“We have seen clearly that the LORD is with you.
So we said,
‘Let there be an oath between us and you.
Let us make a covenant with you:
[29] You will do us no harm,
just as we have not touched you.
We have done nothing but good to you,
and we have let you go in peace.’
You are now the one the LORD has blessed.”

[30] Isaac made a feast for them, and they ate and drank.
[31] Early in the morning they got up and swore an oath to each other.
Then Isaac sent them on their way.
And they left him in peace.

[32] That same day, Isaac’s servants came.
They told him about the well they had dug.
They said to him, “We have found water!”
[33] Isaac named it Shibah.
Shibah means “oath.”
That is why the city is called Beersheba to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Philistines had driven Isaac away. Now they come to him themselves and want peace. They have seen: God is with this man.
> Isaac does not hold a grudge. Even though they treated him badly, he invites them to a meal.
> And on the very day of the peace treaty, his servants find water. Peace and blessing belong together.
> Abraham had already given Beersheba this name (Chapter 21). Isaac confirms the old name: “well of the oath.”

---

## Esau’s wives (Verses 34–35)

[34] When Esau was 40 years old, he married Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite,
and Basemath, the daughter of Elon the Hittite.
[35] These two women made life hard and bitter for Isaac and Rebekah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham very much wanted Isaac not to marry a woman from the peoples of Canaan (Chapter 24). Esau does not care about that. He marries two Hittite women at once.
> This shows again: Esau does not value the heritage of Abraham and God’s promise.
> For his parents this is a great sorrow. It prepares the way for the next story.
