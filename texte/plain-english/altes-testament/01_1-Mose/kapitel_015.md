# Genesis – Chapter 15
### God makes a covenant with Abram

---

## Look at the stars (Verses 1–6)

[1] After these things, the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision.
A vision is a picture that God shows a person, like a dream while awake.
God said,
“Do not be afraid, Abram.
I am your shield.
I am your very great reward.”

[2] Abram said,
“Lord GOD, what will you give me?
I have no children.
The one who will inherit everything I own is Eliezer of Damascus.”
[3] And Abram said,
“Look, you have given me no children.
So a servant born in my household will be my heir.”
An heir is someone who gets everything when the owner dies.

[4] Then the word of the LORD came to him:
“This man will not be your heir.
Instead, a son from your own body will be your heir.”
[5] The LORD brought Abram outside and said,
“Look up at the sky and count the stars, if you can count them.”
And he said to him,
“That is how many your descendants will be.”

[6] Abram believed the LORD.
And the LORD counted it to him as righteousness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abram is afraid. He is old, and God’s promise has not come true yet. He still has no child. Back then, in such a case, a faithful servant inherited everything.
> Abram tells God honestly what is troubling him. That is allowed. God is not offended when we doubt and ask questions.
> God answers with a picture: Look at the night sky. As many stars as there are – that is how many descendants you will have.
> Verse 6 is one of the most famous sentences in the Bible: Abram trusts God. And God says: That is enough. That makes you righteous before me. Not what we achieve, but trust brings us into the right relationship with God. The apostle Paul quotes this sentence in the New Testament (Romans 4).

---

## A strange sign (Verses 7–11)

[7] God said to Abram,
“I am the LORD.
I brought you out of Ur of the Chaldees
to give you this land to own.”
[8] Abram asked,
“Lord GOD, how will I know that I will own it?”

[9] The LORD said to him,
“Bring me a young cow three years old,
a female goat three years old,
a ram three years old,
a turtledove and a young pigeon.”
A ram is a male sheep.
[10] Abram brought him all these animals.
He cut them in half down the middle.
And he laid the halves opposite each other.
But he did not cut the birds in half.
[11] Then birds of prey swooped down on the dead animals.
But Abram drove them away.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abram asks for a sign. That is allowed too. God answers with a solemn ceremony.
> Cutting the animals in half sounds strange to us today. But that is how people made an agreement back then: They laid the halves in two rows. The partners walked between the halves. That meant: If I break my promise, may it happen to me as it happened to these animals.
> In Hebrew, “to make a covenant” literally means: “to cut a covenant.”

---

## God tells about the future (Verses 12–16)

[12] When the sun was going down, Abram fell into a deep sleep.
Then terror and great darkness came over him.

[13] Then God said to Abram,
“You must know for sure:
Your descendants will live as foreigners in a land that does not belong to them.
There they will be made slaves and treated cruelly for 400 years.
[14] But I will also judge the nation they will have to serve.
Afterward, they will leave with great wealth.
[15] But you will go to your ancestors in peace.
You will be buried at a good old age.
[16] In the fourth generation, your descendants will come back here.
For the guilt of the Amorites is not yet full.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God tells what will happen much later: Abram’s descendants will be slaves in Egypt. After that, God will lead them to freedom. The book of Exodus tells about this.
> “To go to your ancestors” is a beautiful old way of saying: to die.
> “The guilt of the Amorites is not yet full”: The peoples in Canaan still get a lot of time. God does not simply take the land away from them. He waits patiently. Only when their wickedness is too great does he step in.
> “Amorites” stands here for all the peoples who lived in Canaan at that time.

---

## God alone passes between the animals (Verses 17–21)

[17] The sun had gone down, and it was dark.
And look: A smoking fire pot and a burning torch
passed between the halves of the animals.

[18] On that day, the LORD made a covenant with Abram.
He said,
“I have given this land to your descendants,
from the river of Egypt to the great river, the Euphrates River:
[19] the land of the Kenites, the Kenizzites, the Kadmonites,
[20] the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Rephaim,
[21] the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Girgashites and the Jebusites.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The smoking fire pot and the torch are signs of God himself. In the Bible, God often shows himself in fire and smoke, for example later at Mount Sinai.
> What is special: Only God passes between the halves of the animals. Abram is asleep. God alone binds himself. In this way he says: I stand behind this promise with my own life. Abram does not have to add anything.
> The “river of Egypt” is probably a stream at the border between Canaan and Egypt. The Euphrates flows through what is now Syria and Iraq. That is a huge area.
> The list of 10 peoples shows: The land is already inhabited. God’s promise will only come true in the distant future.
