# Genesis – Chapter 31
### Jacob runs away from Laban

---

## Time to go (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jacob heard what Laban’s sons were saying:
“Jacob has taken everything that belonged to our father.
He has become rich from what belonged to our father.”
[2] Jacob looked at Laban’s face.
He saw: Laban was not as friendly toward him as before.

[3] Then the LORD said to Jacob,
“Go back to the land of your ancestors, to your relatives.
I will be with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The mood at Laban’s house has turned. Envy and distrust make living together impossible.
> Exactly now, God speaks. He reminds Jacob of his promise at Bethel: “I will bring you back into this land” (Genesis 28:15).

---

## Jacob talks with Rachel and Leah (Verses 4–16)

[4] Jacob sent for Rachel and Leah.
He called them to the field where his flock was.
[5] He said to them,
“I see in your father’s face that he is not as friendly toward me as before.
But the God of my father has been with me.
[6] You know that I have worked for your father with all my strength.
[7] But your father has tricked me.
He has changed my pay ten times.
But God did not let him hurt me.
[8] If he said, ‘The speckled animals will be your pay,’
then the whole flock gave birth to speckled young.
If he said, ‘The striped animals will be your pay,’
then the whole flock gave birth to striped young.
[9] So God has taken away your father’s livestock and given them to me.

[10] At the time when the animals mated, I had a dream.
I looked up and saw:
The male goats that were mating with the flock were striped, speckled and gray-spotted.
[11] The angel of God said to me in the dream, ‘Jacob!’
I said, ‘Here I am.’
[12] He said,
‘Look up and see:
All the male goats that are mating with the flock are striped, speckled and gray-spotted.
For I have seen everything that Laban is doing to you.
[13] I am the God of Bethel.
There you poured oil on a pillar.
There you made a vow to me.
Now get up, leave this land,
and go back to the land where you were born.’”

[14] Rachel and Leah answered him,
“Do we still have any share or inheritance in our father’s house?
[15] Doesn’t he treat us like foreigners?
For he has sold us.
And he has also used up the money he got for us.
[16] All the riches that God has taken away from our father
belong to us and to our children.
So now do whatever God has told you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob tells his wives the whole truth: It was not his sticks that made him rich. It was God. God saw how unfair Laban is, and he made sure that justice was done.
> “Changed my pay ten times” means: again and again, very often.
> For the first time, Rachel and Leah agree! Both are disappointed in their father. He treated them like goods. He kept the bride price – Jacob’s 14 years of work – for himself, instead of giving some of it to his daughters, as was the custom.

---

## The secret escape (Verses 17–21)

[17] Then Jacob got up.
He put his sons and his wives on the camels.
[18] He drove away all his livestock
and took all the things he had gathered,
the livestock he had gained in Paddan Aram.
He wanted to go to his father Isaac, to the land of Canaan.

[19] Laban had gone away to shear his sheep.
Then Rachel stole the teraphim that belonged to her father.
Teraphim are small statues of household gods.
[20] Jacob tricked Laban the Syrian.
He did not tell him that he was running away.
[21] So he ran away with everything he had.
He got up and crossed the River.
That is the Euphrates River.
And he headed toward the mountains of Gilead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Shearing the sheep was a big event. Laban was busy and far away. It was a good chance to escape.
> The household gods (in Hebrew: teraphim) were small statues. People believed they brought luck and protection. Maybe they were also important for the inheritance: Whoever had them counted as the heir of the house.
> The Bible does not say why Rachel steals them. Maybe she wanted to take revenge on her father. Maybe she herself was still attached to these gods.
> Gilead is a hill country east of the Jordan River.

---

## Laban catches up with Jacob (Verses 22–30)

[22] On the third day, Laban was told that Jacob had run away.
[23] He took his relatives with him and chased after him for seven days.
He caught up with him in the mountains of Gilead.
[24] But God came to Laban the Syrian in a dream at night.
He said to him,
“Be careful! Don’t say anything to Jacob, either good or bad.”

[25] Laban caught up with Jacob.
Jacob had set up his tent in the mountains.
Laban and his relatives also set up camp in the mountains of Gilead.
[26] Laban said to Jacob,
“What have you done?
You have tricked me.
You have carried away my daughters like prisoners of war!
[27] Why did you run away secretly?
Why did you trick me and not tell me?
Then I could have sent you away with joy and with songs,
with tambourine and harp.
[28] You did not even let me kiss my grandchildren and my daughters goodbye.
You have acted foolishly.
[29] I have the power to hurt you.
But the God of your father spoke to me last night.
He said, ‘Be careful! Don’t say anything to Jacob, either good or bad.’
[30] Now, you wanted to leave, because you longed so much for your father’s house.
But why have you stolen my gods?”

> **What does this mean?**
> For seven days, Laban chases after Jacob. He is angry and probably wants to take everything away from Jacob again.
> But God steps in and warns Laban in a dream. God protects Jacob, as he promised at Bethel.
> “Either good or bad” means: Do not try to make him turn back, neither with threats nor with nice words.
> Laban plays the sad, loving father. But you can tell: He thinks mainly about his possessions and his gods.

---

## The search for the household gods (Verses 31–35)

[31] Jacob answered Laban,
“I was afraid.
I thought you would take your daughters away from me by force.
[32] Whoever you find your gods with will not stay alive.
Here, in front of our relatives, look for whatever of yours is with me, and take it.”
For Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them.

[33] Laban went into Jacob’s tent,
into Leah’s tent,
and into the tent of the two female servants.
But he did not find them.
He came out of Leah’s tent and went into Rachel’s tent.
[34] Now Rachel had taken the teraphim and put them in the camel’s saddle.
She sat on them.
Laban felt around the whole tent, but he did not find them.
[35] Rachel said to her father,
“Don’t be angry, my lord, that I can’t stand up in front of you.
For I am having my period.”
He searched, but he did not find the teraphim.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob has no idea that Rachel has the gods. With his oath, he puts her life in danger.
> Rachel is as sly as her father and her husband. She sits on the gods and says she is having her monthly bleeding. Back then, a woman counted as “unclean” during this time. Then no one wanted to touch her.
> The scene is also meant to mock: What kind of gods are these, when a woman sits on them and they cannot help themselves? Here the Bible makes fun of the dead idols.

---

## Jacob lets out his anger (Verses 36–42)

[36] Then Jacob became angry and argued with Laban.
Jacob said to Laban,
“What have I done wrong?
What is my sin, that you have chased me so angrily?
[37] You have felt around in all my things.
What have you found that belongs to your house?
Put it here in front of my relatives and your relatives.
Let them judge between the two of us.

[38] I have been with you for 20 years now.
Your sheep and your goats have never lost their young before birth.
And I have never eaten the rams of your flocks.
[39] When an animal was torn apart by wild animals, I did not bring it to you.
I paid for the loss myself.
You made me pay for it, whether it was stolen by day or stolen by night.
[40] This is how it was for me:
In the day, the heat wore me out, and at night, the frost.
And I could not sleep.

[41] I have been in your house for 20 years now.
I worked for you 14 years for your two daughters
and six years for your flock.
And you have changed my pay ten times.
[42] If the God of my father had not been with me,
the God of Abraham, whom Isaac fears,
then you would surely have sent me away now with empty hands.
But God has seen my misery and the hard work of my hands.
And last night he scolded you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now everything that has built up in Jacob for 20 years bursts out of him.
> He was a faithful, honest shepherd. Normally, a shepherd did not have to pay for animals that were torn apart, if he could prove that a wild animal had killed them. Laban still made him pay.
> “The God whom Isaac fears” (literally: “the fear of Isaac”) is a special, old name for God. Maybe it reminds us of how Isaac experienced God’s power on a mountain in the land of Moriah (Chapter 22).

---

## An agreement made of stones (Verses 43–55)

[43] Laban answered Jacob,
“The daughters are my daughters.
The children are my children.
The flocks are my flocks.
Everything you see is mine!
But what can I do today to these daughters of mine, or to the children they have given birth to?
[44] Come, let us make a covenant, you and I.
Let it be a witness between me and you.”
A covenant is a binding agreement.

[45] Then Jacob took a stone and set it up as a pillar.
[46] Jacob said to his relatives,
“Gather stones!”
They took stones and made a heap.
Then they ate there by the heap.
[47] Laban called it Jegar Sahadutha.
But Jacob called it Galeed.
[48] Laban said,
“This heap is a witness between me and you today.”
That is why it was named Galeed,
[49] and also Mizpah, because he said,
“May the LORD watch between me and you,
when we are away from each other.
[50] If you treat my daughters badly,
or if you take other wives in addition to my daughters –
even if no one is with us: Look, God is a witness between me and you.”

[51] Laban said to Jacob,
“Look at this heap, and look at the pillar that I have set up between me and you.
[52] May this heap be a witness, and may the pillar be a witness:
I will not go past this heap to you,
and you will not go past this heap and this pillar to me,
to do harm.
[53] May the God of Abraham and the God of Nahor, the God of their father, judge between us.”
Then Jacob swore an oath by the God whom his father Isaac feared.

[54] Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain.
He invited his relatives to eat a meal.
They ate and stayed the whole night on the mountain.
[55] Early in the morning, Laban got up.
He kissed his grandchildren and his daughters and blessed them.
Then Laban left and went back home.

> **What does this mean?**
> Laban does not admit that he is in the wrong. He even claims: Everything is mine. But he notices: He cannot win against Jacob’s God. That is why he suggests a peace agreement.
> The heap of stones is a border. Neither of them is to cross it to harm the other.
> “Jegar Sahadutha” is Aramaic, Laban’s language. “Galeed” is Hebrew, Jacob’s language. Both names mean the same: “heap of witness.” Already here you can see: Two peoples with two languages part ways.
> “Mizpah” means “watchtower.” The sentence “May the LORD watch between me and you, when we are away from each other” (verse 49) is often used today as a friendly blessing when people say goodbye. But really it is a warning between two men who do not trust each other.
> In the end, they part in peace. At least Laban can say goodbye to his daughters and grandchildren.
