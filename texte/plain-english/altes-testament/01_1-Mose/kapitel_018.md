# Genesis – Chapter 18
### Three guests visit Abraham – Abraham prays for Sodom

---

## Abraham welcomes guests (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre.
Abraham was sitting at the entrance of his tent.
It was the hottest time of the day.
[2] Abraham looked up.
Then he saw: Three men were standing near him.
When he saw them, he ran from the tent entrance to meet them.
He bowed down to the ground
[3] and said,
“My lord, if I have found favor in your eyes,
please do not pass by your servant.
[4] Let someone bring a little water.
Then you can wash your feet
and rest under the tree.
[5] I will get a piece of bread, so that you can strengthen yourselves.
After that, you may go on your way.
After all, you have come to your servant.”
They answered,
“Very well, do as you have said.”

[6] Abraham hurried into the tent to Sarah.
He said,
“Quick! Take about 6 gallons of fine flour.
Knead the dough and bake flat loaves of bread!”
[7] Then Abraham ran to the herd.
He got a tender, good calf and gave it to the servant.
The servant hurried to prepare it.
[8] Abraham took butter, milk and the calf that had been prepared,
and he set it in front of the men.
He himself stood by them under the tree while they ate.

> **What does this mean?**
> It is the heat of midday. No one likes to travel then. Still, old Abraham runs to meet the strangers. In the desert, hospitality was vital for life – and in the Middle East, it is still a great honor today.
> Abraham modestly says: “a piece of bread.” Then he serves a feast: fresh bread, a calf, butter and milk.
> “About 6 gallons” of flour – the Bible says “three seahs.” A seah is about 2 gallons. That is enough for very many people.
> At first, Abraham may not know whom he is serving. The New Testament says: Do not forget to welcome strangers, for in this way some have welcomed angels without knowing it (Hebrews 13:2). This refers to this story.

## Sarah laughs (Verses 9–15)

[9] The men asked him,
“Where is your wife Sarah?”
He answered,
“There, in the tent.”
[10] Then one of them said,
“I will surely come back to you at about this time next year.
And look: Your wife Sarah will have a son.”
Sarah was listening at the entrance of the tent, which was behind him.

[11] Abraham and Sarah were old, very far along in years.
Sarah had long been past the age when women can have children.
[12] So Sarah laughed quietly to herself.
She thought,
“Now that I am old, will I still have the joy of love?
And my lord is old too!”

[13] Then the LORD said to Abraham,
“Why did Sarah laugh and say,
‘Will I really have a child, now that I am old?’
[14] Is anything too hard for the LORD?
At the set time I will come back to you, at this time next year.
And Sarah will have a son.”

[15] Then Sarah denied it.
She said, “I did not laugh.”
For she was afraid.
But he said, “No, you did laugh.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it becomes clear: One of the three guests is the LORD himself. He even knows what Sarah is secretly thinking.
> Sarah laughs – like Abraham in Chapter 17. It is a laugh of disbelief: That is impossible!
> The big question in verse 14 is still true today: “Is anything too hard for the LORD?” Or in other words: Is anything impossible for God?
> God does not embarrass Sarah. He only says calmly: “No, you did laugh.” God accepts us with our doubts too. And soon Sarah will laugh for joy.

---

## God lets Abraham in on his plan (Verses 16–21)

[16] The men got up and set out.
They looked toward Sodom.
Abraham went with them to walk with them part of the way.

[17] Then the LORD said,
“Should I hide from Abraham what I am about to do?
[18] Abraham will surely become a great and mighty nation.
And all the nations of the earth will be blessed through him.
[19] For I know him.
He is to command his children and his household after him
to keep the way of the LORD
and to do what is right and just.
Then the LORD can bring about for Abraham what he has promised him.”

[20] And the LORD said,
“The cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is loud.
And their sin is very serious.
[21] I will go down now and see
whether what they have done is as bad as the reports that have come to me.
If not, I will know.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God treats Abraham like a friend. He tells him what he is planning. Later in the Bible, Abraham is called “God’s friend” (Isaiah 41:8; James 2:23).
> “To do what is right and just” – that is the task for Abraham’s family. It is to be an example for the whole world.
> The “cry” of Sodom is the crying of the victims. Great wrongs are happening in Sodom. The weak cry for help – and God hears it.
> God does not judge too quickly: “I will go down now and see.” He checks carefully before he judges.

## Abraham bargains with God (Verses 22–33)

[22] The men turned away and went toward Sodom.
But Abraham kept standing before the LORD.
[23] Abraham came closer and said,
“Will you really destroy the righteous together with the wicked?
[24] Maybe there are 50 righteous people in the city.
Will you still destroy it?
Will you not spare the place for the sake of the 50 righteous people in it?
[25] Far be it from you to do such a thing!
You cannot kill the righteous together with the wicked,
so that the righteous are treated just like the wicked.
Far be it from you!
Shouldn’t the Judge of all the earth do what is right?”

[26] The LORD said,
“If I find 50 righteous people in the city of Sodom,
then I will spare the whole place for their sake.”

[27] Abraham answered,
“Look, I have dared to speak to the Lord,
although I am only dust and ashes.
[28] Maybe five of the 50 righteous people are missing.
Will you destroy the whole city because five are missing?”
He said,
“I will not destroy it if I find 45 there.”

[29] Abraham spoke to him again and said,
“Maybe 40 will be found there.”
He said,
“I will not do it, for the sake of the 40.”

[30] Abraham said,
“Please do not let the Lord be angry, and I will speak.
Maybe 30 will be found there.”
He said,
“I will not do it if I find 30 there.”

[31] Abraham said,
“Look, I have dared to speak to the Lord.
Maybe 20 will be found there.”
He said,
“I will not destroy it, for the sake of the 20.”

[32] Abraham said,
“Please do not let the Lord be angry, and I will speak just once more.
Maybe 10 will be found there.”
He said,
“I will not destroy it, for the sake of the 10.”

[33] When the LORD had finished speaking with Abraham, he went on his way.
And Abraham went back to his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most amazing prayers in the Bible. Abraham does not pray for himself, but for a foreign, wicked city. But his nephew Lot also lives there.
> Abraham is brave. He reminds God of his own justice: “Shouldn’t the Judge of all the earth do what is right?” And yet he stays humble: “I am only dust and ashes.”
> God does not get angry. Each time, he agrees to Abraham’s request. God wants to save, not to destroy. Even for the sake of 10 righteous people, he would spare a whole city.
> The story shows: We may wrestle with God, bargain with him and pray for others. Prayer changes things.
