# Genesis – Chapter 43
### The second journey to Egypt – with Benjamin

---

## Judah guarantees Benjamin’s safety (Verses 1–10)

[1] The famine in the land was severe.
[2] They had eaten up all the grain they had brought from Egypt.
Then their father said to them,
“Go again and buy us a little more food.”

[3] Judah said to him,
“The man warned us very seriously.
He said,
‘You will not see my face again unless your brother is with you.’
[4] If you send our brother with us,
we will go down and buy food for you.
[5] But if you do not send him, we will not go down.
For the man said to us,
‘You will not see my face again unless your brother is with you.’”

[6] Israel said,
“Why did you treat me so badly?
Why did you tell the man that you had another brother?”
[7] They said,
“The man asked us directly about ourselves and about our family.
He asked,
‘Is your father still alive? Do you have another brother?’
We just answered his questions.
How could we know that he would say, ‘Bring your brother down here’?”

[8] Judah said to his father Israel,
“Send the boy with me.
Then we will get up and go,
so that we may live and not die –
we and you and our little children too.
[9] I will guarantee his safety.
You can hold me responsible for him.
If I do not bring him back to you and set him before you,
then let me carry the blame before you forever.
[10] For if we had not waited so long,
we could have been there and back twice by now.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hunger forces the family to act.
> Judah now takes the lead. He is the one who once suggested selling Joseph. Now he wants to stand up for Benjamin with his own life.
> “To guarantee” means: I will answer for it. If something goes wrong, I carry the responsibility.
> You can tell: Judah has changed. Since the story with Tamar (Chapter 38), he has become a different person.

---

## Jacob lets Benjamin go (Verses 11–15)

[11] Their father Israel said to them,
“If it must be so, then do this:
Take some of the best products of the land in your bags.
Take them down to the man as a gift:
a little balm and a little honey, spices and myrrh, nuts and almonds.
[12] Take twice as much money with you.
And take back the money that was put back in the top of your sacks.
Maybe it was a mistake.
[13] Take your brother too.
Get up and go back to the man.
[14] May God Almighty let the man show you mercy,
so that he will let your other brother and Benjamin go free.
As for me – if I lose my children, then I lose them.”

[15] So the men took the gift.
They took twice as much money with them, and they took Benjamin.
They got up, went down to Egypt and stood before Joseph.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob gives in. He does what he did back when he met Esau: he sends a gift ahead (Chapter 32).
> Balm, honey, spices and myrrh – these are goods like the ones carried by the traders who brought Joseph to Egypt (Genesis 37:25).
> “If I lose my children, then I lose them.” Jacob hands his worry over to God. He can no longer control anything. He has to trust.

---

## Afraid of Joseph’s house (Verses 16–25)

[16] When Joseph saw Benjamin with them, he said to the manager of his house,
“Bring the men into my house.
Slaughter an animal and prepare a meal.
For the men will eat with me at noon.”
[17] The man did what Joseph had told him.
He brought the men to Joseph’s house.

[18] The men were afraid because they were brought to Joseph’s house.
They said,
“We are brought here because of the money that was put back in our sacks the first time.
He wants an excuse to act against us.
He wants to attack us and take us as slaves, along with our donkeys.”

[19] They went up to the manager of Joseph’s house.
They spoke to him at the door of the house.
[20] They said,
“Please, my lord!
We really did come down the first time to buy food.
[21] When we got to the place where we stayed for the night, we opened our sacks.
Look: each man’s money was in the top of his sack.
It was our money, the full weight.
We have brought it back with us.
[22] We have also brought other money with us to buy food.
We do not know who put our money in our sacks.”

[23] He said,
“Peace be with you. Do not be afraid.
Your God, the God of your father, has put treasure in your sacks for you.
I received your money.”
Then he brought Simeon out to them.

[24] The man brought the men into Joseph’s house.
He gave them water, and they washed their feet.
He gave their donkeys food.
[25] They got the gift ready for when Joseph came at noon.
For they had heard that they were to eat a meal there.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers have a guilty conscience. That is why they expect punishment everywhere – even where good things happen to them.
> This time they are honest and tell about the money before anyone asks them. That too is a sign that they have changed.
> The manager speaks like someone who believes in the God of Israel. Maybe Joseph told him about his God.
> “Peace be with you” in Hebrew is “Shalom.” People in Israel still greet each other this way today.

## The feast (Verses 26–34)

[26] When Joseph came home, they brought him the gift they had with them into the house.
They bowed down to the ground before him.
[27] He asked them how they were.
And he asked,
“Is your father well, the old man you told me about?
Is he still alive?”
[28] They said,
“Your servant, our father, is well. He is still alive.”
And they bowed down low.

[29] Joseph looked up and saw his brother Benjamin, his mother’s son.
He asked,
“Is this your youngest brother, the one you told me about?”
And he said,
“May God be kind to you, my son.”
[30] Then Joseph hurried out,
because he was deeply moved by his brother.
He looked for a place to cry.
He went into his room and cried there.
[31] Then he washed his face and came out.
He controlled himself and said,
“Serve the meal.”

[32] They served Joseph by himself,
the brothers by themselves,
and the Egyptians who ate with him by themselves.
For the Egyptians do not eat with the Hebrews.
The Egyptians find that disgusting.

[33] The brothers sat before him, the firstborn according to his rank as the oldest, and the youngest according to his youth.
The men looked at each other in amazement.
[34] Joseph had portions brought to them from his own table.
But Benjamin’s portion was five times as big as anyone else’s.
They drank with him and had a good time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Once again the brothers bow down before Joseph – this time all eleven. Now the second dream has come true as well.
> When Joseph sees his little brother Benjamin, the only other son of his mother, he cannot hold back the tears. Benjamin was a small child when Joseph was sold.
> The Egyptians did not eat with foreigners. They thought they were better. That is why there are three separate tables.
> The brothers are seated exactly in the order of their age. How does this Egyptian know that? They are amazed.
> Benjamin gets five times as much. This is a test: will the brothers become jealous of the new favorite son – as they once did with Joseph? They stay cheerful. No jealousy.
