# Genesis – Chapter 2
### The day of rest, the garden of Eden, man and woman

---

## The seventh day: God rests (Verses 1–3)

[1] So the heavens and the earth were finished, with everything in them.
[2] On the seventh day, God had finished the work he had done.
And on the seventh day he rested from all the work he had done.
[3] God blessed the seventh day.
And he made it holy.
Holy means: The day is very special. It belongs to God.
For on this day God rested from all his work of creating, the work he had done.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is not tired. But he rests anyway. With this, he shows us: Rest is part of life.
> The week has one day that is different from the others. For Jews, this became the Sabbath (Saturday), and for Christians, Sunday.
> For today, this means: You are more than your work. You may take a break. That is God’s gift.

---

## God forms man from the earth (Verses 4–7)

[4] This is the history of the heavens and the earth, when they were created.
It was on the day when the LORD God made the earth and the heavens.

[5] At that time, there were no plants of the field on the earth yet.
And no plant of the field had sprung up yet.
For the LORD God had not yet sent rain on the earth.
And there was no man to work the ground.
[6] But a mist went up from the earth.
It watered the whole surface of the ground.

[7] Then the LORD God formed man from the dust of the ground.
He breathed the breath of life into his nose.
And the man became a living soul.
That means: a living being.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a second story of creation begins. It looks very closely at the human being.
> Here God’s name appears for the first time. In Hebrew, it has four letters: YHWH. It was probably pronounced “Yahweh.” It means something like: “I am here.” Out of deep respect, Jews do not say this name out loud. Instead, they say “the Lord.” That is why many Bibles write: the LORD.
> God forms the human being with his hands, like a potter shapes clay. Then he gives him his own breath.
> This means: We are made from earth – a part of nature. But we live from God’s breath.

---

## The garden of Eden (Verses 8–14)

[8] The LORD God planted a garden in Eden, in the east.
He put the man he had formed there.
[9] The LORD God made many trees grow out of the ground.
They were beautiful to look at, and their fruit was good to eat.
In the middle of the garden was the tree of life.
And there was also the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

[10] A river flowed out of Eden.
It watered the garden.
From there it divided and became four rivers.
[11] The name of the first river is Pishon.
It flows through the whole land of Havilah.
There is gold there.
[12] The gold of that land is good.
There is also bdellium there, a sweet-smelling resin, and the precious stone onyx.
[13] The name of the second river is Gihon.
It flows through the whole land of Cush.
[14] The name of the third river is Hiddekel.
It flows east of Assyria.
The fourth river is the Euphrates.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Eden” means “delight” or “joy.” The garden is a place where people lack nothing.
> Hiddekel is the old name of the Tigris. The Tigris and the Euphrates still exist today. They flow through Turkey, Syria and Iraq. No one knows exactly where the Pishon and the Gihon were. Cush is an old land south of Egypt.
> The two trees in the middle of the garden are very important for the story. One stands for life. The other stands for the question: Who decides what is good and what is evil – God or people?

---

## A task and a limit (Verses 15–17)

[15] The LORD God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden.
The man was to work the garden and take care of it.

[16] The LORD God gave the man a command.
He said, “You may eat from every tree in the garden, as much as you want.
[17] But you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
For on the day you eat from it, you will surely die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The human being gets a task: to tend the garden and protect it. So work is something good from the very beginning.
> God gives great freedom: **All** the trees are allowed. Only one is not.
> This one limit says: Trust me. I know what is good for you. You do not have to decide everything yourself.

---

## Being alone is not good (Verses 18–20)

[18] The LORD God said,
“It is not good for the man to be alone.
I will make him a helper who is right for him.”

[19] The LORD God formed every animal of the field and every bird of the sky out of the ground.
He brought them to the man.
He wanted to see what the man would call them.
Whatever the man called each living creature, that became its name.
[20] The man gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the sky and to every animal of the field.
But for the man, no helper was found who was right for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Until now, God always saw: “It is good.” Now he says for the first time: “It is **not** good” – being alone.
> People need other people. God made us for community.
> “Helper” does not mean “servant” or “assistant” here. In the Bible, even God himself is called a “helper.” It means a partner who stands on the same level.
> The man gives the animals names. This shows: He knows them and is responsible for them.

---

## God makes the woman (Verses 21–25)

[21] Then the LORD God made the man fall into a deep sleep.
While the man was sleeping, God took one of his ribs.
And he closed up the flesh in its place.
[22] From the rib he had taken from the man, the LORD God made a woman.
And he brought her to the man.

[23] Then the man said,
“At last! This is bone of my bones
and flesh of my flesh.
She will be called ‘woman,’
because she was taken out of Man.”

[24] That is why a man leaves his father and his mother.
He joins himself to his wife.
And the two become one flesh.

[25] The man and his wife were both naked.
But they were not ashamed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Old Jewish and Christian teachers said: God did not make the woman from the man’s head, so that she would stand above him. And not from his foot, so that she would stand below him. But from his side, so that she would stand at his side.
> In Hebrew, man is “ish” and woman is “ishah.” The words sound almost the same. They belong together.
> “Becoming one flesh” means: Man and woman join together completely – with body, heart and life.
> They are naked and not ashamed. That means: They have nothing to hide. They trust each other completely.
