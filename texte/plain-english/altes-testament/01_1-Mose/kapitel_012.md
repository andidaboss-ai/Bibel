# Genesis – Chapter 12
### God calls Abram

---

## God’s call and God’s promise (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD said to Abram,
“Leave your country,
leave your relatives
and leave your father’s house.
Go to the land that I will show you.
[2] I will make you into a great nation.
I will bless you
and make your name great.
And you will be a blessing.
[3] I will bless those who bless you.
And whoever treats you with contempt, I will curse.
Through you, all the families of the earth will be blessed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the whole Bible. Here God’s new path with humanity begins.
> Abram is to leave behind everything that gives him security: his homeland, his relatives, his father’s house. And he does not even know where the journey is going. God only says: “I will show you.”
> Remember Babel: There people said, “Let’s make a name for ourselves.” Here God says: **I** will make your name great. What people wanted to grab for themselves, God gives as a gift.
> Five times the word “bless,” “blessing” or “blessed” appears here. After the many curses in Chapters 3 to 11, the story of blessing now begins.
> And the blessing is not only for Abram. Through him, it is to come to **all** the families of the earth. To this day, Jews, Christians and Muslims see Abraham as their father in faith.

## Abram sets out (Verses 4–9)

[4] So Abram went, just as the LORD had told him.
And Lot went with him.
Abram was 75 years old when he left Haran.
[5] Abram took his wife Sarai,
and Lot, his brother’s son,
and everything they owned,
and the people who had come to belong to them in Haran.
They set out to go to the land of Canaan.
And they came into the land of Canaan.

[6] Abram traveled through the land as far as the place of Shechem, to the oak of Moreh.
At that time, the Canaanites lived in the land.
[7] Then the LORD appeared to Abram.
He said, “I will give this land to your descendants.”
Abram built an altar there to the LORD, who had appeared to him.

[8] From there he moved on to the hill country east of Bethel.
He set up his tent so that Bethel was to the west and Ai was to the east.
There he built an altar to the LORD
and called on the name of the LORD.
[9] Then Abram traveled on, farther and farther toward the South.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abram asks no questions. He simply goes. The Bible calls this faith: trusting God so much that you take the first step, even if you do not know the way yet.
> Abram is 75 years old! For God, no one is too old for a new beginning.
> The land still belongs to others, the Canaanites. Abram owns nothing there. He only has God’s promise.
> “The LORD appeared to Abram” means: God showed himself to him in a special way, so that Abram knew: God is here.
> Wherever Abram goes, he builds an altar and prays. In this way he shows: I trust the God who has promised me this land.

## Abram in Egypt (Verses 10–20)

[10] A famine came over the land.
A famine is a time when there is not enough food.
So Abram went down to Egypt.
He wanted to live there as a foreigner.
For the famine in the land was severe.

[11] When he came near Egypt, he said to his wife Sarai,
“Listen: I know that you are a beautiful woman.
[12] When the Egyptians see you, they will say, ‘This is his wife.’
Then they will kill me.
But they will let you live.
[13] Please say that you are my sister.
Then things will go well for me because of you.
And I will stay alive because of you.”

[14] When Abram came into Egypt, the Egyptians saw that the woman was very beautiful.
[15] Pharaoh’s officials also saw her.
Pharaoh is the king of Egypt.
They praised her to Pharaoh.
And the woman was taken into Pharaoh’s house.
[16] Pharaoh treated Abram well because of her.
Abram got sheep and cattle, male donkeys,
male and female servants, female donkeys and camels.

[17] But the LORD struck Pharaoh and his household with terrible plagues
because of Sarai, Abram’s wife.
Plagues are great troubles and illnesses.
[18] Then Pharaoh called Abram.
He said, “What have you done to me?
Why did you not tell me that she was your wife?
[19] Why did you say, ‘She is my sister’?
So I took her to be my wife.
Now here is your wife. Take her and go!”
[20] Pharaoh gave his men orders about Abram.
They sent him away, with his wife and everything he had.

> **What does this mean?**
> Just a moment ago, Abram trusted God completely. Now he becomes afraid and turns to a lie. To protect himself, he puts his wife in great danger. The Bible shows honestly: Even great examples of faith make serious mistakes.
> Sarai really was Abram’s half-sister (we learn this in Chapter 20). But he kept quiet about the most important thing: that she was his wife. A half-truth can also be a lie.
> God steps in and protects Sarai. He keeps his promise, even when Abram fails.
> It is shameful: Of all people, the foreign king behaves more decently here than Abram and confronts him.
