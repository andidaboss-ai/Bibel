# Genesis – Chapter 38
### Judah and Tamar

---

## Judah’s family (Verses 1–5)

[1] At that time, Judah left his brothers and went down.
He stayed with a man from Adullam whose name was Hirah.
[2] There Judah saw the daughter of a Canaanite man named Shua.
He took her as his wife and slept with her.
[3] She became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
Judah named him Er.
[4] She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son.
She named him Onan.
[5] Then she had one more son.
She named him Shelah.
Judah was at Chezib when she gave birth to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the Joseph story, a very different story suddenly comes. It is about Judah – the brother who suggested selling Joseph.
> Judah leaves his brothers and marries a Canaanite woman. Abraham and Isaac did not want that for their sons.
> The story shows how Judah changes. That will become very important later in the Joseph story.

---

## Tamar becomes a widow twice (Verses 6–11)

[6] Judah took a wife for Er, his firstborn.
Her name was Tamar.
[7] But Er, Judah’s firstborn, was wicked in the LORD’s eyes.
So the LORD put him to death.

[8] Then Judah said to Onan,
“Sleep with your brother’s wife.
Do your duty to her as the brother of her husband.
Give your brother descendants.”
[9] But Onan knew that the children would not count as his own.
So whenever he slept with his brother’s wife, he let his seed fall on the ground.
He did this so that he would not give his brother descendants.
[10] What he did was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
So the LORD put him to death too.

[11] Then Judah said to Tamar, his daughter-in-law,
“Stay as a widow in your father’s house,
until my son Shelah is grown up.”
For he thought, “Otherwise he might die too, like his brothers.”
So Tamar went and lived in her father’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then there was an important rule: If a man died without children, his brother was to marry the widow. The first child then counted as the child of the man who had died. So his name lived on, and the widow was taken care of. This is called “levirate marriage” (written down later in Deuteronomy 25).
> Onan uses Tamar: He sleeps with her, but he does not want to give her a child. For then he himself would get a bigger inheritance. He is selfish and leaves the widow without a future.
> Judah blames Tamar for the death of his sons. He sends her home and does not plan ever to give her Shelah. Tamar is stuck: She may not marry another man, but she does not get Shelah either.

---

## Tamar’s plan (Verses 12–19)

[12] Many days later, Judah’s wife, the daughter of Shua, died.
When Judah had been comforted, he went up to Timnah to the men who were shearing his sheep.
He went with his friend Hirah from Adullam.
[13] Tamar was told,
“Look, your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep.”

[14] So she took off her widow’s clothes.
She covered herself with a veil and wrapped herself up.
She sat down at the gate of Enaim, which is on the way to Timnah.
For she saw that Shelah was grown up by now,
and she had not been given to him as his wife.

[15] When Judah saw her, he thought she was a prostitute.
For she had covered her face.
[16] He turned off the road, went over to her and said,
“Please come, let me sleep with you.”
For he did not know that she was his daughter-in-law.
She asked,
“What will you give me if you sleep with me?”
[17] He said,
“I will send you a young goat from the flock.”
She said,
“Will you give me a pledge until you send it?”
[18] He asked,
“What pledge should I give you?”
She said,
“Your seal and your cord, and the staff in your hand.”
He gave them to her and slept with her.
And she became pregnant by him.

[19] Then she got up and went away.
She took off her veil and put her widow’s clothes back on.

> **What does this mean?**
> Tamar waits a long time. Then she realizes: Judah is not keeping his promise. She takes her future into her own hands.
> Her plan is bold. She risks her life with it. But it is her only way to get what is rightfully hers: a child from the family of her husband who died.
> A “pledge” is something you hand over as a security until you have paid.
> The seal (often on a cord) and the staff were like an ID card and a signature today. Everyone could tell by them: This belongs to Judah.
> Judah goes to a prostitute without thinking twice. The Bible does not show him here as a good example.

---

## Judah looks for the woman (Verses 20–23)

[20] Judah sent the young goat with his friend from Adullam.
He was to get the pledge back from the woman.
But he did not find her.
[21] He asked the men of that place,
“Where is the prostitute who was at Enaim by the road?”
They said,
“There has been no prostitute here.”
[22] He went back to Judah and said,
“I did not find her.
And the men of the place also said, ‘There has been no prostitute here.’”
[23] Judah said,
“Let her keep the things.
Otherwise people will laugh at us.
Look, I sent this young goat, but you did not find her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Hebrew original, Hirah uses a different word here than in verse 15. It means a woman who worked as a prostitute at a foreign temple. Hirah probably wants to make the matter sound less embarrassing.
> Judah wants one thing above all: that no one finds out. His reputation matters more to him than anything else.

---

## “She is more righteous than I” (Verses 24–26)

[24] About three months later, Judah was told,
“Your daughter-in-law Tamar has acted like a prostitute.
And look: Because of it, she is pregnant.”
Judah said,
“Bring her out! Let her be burned.”

[25] As she was being brought out, she sent a message to her father-in-law.
She said,
“I am pregnant by the man who owns these things.”
And she said,
“Please look at these and see whose they are:
the seal, the cords and the staff.”

[26] Judah recognized them.
He said,
“She is more righteous than I.
For I did not give her to my son Shelah.”
Righteous means: doing what is right.
After that, he did not sleep with her again.

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah condemns Tamar to death right away. He sees no problem with himself, but for her he wants the harshest punishment. That is hypocrisy.
> Tamar is wise. She does not shame Judah in public. She sends him the things secretly and gives him the chance to tell the truth himself.
> “Please look … and see” – these words again! With exactly these words, Judah and his brothers had shown their father Joseph’s bloody robe (Genesis 37:32). Now Judah himself is proven guilty with these words.
> And then the miracle: Judah admits his guilt. In public. “She is more righteous than I.” For the first time in this family, someone honestly admits a wrong. This is the turning point in Judah’s life.

## Twins (Verses 27–30)

[27] When the time came for her to give birth – look: There were twins in her womb.
[28] While she was giving birth, one of them put out a hand.
The midwife took a scarlet thread and tied it on his hand.
Scarlet is a bright red color.
She said,
“This one came out first.”
[29] But he pulled his hand back.
And look: His brother came out.
The midwife said,
“Why have you broken out like this?”
That is why he was named Perez.
[30] After that, his brother came out, who had the scarlet thread on his hand.
He was named Zerah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Twins again, and again a struggle over who is first – just like with Esau and Jacob.
> Perez means “breaking out.” Zerah means “shining,” maybe because of the red thread.
> Tamar even gets two sons – in place of the two sons of Judah who died.
> Tamar, a foreign woman who had to fight for her rights, becomes an ancestor of King David. Her son Perez is part of David’s family line (Ruth 4:18–22). In the New Testament, Tamar is in the family tree of Jesus – as one of only four women who are named there (Matthew 1:3).
