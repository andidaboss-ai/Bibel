# Genesis – Chapter 1
### God creates the world

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Genesis?

The Bible is really a collection of many books.
Genesis is the first book of the Bible.
It is also called “the First Book of Moses.”
The name “Genesis” means “beginning” or “origin.”
It tells how the world and people came to be.
And it tells how God starts a new path with one family: with Abraham and his descendants.
Jews and Christians have read this book for more than 2,500 years.

---

## In the beginning (Verses 1–2)

[1] In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

[2] The earth had no form yet.
It was empty.
Darkness was over the surface of the deep water.
And God’s Spirit was hovering over the waters.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first sentence of the Bible says the most important thing: Everything that exists comes from God.
> In the beginning, everything was empty, dark and without order.
> But God was already there. His Spirit was very close, like a bird hovering over its nest.
> This is still true today: God is there – even where a life feels empty and dark.

---

## The first day: God makes light (Verses 3–5)

[3] God said, “Let there be light.”
And there was light.
[4] God looked at the light. He saw that it was good.
Then God separated the light from the darkness.
[5] God called the light “day.”
And he called the darkness “night.”
There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the first day.

> **What does this mean?**
> God only has to speak – and it happens. His word has power.
> The first thing God makes is light. Without light, you cannot see anything, and nothing can grow.
> God looks at his work and is glad: It is good.
> In the Bible, a day begins in the evening. That is why it always says “evening” first and then “morning.” Jews still count days this way today.

---

## The second day: God makes the sky (Verses 6–8)

[6] God said, “Let there be an expanse in the middle of the waters.
Let it separate the waters from the waters.”
[7] God made the expanse.
An expanse is a wide, open space.
So he separated the waters under the expanse from the waters above the expanse.
And so it happened.
[8] God called the expanse “sky.”
There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the second day.

> **What does this mean?**
> People back then imagined the sky like a huge bell or dome. Above the dome, they thought, there is water. That is where the rain comes from.
> The Bible speaks in the language of its time. It does not want to be a science textbook.
> Its message is: God creates order. He makes room so that life can begin.

---

## The third day: land, sea and plants (Verses 9–13)

[9] God said, “Let the waters under the sky gather in one place.
Then let the dry land appear.”
And so it happened.
[10] God called the dry land “earth.”
And he called the gathered waters “seas.”
God saw that it was good.

[11] God said, “Let the earth make green things grow:
grass and plants that make seeds,
and fruit trees that bear fruit.
Let the seeds be in the fruit.
Let every plant grow on the earth, each according to its kind.”
And so it happened.
[12] The earth made green things grow:
grass and plants that make seeds, each according to its kind,
and trees that bear fruit with seeds in it, each according to its kind.
God saw that it was good.
[13] There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the third day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now there is solid ground under our feet. And there is something to eat.
> God thinks about the future: Every plant has seeds. So new life can always grow.
> “Each according to its kind” means: An apple seed grows into an apple tree. God made the world with a good order.

---

## The fourth day: sun, moon and stars (Verses 14–19)

[14] God said, “Let there be lights in the expanse of the sky.
Let them separate the day from the night.
Let them be signs.
Let them mark the seasons, the days and the years.
[15] Let them be lights in the expanse of the sky and shine on the earth.”
And so it happened.

[16] God made the two great lights:
the bigger light to rule over the day,
and the smaller light to rule over the night.
He also made the stars.
[17] God put the lights in the expanse of the sky.
They were to shine on the earth.
[18] They were to rule over the day and over the night.
And they were to separate the light from the darkness.
God saw that it was good.
[19] There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the fourth day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “bigger light” is the sun. The “smaller light” is the moon.
> The Bible does not call them by name on purpose. Back then, many peoples worshiped the sun and the moon as gods. The Bible says: These are not gods. God made them, like lamps on a ceiling.
> The sun and the moon help us to divide time: into days, months, years and festivals.

---

## The fifth day: animals in the water and birds (Verses 20–23)

[20] God said, “Let the waters be full of living creatures.
And let birds fly above the earth, across the open sky.”
[21] God created the large sea creatures
and every living creature that moves and swarms in the water, each according to its kind.
And he created every bird with wings, each according to its kind.
God saw that it was good.
[22] God blessed the animals.
To bless means: God wishes something good and gives the power for it.
He said, “Have many young, and become many!
Fill the waters in the seas.
And let the birds become many on the earth.”
[23] There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the fifth day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now there are the first living creatures. Things swim, fly and swarm.
> For the first time, God blesses. He wants life to grow and spread out.
> God loves variety. He wanted every kind – from the whale to the sparrow.

---

## The sixth day: land animals and people (Verses 24–31)

[24] God said, “Let the earth bring out living creatures, each according to its kind:
livestock, creeping animals and wild animals, each according to its kind.”
And so it happened.
[25] God made the wild animals, each according to its kind,
the livestock, each according to its kind,
and every animal that creeps on the ground, each according to its kind.
God saw that it was good.

[26] Then God said, “Let us make man in our image, to be like us.
Let them rule over the fish in the sea
and over the birds in the sky,
over the livestock and over the whole earth,
and over every animal that creeps on the earth.”

[27] So God created man in his own image.
Here, man means: the human being.
He created him in God’s image.
He created them male and female.

[28] God blessed them.
God said to them, “Have many children, and become many!
Fill the earth and bring it under your control.
Rule over the fish in the sea, over the birds in the sky,
and over every living thing that moves on the earth.”

[29] God said, “Look! I have given you every plant on the whole earth that makes seeds,
and every tree that bears fruit with seeds.
This will be your food.
[30] And to every animal on the earth, to every bird in the sky,
and to everything that creeps on the earth and has life in it,
I have given all the green plants for food.”
And so it happened.

[31] God looked at everything he had made.
And look: It was very good.
There was evening, and there was morning.
That was the sixth day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The human being is an “image of God.” That means: Every person reflects something of God. That is why every person has a dignity that no one may take away – no matter what they look like, where they come from, or what they can or cannot do.
> Man and woman are **both** the image of God. They are worth the same.
> “Rule” and “bring under control” do not mean here: use it up and exploit it. A king in the Bible is supposed to care for his land and protect it. In the same way, people are supposed to care for the earth and the animals.
> God says, “Let **us**.” Jews often understand it like this: God is talking with his angels, or he speaks the way a king does, saying “we.” Many Christians see in it a hint of God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
> Interesting: In the beginning, people and animals eat only plants. No one has to lose their life to be someone else’s food. That is how peaceful God’s world was meant to be.
> Six times God saw: “It is good.” Now he sees: “It is **very** good.”
