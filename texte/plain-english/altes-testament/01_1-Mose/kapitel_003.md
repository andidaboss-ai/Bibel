# Genesis – Chapter 3
### People stop trusting God

---

## The serpent and the woman (Verses 1–5)

[1] The serpent was more cunning than any animal of the field that the LORD God had made.
It said to the woman,
“Did God really say, ‘You must not eat from any tree in the garden’?”

[2] The woman answered the serpent,
“We may eat fruit from the trees in the garden.
[3] But not the fruit of the tree in the middle of the garden.
God has said, ‘You must not eat from it. You must not touch it. Or else you will die.’”

[4] Then the serpent said to the woman,
“You will not really die.
[5] For God knows: On the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened.
Then you will be like God.
Then you will know what is good and what is evil.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The serpent begins with a question. And it twists what God said. God had allowed **all** the trees except one. The serpent acts as if God had forbidden everything.
> This is how mistrust begins: “Does God really mean well with me? Or does he want to take something away from me?”
> The woman also exaggerates a little: God had said nothing about touching.
> Then the serpent lies openly: “You will not really die.” And it tempts her: “You will be like God.” This is the very first temptation: wanting to be God yourself and deciding for yourself what is good and evil.
> In this chapter, it is only called “the serpent.” Later, Jews and Christians connected it with the devil (for example in Revelation 12:9).

---

## They eat (Verses 6–7)

[6] The woman looked at the tree.
She saw: The tree is good for food.
It is a delight to the eyes.
And it is attractive, because it makes a person wise.
So she took some of its fruit and ate.
She also gave some to her husband, who was with her.
And he ate it too.

[7] Then the eyes of both of them were opened.
And they knew: We are naked.
They sewed fig leaves together
and made coverings for themselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> The man was standing next to her the whole time. He said nothing and did nothing. Both of them are responsible.
> Their eyes really are opened – but not the way the serpent promised. They do not become like God. They only see their own nakedness and weakness.
> Before, they were not ashamed. Now they want to hide. Trust is broken.

---

## God looks for the human beings (Verses 8–13)

[8] They heard the voice of the LORD God.
He was walking in the garden when the day was getting cool.
Then the man and his wife hid from the LORD God among the trees of the garden.

[9] The LORD God called to the man.
He said to him, “Where are you?”

[10] The man answered,
“I heard your voice in the garden.
I was afraid, because I was naked.
So I hid.”

[11] God said,
“Who told you that you were naked?
Have you eaten from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?”

[12] The man said,
“The woman you gave to be with me –
she gave me fruit from the tree.
And I ate it.”

[13] The LORD God said to the woman,
“What have you done?”
The woman said,
“The serpent tricked me. And I ate.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people run away from God. But God goes after them. He looks for them.
> “Where are you?” Of course God knows where they are. With this question, he gives them the chance to come out by themselves and be honest.
> But both of them pass the blame on. The man says: “It was the woman – and really you, God, because you gave her to me.” The woman says: “It was the serpent.”
> Everyone knows this: When we have done something wrong, we quickly look for someone else to blame.

---

## What comes now (Verses 14–19)

[14] The LORD God said to the serpent,
“Because you have done this, you are cursed.
You are cursed more than all the livestock and more than every animal of the field.
You will crawl on your belly.
You will eat dust all the days of your life.
[15] I will put hostility between you and the woman,
and between your offspring and her offspring.
He will bruise your head.
And you will bruise his heel.”

[16] To the woman God said,
“I will make your pain in childbirth very great.
You will give birth to children in pain.
You will long for your husband.
And he will rule over you.”

[17] And to Adam God said,
“You listened to your wife’s voice.
And you ate from the tree that I commanded you about. I said: ‘You must not eat from it.’
That is why the ground is cursed – because of you.
You will eat from it with hard work all the days of your life.
[18] It will grow thorns and thistles for you.
And you will eat the plants of the field.
[19] You will eat bread by the sweat of your face
until you return to the ground.
For you were taken out of it.
Yes, you are dust,
and you will return to dust.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses explain why life is often so hard: pain in giving birth, hard work, conflict and power struggles between man and woman, and in the end, death.
> Important: God curses the serpent and the ground. But he does not curse the people themselves.
> That the man rules over the woman is **not** God’s good plan. In Chapters 1 and 2, man and woman are of equal worth. Here the Bible describes how things, sadly, often are in a broken world.
> Verse 15 is a first ray of hope: A descendant of the woman will bruise the serpent’s head. Evil will be wounded, and in the end defeated. Christians see in this an early hint of Jesus.
> “Adam” is the Hebrew word for “human being.” It sounds like “adamah,” which means “earth.” So the human being is, so to speak, the “earthling.”

## God still takes care of them (Verses 20–24)

[20] The man called his wife Eve.
That name means “life.”
For she would become the mother of all the living.

[21] The LORD God made clothes from animal skins for Adam and his wife.
And he dressed them.

[22] The LORD God said,
“Look! The man has become like one of us.
He knows good and evil.
Now he must not also reach out his hand,
take from the tree of life, eat,
and live forever …”

[23] So the LORD God sent him out of the garden of Eden.
He was to work the ground he had been taken from.
[24] So God drove the man out.
And at the east of the garden of Eden he placed the cherubim.
Cherubim are mighty angel-like beings.
And he placed a flaming sword that turned in every direction.
They were to guard the way to the tree of life.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of this sad story, there are signs of hope:
> The man calls his wife Eve, that is, “life.” Death was just mentioned – but life goes on.
> God himself makes clothes for the people. The leaves were not enough. God takes care of them, even though they did not trust him.
> God’s sentence in verse 22 stops in the middle. This shows how hard this moment is.
> Having to leave the garden is a punishment. But it is also a protection: The people should not live forever in this broken state.
> The whole rest of the Bible tells how God opens the way back. On the last page of the Bible (Revelation 22), the tree of life is there again – and the way to it is open.
