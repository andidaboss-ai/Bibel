# Genesis – Chapter 24
### A wife for Isaac

---

## Abraham’s task for his servant (Verses 1–9)

[1] Abraham was old, very far along in years.
The LORD had blessed Abraham in everything.
[2] Abraham spoke to his servant, the oldest servant of his household.
He was in charge of everything Abraham owned.
Abraham said,
“Please put your hand under my thigh.
[3] I want you to swear by the LORD, the God of heaven and the God of the earth:
You must not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites, among whom I live.
[4] Instead, you are to go to my country, to my relatives.
There you are to get a wife for my son Isaac.”

[5] The servant said to him,
“What if the woman does not want to follow me to this land?
Should I then take your son back to the land you came from?”
[6] Abraham said to him,
“Be careful not to take my son back there!
[7] The LORD, the God of heaven, took me from my father’s house and from the land where I was born.
He spoke to me and swore to me,
‘I will give this land to your descendants.’
He will send his angel ahead of you.
And you will get a wife for my son from there.
[8] But if the woman does not want to follow you,
then you are free from this oath to me.
Only you must not take my son back there.”

[9] So the servant put his hand under the thigh of his master Abraham
and swore to him about this matter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham is old and thinks about the future. Isaac is to marry a woman from his own relatives, not from the peoples in Canaan who worship other gods.
> “To put your hand under the thigh” was a very solemn way of swearing back then. The thigh stood for one’s descendants and for life.
> Isaac must not go back to Mesopotamia under any circumstances. The land of Canaan is the land of God’s promise. The path leads forward, not back.
> Abraham trusts: God will send his angel ahead.

## The prayer at the well (Verses 10–14)

[10] The servant took 10 of his master’s camels.
He also took many valuable gifts from his master with him.
Then he set out for Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor.

[11] Outside the city, he made the camels kneel down by the well of water.
It was evening, the time when the women come out to draw water.
[12] He prayed,
“LORD, God of my master Abraham,
please let me succeed today.
Show kindness to my master Abraham.
[13] Look, I am standing here by the spring of water.
The daughters of the people of the city are coming out to draw water.
[14] I will say to a young woman,
‘Please tip your jar so that I can drink.’
If she then says,
‘Drink, and I will also give your camels a drink’ –
let her be the one you have chosen for your servant Isaac.
By this I will know that you have shown kindness to my master.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mesopotamia is the land between the Euphrates and Tigris rivers, in what is now Syria and Iraq. The journey there was many hundreds of miles long.
> The servant prays. He does not leave the important decision to chance. He leaves it to God.
> His sign is wisely chosen: After a long journey, a camel can drink up to about 25 gallons of water. Giving water to 10 camels is hard work. A woman who offers to do that on her own is helpful, hardworking and generous.

---

## Rebekah (Verses 15–27)

[15] He had not yet finished praying when Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder.
She was the daughter of Bethuel.
Bethuel was the son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham’s brother.
[16] The young woman was very beautiful.
She was a virgin. No man had slept with her.
She went down to the spring, filled her jar and came back up.

[17] Then the servant ran to meet her and said,
“Please let me drink a little water from your jar.”
[18] She said, “Drink, my lord!”
Quickly she lowered her jar onto her hand and gave him a drink.
[19] When she had given him enough to drink, she said,
“I will also draw water for your camels, until they have had enough to drink.”
[20] Quickly she emptied her jar into the trough.
A trough is a long, open container for animals to drink from.
Then she ran back to the well to draw water.
She drew water for all his camels.

[21] The man watched her in silence.
He wanted to know whether the LORD had made his journey successful or not.

[22] When the camels had finished drinking, the man took a gold ring.
It weighed half a shekel (about 0.2 ounces).
And he took two gold bracelets for her arms.
They weighed 10 shekels (about 4 ounces).
[23] He said,
“Whose daughter are you? Please tell me.
Is there room in your father’s house for us to spend the night?”
[24] She answered him,
“I am the daughter of Bethuel, the son whom Milcah gave birth to for Nahor.”
[25] And she said,
“We have plenty of straw and feed.
And we also have room for you to spend the night.”

[26] Then the man bowed his head and worshiped the LORD.
[27] He said,
“Blessed be the LORD, the God of my master Abraham!
He has not stopped showing his loving kindness and his faithfulness to my master.
As for me, the LORD has led me on the way to the house of my master’s relatives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers even before the servant has finished praying.
> Rebekah does exactly what the servant said in his prayer – and even more. She runs and draws water until all 10 camels have had enough.
> And she belongs to exactly the family from which Isaac’s wife is to come: She is the granddaughter of Abraham’s brother Nahor (see Chapter 22).
> A shekel is a weight of about 0.4 ounces.
> The servant does not forget to thank God. He praises God for “his loving kindness and his faithfulness” – that means: God means well and keeps what he promises.

## Laban welcomes the servant (Verses 28–33)

[28] The young woman ran home and told her mother’s household what had happened.
[29] Rebekah had a brother. His name was Laban.
Laban ran out to the man at the spring.
[30] For he had seen the ring and the bracelets on his sister’s arms.
And he had heard his sister Rebekah say, “This is what the man said to me.”
So he went to the man.
And look: He was still standing by the camels at the spring.
[31] Laban said,
“Come in, you who are blessed by the LORD!
Why are you standing out here?
I have made the house ready, and there is room for the camels.”

[32] So the man came into the house.
The camels were unloaded and given straw and feed.
And water was brought so that he and the men who were with him could wash their feet.
[33] Then food was set in front of him.
But he said,
“I will not eat until I have said what I have to say.”
Laban said, “Speak.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Laban will become important later (in the story of Jacob). Already here you can see: He looks very closely at the gold. He is a man to whom riches mean a lot.
> The servant is completely focused on his task. He will not eat before he has given his message.

## The servant tells everything (Verses 34–49)

[34] He said,
“I am Abraham’s servant.
[35] The LORD has blessed my master greatly.
He has become very rich.
The LORD has given him flocks and herds, silver and gold,
male and female servants, camels and donkeys.
[36] Sarah, my master’s wife, gave birth to a son for my master when she was old.
My master has given him everything he owns.

[37] “My master made me swear. He said,
‘You must not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites, in whose land I live.
[38] Instead, go to my father’s house and to my relatives.
There you are to get a wife for my son.’
[39] I said to my master,
‘What if the woman will not follow me?’
[40] He said to me,
‘The LORD, before whom I walk, will send his angel with you.
He will make your journey successful.
You are to get a wife for my son from my relatives, from my father’s house.
[41] Then you will be free from my oath, when you come to my relatives.
If they do not give her to you, you will be free from my oath.’

[42] “Today I came to the spring.
And I prayed,
‘LORD, God of my master Abraham, if you will make the journey I am on successful –
[43] look, I am standing here by the spring of water.
I will say to the young woman who comes out to draw water,
“Please give me a little water from your jar to drink.”
[44] If she then says to me,
“Drink, and I will also draw water for your camels” –
let her be the woman the LORD has chosen for my master’s son.’

[45] “Before I had finished praying in my heart,
Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder.
She went down to the spring and drew water.
I said to her, ‘Please let me drink.’
[46] Quickly she lowered her jar from her shoulder and said,
‘Drink, and I will also give your camels a drink.’
So I drank, and she also gave the camels a drink.
[47] I asked her,
‘Whose daughter are you?’
She said,
‘The daughter of Bethuel, Nahor’s son, whom Milcah gave birth to for him.’
Then I put the ring in her nose and the bracelets on her arms.
[48] I bowed my head and worshiped the LORD.
I praised the LORD, the God of my master Abraham.
He has led me on the right way to get my master’s brother’s daughter for his son.

[49] “Now tell me:
Will you show kindness and faithfulness to my master?
Then tell me.
If not, tell me that too.
Then I will know whether to turn to the right or to the left.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The servant tells the whole story once more. The Bible repeats it in detail on purpose. This makes it clear: It was not chance at work here, but God.
> “To turn to the right or to the left” means: Then I will know how things will go on, and I can look somewhere else.

## Rebekah says yes (Verses 50–61)

[50] Laban and Bethuel answered,
“This comes from the LORD.
We cannot say anything to you, either bad or good.
[51] Look, here is Rebekah.
Take her and go.
Let her be the wife of your master’s son, as the LORD has said.”

[52] When Abraham’s servant heard their words, he bowed down to the ground before the LORD.
[53] Then the servant brought out jewelry of silver and gold, and clothes.
He gave them to Rebekah.
He also gave valuable gifts to her brother and her mother.

[54] Then they ate and drank, he and the men who were with him.
They stayed overnight.
In the morning they got up, and he said,
“Send me back to my master.”
[55] Her brother and her mother said,
“Let the young woman stay with us a few days, at least 10.
After that she may go.”
[56] But he said to them,
“Do not hold me back!
The LORD has made my journey successful.
Send me away, so that I may go to my master.”

[57] They said,
“We will call the young woman and ask her.”
[58] They called Rebekah and asked her,
“Will you go with this man?”
She answered,
“I will go.”

[59] So they sent their sister Rebekah away,
together with her nurse, and with Abraham’s servant and his men.
[60] They blessed Rebekah and said to her,
“Our sister, may you become the mother of thousands upon thousands!
May your descendants take over the gate of those who hate them.”

[61] Rebekah set out with her servant women.
They rode on the camels and followed the man.
So the servant took Rebekah and went on his way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Even Laban and Bethuel recognize: God has acted here.
> Important: Rebekah is asked. She decides for herself. And she says bravely: “I will go.”
> In this she does the same as Abraham: She leaves her homeland and her family and goes to a foreign land, trusting in God.
> A “nurse” here is a woman who has taken care of a child since she was little, like a nanny.
> Her family’s blessing sounds almost exactly like God’s blessing for Abraham in Chapter 22.

## Isaac and Rebekah (Verses 62–67)

[62] Meanwhile, Isaac had come back from the well Beer Lahai Roi.
He lived in the land of the South.
[63] In the evening, Isaac went out into the field to think and pray quietly.
He looked up.
And look: Camels were coming.

[64] Rebekah also looked up.
When she saw Isaac, she quickly got down from the camel.
[65] She asked the servant,
“Who is that man walking in the field to meet us?”
The servant said,
“It is my master.”
Then she took her veil and covered herself.

[66] The servant told Isaac everything he had done.
[67] Isaac brought Rebekah into the tent of his mother Sarah.
He took Rebekah, and she became his wife.
And he loved her.
So Isaac was comforted after his mother’s death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Beer Lahai Roi is the well where God met Hagar: “You are a God who sees” (Chapter 16).
> Isaac is a quiet, thoughtful person. He goes out in the evening to be alone and to pray.
> When Rebekah takes her veil, that was a custom back then: The bride covered herself before the bridegroom until the wedding.
> The last sentence is very moving: “He loved her.” It is the first time in the Bible that it says clearly that a man loves his wife.
> Isaac grieved for his mother for a long time. Now he finds comfort in his love for Rebekah.
