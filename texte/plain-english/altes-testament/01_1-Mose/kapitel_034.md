# Genesis – Chapter 34
### The wrong done to Dinah and her brothers’ revenge

---

## Dinah is raped (Verses 1–5)

[1] Dinah was the daughter whom Leah had given birth to for Jacob.
She went out to visit the young women of the land.
[2] Shechem saw her.
He was the son of Hamor the Hivite.
Hamor was the prince of the land.
Shechem took her, slept with her and forced her.
[3] After that, his heart was attached to Dinah, Jacob’s daughter.
He loved the young woman and spoke kindly to her.
[4] Shechem said to his father Hamor,
“Get me this young woman as my wife!”

[5] Jacob heard that Shechem had dishonored his daughter Dinah.
But his sons were with his livestock in the field.
So Jacob kept silent until they came home.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter tells of a terrible crime: Dinah is raped. The Bible does not keep quiet about such things. It calls wrong by its name.
> Dinah herself never speaks in the whole chapter. No one asks her how she is. That stands out, and it is sad. All the men talk about her, none of them with her.
> Afterwards Shechem claims that he loves her. But he does not say he is sorry, and he does not let her go home. She stays in his house (see verse 26).
> Jacob keeps silent. He does nothing for his daughter. Maybe because she is Leah’s daughter, not Rachel’s.

---

## Hamor’s offer (Verses 6–12)

[6] Hamor, Shechem’s father, went out to Jacob to talk with him.
[7] Jacob’s sons came in from the field when they heard it.
The men were deeply hurt and very angry.
For Shechem had done a shameful thing in Israel.
He had slept with Jacob’s daughter.
Such a thing must not be done.

[8] Hamor talked with them and said,
“My son Shechem longs with all his heart for your daughter.
Please give her to him as his wife.
[9] Join with us through marriage.
Give your daughters to us, and take our daughters for yourselves.
[10] You can live with us.
The land is open to you.
Live here, trade here and get property in the land.”

[11] Shechem said to Dinah’s father and to her brothers,
“Please be kind to me!
Whatever you tell me, I will give.
[12] Ask me for a very high bride price and many gifts.
I will give whatever you ask of me.
Only give me the young woman as my wife.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here, for the first time in the Bible, the words “in Israel” appear. Jacob’s family is now “Israel,” a people with its own values.
> Hamor talks as if nothing bad had happened. He talks only about marriage and business. He does not say he is sorry for what his son has done.
> Shechem wants to make everything right with money. But a wrong like this cannot simply be paid for.

---

## The brothers’ trick (Verses 13–24)

[13] Jacob’s sons answered Shechem and his father Hamor with a trick.
For Shechem had dishonored their sister Dinah.
[14] They said to them,
“We can’t do this.
We can’t give our sister to a man who is not circumcised.
That would be a disgrace for us.
[15] We will agree only on one condition:
You must become like us.
Every male among you must be circumcised.
[16] Then we will give our daughters to you,
and we will take your daughters for ourselves.
Then we will live with you and become one people.
[17] But if you do not listen to us and are not circumcised,
then we will take our sister and leave.”
To be circumcised means: The foreskin is cut off. It is a sign of belonging to God.

[18] Their words pleased Hamor and his son Shechem.
[19] The young man did not wait to do it.
For he was delighted with Jacob’s daughter.
And he was the most respected person in his father’s whole family.

[20] Hamor and his son Shechem came to the gate of their city.
There they talked with the men of their city.
They said,
[21] “These men are peaceful toward us.
So let them live in the land and trade in it.
Look, the land is large enough for them.
Let us take their daughters as our wives, and let us give them our daughters.
[22] But the men will agree to live with us and become one people only on one condition:
Every male among us must be circumcised, just as they are circumcised.
[23] Then won’t their livestock, their property and all their animals be ours?
Let us just agree with them.
Then they will live with us.”

[24] All the men who went out of the gate of his city listened to Hamor and to his son Shechem.
And every male was circumcised, all who went out of the gate of his city.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers misuse circumcision, the holy sign of the covenant with God, for a sneaky plan.
> But Hamor and Shechem are not honest either. To their own people they say: Soon all their property will be ours! Each side wants to take advantage of the other.
> Back then, important matters were discussed and decided in the gate of the city.

---

## The bloodbath (Verses 25–31)

[25] On the third day, the men were in great pain.
Then two of Jacob’s sons, Simeon and Levi, Dinah’s brothers, each took his sword.
They attacked the city, which did not expect anything, and killed all the males.
[26] They also killed Hamor and his son Shechem with the sword.
They took Dinah out of Shechem’s house and went away.

[27] Then Jacob’s sons came upon the dead and robbed the city.
For the people there had dishonored their sister.
[28] They took their flocks, their herds and their donkeys,
everything that was in the city and in the field.
[29] They took all their wealth.
They took all their little children and their wives as prisoners.
And they robbed everything that was in the houses.

[30] Then Jacob said to Simeon and Levi,
“You have brought trouble on me.
Now the people who live in the land, the Canaanites and the Perizzites, hate me.
I have only a few people.
They will gather together against me and attack me.
Then I will be destroyed, I and my whole family.”
[31] But they said,
“Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon and Levi are Dinah’s full brothers – both are sons of Leah. They take revenge.
> But their revenge goes far beyond any limit. For the crime of one man, they kill a whole city. The other brothers rob everything and take women and children as prisoners. Innocent people suffer for the deed of one person.
> That reminds us of Lamech in Chapter 4: Violence creates more and more violence.
> Jacob thinks only of his own safety. He says no word about Dinah and no word about the people who were killed.
> The brothers are not completely wrong with their question: The wrong done to Dinah was terrible. But their answer to it was also wrong.
> The Bible ends the chapter without a verdict. But at the end of his life, Jacob will sharply blame Simeon and Levi for this deed: “Their anger is cursed, because it was fierce” (Genesis 49:7).
