# Genesis – Chapter 39
### Joseph with Potiphar – and in prison

---

## God is with Joseph (Verses 1–6)

[1] Joseph had been brought down to Egypt.
Potiphar, an Egyptian, bought him from the Ishmaelites who had brought him there.
Potiphar was an officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard.
[2] The LORD was with Joseph.
So everything went well for him.
He lived in the house of his master, the Egyptian.

[3] His master saw that the LORD was with him.
He saw that the LORD made everything Joseph did go well.
[4] So Joseph won his master’s favor.
He served him personally.
Potiphar put him in charge of his house.
He put everything he owned into Joseph’s hands.

[5] From the time when he put him in charge of his house and of everything he owned,
the LORD blessed the Egyptian’s house because of Joseph.
The LORD’s blessing was on everything Potiphar owned, in the house and in the field.
[6] Potiphar left everything he had in Joseph’s hands.
He did not worry about anything himself, except the food he ate.
Joseph had a fine build and was handsome.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph is a slave in a foreign land. He has lost everything: his family, his home, his freedom.
> But one sentence comes up again and again in this chapter: “The LORD was with Joseph.” God has not left him – not even in slavery.
> God even blesses the house of an Egyptian because of Joseph. That reminds us of God’s promise to Abraham: Through you all peoples will be blessed.
> Potiphar trusts Joseph so much that he does not worry about anything anymore. “Except the food” may mean: Back then, Egyptians were not allowed to eat with foreigners (see Genesis 43:32).

---

## Potiphar’s wife (Verses 7–12)

[7] After some time, his master’s wife set her eyes on Joseph.
She said to him,
“Sleep with me!”
[8] But he refused.
He said to his master’s wife,
“Look, my master does not worry about anything in the house, because I am here.
He has put everything he owns into my hands.
[9] No one in this house is greater than I am.
He has kept nothing back from me except you,
because you are his wife.
How could I do such a great evil and sin against God?”

[10] She spoke to Joseph day after day.
But he did not listen to her.
He would not sleep with her, or even be near her.

[11] One day, Joseph went into the house to do his work.
None of the men of the house were inside.
[12] She grabbed him by his clothes and said,
“Sleep with me!”
But he left his clothes in her hand and ran outside.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph is young, alone and in a foreign land. No one would find out. The temptation is great – and it comes every day.
> But Joseph stays firm. He thinks of two things: He does not want to cheat his master, who trusts him. And he does not want to sin against God.
> For Joseph it is clear: Every wrong against a person is also a wrong before God.
> Joseph stays out of her way. And when it gets serious, he runs away. Sometimes running away is the best decision.
> Once again a piece of clothing plays a part: For the second time, Joseph loses his clothes, and again they are used to tell a lie.

---

## The false accusation (Verses 13–20)

[13] She saw that he had left his clothes in her hand and had run outside.
[14] Then she called the men of her house and said to them,
“Look at this!
My husband has brought a Hebrew into our house to make fun of us.
He came in to me and wanted to sleep with me.
And I screamed loudly.
[15] When he heard me scream out loud, he left his clothes beside me and ran outside.”

[16] She kept his clothes beside her until his master came home.
[17] Then she told him the same story.
She said,
“The Hebrew servant whom you have brought to us came in to me to make fun of me.
[18] When I screamed out loud, he left his clothes beside me and ran outside.”

[19] His master heard the words his wife spoke to him:
“This is what your servant did to me!”
Then he became very angry.
[20] Joseph’s master took him and put him into the prison,
the place where the king’s prisoners were kept.
Joseph stayed there in prison.

> **What does this mean?**
> The woman turns everything around. She makes the victim into the guilty one. And she presents Joseph as a foreigner: “a Hebrew,” “the Hebrew servant.” She uses prejudice against strangers.
> She also blames her husband: “whom **you** have brought to us.”
> Joseph did the right thing – and still he is punished. Life is not always fair. The Bible does not keep quiet about that.
> Back then, the punishment for such a crime was actually death. That Potiphar “only” throws Joseph into prison may show: He did not fully believe his wife.

---

## God is with Joseph in prison too (Verses 21–23)

[21] But the LORD was with Joseph.
He was kind to him.
He made the keeper of the prison like him.
[22] The keeper of the prison put all the prisoners in the prison into Joseph’s hands.
Whatever was done there, Joseph was in charge of it.
[23] The keeper of the prison did not worry about anything that was in Joseph’s hands.
For the LORD was with him.
And whatever he did, the LORD made it go well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph has hit the very bottom: from favorite son to slave, from slave to prisoner.
> But here too it is true: “The LORD was with Joseph.” God is not only with us when everything goes well. He is also there in prison, in misfortune, in injustice.
> Joseph does not become bitter. He makes the best of his situation. And once again he is given responsibility.
