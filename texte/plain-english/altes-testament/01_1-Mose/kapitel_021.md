# Genesis – Chapter 21
### Isaac is born – Hagar and Ishmael in the wilderness

---

## At last: Isaac (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD visited Sarah, as he had said.
The LORD did for Sarah what he had promised.
[2] Sarah became pregnant.
She gave birth to a son for Abraham in his old age,
at exactly the time God had told him.
[3] Abraham named the son whom Sarah had given birth to Isaac.
[4] Abraham circumcised his son Isaac when he was eight days old,
just as God had commanded him.
[5] Abraham was 100 years old when his son Isaac was born to him.

[6] Sarah said,
“God has made me laugh.
Everyone who hears about it will laugh with me.”
[7] And she said,
“Who would ever have said to Abraham that Sarah would nurse children?
And yet I have given birth to a son for him in his old age!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham has waited 25 years since God called him in Haran. Now the time has finally come. God keeps his promise – at exactly the time he said.
> Do you remember? Abraham and Sarah both laughed in disbelief (Chapters 17 and 18). Now Sarah laughs again – but this time for joy. The laughter of doubt turns into the laughter of happiness.
> That is why the child is called “Isaac,” which means: “He laughs.”

---

## Trouble at the feast (Verses 8–13)

[8] The child grew and was weaned.
That means: He no longer drank at his mother’s breast.
On the day Isaac was weaned, Abraham gave a great feast.

[9] Then Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom Hagar had given birth to for Abraham, mocking.
Mocking means: making fun of someone.
[10] She said to Abraham,
“Drive out this servant woman and her son!
The son of this servant woman will not inherit together with my son Isaac.”
[11] This was very hard for Abraham, because of his son.

[12] But God said to Abraham,
“Do not let it be so hard for you because of the boy and because of your servant woman.
Listen to everything Sarah tells you.
For your descendants will be named through Isaac.
[13] But I will also make the son of the servant woman into a nation,
because he is your child.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, children were not weaned until they were about two or three years old. A feast for it was common, because many children died young.
> Ishmael is now a teenager, about 16 years old. He is “mocking” – in Hebrew, this sounds like “playing Isaac,” a play on the name Isaac. It is not quite clear what exactly he does. Sarah feels it is a threat to her son.
> Abraham loves Ishmael. It breaks his heart.
> God comforts Abraham: I will take care of Ishmael too. He is your child too, and I will not forget him.

## Hagar and Ishmael in the wilderness (Verses 14–21)

[14] Abraham got up early the next morning.
He took bread and a skin bag of water.
He gave them to Hagar and put them on her shoulder.
He also gave her the boy and sent her away.
She left and wandered around in the wilderness of Beersheba.

[15] When the water in the skin bag was used up,
she put the boy under one of the bushes.
[16] Then she went and sat down across from him, a good distance away, about as far as an arrow flies from a bow.
For she said, “I cannot watch the child die.”
So she sat across from him.
And she cried out loud.

[17] But God heard the voice of the boy.
The angel of God called to Hagar from the sky:
“What is wrong, Hagar?
Do not be afraid!
For God has heard the voice of the boy, there where he is lying.
[18] Get up, lift up the boy and hold him firmly by the hand.
For I will make him into a great nation.”

[19] Then God opened her eyes.
And she saw a well of water.
She went, filled the skin bag with water and gave the boy a drink.

[20] God was with the boy.
He grew up and lived in the wilderness.
He became an archer.
An archer is someone who shoots with a bow and arrows.
[21] He lived in the wilderness of Paran.
His mother got a wife for him from the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a heartbreaking scene. A mother sits in the wilderness and cannot bear to watch her child die of thirst.
> But for the second time, God meets Hagar in the wilderness (the first time was in Chapter 16). Again he sees her. Again he hears.
> The name “Ishmael” means “God hears.” That is exactly what happens here: “God heard the voice of the boy.”
> Maybe the well was there the whole time. God “opened her eyes.” Sometimes help is closer than we can see in our despair.
> The distance an arrow flies from a bow is about 100 yards.

## An agreement at the well (Verses 22–34)

[22] At that time, Abimelech and Phicol, the commander of his army, came to Abraham.
Abimelech said,
“God is with you in everything you do.
[23] So swear to me here by God
that you will not deceive me –
not me, not my children and not my grandchildren.
I have been kind to you.
So you are to be kind to me and to the land where you live as a foreigner.”
[24] Abraham said,
“I will swear it.”

[25] But Abraham complained to Abimelech about a well of water.
Abimelech’s servants had taken it away by force.
[26] Abimelech said,
“I do not know who did this.
You did not tell me.
I have not heard about it until today.”

[27] Then Abraham took sheep and cattle and gave them to Abimelech.
And the two of them made a covenant.
Here a covenant is a firm agreement between two people.
[28] Abraham set apart seven female lambs from the flock.
[29] Abimelech asked Abraham,
“What do these seven female lambs mean that you have set apart?”
[30] Abraham answered,
“You are to take these seven female lambs from my hand.
They are to be proof that I dug this well.”

[31] That is why this place is called Beersheba.
For there the two of them swore an oath to each other.
[32] So they made a covenant at Beersheba.
Then Abimelech and Phicol, the commander of his army, set out
and went back to the land of the Philistines.

[33] Abraham planted a tamarisk tree in Beersheba.
There he called on the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God.
[34] Abraham lived as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines for a long time.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the dry land, a well is vital for life. Quarrels about water were common.
> Abraham and Abimelech settle the quarrel peacefully with an agreement. The seven lambs are like a receipt: Whoever accepts them recognizes that Abraham dug the well.
> “Beersheba” means “well of the oath” or “well of seven.” In Hebrew, “seven” and “swear” sound almost the same. Beersheba is still a large city in the south of Israel today.
> A tamarisk is a tree that grows in the desert and gives shade for a long time. Whoever plants a tree is planning for the future. Abraham is beginning to put down roots in the promised land.
> “The Everlasting God” – Abraham gets to know God better and better. He gives him a new name: God is the one who is always there and always stays.
