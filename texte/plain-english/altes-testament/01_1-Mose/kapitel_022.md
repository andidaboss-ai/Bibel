# Genesis – Chapter 22
### Abraham and Isaac on Mount Moriah

---

## God tests Abraham (Verses 1–2)

[1] After these things, God tested Abraham.
He said to him, “Abraham!”
Abraham answered, “Here I am.”
[2] God said,
“Take your son, your only son, Isaac, whom you love.
Go to the land of Moriah.
Offer him there as a burnt offering, on one of the mountains that I will tell you about.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the hardest and darkest stories in the Bible. Jews call it “the Binding of Isaac.”
> The reader learns right at the beginning: It is a **test**. God does not really want Isaac to be killed. But Abraham does not know that.
> God asks for the unthinkable: the son for whom Abraham waited 25 years. The son through whom all of God’s promises are to come true.
> Notice how God describes Isaac: “your son, your only son, Isaac, whom you love.” Every word makes it harder. God knows exactly how much he is asking.
> A burnt offering is an animal that is burned completely on an altar for God.
> According to Jewish tradition, Moriah is the mountain in Jerusalem where the temple was later built (2 Chronicles 3:1).

---

## The hard road (Verses 3–8)

[3] Abraham got up early in the morning.
He saddled his donkey.
He took two of his young servants with him, and his son Isaac.
He split wood for the burnt offering.
Then he set out for the place God had told him about.

[4] On the third day, Abraham looked up.
Then he saw the place far away.
[5] Abraham said to his young servants,
“Stay here with the donkey.
The boy and I will go over there.
We will worship God.
Then we will come back to you.”

[6] Abraham took the wood for the burnt offering and laid it on his son Isaac.
He himself took the fire and the knife in his hand.
So the two of them went on together.

[7] Then Isaac said to his father Abraham,
“My father?”
Abraham answered, “Here I am, my son.”
Isaac said,
“Here are the fire and the wood.
But where is the lamb for the burnt offering?”
[8] Abraham said,
“God himself will provide the lamb for the burnt offering, my son.”
So the two of them went on together.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham says nothing. He does not argue. He gets up early and sets out. For three days he is on the way – three days in which he could turn back at any time.
> He says to the servants: “Then **we** will come back to you.” Does Abraham hope that God will step in? The New Testament says: Abraham trusted that God can even bring the dead back to life (Hebrews 11:19).
> Isaac carries the wood himself. Here Christians think of Jesus, who had to carry his cross himself.
> Isaac’s question is heartbreaking: “Where is the lamb?” Abraham’s answer is a sentence full of trust: “God himself will provide the lamb.”
> Twice it says: “So the two of them went on together.” Father and son walk the hard road together.

---

## Stop! (Verses 9–14)

[9] They came to the place God had told him about.
There Abraham built the altar.
He laid the wood on it in order.
He tied up his son Isaac and laid him on the altar, on top of the wood.
[10] Then Abraham reached out his hand and took the knife to kill his son.

[11] Then the angel of the LORD called to him from the sky:
“Abraham! Abraham!”
He answered, “Here I am.”
[12] The angel said,
“Do not lay your hand on the boy!
Do not do anything to him!
For now I know that you fear God.
You have not held back your son, your only son, from me.”

[13] Abraham looked up.
Then he saw: Behind him was a ram caught by its horns in the bushes.
Abraham went, took the ram
and offered it as a burnt offering instead of his son.

[14] Abraham named that place “The LORD Will Provide.”
That is why people say to this day,
“On the LORD’s mountain, it will be provided.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the last moment, God steps in. God does **not** want human sacrifices. Many peoples back then sacrificed children to their gods. Here the Bible says very clearly: The God of Israel does not want that. Later he forbids it clearly (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 12:31).
> “To fear God” does not mean: to be afraid of God. It means: to take God so seriously and to trust him so much that he comes first – even before the dearest thing you have.
> An animal dies “instead of” Isaac. God himself has provided, just as Abraham said.
> In Hebrew, the name of the place is “Adonai Yireh.” It means “The LORD sees” and at the same time “The LORD provides.” When God sees, he also provides.
> Christians see in this story a picture of Jesus: What God spared Abraham, God did himself. He gave his own beloved Son (John 3:16; Romans 8:32). In the New Testament, Jesus is called the Lamb of God (John 1:29).
> To this day, many people find this story hard to bear. That is understandable. Jews and Christians, too, have wrestled with it a great deal over the centuries.

## God’s oath (Verses 15–19)

[15] The angel of the LORD called to Abraham a second time from the sky.
[16] He said,
“‘I have sworn by myself,’ says the LORD,
‘because you have done this
and have not held back your son, your only son,
[17] I will bless you greatly.
I will make your descendants very many,
as many as the stars in the sky
and as the sand on the seashore.
Your descendants will take over the gates of their enemies.
[18] Through your descendants, all the nations of the earth will be blessed.
For you have obeyed my voice.’”

[19] Then Abraham went back to his young servants.
They set out and went together to Beersheba.
And Abraham lived in Beersheba.

> **What does this mean?**
> God swears “by myself.” There is no one greater by whom he could swear. That is the strongest oath there is.
> God repeats all his promises – and makes them even bigger: as many as the stars in the sky **and** as the sand on the seashore.
> “To take over the gates of their enemies” means: The descendants will be strong and will stand firm against their enemies.
> Abraham goes back. And Isaac? He is not mentioned here. But we know from the next chapters: He is alive.

## News from the family (Verses 20–24)

[20] After these things, Abraham was told,
“Look, Milcah has also had children for your brother Nahor:
[21] Uz, the firstborn, and Buz, his brother,
Kemuel, the father of Aram,
[22] Chesed, Hazo, Pildash, Jidlaph and Bethuel.”
[23] Bethuel became the father of Rebekah.
Milcah gave birth to these eight sons for Nahor, Abraham’s brother.
[24] His concubine also had children.
Her name was Reumah.
She gave birth to Tebah, Gaham, Tahash and Maacah.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the dramatic story comes another list of names. It seems unimportant, but one name is very important: **Rebekah**. She will later become Isaac’s wife (Chapter 24).
> Back then, a “concubine” was a woman who lived with a man and had children by him, but had fewer rights than the main wife.
> Nahor has 12 sons – just like Ishmael and Jacob later. In the Bible, the number 12 often stands for a complete people.
