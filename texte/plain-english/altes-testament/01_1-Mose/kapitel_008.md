# Genesis – Chapter 8
### The water goes down

---

## God remembers Noah (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then God remembered Noah
and all the wild animals and all the livestock that were with him in the ship.
God made a wind blow over the earth.
Then the water went down.
[2] The springs of the deep were closed, and the windows of the sky too.
And the rain from the sky stopped.
[3] Little by little, the water went back from the earth.
After 150 days, the water had gone down.
[4] In the seventh month, on the 17th day of the month, the ship came to rest
on the mountains of Ararat.
[5] The water kept going down until the tenth month.
On the first day of the tenth month, the tops of the mountains could be seen.

> **What does this mean?**
> “God remembered Noah.” This sentence is the turning point of the whole story. God has not forgotten Noah – and not the animals either.
> A wind blows over the water. That is a reminder of Chapter 1, where God’s Spirit was hovering over the waters. In Hebrew, “wind” and “spirit” are the same word. Now a new creation begins.
> Ararat is a mountain range in the east of what is now Turkey, near Armenia.

---

## The raven and the dove (Verses 6–12)

[6] After 40 days, Noah opened the window of the ship that he had made.
[7] He sent out a raven.
The raven flew back and forth until the water on the earth had dried up.

[8] Then Noah sent out a dove.
He wanted to see if the water on the ground had gone down.
[9] But the dove found no place where her foot could rest.
For there was still water on the whole earth.
So she came back to him into the ship.
Noah reached out his hand, took her, and brought her back to himself into the ship.

[10] Noah waited another seven days.
Then he sent the dove out of the ship again.
[11] In the evening, the dove came back to him.
And look: In her beak was a freshly picked olive leaf.
Then Noah knew: The water on the earth has gone down.

[12] Noah waited another seven days.
Then he sent the dove out again.
This time she did not come back to him anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> Noah has to be very patient. He waits and checks again and again whether the time has come.
> It is described with love: Noah reaches out his hand and brings the tired dove inside.
> To this day, the dove with the olive branch is a sign of **peace** and **hope** all over the world. It comes from this story.
> The fresh green leaf shows: Things are growing again. Life is coming back.

---

## Out of the ark (Verses 13–19)

[13] In the 601st year of Noah’s life, in the first month, on the first day of the month, the water on the earth had dried up.
Noah took the covering off the ship and looked out.
He saw that the ground was dry.
[14] In the second month, on the 27th day of the month, the earth was completely dry.

[15] Then God spoke to Noah.
He said,
[16] “Go out of the ship, you and your wife, your sons and your sons’ wives with you.
[17] Bring out with you every animal that is with you, of all living beings:
the birds, the livestock and every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.
Let them spread out on the earth.
They are to be fruitful and become many on the earth.”
Fruitful means: having many young.

[18] So Noah went out, with his sons, his wife and his sons’ wives.
[19] Every animal also went out of the ship,
every creeping thing and every bird, everything that moves on the earth,
family by family.

> **What does this mean?**
> Noah was in the ark for more than a year. On New Year’s Day, he sees dry land again. A new year, a new beginning for the world.
> Noah does not simply go out when the ground is dry. He waits until God says: “Go out.” He trusts God while waiting just as much as while acting.
> God repeats the blessing from Chapter 1: The animals are to be fruitful and become many. It is like a second creation.

---

## Noah’s thanks and God’s promise (Verses 20–22)

[20] Noah built an altar to the LORD.
An altar is a table of stones or earth on which people bring offerings to God.
He took some of every clean animal and of every clean bird.
And he offered them on the altar as burnt offerings.
That means: He burned them completely for God.

[21] The LORD smelled the pleasant smell.
And the LORD said in his heart,
“I will never again curse the ground because of people.
For the human heart imagines evil from youth on.
And I will never again strike down every living thing, as I have done.
[22] As long as the earth remains,
these will not stop:
seedtime and harvest,
cold and heat,
summer and winter,
day and night.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The first thing Noah does: He says thank you. Before he builds a house, he builds an altar.
> Amazing: People have not become better. God himself says: Their hearts imagine evil. And still he promises: I will never again destroy everything.
> So God does not keep the world going because we are so good. He does it because **he** is so good. This is called grace.
> Every sunrise, every winter and every summer, every harvest is a reminder of this promise.
