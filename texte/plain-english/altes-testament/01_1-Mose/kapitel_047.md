# Genesis – Chapter 47
### Jacob before Pharaoh – Joseph during the famine

---

## The brothers before Pharaoh (Verses 1–6)

[1] Joseph went in to Pharaoh and told him,
“My father and my brothers have come from the land of Canaan,
with their flocks, their herds and everything they own.
Look, they are now in the land of Goshen.”
[2] He had taken five of his brothers with him.
He presented them to Pharaoh.

[3] Pharaoh asked his brothers,
“What is your work?”
They said to Pharaoh,
“Your servants are shepherds, both we and our fathers.”
[4] They also said to Pharaoh,
“We have come to live as foreigners in the land.
For there is no pasture for your servants’ flocks.
The famine is severe in the land of Canaan.
So please let your servants live in the land of Goshen.”

[5] Pharaoh said to Joseph,
“Your father and your brothers have come to you.
[6] The land of Egypt is open to you.
Let your father and your brothers live in the best part of the land.
Let them live in the land of Goshen.
And if you know any capable men among them,
put them in charge of my own livestock.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything goes the way Joseph planned it. The brothers are honest and modest.
> Pharaoh is generous. He even gives Joseph’s family work with his own herds.

---

## Jacob blesses Pharaoh (Verses 7–12)

[7] Then Joseph brought in his father Jacob and presented him to Pharaoh.
And Jacob blessed Pharaoh.
[8] Pharaoh asked Jacob,
“How old are you?”
[9] Jacob said to Pharaoh,
“The years of my journey through life are 130 years.
The years of my life have been few and hard.
They have not reached the years of my fathers’ lives during their journeys.”
[10] Jacob blessed Pharaoh and went out from his presence.

[11] Joseph gave his father and his brothers a place to live.
He gave them land of their own in Egypt, in the best part of the land, in the land of Rameses.
That is what Pharaoh had ordered.
[12] Joseph supplied his father, his brothers and his father’s whole household with bread,
as much as each family needed, according to the number of their children.

> **What does this mean?**
> An amazing scene: an old shepherd blesses the most powerful king in the world. Normally the greater person blesses the lesser one. But Jacob carries God’s blessing. And so through Abraham’s family, Egypt is blessed too.
> Jacob calls his life a “journey.” He was never really at home. And he is honest: they were hard years. He went through much suffering – and much of it was his own fault.
> Rameses is an area in the Nile Delta. The name comes from a later Pharaoh. The storyteller uses the name his readers knew.

---

## The famine gets worse and worse (Verses 13–17)

[13] There was no bread in the whole land,
for the famine was very severe.
The land of Egypt and the land of Canaan were worn out by the famine.
[14] Joseph collected all the money there was in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan
in payment for the grain that people bought.
And Joseph brought the money into Pharaoh’s palace.

[15] When the money was all used up in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan,
all the Egyptians came to Joseph and said,
“Give us bread!
Why should we die in front of you?
Our money is gone.”
[16] Joseph said,
“Give me your livestock.
If your money is gone, I will give you food in exchange for your livestock.”
[17] So they brought their livestock to Joseph.
And Joseph gave them bread in exchange for their horses, their flocks, their herds and their donkeys.
That year he fed them with bread in exchange for all their livestock.

---

## The Egyptians sell themselves and their land (Verses 18–26)

[18] When that year was over, they came to him in the second year and said to him,
“We will not hide it from my lord:
our money is all gone.
And our herds of livestock already belong to my lord.
Nothing is left for my lord except our bodies and our land.
[19] Why should we die before your eyes, both we and our land?
Buy us and our land in exchange for bread.
Then we and our land will be servants to Pharaoh.
Give us seed, so that we may live and not die,
and so that the land will not become a wasteland.”

[20] So Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh.
For every Egyptian sold his field, because the famine was so severe for them.
So the land became Pharaoh’s.
[21] As for the people, he moved them into the cities,
from one end of Egypt to the other.
[22] Only the land of the priests he did not buy.
For the priests received a fixed share from Pharaoh.
They lived on the share that Pharaoh gave them.
That is why they did not sell their land.

[23] Joseph said to the people,
“Look, today I have bought you and your land for Pharaoh.
Here is seed for you. Plant it in the land.
[24] At harvest time, you are to give one fifth to Pharaoh.
Four fifths will be your own:
as seed for the field and as food for you, your households and your little children.”
[25] They said,
“You have saved our lives!
May we find favor with my lord.
We will be Pharaoh’s servants.”

[26] So Joseph made it a law for the land of Egypt, which still stands to this day:
one fifth belongs to Pharaoh.
Only the land of the priests did not become Pharaoh’s.

> **What does this mean?**
> This section is not easy for readers today. Joseph does save the Egyptians from starving to death. But in the end, all the money, all the livestock and all the land belong to Pharaoh. The people have become dependent on him.
> The Egyptians themselves feel that they have been rescued: “You have saved our lives!” A tax of one fifth was rather mild back then. In other countries, rulers often demanded much more.
> Some interpreters see Joseph here as a wise manager in a time of need. Others are critical that he made Pharaoh’s power so much greater. Later, a Pharaoh will use exactly this power to make Joseph’s descendants slaves (Exodus 1).
> The Bible tells it without openly praising or blaming it.

## Jacob wants to be buried in Canaan (Verses 27–31)

[27] So Israel lived in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen.
They got property there.
They had many children and became very many.

[28] Jacob lived in the land of Egypt for 17 years.
So Jacob lived to be 147 years old in all.

[29] When the time came near for Israel to die, he called his son Joseph.
He said to him,
“If I have found favor with you, please put your hand under my thigh.
Show me kindness and faithfulness:
please do not bury me in Egypt.
[30] When I lie down to rest with my fathers,
carry me out of Egypt
and bury me in their grave.”
Joseph said,
“I will do as you have said.”

[31] Israel said,
“Swear it to me!”
And Joseph swore it to him.
Then Israel bowed down in worship at the head of his bed.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Egypt, God’s promise comes true: the family becomes large and numerous.
> Jacob had Joseph with him for 17 years at the beginning of Joseph’s life. Now Jacob has 17 years with Joseph in Egypt. In this way, he gets the lost years back.
> “To put your hand under the thigh” is a solemn oath, as with Abraham and his servant (Chapter 24).
> Jacob does not want to stay in Egypt, not even after his death. His heart belongs to the land God promised. He wants to be buried with Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah and Leah in the cave of Machpelah.
> At the end, Jacob bows over his bed and worships God. He thanks God.
