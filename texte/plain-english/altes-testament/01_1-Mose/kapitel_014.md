# Genesis – Chapter 14
### Abram rescues Lot – Melchizedek blesses Abram

---

## Four kings against five (Verses 1–12)

[1] It was in the days of Amraphel, king of Shinar,
Arioch, king of Ellasar,
Chedorlaomer, king of Elam,
and Tidal, king of Goiim.
[2] These kings made war against
Bera, king of Sodom,
Birsha, king of Gomorrah,
Shinab, king of Admah,
Shemeber, king of Zeboiim,
and against the king of Bela – that is Zoar.
[3] These five joined together in the valley of Siddim.
That is the Salt Sea.

[4] For twelve years they had served Chedorlaomer.
In the thirteenth year they rebelled against him.
[5] In the fourteenth year, Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him came.
They defeated
the Rephaim in Ashteroth Karnaim,
the Zuzim in Ham,
the Emim in Shaveh Kiriathaim,
[6] and the Horites in their hill country of Seir, as far as El Paran, which is at the edge of the wilderness.
[7] Then they turned back and came to En Mishpat – that is Kadesh.
They attacked the whole country of the Amalekites.
And they also defeated the Amorites who lived in Hazazon Tamar.

[8] Then the king of Sodom, the king of Gomorrah, the king of Admah, the king of Zeboiim and the king of Bela – that is Zoar – went out to fight.
They lined up for battle in the valley of Siddim
[9] against Chedorlaomer, king of Elam,
Tidal, king of Goiim,
Amraphel, king of Shinar,
and Arioch, king of Ellasar.
Four kings against the five.

[10] The valley of Siddim was full of tar pits.
The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah ran away, and some fell into them.
The others ran away to the hills.
[11] The winners took all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah and all their food.
Then they went away.
[12] They also took Lot, the son of Abram’s brother, and his goods.
For Lot lived in Sodom.
Then they left.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the first war story in the Bible. Powerful kings from the east – from the region of what is now Iraq and Iran – ruled over small city kings near the Jordan. These had to pay them taxes. When they fight back, war breaks out.
> The peoples in verses 5 and 6 are very ancient peoples about whom we know little today. Some of them were thought to be especially tall.
> The Salt Sea is the Dead Sea. It is so salty that no fish can live in it. To this day, there is tar (asphalt) in the ground in that area.
> Lot wanted the most beautiful land. Now he ends up in the middle of a war and is carried off as a prisoner.

---

## Abram steps in (Verses 13–16)

[13] Someone who had escaped came and told Abram the Hebrew.
At that time, Abram lived by the oaks of Mamre the Amorite.
Mamre was the brother of Eshcol and of Aner.
They were Abram’s allies.
Allies are people who have promised to help each other.

[14] When Abram heard that his relative had been taken prisoner,
he called out his trained men, who had been born in his household.
There were 318 men.
He chased the enemies as far as Dan.
[15] At night he divided his men against them, he and his servants, and attacked them.
He defeated them and chased them as far as Hobah, which is on the left of Damascus.
[16] He brought back all the goods.
He also brought back his relative Lot and his goods,
and the women and the other people too.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Abram is called “the Hebrew” for the first time. That is the old name for the people that later becomes Israel.
> Lot had separated from Abram and taken the better land for himself. Still, Abram does not leave him on his own. He risks his life for him.
> “On the left of Damascus”: When people back then gave directions, they faced east. Then left is north. So Hobah is north of Damascus.
> With only 318 men, Abram defeats the armies of four kings. This shows: God is with him.

---

## Melchizedek (Verses 17–20)

[17] Abram came back after he had defeated Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him.
Then the king of Sodom came out to meet him
in the valley of Shaveh. That is the King’s Valley.

[18] And Melchizedek, king of Salem, brought out bread and wine.
He was a priest of God Most High.
[19] He blessed Abram and said,
“Blessed be Abram by God Most High,
owner of heaven and earth.
[20] And blessed be God Most High,
who has put your enemies into your hand.”
And Abram gave him a tenth of everything.

> **What does this mean?**
> Melchizedek is a mysterious figure. He suddenly appears and disappears again. His name means “king of righteousness.” “Salem” means “peace.” Salem is probably the city that is later called Jerusalem.
> A priest is a person who stands between God and people: He prays for others and brings offerings to God. Melchizedek does not belong to Abram’s family – and still he knows the one true God.
> “A tenth” is one part out of ten. Abram gives a tenth of what he won in battle as thanks to God. Later, this became the custom of giving part of what you own to God.
> Bread and wine: Christians think of the Lord’s Supper. In the New Testament (Hebrews 7), Jesus is compared with Melchizedek.

---

## Abram wants nothing from Sodom (Verses 21–24)

[21] The king of Sodom said to Abram,
“Give me the people.
Keep the goods for yourself.”

[22] But Abram said to the king of Sodom,
“I have lifted up my hand to the LORD, God Most High, owner of heaven and earth,
and I have sworn:
[23] I will not take a thread or a sandal strap,
or anything that is yours.
Then you cannot say, ‘I have made Abram rich.’
[24] I will take nothing for myself,
except what the young men have eaten.
And the share of the men who went with me: Aner, Eshcol and Mamre.
Let them take their share.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Two kings come to Abram. Melchizedek brings a blessing. The king of Sodom offers riches.
> Abram accepts the blessing from God. But he takes nothing from the king of the wicked city of Sodom – not even a thread.
> “To lift up your hand to God” means: to make a solemn promise, an oath.
> Abram does not want anyone to be able to say: Abram became rich through Sodom. He knows: What I have comes from God.
> But he is fair to his allies. They are to get what belongs to them.
