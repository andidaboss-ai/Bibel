# Genesis – Chapter 29
### Jacob, Rachel and Leah

---

## At the well (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jacob went on with his journey.
He came to the land of the people of the east.
[2] There he saw a well in the field.
Three flocks of sheep were lying there beside it.
For people watered the flocks from this well.
The stone on the opening of the well was large.
[3] All the flocks were gathered there.
Then the shepherds rolled the stone away from the opening of the well.
They watered the sheep.
After that, they put the stone back in its place on the opening of the well.

[4] Jacob said to the shepherds,
“My relatives, where are you from?”
They said,
“We are from Haran.”
[5] He asked them,
“Do you know Laban, the son of Nahor?”
They said,
“We know him.”
[6] He asked them,
“Is he doing well?”
They said,
“He is doing well.
Look, his daughter Rachel is coming with the sheep.”

[7] Jacob said,
“Look, it is still the middle of the day.
It is not yet time to gather the livestock together.
Water the sheep, and go and let them eat.”
[8] They said,
“We can’t do that.
First all the flocks must be gathered together.
Then the stone is rolled away from the opening of the well.
Then we water the sheep.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Once again there is a meeting at a well – like with Abraham’s servant and Rebekah (Chapter 24). Back then, wells were meeting places. That is where people got to know each other.
> The stone on the well protected the water. It was so heavy that people could only move it together. So no one could secretly steal water.
> “Laban, the son of Nahor” – here “son” means: descendant. To be exact, Laban was Nahor’s grandson (see Genesis 24:24 and 24:29).

---

## Jacob meets Rachel (Verses 9–14)

[9] Jacob was still talking with them.
Then Rachel came with her father’s sheep.
For she took care of them.
[10] Jacob saw Rachel, the daughter of Laban, his mother’s brother.
And he saw the sheep of Laban, his mother’s brother.
Then Jacob went near.
He rolled the stone away from the opening of the well
and watered the flock of Laban, his mother’s brother.
[11] Jacob kissed Rachel.
And he began to cry out loud.

[12] Jacob told Rachel that he was her father’s relative.
He told her that he was Rebekah’s son.
She ran and told her father.
[13] Laban heard the news about Jacob, his sister’s son.
Then he ran to meet Jacob.
He hugged him, kissed him and brought him to his house.
Jacob told Laban all these things.
[14] Laban said to him,
“Truly, you are my bone and my flesh.”
Jacob stayed with him for a month.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob rolls the heavy stone away all by himself. Love gives him strength.
> He cries for joy and relief. After his long, lonely flight, he has finally found his family.
> Back then, a kiss was a normal greeting between relatives.
> “My bone and my flesh” means: You belong to the family.

---

## Seven years for Rachel (Verses 15–20)

[15] Then Laban said to Jacob,
“Should you work for me for nothing, just because you are my relative?
Tell me what your pay should be.”

[16] Laban had two daughters.
The older one was named Leah.
The younger one was named Rachel.
[17] Leah’s eyes were weak.
But Rachel had a beautiful figure and was lovely to look at.
[18] Jacob loved Rachel.
He said,
“I will work for you seven years for Rachel, your younger daughter.”
[19] Laban said,
“It is better that I give her to you than to another man.
Stay with me.”

[20] So Jacob worked seven years for Rachel.
They seemed to him like only a few days, because he loved her so much.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, a bridegroom had to pay a bride price to the bride’s family. Jacob has no money. He has run away. That is why he offers his work: seven years.
> It is not clear what Leah’s “weak” eyes mean exactly. Maybe she had soft, gentle eyes. Maybe her eyes were weak. In any case, she is less strikingly beautiful than her sister.
> Verse 20 is one of the most beautiful love sentences in the Bible: Seven years seem to Jacob like a few days, because he loves Rachel so much.

---

## The cheater is cheated (Verses 21–30)

[21] Then Jacob said to Laban,
“Give me my wife.
My time of work is over.
I want to sleep with her.”

[22] Laban gathered together all the men of the place and made a feast.
[23] But in the evening, he took his daughter Leah and brought her to Jacob.
And Jacob slept with her.
[24] Laban gave his servant Zilpah to his daughter Leah to be her servant.

[25] In the morning – look! It was Leah!
Jacob said to Laban,
“What have you done to me?
Didn’t I work for you for Rachel?
Why did you trick me?”

[26] Laban said,
“In our place, it is not done to give the younger daughter before the firstborn.
[27] Finish the wedding week of this one.
Then we will give you the other one too.
For her, you will work for me seven more years.”

[28] Jacob did so.
He finished Leah’s wedding week.
Then Laban gave him his daughter Rachel as his wife.
[29] Laban gave his servant Bilhah to his daughter Rachel to be her servant.
[30] Jacob slept with Rachel too.
And he loved Rachel more than Leah.
And he worked for Laban seven more years.

> **What does this mean?**
> How could Jacob not notice? Back then, the bride wore a veil, it was dark, and there was a lot of wine at the feast.
> Now Jacob finds out for himself what it is like to be tricked. He fooled his blind father by pretending to be the older son. Now he is fooled because the older daughter pretends to be the younger one.
> Laban’s answer hits the mark: “In our place, it is not done to give the younger daughter before the firstborn.” That must have hit Jacob hard – he had done exactly that: as the younger one, he pushed himself ahead of the firstborn.
> The Bible does not say this directly. But it shows: What you do to others often comes back to you.
> Back then, a wedding lasted a whole week.
> Later, the law forbids a man to marry two sisters at the same time (Leviticus 18:18). In this chapter, you can see why: It brings a lot of suffering.

## Leah has sons (Verses 31–35)

[31] The LORD saw that Leah was hated.
That means: She was loved less.
So he let her have children.
But Rachel could not have children.

[32] Leah became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
She named him Reuben.
For she said,
“The LORD has looked at my misery.
Now my husband will love me.”

[33] She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son.
She said,
“The LORD has heard that I am hated.
That is why he has given me this son too.”
She named him Simeon.

[34] She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son.
She said,
“Now this time my husband will be joined to me,
because I have given birth to three sons for him.”
That is why he was named Levi.

[35] She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son.
She said,
“This time I will praise the LORD.”
That is why she named him Judah.
Then she stopped having children.

> **What does this mean?**
> Leah suffers very much. She is not loved. The text even says: She was “hated.” It means: pushed aside, loved less.
> But God sees her. Just as he saw Hagar. God is on the side of those who are overlooked.
> The names of the children tell Leah’s feelings:
> Reuben sounds like “Look, a son!” and “He has seen my misery.”
> Simeon sounds like “He has heard.”
> Levi sounds like “joined, connected.”
> Judah sounds like “praise.”
> With the first three sons, Leah still hopes that Jacob will finally love her. With the fourth, she turns to God: “This time I will praise the LORD.” She no longer finds her worth only in Jacob’s love, but with God.
> Later the priests of Israel will come from Levi. From Judah come King David and – according to Christian faith – Jesus. Leah, who was not loved, becomes the mother of the most important tribes.
