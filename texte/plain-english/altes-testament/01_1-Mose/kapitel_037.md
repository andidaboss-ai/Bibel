# Genesis – Chapter 37
### Joseph is sold by his brothers

---

## The favorite son (Verses 1–4)

[1] Jacob lived in the land of Canaan, where his father had lived as a foreigner.
[2] This is the family history of Jacob.
Joseph was 17 years old.
He was feeding the flock with his brothers.
He was a young helper with the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah, his father’s wives.
Joseph brought a bad report about them to their father.

[3] Israel loved Joseph more than all his other children.
For Joseph was the son of his old age.
He made him a robe of many colors.
[4] His brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers.
So they hated him.
They could not speak a friendly word to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the long story of Joseph begins. It goes on until the end of the book of Genesis.
> Jacob makes the same mistake as his parents: He has a favorite son. And he shows it openly to everyone. Joseph is the first son of his beloved Rachel.
> The colorful robe was a precious piece of clothing. It showed: This son is something special. Maybe he was meant to be the heir one day. Shepherds who do dirty work did not wear such things.
> Joseph tells on his brothers to his father. That does not make him more popular with them either.

---

## Joseph’s dreams (Verses 5–11)

[5] Joseph had a dream.
He told it to his brothers.
Then they hated him even more.
[6] He said to them,
“Please listen to this dream that I had:
[7] Look, we were tying up sheaves in the field.
And look: My sheaf got up and stood upright.
And look: Your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf.”
[8] His brothers said to him,
“Do you really want to be king over us?
Do you really want to rule over us?”
And they hated him even more because of his dreams and because of his words.

[9] Then he had yet another dream.
He told this one to his brothers too.
He said,
“Look, I have had another dream:
Look, the sun and the moon and eleven stars bowed down to me.”
[10] He told it to his father and to his brothers.
His father scolded him.
He said to him,
“What is this dream that you have had?
Will I and your mother and your brothers really come
and bow down to the ground before you?”
[11] His brothers were jealous of him.
But his father kept these words in mind.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sheaves are bundles of grain stalks that are tied together at harvest time.
> Joseph’s dreams say: One day the whole family will bow down before him. That will really happen – but very differently from what everyone thinks.
> Joseph is young and tells his dreams without thinking of others. He does not notice how much he hurts his brothers with them.
> The sun and the moon stand for father and mother, the eleven stars for the eleven brothers. Actually, Rachel has already died. Maybe Leah is meant here, or the dream simply speaks of “the parents.”
> Jacob scolds him, but he does not forget the dreams. He suspects: Maybe God is behind them.

---

## Joseph looks for his brothers (Verses 12–17)

[12] His brothers had gone to Shechem to feed their father’s flock.
[13] Israel said to Joseph,
“Your brothers are feeding the flock at Shechem, aren’t they?
Come, I will send you to them.”
Joseph said to him,
“Here I am.”
[14] Israel said to him,
“Go now and see whether your brothers are doing well, and the flock too.
Then come back and tell me.”
So he sent him away from the valley of Hebron.
And Joseph came to Shechem.

[15] A man found him.
And look: Joseph was wandering around in the field.
The man asked him,
“What are you looking for?”
[16] Joseph said,
“I am looking for my brothers.
Please tell me where they are feeding the flock.”
[17] The man said,
“They have left here.
I heard them say, ‘Let’s go to Dothan.’”
So Joseph went after his brothers.
And he found them in Dothan.

> **What does this mean?**
> Shechem lies about 50 miles north of Hebron. Dothan lies about 12 miles farther. That is a long journey for a young man alone.
> Of all places, Shechem – where the brothers had attacked the city (Chapter 34). Maybe that is why Jacob is worried.
> The unknown man in the field seems to be a coincidence. But without him, Joseph would not have found his brothers. God’s way often goes through such small “coincidences.”

---

## The brothers’ plan (Verses 18–24)

[18] The brothers saw him from far away.
Even before he came near them, they made a plan to kill him.
[19] They said to one another,
“Look, here comes this dreamer!
[20] Come on, let’s kill him and throw him into one of the pits.
Then we will say, ‘A wild animal has eaten him.’
Then we will see what becomes of his dreams!”

[21] Reuben heard it.
He saved him from their hands.
He said,
“Let’s not kill him.”
[22] Reuben said to them,
“Do not shed any blood!
Throw him into this pit here in the wilderness.
But do not lay a hand on him.”
For he wanted to save him from their hands and bring him back to his father.

[23] When Joseph came to his brothers, they tore his robe off him,
the robe of many colors that he was wearing.
[24] They took him and threw him into the pit.
The pit was empty. There was no water in it.

> **What does this mean?**
> A pit like this is a deep hole in the rock where people collected rainwater. When it is empty, you cannot get out by yourself.
> Envy has turned into hatred, and hatred into a plan to murder. Just like with Cain and Abel.
> Reuben is the oldest brother. He feels responsible and wants to save Joseph. But he does not dare to stand up openly against the others.
> The first thing the brothers do: They tear off his colorful robe – the sign that their father prefers him.

---

## Joseph is sold (Verses 25–28)

[25] Then they sat down to eat a meal.
They looked up and saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead.
A caravan is a group of traders traveling together.
Their camels were carrying spices, balm and myrrh.
They were on their way down to Egypt to take it there.

[26] Judah said to his brothers,
“What do we gain if we kill our brother and hide his blood?
[27] Come, let’s sell him to the Ishmaelites.
Then we do not have to lay our hands on him.
For he is our brother, our own flesh.”
His brothers listened to him.

[28] Midianite traders passed by.
They pulled Joseph up out of the pit.
They sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for 20 pieces of silver.
And the traders brought Joseph to Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers calmly sit down to eat, while their brother cries for help in the pit (see Genesis 42:21). That shows how hard their hearts have become.
> Balm and myrrh are valuable, sweet-smelling resins from plants. People used them as medicine and for perfume.
> Judah does not want to kill Joseph – but only because you can make money with a living slave. That is hardly better.
> Here both “Ishmaelites” and “Midianites” are named. Maybe it was a mixed caravan. Maybe “Ishmaelites” was a general word for traveling traders. Scholars also explain it this way: Two old stories have been joined together here.
> Back then, 20 pieces of silver was the usual price for a young slave.
> Christians think here of Jesus, who was betrayed by Judas for 30 pieces of silver.

---

## The father is deceived (Verses 29–36)

[29] Reuben came back to the pit.
He saw that Joseph was not in the pit.
Then he tore his clothes.
[30] He went back to his brothers and said,
“The boy is gone!
And I – where can I go now?”

[31] Then they took Joseph’s robe.
They killed a male goat and dipped the robe in the blood.
[32] They took the robe of many colors and brought it to their father.
They said,
“We found this.
Please look at it and see whether it is your son’s robe or not.”

[33] Jacob recognized it and said,
“It is my son’s robe!
A wild animal has eaten him.
Joseph has surely been torn to pieces!”
[34] Jacob tore his clothes.
He put sackcloth around his waist.
And he mourned for his son for many days.
[35] All his sons and all his daughters came to comfort him.
But he refused to be comforted.
He said,
“No, I will go down to my son in Sheol (the world of the dead), still mourning.”
So his father cried for him.

[36] But the Midianites sold Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar.
Potiphar was an officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, tearing your clothes was a sign of great pain and sorrow. People wore “sackcloth” in times of mourning: rough, scratchy clothing made of goat hair.
> Jacob once tricked his father with a goatskin and Esau’s clothes (Chapter 27). Now he himself is tricked with a male goat and his son’s robe. And again the sons say, “Please look at it and see …” – almost the same words.
> The brothers do not lie directly. They let their father come to the wrong conclusion himself. That is especially cruel.
> People imagined “Sheol,” the world of the dead, as a dark place under the earth where all the dead go.
> The last sentence is like a ray of light: Joseph is alive. He is in Egypt. The story is not over yet.
