# Genesis – Chapter 46
### Jacob moves to Egypt with his whole family

---

## God encourages Jacob (Verses 1–4)

[1] Israel set out with everything he had.
He came to Beersheba.
There he offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac.
[2] At night God spoke to Israel in visions.
He said, “Jacob, Jacob!”
He said, “Here I am.”
[3] God said,
“I am God, the God of your father.
Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt.
For there I will make you into a great nation.
[4] I will go down with you to Egypt.
And I will surely bring you up again.
Joseph’s hand will close your eyes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Beersheba is the last place in the south of the land of Canaan. Jacob stops here before he leaves the promised land. Abraham and Isaac prayed here.
> Jacob is probably afraid: may he leave the land that God promised? Back then, Isaac was not allowed to go to Egypt (Genesis 26:2).
> God calms him: this time it is right. And: I am going with you. God is not tied to one land.
> “I will surely bring you up again” – this means Jacob’s descendants. The book of Exodus tells about that.
> “Joseph’s hand will close your eyes” means: Joseph will be with you when you die. That is a great comfort for the old father.

---

## Setting out (Verses 5–7)

[5] Then Jacob set out from Beersheba.
The sons of Israel put their father Jacob, their little children and their wives
on the wagons that Pharaoh had sent to carry him.
[6] They took their livestock and the goods they had gained in the land of Canaan.
So they came to Egypt,
Jacob and all his descendants with him:
[7] his sons and his grandsons,
his daughters and his granddaughters.
He brought all his descendants with him to Egypt.

---

## Jacob’s family: the descendants of Leah (Verses 8–15)

[8] These are the names of the people of Israel who came to Egypt, Jacob and his sons:
Reuben, Jacob’s firstborn.
[9] The sons of Reuben:
Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron and Carmi.
[10] The sons of Simeon:
Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar,
and Shaul, the son of a Canaanite woman.
[11] The sons of Levi:
Gershon, Kohath and Merari.
[12] The sons of Judah:
Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez and Zerah.
But Er and Onan had died in the land of Canaan.
The sons of Perez were Hezron and Hamul.
[13] The sons of Issachar:
Tola, Puvah, Iob and Shimron.
[14] The sons of Zebulun:
Sered, Elon and Jahleel.
[15] These are the sons of Leah, whom she had given birth to for Jacob in Paddan Aram,
together with his daughter Dinah.
All together, his sons and daughters were 33 people.

---

## The descendants of Zilpah (Verses 16–18)

[16] The sons of Gad:
Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni, Ezbon, Eri, Arodi and Areli.
[17] The sons of Asher:
Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi and Beriah,
and their sister Serah.
The sons of Beriah were Heber and Malchiel.
[18] These are the descendants of Zilpah.
Laban had given her to his daughter Leah as a servant.
She gave birth to these for Jacob, 16 people.

---

## The descendants of Rachel (Verses 19–22)

[19] The sons of Rachel, Jacob’s wife:
Joseph and Benjamin.
[20] In the land of Egypt, Manasseh and Ephraim were born to Joseph.
Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, the priest of On, gave birth to them for him.
[21] The sons of Benjamin:
Bela, Becher, Ashbel, Gera, Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim and Ard.
[22] These are the descendants of Rachel who were born to Jacob.
All together they were 14 people.

---

## The descendants of Bilhah (Verses 23–25)

[23] The son of Dan:
Hushim.
[24] The sons of Naphtali:
Jahzeel, Guni, Jezer and Shillem.
[25] These are the descendants of Bilhah.
Laban had given her to his daughter Rachel as a servant.
She gave birth to these for Jacob, all together 7 people.

---

## Seventy people (Verses 26–27)

[26] All the people who came to Egypt with Jacob and were his own descendants
were 66 – not counting the wives of Jacob’s sons.
[27] Then there were the two sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt.
So all the people of Jacob’s family who came to Egypt were 70.

> **What does this mean?**
> This long list is important: it names the families that later become the twelve tribes of Israel.
> The numbers are counted in a somewhat complicated way. For example, Jacob himself is counted among the 33 people of Leah, but Er and Onan are not. Joseph and his sons are counted among the 70, even though they were already in Egypt.
> The number 70 is important. It reminds us of the 70 nations in Chapter 10. Jacob’s family is like a small world of its own. In Egypt, these 70 people will grow into a great nation.
> Women are named too: Dinah, and Serah, the daughter of Asher.

---

## Meeting again (Verses 28–30)

[28] Jacob sent Judah ahead of him to Joseph,
to show him the way to Goshen.
So they came into the land of Goshen.
[29] Joseph got his chariot ready.
He went up to Goshen to meet his father Israel.
When he came to him, he hugged him around the neck.
And he wept on his neck for a long time.

[30] Israel said to Joseph,
“Now I can die,
since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah goes ahead. He is now the leader among the brothers.
> After 22 years, father and son see each other again. The Bible describes it very simply. But everyone can feel how much emotion there is in this moment.
> Jacob has nothing left to wish for. His greatest pain is healed.

---

## Joseph prepares his brothers to meet Pharaoh (Verses 31–34)

[31] Joseph said to his brothers and to his father’s household,
“I will go up and tell Pharaoh.
I will say to him,
‘My brothers and my father’s household, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me.
[32] These men are shepherds.
They have always kept livestock.
They have brought their flocks, their herds and everything they have.’
[33] When Pharaoh calls you and asks,
‘What is your work?’
[34] then you are to say,
‘Your servants have kept livestock from our youth until now, both we and our fathers.’
Then you will be allowed to live in the land of Goshen.
For the Egyptians find all shepherds disgusting.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph is wise. He knows: the Egyptians want nothing to do with shepherds. That is exactly why they will leave his family alone and give it its own area.
> In this way, Israel stays a people of its own and does not mix with the Egyptians. It can keep its faith and its way of life.
