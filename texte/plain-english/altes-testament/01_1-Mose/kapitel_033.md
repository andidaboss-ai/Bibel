# Genesis – Chapter 33
### Jacob and Esau make peace

---

## Meeting again (Verses 1–7)

[1] Jacob looked up.
And look: Esau was coming, and 400 men were with him.
Jacob divided the children among Leah, Rachel and the two female servants.
[2] He put the servants and their children in front.
Behind them he put Leah and her children.
And at the very back he put Rachel and Joseph.
[3] He himself went ahead of them.
He bowed down to the ground seven times, until he came near to his brother.

[4] Esau ran to meet him.
He hugged him, threw his arms around his neck and kissed him.
And they both cried.

[5] Esau looked up and saw the women and the children.
He asked,
“Who are these with you?”
Jacob said,
“They are the children whom God has kindly given to your servant.”
[6] Then the servants came near with their children, and they bowed down.
[7] Leah also came near with her children, and they bowed down.
After them, Joseph and Rachel came near, and they bowed down.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is still afraid. He puts the people he loves most – Rachel and Joseph – at the very back, where they are safest. That shows how unequally he loves his family. The others surely feel that.
> But then Jacob himself goes to the front. He no longer hides behind gifts and messengers. He faces his brother.
> Bowing seven times was how people bowed before a king back then. Jacob shows: I bow to you.
> And then the miracle: Esau runs, hugs, kisses – and both of them cry. No anger, no revenge. 20 years of hatred are gone. God has answered Jacob’s prayer.
> Later Jesus tells the parable of the lost son. There it says, in almost the same words, that the father ran to his son, threw his arms around his neck and kissed him (Luke 15:20).

---

## The gift (Verses 8–11)

[8] Esau asked,
“What do you mean by all these herds that I met?”
Jacob said,
“I wanted my lord to be kind to me.”
[9] Esau said,
“I have enough, my brother.
Keep what you have for yourself.”

[10] Jacob said,
“No, please!
If you are kind to me, then accept my gift from me.
For I have seen your face, as one sees the face of God.
And you have welcomed me kindly.
[11] Please take the gift that I have brought to you.
For God has been kind to me, and I have enough.”
Jacob kept urging him until he took it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esau calls Jacob “my brother.” Jacob still says “my lord.” Esau has already forgiven. Jacob can hardly believe it yet.
> “I have seen your face, as one sees the face of God.” Jacob has just seen God at Peniel. Now, in the face of his brother who has made peace with him, he sees something of God’s grace.
> In Hebrew, the word for “gift” in verse 11 is actually “blessing.” In a way, Jacob gives back the stolen blessing. He makes up for what he can.

## Separate ways (Verses 12–17)

[12] Esau said,
“Let us set out and go on our way.
I will go ahead of you.”
[13] Jacob said to him,
“My lord knows that the children are still small and weak.
And the flocks and herds with me are nursing their young.
If they are driven too hard for even one day, all the flocks will die.
[14] Please let my lord go on ahead of his servant.
I will follow slowly,
at the pace of the livestock in front of me and at the pace of the children,
until I come to my lord in Seir.”

[15] Esau said,
“Then let me leave some of my people with you.”
Jacob said,
“Why do that?
It is enough for me that my lord is kind to me.”

[16] So that day Esau went back on his way to Seir.
[17] But Jacob traveled to Succoth.
There he built himself a house.
And he made shelters for his livestock.
That is why the place is called Succoth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is a caring shepherd: He thinks of the small and weak ones in his flock.
> But he does not go to Esau in Seir, as he said he would. He goes in another direction. Maybe he does not fully trust the peace yet. Maybe he also knows: God has called him into the land of Canaan, not to Seir.
> The brothers have made peace, but they live apart. Sometimes that is the best way to keep peace.
> “Succoth” means “shelters.” Later the Jews celebrate a festival with this name: the Festival of Shelters.

---

## Jacob comes to Shechem (Verses 18–20)

[18] On his way from Paddan Aram, Jacob arrived safely at the city of Shechem in the land of Canaan.
He set up his camp in front of the city.
[19] He bought the piece of land where he had set up his tent.
He bought it from the sons of Hamor, Shechem’s father.
He paid 100 pieces of money for it.
[20] He built an altar there.
He called it El Elohe Israel.
That means: “God, the God of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Safely” – in Hebrew there is a word here that is related to “shalom”: peace, whole, unharmed. God has kept his promise from Bethel: Jacob has come back safe and sound (Genesis 28:21).
> Like Abraham, Jacob also buys a piece of land in the promised land.
> “El” is an old word for God. With the name of the altar, Jacob says: This God is **my** God. He already uses his new name, Israel. At Bethel he had promised that the LORD would be his God. Now he keeps his promise.
