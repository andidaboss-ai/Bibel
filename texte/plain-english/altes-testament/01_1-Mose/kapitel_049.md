# Genesis – Chapter 49
### Jacob’s blessing for his twelve sons – Jacob dies

---

## Gather together! (Verses 1–2)

[1] Jacob called his sons and said,
“Gather together,
so that I can tell you what will happen to you in the days to come.
[2] Come together and listen, you sons of Jacob.
Listen to Israel, your father.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of his life, Jacob speaks to each of his sons. These words are like a poem. In many Bibles, this text is printed as poetry.
> Jacob does not say only good things. He says openly what each one has done and what will become of him. The words are meant not only for the sons, but also for the tribes that will come from them.
> Some pictures in this chapter are hard to understand. Even experts are not sure what exactly some verses mean.

---

## Reuben (Verses 3–4)

[3] “Reuben, you are my firstborn,
my might and the first sign of my strength.
You were first in honor and first in power.
[4] But you boiled over like water.
So you will not stay first.
For you climbed into your father’s bed.
Then you dishonored it.
Yes, he climbed onto my bed!

> **What does this mean?**
> As the oldest, Reuben should have become the leader. But he slept with Bilhah, his father’s concubine (Genesis 35:22). That is why he loses his special right.
> “Boiling over like water” means: out of control, without self-control.
> In fact, the tribe of Reuben did not play a big role in Israel later on.

---

## Simeon and Levi (Verses 5–7)

[5] “Simeon and Levi are brothers.
Their swords are weapons of violence.
[6] My soul, do not take part in their plans.
My honor, do not join their gathering.
For in their anger they killed men.
And in their stubborn pride they cut the leg tendons of cattle.
[7] Their anger is cursed, because it was fierce,
and their rage, because it was cruel.
I will divide them up in Jacob
and scatter them in Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob is remembering the bloodbath in Shechem (Chapter 34). Back then, he thought only of himself. Now he clearly condemns the violence of his sons.
> Important: Jacob does not curse his sons. He curses their anger and their violence.
> “They will be scattered”: later, the tribe of Simeon did not receive an area of its own, but lived in the middle of the area of Judah. The tribe of Levi did not receive its own land either. But the priests came from Levi, and they lived all over the land. So in the end, the curse became a blessing.

---

## Judah (Verses 8–12)

[8] “Judah, your brothers will praise you.
Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies.
Your father’s sons will bow down before you.
[9] Judah is a young lion.
You have come up from the prey, my son.
He crouches down and lies down like a lion, like a lioness.
Who dares to wake him?

[10] The scepter will not leave Judah,
nor the ruler’s staff from between his feet,
until he comes to whom it belongs.
The peoples will obey him.

[11] He ties his young donkey to the vine,
his donkey’s colt to the best vine.
He has washed his clothes in wine,
his robes in the blood of grapes.
[12] His eyes will be red with wine,
his teeth white with milk.

> **What does this mean?**
> Judah receives the greatest blessing. He has changed: he admitted his guilt with Tamar and offered himself for Benjamin. Now he becomes the leader among the brothers.
> The lion is a picture of strength and royal dignity. To this day, the lion is the symbol on the coat of arms of Jerusalem.
> A scepter is a staff that kings carry as a sign of their power. Later King David came from the tribe of Judah, and so did all the kings in Jerusalem.
> “Until he comes to whom it belongs” – this sentence is hard to translate. Very early on, Jews and Christians related it to the Messiah, the coming king whom God will send. Christians see in it a pointer to Jesus. In the book of Revelation, Jesus is called “the Lion from the tribe of Judah” (Revelation 5:5).
> Verses 11 and 12 paint a picture of unbelievable plenty: so much wine that people tie donkeys to fine vines and wash their clothes in it.

---

## Zebulun and Issachar (Verses 13–15)

[13] “Zebulun will live by the shore of the sea.
He will be a harbor for ships.
His border will reach to Sidon.

[14] Issachar is a strong donkey,
lying down between the saddlebags.
[15] He saw that the resting place was good,
and that the land was pleasant.
So he bent his shoulder to carry the load
and became a servant doing forced labor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Later, Zebulun received an area toward the Mediterranean Sea, in the north of the land. Sidon is a port city in what is now Lebanon.
> Issachar is compared to a strong donkey that carries loads. His land was very fertile. But because he wanted an easy life, he submitted to others and had to work for them.

---

## Dan, Gad, Asher and Naphtali (Verses 16–21)

[16] “Dan will judge his people
as one of the tribes of Israel.
[17] Dan will be a snake by the road,
a viper on the path,
that bites the horse’s heels,
so that its rider falls backward.

[18] LORD, I have waited for you to save me.

[19] Gad – a band of raiders will attack him.
But he will attack them at their heels.

[20] Asher’s food will be rich.
He will produce fine food fit for a king.

[21] Naphtali is a doe set free,
who gives birth to beautiful fawns.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Dan” means “judge.” A small tribe, but cunning and dangerous like a snake. Later the judge Samson came from Dan.
> In the middle of the words of blessing, Jacob suddenly sighs to God: “LORD, I have waited for you to save me.” Maybe he realizes: my sons and their descendants will go through much fighting and trouble. In the end, only God can help.
> “Gad” sounds like “band of raiders” and “attack.” The tribe of Gad lived east of the Jordan and often had to defend itself against attackers.
> “Asher” means “happy.” He received a very fertile area on the coast.
> With Naphtali, the Hebrew text is hard to understand. Some Bibles translate “beautiful fawns,” others “beautiful words.” What is clear: Naphtali is compared to a free, quick animal.

---

## Joseph (Verses 22–26)

[22] “Joseph is a fruitful vine,
a fruitful vine by a spring.
His branches climb over the wall.
[23] The archers have attacked him bitterly.
They shot at him and hunted him down.
[24] But his bow stayed firm.
His arms were made strong
by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob –
from there comes the shepherd, the stone of Israel –
[25] by the God of your father, who will help you,
by the Almighty, who will bless you
with blessings from heaven above,
with blessings from the deep waters that lie below,
with blessings of the breasts and of the womb.
[26] The blessings of your father are greater
than the blessings of my ancestors.
They reach beyond the borders of the ancient hills.
They will be on the head of Joseph,
on the head of the one who was set apart from his brothers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph receives the longest and warmest blessing.
> The “archers” are a picture of all those who made Joseph suffer: his brothers, Potiphar’s wife, the prison. But Joseph was not broken. God made him strong.
> Jacob calls God by many beautiful names: the Mighty One of Jacob, the shepherd, the stone of Israel, the Almighty. A stone like this is a rock: firm and safe. You can build on it.
> The blessing is complete: rain from heaven, water from the earth and many children.

---

## Benjamin (Verse 27)

[27] “Benjamin is a hungry wolf.
In the morning he eats up the prey.
And in the evening he divides what he has captured.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Little Benjamin, who was so well protected, becomes a tribe of fighters. In fact, the people of Benjamin were later known as brave and dangerous warriors. Saul, the first king of Israel, came from Benjamin. And the apostle Paul also came from the tribe of Benjamin (Philippians 3:5).

---

## Jacob dies (Verses 28–33)

[28] These are the twelve tribes of Israel.
And this is what their father said to them when he blessed them.
He blessed each one with the blessing that was meant for him.

[29] Then he gave them instructions and said to them,
“I am about to be gathered to my people.
Bury me with my fathers in the cave in the field of Ephron the Hittite,
[30] in the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre, in the land of Canaan.
Abraham bought it, together with the field, from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place.
[31] There they buried Abraham and his wife Sarah.
There they buried Isaac and his wife Rebekah.
And there I buried Leah.
[32] The field and the cave in it were bought from the people of Heth.”

[33] When Jacob had finished giving these instructions to his sons,
he drew his feet up into the bed.
He breathed his last.
And he was gathered to his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here, for the first time in the Bible, we read: “the twelve tribes of Israel.” A family has become a nation.
> Jacob wants to be buried with Leah, not with Rachel. Leah, who fought so long for his love, lies at his side in the end – and with her, the fathers and mothers of faith.
> Here we also learn that Rebekah and Leah have died. The Bible had not told this before.
> Jacob’s death is described as calm and peaceful. He has said everything he wanted to say. Then he lies down and dies.
