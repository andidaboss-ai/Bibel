# Genesis – Chapter 44
### The silver cup – Judah stands up for Benjamin

---

## Joseph’s last test (Verses 1–6)

[1] Joseph gave an order to the manager of his house:
“Fill the men’s sacks with food, as much as they can carry.
And put each man’s money in the top of his sack.
[2] Put my cup, the silver cup,
in the top of the youngest one’s sack, together with his money for the grain.”
The manager did what Joseph had told him.

[3] As soon as it was light in the morning, the men were sent away with their donkeys.
[4] They had just left the city and had not gone far.
Then Joseph said to his manager,
“Get up! Go after the men!
When you catch up with them, say to them,
‘Why have you paid back good with evil?
[5] Is this not the cup my lord drinks from?
Is it not the cup he uses to see the future?
You have done something evil!’”
[6] The manager caught up with them and said these words to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joseph tests his brothers one last time. It is the most important test of all.
> He puts Benjamin in a position where he looks like a thief. The question is: will the brothers abandon Benjamin, the way they once abandoned Joseph? Or will they stand by him?
> Back then, people tried to see the future with certain cups. They poured oil into water and explained the shapes. The Bible does not say whether Joseph really did this. Here he is playing the role of an Egyptian ruler.

---

## The cup is found (Verses 7–13)

[7] They said to him,
“Why does my lord say such things?
Your servants would never do such a thing!
[8] Look: we even brought back to you from the land of Canaan
the money we found in the top of our sacks.
So why would we steal silver or gold from your lord’s house?
[9] If it is found with any of your servants, he must die.
And the rest of us will be my lord’s slaves.”

[10] He said,
“All right, let it be as you say.
But only the one it is found with will be my slave.
The rest of you will go free and be without blame.”

[11] Each man quickly lowered his sack to the ground, and each man opened his sack.
[12] The manager searched.
He started with the oldest and ended with the youngest.
And the cup was found in Benjamin’s sack.

[13] Then they tore their clothes.
Each man loaded his donkey again.
And they went back to the city.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers are so sure that they are innocent. They even swear: whoever has the cup must die.
> The tension keeps rising: the manager starts with the oldest. Sack by sack. And only at the very end, with the youngest, is the cup found.
> Now the brothers could simply have gone home. After all, they were free. Only Benjamin was to stay.
> But they tear their clothes in grief – and all of them turn back. No one leaves Benjamin alone. Back then, they tore Joseph’s robe. Now they tear their own clothes.

---

## Before Joseph (Verses 14–17)

[14] Judah and his brothers came to Joseph’s house.
Joseph was still there.
They threw themselves to the ground before him.
[15] Joseph said to them,
“What is this you have done?
Do you not know that a man like me can see the future?”

[16] Judah said,
“What can we say to my lord?
What can we say?
How can we prove our innocence?
God has uncovered the guilt of your servants.
Look: we are my lord’s slaves,
all of us and also the one the cup was found with.”

[17] But Joseph said,
“I would never do that.
The man the cup was found with will be my slave.
But as for the rest of you, go back in peace to your father.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “God has uncovered the guilt of your servants.” Judah does not mean the cup – they did not steal it. He means the old guilt: what they did to Joseph. He believes God is now punishing them for it.
> Joseph offers them the easy way out: go home. Leave Benjamin here. Exactly the way they once left Joseph behind.
> Now everything depends on what they do.

---

## Judah’s speech (Verses 18–34)

[18] Then Judah came up to him and said,
“Please, my lord!
Let your servant say a word to my lord.
Do not be angry with your servant.
For you are as powerful as Pharaoh.

[19] My lord asked his servants,
‘Do you have a father or a brother?’
[20] We said to my lord,
‘We have an old father.
And we have a young brother, born to him in his old age.
His brother is dead.
He is the only one left of his mother’s sons.
And his father loves him.’
[21] Then you said to your servants,
‘Bring him down to me, so that I can see him with my own eyes.’
[22] We said to my lord,
‘The boy cannot leave his father.
If he leaves his father, his father will die.’
[23] But you said to your servants,
‘Unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you will not see my face again.’

[24] When we went back up to your servant, my father,
we told him what my lord had said.
[25] Then our father said,
‘Go again and buy us a little food.’
[26] We said,
‘We cannot go down.
We will go down only if our youngest brother is with us.
For we cannot see the man’s face unless our youngest brother is with us.’

[27] Your servant, my father, said to us,
‘You know that my wife gave me two sons.
[28] One of them went away from me.
And I said, “He must have been torn to pieces.”
I have not seen him since.
[29] If you take this one from me too, and something bad happens to him,
you will bring my gray hairs down to Sheol in sorrow.’

[30] So now, when I come to your servant, my father,
and the boy is not with us –
his life is bound up with the boy’s life –
[31] when he sees that the boy is not there, he will die.
Then your servants will bring the gray hairs of your servant, our father, down to Sheol in sorrow.

[32] For your servant guaranteed the boy’s safety to my father.
I said,
‘If I do not bring him back to you, then I will carry the blame before my father forever.’
[33] So now, please let your servant stay here as my lord’s slave instead of the boy.
And let the boy go back with his brothers.
[34] For how can I go back to my father if the boy is not with me?
I could not bear to see the misery that would come over my father.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the longest speech in the whole book of Genesis. And one of the most moving.
> Judah tells everything once more. He does not even hide that his father loves Benjamin more than him. He says it without bitterness. In the past, the favoring of one brother drove the brothers to hatred. Now Judah simply accepts it, out of love for his father.
> And then comes the unbelievable part: Judah offers himself as a slave so that Benjamin can go free.
> Judah, who once suggested selling Joseph as a slave, now wants to become a slave himself to save his brother. The brothers have truly changed.
> Christians see here a picture of Jesus, who comes from the tribe of Judah: he gives himself up so that others can be free.
