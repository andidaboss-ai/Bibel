# Genesis – Chapter 40
### Joseph explains the dreams of two prisoners

---

## Two new prisoners (Verses 1–4)

[1] After these things, the butler of the king of Egypt and his baker did wrong against their lord, the king of Egypt.
[2] Pharaoh was angry with his two officers, the chief cup bearer and the chief baker.
[3] He put them under guard in the house of the captain of the guard,
in the prison, the place where Joseph was locked up.
[4] The captain of the guard put Joseph in charge of them.
Joseph took care of them.
They stayed in prison for many days.

> **What does this mean?**
> A cup bearer handed the king his drinks. He had to make sure that no one poisoned the king. It was a job of great trust.
> The chief baker took care of the bread and baked goods at the royal court.
> The Bible does not say what the two did wrong.

---

## Two dreams (Verses 5–8)

[5] One night, both of them had a dream, each man his own dream.
Each dream had its own meaning.
The cup bearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were locked up in the prison, both had a dream.
[6] In the morning, Joseph came in to them.
He looked at them and saw that they were sad.
[7] He asked Pharaoh’s officers, who were under guard with him in his master’s house,
“Why do you look so sad today?”
[8] They said to him,
“We have had a dream.
But there is no one who can explain it.”
Joseph said to them,
“Don’t explanations of dreams belong to God?
Please tell me your dreams.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Egypt, people firmly believed that dreams were messages from the gods. There were experts who explained dreams. In the prison, there was no one like that.
> Joseph pays attention to others. He sees that the two are not doing well, and he asks about it. He himself is in prison, even though he is innocent. Still, he cares for others.
> Joseph says clearly: It is not I who can explain dreams. It is God. He does not take the honor for himself.

---

## The cup bearer’s dream (Verses 9–15)

[9] The chief cup bearer told his dream to Joseph.
He said to him,
“In my dream – look: There was a grapevine in front of me.
[10] There were three branches on the vine.
It was as if it was budding.
It blossomed, and its clusters became ripe grapes.
[11] Pharaoh’s cup was in my hand.
I took the grapes and pressed them into Pharaoh’s cup.
Then I gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand.”

[12] Joseph said to him,
“This is what it means:
The three branches are three days.
[13] Within three days, Pharaoh will lift up your head.
He will give you back your job.
You will give Pharaoh’s cup into his hand again, the way you did before, when you were his cup bearer.

[14] But remember me when things go well for you.
Please be kind to me and tell Pharaoh about me.
Help me get out of this house.
[15] For I was really stolen away from the land of the Hebrews.
And here too I have done nothing for which they should put me into the dungeon.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Lift up your head” means: honor someone again, give them a high position again.
> Joseph asks the cup bearer for help. It is the only time that Joseph speaks of his suffering. He does not say that his brothers sold him. He only says: “I was really stolen away” and “I have done nothing.”
> In Hebrew, “dungeon” is the same word as “pit” in Chapter 37. This is the second time Joseph has ended up in a pit.

---

## The baker’s dream (Verses 16–19)

[16] The chief baker saw that Joseph had given a good explanation.
So he said to Joseph,
“I also had a dream.
Look: There were three baskets of white bread on my head.
[17] In the top basket there were all kinds of baked goods for Pharaoh.
But the birds were eating them out of the basket on my head.”

[18] Joseph answered,
“This is what it means:
The three baskets are three days.
[19] Within three days, Pharaoh will lift up your head – off of you.
He will hang you on a tree.
And the birds will eat your flesh off of you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The baker hopes for good news. But Joseph has to tell him the terrible truth. Joseph is honest, even when it is hard.
> Joseph uses the same words, “lift up your head” – but this time with a terrible meaning: The baker will be put to death.
> Back then, it was especially terrible that the birds would eat the flesh. For the Egyptians, a proper burial was very important for life after death.

---

## It happens as Joseph said (Verses 20–23)

[20] The third day was Pharaoh’s birthday.
He made a feast for all his servants.
In front of all his servants, he lifted up the head of the chief cup bearer and the head of the chief baker.
[21] He gave the chief cup bearer back his job.
And he gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand again.
[22] But he hanged the chief baker,
just as Joseph had explained it to them.

[23] But the chief cup bearer did not remember Joseph.
He forgot him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything happens exactly as Joseph said. God has spoken through him.
> The last sentence is bitter: The cup bearer forgets Joseph. Joseph has to stay in prison for two more years (see Genesis 41:1).
> People forget. But God does not forget. And sometimes God’s timetable is different from ours. In the next chapter it becomes clear why Joseph still had to wait.
