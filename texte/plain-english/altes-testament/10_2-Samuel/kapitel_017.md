# 2 Samuel – Chapter 17
### Ahithophel’s advice against Hushai’s advice

---

## Ahithophel’s second advice (Verses 1–4)

[1] Ahithophel also said to Absalom,
“Let me now choose 12,000 men.
Then I will get up and chase after David tonight.
[2] I will come upon him while he is tired and worn out,
and I will make him afraid.
Then all the people who are with him will flee.
And I will strike down only the king.
[3] And I will bring all the people back to you.
The man you are looking for is worth as much as if all of them came back.
Then all the people will be at peace.”
[4] The plan pleased Absalom and all the elders of Israel very much.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahithophel’s plan is very good: attack at once, while David is worn out. Kill only David and spare everyone else. Then the war is over with one single blow.
> If Absalom had followed this plan, David would probably have died.

---

## Hushai’s different advice (Verses 5–14)

[5] Then Absalom said,
“Now call Hushai the Archite too.
Let’s also hear what he says.”
[6] When Hushai came to Absalom, Absalom said to him,
“Ahithophel has spoken like this.
Shall we do what he says?
If not, you speak.”
[7] Hushai said to Absalom,
“The advice that Ahithophel has given this time is not good.”
[8] Hushai also said,
“You know your father and his men.
They are mighty men, and they are furious,
like a bear in the field whose cubs have been stolen.
Your father is a man of war.
He will not spend the night with the people.
[9] Look, by now he is hidden in some pit or in some other place.
And if some of your men fall right at the beginning,
then whoever hears it will say,
‘There is a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom!’
[10] Then even the bravest man, whose heart is like the heart of a lion, will lose all his courage.
For all Israel knows that your father is a mighty man,
and that the men who are with him are brave.
[11] But my advice is this:
Let all Israel be gathered to you, from Dan to Beersheba,
as many as the sand by the sea.
And you yourself should go into battle.
[12] Then we will come upon him in some place where he can be found.
We will fall on him as the dew falls on the ground.
And not one will be left of him and of all the men who are with him.
[13] And if he goes into a city,
then all Israel will bring ropes to that city,
and we will drag it down into the river,
until not even one small stone is found there.”
[14] Absalom and all the men of Israel said,
“The advice of Hushai the Archite is better than the advice of Ahithophel.”
For the LORD had decided
to defeat the good advice of Ahithophel,
so that the LORD might bring disaster on Absalom.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hushai is a master of words. He makes Absalom afraid of David: your father is as dangerous as a furious mother bear. He will hide and surprise you.
> Then he flatters Absalom’s vanity: you yourself should lead a huge army. Absalom likes that.
> He exaggerates with big pictures: as many as the sand by the sea, like dew on the ground, dragging a city into the river with ropes. The words sound impressive, but they are not realistic.
> Above all, Hushai wins the most important thing for David: time. By the time all Israel is gathered, David can get to safety.
> Verse 14 is the key: God himself has answered David’s prayer: “LORD, please turn the advice of Ahithophel into foolishness” (2 Samuel 15:31). Here God does not work through miracles, but through human decisions.

## The messengers in the well (Verses 15–22)

[15] Then Hushai said to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests,
“This is what Ahithophel advised Absalom and the elders of Israel,
and this is what I have advised.
[16] So now send quickly and tell David,
‘Don’t spend the night at the fords of the wilderness.
Be sure to cross over,
so that the king and all the people who are with him are not swallowed up.’”
[17] Jonathan and Ahimaaz were staying at En Rogel.
A servant girl used to go and report to them,
and they went and told King David.
For they could not risk being seen coming into the city.
[18] But a boy saw them and told Absalom.
Then the two of them went away quickly
and came to the house of a man in Bahurim
who had a well in his courtyard.
And they went down into it.
[19] The woman took a cover and spread it over the opening of the well,
and she spread crushed grain on it.
So no one knew anything.
[20] Absalom’s servants came to the woman at the house and said,
“Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?”
The woman said to them,
“They have gone over the brook of water.”
When they had searched and could not find them,
they went back to Jerusalem.
[21] After they had left,
the two men came up out of the well,
went, and told King David.
They said to David,
“Get up and cross the water quickly.
For this is what Ahithophel has advised against you.”
[22] Then David got up, and all the people who were with him,
and they crossed over the Jordan.
By the light of morning, every one of them had crossed the Jordan.
Not one was missing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hushai does not know for sure whether Absalom might still follow Ahithophel’s advice after all. That is why David is to flee at once.
> Now an exciting chain of messengers begins: Hushai tells the priests, the priests tell a servant girl, the servant girl tells the priests’ sons, the sons tell David.
> En Rogel was a spring in the valley south of Jerusalem.
> Once again a brave woman saves David’s people. She hides the messengers in the well, as Rahab had hidden the spies (Joshua 2). She sends the men chasing them in the wrong direction.
> By morning all of David’s people are safely across the Jordan. No one is missing.

## The end of Ahithophel (Verse 23)

[23] When Ahithophel saw that his advice was not followed,
he saddled his donkey.
He got up and went home to his city.
He put his house in order and hanged himself.
So he died and was buried in his father’s tomb.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahithophel is wise enough to see: if Absalom does not attack at once, David will win. And then he will be punished as a traitor.
> He puts his affairs in order and takes his own life. The Bible tells this plainly, without a judgment.
> There is a similar story in the New Testament: Judas, who betrayed Jesus, also took his own life (Matthew 27:5).

## David in Mahanaim (Verses 24–29)

[24] Then David came to Mahanaim.
And Absalom crossed over the Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.
[25] Absalom put Amasa over the army in place of Joab.
Amasa was the son of a man whose name was Ithra the Israelite,
who had slept with Abigail, the daughter of Nahash, the sister of Zeruiah, Joab’s mother.
[26] Israel and Absalom camped in the land of Gilead.
[27] When David had come to Mahanaim,
Shobi the son of Nahash from Rabbah of the people of Ammon,
and Machir the son of Ammiel from Lodebar,
and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim
[28] brought beds, bowls, clay pots,
wheat, barley, flour, parched grain, beans, lentils, roasted grain,
[29] honey, butter, sheep, and cheese from the herd,
for David and for the people who were with him to eat.
For they said,
“The people are hungry, tired, and thirsty in the wilderness.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mahanaim lies east of the Jordan. That is where Ishbosheth had once been king (Chapter 2:8).
> Amasa is a cousin of Joab and also a nephew of David. This Abigail is not David’s wife from Chapter 25 of 1 Samuel. 1 Chronicles 2:13–17 says that she was a sister of David. Here it says “daughter of Nahash.” Maybe Nahash was another name for Jesse, or the name of her mother. And in 1 Chronicles 2:17 Ithra is called “Jether the Ishmaelite.”
> In David’s greatest need, three men come with everything that is needed. One is an Ammonite, a son of Nahash, the former enemy. One is Machir, who had once taken in Mephibosheth. And one is the rich old man Barzillai.
> They do not ask who will win. They only see: these people are hungry, tired, and thirsty. That is real readiness to help.
