# 2 Samuel – Chapter 4
### The murder of Ishbosheth

---

## Two leaders and a lame child (Verses 1–4)

[1] When Saul’s son heard that Abner was dead in Hebron,
his hands became weak,
and all Israel was alarmed.
[2] Saul’s son had two men who were captains of raiding groups.
The name of one was Baanah, and the name of the other was Rechab.
They were the sons of Rimmon from Beeroth, of the tribe of Benjamin.
For Beeroth also counts as part of Benjamin.
[3] The people of Beeroth had fled to Gittaim
and have lived there as foreigners until today.
[4] Jonathan, Saul’s son, had a son who was lame in his feet.
He was five years old when the news about Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel.
His nurse picked him up and fled.
As she hurried to flee, he fell and became lame.
His name was Mephibosheth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Without Abner, Ishbosheth is helpless. “His hands became weak” means: he loses all his courage.
> In the middle of the story a child is briefly put in: Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan. When the news of the death of his father and grandfather came, his nurse fled with him. He fell and was lame from then on.
> Why is he mentioned here? When Ishbosheth dies, only this lame boy will be left of Saul’s family. He could not become king. David had promised Jonathan to protect his family (1 Samuel 20:15). We hear about Mephibosheth again in Chapter 9.
> As with Ishbosheth, this name was also changed: in 1 Chronicles 8:34 he is called “Merib-baal.”

---

## Ishbosheth is murdered (Verses 5–8)

[5] The sons of Rimmon from Beeroth, Rechab and Baanah, set out.
In the heat of the day they came to the house of Ishbosheth,
while he was taking his rest at noon.
[6] They came into the middle of the house as if they were going to get wheat,
and they stabbed him in the body.
And Rechab and his brother Baanah escaped.
[7] For when they came into the house,
he was lying on his bed in his bedroom.
They struck him and killed him.
They cut off his head and took his head with them.
Then they walked all night along the way through the Arabah.
[8] They brought the head of Ishbosheth to David at Hebron
and said to the king,
“Here is the head of Ishbosheth, the son of Saul, your enemy,
who tried to take your life!
Today the LORD has avenged my lord the king on Saul and on his descendants.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Two of Ishbosheth’s own officers murder him while he sleeps. They act as if they wanted to get grain. It is a cowardly murder of a man who cannot defend himself.
> The old Greek translation tells verse 6 a little differently: the woman who kept the door had been cleaning wheat and had fallen asleep. So the men came into the house without being seen.
> The murderers think like the Amalekite in Chapter 1: David will be glad and reward us. They even use God’s name: “Today the LORD has avenged my lord the king.”

---

## David punishes the murderers (Verses 9–12)

[9] David answered Rechab and his brother Baanah, the sons of Rimmon from Beeroth,
and said to them,
“As the LORD lives, who has rescued my life from all trouble:
[10] When someone told me, ‘Saul is dead,’
and thought he was bringing good news,
I seized him and killed him in Ziklag.
That was the reward I gave him for his news.
[11] How much more, when wicked men have killed a righteous man in his own house,
on his bed,
should I not now demand his blood from your hand
and rid the earth of you?”
[12] David gave the order to his young men, and they killed them.
They cut off their hands and their feet
and hung them up beside the pool in Hebron.
But they took the head of Ishbosheth
and buried it in Abner’s grave in Hebron.

> **What does this mean?**
> David says: it was not you who saved me. The LORD has rescued me from all trouble. I do not need murderers to become king.
> He calls Ishbosheth a “righteous man.” Righteous means: living right before God. Ishbosheth was David’s enemy, but he was innocent of this crime.
> David punishes the murderers harshly. Their hands and feet were cut off, because they had murdered with their hands and had run with their feet to report it. The dead bodies were hung up in public as a warning to everyone. This is a cruel punishment from a harsh time, not an example for today.
> Once again David shows: he does not want to come to power through violence. He waits for God’s way.
