# 2 Samuel – Chapter 10
### War against the Ammonites and the Syrians

---

## David’s messengers are humiliated (Verses 1–5)

[1] After this, the king of the people of Ammon died,
and his son Hanun became king in his place.
[2] David said,
“I will show kindness to Hanun the son of Nahash,
as his father showed kindness to me.”
So David sent his servants
to comfort him about his father.
And David’s servants came into the land of the people of Ammon.
[3] But the princes of the people of Ammon said to Hanun their lord,
“Do you think that David wants to honor your father
because he has sent people to comfort you?
Hasn’t David sent his servants to you
to search the city, to spy it out, and to destroy it?”
[4] So Hanun took David’s servants
and shaved off half of their beards.
He cut off their clothes in the middle, up to their buttocks.
Then he sent them away.
[5] When David was told this, he sent people to meet them,
for the men were deeply ashamed.
The king said,
“Wait at Jericho until your beards have grown.
Then come back.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nahash, Hanun’s father, was the king who had once threatened Jabesh (1 Samuel 11). Later he probably helped David once, maybe when David was running away from Saul. That is why David wants to show his son sympathy in his grief.
> But Hanun’s advisers are suspicious. They think David’s messengers are spies.
> Hanun humiliates the messengers in a terrible way. In those days a beard was a sign of dignity and manhood. A half-shaved beard and clothes that showed the buttocks were a great disgrace.
> David is considerate: he makes sure that his men do not have to show themselves in public until their beards have grown back.
> Whoever humiliates messengers insults the one who sent them. That was practically a declaration of war.

## Joab and Abishai win (Verses 6–14)

[6] The people of Ammon saw that they had made David hate them.
So the people of Ammon sent and hired the Syrians of Beth Rehob
and the Syrians of Zobah, 20,000 foot soldiers,
and the king of Maacah with 1,000 men,
and the men of Tob, 12,000 men.
[7] When David heard about it,
he sent Joab and the whole army of the mighty men.
[8] The people of Ammon came out
and lined up for battle at the entrance of the gate.
The Syrians of Zobah and of Rehob and the men of Tob and Maacah were by themselves in the field.
[9] When Joab saw that the battle lines were set against him in front and behind,
he chose some of the best men of Israel
and lined them up against the Syrians.
[10] He put the rest of the people under the command of his brother Abishai.
And he lined them up against the people of Ammon.
[11] He said,
“If the Syrians are too strong for me, then you must help me.
But if the people of Ammon are too strong for you, then I will come and help you.
[12] Be courageous,
and let’s be strong for our people and for the cities of our God.
And may the LORD do what seems good to him.”
[13] So Joab and the people who were with him went forward into battle against the Syrians.
And they fled from him.
[14] When the people of Ammon saw that the Syrians had fled,
they also fled from Abishai and went into the city.
Then Joab came back from the people of Ammon and came to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ammonites know that David will take revenge. That is why they hire soldiers from the Syrians.
> Joab is in a dangerous position: enemies in front of him and behind him. He divides the army: he himself fights against the Syrians, and his brother Abishai against the Ammonites. They promise to help each other.
> Joab’s words in verse 12 are remarkable: “Be courageous … And may the LORD do what seems good to him.” He does what he can and leaves the result to God. Joab is often harsh and violent, but here he also shows trust in God.

## David defeats the Syrians (Verses 15–19)

[15] When the Syrians saw that they had been defeated by Israel,
they gathered together again.
[16] Hadadezer sent and brought out the Syrians who were beyond the River.
They came to Helam,
with Shobach, the commander of Hadadezer’s army, at their head.
[17] David was told this.
So he gathered all Israel together, crossed the Jordan, and came to Helam.
The Syrians lined up against David and fought with him.
[18] The Syrians fled from Israel.
And David killed 700 chariot fighters of the Syrians and 40,000 horsemen.
And he struck Shobach, the commander of their army, so that he died there.
[19] All the kings who were servants of Hadadezer saw
that they had been defeated by Israel.
Then they made peace with Israel and served them.
So the Syrians were afraid to help the people of Ammon anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a great army of Syrians comes, even from beyond the Euphrates. David himself leads Israel into battle and wins.
> 1 Chronicles 19:18 says: 7,000 chariot fighters and 40,000 foot soldiers. The numbers are sometimes handed down differently in the old manuscripts.
> In the end the Syrians are under Israel’s rule. David’s kingdom is now at the height of its power.
> But the Ammonites are not yet defeated. The war against them goes on in the next chapter. And it is during this very war that David’s most serious sin happens.
