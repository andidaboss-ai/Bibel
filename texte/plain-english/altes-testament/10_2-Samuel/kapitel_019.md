# 2 Samuel – Chapter 19
### David returns to Jerusalem

---

## Joab scolds the grieving king (Verses 1–8)

[1] Joab was told,
“Look, the king is weeping and mourning for Absalom.”
[2] So that day the victory turned into mourning for all the people.
For that day the people heard,
“The king is grieving for his son.”
[3] That day the people sneaked into the city,
the way people sneak away who are ashamed because they ran away in battle.
[4] The king covered his face,
and the king cried with a loud voice,
“My son Absalom, Absalom, my son, my son!”
[5] Then Joab came into the house to the king and said,
“Today you have put to shame all your servants,
who today have saved your life
and the lives of your sons and your daughters,
the lives of your wives and the lives of your concubines.
[6] For you love those who hate you,
and you hate those who love you.
For today you have made it clear that princes and servants mean nothing to you.
For today I see:
if Absalom had lived and all of us had died today,
then you would have been pleased.
[7] So now get up, go out,
and speak to encourage your servants.
For I swear by the LORD:
if you don’t go out, not one man will stay with you tonight.
And that would be worse for you than all the trouble
that has come upon you from your youth until now.”
[8] Then the king got up and sat in the gate.
And all the people were told,
“Look, the king is sitting in the gate.”
Then all the people came before the king.
But Israel had fled, each man to his tent.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles the verse numbers in this chapter are shifted by one. Verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 44.
> The soldiers risked their lives for David. And now, instead of celebrating, they sneak into the city as if they had lost.
> Joab harshly tells David the truth. He killed Absalom against David’s order. Now he even scolds David. Joab is ruthless. But in this matter he is right: a king must also be there for those who were faithful to him.
> David listens to him. He sits down in the gate, where everyone can see him. He puts his private pain aside to do his duty as king.
> “Israel” here means Absalom’s followers. They have fled.

## Israel and Judah want David back (Verses 9–15)

[9] All the people were quarreling throughout all the tribes of Israel. They said,
“The king rescued us from the hand of our enemies,
and he saved us from the hand of the Philistines.
And now he has fled out of the land because of Absalom.
[10] And Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has died in battle.
So why don’t you say a word about bringing the king back?”
[11] King David sent word to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests:
“Speak to the elders of Judah and say,
‘Why are you the last to bring the king back to his house,
since what all Israel is saying has already reached the king,
that they want to bring him back to his house?
[12] You are my brothers.
You are my bone and my flesh.
So why are you the last to bring back the king?’
[13] And say to Amasa,
‘Aren’t you my bone and my flesh?
May God do this to me, and even more,
if you are not commander of the army before me from now on, in place of Joab.’”
[14] So he won the hearts of all the men of Judah, as if they were one man.
They sent word to the king:
“Come back, you and all your servants.”
[15] So the king came back and came to the Jordan.
And Judah came to Gilgal to go and meet the king
and to bring the king over the Jordan.

> **What does this mean?**
> The northern tribes are unsure. They had followed Absalom. Now they want David back, but they hesitate.
> Surprisingly, Judah, David’s own tribe, hesitates the most. Hebron in Judah was the place where Absalom’s rebellion had begun.
> David is politically wise: he wins Judah back by reminding them that they are family. And he makes Amasa, Absalom’s army commander, his own army commander. With this he shows: I want reconciliation, not revenge. And at the same time he takes away the power of Joab, who killed Absalom against his order.
> But this will have consequences (Chapter 20:10).

## Shimei asks for mercy (Verses 16–23)

[16] Shimei the son of Gera, the Benjamite from Bahurim,
hurried and came down with the men of Judah to meet King David.
[17] With him were 1,000 men from Benjamin,
and Ziba the servant of Saul’s house,
and his 15 sons and his 20 servants with him.
They went through the Jordan in front of the king.
[18] A ferry boat went across to bring over the king’s household
and to do whatever he thought good.
Shimei the son of Gera fell down before the king
when the king had come over the Jordan.
[19] He said to the king,
“Don’t let my lord count me guilty.
Don’t remember the wrong that your servant did
on the day my lord the king left Jerusalem.
Don’t let the king take it to heart.
[20] For your servant knows that I have sinned.
That is why I have come today as the first of the whole house of Joseph
to go down and meet my lord the king.”
[21] But Abishai the son of Zeruiah answered,
“Shouldn’t Shimei be put to death for this,
because he cursed the LORD’s anointed?”
[22] David said,
“What do I have to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah,
that you should be against me today?
Should anyone be put to death in Israel today?
Don’t I know that today I am king over Israel?”
[23] The king said to Shimei,
“You will not die.”
And the king swore it to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Shimei, who had cursed David, now comes first and asks for forgiveness. He brings 1,000 men from Benjamin with him. That is also wise: he shows that Saul’s tribe now stands with David.
> “The house of Joseph” here means the northern tribes, to which Benjamin is counted.
> Abishai wants to kill Shimei again. But David says: today is a day of reconciliation, not of revenge. Like Saul once after his victory at Jabesh (1 Samuel 11:13).
> David forgives Shimei and swears to him that he does not have to die. But shortly before his death David will tell his son Solomon to settle accounts with Shimei (1 Kings 2:8–9).

## Mephibosheth meets David (Verses 24–30)

[24] Mephibosheth the son of Saul also came down to meet the king.
He had not cared for his feet, he had not trimmed his beard,
and he had not washed his clothes,
from the day the king left
until the day he came home in peace.
[25] When he came to Jerusalem to meet the king,
the king said to him,
“Why didn’t you go with me, Mephibosheth?”
[26] He answered,
“My lord, O king, my servant deceived me.
For your servant said,
‘I will saddle a donkey for myself, so that I can ride on it and go with the king.’
For your servant is lame.
[27] And he has slandered your servant to my lord the king.
But my lord the king is like an angel of God.
So do what is good in your eyes.
[28] For my father’s whole house were nothing but dead men before my lord the king.
And yet you put your servant among those who eat at your own table.
So what right do I still have to complain any more to the king?”
[29] The king said to him,
“Why do you keep talking about your affairs?
I say: you and Ziba divide the land.”
[30] Mephibosheth said to the king,
“Yes, let him take it all,
since my lord the king has come home in peace to his own house.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mephibosheth has been mourning the whole time: he did not care for his feet, did not shave, did not wear clean clothes. That suggests that he was faithful to David and that Ziba lied (Chapter 16:3).
> He explains: I wanted to come along, but I am lame. Ziba was supposed to saddle a donkey for me, but he deceived me and went to you alone.
> David does not know whom to believe. He chooses a compromise: divide the land. That is not a just solution if Mephibosheth is telling the truth.
> Mephibosheth’s answer shows his heart: “Yes, let him take it all.” The main thing is that you are back. He does not care about property, but about David.
> Verse 24 says “the son of Saul.” It means “grandson.” The Hebrew word for “son” can also mean “descendant.”

## Old Barzillai (Verses 31–40)

[31] Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim
and went to the Jordan with the king, to go with him over the Jordan.
[32] Barzillai was a very old man, 80 years old.
He had provided food for the king while he was staying at Mahanaim,
for he was a very great man.
[33] The king said to Barzillai,
“Come over with me,
and I will provide for you with me in Jerusalem.”
[34] Barzillai said to the king,
“How many more days and years do I have to live,
that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?
[35] I am 80 years old today.
Can I still tell good from bad?
Can your servant still taste what I eat or what I drink?
Can I still hear the voices of singing men and singing women?
Why then should your servant be a burden to my lord the king?
[36] Your servant will just go over the Jordan with the king.
Why should the king pay me back with such a reward?
[37] Please let your servant turn back,
so that I may die in my own city, by the grave of my father and my mother.
But here is your servant Chimham.
Let him go over with my lord the king.
And do for him what seems good to you.”
[38] The king answered,
“Chimham will go over with me,
and I will do for him what seems good to you.
And whatever you ask of me, I will do for you.”
[39] All the people went over the Jordan,
and the king went over too.
Then the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him,
and he went back to his own place.
[40] So the king went on to Gilgal,
and Chimham went on with him.
All the people of Judah had brought the king over,
and also half the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Barzillai is a faithful, generous old man. He provided for David in his time of need, without asking for anything in return.
> David would like to reward him. But Barzillai humbly says no. He honestly describes what it is like to grow old: you taste less, you hear less well, you can no longer enjoy life at the royal court. He wants to die at home, near his parents.
> Instead of asking for himself, he asks for Chimham, probably his son (1 Kings 2:7).
> This is a beautiful, calm scene after so much violence: faithfulness is answered with faithfulness. David does not forget his friends.

## Quarrel between Israel and Judah (Verses 41–43)

[41] Then all the men of Israel came to the king
and said to the king,
“Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away
and brought the king and his household over the Jordan,
and all David’s men with him?”
[42] All the men of Judah answered the men of Israel,
“Because the king is a close relative of ours.
So why are you angry about this?
Have we eaten anything at the king’s cost?
Or has he given us any gift?”
[43] The men of Israel answered the men of Judah and said,
“We have 10 shares in the king,
and we also have more claim to David than you do.
So why did you despise us?
Weren’t we the first to speak of bringing back our king?”
But the words of the men of Judah were harsher than the words of the men of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The war is hardly over when a new quarrel begins. The 10 northern tribes feel left out. Judah has brought the king back on its own.
> “10 shares”: the northern tribes are 10 tribes, Judah is one. They say: there are more of us.
> Judah answers harshly and arrogantly. In the next chapter this quarrel between north and south leads to a new rebellion. And much later the kingdom will split along exactly this line (1 Kings 12).
