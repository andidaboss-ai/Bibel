# 2 Samuel – Chapter 18
### The death of Absalom

---

## “Deal gently with Absalom!” (Verses 1–5)

[1] David counted the people who were with him
and put captains of thousands and captains of hundreds over them.
[2] David sent the people out:
a third under the command of Joab,
a third under the command of Abishai the son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother,
and a third under the command of Ittai the Gittite.
The king said to the people,
“I will surely go out with you myself too.”
[3] But the people said,
“You must not go out.
For if we run away, they will not care about us.
And even if half of us die, they will not care about us.
But you are worth 10,000 of us.
So now it is better for you to be ready to help us from the city.”
[4] The king said to them,
“I will do what seems best to you.”
The king stood beside the gate,
and all the people went out by hundreds and by thousands.
[5] The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai,
“For my sake, deal gently with the young man Absalom.”
And all the people heard when the king gave all the captains this command about Absalom.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is an experienced army leader. He divides his army into three groups. Ittai, the faithful Philistine, also gets a group.
> The people do not want David to fight with them. If he dies, everything is lost. The enemy’s only goal is David.
> David’s last command is a command as a father, not as a king: “Deal gently with the young man Absalom.” Absalom wants to kill him, but David still loves his son.

---

## The battle in the forest (Verses 6–8)

[6] So the people went out into the field against Israel.
And the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.
[7] The people of Israel were defeated there by David’s servants.
And there was a great slaughter there that day: 20,000 men.
[8] For the battle spread out there over the whole countryside.
And the forest devoured more people that day than the sword devoured.

> **What does this mean?**
> The forest of Ephraim lay east of the Jordan, in the land of Gilead.
> The rough land, with ravines, rocks, and thick bushes, is a trap for Absalom’s large army. David’s experienced fighters know their way around better.
> “The forest devoured more people than the sword”: many died while running away, in pits, swamps, or in the undergrowth.

---

## Absalom hangs in the oak tree (Verses 9–15)

[9] Absalom happened to meet David’s servants.
Absalom was riding on his mule.
The mule went under the thick branches of a great oak tree.
His head got caught in the oak,
and he was left hanging between the sky and the earth.
And the mule that was under him went on.
[10] A man saw it and told Joab. He said,
“Look, I saw Absalom hanging in an oak tree.”
[11] Joab said to the man who told him,
“Look, you saw it.
Why didn’t you strike him to the ground there?
I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt.”
[12] The man said to Joab,
“Even if I received 1,000 pieces of silver in my hand,
I still would not stretch out my hand against the king’s son.
For we heard the king command you, Abishai, and Ittai,
‘Be careful that no one touches the young man Absalom.’
[13] Otherwise, if I had acted falsely against his life
– and nothing is hidden from the king –,
then you yourself would have turned against me.”
[14] Then Joab said,
“I’m not going to wait like this with you.”
He took three darts in his hand
and thrust them into Absalom’s heart while he was still alive in the middle of the oak.
[15] Ten young men who carried Joab’s armor surrounded Absalom.
They struck him and killed him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Absalom gets caught by his head in the branches, probably by his long, thick hair that he was so proud of (Chapter 14:26). His mule, a sign of royal dignity, runs away. He hangs helplessly “between the sky and the earth.”
> The simple soldier obeys the king. He says: I would not kill him for any money in the world. The king has forbidden it.
> But Joab does not obey. He knows: as long as Absalom lives, there will be no peace. He kills him, even though he cannot defend himself. Joab acts out of political calculation, not by the king’s command. It is not the first time (Chapter 3:27).
> Ten pieces of silver are about 4 ounces of silver, and 1,000 pieces of silver are about 25 pounds.

## A heap of stones instead of a monument (Verses 16–18)

[16] Joab blew the trumpet,
and the people came back from chasing Israel.
For Joab held the people back.
[17] They took Absalom,
threw him into a great pit in the forest,
and piled a very large heap of stones over him.
And all Israel fled, each one to his own tent.
[18] During his lifetime Absalom had taken and set up for himself the pillar
that is in the king’s valley.
For he said,
“I have no son to keep my name remembered.”
He called the pillar by his own name.
To this day it is called Absalom’s monument.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Absalom’s death the war is over. Joab ends the bloodshed.
> Absalom gets no royal grave. He is thrown into a pit. A heap of stones over a dead man was a sign of shame, as with Achan (Joshua 7:26).
> Absalom had set up a monument for himself so that people would remember him. But his grave is not the monument, but the heap of stones in the forest.
> Chapter 14:27 said that he had three sons. Maybe they had died young.
> Today in the Kidron Valley in Jerusalem there is a tomb that people call “Absalom’s Tomb.” But it was built much later, around the time of Jesus.

## The messengers (Verses 19–32)

[19] Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said,
“Please let me run and take the news to the king,
that the LORD has given him justice against his enemies.”
[20] Joab said to him,
“You must not be the one to bring news today.
You can bring news another day.
But today you must not bring news,
because the king’s son is dead.”
[21] Then Joab said to the Cushite,
“Go, tell the king what you have seen!”
The Cushite bowed down to Joab and ran.
[22] Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said to Joab again,
“Whatever happens, please let me also run after the Cushite.”
Joab said,
“Why do you want to run, my son?
You will get no reward for the news.”
[23] “Whatever happens,” he said, “I will run.”
Joab said to him,
“Run!”
Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the Plain
and got ahead of the Cushite.

[24] David was sitting between the two gates.
The watchman went up to the roof of the gate, on the wall.
He looked up
and saw a man running alone.
[25] The watchman shouted and told the king.
The king said,
“If he is alone, there is news in his mouth.”
He came closer and closer.
[26] Then the watchman saw another man running.
The watchman called to the gatekeeper and said,
“Look, a man is running alone!”
The king said,
“He also brings news.”
[27] The watchman said,
“I think the first one runs like Ahimaaz the son of Zadok.”
The king said,
“He is a good man, and he comes with good news.”
[28] Ahimaaz called out and said to the king,
“All is well!”
He bowed down before the king with his face to the ground and said,
“Blessed is the LORD your God,
who has handed over the men who raised their hand against my lord the king!”
[29] The king said,
“Is the young man Absalom all right?”
Ahimaaz answered,
“When Joab sent the king’s servant, that is, me, your servant,
I saw a great commotion.
But I don’t know what it was.”
[30] The king said,
“Step aside and stand here.”
He stepped aside and stood still.
[31] Then the Cushite came.
The Cushite said,
“Good news for my lord the king!
For today the LORD has given you justice against all those who rose up against you.”
[32] The king said to the Cushite,
“Is the young man Absalom all right?”
The Cushite answered,
“May the enemies of my lord the king,
and all who rise up against you to do you harm,
be as that young man is.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joab knows: for David this is not good news. Whoever tells him that Absalom is dead brings him pain. That is why he wants to protect Ahimaaz, the young son of the priest. He sends a Cushite, a man from Africa, from the land south of Egypt, probably a servant.
> Ahimaaz insists on running. He is faster. But when he stands before David, he does not dare to tell the truth.
> David asks both messengers only one thing: “Is the young man Absalom all right?” He does not care about the victory. He thinks only of his son.
> The Cushite says it carefully, but clearly: may all your enemies be as he is. Then David understands.

## “My son Absalom!” (Verse 33)

[33] Then the king was deeply shaken.
He went up to the room over the gate and wept.
And as he went, he said,
“My son Absalom!
My son, my son Absalom!
I wish I had died instead of you,
Absalom, my son, my son!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most heartbreaking sentences in the Bible. David has won, but he has lost his son. He says it again and again: “My son, my son.” He wishes he had died in his place.
> Absalom wanted to kill him. But David still loves him. A father’s love does not stop, even when the son betrays him.
> Maybe David also feels his own guilt: he did not punish Amnon. He never really talked things through with Absalom. And the sword that Nathan had announced has now struck his third son.
> Many Christians see in David’s lament a picture of God’s love: God loves people, even those who rebel against him. And Christians believe that in Jesus, God really did die in people’s place.
> In some Bibles this verse is already Chapter 19, verse 1.
