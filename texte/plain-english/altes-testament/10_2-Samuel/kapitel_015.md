# 2 Samuel – Chapter 15
### Absalom’s rebellion and David’s escape

---

## Absalom steals the hearts (Verses 1–6)

[1] After this, Absalom got a chariot and horses for himself,
and 50 men to run in front of him.
[2] Absalom got up early
and stood beside the road to the gate.
When anyone had a legal case and was coming to the king for a judgment,
Absalom called him over and said,
“What city are you from?”
He said,
“Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel.”
[3] Absalom said to him,
“Look, your case is good and right.
But there is no one appointed by the king to hear you.”
[4] And Absalom also said,
“Oh, if only I were made judge in the land!
Then everyone who has a dispute or a legal case could come to me,
and I would give him justice!”
[5] And when anyone came near to bow down to him,
he stretched out his hand, took hold of him, and kissed him.
[6] Absalom did this to all the Israelites who came to the king for a judgment.
So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Absalom is preparing to overthrow the king. With a chariot, horses, and a bodyguard, he shows himself like a king.
> He stands at the city gate, where people came to get justice. He tells everyone: you are right! But the king does not listen to you. If I were king, everything would be better.
> He promises everyone everything. He is friendly, kisses people, and acts as if he were one of them. That is still the method of people who lead others astray today: they stir up discontent, flatter, and make big promises.
> “He stole the hearts”: he does not win people honestly, but by trickery.
> Maybe Absalom was also right with his criticism. During this time David seems to have cared little about justice in the land. David had not done justice in the case of Amnon and Tamar either.

## The conspiracy in Hebron (Verses 7–12)

[7] At the end of 40 years, Absalom said to the king,
“Please let me go to Hebron
and keep my vow that I made to the LORD.
[8] For your servant made a vow while I was living at Geshur in Syria.
I said,
‘If the LORD really brings me back to Jerusalem,
then I will serve the LORD.’”
[9] The king said to him,
“Go in peace.”
So he got up and went to Hebron.
[10] But Absalom sent spies throughout all the tribes of Israel and said,
“As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet,
then say, ‘Absalom is king in Hebron!’”
[11] With Absalom went 200 men from Jerusalem.
They had been invited, and they went along innocently.
They did not know anything.
[12] While Absalom was offering the sacrifices,
he sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s adviser, from his city Giloh.
The conspiracy became strong,
for the people with Absalom kept growing in number.

> **What does this mean?**
> “At the end of 40 years”: that is what the Hebrew text and the WEB, our English source text, say. But that does not fit into David’s lifetime. Some old translations have “after four years.” That fits better.
> Absalom lies to his father. He uses a devout vow as an excuse. David says “Go in peace,” without suspecting what his son is planning. It is the last word David says to Absalom.
> Absalom chooses Hebron. That is where David himself had first become king. Absalom wants to repeat David’s story, against David.
> The 200 men who go along without knowing anything are like hostages. Everyone is supposed to think that they belong to Absalom.
> Ahithophel was David’s wisest adviser. That he goes over to Absalom is a heavy blow. Maybe he had a personal reason: Ahithophel was probably Bathsheba’s grandfather (compare Chapter 11:3 and 23:34).

## David flees from Jerusalem (Verses 13–18)

[13] A messenger came to David and said,
“The hearts of the men of Israel are with Absalom.”
[14] Then David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem,
“Get up! Let’s flee!
Otherwise none of us will escape from Absalom.
Hurry and leave,
so that he does not catch up with us quickly, bring disaster on us,
and strike the city with the edge of the sword.”
[15] The king’s servants said to the king,
“Look, your servants are ready to do whatever my lord the king chooses.”
[16] The king went out, and his whole household after him.
The king left ten women behind, who were concubines, to take care of the house.
[17] The king went out, and all the people after him.
And they stopped at Beth Merhak.
[18] All his servants passed by beside him,
and all the Cherethites and all the Pelethites
and all the Gittites, 600 men who had followed him from Gath,
passed by in front of the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> David flees at once. He does not want Jerusalem to be destroyed in a battle. He wants no bloodshed in the city.
> The great king who conquered Jerusalem now has to flee from his own son.
> He leaves ten concubines behind to take care of the palace. That will have terrible consequences (Chapter 16:21–22).
> “Beth Merhak” means “the distant house.” It probably means the last house at the edge of the city.
> David’s most faithful people are his foreign soldiers: Cherethites, Pelethites, and men from Gath.

## Ittai’s faithfulness (Verses 19–23)

[19] Then the king said to Ittai the Gittite,
“Why do you also go with us?
Go back and stay with the king.
For you are a foreigner and also an exile.
Go back to your own place.
[20] You came only yesterday.
Should I make you wander up and down with us today,
since I go wherever I can?
Go back, and take your brothers back with you.
May mercy and faithfulness be with you.”
[21] Ittai answered the king and said,
“As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives,
wherever my lord the king is, whether for death or for life,
your servant will be there too.”
[22] David said to Ittai,
“Go and cross over.”
So Ittai the Gittite crossed over,
with all his men and all the little children who were with him.
[23] The whole country wept with a loud voice
as all the people crossed over.
The king himself also crossed over the brook Kidron,
and all the people crossed over toward the road to the wilderness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ittai is a Philistine from Gath, the city of Goliath. He has been with David only a short time. David says: you do not have to go into misfortune with me. Stay with the new king.
> But Ittai answers with an oath of faithfulness that reminds us of Ruth: “Where you go, I will go” (Ruth 1:16–17). A foreigner is more faithful than David’s own son.
> “Whether for death or for life”: Ittai stays, no matter what comes.
> The brook Kidron flows in the valley east of Jerusalem. Much later, in the night before his death, Jesus also went over the brook Kidron to the Mount of Olives (John 18:1). He too was betrayed by someone close to him.

## The ark stays in Jerusalem (Verses 24–29)

[24] Zadok also came, and all the Levites with him.
They were carrying the ark of the covenant of God.
They set down the ark of God,
and Abiathar went up until all the people had finished leaving the city.
[25] The king said to Zadok,
“Carry the ark of God back into the city.
If I find favor in the LORD’s eyes,
he will bring me back
and let me see both it and his dwelling place again.
[26] But if he says, ‘I have no delight in you,’
then here I am.
Let him do to me as seems good to him.”
[27] The king also said to Zadok the priest,
“Aren’t you a seer?
Go back into the city in peace,
and your two sons with you,
your son Ahimaaz and Jonathan the son of Abiathar.
[28] Look, I will wait at the fords of the wilderness
until word comes from you to tell me what is happening.”
[29] So Zadok and Abiathar carried the ark of God back to Jerusalem,
and they stayed there.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests want to take the ark along, as a sign that God is on David’s side. But David refuses. He has learned from history: the ark is not a good-luck charm (1 Samuel 4).
> David puts his fate completely into God’s hands: “Let him do to me as seems good to him.” That is real trust. David does not want to fight for his throne by force. He waits for God.
> At the same time David is wise. The priests and their sons stay in Jerusalem and are to send him news.
> Verse 27: A seer is a prophet.

## On the Mount of Olives (Verses 30–37)

[30] David went up the Mount of Olives
and wept as he went up.
He had his head covered and went barefoot.
And all the people who were with him each covered his head,
and they went up, weeping as they went.
[31] Someone told David,
“Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom.”
David said,
“LORD, please turn the advice of Ahithophel into foolishness.”
[32] When David came to the top, where God was worshiped,
Hushai the Archite came to meet him,
with his robe torn and earth on his head.
[33] David said to him,
“If you go on with me, you will be a burden to me.
[34] But if you go back to the city and tell Absalom,
‘I will be your servant, O king.
As I have been your father’s servant in the past, so I will now be your servant,’
then you can defeat the advice of Ahithophel for me.
[35] Don’t you have Zadok and Abiathar the priests there with you?
So whatever you hear from the king’s house,
tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.
[36] Look, they have their two sons there with them,
Ahimaaz, Zadok’s son, and Jonathan, Abiathar’s son.
Send me everything you hear through them.”
[37] So Hushai, David’s friend, came into the city.
And Absalom came into Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> David goes up the Mount of Olives weeping, barefoot, and with his head covered. These are signs of deep grief and humiliation. The king is broken.
> According to its heading, Psalm 3 is a prayer of David when he fled from Absalom: “LORD, how many enemies I have now! … But you, LORD, are a shield all around me.”
> David prays a short prayer: “LORD, please turn the advice of Ahithophel into foolishness.” And right after that, Hushai comes. He is the answer to David’s prayer.
> Hushai is called “David’s friend.” That was probably a title of honor for a close adviser of the king. He is to pretend to go over to Absalom and to spoil Ahithophel’s plans.
> The last verse shows the tension: Hushai comes into the city, and at the same time Absalom comes. The game begins.
