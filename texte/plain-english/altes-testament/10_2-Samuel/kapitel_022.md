# 2 Samuel – Chapter 22
### David’s song of thanks: The LORD is my rock

---

## The LORD is my rock (Verses 1–4)

[1] David spoke the words of this song to the LORD
on the day the LORD rescued him from the hand of all his enemies
and from the hand of Saul.
[2] He said:
“The LORD is my rock, my fortress, and my rescuer, yes, mine.
[3] God is my rock, in whom I take refuge,
my shield and the horn of my salvation,
my high tower and my refuge.
My Savior, you save me from violence.
[4] I call on the LORD, who is worthy to be praised.
So I will be saved from my enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of the book there is a great song of thanks. Almost exactly the same song is also in the book of Psalms, as Psalm 18.
> It is one of the oldest prayers in the Bible. David looks back on his whole life: being hunted by Saul, the wars, the dangers. And he says: God has always rescued me.
> David uses many pictures for God: rock, fortress, shield, high tower, refuge. These are pictures of safety. When David was running away, he often hid among rocks and in mountain strongholds. But he knows: the real protection was not the rock, but God.
> “The horn of my salvation”: the horn is a picture for strength, as with Hannah (1 Samuel 2:1).

---

## In my distress I called to the LORD (Verses 5–7)

[5] For the waves of death surrounded me.
The floods of wickedness made me afraid.
[6] The cords of Sheol were all around me.
The traps of death caught me.
[7] In my distress I called on the LORD.
Yes, I called to my God.
He heard my voice from his temple.
My cry came into his ears.

> **What does this mean?**
> David describes the fear of death with pictures: like someone drowning in the waves, like an animal in a trap.
> “Sheol” is the Hebrew word for the world of the dead.
> And then comes the most important sentence: “In my distress I called on the LORD. … He heard my voice.” Many people have prayed these words in hard times.
> “Temple” here means God’s dwelling in heaven. The temple in Jerusalem did not exist yet in David’s time.

---

## God comes to help (Verses 8–20)

[8] Then the earth shook and trembled.
The foundations of heaven quaked and were shaken,
because he was angry.
[9] Smoke went up out of his nostrils.
Consuming fire came out of his mouth.
Coals were set burning by it.
[10] He bent the heavens and came down.
Thick darkness was under his feet.
[11] He rode on a cherub and flew.
Yes, he was seen on the wings of the wind.
[12] He made darkness a shelter around himself,
gathered waters and thick clouds of the skies.
[13] At the brightness before him,
coals of fire were set burning.
[14] The LORD thundered from heaven.
The Most High let his voice be heard.
[15] He shot out arrows and scattered them,
lightning, and threw them into confusion.
[16] Then the channels of the sea appeared.
The foundations of the world were laid bare
by the LORD’s rebuke,
at the blast of the breath of his nostrils.
[17] He reached down from on high and took hold of me.
He drew me out of many waters.
[18] He rescued me from my strong enemy,
from those who hated me,
for they were too mighty for me.
[19] They came against me on the day of my disaster,
but the LORD was my support.
[20] He also brought me out into a wide, open place.
He rescued me, because he delighted in me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it becomes dramatic. God comes down from heaven like a mighty thunderstorm: earthquake, fire, smoke, thunder, lightning. These are pictures, not a description of one particular event. They say: when a person cries out to God, God moves heaven and earth.
> The pictures remind us of Mount Sinai (Exodus 19) and of the crossing through the sea (Exodus 14).
> And then it becomes very gentle: the great God reaches down and draws one single person out of the water. The name Moses sounds like “drawn out of the water.”
> “He brought me out into a wide, open place”: a person who is afraid feels hemmed in. God gives space and freedom.
> “Because he delighted in me”: God rescues out of love.

---

## God rewards faithfulness (Verses 21–28)

[21] The LORD rewarded me according to my righteousness.
He rewarded me according to the cleanness of my hands.
[22] For I have kept the ways of the LORD,
and I have not wickedly turned away from my God.
[23] For all his laws were before me.
I did not turn away from his commands.
[24] I was also blameless toward him.
I kept myself from my sin.
[25] So the LORD has rewarded me according to my righteousness,
according to my cleanness in his eyes.
[26] With the merciful you show yourself merciful.
With the blameless man you show yourself blameless.
[27] With the pure you show yourself pure.
With the crooked you show yourself shrewd.
[28] You save the humble people,
but your eyes are on the proud, to bring them down.

> **What does this mean?**
> David says: I was righteous, I kept God’s commands. How can he say that, after what happened with Bathsheba and Uriah?
> Maybe the song comes from the time before this sin, when David fled from Saul and spared him twice. At that time he really was innocent. Or David means his basic faithfulness to God: he never served other gods. And when he had sinned, he repented. In the Bible “righteous” does not mean “without faults,” but: in the right relationship with God.
> Verses 26 and 27 say: God meets every person the way that person is. Whoever is merciful experiences mercy. Whoever is false is beaten with his own weapons.
> God is on the side of the humble, not of the proud. Hannah had already sung about this (1 Samuel 2:7–8).

---

## With my God I leap over a wall (Verses 29–37)

[29] For you are my lamp, LORD.
The LORD lights up my darkness.
[30] For with you I run against a troop.
With my God I leap over a wall.
[31] As for God, his way is perfect.
The word of the LORD has been tested.
He is a shield to all who take refuge in him.
[32] For who is God, besides the LORD?
And who is a rock, besides our God?
[33] God is my strong fortress.
He makes my way perfect.
[34] He makes his feet like the feet of a deer
and sets me on my high places.
[35] He teaches my hands to fight,
so that my arms can bend a bow of bronze.
[36] You have also given me the shield of your salvation.
Your gentleness has made me great.
[37] You have made room for my steps under me.
My feet have not slipped.

> **What does this mean?**
> “You are my lamp”: God makes the darkness bright. In Chapter 21:17 David himself was called “the lamp of Israel.” But David knows: his light comes from God.
> “With my God I leap over a wall”: this sentence has become very well known. It means: with God I can get past obstacles that seem impossible to overcome. Many people have written it down to give them courage.
> “The feet of a deer”: deer climb safely on steep rocks without falling. In the same way God gives a firm footing.
> “Your gentleness has made me great”: a beautiful thought. Not God’s power alone, but his kindness and gentleness made David great.
> “His feet” in verse 34: that is what the main Hebrew text says. Psalm 18:33 has “my feet.”

---

## Victory over the enemies (Verses 38–46)

[38] I have chased my enemies and destroyed them.
I did not turn back until they were wiped out.
[39] I have wiped them out and struck them through, so that they cannot get up.
Yes, they have fallen under my feet.
[40] For you have armed me with strength for the battle.
You have brought down under me those who rose up against me.
[41] You have also made my enemies turn their backs to me,
so that I could cut off those who hate me.
[42] They looked, but there was no one to save them.
They even looked to the LORD, but he did not answer them.
[43] Then I beat them as small as the dust of the earth.
I crushed them like the mud of the streets and spread them out.
[44] You have also rescued me from the quarrels of my people.
You have kept me to be the head of the nations.
A people whom I have not known will serve me.
[45] The foreigners will submit to me.
As soon as they hear of me, they will obey me.
[46] The foreigners will fade away
and come trembling out of their hiding places.

> **What does this mean?**
> This part is a victory song, like the songs kings sang in those days. The language is harsh and warlike. It reflects David’s world, in which people fought to survive.
> David gives God the honor for every victory: “You have armed me with strength for the battle.”
> “The quarrels of my people”: this probably means the rebellions of Absalom and Sheba.
> We read these verses as a king’s thanks for being rescued from great danger, not as a call to destroy enemies. Jesus teaches: “Love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44).

---

## Praise for God’s faithfulness (Verses 47–51)

[47] The LORD lives!
Blessed be my rock!
Exalted be God, the rock of my salvation,
[48] the God who takes revenge for me
and brings down peoples under me,
[49] who brings me away from my enemies.
Yes, you lift me up above those who rise up against me.
You rescue me from the violent man.
[50] So I will give thanks to you, LORD, among the nations,
and I will sing praises to your name.
[51] He gives great salvation to his king
and shows loving kindness to his anointed,
to David and to his descendants, forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The song ends the way it began: with the picture of the rock. God is the firm ground in David’s life.
> “I will give thanks to you, LORD, among the nations”: not only Israel, but all nations are to hear about God. Paul quotes this verse in his letter to the Romans. He says: through Jesus, people from all nations are also to praise God (Romans 15:9).
> The last verse reminds us of God’s promise in Chapter 7: God’s loving kindness stays with David and his descendants forever. “His anointed” is in Hebrew “his mashiach,” Messiah.
