# 2 Samuel – Chapter 8
### David’s victories and his government

---

## Victories over the Philistines and Moab (Verses 1–2)

[1] After this, David struck the Philistines and brought them under his control.
And David took the bridle of the mother city out of the hand of the Philistines.
[2] He also defeated Moab.
He measured them with a line,
by making them lie down on the ground.
He measured two lengths of the line to be put to death,
and one full length of the line to be kept alive.
So the Moabites became David’s servants and brought him tribute.
Tribute is money or goods that a defeated people had to pay.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is a summary of David’s wars. It shows that God’s promise “I will give you rest from all your enemies” (Chapter 7:11) is coming true.
> “The bridle of the mother city” is hard to understand in Hebrew. It says “Metheg Ammah.” Maybe it is the name of a place. Maybe it means: David took over the rule of the main city of the Philistines. 1 Chronicles 18:1 says: he took Gath.
> What David does with the Moabites is cruel. He has captured soldiers lie down on the ground in rows and has two thirds of them killed. In those days this was a practice of war to weaken a defeated people. The Bible tells it without praising it. It is not an example.
> This is all the more shocking because David’s great-grandmother Ruth was a Moabite, and the king of Moab had once protected David’s parents (1 Samuel 22:3–4). The text does not say why David acted so harshly.

---

## Victories over Zobah and Damascus (Verses 3–8)

[3] David also struck Hadadezer the son of Rehob, the king of Zobah,
as he went to win back his rule at the River.
[4] David captured 1,700 horsemen and 20,000 foot soldiers from him.
David hamstrung the chariot horses,
but he kept enough of them for 100 chariots.
[5] When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadadezer, the king of Zobah,
David struck down 22,000 men of the Syrians.
[6] Then David put groups of soldiers in Syria of Damascus.
And the Syrians became David’s servants and brought him tribute.
The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.
[7] David took the shields of gold that the servants of Hadadezer had carried
and brought them to Jerusalem.
[8] And from Betah and from Berothai, cities of Hadadezer,
King David took a great amount of bronze.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Hebrew the “Syrians” are called “Aram.” Many Bibles say “Arameans.” They lived in the north, in today’s Syria. Zobah and Damascus were kingdoms of the Arameans.
> “The River” is the Euphrates. David’s kingdom now reached far to the north.
> David makes the horses lame by cutting a tendon in their legs. That is what “hamstrung” means. Then they can no longer be used in war. Joshua already did this (Joshua 11:6). God did not want Israel to rely on horses and war chariots (Deuteronomy 17:16; Psalm 20:7).
> The numbers are a little different in 1 Chronicles 18:4: 1,000 chariots, 7,000 horsemen, and 20,000 foot soldiers.
> “The LORD gave David victory wherever he went”: this sentence appears twice in the chapter (verses 6 and 14). The storyteller stresses: the victories come from God.

## Gifts from Hamath (Verses 9–14)

[9] Toi, the king of Hamath, heard
that David had struck the whole army of Hadadezer.
[10] Then Toi sent his son Joram to King David,
to greet him and to congratulate him,
because he had fought against Hadadezer and struck him.
For Hadadezer had been at war with Toi.
Joram brought with him objects of silver, gold, and bronze.
[11] King David also dedicated these to the LORD,
together with the silver and gold that he had dedicated from all the nations he had brought under his control:
[12] from Syria, from Moab, from the people of Ammon, from the Philistines, from Amalek,
and from the plunder of Hadadezer, son of Rehob, king of Zobah.
[13] David made a name for himself
when he came back from striking down 18,000 men of the Syrians in the Valley of Salt.
[14] He put groups of soldiers in Edom.
Throughout all Edom he put groups of soldiers,
and all the Edomites became David’s servants.
The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hamath was a kingdom even farther north. Its king is glad that David has defeated his enemy. He sends gifts.
> David does not keep the treasures for himself. He dedicates them to the LORD. Later the temple was built from them (1 Chronicles 18:8).
> Verse 13: the Valley of Salt lies south of the Dead Sea, in the land of Edom. That is why 1 Chronicles 18:12 and the heading of Psalm 60 say “Edom” instead of “Syrians.” In Hebrew the words for Aram and Edom look very much alike. A scribe probably mixed them up.

## David’s officials (Verses 15–18)

[15] David ruled over all Israel.
And David did what was just and right for all his people.
[16] Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the army.
Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud was recorder.
[17] Zadok the son of Ahitub and Ahimelech the son of Abiathar were priests.
Seraiah was scribe.
[18] Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites.
And David’s sons were chief ministers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 is the most important sentence in this chapter: David “did what was just and right for all his people.” That is the real task of a king. Not only to win wars, but to make sure there is justice, for everyone.
> David now has a real state with officials: an army commander, a recorder who kept the records, priests, and a scribe.
> Zadok is mentioned here for the first time. Later he becomes the most important priest under Solomon.
> In 1 Samuel 22:20 Abiathar was the son of Ahimelech. Here it is the other way around. Maybe Abiathar’s son had the same name as his grandfather, or the names were switched when the text was copied.
> The “Cherethites and Pelethites” were David’s bodyguard, soldiers from other countries who were paid to serve, probably from Crete and from the people of the Philistines.
> “Chief ministers”: that is what our English source text says. The Hebrew text literally says “priests.” That is unusual, because priests were supposed to come from the tribe of Levi. 1 Chronicles 18:17 says: “chief officials serving the king.”
