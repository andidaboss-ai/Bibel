# 2 Samuel – Chapter 3
### Abner changes sides and is murdered

---

## David grows stronger (Verses 1–5)

[1] There was a long war between Saul’s house and David’s house.
David grew stronger and stronger,
but Saul’s house grew weaker and weaker.
[2] Sons were born to David in Hebron.
His firstborn was Amnon, from Ahinoam of Jezreel.
[3] His second was Chileab, from Abigail, the wife of Nabal from Carmel.
The third was Absalom, the son of Maacah, the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur.
[4] The fourth was Adonijah, the son of Haggith.
The fifth was Shephatiah, the son of Abital.
[5] The sixth was Ithream, from Eglah, David’s wife.
These were born to David in Hebron.

> **What does this mean?**
> David now has six wives and six sons. In those days it was usual for kings to have several wives, often for political reasons. Maacah, for example, was the daughter of a king. In this way David made alliances.
> But the law warned the king against having many wives (Deuteronomy 17:17). This list is like a dark preview: Amnon, Absalom, and Adonijah will later bring great misfortune to David’s family.
> In 1 Chronicles 3:1 Chileab is called “Daniel.” We hear nothing more about him.

---

## Abner and Ishbosheth quarrel (Verses 6–11)

[6] While there was war between Saul’s house and David’s house,
Abner made himself more and more powerful in Saul’s house.
[7] Saul had had a concubine whose name was Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
And Ishbosheth said to Abner,
“Why have you slept with my father’s concubine?”
[8] Then Abner was very angry about Ishbosheth’s words, and he said,
“Am I a dog’s head that belongs to Judah?
Today I show kindness to the house of your father Saul,
to his brothers, and to his friends.
I have not handed you over to David.
And yet today you accuse me of a wrong about this woman!
[9] May God do this to Abner, and even more,
if I don’t do for David exactly what the LORD has sworn to him:
[10] to take the kingdom away from Saul’s house
and to set up David’s throne over Israel and over Judah,
from Dan to Beersheba.”
[11] Ishbosheth could not answer Abner another word,
because he was afraid of him.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days, whoever slept with the wife of a king who had died was claiming his throne. That is why Ishbosheth is so worried. He accuses Abner of wanting to become king himself.
> The text does not say whether Abner really did it. Rizpah is treated here like an object in the power struggle of men. Later she shows great faithfulness and dignity (Chapter 21:10).
> Abner is offended. “A dog’s head” is an insult for someone who is worth nothing.
> Interesting: Abner knows that God has promised David the kingdom. And yet he has fought against David until now. Now he changes sides, out of hurt pride.

---

## Abner makes a deal with David (Verses 12–21)

[12] Abner sent messengers to David on his behalf and said,
“Whose is the land?”
And he also said,
“Make your agreement with me.
And my hand will be with you
to bring all Israel over to you.”
[13] David said,
“Good. I will make a treaty with you.
But I ask one thing of you:
You will not see my face
unless you first bring Michal, Saul’s daughter,
when you come to see my face.”
[14] David sent messengers to Ishbosheth, Saul’s son, and said,
“Give me my wife Michal,
whom I was given to marry for 100 foreskins of the Philistines.”
[15] So Ishbosheth sent for her
and took her from her husband, Paltiel the son of Laish.
[16] Her husband went with her.
He wept as he went and followed her to Bahurim.
Then Abner said to him,
“Go! Go back!”
And he went back.

> **What does this mean?**
> David demands Michal back. He had married her lawfully, but Saul had taken her away from him (1 Samuel 25:44). As Saul’s daughter she also strengthens David’s claim to the throne of all Israel.
> Here it says “100 foreskins,” as Saul had demanded. In 1 Samuel 18:27 David brought 200.
> Paltiel loves Michal. He runs after her weeping until Abner sends him away. It is a sad scene. No one asks what Michal wants. Once again she is pushed back and forth between men.

[17] Abner talked with the elders of Israel and said,
“For a long time you have wanted David to be king over you.
[18] Now then, do it!
For the LORD has spoken about David and said,
‘By the hand of my servant David
I will save my people Israel from the hand of the Philistines
and from the hand of all their enemies.’”
[19] Abner also spoke to the people of Benjamin.
Then Abner also went to Hebron to tell David everything
that seemed good to Israel and to the whole house of Benjamin.
[20] So Abner came to David at Hebron, and 20 men with him.
David made a feast for Abner and the men who were with him.
[21] Abner said to David,
“I will get up and go
and gather all Israel to my lord the king.
Then they can make a covenant with you,
and you can rule over everything your heart desires.”
So David sent Abner away,
and he went in peace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abner prepares everything. He talks with the elders in the north, and especially with the tribe of Benjamin, Saul’s own tribe. Without Benjamin it would have been hard to come to an agreement.
> David welcomes Abner in a friendly way, even though he was his enemy for years. Three times the text stresses: he went “in peace.” David wants to make peace, not to take revenge.

---

## Joab murders Abner (Verses 22–30)

[22] Then David’s servants and Joab came back from a raid
and brought a great amount of plunder with them.
But Abner was no longer with David in Hebron,
for David had sent him away, and he had gone in peace.
[23] When Joab and the whole army that was with him arrived,
people told Joab,
“Abner the son of Ner came to the king.
And the king has sent him away, and he has gone in peace.”
[24] Then Joab came to the king and said,
“What have you done?
Look, Abner came to you.
Why have you sent him away, so that he is already gone?
[25] You know Abner the son of Ner.
He came to deceive you,
and to find out about your going out and your coming in,
and to find out everything you do.”
[26] When Joab had left David,
he sent messengers after Abner.
They brought him back from the well of Sirah.
But David did not know it.
[27] When Abner had come back to Hebron,
Joab took him aside into the middle of the gate
to speak with him quietly.
And there he struck him in the body, so that he died,
because of the blood of his brother Asahel.
[28] Afterward, when David heard it, he said,
“I and my kingdom are forever innocent before the LORD
of the blood of Abner the son of Ner.
[29] Let it fall on the head of Joab and on all his father’s house.
May the house of Joab never be without someone who has a discharge, or who has leprosy,
or who leans on a staff, or who falls by the sword,
or who lacks bread.”
[30] So Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner,
because he had killed their brother Asahel at Gibeon in the battle.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joab is furious. He says that Abner is a spy. But his real reasons are different: he wants revenge for his brother Asahel. And maybe he is afraid that Abner will take away his place as army commander.
> Joab secretly brings Abner back and murders him in the gate of Hebron. That is a serious crime. Hebron was a city of refuge (Joshua 20:7), where no one could be killed without a trial. And Asahel had died in battle, not by murder. Abner had even warned him.
> David curses Joab and his family. A “discharge” and “leprosy” were illnesses that made a person unclean. To “lean on a staff” perhaps means being lame or weak.
> David declares in public: I have nothing to do with this. That was important, because otherwise the northern tribes would have thought that David had led Abner into a trap.

---

## David mourns for Abner (Verses 31–39)

[31] David said to Joab and to all the people who were with him,
“Tear your clothes,
put on sackcloth,
and mourn in front of Abner.”
Sackcloth is rough cloth that people wore when they mourned.
And King David walked behind the bier.
[32] They buried Abner in Hebron.
And the king lifted up his voice and wept at Abner’s grave.
And all the people wept.
[33] The king sang a song of mourning for Abner and said,
“Should Abner die as a fool dies?
[34] Your hands were not tied,
and your feet were not put in chains.
As a man falls before wicked people, so you fell.”
And all the people wept over him again.
[35] All the people came to urge David to eat bread while it was still day.
But David swore and said,
“May God do this to me, and even more,
if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down.”
[36] All the people noticed it,
and it pleased them,
just as everything the king did pleased all the people.
[37] So that day all the people and all Israel understood
that it was not the king’s will to kill Abner the son of Ner.
[38] The king said to his servants,
“Don’t you know that a prince and a great man has fallen today in Israel?
[39] Today I am weak, even though I am anointed king.
These men, the sons of Zeruiah, are too hard for me.
May the LORD pay back the evildoer according to his wickedness.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David even makes Joab mourn for Abner. He himself walks behind the coffin, weeps, and fasts.
> His short song of mourning says: Abner was not a criminal who was tied up and executed. He was free and did not suspect anything. He was murdered by treachery.
> The people see David’s grief and believe him. Through this David also wins trust in the north.
> But David admits: I am too weak against Joab and Abishai. He does not punish Joab. Joab is too powerful and too important for the army. That is a weakness of David that will later bring much more suffering. Only shortly before his death does David tell his son Solomon to settle accounts with Joab (1 Kings 2:5–6).
