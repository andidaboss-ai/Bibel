# 2 Samuel – Chapter 7
### God’s promise to David

---

## David wants to build a house for God (Verses 1–3)

[1] The king was living in his house,
and the LORD had given him rest from all his enemies all around.
[2] Then the king said to Nathan the prophet,
“Look now, I live in a house of cedar,
but the ark of God lives inside tent curtains.”
[3] Nathan said to the king,
“Go, do everything that is in your heart,
for the LORD is with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David now has a palace. He has peace. And he thinks: it is not right that I live in a beautiful house while God’s ark stands only in a tent. He wants to build a temple for God.
> Here the prophet Nathan appears for the first time. He will become very important in David’s life (Chapter 12).
> At first Nathan says without thinking long: go ahead! He gives his own opinion. But he has not yet asked God.

---

## God’s answer through Nathan (Verses 4–17)

[4] That same night the word of the LORD came to Nathan:
[5] “Go and tell my servant David,
‘The LORD says:
“Should you build me a house for me to live in?
[6] For I have not lived in a house
since the day I brought the people of Israel up out of Egypt, even to this day.
Instead I have moved around in a tent and in a dwelling place.
[7] In all the places where I have walked with all the people of Israel,
did I ever say a word to anyone from the tribes of Israel
whom I commanded to be shepherd of my people Israel?
Did I ever say, ‘Why have you not built me a house of cedar?’”’

[8] So now tell my servant David this:
‘The LORD of Armies says:
“I took you from the sheep pen, from following the sheep,
to be prince over my people, over Israel.
[9] I have been with you wherever you went,
and I have cut off all your enemies in front of you.
I will make your name great,
like the name of the great ones on the earth.
[10] I will choose a place for my people Israel
and I will plant them,
so that they can live in their own place and not be moved anymore.
Wicked people will not oppress them anymore, as at the beginning,
[11] and as from the day I commanded judges to be over my people Israel.
I will give you rest from all your enemies.
And the LORD tells you that the LORD will make you a house.

[12] When your days are over and you die and join your ancestors,
I will raise up your descendant after you,
who will come from your own body,
and I will make his kingdom firm.
[13] He will build a house for my name,
and I will make the throne of his kingdom firm forever.
[14] I will be his father, and he will be my son.
If he does wrong, I will correct him with the rod of men
and with the blows of human beings.
[15] But my loving kindness will not leave him,
as I took it away from Saul, whom I removed before you.
[16] Your house and your kingdom will be made sure forever before you.
Your throne will be made firm forever.”’”
[17] Nathan told David all these words, just as he had seen them in this vision.

> **What does this mean?**
> God says no to David’s plan. But it is a loving no, with a much bigger yes.
> God says: I have never needed a house. I have moved around with my people, in a tent. I am not tied to one place.
> Then comes a play on the word “house”: David wanted to build God a “house,” a temple. But God says: you will not build me a house. I will build you a “house,” that is, a family, a royal family that will last forever.
> This is one of the most important promises in the whole Bible. It is called the “covenant with David.”
> The descendant who will build the temple is first of all Solomon, David’s son. He builds the temple in Jerusalem (1 Kings 6).
> “I will be his father, and he will be my son”: the king is to have a special, close relationship with God. If he does wrong, God will punish him, but God will not leave him as he left Saul. God’s loving kindness remains.
> “Forever”: David’s royal line lasted in Jerusalem for about 400 years. Then Jerusalem was destroyed by the Babylonians, and there was no king anymore. But the people of Israel held on to God’s promise. From this grew the hope for the Messiah, the Anointed One, a descendant of David who will rule forever (Isaiah 9:7; Psalm 89).
> Christians believe that this promise was fulfilled in Jesus. In the Gospel of Luke the angel says to Mary about Jesus: “The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David. … There will be no end to his Kingdom” (Luke 1:32–33). The letter to the Hebrews applies verse 14 to Jesus (Hebrews 1:5).

## David’s prayer (Verses 18–29)

[18] Then King David went in, sat before the LORD, and said,
“Who am I, Lord GOD,
and what is my house, that you have brought me this far?
[19] And this was still a small thing in your eyes, Lord GOD.
You have also spoken about your servant’s house for a long time to come.
And this is the way of people, Lord GOD!
[20] What more can David say to you?
For you know your servant, Lord GOD.
[21] For the sake of your word, and as your own heart wanted, you have done all these great things,
to let your servant know it.
[22] That is why you are great, LORD God.
For there is no one like you,
and there is no God besides you,
according to everything we have heard with our ears.
[23] What one nation on the earth is like your people, like Israel?
God went to rescue them for himself as a people,
to make a name for himself,
and to do great things for you and awesome things for your land,
in front of your people, whom you rescued for yourself out of Egypt,
from the nations and their gods.
[24] You made your people Israel your own people forever.
And you, LORD, became their God.

[25] “Now, LORD God,
the word that you have spoken about your servant and about his house,
confirm it forever, and do as you have said.
[26] Let your name be great forever, so that people say,
‘The LORD of Armies is God over Israel.
And the house of your servant David will be made firm before you.’
[27] For you, LORD of Armies, the God of Israel,
have shown your servant and said,
‘I will build you a house.’
That is why your servant has found the courage in his heart to pray this prayer to you.
[28] “Now, Lord GOD, you are God, and your words are truth,
and you have promised this good thing to your servant.
[29] So now, may it please you to bless the house of your servant,
so that it may continue forever before you.
For you, Lord GOD, have spoken it.
Let the house of your servant be blessed forever with your blessing.”

> **What does this mean?**
> David is overwhelmed. He sits down before God and prays. The prayer begins with a humble question: “Who am I?” I was a shepherd. And you have made me king and promised me a future that lasts forever.
> David wanted to do something for God. But he learns: God does much more for him. That is what grace is: God gives before we can give.
> “This is the way of people” in verse 19 is hard to understand in Hebrew. Maybe David means: this is how people talk with each other, but you, God, talk to me like this!
> David does not only praise God for himself, but for the whole people of Israel. God rescued this people out of Egypt.
> Verse 23 is a little difficult in Hebrew. It switches between “you” as one person and “you” as many. There is a similar text in 1 Chronicles 17:21.
> At the end David asks: do what you have promised. That is a good way to pray: we take God at his word.
