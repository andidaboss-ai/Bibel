# Amos – Chapter 5
### Let justice roll on like rivers

---

## A song of mourning for Israel (Verses 1–3)

[1] Listen to this word, house of Israel.
I sing it as a song of mourning over you:
[2] “The virgin Israel has fallen.
She will not get up again.
She lies thrown down on her land.
There is no one to lift her up.”
[3] For the Lord GOD says:
“The city that sent out 1,000 soldiers
will have 100 left.
The city that sent out 100
will have 10 left
for the house of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Amos sings a funeral song, as if Israel had already died. The people are still in the middle of their wealth. But Amos already sees the end coming.
> The “virgin Israel” is a picture for the people as a young woman who dies before her life has really begun.
> Verse 3: Only one tenth will survive. That is how terrible the war will be.

---

## Seek me, and you will live (Verses 4–9)

[4] For the LORD says to the house of Israel:
“Seek me, and you will live!
[5] But do not seek Bethel.
Do not go to Gilgal.
And do not cross over to Beersheba.
For Gilgal will surely be taken away as prisoners,
and Bethel will come to nothing.
[6] Seek the LORD, and you will live.
Otherwise he will break out like a fire in the house of Joseph.
It will burn it up,
and there will be no one in Bethel to put it out.
[7] You turn justice into wormwood.
You throw righteousness down to the ground!
Wormwood is a very bitter plant.
Righteousness means: doing what is right.
[8] Seek him who made the Pleiades and Orion.
He turns the shadow of death into morning.
He makes the day dark as night.
He calls for the waters of the sea
and pours them out over the land.
The LORD is his name.
[9] He brings sudden destruction on the strong,
so that destruction comes on the fortress.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Seek me, and you will live!” In the middle of all the words of judgment, here is a simple, clear way: Turn to God!
> Verse 5: But not in the holy places of Bethel, Gilgal, and Beersheba. People pray and sacrifice a lot there. But God cannot be found there, because the worship goes together with injustice. You do not seek God in certain places. You seek him by living a just life.
> Verse 7: Wormwood is a very bitter plant. Justice should be sweet and good, but they have made it bitter.
> Verse 8: The Pleiades and Orion are groups of stars. God made the stars. He decides day and night. And this great God wants you to seek him.

---

## Injustice in the gate (Verses 10–13)

[10] They hate the one who speaks up against wrong in the city gate.
They hate the one who speaks honestly.
[11] You trample on the poor.
You take a tax of wheat from them.
That is why this will happen:
You have built houses of cut stone,
but you will not live in them.
You have planted beautiful vineyards,
but you will not drink their wine.
[12] For I know how many wrongs you have done
and how great your sins are.
You make life hard for people who do right.
You take bribes.
You turn the needy away in the courts.
[13] That is why a wise person keeps quiet in such a time,
for it is an evil time.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “gate” was the place in a city where court was held. The elders sat there and judged.
> Verse 10: Whoever tells the truth there and speaks up against injustice is hated.
> Verse 11: The rich take grain from the poor farmers as a tax. With it they build splendid stone houses for themselves. But they will not live in them for long.
> Verse 12: Bribes and unjust judgments. The poor do not get justice in court.
> Verse 13: “A wise person keeps quiet.” In such an evil time it is dangerous to tell the truth. Some also understand the sentence as bitter irony: Many people would rather stay quiet, even though they should speak. Amos himself does not stay quiet.

---

## Hate evil, love good (Verses 14–17)

[14] Seek what is good, and not what is evil,
so that you may live.
Then the LORD, the God of Armies, will be with you,
as you say he is.
[15] Hate what is evil.
Love what is good.
Make sure there is justice in the courts.
Maybe the LORD, the God of Armies,
will be kind to the rest of Joseph.”
[16] That is why the LORD, the God of Armies, the Lord, says:
“There will be crying in all the wide streets.
In all the streets they will say, ‘Oh no! Oh no!’
They will call the farmer to mourn,
and those who are skilled in songs of mourning to cry aloud.
[17] There will be crying in all the vineyards,
for I will pass through the middle of you,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Seek what is good, and not what is evil, so that you may live.” The people said: “God is with us.” Amos says: God is only with you if you seek what is good.
> Verse 15: A clear plan in three steps: Hate what is evil. Love what is good. Make sure there is justice in court. And then: “Maybe God will be kind.” Amos promises nothing, but he leaves room for hope.
> Verse 17: “I will pass through the middle of you.” This reminds us of the night in Egypt when God went through the land (Exodus 12:12). But this time it hits Israel itself.

---

## The day of the LORD is darkness (Verses 18–20)

[18] “How terrible it will be for you who wish for the day of the LORD!
Why do you long for the day of the LORD?
It is darkness, and not light.
[19] It is as if a man ran away from a lion,
and a bear met him.
Or he went into the house
and leaned his hand on the wall,
and a snake bit him.
[20] Won’t the day of the LORD be darkness, and not light?
Very dark, with no brightness in it?

> **What does this mean?**
> The Israelites were looking forward to the “day of the LORD.” They thought: On that day God will defeat our enemies and make us great.
> Amos says: Careful! For you this day will be dark, because you yourselves are unjust.
> Verse 19: A picture with dark humor: Someone barely escapes a lion. He runs right into a bear. He escapes that too and saves himself by going home. Tired, he leans against the wall, and then a snake bites him. There is no escape.

---

## I hate your feasts (Verses 21–27)

[21] I hate your feasts. I despise them.
I cannot stand your holy gatherings.
[22] Yes, even if you offer me your burnt offerings and grain offerings,
I will not accept them.
And I will not look at the peace offerings of your fattened animals.
[23] Take the noise of your songs away from me!
I will not listen to the music of your harps.
[24] But let justice roll on like rivers,
and righteousness like a mighty stream.
[25] “House of Israel, did you bring me sacrifices and offerings
during the forty years in the wilderness?
[26] You also carried the tent of your king
and the stand for your idols,
the star of your god,
which you made for yourselves.
[27] That is why I will make you go away as prisoners
to a land beyond Damascus,”
says the LORD, whose name is the God of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most famous texts in the Bible!
> Verses 21–23: God says with hard words: I hate your worship! Your feasts, your sacrifices, your songs and harps: I want none of it. Why? Because they are celebrated by people who at the same time treat the poor badly. Beautiful worship cannot make up for injustice.
> Verse 24: What God wants instead: “But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream.” Not just a drop now and then, but a stream that never dries up. Justice should flow through all of life.
> Martin Luther King Jr. quoted this sentence in 1963 in his famous speech “I Have a Dream,” in the struggle against racial segregation in the US. It is also written on the Civil Rights Memorial in Montgomery.
> Verse 25: A question: In the wilderness, in the time of your first love for God, was it about sacrifices? No, it was about faithfulness.
> Verse 26: A difficult verse. The Hebrew probably has the names of star gods there (Sakkuth and Kaiwan, gods of Assyria). The WEB translates them as “the tent of your king” and “the star of your god.” Stephen quotes this verse in Acts 7:42–43.
