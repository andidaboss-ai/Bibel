# Amos – Chapter 4
### And yet you did not come back to me

---

## You cows of Bashan (Verses 1–3)

[1] Listen to this word, you cows of Bashan,
you who live on the mountain of Samaria,
you who treat the poor badly,
you who crush the needy,
you who say to your husbands,
“Bring us drinks!”
[2] The Lord GOD has made a promise by his holiness:
“The days will come on you
when they will take you away with hooks,
and the last of you with fish hooks.
[3] You will go out through the holes in the wall,
each woman straight ahead.
And you will throw yourselves into Harmon,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bashan (east of the Sea of Galilee) was famous for its well-fed, strong cattle. Amos calls the rich women of Samaria “cows of Bashan”: well-fed and full, while others go hungry. They keep asking their husbands for drinks for their parties. And the money for it comes from using the poor.
> Important: Amos does not criticize women in general here. He criticizes rich people who live at the cost of the poor. The men are criticized just as harshly in other chapters (for example 6:1–6). And the picture is not an insult about how someone looks. It is about greed and injustice.
> Verses 2–3: Like cattle on hooks, they will be led away through holes in the destroyed city wall. Pictures from Assyria really show how prisoners were led away on hooks or ropes. No one knows where “Harmon” was.

---

## Religious, but unjust (Verses 4–5)

[4] “Go to Bethel, and sin!
Go to Gilgal, and sin even more!
Bring your sacrifices every morning,
and your tithes every three days.
A tithe is a tenth of what you earn. It was given to God.
[5] Offer a thank offering of bread made with yeast.
Announce your free gifts and brag about them.
For this is what you like to do, you people of Israel,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bitter irony: Amos sounds like a priest who invites people to worship. But he says, “Go to Bethel, and sin!” The eager worship in Bethel and Gilgal is sin, because it goes together with injustice in everyday life.
> Verse 5: They give gifts, but only to brag about them. “For this is what YOU like to do.” They care about their own reputation, not about God. Later Jesus warns: “So when you do merciful deeds, don’t sound a trumpet in front of you” (Matthew 6:2).

---

## Five times: And yet you did not come back to me (Verses 6–11)

[6] “I gave you clean teeth in all your cities.
There was no bread in all your towns.
And yet you did not come back to me,”
says the LORD.
[7] “I also held back the rain from you
when it was still three months until the harvest.
I let it rain on one city,
and I did not let it rain on another city.
One field got rain,
and the field where it did not rain dried up.
[8] So two or three cities stumbled to one city
to drink water.
But they did not get enough.
And yet you did not come back to me,”
says the LORD.
[9] “Many times I struck your gardens and your vineyards
with plant diseases that made them dry up and rot.
And swarms of locusts ate your fig trees and your olive trees.
And yet you did not come back to me,”
says the LORD.
[10] “I sent plagues among you, as I did in Egypt.
I killed your young men with the sword,
and I took away your horses.
I filled your noses with the stink of your camp.
And yet you did not come back to me,”
says the LORD.
[11] “I destroyed some of you,
as God destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah.
You were like a burning stick pulled out of the fire.
And yet you did not come back to me,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The same sad sentence comes five times: “And yet you did not come back to me.” You can feel God’s disappointment.
> Verse 6: “I gave you clean teeth” is a bitter expression: There was nothing to eat, so the teeth stayed clean. It means famine.
> Verses 7–8: The rain did not fall evenly. People had to stumble from city to city to find water.
> Verse 11: “You were like a burning stick pulled out of the fire.” They barely escaped. But even that did not make them think.
> This list explains disasters as wake-up calls from God. Important: This is the prophet’s explanation of one particular time. It does not mean that every drought, every plague, or every war today is a punishment from God for the people it hits. Jesus clearly speaks against this way of thinking (Luke 13:1–5). But a crisis can be a reason to think about your own life.

---

## Get ready to meet your God (Verses 12–13)

[12] “That is why I will do this to you, Israel.
Because I will do this to you,
get ready to meet your God, Israel!
[13] For look, he is the one who forms the mountains
and creates the wind.
He tells people what they are thinking.
He turns the morning into darkness
and walks on the high places of the earth.
The LORD, the God of Armies, is his name.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Get ready to meet your God, Israel!” That sounds like a threat, and it is one. But it is also a last invitation: You will meet God, whether you want to or not. Get ready!
> Verse 13: A song of praise to God’s greatness: He made the mountains and the wind. He even knows what people are thinking. Short songs of praise like this come several times in Amos (compare 5:8–9; 9:5–6).
