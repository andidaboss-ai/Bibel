# Amos – Chapter 9
### The fallen tent of David

---

## The fifth vision: No one escapes (Verses 1–6)

[1] I saw the Lord standing beside the altar.
He said,
“Strike the tops of the pillars,
so that the doorways shake.
Break them into pieces on the heads of all of them.
I will kill the last of them with the sword.
Not one of them will run away.
Not one of them will escape.
[2] Even if they dig down into Sheol (the world of the dead),
my hand will take them from there.
Even if they climb up to heaven,
I will bring them down from there.
[3] Even if they hide on the top of Carmel,
I will search for them and take them out from there.
Even if they hide from my sight at the bottom of the sea,
I will give an order to the sea snake there,
and it will bite them.
[4] Even if they are taken away as prisoners by their enemies,
I will give an order to the sword there,
and it will kill them.
I will keep my eyes on them
for evil and not for good.”
[5] For the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
is the one who touches the land, and it melts.
All who live in it will mourn.
The whole land will rise like the River
and sink again like the River of Egypt.
[6] He is the one who builds his rooms in the heavens.
He has set his dome over the earth.
He calls for the waters of the sea
and pours them out over the land.
The LORD is his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the fifth vision, God himself stands at the altar, probably in Bethel. The holy place that the people trusted in will be destroyed.
> Verses 2–4: There is no place where you can hide from God: not in the place of the dead and not in heaven, not on the mountain and not at the bottom of the sea. This reminds us of Psalm 139:7–10. There it is a comfort: If I go up to heaven, God is there too. Here it is a warning.
> Verses 5–6: Again a song of praise to God’s greatness, as in 4:13 and 5:8–9.

## Are you not like the Ethiopians? (Verses 7–10)

[7] “People of Israel,
are you not like the people of the Ethiopians to me?”
says the LORD.
“Didn’t I bring Israel up out of the land of Egypt,
and the Philistines from Caphtor,
and the Syrians from Kir?
[8] Look, the eyes of the Lord GOD
are on the sinful kingdom.
I will destroy it from the face of the earth.
But I will not destroy the house of Jacob completely,”
says the LORD.
[9] “For look, I will give the command.
I will shake the house of Israel among all the nations,
the way grain is shaken in a sieve.
But not even the smallest grain will fall to the ground.
[10] All the sinners among my people will die by the sword.
They are the ones who say,
‘Disaster will not catch up with us or meet us.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: One of the most amazing verses in the Bible! Israel thought it was something special, because God had freed it from Egypt. God says: You are not more important to me than the Ethiopians. The Hebrew says “Cushites.” This means the people of Cush, south of Egypt, who had dark skin. And I also led other peoples: the Philistines from Caphtor (probably Crete) and the Syrians from Kir.
> The message: God is the God of all peoples. He loves all people the same, no matter where they come from or what color their skin is. He holds the history of all peoples in his hand. Being chosen does not mean that you are worth more than others.
> Verse 8: But God will not destroy his people completely.
> Verse 9: The picture of the sieve: God shakes the people the way grain is sifted. The stones and the dirt fall through, but not one single good grain is lost. God knows each person.

## The tent of David will be set up again (Verses 11–15)

[11] “On that day I will set up again the tent of David that has fallen.
I will close up its cracks.
I will set up its ruins again.
I will build it as it was in the days long ago.
[12] Then they will take what is left of Edom
and all the nations that are called by my name,”
says the LORD, who does this.
[13] “Look, the days are coming,”
says the LORD,
“when the one who plows will catch up with the one who harvests,
and the one who treads grapes will catch up with the one who sows seed.
Sweet wine will drip from the mountains
and flow from the hills.
[14] I will bring my people Israel back from captivity.
They will rebuild the ruined cities and live in them.
They will plant vineyards and drink their wine.
They will also make gardens and eat their fruit.
[15] I will plant them on their land.
They will never again be pulled up out of their land
that I have given them,”
says the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> After so much judgment, the book ends with hope!
> Verse 11: The “tent of David” is the royal family of David. It has fallen down like a hut made of branches. But God will set it up again.
> Verse 12: It is remarkable: “all the nations that are called by my name.” Other peoples belong to God too. In the New Testament, James quotes these verses at the meeting of the apostles in Jerusalem. He uses them to show that non-Jews may also belong to the church of Jesus (Acts 15:15–17).
> Verse 13: A picture of unbelievable plenty: The harvest is so big that people are not yet finished harvesting when it is already time to plow again.
> Verse 14: The curses from Chapter 5 are turned around. There it said: “You will not live in them … you will not drink their wine” (5:11). Now it says: “They will rebuild the ruined cities and live in them. They will plant vineyards and drink their wine.”
> Verse 15: “They will never again be pulled up out of their land.” God’s last word is salvation.

## Looking back: What did we read in the book of Amos?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A shepherd as a prophet:** Amos was a simple herdsman and farmer from Tekoa, not a professional prophet. God sent him to the rich northern kingdom of Israel (7:14–15).
> **Justice for the poor:** The great theme of Amos is social justice. He accuses the rich: They sell “the needy for a pair of sandals.” They cheat with false scales, they twist justice in court, and they live in luxury while others go hungry.
> **No exception for God’s own people:** God judges not only the neighboring peoples, but especially Israel too. “I have chosen only you of all the families of the earth. That is why I will punish you for all your sins” (3:2).
> **Worship without justice:** “I hate your feasts. I despise them. … But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream” (5:21–24). Martin Luther King Jr. made these words a motto of the civil rights movement.
> **Seek me and live:** “Seek what is good, and not what is evil, so that you may live” (5:14).
> **A prophet who prays:** Amos speaks up for his people: “Lord GOD, forgive, please! How could Jacob stand? For he is small” (7:2).
> **God of all peoples:** “People of Israel, are you not like the people of the Ethiopians to me?” (9:7). God loves all peoples the same.
> **Hunger for God’s word:** God says: “I will send a hunger into the land. It will not be a hunger for bread, and not a thirst for water, but a hunger for hearing the words of the LORD” (8:11).
> **Hope at the end:** God will set up again the fallen tent of David (9:11).
> **For today:** Amos asks us: How do we treat poor and weak people? Do our faith and our everyday life fit together? Where is justice needed, in our town, our country, the world?
> **What comes next?** Next comes Obadiah, the shortest book of the Old Testament, only one chapter long. It is a word against Edom, which was glad about the disaster of its brother people.
