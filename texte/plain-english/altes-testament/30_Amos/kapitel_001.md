# Amos – Chapter 1
### The LORD roars from Zion

---

## Before we begin: Who was Amos?

Amos was not a professional prophet. He was a herdsman from Tekoa, a small town south of Bethlehem in Judah. He also grew sycamore figs (Amos 7:14). God called him away from his herds and sent him to the northern kingdom of Israel.
He worked around 760 BC, in the time of King Jeroboam II. It was a time of wealth: The rich built summer houses and winter houses with ivory. They held great feasts and brought many sacrifices. At the same time, the poor were used, cheated, and treated unfairly in court.
Amos is the great prophet of social justice. His most famous sentence is: “But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream” (Amos 5:24). Martin Luther King Jr. quoted this sentence in 1963 in his famous speech. In it, he spoke about his dream: Black people and white people should have the same rights.
The book has 9 chapters:
– Chapters 1–2: Words of judgment against the neighboring peoples and against Israel.
– Chapters 3–6: Speeches against injustice in Israel.
– Chapters 7–9: Five visions and a look ahead to a new beginning.
His name probably means “carried” or “burden.”

---

## Amos from Tekoa (Verses 1–2)

[1] These are the words of Amos.
He was one of the herdsmen of Tekoa.
He saw these words about Israel
in the days of Uzziah, the king of Judah,
and in the days of Jeroboam, the son of Joash, the king of Israel.
It was two years before the earthquake.
[2] He said:
“The LORD will roar from Zion.
He will let his voice be heard from Jerusalem.
The pastures of the shepherds will mourn,
and the top of Carmel will dry up.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “It was two years before the earthquake.” At that time there was an earthquake so great that people still remembered it centuries later (compare Zechariah 14:5). Archaeologists have found signs of a strong earthquake around 760 BC in several places.
> Verse 2: God “roars” like a lion. Amos was a shepherd. He knew the roar of lions that threatened his herds. When a lion roars, everyone is afraid. That is how shaking God’s voice is.
> Carmel was a very green, fertile mountain range. If even Carmel dries up, it is a disaster.

## Against Damascus (Verses 3–5)

[3] The LORD says:
“Damascus has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they have threshed Gilead with threshing tools made of iron.
[4] So I will send a fire into the house of Hazael.
It will burn up the palaces of Ben Hadad.
[5] I will break the bar on the gate of Damascus.
I will cut off the people who live in the valley of Aven,
and the one who holds the scepter in the house of Eden.
And the people of Syria will be taken away as prisoners to Kir,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a series of words of judgment begins against seven neighboring peoples, and then against Israel itself.
> “Three times, yes, four times” is a typical way of speaking. It means: The measure is more than full.
> Damascus was the capital of Aram (Syria). Hazael and Ben Hadad were kings of Aram.
> The crime: They “threshed Gilead with threshing tools made of iron.” Gilead is an area east of the Jordan. A threshing tool was a heavy board with iron spikes. People used it to thresh grain. This is a picture for unbelievable cruelty in war against people.
> Verse 5: A scepter is a staff that a king holds.

## Against Gaza and Tyre (Verses 6–10)

[6] The LORD says:
“Gaza has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they took away a whole community as prisoners
to hand them over to Edom.
[7] So I will send a fire on the wall of Gaza.
It will burn up its palaces.
[8] I will cut off the people who live in Ashdod,
and the one who holds the scepter in Ashkelon.
I will turn my hand against Ekron.
And the rest of the Philistines will die,”
says the Lord GOD.
[9] The LORD says:
“Tyre has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they handed over a whole community to Edom.
And they did not remember the agreement between brothers.
[10] So I will send a fire on the wall of Tyre.
It will burn up its palaces.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gaza, the other cities of the Philistines, and Tyre carried off whole villages. They sold the people as slaves to Edom. That was human trafficking on a large scale.
> Verse 9: In doing this, Tyre broke an “agreement between brothers,” a treaty with Israel (compare 1 Kings 5:12, where King Hiram of Tyre and Solomon made a covenant).
> It is remarkable: Here God also judges foreign peoples who do not believe in him. All people are responsible for cruelty, human trafficking, and breaking treaties. There is a conscience that all people have.

## Against Edom and Ammon (Verses 11–15)

[11] The LORD says:
“Edom has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For he chased his brother with the sword
and threw away all pity.
His anger raged all the time,
and he held on to his rage forever.
[12] So I will send a fire on Teman.
It will burn up the palaces of Bozrah.”
[13] The LORD says:
“The people of Ammon have sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they ripped open the pregnant women of Gilead
to make their own land bigger.
[14] So I will light a fire in the wall of Rabbah.
It will burn up its palaces.
There will be shouting on the day of battle
and a storm on the day of the whirlwind.
[15] Their king will be taken away as a prisoner,
he and his princes together,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Edom (the descendants of Esau) chased his “brother” Israel, without pity and with endless hate. Hate that never stops destroys.
> Verse 13: The people of Ammon killed pregnant women in a cruel way, only to make their land bigger. That is one of the worst war crimes. God sees this injustice, and he will judge it.
> Here the Bible clearly says: Violence against people who cannot defend themselves, especially against women and children, is a serious wrong, no matter who does it.
