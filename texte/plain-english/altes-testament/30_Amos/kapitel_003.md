# Amos – Chapter 3
### The lion has roared

---

## Precisely because you are chosen (Verses 1–2)

[1] Listen to this word that the LORD has spoken against you,
you people of Israel,
against the whole family that I brought up out of the land of Egypt.
He says:
[2] “I have chosen only you
of all the families of the earth.
That is why I will punish you for all your sins.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A surprising sentence! The Israelites probably thought: “We are God’s chosen people. So we are safe.” Amos turns this around: Precisely because you are chosen, precisely because you know God so well, you have a greater responsibility.
> Being chosen is not a privilege. It is a task. Jesus says something similar: “To whomever much is given, of him will much be required” (Luke 12:48).
> The Hebrew word for “chosen” actually means “known.” It describes a close, personal relationship.

---

## Nothing happens without a reason (Verses 3–8)

[3] Do two people walk together
if they have not agreed to meet?
[4] Will a lion roar in the bushes
when he has no prey?
Does a young lion cry out from his den
if he has caught nothing?
[5] Can a bird fall into a trap on the ground
if no trap has been set for it?
Does a trap spring up from the ground
when there is nothing to catch?
[6] When the trumpet sounds the alarm in a city,
are the people not afraid?
When disaster comes to a city,
has the LORD not done it?
[7] Surely the Lord GOD does nothing
without first showing his secret plan to his servants, the prophets.
[8] The lion has roared.
Who will not be afraid?
The Lord GOD has spoken.
Who can do anything but prophesy?

> **What does this mean?**
> Amos asks a series of questions from the everyday life of a shepherd and farmer. The answer is always: No! Everything has a cause.
> – Two people walk together because they have agreed to meet.
> – A lion roars because he has prey.
> – A trap snaps shut because something has stepped into it.
> And then comes the point (verse 8): I, Amos, speak because God has spoken. I cannot do anything else. Just as people are afraid when a lion roars, I must prophesy when God speaks. In this way Amos defends his task.
> Verse 6: “When disaster comes to a city, has the LORD not done it?” This is a difficult sentence. Here Amos means the disaster that God sends as judgment on Israel. He wants to say: It is no accident. It has a meaning. This verse must not be understood to mean that every disaster that happens to a person is a punishment from God (compare Luke 13:1–5; John 9:2–3).
> Verse 7: God does not act in secret. He warns beforehand through his prophets. He always gives a chance to turn back.

## Injustice in the palaces (Verses 9–12)

[9] Announce it in the palaces of Ashdod
and in the palaces in the land of Egypt. Say:
“Gather on the mountains of Samaria.
See how much unrest there is in the city
and how people are treated badly there.”
[10] “Yes, they do not know how to do what is right,”
says the LORD.
“They pile up stolen goods and loot in their palaces.”
[11] That is why the Lord GOD says:
“An enemy will overrun the land.
He will tear down your strong places,
and your palaces will be robbed.”
[12] The LORD says:
“A shepherd may save two legs or a piece of an ear
out of the mouth of the lion.
In the same way the people of Israel will be saved,
those who sit in Samaria in the corner of a couch
and on the silk cushions of a bed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: An unusual call: The foreign peoples (Philistines from Ashdod, Egyptians) are to come and watch how much injustice happens in Samaria. Even the foreign peoples would be amazed at it.
> Verse 10: “Yes, they do not know how to do what is right.” The rich in their palaces pile up wealth that they gained through robbery and violence.
> Verse 12: A bitter picture from the life of a shepherd: When a lion had killed a sheep, the shepherd had to show what was left as proof that he had not stolen it (compare Exodus 22:13). In the same way, only a pitiful remnant will be left of the rich Israelites who lie comfortably on their couches and silk cushions.

---

## Houses of ivory (Verses 13–15)

[13] “Listen, and speak as witnesses against the house of Jacob,”
says the Lord GOD, the God of Armies.
[14] “For on the day when I punish Israel for its sins,
I will also punish the altars of Bethel.
The horns of the altar will be cut off
and fall to the ground.
[15] I will strike the winter house together with the summer house.
The houses of ivory will be destroyed,
and the great houses will come to an end,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Bethel was the most important holy place in the northern kingdom. The “horns of the altar” were a place of safety (1 Kings 1:50). When they are cut off, there is no more safety.
> Verse 15: The rich had a winter house (in the warm Jordan valley) and a summer house (in the cool mountains). Their houses were decorated with ivory. Archaeologists have really found many beautiful ivory carvings from this time in Samaria.
> The message: Luxury that was gained at the cost of the poor does not last.
> For today: Amos asks us how our wealth is connected with the need of others, also around the world.
