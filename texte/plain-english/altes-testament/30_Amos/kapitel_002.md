# Amos – Chapter 2
### Judgment on Judah and Israel too

---

## Against Moab (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD says:
“Moab has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For he burned the bones of the king of Edom into lime.
Lime is a white powder.
[2] So I will send a fire on Moab.
It will burn up the palaces of Kerioth.
Moab will die in a great noise,
with shouting and with the sound of the trumpet.
[3] I will cut off the judge from among them.
And I will kill all its princes with him,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moab burned the bones of a dead king of Edom. That was a terrible insult to the dead. It is remarkable: Edom was no friend of Israel. And yet God takes this deed seriously. The dignity of a dead person must be respected, even the dignity of an enemy.

---

## Against Judah (Verses 4–5)

[4] The LORD says:
“Judah has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they have rejected the law of the LORD.
They have not kept his rules.
Their lies have led them the wrong way,
the same lies their fathers followed.
[5] So I will send a fire on Judah.
It will burn up the palaces of Jerusalem.”

> **What does this mean?**
> You can imagine how the listeners in Israel nodded in agreement at the words so far: “Yes, those evil neighboring peoples!” Now it is Judah’s turn, the brother kingdom in the south. Here, too, they may still be nodding.
> For Judah the charge is different: They rejected God’s law. Whoever knows God’s commandments has a greater responsibility.

---

## Against Israel (Verses 6–8)

[6] The LORD says:
“Israel has sinned three times, yes, four times.
So I will not take back its punishment.
For they sell the righteous for silver,
and the needy for a pair of sandals.
Righteous means: a person who does what is right.
[7] They trample the heads of the poor into the dust of the earth.
They deny justice to people who are treated badly.
A man and his father use the same young woman.
In this way they make my holy name unclean.
[8] They lie down beside every altar
on clothes they took from the poor as a pledge.
A pledge is something you give as a promise that you will pay back a debt.
In the house of their God they drink wine
that they took from people who had to pay a fine.

> **What does this mean?**
> And now, as the eighth people, it is Israel’s turn, the listeners themselves! The trap snaps shut. And the list is the longest.
> Israel’s crimes are not war crimes against strangers. They are wrongs within their own people, against their own poor:
> – Verse 6: Whoever cannot pay his debts is sold into slavery. Sometimes this happens because of a small amount, the price of a pair of sandals.
> – Verse 7: The poor are trampled into the dust. In court they do not get justice.
> – Verse 7: “A man and his father use the same young woman.” This is probably a young maid or servant in the house. Father and son use her sexually. She cannot defend herself, and she depends on them. Amos calls this making God’s name unclean. This is sexual abuse, and the guilt lies with the men who do it.
> – Verse 8: Poor people had to give their outer cloak as a pledge. The law said: The cloak must be given back before night, because it is the only blanket (Exodus 22:26–27). But the rich keep it and lie down on it during worship! And they drink wine that was paid for with unjust fines.
> For Amos it is clear: Worship and injustice do not go together.

## What I did for you (Verses 9–12)

[9] “And yet I destroyed the Amorite before them.
He was as tall as the cedars,
and he was as strong as the oaks.
But I destroyed his fruit above
and his roots below.
[10] I also brought you up out of the land of Egypt.
I led you forty years in the wilderness,
so that you could take the land of the Amorite.
[11] I chose some of your sons to be prophets,
and some of your young men to be Nazirites.
Isn’t this true, you people of Israel?”
says the LORD.
[12] “But you gave the Nazirites wine to drink.
And you commanded the prophets,
‘Don’t prophesy!’
To prophesy means: to speak a message from God.

> **What does this mean?**
> God reminds them of everything he has done for Israel: the rescue from Egypt, the guidance through the wilderness, the land.
> Verses 11–12: God also gave them people who were to be an example: prophets and Nazirites. Nazirites were people who had given themselves to God in a special way and did not drink wine (Numbers 6). But the people tempted the Nazirites to drink, and they told the prophets to be quiet. They did not want to hear uncomfortable voices.

## No one will escape (Verses 13–16)

[13] Look, I will crush you where you are,
the way a cart full of grain crushes the ground.
[14] The fast runner will not be able to flee.
The strong man will not keep his strength.
The mighty warrior will not save his life.
[15] The one who uses the bow will not stand firm.
The one who is fast on his feet will not escape.
The one who rides the horse will not save his life.
[16] And the bravest of the mighty warriors
will run away naked on that day,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> When the judgment comes, no strength, no speed, and no weapon will help. Even the bravest warrior will drop everything and run away naked.
> This came true when the Assyrians conquered the northern kingdom of Israel in the year 722 BC.
