# Amos – Chapter 6
### How terrible for those who feel safe

---

## How terrible for those who are sure of themselves (Verses 1–7)

[1] How terrible it will be for those who live at ease in Zion,
and for those who feel safe on the mountain of Samaria!
They are the important men of the first of the nations.
The house of Israel comes to them.
[2] Go over to Calneh, and look.
From there go to Hamath the great.
Then go down to Gath of the Philistines.
Are they better than these kingdoms?
Is their land bigger than your land?
[3] How terrible for you who push the evil day far away,
and yet bring the rule of violence near!
[4] You lie on beds of ivory
and stretch out on your couches.
You eat lambs from the flock
and calves from the middle of the stall.
[5] You play on the strings of the harp.
You make up musical instruments for yourselves, like David.
[6] You drink wine from bowls.
You rub yourselves with the best oils.
But they do not grieve over the ruin of Joseph.
[7] That is why they will now be the first to be taken away as prisoners.
The feasting and lazing around will come to an end.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it is the rich men’s turn, the upper class in Jerusalem (Zion) and in Samaria.
> Verse 1: They feel safe and carefree. They think they are “the important men of the first of the nations.”
> Verse 2: Amos says: Look at other cities (Calneh, Hamath, Gath) that have already fallen. You are not better than they are.
> Verses 4–6: A lively picture of luxury: beds of ivory, lying lazily on couches, eating only the most tender lambs and calves, making music, drinking wine from large bowls, taking care of themselves with expensive oils.
> Verse 6: The worst thing is not the luxury itself. It is the end of the verse: “… But they do not grieve over the ruin of Joseph.” To grieve means: to be very sad. The people are going to ruin, the poor are suffering, and they do not care.
> Verse 7: Whoever sits at the front of the table now will soon be the first to be taken away as a prisoner.
> For today: Amos asks whether we see the suffering of other people, or whether we make ourselves comfortable and look away.

## God hates pride (Verses 8–11)

[8] “The Lord GOD has made a promise by himself,”
says the LORD, the God of Armies:
“I hate the pride of Jacob.
I hate his strong places.
That is why I will hand over the city
with everything that is in it.
[9] And this will happen:
If ten men are left in one house,
they will die.
[10] Then a relative will come to carry out the bodies.
He is the one who will burn them.
He will bring the bodies out of the house.
He will ask the one who is in the farthest room of the house,
‘Is there anyone else with you?’
And he will say, ‘No.’
Then the relative will say, ‘Hush!
We must not say the name of the LORD.’
[11] For look, the LORD gives the command,
and the great house will be smashed to pieces,
and the little house into bits.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: A frightening scene after the disaster (maybe war or plague): In one house everyone has died. A relative comes to bring out the dead. He finds one last survivor who is hiding. And he whispers: “Hush! Don’t say God’s name!” They are afraid that God could send even more disaster.
> Verse 11: Great houses and little houses, rich and poor: Everyone is hit.

---

## Justice turned into poison (Verses 12–14)

[12] Do horses run on rocky cliffs?
Does anyone plow there with oxen?
But you have turned justice into poison,
and the fruit of righteousness into bitterness.
[13] You are happy about a thing of nothing.
You say,
‘Haven’t we taken horns for ourselves by our own strength?’
Horns are a picture of power.
[14] “For look, I will make a nation rise up against you, house of Israel,”
says the LORD, the God of Armies.
“They will treat you harshly
from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of the Arabah.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Two silly questions: Do horses run over rocks? Does anyone plow rocks? No, that is crazy. Just as crazy is turning justice into poison. And that is exactly what you are doing!
> Verse 13: There is probably a play on words here: Israel is happy about military victories. Many scholars think of the capture of the towns of Lo Debar (that sounds like “nothing”) and Karnaim (that means “horns”). They say: “We did this by our own strength!” Amos mocks them: You are happy about a “nothing.”
> Verse 14: God will send a nation (Assyria) that will oppress the whole land, from the north (Hamath) to the south (Arabah). That is exactly the area that King Jeroboam II had conquered (2 Kings 14:25).
