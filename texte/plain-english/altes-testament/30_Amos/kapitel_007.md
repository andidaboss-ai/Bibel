# Amos – Chapter 7
### Visions and a quarrel in Bethel

---

## Two visions: locusts and fire (Verses 1–6)

[1] This is what the Lord GOD showed me:
He was forming locusts
when the late crop was just starting to grow.
It was the late crop after the king’s harvest.
[2] When the locusts had finished eating the grass of the land,
I said,
“Lord GOD, forgive, please!
How could Jacob stand?
For he is small.”
[3] Then the LORD changed his mind about this.
“It will not happen,” said the LORD.
[4] This is what the Lord GOD showed me:
The Lord GOD called for judgment by fire.
It dried up the great deep sea,
and it would have burned up the land.
[5] Then I said,
“Lord GOD, stop, please!
How could Jacob stand?
For he is small.”
[6] Then the LORD changed his mind about this.
“This will not happen either,” said the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapters 7–9, Amos has five visions.
> Vision 1: Locusts eat the late crop. That was especially bad: The first harvest went to the king as a tax (“the king’s harvest”). The second harvest was what ordinary people had to live on.
> Vision 2: A huge fire dries up even the sea.
> Both times Amos speaks up for his people: “Lord GOD, forgive, please!” and “Lord GOD, stop, please! How could Jacob stand? For he is small.” And God listens to him: “It will not happen.”
> That is remarkable: Amos, the hard prophet of judgment, also prays for others. He loves his people. And God lets himself be moved by prayer. Prayer changes things.

---

## The third vision: the plumb line (Verses 7–9)

[7] This is what he showed me:
The Lord was standing beside a wall
that had been built with a plumb line.
He had a plumb line in his hand.
[8] The LORD said to me,
“Amos, what do you see?”
I said, “A plumb line.”
Then the Lord said,
“Look, I will put a plumb line in the middle of my people Israel.
I will not pass them by any more.
[9] The high places of Isaac will be left empty.
The holy places of Israel will be destroyed.
And I will rise up against the house of Jeroboam with the sword.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A plumb line is a weight on a string. Builders use it to check whether a wall stands straight. God measures his people with the plumb line. And it shows: The wall is crooked. Israel does not stand straight before God.
> This time Amos no longer prays for the people. God says, “I will not pass them by any more.” That means: He will not spare them any more.
> “Isaac” is another name for the people of Israel here.

## Amaziah against Amos (Verses 10–17)

[10] Then Amaziah, the priest of Bethel,
sent a message to Jeroboam, the king of Israel:
“Amos has made a secret plan against you
in the middle of the house of Israel.
The land cannot bear all his words.
[11] For Amos says,
‘Jeroboam will die by the sword,
and Israel will surely be taken away from its land as prisoners.’”
[12] Amaziah also said to Amos,
“You seer, go, flee to the land of Judah.
Earn your bread there,
and prophesy there.
[13] But don’t prophesy at Bethel any more.
For it is the king’s holy place,
and it is a royal house!”
[14] Then Amos answered Amaziah,
“I was not a prophet,
and I was not a prophet’s son.
I was a herdsman,
and I grew sycamore figs.
[15] The LORD took me away from following the flock.
The LORD said to me,
‘Go, prophesy to my people Israel.’
[16] So now listen to the word of the LORD.
You say,
‘Don’t prophesy against Israel,
and don’t preach against the house of Isaac.’
[17] That is why the LORD says:
‘Your wife will become a prostitute in the city.
Your sons and your daughters will be killed by the sword.
Your land will be measured with a line and divided up.
You yourself will die in an unclean land.
And Israel will surely be taken away from its land as prisoners.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Amaziah was the highest priest at the king’s holy place in Bethel. He reports Amos to the king: “Amos has made a secret plan against you … The land cannot bear all his words.”
> Verses 12–13: A seer is a prophet who sees things from God. Then Amaziah says to Amos: “You seer, go, flee to the land of Judah. Earn your bread there, and prophesy there.” That means: Make your money there by prophesying! And: “But don’t prophesy at Bethel any more. For it is the king’s holy place.” It is telling: He does not call it “God’s holy place,” but “the king’s holy place.” Religion serves politics.
> Verses 14–15: Amos’s famous answer: “I was not a prophet, and I was not a prophet’s son. I was a herdsman, and I grew sycamore figs.” That means: He is not a professional prophet. He does not earn money with it. And then: “The LORD took me away from following the flock.” Amos does not speak because he is paid for it. He speaks because God sent him.
> Verse 17: A hard word to Amaziah. It describes what happens to a priest’s family when enemies conquer the land: The men are killed. The women become victims of sexual violence and exploitation. The land is divided up. And he himself dies in exile. That is a terrible injustice of war. The wife here is a victim, not guilty.
