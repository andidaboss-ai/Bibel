# Amos – Chapter 8
### A hunger for God’s word

---

## The fourth vision: a basket of summer fruit (Verses 1–3)

[1] This is what the Lord GOD showed me:
a basket of summer fruit.
[2] He said,
“Amos, what do you see?”
I said, “A basket of summer fruit.”
Then the LORD said to me,
“The end has come for my people Israel.
I will not pass them by any more.
[3] On that day the songs of the temple will turn into crying,”
says the Lord GOD.
“There will be many dead bodies.
In every place they will throw them out in silence.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A play on words in Hebrew: “Summer fruit” is “qayits,” and “end” is “qets.” The two words sound almost the same. The ripe fruit at the end of summer becomes a sign: Israel is ripe for the end.
> Verse 3: The happy songs in the temple turn into songs of mourning. And at the end there is only silence. A shaking silence after the disaster.

---

## The cheaters at the market (Verses 4–8)

[4] Listen to this, you who want to swallow up the needy
and destroy the poor of the land.
[5] You say,
“When will the new moon feast be over,
so that we can sell grain?
And when will the Sabbath be over,
so that we can sell wheat at the market?
We will make the ephah small
and the shekel large.
We will cheat with false scales.
[6] We will buy the poor for silver,
and the needy for a pair of sandals.
And we will sell the trash along with the wheat.”
[7] The LORD has made a promise by the pride of Jacob:
“Surely I will never forget anything they have done.
[8] Won’t the land shake because of this?
Won’t everyone who lives in it mourn?
Yes, the whole land will rise like the River.
It will be stirred up and sink again,
like the River of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> A lively picture of dishonest traders:
> Verse 5: They do keep the Sabbath and the holidays, but only because they have to. They can hardly wait until the holiday is over and they can do business again.
> And this is how they do business: They make the ephah (the measure for grain) smaller, so that the customer gets less. They make the shekel (the weight for money) heavier, so that the customer pays more. And the scales are false. Cheating three times over!
> Verse 6: They even sell the trash from the grain to the poor. And whoever cannot pay his debts is sold as a slave.
> Verse 7: God does not forget this.
> For today: Cheating in business, using workers, charging too much interest, and taking advantage of the weak: God sees all of it.

---

## The sun goes down at noon (Verses 9–10)

[9] “And on that day this will happen,”
says the Lord GOD:
“I will make the sun go down at noon.
I will make the earth dark in broad daylight.
[10] I will turn your feasts into mourning,
and all your songs into songs of sorrow.
I will make all of you wear sackcloth around your waists.
I will make every head bald.
Sackcloth is rough cloth that people wore when they mourned.
I will make it like the mourning for an only son.
And its end will be like a bitter day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: The sun goes down in the middle of the day. Maybe Amos is thinking of an eclipse of the sun. In the year 763 BC there was a great eclipse of the sun. Texts from Assyria mention it.
> The Gospels report that when Jesus died, darkness came over the land at noon (Mark 15:33).
> Verse 10: The mourning will be as deep as the mourning of parents for their only child. That is the greatest pain anyone can imagine.

---

## A hunger for the word of the LORD (Verses 11–14)

[11] “Look, the days are coming,”
says the Lord GOD,
“when I will send a hunger into the land.
It will not be a hunger for bread,
and not a thirst for water,
but a hunger for hearing the words of the LORD.
[12] They will wander from sea to sea,
and from the north to the east.
They will run back and forth
to look for the word of the LORD,
but they will not find it.
[13] On that day the beautiful young women
and the young men will faint from thirst.
[14] Some make promises by the sin of Samaria.
They say, ‘As surely as your god lives, Dan,’
and, ‘As surely as the way to Beersheba lives.’
They will fall and never get up again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: An unusual hunger: not for bread, but for God’s word. The people did not want to listen to the prophets (compare 2:12; 7:12–13). Now God will be silent. And then they will notice how much they need God’s word. They will look everywhere and not find it.
> Jesus says: “People do not live by bread alone. They live by every word that comes out of God’s mouth” (Matthew 4:4; compare Deuteronomy 8:3).
> Verse 14: Dan and Beersheba were holy places in the far north and the far south. The people made promises by their idols and their pilgrim roads. But these idols cannot help.
> For today: Many people feel an emptiness inside, a “hunger” for meaning and for God, even when they have everything they need.
