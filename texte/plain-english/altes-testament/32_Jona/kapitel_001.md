# Jonah – Chapter 1
### Jonah runs away from God

---

## Before we begin: Who was Jonah?

The book of Jonah is different from the other books of the prophets. It has hardly any words of a prophet. Instead it tells an exciting story about a prophet.
A prophet Jonah, the son of Amittai, is also named in 2 Kings 14:25. He lived in the 8th century BC in the northern kingdom of Israel, in the time of King Jeroboam II.
Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, Israel’s most dangerous enemy. People were afraid of the Assyrians because they were so cruel. Later they destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel (722 BC).
And of all places, Jonah is to go to these enemies!
Many scholars think that the book was written down later. They read it as a story that teaches something. Others read it as a report of things that really happened. Jesus himself speaks about Jonah (Matthew 12:39–41).
In Judaism, the book of Jonah is read on the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), the highest holy day. For it is about turning back and forgiveness.
The big question of the book: May God show mercy even to the enemies? The book has 4 chapters.

---

## Jonah runs away (Verses 1–3)

[1] The word of the LORD came to Jonah, the son of Amittai. He said,
[2] “Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city.
Preach against it,
for their evil has come up before me.”
[3] But Jonah got up
to run away to Tarshish, away from the LORD.
He went down to Joppa
and found a ship going to Tarshish.
He paid the fare and went down into the ship,
to go with them to Tarshish,
away from the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> God sends Jonah to Nineveh. It lies in the east (in what is now Iraq, near Mosul). Jonah gets up and runs in exactly the opposite direction: to Tarshish, far in the west, probably in Spain. At that time it was the end of the known world.
> Why does Jonah run away? We only find out in Chapter 4: He knew that God is merciful, and he did not want the enemies to be saved.
> It stands out: Jonah always goes “down”: down to Joppa, down into the ship, later down into the bottom of the ship and down into the sea. Whoever runs away from God goes downward.
> Joppa is today’s Jaffa, a part of Tel Aviv.

---

## The storm (Verses 4–6)

[4] But the LORD sent a great wind on the sea.
There was a mighty storm on the sea,
so that the ship was about to break apart.
[5] Then the sailors were afraid.
Each man cried out to his own god.
They threw the cargo that was in the ship into the sea
to make the ship lighter.
But Jonah had gone down into the lowest part of the ship.
He was lying down and was fast asleep.
[6] So the captain of the ship came to him and said,
“What are you doing, sleeper?
Get up, call on your God!
Maybe your God will think of us,
so that we will not die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> You cannot run away from God (compare Psalm 139:7–10). God sends a storm.
> The sailors come from different peoples and pray to different gods. They do everything they can to save the ship.
> And Jonah? He is asleep. Of all people, the captain, a man who does not know God, has to tell the prophet: “Get up, call on your God!” These are almost the same words God had used to call him: “Get up, go!”

---

## The lot falls on Jonah (Verses 7–10)

[7] They all said to each other,
“Come! Let’s throw lots,
so that we can find out who is to blame for this disaster that has come on us.”
Throwing lots was a way of making a decision, a little like throwing dice.
So they threw lots,
and the lot fell on Jonah.
[8] Then they asked him,
“Please tell us, who is to blame for this disaster that has come on us?
What is your work?
Where do you come from?
What is your country?
What people do you belong to?”
[9] He said to them,
“I am a Hebrew.
I fear the LORD, the God of heaven,
who made the sea and the dry land.”
[10] Then the men were terribly afraid.
They said to him,
“What have you done?”
For the men knew that he was running away from the LORD,
because he had told them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The lot points to Jonah. The sailors question him.
> Verse 9: Jonah’s confession sounds faithful: “I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land.” But it does not fit together: He says he fears the God who made the sea. And at the same time he tries to run away from him across the sea.
> Verse 10: The sailors understand right away how dangerous this is: “What have you done?”

---

## Jonah is thrown into the sea (Verses 11–16)

[11] Then they said to him,
“What should we do to you,
so that the sea will become calm for us?”
For the sea was getting more and more stormy.
[12] He said to them,
“Pick me up and throw me into the sea.
Then the sea will become calm for you.
For I know that this great storm has come on you because of me.”
[13] But the men rowed as hard as they could
to get back to the land.
But they could not,
for the sea was getting more and more stormy against them.
[14] So they cried out to the LORD and said,
“Please, LORD, we beg you,
don’t let us die because of this man’s life.
Don’t hold us guilty for killing an innocent man.
For you, LORD, have done what you wanted.”
[15] Then they picked up Jonah and threw him into the sea.
And the sea stopped raging.
[16] Then the men were terribly afraid of the LORD.
They offered a sacrifice to the LORD
and made promises to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonah knows: It is my fault. He is ready to let them throw him into the sea. But it seems he is still not ready to turn back and pray.
> Verse 13: The sailors are amazingly kind: They do not want to sacrifice Jonah. They row as hard as they can to save him.
> Verse 14: Then they pray to the LORD, the God of Israel, and ask for forgiveness.
> Verse 16: In the end the sailors, who did not know God, fear the LORD. They offer a sacrifice and make promises. The irony of the story: The prophet who runs away brings people from other peoples to God, without even wanting to.
> The sailors show more kindness and more respect for God than the prophet. The book of Jonah shows again and again: God works even among people from whom you would not expect it.

---

## The huge fish (Verse 17)

[17] The LORD prepared a huge fish to swallow up Jonah.
And Jonah was in the belly of the fish for three days and three nights.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles this verse is Chapter 2, verse 1. That is why the verses in Chapter 2 are moved by one there.
> God does not let Jonah drown. He “prepares” a huge fish that saves him. The Bible says “fish,” not “whale.” The whale comes from later translations and pictures.
> “Three days and three nights”: Jesus compares this with his death and his resurrection. “For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish, so the Son of Man will be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth” (Matthew 12:40).
