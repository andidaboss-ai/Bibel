# Jonah – Chapter 4
### Jonah is angry, and God has pity

---

## Jonah is upset about God’s grace (Verses 1–4)

[1] But Jonah did not like this at all,
and he became angry.
[2] He prayed to the LORD and said,
“Please, LORD, isn’t this what I said
when I was still in my own country?
That is why I hurried to run away to Tarshish.
For I knew that you are a gracious and merciful God.
You are slow to become angry and full of loving kindness.
You change your mind about doing harm.
[3] So now, LORD, please take my life from me.
For it is better for me to die than to live.”
[4] The LORD said,
“Is it right for you to be angry?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now at last we find out why Jonah ran away. It was not because he was afraid of the Assyrians. It was because he knew: God is merciful. He will forgive them if they turn back. And Jonah did not want that. The enemies should be punished!
> Verse 2: Jonah quotes an old confession about God (Exodus 34:6; Joel 2:13). The beautiful thing about it: He complains about exactly what is most wonderful about God.
> Verse 3: Jonah would rather die than see God spare his enemies. He is bitter and in despair.
> Verse 4: God does not scold him. He asks a question: “Is it right for you to be angry?” He invites Jonah to think about it.
> If you yourself have thoughts that you would rather die: You are not alone. Talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## The vine and the worm (Verses 5–9)

[5] Then Jonah went out of the city
and sat down on the east side of the city.
There he made himself a shelter
and sat under it in the shade.
He waited to see what would happen to the city.
[6] The LORD God prepared a vine.
He made it grow up over Jonah,
so that it would give shade over his head
and take away his discomfort.
Jonah was very glad about the vine.
[7] But at dawn the next day, God prepared a worm.
It chewed on the vine,
so that the vine dried up.
[8] When the sun came up,
God prepared a hot east wind.
The sun beat down on Jonah’s head,
so that he felt weak.
He asked to die.
He said, “It is better for me to die than to live.”
[9] God said to Jonah,
“Is it right for you to be angry about the vine?”
He said,
“I am right to be angry, angry enough to die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonah waits outside the city. Maybe he still hopes that God will destroy the city after all.
> Again God “prepares” something, as with the fish in Chapter 1: this time a vine (a plant that grows fast; it is not certain which plant is meant; people often think of the castor oil plant), then a worm and a hot wind.
> Jonah is very glad about the shade. That is the only time in the book that he is glad. Then the plant dries up, and Jonah is in despair again.
> God uses the plant as a lesson. The explanation comes right away.

---

## Shouldn’t I have pity? (Verses 10–11)

[10] The LORD said,
“You feel sorry for the vine.
You did not work for it,
and you did not make it grow.
It came up in one night
and died in one night.
[11] So shouldn’t I feel sorry for Nineveh, that great city?
In it there are more than 120,000 people
who cannot tell their right hand from their left hand.
And there are also many animals.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s answer is a comparison: Jonah, you feel sorry for a plant that you did nothing for. Then shouldn’t I feel sorry for a whole city full of people whom I created?
> “Who cannot tell their right hand from their left hand”: Maybe this means children, or people who do not know any better. God looks at them with mercy.
> “And there are also many animals”: God cares even about the animals.
> The book ends with a question. We do not find out what Jonah answered. The question is meant for us readers: Are we glad when God shows mercy to people we do not like, even to our enemies?

## Looking back: What did we read in the book of Jonah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A prophet runs away:** Jonah is supposed to go to Nineveh, the capital of the enemies, and he runs away in the other direction (Chapter 1).
> **You cannot run away from God:** Storm, lot, sea, the huge fish: God does not let go of Jonah, but he does not let him drown either.
> **Prayer from the depths:** In the belly of the fish, Jonah prays: “Salvation belongs to the LORD” (2:9).
> **A second chance:** God calls Jonah a second time (3:1).
> **The enemies turn back:** The sailors who did not know God and the whole city of Nineveh turn to God. And God has mercy.
> **God’s mercy upsets Jonah:** “For I knew that you are a gracious and merciful God” (4:2).
> **God loves all people:** “So shouldn’t I feel sorry for Nineveh, that great city?” (4:11). God’s love is for all peoples, even for enemies.
> **Jonah and Jesus:** Jesus speaks about the “sign of Jonah”: As Jonah was in the fish for three days, he will be in the grave for three days (Matthew 12:39–41).
> **For today:** Whom do I not want to receive God’s grace? Where am I running away from what God wants from me? And: God gives second chances, to me too.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Micah. He lived in the time of Isaiah and spoke up for the poor. The famous sentence “Human being, he has shown you what is good” comes from him (Micah 6:8). And he announces that a ruler will come from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2; in some Bibles 5:1).
