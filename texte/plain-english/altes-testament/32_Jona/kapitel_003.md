# Jonah – Chapter 3
### Nineveh turns back

---

## The second chance (Verses 1–4)

[1] The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time. He said,
[2] “Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city.
Preach to it the message that I give you.”
[3] So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh,
as the LORD had told him.
Nineveh was a very great city.
It took three days to walk across it.
[4] Jonah began to go into the city.
He walked for one day.
He cried out and said,
“In forty days, Nineveh will be destroyed!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “A second time.” God gives Jonah a second chance. He does not give up on people, not even when they have run away.
> This time Jonah obeys.
> Verse 3: “Three days”: Nineveh was huge. Maybe this means the whole area with the towns around it. Or it means the time you needed to visit all the streets and squares.
> Verse 4: Jonah’s sermon is very short. In Hebrew it is only five words. Not a word about turning back or about grace, only the announcement that the city will be destroyed.

---

## The whole city turns back (Verses 5–9)

[5] The people of Nineveh believed God.
They announced a fast
and put on sackcloth,
from the greatest to the least.
To fast means: to eat nothing for a time.
[6] The news reached the king of Nineveh.
He got up from his throne,
took off his royal robe,
covered himself with sackcloth,
and sat in ashes.
[7] He sent out a command through Nineveh.
It was the order of the king and his nobles:
“No person and no animal, no cattle and no sheep,
may taste anything.
They must not eat,
and they must not drink water.
[8] But people and animals
must be covered with sackcloth.
And they must cry out to God with all their strength.
Yes, everyone must turn away from his evil way
and from the violence that is in his hands.
[9] Who knows? Maybe God will turn and change his mind.
Maybe he will turn away from his burning anger,
so that we will not die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Something unexpected happens: The whole city believes God and turns back. From the poorest person to the king.
> Sackcloth and ashes were signs of mourning and of being sorry for wrongs.
> Verses 7–8: Even the animals are to fast and wear sackcloth. That sounds strange to us, almost funny. It shows how serious the whole city is.
> Verse 8: The king names the real problem: “Everyone must turn away from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands.” Turning back does not only mean fasting and praying. It also means: stopping the injustice.
> Verse 9: “Who knows?” The king has no guarantee. He simply hopes for God’s mercy.
> Jesus says later: The people of Nineveh turned back when Jonah preached (Matthew 12:41).

---

## God has mercy (Verse 10)

[10] God saw what they did.
He saw that they turned away from their evil way.
Then God changed his mind about the disaster he had said he would bring on them.
And he did not do it.

> **What does this mean?**
> God sees that the people turn back, and he spares the city.
> This shows: God’s words of judgment are often warnings. They are meant to make people turn back. When people turn back, God is ready not to let the disaster come (compare Jeremiah 18:7–8).
> God does not “change his mind” because of a mood. He stays true to himself: He is merciful, and he wants people to live.
> It is remarkable: Here it is Israel’s enemies who turn back. God’s mercy is for them too.
