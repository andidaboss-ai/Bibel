# 1 Kings – Chapter 8
### The dedication of the temple

---

## The ark comes into the temple (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then Solomon gathered the elders of Israel,
all the heads of the tribes, the princes of the families of the children of Israel,
to King Solomon in Jerusalem,
to bring up the ark of the LORD’s covenant out of David’s city, which is Zion.
[2] All the men of Israel gathered to King Solomon at the feast,
in the month of Ethanim, which is the seventh month.
[3] All the elders of Israel came,
and the priests picked up the ark.
[4] They brought up the LORD’s ark,
the Tent of Meeting, and all the holy things that were in the Tent.
The priests and the Levites brought these up.
[5] King Solomon and the whole congregation of Israel, who had gathered to him,
were with him before the ark.
They sacrificed sheep and cattle, so many that they could not be counted or numbered.
[6] The priests brought the ark of the LORD’s covenant to its place,
into the inner room of the house, to the most holy place,
under the wings of the cherubim.
[7] For the cherubim spread their wings out over the place of the ark,
and the cherubim covered the ark and its poles from above.
[8] The poles were so long
that the ends of the poles could be seen from the holy place in front of the inner room.
But they could not be seen from outside.
They are there to this day.
[9] There was nothing in the ark except the two stone tablets
that Moses put there at Horeb,
when the LORD made a covenant with the children of Israel,
when they came out of the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month of Ethanim is about September to October. “The feast” is the Feast of Tabernacles, the great feast in the fall. At this feast people remembered how God had gone with Israel in the wilderness.
> The ark is carried from the fortress of Zion, where David had brought it (2 Samuel 6), up into the new temple. This time it is carried the right way, by the priests.
> The ark is placed under the wings of the cherubim, in the most holy place. From now on, this is the holiest place in Israel.
> In the ark there were only the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments. At the heart of the temple there is not gold, but God’s word and his covenant.
> “To this day”: when this text was written, the temple was probably still standing. Later, when Jerusalem was destroyed, the ark was lost. No one knows what happened to it.
> Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai.

## The cloud fills the house (Verses 10–13)

[10] When the priests had come out of the holy place,
the cloud filled the LORD’s house,
[11] so that the priests could not stand to do their service because of the cloud.
For the glory of the LORD filled the LORD’s house.
[12] Then Solomon said,
“The LORD has said that he would live in the thick darkness.
[13] I have surely built you a house to live in,
a place for you to live in forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point: God himself accepts the house. The cloud, the sign of his presence, fills the temple. It was the same with the Tabernacle in the wilderness (Exodus 40:34–35).
> God’s glory is so strong that even the priests cannot stay.
> “Live in the thick darkness”: God is a mystery. No one can see him. At Mount Sinai, too, God was in a thick, dark cloud (Exodus 20:21). The most holy place had no windows.

---

## Solomon blesses the people (Verses 14–21)

[14] The king turned his face around
and blessed the whole assembly of Israel.
And the whole assembly of Israel was standing.
[15] He said,
“Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
who spoke with his mouth to David your father
and has fulfilled it with his hand.
He said,
[16] ‘Since the day that I brought my people Israel out of Egypt,
I did not choose any city out of all the tribes of Israel to build a house,
so that my name might be there.
But I chose David to be over my people Israel.’
[17] It was in the heart of my father David
to build a house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.
[18] But the LORD said to my father David,
‘It was in your heart to build a house for my name.
You did well that it was in your heart.
[19] But you will not build the house.
Your son, who will come from your own body,
he will build the house for my name.’
[20] The LORD has kept the word that he spoke.
For I have taken the place of my father David,
and I sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised.
And I have built the house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.
[21] There I have set a place for the ark,
in which is the covenant of the LORD
that he made with our fathers
when he brought them out of the land of Egypt.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon says again and again: God has kept his word. What he promised David, he has fulfilled.
> Verse 18 is beautiful: God praises David for the good wish in his heart, even though he was not allowed to carry it out himself. God already sees the good intention.

## Solomon’s prayer: God, listen! (Verses 22–30)

[22] Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD,
in front of the whole assembly of Israel,
and spread out his hands toward heaven.
[23] He said,
“LORD, the God of Israel,
there is no God like you, in heaven above or on earth below.
You keep covenant and loving kindness with your servants
who walk before you with all their heart.
[24] You have kept for your servant David my father what you promised him.
Yes, you spoke with your mouth and have fulfilled it with your hand, as it is today.
[25] And now, LORD, God of Israel,
keep for your servant David my father what you promised him when you said,
‘You will never be without a man before me to sit on the throne of Israel,
if only your children are careful how they live,
to walk before me as you have walked before me.’
[26] And now, God of Israel, please let your word come true,
which you spoke to your servant David my father.

[27] But will God really live on the earth?
Heaven and the highest heaven cannot hold you,
how much less this house that I have built!
[28] Yet turn to the prayer of your servant and to his request, LORD my God,
and listen to the cry and the prayer that your servant prays before you today.
[29] May your eyes be open toward this house night and day,
toward the place of which you have said, ‘My name will be there.’
Listen to the prayer that your servant prays toward this place.
[30] Listen to the request of your servant and of your people Israel
when they pray toward this place.
Yes, hear in heaven, the place where you live.
And when you hear, forgive.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon prays with his hands spread out toward heaven. In those days that was a common way to stand when praying.
> Verse 27 is one of the most important sentences of the prayer: God is so great that not even heaven can hold him. How then could a house of stone? Solomon knows: the temple is not God’s prison. God is everywhere. But the temple is a place where people can meet God and pray to him.
> The most important word in the whole prayer is: “Listen.” And then: “Forgive.” Solomon knows that people become guilty again and again. That is why the temple is above all a place of forgiveness.
> To this day, Jews all over the world pray facing toward Jerusalem.

## Seven requests for the people (Verses 31–53)

[31] If someone sins against his neighbor
and is made to take an oath,
and he comes and swears before your altar in this house,
[32] then hear in heaven and act,
and judge your servants:
condemn the guilty one and bring what he has done on his own head.
And declare the righteous one innocent and give him what his righteousness deserves.

[33] When your people Israel are defeated by the enemy
because they have sinned against you,
if they turn back to you and confess your name,
and pray and make their request to you in this house,
[34] then hear in heaven
and forgive the sin of your people Israel,
and bring them back to the land that you gave to their fathers.

[35] When the sky is shut and there is no rain
because they have sinned against you,
if they pray toward this place and confess your name
and turn from their sin when you bring them low,
[36] then hear in heaven
and forgive the sin of your servants and of your people Israel,
when you teach them the good way in which they should walk.
And send rain on your land, which you have given to your people as their inheritance.

[37] If there is famine in the land,
if there is plague, if there is blight, mildew, locusts, or caterpillars,
if their enemy surrounds them in the land of their cities,
whatever trouble, whatever sickness there is,
[38] whatever prayer and request
is made by any person or by all your people Israel,
who each know the trouble of his own heart
and spread out his hands toward this house,
[39] then hear in heaven, the place where you live,
and forgive and act,
and give to each one according to all his ways,
because you know his heart.
For you, and you alone, know the hearts of all people.
[40] Do this so that they may fear you all the days that they live in the land that you gave to our fathers.

[41] And also the foreigner, who is not one of your people Israel,
when he comes from a far country because of your name
[42] – for they will hear of your great name,
of your mighty hand and of your outstretched arm –
when he comes and prays toward this house,
[43] then hear in heaven, the place where you live,
and do everything that the foreigner calls to you for,
so that all the peoples of the earth may know your name
and fear you, as your people Israel do,
and so that they may know that this house that I have built is called by your name.

[44] If your people go out to battle against their enemy,
by whatever way you send them,
and they pray to the LORD toward the city that you have chosen
and toward the house that I have built for your name,
[45] then hear in heaven their prayer and their request,
and stand up for their cause.

[46] If they sin against you
– for there is no one who doesn’t sin –
and you are angry with them and hand them over to the enemy,
so that they carry them away as captives to the land of the enemy, far away or near,
[47] and if they then come to their senses in the land where they have been carried as captives,
and turn back and make their request to you in the land of those who carried them away,
and say,
‘We have sinned and have done wrong. We have acted wickedly,’
[48] if they return to you with all their heart and with all their soul
in the land of their enemies who carried them away,
and pray to you toward their land that you gave to their fathers,
toward the city that you have chosen,
and toward the house that I have built for your name,
[49] then hear their prayer and their request in heaven, the place where you live,
and stand up for their cause.
[50] And forgive your people who have sinned against you,
and all the wrongs they have done against you.
And let them find compassion with those who carried them away,
so that these will have compassion on them.
[51] For they are your people and your inheritance,
whom you brought out of Egypt, out of the middle of the furnace for iron.
[52] May your eyes be open to the request of your servant
and to the request of your people Israel,
so that you listen to them whenever they cry out to you.
[53] For you set them apart from all the peoples of the earth to be your inheritance,
as you spoke through your servant Moses
when you brought our fathers out of Egypt, Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon prays for many situations in which people in need pray to God: in a dispute in court, in a defeat in war, in a drought, in famine and sickness, and even as captives in a foreign land.
> Again and again: “Hear in heaven and forgive.” Solomon knows: “There is no one who doesn’t sin” (verse 46). That is why everyone needs God’s forgiveness.
> Verse 38: God is to hear the prayer of those “who each know the trouble of his own heart.” God also hears the very personal prayer of a single person.
> The request for the foreigner is especially beautiful (verses 41–43): people from other nations should also be allowed to pray to God, and God should answer them. The temple is not only there for Israel. Later Jesus quoted the prophet Isaiah: “My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations” (Mark 11:17; Isaiah 56:7).
> The last request (verses 46–53) sounds like a sense of what was coming: what if Israel is taken captive? That is exactly what happened later, in the exile to Babylon. There Daniel prayed three times a day with his windows open toward Jerusalem (Daniel 6:10). For the captives these verses were a great hope: even far away, God hears us.
> “The furnace for iron” is a picture of the hard slavery in Egypt.

## Solomon’s blessing (Verses 54–61)

[54] When Solomon had finished praying all this prayer and request to the LORD,
he got up from in front of the LORD’s altar,
where he had been kneeling on his knees with his hands spread out toward heaven.
[55] He stood and blessed the whole assembly of Israel with a loud voice, saying,
[56] “Blessed be the LORD,
who has given rest to his people Israel, just as he promised.
Not one word has failed of all his good promise
that he promised through his servant Moses.
[57] May the LORD our God be with us as he was with our fathers.
May he not leave us or abandon us,
[58] so that he may turn our hearts to himself,
to walk in all his ways,
and to keep his commandments, his statutes, and his ordinances,
which he commanded our fathers.
[59] And may these words of mine, with which I have made my request before the LORD,
be near to the LORD our God day and night,
so that he may stand up for the cause of his servant and the cause of his people Israel,
as each day needs,
[60] so that all the peoples of the earth may know
that the LORD himself is God. There is no one else.
[61] So let your heart be fully devoted to the LORD our God,
to walk in his statutes and to keep his commandments, as it is today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon had been kneeling while he prayed. Now he stands up and blesses the people.
> “Not one word has failed of all his good promise”: that is a great confession of faith. God keeps what he promises. You can rely on his word.
> Solomon asks: may God turn our hearts to himself. He knows: we cannot stay faithful to God by our own strength. We need God’s help for that.
> At the end comes the warning: “Let your heart be fully devoted to the LORD our God.” Sadly, it is Solomon himself who will not follow this warning later (1 Kings 11:4).

## The great feast (Verses 62–66)

[62] The king, and all Israel with him, offered sacrifices before the LORD.
[63] As peace offerings, which he offered to the LORD, Solomon offered
22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep.
So the king and all the children of Israel dedicated the LORD’s house.
[64] On the same day the king made holy the middle of the court that was in front of the LORD’s house.
For there he offered the burnt offering, the grain offering, and the fat of the peace offerings.
For the bronze altar that was before the LORD
was too small to hold the burnt offering, the grain offering, and the fat of the peace offerings.
[65] So Solomon held the feast at that time,
and all Israel with him, a great assembly,
from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt,
before the LORD our God,
seven days and seven more days, 14 days in all.
[66] On the eighth day he sent the people away.
They blessed the king
and went to their tents, joyful and glad in their hearts
for all the goodness that the LORD had shown to his servant David and to his people Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number of animals offered is huge. The meat of the peace offerings was eaten together. So it became a great feast for the whole people.
> “From the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt”: people came from the whole land, from the far north to the far south. Some Bibles call the place in the north “Lebo Hamath.”
> The feast lasted 14 days: seven days for the dedication and seven days for the Feast of Tabernacles.
> “On the eighth day”: this probably means the day after the second feast. 2 Chronicles 7:9–10 tells it more exactly.
> The people go home joyful. This is the high point of Israel’s history under the kings: God lives in the middle of his people, and everyone is thankful.
