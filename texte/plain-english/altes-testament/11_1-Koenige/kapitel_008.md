# 1 Kings – Chapter 8
### The dedication of the temple

---

## The ark comes into the temple (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then Solomon called the elders of Israel to come to him in Jerusalem.
He called all the heads of the tribes
and the leaders of the families of the people of Israel.
They were to bring up the ark of the LORD’s covenant out of David’s city.
David’s city is Zion.
[2] All the men of Israel came together to King Solomon at the feast.
It was in the month of Ethanim, the seventh month.
[3] All the elders of Israel came.
The priests picked up the ark.
[4] They brought up the LORD’s ark,
the Tent of Meeting, and all the holy things that were in the Tent.
The priests and the Levites brought these up.
[5] King Solomon and the whole community of Israel were gathered with him before the ark.
They sacrificed sheep and cattle.
There were so many that no one could count them.
[6] The priests brought the ark of the LORD’s covenant to its place.
They brought it into the inner room of the house, the most holy place.
They put it under the wings of the cherubim.
Cherubim are heavenly beings with wings.
[7] The cherubim spread out their wings over the place of the ark.
So the cherubim covered the ark and its poles from above.
[8] The poles were very long.
The ends of the poles could be seen from the holy place in front of the inner room.
But they could not be seen from outside.
They are there to this day.
[9] There was nothing in the ark except the two stone tablets.
Moses had put them there at Horeb.
That was when the LORD made a covenant with the people of Israel,
when they came out of the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month of Ethanim is about September to October. “The feast” is the Feast of Tabernacles, the great feast in the fall. At this feast people remembered how God had gone with Israel in the wilderness.
> The ark is carried from the fortress of Zion, where David had brought it (2 Samuel 6), up into the new temple. This time it is carried the right way, by the priests.
> The ark is placed under the wings of the cherubim, in the most holy place. From now on, this is the holiest place in Israel.
> In the ark there were only the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments. At the heart of the temple there is not gold, but God’s word and his covenant.
> “To this day”: when this text was written, the temple was probably still standing. Later, when Jerusalem was destroyed, the ark was lost. No one knows what happened to it.
> Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai.

---

## The cloud fills the house (Verses 10–13)

[10] The priests came out of the holy place.
Then the cloud filled the LORD’s house.
[11] Because of the cloud, the priests could not stand there to do their work.
The glory of the LORD filled the LORD’s house.
[12] Then Solomon said,
“The LORD has said that he would live in the thick darkness.
[13] I have built you a house to live in.
It is a place for you to live in forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point: God himself accepts the house. The cloud, the sign of his presence, fills the temple. It was the same with the Tabernacle in the wilderness (Exodus 40:34–35).
> God’s glory is so strong that even the priests cannot stay.
> “Live in the thick darkness”: God is a mystery. No one can see him. At Mount Sinai, too, God was in a thick, dark cloud (Exodus 20:21). The most holy place had no windows.

---

## Solomon blesses the people (Verses 14–21)

[14] The king turned around.
He blessed the whole assembly of Israel,
while the whole assembly of Israel stood there.
[15] He said,
“Praise the LORD, the God of Israel!
With his mouth he made a promise to David, your father.
With his hand he has done what he promised.
He said,
[16] ‘I brought my people Israel out of Egypt.
Since that day, I have not chosen a city in any of the tribes of Israel
to build a house where my name would be.
But I chose David to rule over my people Israel.’
[17] My father David wanted with all his heart
to build a house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.
[18] But the LORD said to my father David,
‘You wanted with all your heart to build a house for my name.
It was good that you wanted this.
[19] But you will not build the house.
Your own son will build the house for my name.’
[20] The LORD has kept the promise that he made.
I have taken the place of my father David.
I sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised.
And I have built the house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.
[21] There I have made a place for the ark.
In it is the covenant of the LORD.
He made it with our fathers when he brought them out of the land of Egypt.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon says again and again: God has kept his word. What he promised David, he has fulfilled.
> Verse 18 is beautiful: God praises David for the good wish in his heart, even though he was not allowed to carry it out himself. God already sees the good intention.

## Solomon’s prayer: God, listen! (Verses 22–30)

[22] Solomon stood in front of the altar of the LORD,
before the whole assembly of Israel.
He spread out his hands toward heaven.
[23] He said,
“LORD, God of Israel,
there is no God like you, in heaven above or on earth below.
You keep your covenant and your faithful love with your servants
who walk before you with all their heart.
[24] You have kept your promise to your servant, my father David.
Yes, you spoke with your mouth, and with your hand you have done it, as we see today.
[25] And now, LORD, God of Israel,
keep the promise you made to your servant, my father David.
You said to him,
‘There will always be a man from your family on the throne of Israel before me,
if only your children are careful how they live
and walk before me as you have walked before me.’
[26] And now, God of Israel, please let your word come true,
the word you spoke to your servant, my father David.

[27] But will God really live on the earth?
Look, the heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot hold you.
So this house that I have built can hold you even less!
[28] But LORD, my God, please turn to the prayer of your servant and to his plea.
Listen to the cry and the prayer that your servant prays before you today.
[29] May your eyes be open toward this house night and day.
You have said about this place, ‘My name will be there.’
Listen to the prayer that your servant prays toward this place.
[30] Listen to the plea of your servant and of your people Israel
when they pray toward this place.
Yes, listen in heaven, where you live.
And when you listen, forgive.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon prays with his hands spread out toward heaven. In those days that was a common way to stand when praying.
> Verse 27 is one of the most important sentences of the prayer: God is so great that not even heaven can hold him. How then could a house of stone? Solomon knows: the temple is not God’s prison. God is everywhere. But the temple is a place where people can meet God and pray to him.
> The most important word in the whole prayer is: “Listen.” And then: “Forgive.” Solomon knows that people become guilty again and again. That is why the temple is above all a place of forgiveness.
> To this day, Jews all over the world pray facing toward Jerusalem.

## Seven requests for the people (Verses 31–53)

[31] Someone may sin against his neighbor
and be made to swear an oath.
He may come and swear in front of your altar in this house.
[32] Then listen in heaven and act.
Judge your servants.
Find the guilty one guilty, and let what he did come back on his own head.
Find the one who did right innocent, and reward him for the right he has done.

[33] Your people Israel may lose a battle against an enemy
because they have sinned against you.
Then they may turn back to you and praise your name.
They may pray and plead with you in this house.
[34] Then listen in heaven.
Forgive the sin of your people Israel.
Bring them back to the land that you gave to their fathers.

[35] The sky may be shut, so that there is no rain,
because the people have sinned against you.
You may punish them.
Then they may pray toward this place and praise your name
and turn away from their sin.
[36] Then listen in heaven.
Forgive the sin of your servants and of your people Israel.
Teach them the good way in which they should live.
And send rain on your land.
You have given it to your people to own.

[37] There may be famine in the land.
There may be disease, or crops may dry up or get moldy.
There may be locusts or caterpillars.
An enemy may surround them in their land and their cities.
There may be any trouble or any sickness.
[38] Then any person or all your people Israel may pray and plead.
Each one may know the trouble of his own heart
and spread out his hands toward this house.
[39] Then listen in heaven, where you live.
Forgive and act.
Give each person what all his ways deserve.
You know his heart.
You alone know the hearts of all people.
[40] Then they will have respect for you all the days they live in the land that you gave to our fathers.

[41] A foreigner who is not one of your people Israel
may also come from a far country because of your name.
[42] People will hear of your great name,
of your strong hand and of your outstretched arm.
So a foreigner may come and pray toward this house.
[43] Then listen in heaven, where you live.
Do everything that the foreigner asks you for.
Then all the peoples of the earth will know your name.
They will have respect for you, as your people Israel do.
And they will know that this house that I have built is called by your name.

[44] Your people may go out to battle against their enemy,
wherever you send them.
They may pray to the LORD toward the city that you have chosen
and toward the house that I have built for your name.
[45] Then listen in heaven to their prayer and their plea.
Help them win their cause.

[46] They may sin against you.
There is no one who doesn’t sin.
You may be angry with them and hand them over to the enemy.
The enemy may take them away as prisoners to the enemy’s land, far away or near.
[47] Then they may come to their senses in the land where they are prisoners.
They may turn back and plead with you in the land of those who took them away.
They may say,
‘We have sinned and done wrong. We have done evil.’
[48] They may come back to you with all their heart and with all their soul
in the land of their enemies who took them away.
They may pray to you toward their land, which you gave to their fathers,
toward the city that you have chosen,
and toward the house that I have built for your name.
[49] Then listen in heaven, where you live, to their prayer and their plea.
Help them win their cause.
[50] Forgive your people who have sinned against you.
Forgive all the wrong things they have done against you.
Make those who took them away kind to them,
so that they show them mercy.
[51] They are your people.
They belong to you.
You brought them out of Egypt, out of the furnace for iron.
[52] May your eyes be open to the plea of your servant
and to the plea of your people Israel.
Listen to them whenever they cry out to you.
[53] You set them apart from all the peoples of the earth to belong to you.
You said this through your servant Moses
when you brought our fathers out of Egypt, Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon prays for many situations in which people in need pray to God: in a dispute in court, in a defeat in war, in a drought, in famine and sickness, and even as captives in a foreign land.
> Again and again: “Listen in heaven.” And: “Forgive.” Solomon knows: “There is no one who doesn’t sin” (verse 46). That is why everyone needs God’s forgiveness.
> Verse 38 says: “Each one may know the trouble of his own heart.” God also hears the very personal prayer of a single person.
> The request for the foreigner is especially beautiful (verses 41–43): people from other nations should also be allowed to pray to God, and God should answer them. The temple is not only there for Israel. Later Jesus quoted the prophet Isaiah: “My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations” (Mark 11:17; Isaiah 56:7).
> The last request (verses 46–53) sounds like a sense of what was coming: what if Israel is taken captive? That is exactly what happened later, in the exile to Babylon. There Daniel prayed three times a day with his windows open toward Jerusalem (Daniel 6:10). For the captives these verses were a great hope: even far away, God hears us.
> “The furnace for iron” is a picture of the hard slavery in Egypt.

## Solomon’s blessing (Verses 54–61)

[54] Solomon finished praying all this prayer and plea to the LORD.
He had been kneeling in front of the LORD’s altar,
with his hands spread out toward heaven.
Then he got up.
[55] He stood and blessed the whole assembly of Israel with a loud voice.
He said,
[56] “Praise the LORD!
He has given rest to his people Israel, just as he promised.
Not one word has failed of all his good promise
that he made through his servant Moses.
[57] May the LORD our God be with us as he was with our fathers.
May he never leave us or give up on us.
[58] May he turn our hearts to himself,
so that we live in all his ways
and keep his commandments, his rules, and his laws.
He gave them to our fathers.
[59] I have pleaded with the LORD with these words.
May they stay near the LORD our God day and night.
Then he will help his servant and his people Israel win their cause,
as each day needs.
[60] Then all the peoples of the earth will know
that the LORD is God.
There is no one else.
[61] So let your heart be fully devoted to the LORD our God.
Live by his rules and keep his commandments, as you do today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon had been kneeling while he prayed. Now he stands up and blesses the people.
> “Not one word has failed of all his good promise”: that is a great confession of faith. God keeps what he promises. You can rely on his word.
> Solomon asks: may God turn our hearts to himself. He knows: we cannot stay faithful to God by our own strength. We need God’s help for that.
> At the end comes the warning: “Let your heart be fully devoted to the LORD our God.” Sadly, it is Solomon himself who will not follow this warning later (1 Kings 11:4).

## The great feast (Verses 62–66)

[62] The king and all Israel with him offered sacrifices before the LORD.
[63] Solomon offered peace offerings to the LORD:
22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep.
In this way the king and all the people of Israel dedicated the LORD’s house.
To dedicate means: to give something to God in a special way.
[64] On the same day the king made the middle of the courtyard holy.
It was in front of the LORD’s house.
There he offered the burnt offering, the grain offering, and the fat of the peace offerings.
The bronze altar before the LORD was too small for all of them.
[65] So at that time Solomon held the feast, and all Israel with him.
It was a great assembly.
People came from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt.
They celebrated before the LORD our God
for seven days and seven more days, 14 days in all.
[66] On the eighth day he sent the people away.
They blessed the king.
Then they went home to their tents.
They were joyful and glad in their hearts
for all the good things the LORD had done for his servant David and for his people Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number of animals offered is huge. The meat of the peace offerings was eaten together. So it became a great feast for the whole people.
> “From the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt”: people came from the whole land, from the far north to the far south. Some Bibles call the place in the north “Lebo Hamath.”
> The feast lasted 14 days: seven days for the dedication and seven days for the Feast of Tabernacles.
> “On the eighth day”: this probably means the day after the second feast. 2 Chronicles 7:9–10 tells it more exactly.
> The people go home joyful. This is the high point of Israel’s history under the kings: God lives in the middle of his people, and everyone is thankful.
