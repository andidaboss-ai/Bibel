# 1 Kings – Chapter 22
### The prophet Micaiah and Ahab’s death

---

## Ahab and Jehoshaphat plan a war (Verses 1–12)

[1] For three years there was no war between Syria and Israel.
[2] In the third year Jehoshaphat the king of Judah came down to the king of Israel.
[3] The king of Israel said to his servants,
“You know that Ramoth Gilead belongs to us,
and we do nothing to take it back from the king of Syria?”
[4] He said to Jehoshaphat,
“Will you go with me to battle at Ramoth Gilead?”
Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel,
“I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses.”
[5] Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel,
“Please ask first for the word of the LORD.”
[6] Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets, about 400 men,
and said to them,
“Should I go to battle against Ramoth Gilead, or should I hold back?”
They said,
“Go up, for the Lord will hand it over to the king.”
[7] But Jehoshaphat said,
“Isn’t there a prophet of the LORD here whom we can ask?”
[8] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“There is still one man through whom we can ask the LORD:
Micaiah the son of Imlah.
But I hate him,
for he never prophesies good about me, only evil.”
Jehoshaphat said,
“The king should not say that.”
[9] Then the king of Israel called an official and said,
“Quickly bring Micaiah the son of Imlah.”
[10] The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah
were each sitting on his throne, dressed in their royal robes,
in an open place at the entrance of the gate of Samaria.
And all the prophets were prophesying before them.
[11] Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah made himself horns of iron and said,
“The LORD says:
‘With these you will push the Syrians until they are destroyed.’”
[12] All the prophets prophesied the same and said,
“Go up to Ramoth Gilead and you will win.
For the LORD will hand it over to the king.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat is a good king of Judah. He has made an alliance with Ahab. Later his son married Ahab’s daughter (2 Kings 8:18).
> Ramoth Gilead was an important border city east of the Jordan.
> Jehoshaphat wants to ask God first. That is good. But Ahab’s 400 prophets all say what the king wants to hear. Jehoshaphat senses: something is wrong here. He asks for a real prophet of the LORD.
> Ahab’s words give him away: “I hate him, for he never prophesies good about me.” Many people do not want to hear the truth, but only to be told they are right.
> The horns of iron are an action that is a sign: as a bull pushes with its horns, Ahab is to defeat his enemies.
> This Micaiah is not the prophet Micah, after whom a book of the Bible is named.

---

## Micaiah tells the truth (Verses 13–28)

[13] The messenger who had gone to call Micaiah said to him,
“Look, the prophets are all telling the king good things, as with one mouth.
Please let your word be like the word of one of them, and speak good.”
[14] Micaiah said,
“As the LORD lives,
what the LORD says to me, that is what I will speak.”
[15] When he came to the king, the king said to him,
“Micaiah, should we go to battle at Ramoth Gilead, or should we hold back?”
He answered him,
“Go up and you will win.
The LORD will hand it over to the king.”
[16] The king said to him,
“How many times must I make you swear
to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?”
[17] He said,
“I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains,
like sheep that have no shepherd.
And the LORD said,
‘These have no master.
Let each of them go back home in peace.’”
[18] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“Didn’t I tell you that he would not prophesy good about me, only evil?”
[19] Micaiah said,
“So hear the word of the LORD:
I saw the LORD sitting on his throne,
and the whole army of heaven was standing by him,
on his right and on his left.
[20] The LORD said,
‘Who will lure Ahab,
so that he will go up and fall at Ramoth Gilead?’
One said one thing, and another said another.
[21] Then a spirit came out, stood before the LORD, and said,
‘I will lure him.’
[22] The LORD asked him,
‘How?’
He said,
‘I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’
He said,
‘You will lure him, and you will also succeed.
Go out and do so.’
[23] So now the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours.
And the LORD has spoken disaster against you.”
[24] Then Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah came up, struck Micaiah on the cheek, and said,
“Which way did the Spirit of the LORD go from me to speak to you?”
[25] Micaiah said,
“You will see on the day
when you go into an inner room to hide.”
[26] The king of Israel said,
“Take Micaiah and bring him back to Amon the governor of the city
and to Joash the king’s son.
[27] Say:
‘The king says:
Put this fellow in prison,
and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction,
until I come back in peace.’”
[28] Micaiah said,
“If you really come back in peace,
the LORD has not spoken through me.”
And he said,
“Listen, all you people!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Micaiah is under great pressure: 400 prophets against one. But he stands firm: “What the LORD says to me, that is what I will speak.”
> At first Micaiah mockingly repeats the words of the others. Ahab notices right away that he does not mean it. Then Micaiah tells the truth: Israel will be like sheep without a shepherd. That means: the king, the shepherd of the people, will die.
> The vision of the heavenly council around God’s throne is hard to understand. Does God really let people lie? You have to look closely: Micaiah is telling Ahab the whole plan! God warns Ahab openly. Ahab has the choice. He wanted to hear the lie, and God leaves him with the lie he has chosen. Paul writes something similar in 2 Thessalonians 2:10–11.
> The true prophet is struck and locked up. That has happened to many faithful people in history. Telling the truth can cost a lot.
> “Bread of affliction and water of affliction” means: only very little bread and water, just enough to survive.
> Micaiah’s last sentence, “Listen, all you people!”, is the same call as at the beginning of the book of the prophet Micah (Micah 1:2).

## Ahab’s death (Verses 29–40)

[29] So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah went up to Ramoth Gilead.
[30] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“I will disguise myself and go into the battle.
But you put on your royal robes.”
The king of Israel disguised himself and went into the battle.
[31] The king of Syria had commanded his 32 chariot commanders,
“Don’t fight with anyone, small or great,
except only with the king of Israel.”
[32] When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said,
“Surely that is the king of Israel!”
And they turned to fight against him.
Then Jehoshaphat cried out.
[33] When the chariot commanders saw that it was not the king of Israel,
they turned back from chasing him.
[34] A man drew his bow without aiming
and struck the king of Israel between the pieces of his armor.
Then he said to the driver of his chariot,
“Turn around and take me out of the battle,
for I am badly wounded.”
[35] The battle grew fiercer that day.
The king was propped up in his chariot, facing the Syrians,
and he died in the evening.
The blood ran out of the wound onto the floor of the chariot.
[36] At sunset a cry went through the army:
“Every man to his city, and every man to his country!”
[37] So the king died and was brought to Samaria.
And they buried the king in Samaria.
[38] They washed the chariot at the pool of Samaria.
And the dogs licked up his blood
where the prostitutes washed themselves,
according to the word of the LORD that he had spoken.
[39] The rest of the acts of Ahab and all that he did,
the ivory house that he built,
and all the cities that he built,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[40] So Ahab slept with his fathers.
And his son Ahaziah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab tries to escape God’s word. He disguises himself, and Jehoshaphat is to stand out as king. That is cowardly and dangerous for Jehoshaphat.
> But an arrow shot “without aiming” hits exactly the gap in Ahab’s armor. What looks like chance is the fulfillment of God’s word.
> But we must also give Ahab credit: he bravely stays standing in his chariot until evening, so that his army does not lose heart.
> Micaiah’s word comes true: the army scatters “like sheep that have no shepherd.” And Elijah’s word comes true: dogs lick Ahab’s blood (1 Kings 21:19). In Chapter 21 it said “in the place where” Naboth died. But Ahab’s blood was licked up in Samaria. Because Ahab was sorry, God put off part of the judgment (1 Kings 21:29). The rest came true with his son in Jezreel (2 Kings 9:25–26).
> The “ivory house”: in Samaria archaeologists have found many artistic ivory carvings from this time. They were used to decorate furniture and walls.
> Ahab died about 853 BC.

## Jehoshaphat, king of Judah (Verses 41–50)

[41] Jehoshaphat the son of Asa became king over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel.
[42] Jehoshaphat was 35 years old when he became king,
and he ruled 25 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Azubah the daughter of Shilhi.
[43] He walked in all the way of his father Asa.
He did not turn away from it,
and he did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
But the high places were not taken away.
The people still sacrificed and burned incense on the high places.
[44] Jehoshaphat made peace with the king of Israel.
[45] The rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat,
the strength that he showed, and how he fought,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[46] He put out of the land the rest of the sodomites
who were left from the days of his father Asa.
[47] There was no king in Edom at that time.
A deputy ruled.
[48] Jehoshaphat built ships of Tarshish to go to Ophir for gold.
But they did not go, for the ships were wrecked at Ezion Geber.
[49] Then Ahaziah the son of Ahab said to Jehoshaphat,
“Let my servants go with your servants in the ships.”
But Jehoshaphat did not want to.
[50] Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers
and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David.
And his son Jehoram became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat was a good king, like his father Asa. 2 Chronicles 17–20 tells much more about him.
> He made peace with Israel. After many years of war between north and south, that was good. But the close ties with the house of Ahab later brought great disaster to Judah.
> Like Solomon, Jehoshaphat wanted to send ships to Ophir. But the ships were broken even before they set out.
> In verse 46 our English source text again says “sodomites” (see the explanation for 1 Kings 14:24).
> At that time Edom was under the rule of Judah. That is why Jehoshaphat could use the port of Ezion Geber.
> In some Bibles the verse numbers from here on are shifted by one: the second part of verse 43 is verse 44 there. Verse 44 here is verse 45 there, and so on up to verse 53 here, which is verse 54 there.
> Jehoshaphat ruled from about 870 to 848 BC.

## Ahaziah, king of Israel (Verses 51–53)

[51] Ahaziah the son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria
in the 17th year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.
He ruled two years over Israel.
[52] He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight.
He walked in the way of his father and in the way of his mother,
and in the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
who had made Israel sin.
[53] He served Baal and worshiped him
and made the LORD, the God of Israel, angry,
in every way that his father had done.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab’s son carries on like his parents, Ahab and Jezebel. The book ends in the middle of the story. The story of Ahaziah goes on in 2 Kings. In the beginning the two books were one single book.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of 1 Kings?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of 1 Kings tells about 120 years of history, from about 970 to 850 BC.
> **The splendor:** Solomon becomes king. He asks God for wisdom, not for wealth, and receives both. He builds the temple in Jerusalem. God fills the house with his glory. Solomon’s prayer at the dedication shows: God is greater than any heaven, and he hears everyone who prays to him, even the foreigner.
> **The fall:** But Solomon’s heart becomes divided. Many wives, much gold, and other gods pull him away from God. After his death the kingdom breaks apart: Israel in the north, Judah in the south.
> **Two kingdoms:** In the north Jeroboam sets up golden calves. Murders of kings and struggles for power follow there. In the south David’s family stays on the throne, “a lamp in Jerusalem,” as God had promised.
> **The prophets:** The darker it gets, the more strongly God’s messengers appear: Ahijah, Jehu, the man of God from Judah, Micaiah, and above all Elijah. They tell the kings the truth, often at the risk of their lives. On Mount Carmel Elijah shows who the true God is. And at Mount Horeb he learns that God also speaks to him in a quiet voice.
> **The lesson of the book:** What matters is not power, wealth, or great buildings. What counts is a heart that is wholly devoted to God. God stays faithful, even when people are unfaithful. He keeps his promises, and he gives even bad people like Ahab the chance to turn back.
> **What comes next?** In 2 Kings Elijah is taken up to heaven, and Elisha becomes his successor. The story of the two kingdoms goes on, until they fall.
