# 1 Kings – Chapter 22
### The prophet Micaiah and Ahab’s death

---

## Ahab and Jehoshaphat plan a war (Verses 1–12)

[1] For three years there was no war between Syria and Israel.
[2] In the third year Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, went down to visit the king of Israel.
[3] The king of Israel said to his officials,
“You know that Ramoth Gilead belongs to us.
But we do nothing to take it back from the king of Syria.”
[4] He asked Jehoshaphat,
“Will you go with me to fight at Ramoth Gilead?”
Jehoshaphat answered the king of Israel,
“I am with you.
My people are your people.
My horses are your horses.”
[5] Jehoshaphat also said to the king of Israel,
“But first, please ask what the LORD says.”
[6] So the king of Israel called the prophets together.
There were about 400 men.
He asked them,
“Should I go to fight against Ramoth Gilead, or should I stay here?”
They said,
“Go up! The Lord will hand it over to the king.”
[7] But Jehoshaphat asked,
“Isn’t there a prophet of the LORD here?
Then we could ask him.”
[8] The king of Israel answered Jehoshaphat,
“There is still one man through whom we can ask the LORD.
It is Micaiah, the son of Imlah.
But I hate him.
He never says anything good about me, only bad things.”
Jehoshaphat said,
“The king should not say that.”
[9] So the king of Israel called an official and said,
“Bring Micaiah, the son of Imlah, quickly.”
[10] The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah,
were each sitting on a throne.
They were wearing their royal robes.
They sat in an open place at the entrance of the gate of Samaria.
All the prophets were prophesying in front of them.
[11] Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, had made himself horns of iron.
He said,
“The LORD says:
‘With these horns you will push the Syrians until they are destroyed.’”
[12] All the prophets were saying the same thing.
They said,
“Go up to Ramoth Gilead! You will win.
The LORD will hand it over to the king.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat is a good king of Judah. He has made an alliance with Ahab. Later his son married Ahab’s daughter (2 Kings 8:18).
> Ramoth Gilead was an important border city east of the Jordan.
> Jehoshaphat wants to ask God first. That is good. But Ahab’s 400 prophets all say what the king wants to hear. Jehoshaphat senses: something is wrong here. He asks for a real prophet of the LORD.
> Ahab’s words give him away: “But I hate him. He never says anything good about me.” Many people do not want to hear the truth, but only to be told they are right.
> The horns of iron are an action that is a sign: as a bull pushes with its horns, Ahab is to defeat his enemies.
> This Micaiah is not the prophet Micah, after whom a book of the Bible is named.

---

## Micaiah tells the truth (Verses 13–28)

[13] The messenger who had gone to get Micaiah said to him,
“Listen, all the prophets agree.
They are telling the king good things.
Please say the same as they do.
Say something good.”
[14] But Micaiah said,
“As surely as the LORD lives,
I will say what the LORD tells me.”
[15] Micaiah came to the king.
The king asked him,
“Micaiah, should we go to fight at Ramoth Gilead, or should we stay here?”
Micaiah answered,
“Go up! You will win.
The LORD will hand it over to the king.”
[16] The king said to him,
“How many times must I make you swear an oath?
In the name of the LORD, tell me nothing but the truth!”
[17] Then Micaiah said,
“I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains,
like sheep that have no shepherd.
And the LORD said,
‘These people have no master.
Let each of them go home in peace.’”
[18] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“Didn’t I tell you?
He never says anything good about me, only bad things.”
[19] Micaiah said,
“So listen to the word of the LORD.
I saw the LORD sitting on his throne.
The whole army of heaven was standing around him,
on his right and on his left.
[20] The LORD asked,
‘Who will trick Ahab,
so that he goes up to Ramoth Gilead and dies there?’
One said one thing, and another said something else.
[21] Then a spirit came forward.
It stood in front of the LORD and said,
‘I will trick him.’
[22] The LORD asked it,
‘How?’
The spirit answered,
‘I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’
The LORD said,
‘You will trick him, and you will succeed.
Go and do it.’
[23] So now the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours.
The LORD has decided to bring disaster on you.”
[24] Then Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, came up to Micaiah.
He slapped Micaiah on the cheek and said,
“Which way did the Spirit of the LORD go
when he left me to speak to you?”
[25] Micaiah said,
“You will find out on the day
when you go into an inner room to hide.”
[26] The king of Israel said,
“Take Micaiah.
Bring him back to Amon, the governor of the city,
and to Joash, the king’s son.
[27] Tell them,
‘The king says:
“Put this man in prison.
Give him only the bread of suffering and the water of suffering,
until I come back safely.”’”
[28] Micaiah said,
“If you ever come back safely,
then the LORD has not spoken through me.”
And he said,
“Listen, all you people!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Micaiah is under great pressure: 400 prophets against one. But he stands firm: “I will say what the LORD tells me.”
> At first Micaiah mockingly repeats the words of the others. Ahab notices right away that he does not mean it. Then Micaiah tells the truth: Israel will be like sheep without a shepherd. That means: the king, the shepherd of the people, will die.
> The vision of the heavenly council around God’s throne is hard to understand. Does God really let people lie? You have to look closely: Micaiah is telling Ahab the whole plan! God warns Ahab openly. Ahab has the choice. He wanted to hear the lie, and God leaves him with the lie he has chosen. Paul writes something similar in 2 Thessalonians 2:10–11.
> The true prophet is struck and locked up. That has happened to many faithful people in history. Telling the truth can cost a lot.
> “The bread of suffering and the water of suffering” means: only very little bread and water, just enough to survive.
> Micaiah’s last sentence, “Listen, all you people!”, is the same call as at the beginning of the book of the prophet Micah (Micah 1:2).

## Ahab’s death (Verses 29–40)

[29] So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, went up to Ramoth Gilead.
[30] The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat,
“I will dress so that no one knows me, and go into the battle.
But you wear your royal robes.”
So the king of Israel dressed so that no one would know him.
He went into the battle.
[31] The king of Syria had given an order to his 32 chariot commanders.
He had said,
“Do not fight with anyone, small or great.
Fight only with the king of Israel.”
[32] The chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat.
They said,
“That must be the king of Israel!”
So they turned to attack him.
Then Jehoshaphat cried out.
[33] The chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel.
So they stopped chasing him.
[34] But a man shot an arrow from his bow without aiming.
It hit the king of Israel between the pieces of his armor.
The king said to the driver of his chariot,
“Turn around and take me out of the battle.
I am badly wounded.”
[35] The battle grew worse that day.
The king was held up in his chariot, facing the Syrians.
In the evening he died.
The blood from his wound ran onto the floor of the chariot.
[36] At sunset a shout went through the army:
“Every man go back to his city!
Every man go back to his land!”
[37] So the king died.
He was brought to Samaria.
They buried the king in Samaria.
[38] They washed the chariot at the pool of Samaria.
The dogs licked up his blood there.
That was the place where the prostitutes washed themselves.
It happened as the LORD had said.
[39] The rest of Ahab’s story and everything he did
are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
It also tells about the ivory house and all the cities he built.
[40] So Ahab died and joined his ancestors.
His son Ahaziah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab tries to escape God’s word. He disguises himself, and Jehoshaphat is to stand out as king. That is cowardly and dangerous for Jehoshaphat.
> But an arrow shot “without aiming” hits exactly the gap in Ahab’s armor. What looks like chance is the fulfillment of God’s word.
> But we must also give Ahab credit: he bravely stays standing in his chariot until evening, so that his army does not lose heart.
> Micaiah’s word comes true: the army scatters “like sheep that have no shepherd.” And Elijah’s word comes true: dogs lick Ahab’s blood (1 Kings 21:19). In Chapter 21 it said “in the place where” Naboth died. But Ahab’s blood was licked up in Samaria. Because Ahab was sorry, God put off part of the judgment (1 Kings 21:29). The rest came true with his son in Jezreel (2 Kings 9:25–26).
> The “ivory house”: in Samaria archaeologists have found many artistic ivory carvings from this time. They were used to decorate furniture and walls.
> Ahab died about 853 BC.

## Jehoshaphat, king of Judah (Verses 41–50)

[41] Jehoshaphat, the son of Asa, became king over Judah.
It was in the fourth year of Ahab, the king of Israel.
[42] Jehoshaphat was 35 years old when he became king.
He ruled for 25 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Azubah, the daughter of Shilhi.
[43] He lived the way his father Asa had lived.
He did not turn away from it.
He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
But the high places were not removed.
The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places.
[44] Jehoshaphat made peace with the king of Israel.
[45] The rest of Jehoshaphat’s story is written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah.
It tells about the strength he showed and how he fought.
[46] There were still some male shrine prostitutes left from the days of his father Asa.
Jehoshaphat sent them out of the land.
[47] At that time there was no king in Edom.
A governor ruled there.
[48] Jehoshaphat built ships of Tarshish.
They were to go to Ophir to get gold.
But they never sailed.
The ships were wrecked at Ezion Geber.
[49] Then Ahaziah, the son of Ahab, said to Jehoshaphat,
“Let my servants sail with your servants in the ships.”
But Jehoshaphat did not want that.
[50] Jehoshaphat died and joined his ancestors.
He was buried with his ancestors in the city of his father David.
His son Jehoram became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoshaphat was a good king, like his father Asa. 2 Chronicles 17–20 tells much more about him.
> He made peace with Israel. After many years of war between north and south, that was good. But the close ties with the house of Ahab later brought great disaster to Judah.
> Like Solomon, Jehoshaphat wanted to send ships to Ophir. But the ships were broken even before they set out.
> In verse 46 the WEB, our English source text, again says “sodomites.” We write “male shrine prostitutes” (see the explanation for 1 Kings 14:24).
> At that time Edom was under the rule of Judah. That is why Jehoshaphat could use the port of Ezion Geber.
> In some Bibles the verse numbers from here on are shifted by one: the second part of verse 43 is verse 44 there. Verse 44 here is verse 45 there, and so on up to verse 53 here, which is verse 54 there.
> Jehoshaphat ruled from about 870 to 848 BC.

## Ahaziah, king of Israel (Verses 51–53)

[51] Ahaziah, the son of Ahab, became king over Israel in Samaria.
It was in the 17th year of Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah.
Ahaziah ruled over Israel for two years.
[52] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He lived the way his father and his mother had lived.
He lived the way Jeroboam, the son of Nebat, had lived.
Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[53] Ahaziah served Baal and worshiped him.
He made the LORD, the God of Israel, angry,
just as his father had done.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahab’s son carries on like his parents, Ahab and Jezebel. The book ends in the middle of the story. The story of Ahaziah goes on in 2 Kings. In the beginning the two books were one single book.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of 1 Kings?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of 1 Kings tells about 120 years of history, from about 970 to 850 BC.
> **The splendor:** Solomon becomes king. He asks God for wisdom, not for wealth, and receives both. He builds the temple in Jerusalem. God fills the house with his glory. Solomon’s prayer at the dedication shows: God is greater than any heaven, and he hears everyone who prays to him, even the foreigner.
> **The fall:** But Solomon’s heart becomes divided. Many wives, much gold, and other gods pull him away from God. After his death the kingdom breaks apart: Israel in the north, Judah in the south.
> **Two kingdoms:** In the north Jeroboam sets up golden calves. Murders of kings and struggles for power follow there. In the south David’s family stays on the throne, “a lamp in Jerusalem,” as God had promised.
> **The prophets:** The darker it gets, the more strongly God’s messengers appear: Ahijah, Jehu, the man of God from Judah, Micaiah, and above all Elijah. They tell the kings the truth, often at the risk of their lives. On Mount Carmel Elijah shows who the true God is. And at Mount Horeb he learns that God also speaks to him in a quiet voice.
> **The lesson of the book:** What matters is not power, wealth, or great buildings. What counts is a heart that is wholly devoted to God. God stays faithful, even when people are unfaithful. He keeps his promises, and he gives even bad people like Ahab the chance to turn back.
> **What comes next?** In 2 Kings Elijah is taken up to heaven, and Elisha becomes his successor. The story of the two kingdoms goes on, until they fall.
