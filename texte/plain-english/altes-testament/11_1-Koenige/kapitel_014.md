# 1 Kings – Chapter 14
### Ahijah’s word against Jeroboam, and King Rehoboam

---

## Jeroboam’s sick son (Verses 1–6)

[1] At that time Abijah the son of Jeroboam became sick.
[2] Jeroboam said to his wife,
“Please get up and disguise yourself,
so that no one will recognize you as Jeroboam’s wife.
Go to Shiloh.
Ahijah the prophet is there,
who told me that I would be king over this people.
[3] Take with you ten loaves of bread, some cakes, and a jar of honey,
and go to him.
He will tell you what will happen to the child.”
[4] Jeroboam’s wife did so.
She got up, went to Shiloh, and came to Ahijah’s house.
Ahijah could not see,
for his eyes had become stiff because of his age.
[5] The LORD said to Ahijah,
“Jeroboam’s wife is coming
to ask you about her son, for he is sick.
Tell her this and that.
When she comes in, she will pretend to be another woman.”
[6] When Ahijah heard the sound of her feet as she came in at the door, he said,
“Come in, wife of Jeroboam!
Why do you pretend to be someone else?
I have been sent to you with heavy news.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeroboam is in trouble. His son is sick. He does not go to his golden calves, but sends his wife to the true prophet of the LORD. In his trouble he knows where the truth is.
> But he sends his wife in disguise. He wants to trick God. But the blind prophet “sees” more than anyone else: God has told him who is coming.
> Do not mix up this Abijah with Abijam, the king of Judah (Chapter 15).

---

## The heavy news (Verses 7–16)

[7] Go, tell Jeroboam:
‘The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
“I lifted you up from among the people
and made you prince over my people Israel.
[8] I tore the kingdom away from David’s house and gave it to you.
But you have not been like my servant David,
who kept my commandments
and followed me with all his heart
and did only what was right in my eyes.
[9] Instead you have done evil, more than all who were before you.
You have gone and made yourself other gods, cast metal images,
to make me angry.
And you have thrown me behind your back.
[10] So I will bring disaster on the house of Jeroboam.
I will cut off from Jeroboam everyone who urinates on a wall,
whether slave or free in Israel.
I will sweep away the house of Jeroboam completely,
as a man sweeps away dung until it is all gone.
[11] Whoever belongs to Jeroboam and dies in the city, the dogs will eat.
And whoever dies in the field, the birds of the sky will eat.
For the LORD has spoken it.”’
[12] And you, get up and go to your house.
When your feet enter the city, the child will die.
[13] All Israel will mourn for him and bury him.
For he alone of Jeroboam’s family will come to a grave,
because something good toward the LORD, the God of Israel, has been found in him,
in the house of Jeroboam.
[14] And the LORD will raise up for himself a king over Israel
who will cut off the house of Jeroboam.
This is the day! What? Even now!
[15] For the LORD will strike Israel,
as a reed is shaken back and forth in the water.
And he will pull Israel up by the roots out of this good land that he gave to their fathers,
and will scatter them beyond the River,
because they have made their Asherah poles
and have made the LORD angry.
[16] He will give Israel up because of the sins of Jeroboam,
which he has committed
and with which he has made Israel sin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The same prophet who once promised Jeroboam the kingdom now has to announce his end. God had given Jeroboam everything. But Jeroboam “threw God behind his back,” that is, he forgot him on purpose.
> “Everyone who urinates on a wall” is a crude expression of the language back then. It means all male descendants. “Slave or free” probably means: everyone, with no exception.
> For the dead not to be buried, but to be eaten by animals, was seen as the worst disgrace.
> In the middle of this harsh judgment there is a tender word: in the sick boy there was “something good toward the LORD.” He may die in peace and be buried with honor. God sees the good even in a child from a bad family.
> “Beyond the River”: this means the Euphrates. Here the later carrying away of Israel by the Assyrians (2 Kings 17) is already announced.
> “Asherah poles” were holy wooden poles for the goddess Asherah, a goddess of fertility.

## The death of the child and Jeroboam’s end (Verses 17–20)

[17] Jeroboam’s wife got up, went away, and came to Tirzah.
As she stepped onto the threshold of the house, the child died.
[18] All Israel buried him and mourned for him,
as the LORD had said through his servant Ahijah the prophet.
[19] The rest of the acts of Jeroboam, how he fought and how he ruled,
are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
[20] Jeroboam ruled for 22 years.
Then he slept with his fathers,
and his son Nadab became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> For a mother this is unbearably hard: she comes home, and at that very moment her child dies. The Bible tells it without making it look nicer.
> Anyone who has lost a child today is not alone in their grief. Lament has its place in the Bible. Help is available, for example: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> At that time Tirzah was the capital of the northern kingdom.
> The “book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel” is an old book that is lost today. It is not the same as the books of Chronicles in the Bible.
> Jeroboam ruled from about 930 to 909 BC.

## Rehoboam, king of Judah (Verses 21–31)

[21] Rehoboam the son of Solomon ruled in Judah.
Rehoboam was 41 years old when he became king,
and he ruled 17 years in Jerusalem,
the city that the LORD had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there.
His mother’s name was Naamah the Ammonitess.
[22] Judah did what was evil in the LORD’s sight.
They made him jealous with the sins that they committed,
more than all that their fathers had done.
[23] For they also built themselves high places, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles
on every high hill and under every green tree.
[24] There were also sodomites in the land.
They did all the disgusting things of the nations
that the LORD had driven out before the children of Israel.
[25] In the fifth year of King Rehoboam,
Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem.
[26] He took away the treasures of the LORD’s house and the treasures of the king’s house.
He took away everything,
including all the gold shields that Solomon had made.
[27] King Rehoboam made shields of bronze in their place
and gave them to the commanders of the guard,
who guarded the door of the king’s house.
[28] Every time the king went into the LORD’s house,
the guards carried them
and then brought them back to the guard room.
[29] The rest of the acts of Rehoboam and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[30] There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam the whole time.
[31] Rehoboam slept with his fathers
and was buried with his fathers in David’s city.
His mother’s name was Naamah the Ammonitess.
And his son Abijam became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Even in the southern kingdom of Judah, where the temple stands, the worship of idols spreads. The temple alone does not make a people faithful.
> “Sacred pillars” and “Asherah poles” were signs of Canaanite gods. “Under every green tree” means: everywhere in the land.
> Verse 24: our English source text says “sodomites.” The Hebrew says “qadesh,” word for word “one who is set apart.” This probably means people who performed sexual acts in the service of other gods (so-called cult prostitution). Scholars disagree about what exactly this looked like. The verse is speaking about the worship of idols. It should not be used to look down on people.
> The military campaign of Shishak (Pharaoh Shoshenq I) is also known from outside the Bible: in the temple of Karnak in Egypt there is an inscription with a list of the places he conquered in Palestine. That was about 925 BC.
> Solomon’s gold shields are gone. Rehoboam replaces them with bronze. That is a sad picture: from the outside it still looks similar, but the shine is lost.
> Rehoboam’s mother was an Ammonite, one of Solomon’s foreign wives. That it is mentioned twice reminds us of Chapter 11.
> Rehoboam ruled from about 930 to 913 BC.
