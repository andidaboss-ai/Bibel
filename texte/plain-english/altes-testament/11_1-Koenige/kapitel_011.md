# 1 Kings – Chapter 11
### Solomon’s heart turns away

---

## Solomon’s many wives (Verses 1–8)

[1] King Solomon loved many foreign women,
together with the daughter of Pharaoh:
women of the Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Sidonians, and Hittites,
[2] from the nations about which the LORD had said to the people of Israel,
“You must not go among them, and they must not come among you.
For they will surely turn your heart away to their gods.”
Solomon held on to these women in love.
[3] He had 700 wives who were princesses, and 300 concubines.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
His wives turned his heart away.
[4] When Solomon was old, his wives turned his heart away to other gods.
And his heart was no longer wholly with the LORD his God,
as the heart of his father David had been.
[5] For Solomon followed Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians,
and Milcom, the disgusting idol of the Ammonites.
[6] Solomon did what was evil in the LORD’s sight
and did not follow the LORD fully, as his father David had.
[7] Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh, the disgusting idol of Moab,
on the mountain that is east of Jerusalem,
and for Molech, the disgusting idol of the people of Ammon.
[8] He did the same for all his foreign wives,
who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the deep fall of the wise king. In those days many marriages were politics: a king married the daughter of another king to make an alliance.
> The problem is not that the women were foreigners. Ruth was also a Moabite, and she was David’s great-grandmother. The problem is: Solomon worships their gods along with them. His heart is divided.
> God had warned against exactly this (Exodus 34:16; Deuteronomy 7:3–4). And Deuteronomy 17:17 says: the king should not take many wives, so that his heart does not turn away.
> The numbers 700 and 300 are huge. They show how Solomon had lost all sense of limits.
> “Disgusting idol” is a strong word for a false god that God hates.
> Ashtoreth was a goddess of fertility and war. Chemosh was the god of the Moabites. Molech is connected with cruel sacrifices of children (Leviticus 18:21). In verses 5 and 33 the god of the Ammonites is called “Milcom.” Many experts think that the same god is meant in verse 7.
> “The mountain east of Jerusalem” is the Mount of Olives. Later King Josiah had these high places destroyed (2 Kings 23:13).
> Solomon, who built the temple, now builds altars for other gods. This shows: even a great beginning does not protect you from a bad end. You have to guard your heart every day.

## God announces the splitting of the kingdom (Verses 9–13)

[9] The LORD was angry with Solomon,
because his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel,
who had appeared to him twice
[10] and had commanded him about this very thing: that he should not follow other gods.
But he did not keep what the LORD commanded.
[11] So the LORD said to Solomon,
“Because you have done this
and have not kept my covenant and my laws, which I commanded you,
I will surely tear the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant.
[12] But I will not do it in your lifetime, for the sake of your father David.
I will tear it out of your son’s hand.
[13] But I will not tear away the whole kingdom.
I will give one tribe to your son,
for the sake of my servant David
and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God had appeared to Solomon twice (Chapters 3 and 9). He had received so much. That makes his unfaithfulness all the more serious.
> The punishment is hard: the kingdom will be split. But even now God stays faithful to his promise to David. One part of the kingdom stays with David’s family, because of Jerusalem.
> “One tribe”: this means Judah. Later the small tribe of Benjamin was added to it (1 Kings 12:21).

## Hadad the Edomite (Verses 14–22)

[14] The LORD raised up an enemy against Solomon:
Hadad the Edomite.
He came from the royal family in Edom.
[15] For when David was in Edom,
and Joab the commander of the army had gone up to bury the dead,
he had struck down every male in Edom
[16] – for Joab and all Israel stayed there six months,
until he had wiped out every male in Edom.
[17] Then Hadad fled, he and some Edomites of his father’s servants with him,
to go to Egypt.
Hadad was still a little child at that time.
[18] They set out from Midian and came to Paran.
They took men with them from Paran
and came to Egypt, to Pharaoh king of Egypt.
He gave him a house, gave him food, and gave him land.
[19] Hadad found great favor with Pharaoh,
so that he gave him as his wife the sister of his own wife,
the sister of Queen Tahpenes.
[20] The sister of Tahpenes gave birth to his son Genubath.
Tahpenes raised him in Pharaoh’s house until he no longer nursed.
And Genubath was in Pharaoh’s house among the sons of Pharaoh.
[21] When Hadad heard in Egypt that David died and joined his ancestors
and that Joab the commander of the army was dead,
Hadad said to Pharaoh,
“Let me go, so that I may go to my own country.”
[22] Then Pharaoh said to him,
“But what have you been missing with me, that you now want to go to your own country?”
He answered,
“Nothing, but please let me go.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a dark side of David’s wars is told: Joab killed all the men in Edom that he could find (compare 2 Samuel 8:13–14). Little Hadad survived and fled to Egypt.
> The Bible tells it honestly: violence creates new hatred. The boy who fled back then later becomes an enemy of Israel.
> “Died and joined his ancestors” is an old way of saying that someone died.
> Midian and Paran are desert areas in the south, between Edom and Egypt.

## Rezon of Damascus (Verses 23–25)

[23] God raised up another enemy against him:
Rezon the son of Eliada,
who had fled from his master Hadadezer king of Zobah.
[24] He gathered men around him and became the leader of a band,
when David killed the men of Zobah.
They went to Damascus, lived there, and ruled in Damascus.
[25] He was an enemy of Israel all the days of Solomon,
in addition to the harm that Hadad did.
He hated Israel and ruled over Syria.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rezon founded his own kingdom in Damascus. Later Damascus, the kingdom of the Arameans (Syrians), will be a dangerous enemy of Israel.
> From the outside Solomon’s kingdom looked splendid and peaceful. But at the edges it was already crumbling.
> In Hebrew “Syria” is “Aram.”

## Jeroboam and the prophet Ahijah (Verses 26–40)

[26] Jeroboam the son of Nebat also lifted up his hand against the king.
He was an Ephraimite from Zeredah, a servant of Solomon.
His mother’s name was Zeruah, and she was a widow.
[27] This was the reason why he lifted up his hand against the king:
Solomon built the Millo
and closed up the gap in the city of his father David.
[28] Jeroboam was a strong, brave man.
Solomon saw that the young man was hard-working,
and he put him in charge of all the forced labor of the house of Joseph.
[29] At that time, when Jeroboam went out of Jerusalem,
the prophet Ahijah the Shilonite met him on the way.
Ahijah had put on a new cloak.
And the two of them were alone in the field.
[30] Ahijah took the new cloak that he was wearing
and tore it into 12 pieces.
[31] He said to Jeroboam,
“Take 10 pieces for yourself.
For the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘I will tear the kingdom out of the hand of Solomon
and will give 10 tribes to you
[32] – but he will keep one tribe,
for the sake of my servant David
and for the sake of Jerusalem, the city that I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel –
[33] because they have abandoned me
and have worshiped Ashtoreth the goddess of the Sidonians, Chemosh the god of Moab,
and Milcom the god of the people of Ammon.
They have not walked in my ways,
to do what is right in my eyes,
and to keep my laws and my rules, as his father David did.
[34] But I will not take the whole kingdom out of his hand.
I will make him prince all the days of his life,
for the sake of my servant David, whom I chose,
who kept my commandments and my laws.
[35] But I will take the kingdom out of his son’s hand
and will give it to you, 10 tribes.
[36] I will give one tribe to his son,
so that my servant David may always have a lamp before me in Jerusalem,
the city that I have chosen for myself to put my name there.
[37] But I will take you,
and you will rule over everything that your soul desires,
and you will be king over Israel.
[38] If you listen to everything that I command you,
and walk in my ways,
and do what is right in my eyes,
and keep my laws and my commandments, as my servant David did,
then I will be with you
and build you a lasting house, as I built for David,
and I will give Israel to you.
[39] Because of this I will bring the descendants of David low, but not forever.’”
[40] That is why Solomon tried to kill Jeroboam.
But Jeroboam got up and fled to Egypt,
to Shishak king of Egypt.
And he stayed in Egypt until Solomon died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeroboam was a gifted overseer of the workers from the “house of Joseph,” that is, from the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh in the north. He saw for himself how heavy the burdens on the people were.
> The prophet Ahijah does an action that is a sign: he tears a new cloak into 12 pieces, for the 12 tribes. Jeroboam gets 10. The picture is strong: something new and whole is torn apart.
> 10 and 1 together are only 11. The 12th tribe, Levi, had no land of its own. Or Benjamin is counted here as part of Judah. The Bible does not explain the numbers exactly.
> “A lamp in Jerusalem”: David’s family is not to die out. From it come the kings of Judah. Christians believe that Jesus later came from this family line. Jews expect the Messiah from the house of David.
> God makes Jeroboam a great offer: the same as David, if he stays faithful. The next chapter shows whether he accepts it.
> “But not forever” (verse 39): in the middle of the punishment there is already hope.
> Shishak is Pharaoh Shoshenq I. He is also known from Egyptian sources. He ruled from about 945 to 924 BC.

## Solomon’s death (Verses 41–43)

[41] The rest of the acts of Solomon,
everything he did, and his wisdom,
aren’t they written in the book of the acts of Solomon?
[42] Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all Israel for 40 years.
[43] Solomon died and joined his ancestors
and was buried in the city of his father David.
And his son Rehoboam became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “book of the acts of Solomon” is an old book that is lost today. The writer used it as a source.
> Solomon ruled from about 970 to 930 BC.
> Solomon’s life is a great lesson: he began with a humble prayer for wisdom. He built the temple and was famous in the whole world. But in the end he let wealth, power, and other gods pull his heart away. Wisdom alone is not enough. What matters is that the heart stays with God.
