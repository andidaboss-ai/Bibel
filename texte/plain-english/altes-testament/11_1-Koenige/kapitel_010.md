# 1 Kings – Chapter 10
### The queen of Sheba and Solomon’s wealth

---

## The queen of Sheba visits Solomon (Verses 1–13)

[1] The queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s fame, connected with the name of the LORD.
So she came to test him with hard questions.
[2] She came to Jerusalem with a very large caravan,
with camels that carried spices, very much gold, and precious stones.
When she had come to Solomon,
she talked with him about everything that was in her heart.
[3] Solomon answered all her questions.
There was nothing hidden from the king that he did not explain to her.
[4] The queen of Sheba saw all the wisdom of Solomon,
the house that he had built,
[5] the food on his table, how his servants sat,
how his officials served, their clothing, his cupbearers,
and the stairway by which he went up to the LORD’s house.
Then she was left breathless.
[6] She said to the king,
“What I heard in my own land
about your deeds and your wisdom was true.
[7] But I didn’t believe the words
until I came and saw it with my own eyes.
Not even half was told to me!
Your wisdom and your wealth are greater than the fame I heard about.
[8] Happy are your men,
happy are these servants of yours who always stand before you and hear your wisdom.
[9] Blessed be the LORD your God,
who was pleased with you and set you on the throne of Israel.
Because the LORD loves Israel forever,
he has made you king,
to do justice and righteousness.”
[10] She gave the king about 4½ tons of gold,
a very great amount of spices, and precious stones.
Never again was there such a great amount of spices
as the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.
[11] Hiram’s fleet, which brought gold from Ophir,
also brought from Ophir great amounts of almug wood and precious stones.
[12] The king made pillars of the almug wood for the LORD’s house and for the king’s house,
and also harps and stringed instruments for the singers.
No such almug wood ever came again, and it has not been seen to this day.
[13] King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she wanted, whatever she asked for,
besides what Solomon gave her out of his royal wealth.
Then she turned and went back to her own land, she and her servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sheba was probably in the south of Arabia, in what is Yemen today. It was rich from trading in incense and spices. Some people also think of Ethiopia.
> The queen travels more than 1,200 miles. She does not come only because of the wealth, but because of the wisdom, and “connected with the name of the LORD.” She wants to know where this wisdom comes from.
> “She was left breathless”: word for word it says, “There was no more spirit in her.” She was completely overwhelmed.
> A foreign queen praises the God of Israel (verse 9). She sees: a king is there to do justice and righteousness. That is the purpose of power.
> Later Jesus reminded people of her: the “Queen of the South” came “from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon. And someone greater than Solomon is here” (Matthew 12:42).
> In Ethiopia there is an old tradition that the queen and Solomon had a son, Menelik. That is not in the Bible.
> The Bible says “120 talents of gold.” A talent is about 75 pounds.
> No one knows for sure what “almug wood” was, maybe red sandalwood. Instead of “pillars,” the Hebrew word could also be translated “railings” or “supports.”

## Solomon’s gold (Verses 14–22)

[14] The gold that came to Solomon in one year weighed about 25 tons.
[15] Besides that, there was what the traders brought, the business of the merchants,
and of all the kings of the mixed peoples and of the governors of the land.
[16] King Solomon made 200 large shields of beaten gold.
He used about 15 pounds of gold for each shield.
[17] And he made 300 small shields of beaten gold.
He used almost 4 pounds of gold for each shield.
The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.
[18] The king also made a great throne of ivory
and covered it with the finest gold.
[19] The throne had six steps.
The top of the throne was round at the back.
There were armrests on both sides of the seat,
and two lions stood beside the armrests.
[20] Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, on one side and on the other.
Nothing like it was made in any kingdom.
[21] All of King Solomon’s drinking cups were made of gold,
and all the things in the House of the Forest of Lebanon were made of pure gold.
None were made of silver, because silver was thought of as worth little in the days of Solomon.
[22] For the king had a fleet of ships of Tarshish at sea, together with Hiram’s fleet.
Once every three years the fleet of Tarshish came
and brought gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

> **What does this mean?**
> The numbers are huge. The Bible says “666 talents of gold.” A talent is about 75 pounds.
> For the shields the Bible says “600 shekels” (a shekel is about 0.4 ounces) and “three minas” (a mina is about 1¼ pounds). The gold shields were not for fighting. They were for showing off, for feasts and parades.
> The House of the Forest of Lebanon was a large hall in Solomon’s palace (1 Kings 7:2).
> “Ships of Tarshish” were large ships for long journeys across the sea. It is not certain where Tarshish was, maybe in Spain. The translation “peacocks” is not certain. Some translate “baboons.”
> This description shows Solomon’s splendor. But there is a catch: Deuteronomy 17:16–17 says that a king should not pile up many horses, many wives, and a lot of silver and gold. That is exactly what Solomon does. The next chapter shows where this leads.

## Solomon’s wisdom, chariots, and horses (Verses 23–29)

[23] So King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.
[24] The whole world wanted to see Solomon,
to hear his wisdom, which God had put into his heart.
[25] Year after year, everyone brought his tribute:
things of silver, things of gold, clothing, armor, spices, horses, and mules.
[26] Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen.
He had 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen.
He kept them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.
[27] In Jerusalem the king made silver as common as stones,
and cedars as common as the sycamore trees in the lowland.
[28] The horses that Solomon had were brought from Egypt.
The king’s merchants got them in herds, each herd at a price.
[29] A chariot was brought from Egypt for 600 shekels of silver,
and a horse for 150 shekels.
And in the same way they sold them to all the kings of the Hittites and to the kings of Syria.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon is described as the richest and wisest king in the world. But verse 24 says it clearly: his wisdom came from God.
> Solomon was also a horse trader. He bought horses and chariots and sold them on to other kings.
> The Bible says “600 shekels of silver” and “150 shekels.” A shekel is about 0.4 ounces.
> “From Egypt”: some experts think that the text first said “Muzri,” a land in what is Turkey today. In Hebrew the name sounds almost the same as “Egypt.”
> In Hebrew “Syria” is “Aram,” the land of the Arameans.
> The sycamore tree was a very ordinary tree. Cedars were precious. Under Solomon, precious things were as everyday as ordinary things.
> Again this points to Deuteronomy 17:16: the king should not get horses from Egypt. The splendor has a dark side.
