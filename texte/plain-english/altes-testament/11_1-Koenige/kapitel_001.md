# 1 Kings – Chapter 1
### Who will follow David as king?

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of 1 Kings?

The books of Kings tell the story of the kings of Israel, from Solomon to the fall of Jerusalem. That is about 400 years. In the beginning, 1 Kings and 2 Kings were one single book.
The book of 1 Kings has two big parts:
The first part (Chapters 1–11) is about King Solomon. He is famous for his wisdom and his wealth. He builds the temple in Jerusalem. But at the end of his life he turns to other gods.
In the second part (Chapters 12–22) the kingdom splits into two parts: in the north the kingdom of Israel, in the south the kingdom of Judah. Many kings follow one after another. Most of the kings in the north serve other gods. Then the great prophet Elijah appears. He fights against King Ahab and Queen Jezebel and against the idol Baal.
Again and again the book asks: did the king obey the LORD or not? It does not judge the kings by their wealth or their wars, but by their faithfulness to God.
The books of Kings were probably put together during the time of the exile in Babylon. They want to explain: why did Israel lose its land? The answer is: because the kings and the people were unfaithful to God again and again. But the book also shows: God keeps his promises to David.

## The old King David (Verses 1–4)

[1] King David was old and far along in years.
They covered him with clothes, but he could not keep warm.
[2] So his servants said to him,
“Let a young virgin be found for my lord the king.
Let her stand before the king and take care of him.
Let her lie in your arms, so that my lord the king may keep warm.”
[3] So they looked for a beautiful young woman in all the land of Israel.
They found Abishag the Shunammite and brought her to the king.
[4] The young woman was very beautiful.
She took care of the king and served him,
but the king did not have sexual relations with her.

> **What does this mean?**
> The great King David is now an old, sick man. He is cold, he is weak.
> The servants bring a young woman, Abishag. She is to take care of him and keep him warm. In those days people believed that the body warmth of a young person could give an old person strength for life.
> For Abishag this was surely not a free choice. In those days young women were often not asked.
> “The king did not have sexual relations with her”: this also shows that David is so weak that he can no longer rule. This is the moment when the struggle over who will follow him begins.

## Adonijah wants to become king (Verses 5–10)

[5] Then Adonijah the son of Haggith became proud and said,
“I will be king.”
He got himself chariots and horsemen
and 50 men to run in front of him.
[6] His father had never once upset him by asking,
“Why have you done this?”
He was also a very handsome man.
He was born after Absalom.
[7] He talked things over with Joab the son of Zeruiah and with Abiathar the priest.
And they followed Adonijah and helped him.
[8] But Zadok the priest, Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, Nathan the prophet,
Shimei, Rei, and David’s mighty men were not on Adonijah’s side.
[9] Adonijah killed sheep, cattle, and fattened animals by the stone of Zoheleth, which is beside En Rogel.
He invited all his brothers, the king’s sons,
and all the men of Judah, the king’s servants.
[10] But he did not invite Nathan the prophet, Benaiah, the mighty men,
or his brother Solomon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Adonijah is David’s fourth son (2 Samuel 3:4). Amnon and Absalom are dead, and the second son, Chileab, is not mentioned anymore. So Adonijah is now probably the oldest living son. He believes that he has the right to the throne.
> He does it exactly like Absalom: chariots, horses, 50 runners (2 Samuel 15:1). And he is also handsome, like Absalom.
> “His father had never once upset him”: David never corrected his sons. He spoiled them and set them no limits. That is a weakness that runs like a red thread through David’s family.
> There are now two groups at the royal court: Joab and Abiathar are for Adonijah. Zadok, Benaiah, and Nathan are for Solomon.
> Adonijah is already holding a feast, as if he were king. But he does not invite Solomon. He knows who his rival is.

## Nathan and Bathsheba step in (Verses 11–27)

[11] Then Nathan said to Bathsheba, the mother of Solomon,
“Haven’t you heard that Adonijah the son of Haggith has become king,
and our lord David doesn’t know it?
[12] So come now, let me give you advice,
so that you can save your own life and the life of your son Solomon.
[13] Go in to King David and say to him,
‘My lord the king, didn’t you swear to your servant and say,
“Surely your son Solomon will be king after me,
and he will sit on my throne”?
Why then has Adonijah become king?’
[14] And while you are still talking there with the king,
I will come in after you and confirm your words.”
[15] So Bathsheba went in to the king in his room.
The king was very old,
and Abishag the Shunammite was serving the king.
[16] Bathsheba bowed and showed respect to the king.
The king said, “What would you like?”
[17] She said to him,
“My lord, you swore to your servant by the LORD your God,
‘Surely your son Solomon will be king after me,
and he will sit on my throne.’
[18] But now Adonijah has become king.
And you, my lord the king, don’t know it.
[19] He has killed cattle, fattened animals, and sheep in great numbers,
and he has invited all the king’s sons,
Abiathar the priest, and Joab the commander of the army.
But he has not invited your servant Solomon.
[20] My lord the king, the eyes of all Israel are on you.
They are waiting for you to tell them
who will sit on the throne of my lord the king after him.
[21] Otherwise this will happen:
when my lord the king dies and joins his ancestors,
I and my son Solomon will be treated as criminals.”

[22] While she was still talking with the king, Nathan the prophet came in.
[23] They told the king,
“Nathan the prophet is here!”
When he came in before the king, he bowed down before the king with his face to the ground.
[24] Nathan said,
“My lord the king, have you said,
‘Adonijah will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne’?
[25] For today he has gone down
and has killed cattle, fattened animals, and sheep in great numbers.
He has invited all the king’s sons, the commanders of the army, and Abiathar the priest.
And they are eating and drinking with him and saying,
‘Long live King Adonijah!’
[26] But he has not invited me, your servant,
or Zadok the priest, or Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, or your servant Solomon.
[27] Was this done by my lord the king,
and you have not told your servants
who should sit on the throne of my lord the king after him?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nathan, the prophet who once called David to turn back with the words “You are the man!”, steps in again.
> Bathsheba now has a very different role than before. She is no longer the woman who was taken, but a wise, strong mother who fights for her son’s future.
> She reminds David of an oath that Solomon would become king. This oath is not mentioned earlier in the Bible. But at his birth God had already called Solomon “Jedidiah,” which means loved by the LORD (2 Samuel 12:25).
> Nathan and Bathsheba plan this together. They know: if Adonijah becomes king, Solomon and Bathsheba are in danger of their lives. New kings often killed their rivals.

## David decides: Solomon is to be king (Verses 28–40)

[28] Then King David answered,
“Call Bathsheba in to me.”
She came in before the king and stood before the king.
[29] The king made a vow and said,
“As the LORD lives, who has rescued my life from all trouble:
[30] as I swore to you by the LORD, the God of Israel,
‘Surely your son Solomon will be king after me,
and he will sit on my throne in my place,’
so I will surely do it today.”
[31] Then Bathsheba bowed with her face to the ground,
showed respect to the king, and said,
“May my lord King David live forever!”
[32] King David said,
“Call Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada to me.”
They came before the king.
[33] The king said to them,
“Take with you the servants of your lord.
Have my son Solomon ride on my own mule,
and bring him down to Gihon.
[34] Let Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet anoint him there as king over Israel.
Blow the trumpet and say,
‘Long live King Solomon!’
[35] Then come up after him.
He is to come and sit on my throne.
For he will be king in my place.
I have appointed him to be prince over Israel and over Judah.”
[36] Benaiah the son of Jehoiada answered the king and said,
“Amen. May the LORD, the God of my lord the king, say so too.
[37] As the LORD has been with my lord the king,
so may he be with Solomon,
and may he make his throne even greater than the throne of my lord King David.”
[38] So Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah the son of Jehoiada,
and the Cherethites and the Pelethites went down.
They had Solomon ride on King David’s mule
and brought him to Gihon.
[39] Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the Tent and anointed Solomon.
They blew the trumpet,
and all the people said,
“Long live King Solomon!”
[40] All the people came up after him.
And the people played on flutes and rejoiced with great joy,
so that the earth shook with their noise.

> **What does this mean?**
> The old David wakes up once more. He acts quickly and firmly.
> Solomon rides on David’s own mule. That is a sign for everyone: this is the king’s successor.
> Gihon is a spring in the valley east of Jerusalem, not far from En Rogel, where Adonijah is celebrating. The two celebrations take place almost within earshot of each other.
> Solomon is anointed, like David and Saul before him. He is anointed with oil from the Tent in which the ark stood.
> “Long live King Solomon!”: the people celebrate so loudly that the earth shakes.

## Adonijah hears the news (Verses 41–53)

[41] Adonijah and all the guests who were with him heard it
just as they had finished eating.
When Joab heard the sound of the trumpet, he said,
“What is this noise?
Why is the city in an uproar?”
[42] While he was still speaking,
Jonathan the son of Abiathar the priest came.
Adonijah said,
“Come in, for you are a worthy man, and you bring good news.”
[43] Jonathan answered Adonijah,
“Truly, our lord King David has made Solomon king.
[44] The king has sent with him Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah the son of Jehoiada,
and the Cherethites and the Pelethites.
And they have had him ride on the king’s mule.
[45] Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet have anointed him king at Gihon.
And they have come up from there rejoicing,
so that the city is ringing with it.
That is the noise that you have heard.
[46] And Solomon is already sitting on the throne of the kingdom.
[47] And the king’s servants have come
to bless our lord King David.
They said,
‘May your God make the name of Solomon better than your name,
and make his throne greater than your throne.’
And the king bowed down on his bed.
[48] And the king also said this:
‘Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
who today has given someone to sit on my throne,
and my eyes can still see it.’”
[49] Then all of Adonijah’s guests were afraid.
They got up, and each one went his way.
[50] Adonijah was afraid of Solomon.
He got up, went, and held on to the horns of the altar.
[51] Solomon was told,
“Adonijah is afraid of King Solomon.
He is holding on to the horns of the altar and saying,
‘Let King Solomon first swear to me
that he will not kill his servant with the sword.’”
[52] Solomon said,
“If he shows himself a worthy man,
not one hair of his will fall to the ground.
But if wickedness is found in him, he will die.”
[53] So King Solomon sent for him,
and they brought him down from the altar.
He came and bowed down to King Solomon.
And Solomon said to him,
“Go to your house.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the feast the news comes. Adonijah expects good news, but it is the end of his plans. All the guests run away.
> Adonijah flees to the altar and holds on to its “horns,” the corners of the altar. The altar was a place of protection. Whoever held on to it could not be killed, unless he had murdered someone on purpose (Exodus 21:14).
> At the beginning Solomon is generous: if you behave well, nothing will happen to you. But he sets a clear condition.
