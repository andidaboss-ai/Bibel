# 1 Kings – Chapter 15
### The kings Abijam, Asa, Nadab, and Baasha

---

## Abijam, king of Judah (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, the son of Nebat,
Abijam became king over Judah.
[2] He ruled for three years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Maacah, the daughter of Abishalom.
[3] He committed all the same sins that his father had committed before him.
His heart was not fully devoted to the LORD his God,
as the heart of his father David had been.
[4] But for David’s sake the LORD his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem.
That means: God let Abijam have a son who became king after him,
and God kept Jerusalem strong.
[5] David had done what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
All his life he had not turned away from anything that the LORD commanded him.
The only exception was the matter of Uriah the Hittite.
[6] There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam as long as he lived.
[7] The rest of Abijam’s story and everything he did
are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah.
There was war between Abijam and Jeroboam.
[8] Abijam died and joined his ancestors.
They buried him in David’s city.
His son Asa became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> From now on the book takes turns telling about the kings in the south (Judah) and in the north (Israel). The years are always compared with the other kingdom: “In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam.”
> Abishalom is probably Absalom, the son of David. Then Maacah was his daughter or granddaughter.
> In 2 Chronicles 13 this king is called Abijah.
> Again we hear: God stays faithful “for David’s sake.” The “lamp” in Jerusalem does not go out.
> Verse 5 is honest: David, too, was not without guilt. His great wrong was the matter of Bathsheba and her husband Uriah (2 Samuel 11). The Bible does not keep quiet about it.
> Verse 6: here it says “Rehoboam” once again. Some old copies of the Bible have “Abijam” instead. In any case, the war between north and south went on.
> Abijam ruled from about 913 to 911 BC.

---

## Asa, a good king of Judah (Verses 9–15)

[9] In the twentieth year of Jeroboam, the king of Israel,
Asa became king over Judah.
[10] He ruled for 41 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Maacah, the daughter of Abishalom.
[11] Asa did what was right in the LORD’s eyes, as his father David had done.
[12] He sent the sodomites out of the land.
He removed all the idols that his fathers had made.
[13] He also removed his mother Maacah from her place as queen.
She had made a disgusting image for Asherah.
Asa cut down her image and burned it at the brook Kidron.
[14] But the high places were not removed.
Even so, Asa’s heart was fully devoted to the LORD as long as he lived.
[15] He brought into the LORD’s house the gifts that his father had set apart for God.
He also brought the gifts that he himself had set apart:
silver, gold, and other objects.

> **What does this mean?**
> Finally a good king! Asa clears away the worship of idols.
> “As his father David”: this means his ancestor David. In Hebrew “father” can also mean “ancestor.” It is the same with “his mother Maacah”: according to verse 2, Maacah was the mother of Abijam, so she was really Asa’s grandmother. As “queen mother” she had an important position at the royal court.
> Asa removes her because she made an idol. That takes courage: he puts God above his own family.
> The “high places” were altars on hills all over the land. Many of them were probably dedicated to the LORD, but according to Deuteronomy 12 people were to sacrifice only at the one place that God chooses. Asa did not do away with them. Even so, the Bible praises his honest heart.
> The brook Kidron flows in the valley east of Jerusalem.
> In verse 12 our English source text again says “sodomites.” See the explanation for 1 Kings 14:24.

## Asa and Baasha at war (Verses 16–24)

[16] There was war between Asa and Baasha, the king of Israel, as long as they lived.
[17] Baasha, the king of Israel, attacked Judah.
He built up the town of Ramah into a fortress.
He wanted to stop anyone from going out to Asa, the king of Judah, or coming in to him.
[18] Then Asa took all the silver and gold
that was left in the treasure rooms of the LORD’s house and of the king’s house.
He gave it to his servants.
King Asa sent them to Ben Hadad in Damascus.
Ben Hadad was the king of Syria.
He was the son of Tabrimmon, the son of Hezion.
Asa sent him this message:
[19] “Let us make a treaty between me and you,
like the one between my father and your father.
I have sent you a gift of silver and gold.
Go and break your treaty with Baasha, the king of Israel,
so that he will leave me alone.”
[20] Ben Hadad did what King Asa asked.
He sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel.
He attacked Ijon, Dan, Abel Beth Maacah, all of Chinneroth,
and all the land of Naphtali.
[21] Baasha heard about it.
Then he stopped building up Ramah.
He stayed in Tirzah.
[22] Then King Asa called all the people of Judah together.
No one was allowed to stay home.
They carried away the stones and the wood that Baasha had used to build up Ramah.
King Asa used them to build up Geba in Benjamin, and Mizpah.
[23] The rest of Asa’s story is written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah.
It tells about all his strength, all that he did, and the cities that he built.
But when he was old, he got a disease in his feet.
[24] Asa died and joined his ancestors.
He was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David.
His son Jehoshaphat became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ramah was only about 5 miles north of Jerusalem. Baasha built it up into a fortress to cut off Jerusalem.
> Asa defends himself with money instead of trust in God: he pays the king of Syria (Aram) to attack Israel in the north. The plan works. But for it he takes the treasures out of the temple. And he brings a foreign great power into the land, which later becomes dangerous for both kingdoms. Later the prophet Hanani scolds him for this (2 Chronicles 16:7–9).
> Chinneroth is the area around the Sea of Galilee. Naphtali is the land in the north of Galilee.
> In Hebrew “Syria” is “Aram,” the land of the Arameans.
> Asa ruled from about 911 to 870 BC, so a very long time.

## Nadab and Baasha, kings of Israel (Verses 25–34)

[25] Nadab, the son of Jeroboam, became king over Israel.
It was in the second year of Asa, the king of Judah.
Nadab ruled over Israel for two years.
[26] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He lived the way his father had lived.
He committed the same sin that his father had made Israel commit.
[27] Baasha, the son of Ahijah, from the family of Issachar, made a plot against him.
Baasha killed him at Gibbethon, a town that belonged to the Philistines.
Nadab and all Israel were attacking Gibbethon at that time.
[28] Baasha killed him in the third year of Asa, the king of Judah.
Then Baasha became king in his place.
[29] As soon as he was king, he killed Jeroboam’s whole family.
He did not leave alive anyone in Jeroboam’s family.
He destroyed them all.
This was what the LORD had said through his servant Ahijah from Shiloh.
[30] It happened because of the sins of Jeroboam.
Jeroboam sinned, and he made Israel sin.
He made the LORD, the God of Israel, angry.
[31] The rest of Nadab’s story and everything he did
are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
[32] There was war between Asa and Baasha, the king of Israel, as long as they lived.
[33] In the third year of Asa, the king of Judah,
Baasha, the son of Ahijah, became king over all Israel.
He ruled in Tirzah for 24 years.
[34] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He lived the way Jeroboam had lived.
He committed the same sin that Jeroboam had made Israel commit.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the northern kingdom a sad series begins: a king is murdered, and the murderer becomes king. Baasha kills Nadab and the whole family of Jeroboam.
> In this way Ahijah’s word comes true (1 Kings 14:10). But that does not make Baasha’s murder good. Baasha himself is held to account for his deeds (1 Kings 16:7). The Bible shows: God can even fit evil deeds into his plan, but he does not approve of the violence.
> And Baasha makes exactly the same mistakes as Jeroboam. He has learned nothing.
> This Ahijah, Baasha’s father, is not the prophet Ahijah from Shiloh.
> Gibbethon was in the west, on the border with the land of the Philistines.
> Nadab ruled from about 909 to 908 BC, Baasha from about 908 to 886.
