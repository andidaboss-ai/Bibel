# 1 Kings – Chapter 15
### The kings Abijam, Asa, Nadab, and Baasha

---

## Abijam, king of Judah (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
Abijam became king over Judah.
[2] He ruled three years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Maacah the daughter of Abishalom.
[3] He walked in all the sins of his father, which his father had done before him.
And his heart was not wholly with the LORD his God,
as the heart of his father David had been.
[4] But for David’s sake the LORD his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem,
by setting up his son after him and letting Jerusalem stand firm.
[5] For David had done what was right in the LORD’s eyes
and had not turned away from anything that he commanded him all the days of his life,
except only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite.
[6] There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days of his life.
[7] The rest of the acts of Abijam and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
And there was war between Abijam and Jeroboam.
[8] Abijam slept with his fathers,
and they buried him in David’s city.
And his son Asa became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> From now on the book takes turns telling about the kings in the south (Judah) and in the north (Israel). The years are always compared with the other kingdom: “in the eighteenth year of Jeroboam.”
> Abishalom is probably Absalom, the son of David. Then Maacah was his daughter or granddaughter.
> In 2 Chronicles 13 this king is called Abijah.
> Again we hear: God stays faithful “for David’s sake.” The “lamp” in Jerusalem does not go out.
> Verse 5 is honest: David, too, was not without guilt. His great wrong was the matter of Bathsheba and her husband Uriah (2 Samuel 11). The Bible does not keep quiet about it.
> Verse 6: here it says “Rehoboam” once again. Some old copies of the Bible have “Abijam” instead. In any case, the war between north and south went on.
> Abijam ruled from about 913 to 911 BC.

## Asa, a good king of Judah (Verses 9–15)

[9] In the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel,
Asa became king over Judah.
[10] He ruled 41 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Maacah the daughter of Abishalom.
[11] Asa did what was right in the LORD’s eyes, as his father David had done.
[12] He put the sodomites out of the land
and removed all the idols that his fathers had made.
[13] He also removed his mother Maacah from being queen,
because she had made a disgusting image for an Asherah.
Asa cut down her image and burned it at the brook Kidron.
[14] But the high places were not taken away.
Even so, Asa’s heart was wholly with the LORD all his days.
[15] He brought into the LORD’s house the things that his father had dedicated
and the things that he himself had dedicated:
silver, gold, and other things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Finally a good king! Asa clears away the worship of idols.
> “As his father David”: this means his ancestor David. In Hebrew “father” can also mean “ancestor.” It is the same with “his mother Maacah”: according to verse 2, Maacah was the mother of Abijam, so she was really Asa’s grandmother. As “queen mother” she had an important position at the royal court.
> Asa removes her because she made an idol. That takes courage: he puts God above his own family.
> The “high places” were altars on hills all over the land. Many of them were probably dedicated to the LORD, but according to Deuteronomy 12 people were to sacrifice only at the one place that God chooses. Asa did not do away with them. Even so, the Bible praises his honest heart.
> The brook Kidron flows in the valley east of Jerusalem.
> In verse 12 our English source text again says “sodomites.” See the explanation for 1 Kings 14:24.

## Asa and Baasha at war (Verses 16–24)

[16] There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days.
[17] Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and built up Ramah,
so that he could stop anyone from going out or coming in to Asa king of Judah.
[18] Then Asa took all the silver and gold
that was left in the treasures of the LORD’s house and in the treasures of the king’s house,
and gave it to his servants.
King Asa sent them to Ben Hadad,
the son of Tabrimmon, the son of Hezion, king of Syria, who lived in Damascus,
with this message:
[19] “Let there be a treaty between me and you,
like the one between my father and your father.
I have sent you a present of silver and gold.
Go, break your treaty with Baasha king of Israel,
so that he will go away from me.”
[20] Ben Hadad listened to King Asa
and sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel.
He struck Ijon, Dan, Abel Beth Maacah, and all Chinneroth,
with all the land of Naphtali.
[21] When Baasha heard about it, he stopped building up Ramah
and stayed in Tirzah.
[22] Then King Asa called out all Judah. No one was let off.
They carried away the stones and the timber of Ramah, with which Baasha had been building.
And with them King Asa built up Geba of Benjamin, and Mizpah.
[23] The rest of all the acts of Asa,
all his strength, all that he did, and the cities that he built,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
But in his old age he became sick in his feet.
[24] Asa slept with his fathers
and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David.
And his son Jehoshaphat became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ramah was only about 5 miles north of Jerusalem. Baasha built it up into a fortress to cut off Jerusalem.
> Asa defends himself with money instead of trust in God: he pays the king of Syria (Aram) to attack Israel in the north. The plan works. But for it he takes the treasures out of the temple. And he brings a foreign great power into the land, which later becomes dangerous for both kingdoms. Later the prophet Hanani scolds him for this (2 Chronicles 16:7–9).
> Chinneroth is the area around the Sea of Galilee. Naphtali is the land in the north of Galilee.
> In Hebrew “Syria” is “Aram,” the land of the Arameans.
> Asa ruled from about 911 to 870 BC, so a very long time.

## Nadab and Baasha, kings of Israel (Verses 25–34)

[25] Nadab the son of Jeroboam became king over Israel in the second year of Asa king of Judah.
He ruled over Israel two years.
[26] He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight
and walked in the way of his father
and in his sin with which he had made Israel sin.
[27] Baasha the son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, plotted against him.
Baasha struck him down at Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines.
For Nadab and all Israel were surrounding Gibbethon at the time.
[28] In the third year of Asa king of Judah, Baasha killed him
and became king in his place.
[29] As soon as he was king, he struck down the whole house of Jeroboam.
He did not leave Jeroboam anyone who breathed,
until he had destroyed him,
according to the word of the LORD that he had spoken through his servant Ahijah the Shilonite,
[30] because of the sins of Jeroboam, which he committed
and with which he made Israel sin,
because he had made the LORD, the God of Israel, angry.
[31] The rest of the acts of Nadab and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[32] There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days.
[33] In the third year of Asa king of Judah,
Baasha the son of Ahijah became king over all Israel in Tirzah,
for 24 years.
[34] He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight
and walked in the way of Jeroboam
and in his sin with which he had made Israel sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the northern kingdom a sad series begins: a king is murdered, and the murderer becomes king. Baasha kills Nadab and the whole family of Jeroboam.
> In this way Ahijah’s word comes true (1 Kings 14:10). But that does not make Baasha’s murder good. Baasha himself is held to account for his deeds (1 Kings 16:7). The Bible shows: God can even fit evil deeds into his plan, but he does not approve of the violence.
> And Baasha makes exactly the same mistakes as Jeroboam. He has learned nothing.
> This Ahijah, Baasha’s father, is not the prophet Ahijah from Shiloh.
> Gibbethon was in the west, on the border with the land of the Philistines.
> Nadab ruled from about 909 to 908 BC, Baasha from about 908 to 886.
