# 1 Kings – Chapter 3
### Solomon asks for wisdom

---

## Solomon and the high places (Verses 1–4)

[1] Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.
He took Pharaoh’s daughter as his wife
and brought her into David’s city,
until he had finished building his own house, the LORD’s house,
and the wall around Jerusalem.
[2] But the people sacrificed at the high places,
because no house had been built yet for the LORD’s name.
[3] Solomon loved the LORD
and followed the laws of his father David.
Only, he also sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.
[4] The king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there,
for that was the great high place.
Solomon offered 1,000 burnt offerings on that altar.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon marries a daughter of Pharaoh. That shows how powerful Israel has become: the king of Egypt gives his daughter to a king of Israel. In those days marriages like this were political alliances.
> But the law warned: a king should not have many wives, and he should not lead the people back to Egypt (Deuteronomy 17:16–17). Later Solomon will have many foreign wives, and they will draw his heart to other gods (Chapter 11).
> “High places” were places on hills where people offered sacrifices. Because there was no temple yet, people also sacrificed to the LORD there. Later the high places were forbidden, because people often served other gods there too.
> “Solomon loved the LORD”: at the beginning, Solomon is completely turned toward God.
> In those days the Tent of Meeting from the time of Moses stood in Gibeon (2 Chronicles 1:3).

---

## “Ask for what I should give you” (Verses 5–9)

[5] In Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at night.
And God said,
“Ask for what I should give you.”
[6] Solomon said,
“You have shown great loving kindness to your servant David, my father,
because he walked before you in truth, in righteousness, and with an honest heart.
And you have kept this great loving kindness for him
and have given him a son to sit on his throne, as it is today.
[7] And now, LORD my God,
you have made your servant king in the place of my father David.
I am only a little child.
I don’t know how to go out or come in.
[8] And your servant is in the middle of your people whom you have chosen,
a great people, so many that they cannot be numbered or counted.
[9] So give your servant an understanding heart
to judge your people,
so that I can tell the difference between good and evil.
For who is able to judge this great people of yours?”

> **What does this mean?**
> God says to Solomon: make a wish! What would you wish for?
> Solomon begins with thanks: you were so good to my father. Then he says honestly: I am still young and have no experience. I don’t know how to lead a whole people.
> “To go out or come in” means: I don’t know how to act as a leader.
> And then he does not ask for wealth or power, but for an “understanding heart.” In Hebrew it says word for word: “a listening heart.” A heart that can listen to God and to people. Only someone who listens well can judge fairly.
> That is a wonderful request, for us today too: God, give me a listening heart.

---

## God gives more than was asked (Verses 10–15)

[10] This request pleased the Lord, that Solomon had asked for this.
[11] God said to him,
“Because you have asked for this,
and have not asked for a long life for yourself,
or asked for riches for yourself,
or asked for the life of your enemies,
but have asked for understanding for yourself, to know what is just,
[12] I have done what you asked.
I have given you a wise and understanding heart,
so that there has been no one like you before you,
and no one like you will arise after you.
[13] I have also given you what you have not asked for,
both riches and honor,
so that there will be no one like you among the kings for all your days.
[14] And if you walk in my ways
and keep my laws and my commandments,
as your father David walked,
then I will give you a long life.”
[15] Solomon woke up, and it was a dream.
Then he came to Jerusalem.
He stood before the ark of the LORD’s covenant,
offered burnt offerings and peace offerings,
and made a feast for all his servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is pleased with Solomon’s request. Solomon did not think of himself, but of the people and of justice.
> And God gives him more than he asked for: wisdom, and also wealth and honor. Later Jesus said something similar: “But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well” (Matthew 6:33).
> But the long life has a condition: if you walk in my ways. God’s gifts bring a duty with them.
> After the dream Solomon goes to Jerusalem, before the ark. He celebrates with all his servants.

## The judgment between the two mothers (Verses 16–28)

[16] Then two women who were prostitutes came to the king
and stood before him.
[17] One of the women said,
“Oh, my lord, this woman and I live in the same house.
I gave birth to a child while she was in the house.
[18] On the third day after I gave birth,
this woman also gave birth.
We were together.
There was no stranger with us in the house,
just the two of us in the house.
[19] This woman’s child died in the night,
because she lay on him.
[20] She got up at midnight
and took my son from beside me while your servant was asleep.
She laid him in her arms,
and she laid her dead child in my arms.
[21] When I got up in the morning to nurse my child,
he was dead.
But when I looked at him closely in the morning,
it was not my son, the one I had given birth to.”
[22] The other woman said,
“No! The living one is my son, and the dead one is your son.”
The first one said,
“No! The dead one is your son, and the living one is my son.”
So they argued before the king.

[23] Then the king said,
“One says, ‘This is my son who is alive, and your son is the dead one.’
And the other says, ‘No! Your son is the dead one, and my son is the living one.’”
[24] The king said,
“Bring me a sword.”
So they brought a sword before the king.
[25] The king said,
“Divide the living child in two,
and give half to the one and half to the other.”
[26] Then the woman whose child was alive spoke to the king,
for her heart longed for her son:
“Oh, my lord, give her the living child,
and do not kill him in any way!”
But the other said,
“He will be neither mine nor yours. Divide him!”
[27] Then the king answered,
“Give the first woman the living child,
and certainly do not kill him.
She is his mother.”
[28] All Israel heard about the judgment that the king had made.
And they were in awe of the king.
For they saw that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice.

> **What does this mean?**
> Right after the dream, Solomon’s wisdom shows itself. Two women are fighting over a child. There are no witnesses. It is one woman’s word against the other’s.
> Something worth noticing: the women are prostitutes, people who were looked down on in society. But the king listens to them too. Justice is for everyone, not only for people who are respected.
> Solomon commands something terrible: to divide the child. But he does not mean it. He wants to test the hearts of the women.
> The real mother would rather lose her child than see him die. Her love does not think of herself. The other woman only wants neither of them to get the child. That is how the truth comes out.
> The “listening heart” that Solomon had asked for shows itself here: he does not only listen to the words, but to the heart.
> This “judgment of Solomon” is still famous all over the world today. People speak of “the wisdom of Solomon” when someone solves a difficult dispute wisely.
