# 1 Kings – Chapter 2
### David’s death and Solomon’s hard hand

---

## David’s last instructions (Verses 1–9)

[1] When the time came near for David to die,
he gave instructions to his son Solomon and said,
[2] “I am going the way of all the earth.
So be strong, and show yourself a man.
[3] Keep the instructions of the LORD your God.
Walk in his ways, and keep his statutes, his commandments, his ordinances, and his testimonies,
as it is written in the law of Moses,
so that you may do well in all that you do and wherever you turn.
[4] Then the LORD will keep his word that he spoke about me:
‘If your children are careful how they live
and walk before me in truth, with all their heart and with all their soul,
you will never be without a man on the throne of Israel.’

[5] You also know what Joab the son of Zeruiah did to me,
what he did to the two commanders of the armies of Israel,
to Abner the son of Ner and to Amasa the son of Jether.
He killed them and shed the blood of war in a time of peace.
He put the blood of war on the belt around his waist
and on the sandals on his feet.
[6] So act according to your wisdom,
and don’t let his gray head go down to Sheol in peace.
Sheol is the place of the dead.
[7] But show kindness to the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite.
Let them be among those who eat at your table.
For they came to help me in this way when I fled from your brother Absalom.

[8] And there is Shimei the son of Gera with you, the Benjamite from Bahurim.
He cursed me with a terrible curse
on the day when I went to Mahanaim.
But he came down to meet me at the Jordan,
and I swore to him by the LORD,
‘I will not put you to death with the sword.’
[9] But now do not treat him as innocent.
For you are a wise man,
and you will know what you should do to him.
You shall bring his gray head down to Sheol with blood.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am going the way of all the earth” means: I am going to die, like all people.
> David’s first advice is good: keep God’s commandments. Then your kingdom will last. God’s promise to David (2 Samuel 7) has a condition: faithfulness to God.
> But then come instructions that are hard to read. David asks Solomon to settle accounts with Joab and Shimei.
> With Joab it can be understood: he murdered two men by treachery, Abner and Amasa, and was never punished for it. Back then David was too weak to punish him (2 Samuel 3:39). Now Solomon is to bring the justice that was missed.
> With Shimei it is harder. David had sworn to him that he would not kill him. Now he tells Solomon: you are not bound by my oath. That is a dark moment at the end of David’s life.
> The Bible tells honestly that David, too, was a person with weaknesses right to the end.
> But for Barzillai, who helped David in his time of need, there is thanks and faithfulness.

## David’s death (Verses 10–12)

[10] David died and joined his ancestors
and was buried in David’s city.
[11] The time that David ruled over Israel was 40 years.
He ruled 7 years in Hebron,
and he ruled 33 years in Jerusalem.
[12] Solomon sat on the throne of his father David,
and his kingdom was firmly established.

> **What does this mean?**
> David’s death is told in just a few words. The shepherd boy from Bethlehem who defeated Goliath, the king, poet, and sinner, dies.
> “David’s city” is the fortress of Zion in Jerusalem, which David had captured.
> 2 Samuel 5:5 was more exact: seven and a half years in Hebron. Here the number is rounded.

## Adonijah asks for Abishag (Verses 13–25)

[13] Then Adonijah the son of Haggith came to Bathsheba, the mother of Solomon.
She said, “Do you come in peace?”
He said, “In peace.”
[14] And he went on,
“I have something to tell you.”
She said, “Go ahead.”
[15] He said,
“You know that the kingdom was mine,
and that all Israel was expecting me to be king.
But the kingdom has turned around and has become my brother’s,
for it came to him from the LORD.
[16] Now I have one request to ask of you.
Don’t turn me down.”
She said to him, “Go ahead.”
[17] He said,
“Please speak to King Solomon,
for he will not tell you ‘no,’
and ask him to give me Abishag the Shunammite as my wife.”
[18] Bathsheba said,
“All right. I will speak to the king for you.”

[19] So Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak to him for Adonijah.
The king got up to meet her and bowed down to her.
Then he sat down on his throne
and had a throne set up for the king’s mother.
And she sat at his right hand.
[20] Then she said,
“I have one small request to ask of you.
Don’t turn me down.”
The king said to her,
“Ask, my mother, for I will not turn you down.”
[21] She said,
“Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to your brother Adonijah as his wife.”
[22] King Solomon answered his mother,
“Why do you ask for Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah?
You might as well ask for the kingdom for him too,
for he is my older brother,
for him and for Abiathar the priest and for Joab the son of Zeruiah!”
[23] Then King Solomon swore by the LORD and said,
“May God do this to me and even more,
if Adonijah has not spoken this word against his own life.
[24] And now, as the LORD lives,
who has made me firm and set me on the throne of my father David,
and who has made me a house as he promised:
Adonijah will surely be put to death today.”
[25] King Solomon sent Benaiah the son of Jehoiada.
He struck him down, so that he died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Adonijah acts as if he has accepted his defeat. But then he asks for Abishag, the young woman who had taken care of David.
> In those days the rule was: whoever takes the women of the dead king lays claim to the throne (compare 2 Samuel 16:21–22). Solomon understands at once: this is a hidden grab for the crown.
> Bathsheba does not notice it, or she notices it and lets Adonijah walk into the trap. The text does not say.
> Solomon shows his mother great honor: he stands up, bows, and has a throne set up for her at his right hand. The king’s mother had an important position at the royal court.
> Once again Abishag is not asked. She is only a tool in the men’s struggle for power.
> Solomon has his brother killed. In this way he does not directly break his promise from chapter 1 (1 Kings 1:52), because he sees “wickedness” in Adonijah’s request.

## Abiathar and Joab (Verses 26–35)

[26] The king said to Abiathar the priest,
“Go to Anathoth, to your own fields,
for you deserve death.
But I will not put you to death at this time,
because you carried the ark of the Lord GOD before my father David,
and because you suffered in everything in which my father suffered.”
[27] So Solomon drove Abiathar out,
so that he was no longer priest to the LORD.
In this way the word of the LORD was to be fulfilled,
which he had spoken in Shiloh about the house of Eli.

[28] This news came to Joab.
For Joab had followed Adonijah,
although he had not followed Absalom.
Joab fled to the Tent of the LORD
and held on to the horns of the altar.
[29] King Solomon was told,
“Joab has fled to the Tent of the LORD,
and he is by the altar.”
Then Solomon sent Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and said,
“Go, strike him down.”
[30] Benaiah came to the Tent of the LORD and said to him,
“The king says, ‘Come out!’”
He said,
“No, I will die here.”
Benaiah brought the answer back to the king and said,
“This is what Joab said, and this is how he answered me.”
[31] The king said to him,
“Do as he has said.
Strike him down and bury him.
In this way you will take away the blood that Joab shed without cause
from me and from my father’s house.
[32] The LORD will bring his blood back on his own head.
For he attacked two men who were more righteous and better than he was,
and killed them with the sword, and my father David did not know it:
Abner the son of Ner, commander of the army of Israel,
and Amasa the son of Jether, commander of the army of Judah.
[33] So their blood will come back on the head of Joab and on the head of his descendants forever.
But for David and his descendants, for his house and for his throne,
there will be peace from the LORD forever.”
[34] Then Benaiah the son of Jehoiada went up,
struck him down, and killed him.
And he was buried in his own house in the wilderness.
[35] The king put Benaiah the son of Jehoiada over the army in his place.
And the king put Zadok the priest in the place of Abiathar.

> **What does this mean?**
> Abiathar had served David faithfully, when he was fleeing from Saul and from Absalom. That is why Solomon spares his life. But he loses his office as priest.
> In this way an old word comes true: Eli’s family had been told that they would lose the office of priest (1 Samuel 2:30–35). Abiathar was the last priest from Eli’s family.
> Anathoth was a village north of Jerusalem. Later the prophet Jeremiah came from a family of priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1).
> Joab flees to the altar like Adonijah. But the protection of the altar was not for murderers (Exodus 21:14). Joab had murdered Abner and Amasa.
> So ends the life of the man who brought David many victories, but who also shed so much blood. He dies the way he lived: by the sword.
> Benaiah becomes the new commander of the army, Zadok becomes the chief priest. His descendants remain priests until much later times.

## Shimei’s end (Verses 36–46)

[36] The king sent for Shimei and said to him,
“Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and live there.
And don’t go anywhere else from there.
[37] For on the day you go out and cross the brook Kidron,
know for certain that you will surely die.
Your blood will be on your own head.”
[38] Shimei said to the king,
“What you say is good.
As my lord the king has said, so your servant will do.”
And Shimei lived in Jerusalem for a long time.
[39] At the end of three years, two of Shimei’s slaves ran away
to Achish, son of Maacah, king of Gath.
Shimei was told,
“Your slaves are in Gath.”
[40] So Shimei got up, saddled his donkey,
and went to Achish in Gath to look for his slaves.
And Shimei went and brought his slaves back from Gath.
[41] Solomon was told
that Shimei had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and had come back again.
[42] The king sent for Shimei and said to him,
“Didn’t I make you swear by the LORD and warn you,
‘Know for certain that on the day you go out and walk anywhere else,
you will surely die’?
And you said to me,
‘What I have heard is good.’
[43] Why then have you not kept the oath of the LORD
and the commandment that I gave you?”
[44] The king also said to Shimei,
“You know in your heart all the wickedness that you did to my father David.
So the LORD will bring your wickedness back on your own head.
[45] But King Solomon will be blessed,
and the throne of David will stand firm before the LORD forever.”
[46] Then the king gave the command to Benaiah the son of Jehoiada.
He went out and struck him down, so that he died.
The kingdom was firmly established in the hand of Solomon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon gives Shimei a chance: he may live, but he must stay in Jerusalem, where he can be watched. That way he cannot stir up a revolt in the tribe of Benjamin.
> After three years Shimei breaks the agreement in order to bring back two slaves who had run away. Solomon has him killed.
> The chapter ends with the sentence: “The kingdom was firmly established in the hand of Solomon.” Solomon has removed all possible enemies: Adonijah, Joab, Shimei. Abiathar is sent away.
> This is a hard chapter. It shows what happened in those days when power changed hands. The Bible makes nothing look nicer than it was. We do not read it as an example to follow, but as an honest report about the power politics of that time.
> That two slaves run away reminds us that there was slavery in Israel in those days. Actually, the law of Moses had commanded that slaves who had run away should not be sent back (Deuteronomy 23:15).
