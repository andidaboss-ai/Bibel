# 1 Kings – Chapter 4
### Solomon’s kingdom and his wisdom

---

## Solomon’s chief officials (Verses 1–6)

[1] King Solomon was king over all Israel.
[2] These were his top officials:
Azariah, the son of Zadok, was the priest.
[3] Elihoreph and Ahijah, the sons of Shisha, were scribes.
A scribe is a writer who keeps the records.
Jehoshaphat, the son of Ahilud, was the recorder.
[4] Benaiah, the son of Jehoiada, was in charge of the army.
Zadok and Abiathar were priests.
[5] Azariah, the son of Nathan, was in charge of the officers.
Zabud, the son of Nathan, was the chief minister and the king’s friend.
[6] Ahishar was in charge of the palace.
Adoniram, the son of Abda, was in charge of the men who had to do forced labor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon has a large royal court with many officials. The kingdom is now a real state.
> We already know some of the names: Benaiah, Zadok, Jehoshaphat. The sons of Nathan may be sons of the prophet Nathan.
> Abiathar is still named as a priest, although Solomon had sent him away (1 Kings 2:27). The list probably comes from different times.
> “The king’s friend” was a title of honor for a close adviser, like Hushai for David.
> Adoniram is in charge of the forced labor. Under Solomon the burden of work for the people gets heavier and heavier. Later this will lead to the splitting of the kingdom (Chapter 12).

---

## Twelve officers (Verses 7–19)

[7] Solomon had twelve officers over all Israel.
They supplied food for the king and his household.
Each one had to supply the food for one month of the year.
[8] These are their names:
Ben Hur, in the hill country of Ephraim.
[9] Ben Deker, in Makaz, in Shaalbim, Beth Shemesh, and Elon Beth Hanan.
[10] Ben Hesed, in Arubboth.
Socoh and all the land of Hepher belonged to him.
[11] Ben Abinadab, in all the hills of Dor.
His wife was Taphath, Solomon’s daughter.
[12] Baana, the son of Ahilud, in Taanach and Megiddo.
He also had all of Beth Shean, which is next to Zarethan, below Jezreel.
His area went from Beth Shean to Abel Meholah and on past Jokmeam.
[13] Ben Geber, in Ramoth Gilead.
The towns of Jair, the son of Manasseh, in Gilead belonged to him.
The region of Argob in Bashan also belonged to him.
There were 60 great cities there, with walls and bronze bars on the gates.
[14] Ahinadab, the son of Iddo, in Mahanaim.
[15] Ahimaaz, in Naphtali.
He also married Basemath, the daughter of Solomon.
[16] Baana, the son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth.
[17] Jehoshaphat, the son of Paruah, in Issachar.
[18] Shimei, the son of Ela, in Benjamin.
[19] Geber, the son of Uri, in the land of Gilead.
This was the country of Sihon, king of the Amorites, and of Og, king of Bashan.
He was the only officer in that land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon divides the land into twelve districts. Each district has to provide food for the whole royal court for one month a year. That was a kind of tax.
> Interesting: the districts do not follow the old borders of the tribes exactly. And Judah, Solomon’s own tribe, is not named. Maybe Judah had to pay less. Later that made the northern tribes very angry.
> Two of the officers are married to daughters of Solomon. In this way Solomon ties important men to his family.
> Ahimaaz may be the son of Zadok who had served David as a messenger (2 Samuel 18). And Baana the son of Hushai may be the son of David’s faithful friend.

## Wealth and peace (Verses 20–28)

[20] There were as many people in Judah and Israel as there is sand by the sea.
They ate and drank and were happy.
[21] Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the River to the land of the Philistines.
He ruled as far as the border of Egypt.
These kingdoms brought tribute and served Solomon as long as he lived.
Tribute is money or goods that a defeated people must pay to the ruler.
[22] Each day Solomon’s court needed this much food:
30 cors of fine flour and 60 measures of meal,
[23] 10 fattened cattle, 20 cattle from the pastures, and 100 sheep.
There were also deer, gazelles, roebucks, and fattened birds.
[24] He ruled over everything on this side of the River,
from Tiphsah to Gaza.
He ruled over all the kings on this side of the River.
He had peace on all sides.
[25] Judah and Israel lived in safety, from Dan to Beersheba.
Each man lived under his vine and under his fig tree.
It was like this as long as Solomon lived.
[26] Solomon had 40,000 stalls of horses for his chariots.
He had 12,000 horsemen.
[27] The officers supplied food for King Solomon
and for all who came to eat at King Solomon’s table.
Each one did it in his month.
They made sure that nothing was missing.
[28] They also brought barley and straw for the horses and the fast horses.
They brought it to the place where the officers were.
Each one did what he was told to do.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles these verses are already chapter 5, verses 1–8. From here to the end of this chapter, the numbering is shifted by one chapter.
> This is the golden age of Israel. The people are many, they have enough to eat, they live in peace and joy. God’s promise to Abraham seems to have come true: “I will make your descendants very many, as many as the stars in the sky and as the sand on the seashore” (Genesis 22:17).
> “The River” is the Euphrates. Solomon’s power reaches from the Euphrates in the north to Egypt in the south.
> “Each man lived under his vine and under his fig tree”: this is a picture of peace and wealth: everyone can live calmly from what he grows. Later the prophets used this picture for the time of peace that God will give (Micah 4:4).
> Solomon’s court uses huge amounts. The Bible says “30 cors of fine flour and 60 measures of meal.” Here “measure” is also a cor. A cor is about 6 bushels. So 60 cors are more than 350 bushels. This shows how big the royal court was, maybe several thousand people.
> 40,000 stalls: 2 Chronicles 9:25 says 4,000. That fits better with the 12,000 horsemen.
> Horses and chariots in large numbers: the law had warned the king against this (Deuteronomy 17:16). Behind the shine, the first shadows already show.

## Solomon’s wisdom (Verses 29–34)

[29] God gave Solomon very much wisdom and understanding.
He gave him a mind as wide as the sand on the seashore.
[30] Solomon was wiser than all the people of the east.
He was wiser than all the wise people of Egypt.
[31] He was wiser than anyone else.
He was wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol.
He was famous in all the nations around.
[32] He spoke 3,000 proverbs.
A proverb is a short, wise saying.
He wrote 1,005 songs.
[33] He spoke about trees,
from the cedar in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall.
He also spoke about animals, birds, creeping animals, and fish.
[34] People from all nations came to hear Solomon’s wisdom.
All the kings of the earth had heard of his wisdom, and they sent them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon’s wisdom was famous in the whole world. In those days there were famous teachers of wisdom in Egypt and in the east, in Mesopotamia. Solomon is greater than all of them.
> Ethan and Heman were famous wise men. Psalm 88 and Psalm 89 are named after them.
> Solomon wrote proverbs and songs. Many proverbs in the book of Proverbs are said to come from him (Proverbs 1:1), and so is the Song of Songs (Song of Songs 1:1).
> Solomon is also interested in nature: plants and animals. The cedar is the biggest and most splendid tree, hyssop is a small plant that grows in cracks in walls. He observes everything, from the biggest to the smallest. In the Bible, wisdom also means understanding God’s creation.
