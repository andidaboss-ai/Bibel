# 1 Kings – Chapter 13
### The man of God from Judah

---

## A word against the altar in Bethel (Verses 1–10)

[1] A man of God came out of Judah to Bethel, sent by the word of the LORD.
Jeroboam was standing by the altar to burn incense.
[2] By the word of the LORD he cried out against the altar:
“Altar! Altar! The LORD says:
‘A son will be born to David’s house, Josiah by name.
On you he will sacrifice the priests of the high places
who burn incense on you.
And people will burn human bones on you.’”
[3] On the same day he gave a sign and said,
“This is the sign that the LORD has spoken:
the altar will split apart,
and the ashes that are on it will be poured out.”
[4] When the king heard what the man of God cried out against the altar in Bethel,
Jeroboam stretched out his hand from the altar and said,
“Seize him!”
Then his hand, which he had stretched out against him, dried up,
so that he could not pull it back to himself.
[5] The altar also split apart,
and the ashes poured out from the altar,
just as the sign had said that the man of God had given by the word of the LORD.
[6] Then the king said to the man of God,
“Please ask the LORD your God for his favor,
and pray for me, so that my hand may be given back to me.”
The man of God prayed to the LORD,
and the king’s hand was given back to him and became as it was before.
[7] The king said to the man of God,
“Come home with me and refresh yourself.
I will give you a reward.”
[8] The man of God said to the king,
“Even if you gave me half of your house,
I would not go in with you.
I would not eat bread or drink water in this place.
[9] For this is what I was commanded by the word of the LORD:
‘You shall eat no bread and drink no water,
and do not go back by the way that you came.’”
[10] So he went another way
and did not go back by the way that he had come to Bethel.

> **What does this mean?**
> A prophet from Judah, whose name we do not know, comes to Bethel, right to Jeroboam’s new altar. He announces: a king named Josiah will make this altar unclean.
> That is an amazing prediction: Josiah lived about 300 years later. 2 Kings 23:15–18 tells how he did exactly that. He burned bones on the altar there, so that the altar became unclean and could no longer be used.
> Jeroboam wants to have the prophet arrested. But his hand goes stiff. When the prophet prays for him, it becomes healthy again. The prophet even prays for the king who wanted to arrest him.
> The prophet is not allowed to eat or drink anything in this place. In those days eating together meant: we are joined to each other. The prophet is to show: there can be no fellowship with this false worship.

## The old prophet lies (Verses 11–19)

[11] An old prophet lived in Bethel.
One of his sons came and told him everything that the man of God had done that day in Bethel.
They also told their father the words that he had said to the king.
[12] Their father asked them,
“Which way did he go?”
His sons had seen which way the man of God had gone, the one who had come from Judah.
[13] He said to his sons,
“Saddle the donkey for me.”
So they saddled the donkey for him, and he rode on it.
[14] He went after the man of God and found him sitting under an oak.
He asked him,
“Are you the man of God who came from Judah?”
He answered,
“I am.”
[15] Then he said to him,
“Come home with me and eat bread.”
[16] He said,
“I may not go back with you or go in with you.
I will not eat bread or drink water with you in this place.
[17] For it was said to me by the word of the LORD,
‘You shall eat no bread or drink water there.
And do not go back by the way that you came.’”
[18] He said to him,
“I am also a prophet, as you are.
And an angel spoke to me by the word of the LORD and said,
‘Bring him back with you into your house, so that he may eat bread and drink water.’”
He lied to him.
[19] So he went back with him, ate bread in his house, and drank water.

> **What does this mean?**
> Why does the old prophet lie? The Bible does not say. Maybe he wanted to have the famous guest at his house. Maybe he wanted to test whether he would stay faithful.
> The man of God had received a clear task from God himself. Now someone comes and says: “An angel told me something different.” And he believes him.
> The lesson is serious: if God has told you something clearly, do not let others turn you away from it, not even religious people who appeal to God or an angel. Paul writes something similar in Galatians 1:8.

## The lion on the road (Verses 20–32)

[20] As they were sitting at the table,
the word of the LORD came to the prophet who had brought him back.
[21] And he cried out to the man of God who had come from Judah:
“The LORD says:
‘Because you have disobeyed the word of the LORD
and have not kept the commandment that the LORD your God gave you,
[22] but came back
and have eaten bread and drunk water in the place
of which he said to you, “Eat no bread and drink no water,”
your body will not come to the tomb of your fathers.’”
[23] After he had eaten bread and had drunk,
he saddled the donkey for the prophet whom he had brought back.
[24] When he had gone, a lion met him on the road and killed him.
His body was thrown down on the road,
and the donkey stood beside it.
The lion also stood beside the body.
[25] Men passed by
and saw the body thrown down on the road and the lion standing beside the body.
They came and told it in the city where the old prophet lived.
[26] When the prophet who had brought him back from the way heard it, he said,
“It is the man of God who disobeyed the word of the LORD.
That is why the LORD has handed him over to the lion,
which has torn him and killed him,
as the LORD had told him by his word.”
[27] He said to his sons,
“Saddle the donkey for me.”
And they saddled it.
[28] He went and found the body thrown down on the road,
and the donkey and the lion standing beside the body.
The lion had not eaten the body or torn the donkey.
[29] The prophet picked up the body of the man of God,
laid it on the donkey, and brought it back.
He came to the city of the old prophet
to mourn for him and to bury him.
[30] He laid the body in his own grave.
And they mourned over him and said,
“Alas, my brother!”
[31] After he had buried him, he said to his sons,
“When I am dead,
bury me in the tomb in which the man of God is buried.
Lay my bones beside his bones.
[32] For the word that he cried out by the word of the LORD against the altar in Bethel
and against all the houses of the high places in the cities of Samaria
will surely happen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is hard to understand. Of all people, the liar receives a real word from God. And the man who was lied to has to die.
> The text probably wants to show: God’s word is serious. If even the prophet who brings it has to die because he did not obey it, how much more serious is the word against the altar in Bethel!
> The lion acts very unnaturally: it eats neither the dead man nor the donkey. Everyone sees: this is no accident, this is a sign from God.
> “Your body will not come to the tomb of your fathers” meant: to die and be buried in a foreign place. In those days that was very bad.
> At the end the old prophet mourns honestly: “Alas, my brother!” He even wants to be buried next to him. He has understood that the man from Judah was a true prophet.
> 300 years later King Josiah spares exactly this tomb (2 Kings 23:17–18).
> “Samaria”: this city was only built later (1 Kings 16:24). Here the name is already used for the whole northern kingdom.

## Jeroboam does not turn back (Verses 33–34)

[33] After this, Jeroboam did not turn from his evil way.
Again he made priests of the high places from among all the people.
Whoever wanted to, he consecrated, so that there would be priests of the high places.
[34] This became a sin for the house of Jeroboam,
so that it was cut off and destroyed from the face of the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeroboam has experienced a miracle in his own body. He has seen the altar split apart. Even so, he changes nothing.
> Anyone who wanted to could become a priest under him. Word for word, “consecrate” here means: “to fill the hand.” It means putting someone into the office of priest.
