# 1 Kings – Chapter 13
### The man of God from Judah

---

## A word against the altar in Bethel (Verses 1–10)

[1] The LORD sent a man of God from Judah to Bethel with a message.
Jeroboam was standing by the altar to burn incense.
[2] The man of God shouted against the altar the message of the LORD:
“Altar! Altar! The LORD says:
‘A son will be born to David’s family.
His name will be Josiah.
He will sacrifice on you the priests of the high places
who burn incense on you.
Human bones will be burned on you.’”
[3] On the same day the man of God gave a sign.
He said, “This is the sign that the LORD has given:
The altar will split apart.
The ashes on it will be poured out.”
[4] King Jeroboam heard what the man of God shouted against the altar in Bethel.
He stretched out his hand from the altar and said,
“Grab him!”
But the hand that he stretched out against him dried up.
He could not pull it back.
[5] The altar also split apart,
and the ashes poured out of the altar.
It happened just as the man of God had said in the sign from the LORD.
[6] Then the king said to the man of God,
“Please ask the LORD your God to be kind to me.
Pray for me, so that my hand becomes well again.”
The man of God prayed to the LORD.
Then the king’s hand became well again, as it was before.
[7] The king said to the man of God,
“Come home with me and have something to eat.
I will give you a gift.”
[8] But the man of God said to the king,
“Even if you gave me half of everything you own,
I would not go in with you.
I will not eat bread or drink water in this place.
[9] The LORD commanded me with his word:
‘Do not eat bread, and do not drink water.
And do not go back by the same way that you came.’”
[10] So he went another way.
He did not go back by the way he had come to Bethel.

> **What does this mean?**
> A prophet from Judah, whose name we do not know, comes to Bethel, right to Jeroboam’s new altar. He announces: a king named Josiah will make this altar unclean.
> That is an amazing prediction: Josiah lived about 300 years later. 2 Kings 23:15–18 tells how he did exactly that. He burned bones on the altar there, so that the altar became unclean and could no longer be used.
> Jeroboam wants to have the prophet arrested. But his hand goes stiff. When the prophet prays for him, it becomes healthy again. The prophet even prays for the king who wanted to arrest him.
> The prophet is not allowed to eat or drink anything in this place. In those days eating together meant: we are joined to each other. The prophet is to show: there can be no fellowship with this false worship.

## The old prophet lies (Verses 11–19)

[11] An old prophet lived in Bethel.
One of his sons came and told him everything that the man of God had done that day in Bethel.
His sons also told their father what the man of God had said to the king.
[12] Their father asked them,
“Which way did he go?”
His sons had seen which way the man of God from Judah had gone.
[13] He said to his sons,
“Saddle the donkey for me.”
So they saddled the donkey for him, and he rode on it.
[14] He followed the man of God and found him sitting under an oak tree.
He asked him,
“Are you the man of God who came from Judah?”
He answered,
“I am.”
[15] Then the old prophet said to him,
“Come home with me and eat some bread.”
[16] But he said,
“I must not go back with you or go into your house.
I will not eat bread or drink water with you in this place.
[17] The LORD told me with his word,
‘Do not eat bread or drink water there.
And do not go back by the same way that you came.’”
[18] The old prophet said to him,
“I am a prophet too, just like you.
An angel gave me a message from the LORD.
He said, ‘Bring him back with you to your house, so that he can eat bread and drink water.’”
But the old prophet was lying to him.
[19] So the man of God went back with him.
He ate bread in his house and drank water.

> **What does this mean?**
> Why does the old prophet lie? The Bible does not say. Maybe he wanted to have the famous guest at his house. Maybe he wanted to test whether he would stay faithful.
> The man of God had received a clear task from God himself. Now someone comes and says: “An angel told me something different.” And he believes him.
> The lesson is serious: if God has told you something clearly, do not let others turn you away from it, not even religious people who appeal to God or an angel. Paul writes something similar in Galatians 1:8.

## The lion on the road (Verses 20–32)

[20] They were sitting at the table.
Then the word of the LORD came to the prophet who had brought him back.
[21] He shouted to the man of God from Judah,
“The LORD says:
‘You have not obeyed the word of the LORD.
You have not kept the command that the LORD your God gave you.
[22] You came back.
You ate bread and drank water in the place where he told you, “Do not eat bread, and do not drink water.”
So your body will not come to the tomb of your ancestors.’”
[23] The man of God had eaten bread and had drunk.
Then the old prophet saddled the donkey for the prophet he had brought back.
[24] The man of God left.
On the road a lion met him and killed him.
His body lay thrown down on the road.
The donkey stood beside it.
The lion also stood beside the body.
[25] Some men passed by.
They saw the body lying on the road and the lion standing beside it.
They went and told about it in the city where the old prophet lived.
[26] The prophet who had brought him back from his way heard about it.
He said,
“It is the man of God who did not obey the word of the LORD.
So the LORD has handed him over to the lion.
The lion has attacked him and killed him,
just as the LORD had told him.”
[27] He said to his sons,
“Saddle the donkey for me.”
And they saddled it.
[28] He went and found the body lying on the road.
The donkey and the lion were standing beside the body.
The lion had not eaten the body.
It had not attacked the donkey.
[29] The prophet picked up the body of the man of God.
He laid it on the donkey and brought it back.
He came to his own city to mourn for him and to bury him.
[30] He laid the body in his own grave.
They mourned over him and said,
“Oh, my brother!”
[31] After he had buried him, he said to his sons,
“When I die,
bury me in the tomb where the man of God is buried.
Lay my bones beside his bones.
[32] The LORD gave him a message against the altar in Bethel
and against all the temples on the high places in the cities of Samaria.
What he shouted will surely happen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is hard to understand. Of all people, the liar receives a real word from God. And the man who was lied to has to die.
> The text probably wants to show: God’s word is serious. If even the prophet who brings it has to die because he did not obey it, how much more serious is the word against the altar in Bethel!
> The lion acts very unnaturally: it eats neither the dead man nor the donkey. Everyone sees: this is no accident, this is a sign from God.
> “Your body will not come to the tomb of your ancestors” meant: to die and be buried in a foreign place. In those days that was very bad.
> At the end the old prophet mourns honestly: “Oh, my brother!” He even wants to be buried next to him. He has understood that the man from Judah was a true prophet.
> 300 years later King Josiah spares exactly this tomb (2 Kings 23:17–18).
> “Samaria”: this city was only built later (1 Kings 16:24). Here the name is already used for the whole northern kingdom.

## Jeroboam does not turn back (Verses 33–34)

[33] Even after this, Jeroboam did not turn away from his evil ways.
Again he made priests for the high places from all kinds of people.
Anyone who wanted to could become a priest.
Jeroboam consecrated him as a priest for the high places.
To consecrate means: to set someone apart for a holy task.
[34] This was the sin of Jeroboam’s family.
Because of it, his family was cut off and destroyed from the face of the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeroboam has experienced a miracle in his own body. He has seen the altar split apart. Even so, he changes nothing.
> Anyone who wanted to could become a priest under him. Word for word, “consecrate” here means: “to fill the hand.” It means putting someone into the office of priest.
