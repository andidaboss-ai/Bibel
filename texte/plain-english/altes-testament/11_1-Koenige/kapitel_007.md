# 1 Kings – Chapter 7
### Solomon’s palace and the furnishings for the temple

---

## Solomon’s palace (Verses 1–12)

[1] Solomon spent 13 years building his own house,
and he finished his whole house.
[2] For he built the House of the Forest of Lebanon.
It was about 150 feet long, 75 feet wide, and 45 feet high,
on four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams on the pillars.
[3] It was covered with cedar above,
over the 45 beams that were on the pillars, 15 in a row.
[4] There were beams in three rows,
and window was facing window in three rows.
[5] All the doors and posts were made square with beams,
and window was facing window in three rows.
[6] He made the hall of pillars.
It was about 75 feet long and 45 feet wide,
with a porch in front of them,
and pillars and a threshold in front of them.
[7] He made the hall of the throne, where he was to judge, the hall of judgment.
It was covered with cedar from floor to floor.
[8] His house where he was to live, in the other court behind the hall,
was built in the same way.
He also made a house for Pharaoh’s daughter, whom Solomon had taken as his wife,
like this hall.
[9] All these buildings were made of costly stones,
of stones cut to measure and sawed with saws, inside and outside,
from the foundation to the top edge,
and on the outside to the great court.
[10] The foundation was made of costly stones, great stones,
stones of about 15 feet and stones of about 12 feet.
[11] Above them were costly stones, cut to measure, and cedar wood.
[12] The great court around had three rows of cut stone and one row of cedar beams,
like the inner court of the LORD’s house and the porch of the house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Solomon spent 7 years building the temple, but 13 years building his own palace (1 Kings 6:38). And the palace was much bigger than the temple. The storyteller simply puts the two side by side. You can ask yourself: what was more important to Solomon?
> The Bible gives the measurements in cubits: the House of the Forest of Lebanon was 100 cubits long, 50 wide, and 30 high, the hall of pillars 50 by 30 cubits, the foundation stones 10 and 8 cubits. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The building was called the “House of the Forest of Lebanon” because it had so many pillars of cedar wood from Lebanon. Inside it looked like a forest. Later weapons were also kept there (1 Kings 10:17).
> In the hall of judgment the king judged cases, as Solomon did in the dispute of the two mothers (Chapter 3).
> “From floor to floor”: that is what the Hebrew text says. Maybe it means: from the floor to the ceiling beams.

## Hiram, the bronze worker (Verses 13–14)

[13] King Solomon sent for Hiram and brought him from Tyre.
[14] He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali,
and his father was a man of Tyre, a worker in bronze.
He was filled with wisdom, understanding, and skill
to do all kinds of work in bronze.
He came to King Solomon and did all his work.

> **What does this mean?**
> This Hiram is not King Hiram, but a craftsman with the same name.
> He is half Israelite, half foreigner. He is said to be “filled with wisdom,” like Bezalel, who built the Tabernacle in the wilderness (Exodus 31:3). Skill with one’s hands is also a gift of God.

## The two pillars, Jachin and Boaz (Verses 15–22)

[15] He shaped the two pillars of bronze.
Each was about 27 feet high,
and a line of about 18 feet went around each of them.
[16] He made two capitals of cast bronze
to set on the tops of the pillars.
A capital is the top part of a pillar.
One capital was about 7½ feet high,
and the other capital was about 7½ feet high.
[17] There were nets of checker work and wreaths of chain work for the capitals on the top of the pillars,
seven for one capital and seven for the other capital.
[18] So he made the pillars.
And there were two rows of pomegranates around the one network,
to cover the capitals on the top of the pillars.
And he did the same for the other capital.
[19] The capitals on the top of the pillars in the porch
were made like lilies, about 6 feet.
[20] There were also capitals above on both pillars,
close to the rounded part that was beside the network.
And there were 200 pomegranates in rows around the other capital.
[21] He set up the pillars at the porch of the temple.
He set up the right pillar and named it Jachin.
And he set up the left pillar and named it Boaz.
[22] On the tops of the pillars was lily work.
So the work on the pillars was finished.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible gives the measurements in cubits: the pillars were 18 cubits high and 12 cubits around, the capitals 5 cubits high each, the lily work 4 cubits.
> The two pillars stood by themselves in front of the entrance of the temple. They did not hold anything up. They were signs.
> Their names have a meaning: “Jachin” means “He (God) will make firm.” “Boaz” probably means “In him is strength.” Whoever went into the temple was reminded: God gives support and strength. Boaz was also the name of David’s great-grandfather (Ruth 4).
> Pomegranates and lilies were signs of fruitfulness, life, and beauty.
> Some details in these verses are hard to understand in Hebrew. Bible scholars imagine the capitals in different ways.

## The sea of bronze (Verses 23–26)

[23] He made the cast sea.
It was about 15 feet from rim to rim, round in shape.
It was about 7½ feet high.
And a line of about 45 feet went around it.
[24] Under its rim all around there were buds that went around it
for about 15 feet, all around the sea.
The buds were in two rows,
cast together with it when it was cast.
[25] It stood on 12 oxen:
3 looked toward the north, 3 looked toward the west,
3 looked toward the south, and 3 looked toward the east.
The sea was set on top of them,
and their back parts were all turned inward.
[26] It was a handbreadth thick.
A handbreadth is about 3 inches.
Its rim was made like the rim of a cup, like a lily flower.
It held about 11,500 gallons.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “sea” was a huge water basin made of bronze. 2 Chronicles 4:6 tells us what it was for: the priests washed in it.
> The Bible says: 10 cubits across, 5 cubits high, 30 cubits around. It held 2,000 baths. A bath is about 6 gallons.
> Some people ask: if it is 10 cubits across, it should be more than 31 cubits around, not 30. The Bible rounds the number here. Or the width was measured on the outside of the rim, and the line around it was measured below. The Bible is not a math book. It describes the basin in round numbers.
> In verse 24 the WEB, our English source text, says “ten cubits.” The Hebrew probably means “ten to a cubit,” that is, 10 buds for each cubit.
> The 12 oxen remind us of the 12 tribes of Israel.
> The word “sea” may also remind us that God rules over the sea and has tamed it (Genesis 1; Psalm 93).

## The 10 carts with basins (Verses 27–39)

[27] He made the 10 stands of bronze.
Each stand was about 6 feet long, 6 feet wide, and 4½ feet high.
[28] The stands were made like this:
they had panels, and the panels were between the frames.
[29] On the panels between the frames were lions, oxen, and cherubim.
And on the frames there was a stand above.
Below the lions and oxen were hanging wreaths.
[30] Each stand had four bronze wheels and bronze axles.
And its four feet had supports.
The supports were cast below the basin, with wreaths at the side of each.
[31] Its opening inside the top and above was about 18 inches.
The opening was round like the work of a stand, about 27 inches.
There were also carvings at its opening.
And their panels were square, not round.
[32] The four wheels were under the panels,
and the axles of the wheels were on the stand.
A wheel was about 27 inches high.
[33] The wheels were made like the wheels of a chariot.
Their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all cast metal.
[34] There were four supports at the four corners of each stand.
The supports were of one piece with the stand.
[35] On the top of the stand there was a round band, about 9 inches high.
And on the top of the stand, its supports and its panels were of one piece with it.
[36] On the surfaces of its supports and on its panels
he carved cherubim, lions, and palm trees,
each in its space, with wreaths all around.
[37] So he made the 10 stands.
All of them had one casting, one measure, and one form.
[38] He made 10 basins of bronze.
One basin held about 230 gallons.
Each basin measured about 6 feet.
There was one basin on each of the 10 stands.
[39] He set the stands,
five on the right side of the house and five on the left side of the house.
He set the sea on the right side of the house, toward the east and the south.

> **What does this mean?**
> These were water basins on wheels that could be moved. In them the parts of the animals for the offerings were washed (2 Chronicles 4:6).
> The Bible gives the measurements in cubits: the stands were 4 by 4 by 3 cubits, the opening 1 and 1½ cubits, the wheels 1½ cubits high, the band half a cubit. Each basin held 40 baths and measured 4 cubits.
> Archaeologists have found similar smaller carts with basins from this time in Cyprus. In Hebrew the description is hard to understand in many places.
> Again we see the pictures of lions, oxen, cherubim, and palm trees: strength, fruitfulness, and God’s nearness.

## More of Hiram’s work (Verses 40–47)

[40] Hiram made the pots, the shovels, and the basins.
So Hiram finished all the work that he did for King Solomon in the LORD’s house:
[41] the two pillars;
the two bowls of the capitals on the top of the pillars;
the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals on the top of the pillars;
[42] the 400 pomegranates for the 2 networks,
2 rows of pomegranates for each network,
to cover the 2 bowls of the capitals on the pillars;
[43] the 10 stands and the 10 basins on the stands;
[44] the single sea and the 12 oxen under the sea;
[45] the pots, the shovels, and the basins.
All these things that Hiram made for King Solomon for the LORD’s house
were made of polished bronze.
[46] The king cast them in the plain of the Jordan,
in the clay ground between Succoth and Zarethan.
[47] Solomon left all the things unweighed, because there were so very many of them.
The weight of the bronze could not be found out.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here everything is counted up once more, like in a list.
> The large bronze pieces were cast in the Jordan valley. The clay ground there was good for making molds.
> So much bronze was used that it could not be weighed. This shows the great wealth.

## The gold furnishings (Verses 48–51)

[48] Solomon made all the things that were in the LORD’s house:
the golden altar,
and the table of gold on which the show bread lay;
[49] and the lampstands of pure gold,
five on the right side and five on the left, in front of the inner room;
and the flowers, the lamps, and the tongs of gold;
[50] the cups, the snuffers, the basins, the spoons, and the fire pans of pure gold;
and the hinges of gold, both for the doors of the inner house, the most holy place,
and for the doors of the house, the main hall.
[51] So all the work that King Solomon did in the LORD’s house was finished.
Solomon brought in the things that his father David had dedicated:
the silver, the gold, and the vessels.
He put them in the treasuries of the LORD’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Outside there was bronze, inside there was gold. The closer you came to the most holy place, the more precious everything became.
> The “golden altar” was the altar of incense. The 12 loaves of show bread lay on the table (Leviticus 24:5–9). In the Tabernacle there was one lampstand with seven branches. In the temple there were 10 lampstands.
> Solomon also brings in the treasures that David had collected. So the temple is the shared work of father and son.
