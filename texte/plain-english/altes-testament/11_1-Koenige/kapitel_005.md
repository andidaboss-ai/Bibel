# 1 Kings – Chapter 5
### Getting ready to build the temple

---

## Solomon writes to Hiram (Verses 1–6)

[1] Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon,
for he had heard that they had anointed him king in the place of his father.
And Hiram had always loved David.
[2] Solomon sent a message to Hiram:
[3] “You know that my father David could not build a house for the name of the LORD his God
because of the wars that were around him on every side,
until the LORD put his enemies under the soles of his feet.
[4] But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side.
There is no enemy and no misfortune.
[5] So I plan to build a house for the name of the LORD my God,
as the LORD said to my father David:
‘Your son, whom I will set on your throne in your place,
will build the house for my name.’
[6] So now give the command that cedar trees be cut for me in Lebanon.
My servants will be with your servants.
And I will pay you wages for your servants, whatever you say.
For you know that there is no one among us who knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles this chapter is numbered differently. Verse 1 here is chapter 5, verse 15 there. Verse 18 here is verse 32 there.
> Hiram was a friend of David. He had already built a palace for David (2 Samuel 5:11).
> Solomon explains: my father could not build the temple because he had to fight wars. 1 Chronicles 22:8 gives another reason as well: David had shed too much blood.
> Now there is peace. And Solomon fulfills the promise that God had given to David (2 Samuel 7:13).
> “A house for the name of the LORD”: God does not really live in a house like a person. But his “name,” that is, his presence, is to be there.
> The Sidonians, the people of Tyre and Sidon, were famous craftsmen and seafarers. Israel itself had no experts that good.

## The agreement with Hiram (Verses 7–12)

[7] When Hiram heard Solomon’s words, he was very glad and said,
“Blessed be the LORD today,
who has given David a wise son to rule over this great people.”
[8] Hiram sent a message to Solomon:
“I have heard the message that you sent me.
I will do everything you wish with the cedar timber and the cypress timber.
[9] My servants will bring it down from Lebanon to the sea.
I will make it into rafts to go by sea to the place that you name to me.
There I will have them broken up, and you can take the timber.
And you will do what I wish, by giving food for my household.”
[10] So Hiram gave Solomon cedar timber and cypress timber, as much as he wanted.
[11] And Solomon gave Hiram 20,000 cors of wheat as food for his household,
and 20 cors of pure oil.
Solomon gave this to Hiram year after year.
[12] The LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as he had promised him.
And there was peace between Hiram and Solomon,
and the two of them made a treaty with each other.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hiram, a king who does not worship the LORD as his God, praises the God of Israel. Solomon’s fame also makes God known among other peoples.
> It is a trade agreement: timber for food. Tyre was a city by the sea with little farmland. Israel had a lot of grain.
> The timber was carried in rafts on the sea along the coast, probably to Joppa. From there it was brought to Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 2:16).
> The Bible says “20,000 cors of wheat” and “20 cors of oil.” A cor is about 6 bushels, or about 58 gallons. The old Greek translation and 2 Chronicles 2:10 have a much larger amount of oil.
> “The LORD gave Solomon wisdom”: his wisdom also shows in the fact that he makes peace with his neighbors.

## The forced labor (Verses 13–18)

[13] King Solomon drafted forced laborers out of all Israel.
There were 30,000 men.
[14] He sent them to Lebanon, 10,000 a month, taking turns.
They were in Lebanon for one month and at home for two months.
And Adoniram was over the men who had to do forced labor.
[15] Solomon had 70,000 who carried loads
and 80,000 who cut stone in the mountains,
[16] besides Solomon’s chief officers who were over the work:
3,300 who ruled over the people who did the work.
[17] The king gave the command,
and they cut out large stones, costly stones,
to lay the foundation of the house with shaped stone.
[18] Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders and the Gebalites
cut them and prepared the timber and the stones to build the house.

> **What does this mean?**
> To build the temple, Solomon needs many workers. He forces men from Israel to work. They have to take turns working in Lebanon for one month, and then they may go home for two months.
> This reminds us of Samuel’s warning: the king will take your sons for his work (1 Samuel 8:16). And it reminds us of the forced labor that Israel once had to do in Egypt (Exodus 1:11). The splendid temple was built with the burden of many people.
> Chapter 9 says that Solomon did not make Israelites into slaves (1 Kings 9:22). The 150,000 who carried loads and cut stone were probably mostly non-Israelites who were still living in the land (2 Chronicles 2:17–18).
> 2 Chronicles 2:18 says 3,600 overseers instead of 3,300.
> Gebal is the city of Byblos in what is Lebanon today. Its people were famous stonemasons.
