# 1 Kings – Chapter 20
### Ahab’s wars against Syria

---

## Ben Hadad surrounds Samaria (Verses 1–12)

[1] Ben Hadad the king of Syria gathered his whole army.
32 kings were with him, with horses and chariots.
He went up, surrounded Samaria, and fought against it.
[2] He sent messengers into the city to Ahab king of Israel and said to him,
“Ben Hadad says:
[3] ‘Your silver and your gold are mine.
Your wives and your children, the best ones, are mine too.’”
[4] The king of Israel answered,
“It is as you say, my lord the king.
I am yours, with all that I have.”
[5] The messengers came again and said,
“Ben Hadad says:
‘I sent to you and said,
“You must give me your silver, your gold, your wives, and your children.”
[6] But tomorrow about this time I will send my servants to you.
They will search your house and the houses of your servants.
Whatever you like, they will take in their hands and carry away.’”
[7] Then the king of Israel called all the elders of the land and said,
“Please notice how this man is looking for trouble.
He sent to me for my wives and my children,
for my silver and my gold,
and I did not refuse him.”
[8] All the elders and all the people said to him,
“Don’t listen, and don’t agree.”
[9] So he said to the messengers of Ben Hadad,
“Tell my lord the king:
‘Everything you first asked of your servant I will do.
But this thing I cannot do.’”
The messengers left and brought him the answer.
[10] Ben Hadad sent word to him:
“May the gods do this to me and even more,
if the dust of Samaria is enough for each of the people who follow me to get a handful.”
[11] The king of Israel answered,
“Tell him:
‘Don’t let the one who puts on his armor boast like the one who takes it off.’”
[12] Ben Hadad heard this answer while he was drinking with the kings in the tents.
He said to his servants,
“Get ready to attack!”
And they got ready to attack the city.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ben Hadad was the king of the Arameans in Damascus. “Ben Hadad” means “son of Hadad.” Hadad was a weather god. Several kings had this name.
> The 32 kings were probably rulers of small cities and tribes who were his allies.
> Ben Hadad wants to humiliate Ahab. First Ahab is to submit. Then Ben Hadad even wants to have Ahab’s house plundered. Then the elders say: enough!
> Verse 10 means: we are so many that there will not even be enough dust left of Samaria for each of us.
> Ahab’s answer is a proverb: someone who is only going into battle should not boast like someone who has already won. In other words: don’t celebrate too soon.

## An unexpected victory (Verses 13–22)

[13] A prophet came to Ahab king of Israel and said,
“The LORD says:
‘Have you seen this whole great crowd?
I will hand it over to you today.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.’”
[14] Ahab asked,
“Through whom?”
He said,
“The LORD says:
‘Through the young men of the leaders of the provinces.’”
Then he asked,
“Who should begin the battle?”
He answered,
“You.”
[15] So he called up the young men of the leaders of the provinces.
There were 232.
After them he called up all the people, all the people of Israel: 7,000.
[16] They went out at noon.
But Ben Hadad was drinking himself drunk in the tents,
he and the kings, the 32 kings who were helping him.
[17] The young men of the leaders of the provinces went out first.
Ben Hadad sent out scouts, and they told him,
“Men are coming out from Samaria.”
[18] He said,
“If they have come out for peace, take them alive.
And if they have come out for war, take them alive too.”
[19] So they went out of the city,
the young men of the leaders of the provinces and the army that followed them.
[20] Each one killed his man.
The Syrians fled, and Israel chased them.
Ben Hadad the king of Syria escaped on a horse with some horsemen.
[21] The king of Israel went out,
struck the horses and the chariots,
and brought a great defeat on the Syrians.
[22] The prophet came to the king of Israel and said to him,
“Go, make yourself strong, and think about what you must do.
For when the year comes around again, the king of Syria will come up against you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Even though Ahab is a bad king, God helps Israel. He even sends a prophet. The reason: “Then you will know that I am the LORD.” God gives Ahab one more chance to know him.
> The army is small: at first only 232 young men. They attack at noon, when Ben Hadad is already drunk. God does not win through large numbers.
> “When the year comes around again” means: in spring. In those days military campaigns began in spring.

## God of the hills and of the valleys (Verses 23–30)

[23] The servants of the king of Syria said to him,
“Their god is a god of the hills.
That is why they were stronger than we were.
But let’s fight against them in the plain,
and we will surely be stronger than they are.
[24] Do this:
remove the kings, each one from his place,
and put captains in their place.
[25] Gather an army like the army that you lost,
horse for horse and chariot for chariot.
We will fight against them in the plain,
and we will surely be stronger than they are.”
He listened to their advice and did so.
[26] When the year came around again,
Ben Hadad called up the Syrians
and went up to Aphek to fight against Israel.
[27] The people of Israel were also called up and given supplies,
and they went out against them.
The people of Israel camped opposite them like two little flocks of young goats.
But the Syrians filled the countryside.
[28] A man of God came and said to the king of Israel,
“The LORD says:
‘Because the Syrians have said,
“The LORD is a god of the hills, but he is not a god of the valleys,”
I will hand over this whole great crowd to you.
And you will know that I am the LORD.’”
[29] They camped opposite each other for seven days.
On the seventh day the battle began.
The people of Israel killed 100,000 Syrian foot soldiers in one day.
[30] The rest fled to Aphek, into the city.
Then the wall fell on the 27,000 men who were left.
Ben Hadad fled and came into the city, into an inner room.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Arameans thought: each god is only in charge of a certain area. The God of Israel is a god of the hills, so we will fight in the valley.
> God shows: I am not tied to one place. I am the Lord over hills and valleys, over the whole world.
> Aphek was probably east of the Sea of Galilee, on a plain.
> The numbers are very large. In old reports of wars, numbers were often exaggerated or given in round numbers. Some experts think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” here means a military unit.

## Ahab sets Ben Hadad free (Verses 31–34)

[31] His servants said to him,
“Look, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful kings.
Please let us put sackcloth around our waists and ropes on our heads,
and go out to the king of Israel.
Maybe he will spare your life.”
[32] So they put sackcloth around their waists and ropes on their heads,
came to the king of Israel, and said,
“Your servant Ben Hadad says, ‘Please let me live.’”
He said,
“Is he still alive? He is my brother.”
[33] The men were watching closely
and quickly took up this word and said,
“Your brother Ben Hadad!”
He said,
“Go, bring him.”
Then Ben Hadad came out to him,
and he had him come up into his chariot.
[34] Ben Hadad said to him,
“I will give back the cities that my father took from your father.
And you may set up market streets for yourself in Damascus,
as my father did in Samaria.”
Ahab said,
“With this agreement I will let you go.”
So he made an agreement with him and let him go.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sackcloth (rough cloth) and ropes around the neck were signs of surrender and of asking for mercy.
> Ahab calls his enemy “my brother.” Among kings that meant: we are allies of equal rank. Ahab makes a good deal for himself: cities and trading rights.
> At first sight this looks generous. But God had given the victory, and Ahab does not ask God what he should do. He only acts for his own advantage. Ben Hadad will fight against Israel again later.

## A prophet with a parable (Verses 35–43)

[35] By the word of the LORD a man from the sons of the prophets said to his companion,
“Please strike me!”
But the man refused to strike him.
[36] Then he said to him,
“Because you have not obeyed the voice of the LORD,
as soon as you leave me, a lion will kill you.”
And as soon as he left him, a lion found him and killed him.
[37] Then he found another man and said,
“Please strike me!”
The man struck him and wounded him.
[38] So the prophet went and waited for the king by the road.
He disguised himself with his headband over his eyes.
[39] As the king passed by, he called out to the king,
“Your servant went out into the middle of the battle.
And a man came over and brought a man to me and said,
‘Guard this man!
If he goes missing in any way, your life will be for his life,
or else you must pay a talent of silver.’
[40] While your servant was busy here and there, he was gone.”
The king of Israel said to him,
“So your judgment will be. You yourself have decided it.”
[41] Then he quickly took the headband away from his eyes.
And the king of Israel recognized that he was one of the prophets.
[42] He said to him,
“The LORD says:
‘Because you have let go out of your hand the man whom I had set apart for destruction,
your life will take the place of his life,
and your people will take the place of his people.’”
[43] The king of Israel went home sullen and angry,
and came to Samaria.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “sons of the prophets” were groups of students who lived and learned together with a prophet.
> The prophet needs a real wound so that he looks like a wounded soldier. The story with the lion is hard. It reminds us of Chapter 13: a clear word of God must be taken seriously.
> The prophet tells the king a parable, as Nathan once did with David (2 Samuel 12). With his own judgment, Ahab declares himself guilty.
> The Bible says “a talent of silver.” A talent is about 75 pounds. That was a huge sum, which a simple soldier could never pay.
> “Set apart for destruction” means: God had handed Ben Hadad over to judgment. Ahab set him free for his own advantage. It is important to see: this is about a king who is waging war. It is not a command to people today to kill others.
> Ahab is “sullen and angry.” He is upset, but he does not turn back.
