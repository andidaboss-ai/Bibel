# 1 Kings – Chapter 17
### Elijah, the ravens, and the widow

---

## Elijah announces the drought (Verses 1–7)

[1] Elijah the Tishbite, one of the settlers of Gilead, said to Ahab,
“As the LORD, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I stand:
in these years there will be no dew or rain,
except at my word.”
[2] Then the word of the LORD came to him:
[3] “Go away from here, turn east,
and hide yourself by the brook Cherith, which is east of the Jordan.
[4] You will drink from the brook.
And I have commanded the ravens to feed you there.”
[5] So he went and did what the LORD had said.
He went and lived by the brook Cherith, which is east of the Jordan.
[6] The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning,
and bread and meat in the evening.
And he drank from the brook.
[7] After a while the brook dried up,
because there was no rain in the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now one of the greatest prophets of the Bible appears: Elijah. His name means “My God is the LORD.” Even his name is a message against the worship of Baal.
> Elijah announces a drought. That is a direct challenge to Baal, who was supposed to be the god of rain. Who really has power over the rain?
> “Before whom I stand” means: whom I serve.
> God takes care of Elijah in an unusual way: ravens bring him food. Ravens were seen as unclean birds. God can also provide through helpers you would not expect.
> It is not certain where Tishbe was. Gilead is the hill country east of the Jordan.
> The WEB, our English source text, says “before the Jordan.” This probably means “east of the Jordan,” because Elijah is to “turn east.”

---

## The widow in Zarephath (Verses 8–16)

[8] Then the word of the LORD came to him:
[9] “Get up, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and stay there.
I have commanded a widow there to take care of you.”
[10] So he got up and went to Zarephath.
When he came to the gate of the city,
a widow was there gathering sticks.
He called to her and said,
“Please get me a little water in a jar, so that I may drink.”
[11] As she was going to get it, he called to her and said,
“Please also bring me a piece of bread.”
[12] She said,
“As the LORD your God lives,
I don’t have anything baked,
only a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug.
I am gathering two sticks.
Then I will go in and bake it for me and my son.
Then we will eat it and die.”
[13] Elijah said to her,
“Don’t be afraid.
Go and do as you have said.
But first make me a little cake from it and bring it out to me.
After that, make some for yourself and your son.
[14] For the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘The jar of flour will not run out,
and the jug of oil will not run dry,
until the day that the LORD sends rain on the earth.’”
[15] She went and did as Elijah had said.
And she, he, and her household ate for many days.
[16] The jar of flour did not run out,
and the jug of oil did not run dry,
as the LORD had said through Elijah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Of all places, God sends Elijah to Sidon, Jezebel’s homeland, right into the land of Baal. And there a poor foreign widow helps him.
> The woman has almost nothing left. She expects that she and her son will starve. Even so, she shares the last she has. That takes great trust.
> Elijah’s first words are: “Don’t be afraid.” And God takes care of all three of them, day after day.
> In Nazareth Jesus reminded people of this woman: there were many widows in Israel, but Elijah was sent to a foreign woman (Luke 4:25–26). God’s goodness is not only for one people.

## The widow’s son comes back to life (Verses 17–24)

[17] After this, the son of the woman who owned the house became sick.
His sickness was so bad that there was no breath left in him.
[18] She said to Elijah,
“What do I have to do with you, you man of God?
You have come to me to remind me of my sin
and to kill my son!”
[19] He said to her,
“Give me your son.”
He took him from her arms
and carried him up into the upper room where he was staying,
and laid him on his own bed.
[20] He cried out to the LORD and said,
“LORD my God,
have you also brought disaster on the widow I am staying with,
by killing her son?”
[21] He stretched himself out over the child three times,
cried out to the LORD, and said,
“LORD my God, please let this child’s soul come back into him.”
[22] The LORD listened to Elijah’s voice.
And the child’s soul came back into him, and he came back to life.
[23] Elijah took the child,
brought him down from the upper room into the house,
and gave him to his mother.
And Elijah said,
“Look, your son is alive.”
[24] The woman said to Elijah,
“Now I know that you are a man of God
and that the word of the LORD in your mouth is truth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The mother is in despair. She thinks: my child is dying because of my guilt. Many people have this thought when something terrible happens. The Bible shows: sickness and death are not a punishment for the mother. God gives the child his life back.
> Elijah, too, honestly complains to God: “Have you also brought disaster on the widow I am staying with, by killing her son?” We are allowed to complain to God and to ask him questions.
> This is the first time in the Bible that a dead person is raised to life. Elijah’s successor Elisha later does something similar (2 Kings 4). And Jesus also raised the son of a widow, in Nain (Luke 7:11–17).
> In Hebrew, the word “soul” here means the life force of a person.
> At the end the foreign woman confesses: the word of the LORD is truth.
