# 1 Kings – Chapter 6
### Building the temple

---

## When and how big (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the 480th year after the people of Israel had come out of the land of Egypt,
in the fourth year of Solomon’s rule over Israel,
in the month of Ziv, which is the second month,
he began to build the LORD’s house.
[2] The house that King Solomon built for the LORD
was about 90 feet long, 30 feet wide, and 45 feet high.
[3] The porch in front of the main hall of the house was 30 feet long,
along the width of the house,
and 15 feet deep in front of the house.
[4] He made windows with fixed lattice work for the house.
[5] Against the wall of the house he built floors all around,
against the walls of the house all around, both of the main hall and of the inner room.
And he made side rooms all around.
[6] The lowest floor was about 7½ feet wide,
the middle one was 9 feet wide,
and the third was 10½ feet wide.
For on the outside he made ledges in the wall of the house all around,
so that the beams would not have to be set into the walls of the house.
[7] When the house was being built, it was built of stone
that had been prepared at the quarry.
No hammer or ax or any iron tool was heard in the house
while it was being built.
[8] The door to the middle side rooms was on the right side of the house.
People went up by winding stairs to the middle floor,
and from the middle floor to the third.
[9] So he built the house and finished it.
He covered the house with beams and boards of cedar.
[10] He built the floors all along the house, each about 7½ feet high.
And they rested on the house with cedar beams.

> **What does this mean?**
> The building of the temple is a high point in the history of Israel. That is why the time is given exactly: 480 years after the exodus from Egypt. 480 is 12 times 40: 12 generations of 40 years each. Maybe the number is also meant as a symbol. Scholars usually date the building of the temple to about 960 BC.
> The month of Ziv is about April to May, in spring.
> The Bible gives the measurements in cubits. A cubit is about 18 inches. The temple was 60 cubits long, 20 wide, and 30 high. The porch was 20 by 10 cubits. The floors were 5, 6, and 7 cubits wide and each 5 cubits high.
> The temple was not huge, about the size of a small church today. But it was very splendid. It was not meant for large gatherings. The people gathered outside in the courtyards. Inside, only the priests served.
> Verse 7: the stones were already shaped at the quarry. At the temple itself no noise of tools was heard. God’s house was to be built in silence and awe. Iron was also connected with war and weapons (compare Exodus 20:25).

---

## God’s promise during the building (Verses 11–14)

[11] Then the word of the LORD came to Solomon:
[12] “About this house that you are building:
if you walk in my laws
and follow my rules
and keep all my commandments and walk in them,
then I will fulfill with you my word that I spoke to your father David.
[13] I will live among the people of Israel
and will not abandon my people Israel.”
[14] So Solomon built the house and finished it.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the description of the building, God speaks. He reminds Solomon: the splendid building is not the most important thing, but obedience. God does not live with his people because of the stones and the gold, but when the people are faithful to him.
> Later the prophets warned: do not trust in the temple, as if God had to protect you no matter how you live (Jeremiah 7:4). The temple was in fact destroyed later, when the people became unfaithful to God.

## Inside the temple (Verses 15–22)

[15] He built the walls of the house on the inside with cedar boards,
from the floor of the house to the beams of the ceiling.
He covered them on the inside with wood.
And he covered the floor of the house with cypress boards.
[16] He built the back 30 feet of the house with cedar boards,
from the floor to the ceiling.
He built this inside as an inner room, as the most holy place.
[17] The main hall in front of it was 60 feet long.
[18] On the inside of the house there was cedar,
carved with buds and open flowers.
All was cedar. No stone could be seen.
[19] He prepared an inner room in the middle of the house,
to set the ark of the LORD’s covenant there.
[20] The inner room was 30 feet long, 30 feet wide, and 30 feet high.
He covered it with pure gold.
And he covered the altar with cedar.
[21] So Solomon covered the inside of the house with pure gold.
He stretched gold chains across in front of the inner room,
and he covered it with gold.
[22] He covered the whole house with gold, until the whole house was finished.
He also covered with gold the whole altar that belonged to the inner room.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple had three parts: the porch, the main hall (the holy place), and at the very back the most holy place.
> The Bible says: the most holy place was 20 cubits long, wide, and high, a perfect cube. The main hall was 40 cubits long. The cube was a sign of perfection. In the New Testament the heavenly Jerusalem is also described as a cube (Revelation 21:16).
> The ark of the covenant is put into the most holy place. Only the high priest was allowed to go in there, once a year on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16).
> The walls were covered with cedar, with carved flowers and buds, and everything was covered with gold. The temple was like a garden, like the Garden of Eden, where God and people met.
> The “altar” in front of the most holy place was the altar of incense, on which incense was burned.

## The cherubim (Verses 23–28)

[23] In the inner room he made two cherubim of olive wood,
each 15 feet high.
[24] One wing of the cherub was 7½ feet long,
and the other wing of the cherub was 7½ feet long.
From the tip of one wing to the tip of the other wing was 15 feet.
[25] The other cherub was also 15 feet.
Both cherubim had the same size and the same shape.
[26] One cherub was 15 feet high, and so was the other cherub.
[27] He set the cherubim inside the inner room.
The wings of the cherubim were spread out,
so that the wing of one touched one wall
and the wing of the other cherub touched the other wall.
And their wings touched each other in the middle of the room.
[28] He covered the cherubim with gold.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cherubim are angel-like beings with wings. In the Bible they guarded what is holy. After the first sin, cherubim guarded the way to the tree of life (Genesis 3:24).
> The Bible says: each cherub was 10 cubits high, each wing 5 cubits long. Together their wings reached from wall to wall, across the whole width of the most holy place: 20 cubits.
> The ark of the covenant stood under the wings of the cherubim. The ark was seen as the footstool of God’s throne. People thought of God as sitting on his throne, unseen, above the cherubim (1 Samuel 4:4).
> Important: there was no image of God in the temple. The place above the cherubim was empty. God cannot be pictured (Exodus 20:4).

## Carvings and doors (Verses 29–36)

[29] On all the walls of the house all around he carved figures of cherubim,
palm trees, and open flowers, inside and outside.
[30] He covered the floor of the house with gold, inside and outside.
[31] For the entrance of the inner room he made doors of olive wood.
The top beam and the door posts were a fifth part of the wall.
[32] So he made two doors of olive wood.
He carved on them figures of cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers,
and covered them with gold.
He spread the gold over the cherubim and over the palm trees.
[33] For the entrance of the main hall he also made door posts of olive wood,
out of a fourth part of the wall,
[34] and two doors of cypress wood.
The two leaves of one door were folding,
and the two leaves of the other door were folding.
[35] He carved cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers,
and covered them with gold fitted exactly on the carving.
[36] He built the inner court
with three rows of cut stone and one row of cedar beams.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everywhere in the temple there are cherubim, palm trees, and flowers. It is like a picture of paradise. Whoever enters the temple is meant to feel as if they were in God’s garden.
> “A fifth part of the wall” and “a fourth part of the wall”: the Hebrew words in verses 31 and 33 are hard to understand. Maybe they describe the shape of the door frames.
> The “inner court” was the court of the priests, right around the temple. The great altar for burnt offerings stood there.

## Seven years of building (Verses 37–38)

[37] In the fourth year, in the month of Ziv, the foundation of the LORD’s house was laid.
[38] And in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month,
the house was finished in all its parts and according to all its plans.
So he spent seven years building it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month of Bul is about October to November.
> From the second month of the fourth year to the eighth month of the eleventh year is, counted exactly, seven and a half years. The Bible says it in round numbers: seven years. In the Bible the number seven often stands for completeness, like the seven days of creation.
> In the next chapter we will read that Solomon spent 13 years building his own palace, almost twice as long (1 Kings 7:1).
