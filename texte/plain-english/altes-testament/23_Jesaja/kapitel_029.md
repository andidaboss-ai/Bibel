# Isaiah – Chapter 29
### They honor me with their lips

---

## How terrible for Ariel (Verses 1–8)

[1] How terrible for Ariel!
Ariel, the city where David camped!
Add year to year,
let the feasts come around,
[2] then I will bring distress on Ariel,
and there will be mourning and lament.
She will be to me like an altar hearth.
[3] I will camp all around you
and surround you with troops.
I will build siege works against you.
[4] You will be brought low
and will speak from the ground.
Your words will mumble out of the dust.
Your voice will come out of the ground
like the voice of someone who talks with the spirits of the dead,
and your words will whisper out of the dust.
[5] But the crowd of your enemies will be like fine dust,
and the crowd of the violent like chaff that blows away.
Yes, it will happen in an instant, suddenly.
[6] She will be visited by the LORD of Armies
with thunder, with earthquake, with great noise,
with whirlwind and storm,
and with the flame of a consuming fire.
[7] The crowd of all the nations that fight against Ariel,
all who fight against her and her fortress and bring distress on her,
will be like a dream, like a vision in the night.
[8] It will be like when a hungry man dreams,
and look, he is eating,
but he wakes up, and his hunger is not satisfied.
Or like when a thirsty man dreams,
and look, he is drinking,
but he wakes up, and look, he is faint and still thirsty.
That is how it will be for the crowd of all the nations
that fight against Mount Zion.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Ariel” is a name for Jerusalem. It perhaps means “lion of God” or “altar hearth.” A play on words: Jerusalem itself will become like a burning altar.
> Verse 1: Year after year, people celebrate the feasts in Jerusalem, purely out of habit. But God will bring the city into distress.
> Verse 4: Jerusalem will be brought so low that her voice will sound only like a whisper from the ground.
> Verses 5–8: But then the turn: The enemies suddenly disappear like dust in the wind. They wake up as if from a dream in which they ate, and they are still hungry. Their dream of conquering Jerusalem bursts.

---

## A sealed book (Verses 9–14)

[9] Stop and be amazed!
Blind yourselves and be blind!
They are drunk, but not from wine.
They stagger, but not from strong drink.
[10] For the LORD has poured out on you a spirit of deep sleep
and has closed your eyes, the prophets,
and covered your heads, the seers.
[11] All vision has become for you like the words of a sealed book,
which people give to someone who can read, saying, “Please read this!”
And he says, “I can’t, for it is sealed.”
[12] And the book is given to someone who cannot read, saying, “Please read this!”
And he says, “I can’t read.”
[13] The Lord said,
“Because this people come near with their mouth
and honor me with their lips,
but keep their heart far from me,
and their fear of me is only a rule
that people have taught,
[14] so, look, I will again do wonderful things with this people,
wonderful and amazing things.
The wisdom of their wise men will perish,
and the understanding of their clever men will be hidden.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–12: The people are spiritually blind. God’s message is like a sealed book for them: The educated say “It is sealed,” the uneducated say “I can’t read.” No one wants to make the effort to really understand God’s word.
> Verse 13 is a very important verse: People pray with their mouth, but their heart is far away from God. Their faith consists only of rules learned by heart. Jesus quotes this verse (Matthew 15:8–9).
> This danger exists in every religion and at every time: pious words without a real heart.
> Verse 14: Paul quotes this verse: God brings the wisdom of the wise to nothing (1 Corinthians 1:19).

## The potter and the clay (Verses 15–24)

[15] How terrible for those who hide their plans deep from the LORD,
whose deeds are done in the dark, and who say,
“Who sees us?” and “Who knows us?”
[16] You turn everything upside down!
Should the potter be thought of as equal to the clay,
so that the thing made could say about the one who made it,
“He did not make me,”
or the thing formed say about the one who formed it,
“He has no understanding”?
[17] Isn’t it only a very little while,
and Lebanon will be turned into a fruitful field,
and the fruitful field will be thought of as a forest?
[18] On that day, the deaf will hear the words of the book,
and the eyes of the blind will see out of gloom and darkness.
[19] The humble will also have more and more joy in the LORD,
and the poor among people will rejoice in the Holy One of Israel.
[20] For the violent person will come to nothing,
and the mocker will be gone,
and all who are on the lookout to do evil will be cut off,
[21] who make a person guilty by a word,
and set a trap for the one who speaks up for justice at the gate,
and take away the rights of the innocent with false testimony.
[22] So the LORD, who redeemed Abraham,
says about the house of Jacob:
“Jacob will no longer be ashamed,
and his face will no longer turn pale.
[23] But when he sees his children, the work of my hands, among them,
they will treat my name as holy.
Yes, they will treat the Holy One of Jacob as holy
and will stand in awe of the God of Israel.
[24] Those who go astray in spirit will also come to understanding,
and those who grumble will accept teaching.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: Some people believe they can hide their plans from God. They say: “Who sees us?” and “Who knows us?”
> Verse 16: Isaiah’s answer with a picture: Can the clay pot say about the potter, “He did not make me”? People are God’s creatures and cannot put themselves above him. Paul takes up this picture (Romans 9:20–21).
> Verses 17–19: Soon everything will be turned around: The deaf hear, the blind see, the poor rejoice. Jesus shows such signs in his work (Matthew 11:5).
> Verses 20–21: The violent and the corrupt judges, who condemn innocent people with lies, will come to an end.
> Verses 22–24: God will renew his people. Even the confused and the grumblers will understand.
