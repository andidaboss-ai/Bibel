# Isaiah – Chapter 33
### Who can live with the consuming fire?

---

## A prayer in distress (Verses 1–6)

[1] How terrible for you, destroyer, who have not been destroyed yourself,
and traitor, whom no one has betrayed!
When you have finished destroying, you will be destroyed,
and when you have finished betraying, you will be betrayed.
[2] LORD, be gracious to us!
We have waited for you.
Be our strength every morning,
our salvation also in the time of trouble.
[3] At the noise of the thunder, the peoples have fled.
When you rise up, the nations are scattered.
[4] Your plunder will be gathered as the caterpillar gathers.
People will leap on it as locusts leap.
[5] The LORD is lifted up, for he lives on high.
He has filled Zion with justice and righteousness.
[6] There will be stability in your times,
plenty of salvation, wisdom, and knowledge.
The fear of the LORD is your treasure.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A cry of “How terrible” against the destroyer, probably Assyria. Whoever destroys and betrays others will in the end be destroyed and betrayed himself.
> Verse 2: A beautiful short prayer: “LORD, be gracious to us! … Be our strength every morning!” You can pray it every morning.
> Verse 6: The greatest treasure is the fear of God.

---

## The land mourns (Verses 7–13)

[7] Look, their brave men cry out in the streets.
The messengers of peace weep bitterly.
[8] The highways are deserted.
Travelers stay away.
The covenant is broken.
He has despised the cities.
He has no respect for anyone.
[9] The land mourns and withers away.
Lebanon is ashamed and dries up.
Sharon is like a desert,
and Bashan and Carmel are stripped bare.
[10] “Now I will rise up,” says the LORD.
“Now I will lift myself up.
Now I will be lifted high.
[11] You are pregnant with chaff.
You will give birth to stubble.
Your breath is a fire that will burn you up.
[12] The peoples will be like lime being burned,
like thorns cut down and burned in the fire.
[13] Hear, you who are far away, what I have done,
and you who are near, recognize my power!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: The peace talks have failed. The messengers of peace weep. The enemy does not keep any treaty. Hezekiah had paid tribute to Assyria, but the Assyrian king attacked anyway (2 Kings 18:14–17).
> Verse 9: Even the most beautiful regions (Lebanon, Sharon, Bashan, Carmel) dry up.
> Verse 10: Now God steps in: “Now I will rise up.”
> Verse 11: The plans of the enemies are like chaff. Their own breath, their rage, will burn them up.

## Who may live with God? (Verses 14–16)

[14] The sinners in Zion are afraid.
Trembling has seized the godless.
“Who among us can live with the consuming fire?
Who among us can live with everlasting burning?”
[15] Whoever lives righteously and speaks honestly,
whoever rejects profit from oppression,
whoever waves his hands to refuse a bribe,
whoever closes his ears so as not to hear of bloodshed
and shuts his eyes so as not to look at evil,
[16] he will live on high.
His place of refuge will be a fortress of rock.
His bread will be given to him.
His water will never run dry.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: God is holy like a consuming fire. Who can stand in his presence?
> Verse 15: The answer is very practical: whoever lives honestly, whoever does not profit from oppression, whoever does not take bribes, whoever does not join in plans for violence and does not stare at evil.
> There are similar questions and answers in Psalm 15 and Psalm 24.
> Verse 16: Such a person is safe with God as in a fortress of rock and is provided for.

---

## You will see the king in his beauty (Verses 17–24)

[17] Your eyes will see the king in his beauty.
They will see a land that stretches far away.
[18] Your heart will think back on the terror:
“Where is the one who counted?
Where is the one who weighed?
Where is the one who counted the towers?”
[19] You will no longer see the fierce people,
a people of strange speech that you cannot understand,
with a foreign language that you cannot make sense of.
[20] Look at Zion, the city of our festivals!
Your eyes will see Jerusalem,
a quiet home,
a tent that will not be taken down.
Its stakes will never be pulled up,
and none of its ropes will break.
[21] But there the LORD will be with us in majesty,
a place of wide rivers and streams,
on which no galley with oars will go,
and no proud ship will pass by.
[22] For the LORD is our judge.
The LORD is our lawgiver.
The LORD is our king.
He will save us.
[23] Your ropes hang loose.
They could not hold the base of their mast firm.
They could not spread the sail.
Then a great amount of plunder was divided.
Even the lame carried off plunder.
[24] No one who lives there will say, “I am sick.”
The people who live there will be forgiven their guilt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Instead of the enemy rulers, they will see the true king in his beauty.
> Verses 18–19: The time of terror is over: no more tax collectors, no more foreign soldiers whose language you cannot understand.
> Verse 20: Jerusalem will be like a tent that is never taken down again. A picture of lasting safety.
> Verse 21: God himself is like a wide river for Jerusalem that protects the city. But no enemy warships can sail on this river.
> Verse 22: God holds all offices together: judge, lawgiver, king.
> Verse 23: The enemy ship lies there helpless, and even the lame can carry off plunder.
> Verse 24: The most beautiful thing: No one is sick anymore, and all guilt is forgiven.
