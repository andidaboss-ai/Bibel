# Isaiah – Chapter 22
### The valley of vision

---

## Jerusalem celebrates instead of mourning (Verses 1–14)

[1] The burden about the valley of vision.
What is the matter with you, that you have all gone up to the rooftops?
[2] You noisy, wild city, you cheerful town:
Your dead were not killed by the sword,
and they did not die in battle.
[3] All your leaders fled together.
They were tied up by the archers.
All of you who were found were tied up together.
They fled far away.
[4] So I said, “Look away from me!
I will weep bitterly.
Don’t try to comfort me
about the destruction of the daughter of my people.
[5] For it is a day of confusion, of trampling, and of panic
from the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
in the valley of vision,
a tearing down of the walls
and a crying out to the mountains.”
[6] Elam carried the quiver,
with chariots full of men and horsemen,
and Kir uncovered the shield.
[7] Your finest valleys were full of chariots,
and the horsemen took their positions at the gate.
[8] He took away Judah’s protective cover.
And on that day you looked to the weapons in the House of the Forest.
[9] You saw that the cracks in the walls of the city of David were many,
and you collected the water of the lower pool.
[10] You counted the houses of Jerusalem,
and you tore down houses to strengthen the wall.
[11] You also made a reservoir between the two walls
for the water of the old pool.
But you did not look to the one who had done this,
and you did not pay attention to the one who planned it long ago.
[12] On that day, the Lord, the LORD of Armies, called
for weeping, for mourning, for shaving the head,
and for putting on sackcloth.
[13] But look, there is joy and celebration,
killing cattle and killing sheep,
eating meat and drinking wine:
“Let’s eat and drink, for tomorrow we die!”
[14] The LORD of Armies revealed himself in my ears:
“Surely this guilt will not be forgiven you until you die,”
says the Lord, the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The valley of vision” is Jerusalem, the city where God gave visions through prophets.
> Verses 1–3: The city celebrates on the rooftops. Perhaps because the enemy has withdrawn. But Isaiah sees: The leaders have fled like cowards and have been captured. There is no reason to celebrate.
> Verse 4: Isaiah weeps bitterly over his people.
> Verses 8–11: The people prepare wisely for war: weapons from the armory (the “House of the Forest”), repairing walls, securing water. At that time, Hezekiah had a water tunnel built, which can still be seen in Jerusalem today (2 Kings 20:20). But the people have forgotten God, who holds everything in his hands.
> Verse 13: “Let’s eat and drink, for tomorrow we die!” Instead of turning back, they celebrate pointlessly. Paul quotes this sentence (1 Corinthians 15:32).
> Verse 14: A very harsh sentence. It shows how seriously God takes this indifference.

## Shebna and Eliakim (Verses 15–25)

[15] This is what the Lord, the LORD of Armies, says:
“Go to this steward,
to Shebna, who is in charge of the palace, and say,
[16] ‘What are you doing here?
Who do you have here, that you have cut out a tomb for yourself here?’
He cuts out a tomb for himself up high
and chisels a home for himself in the rock!
[17] Look, the LORD will overpower you
and hurl you away violently.
Yes, he will grab you firmly.
[18] He will surely roll you up tightly
and throw you like a ball into a wide land.
There you will die,
and there your splendid chariots will be,
you disgrace to your master’s house.
[19] I will push you out of your office.
You will be pulled down from your position.
[20] On that day it will happen
that I will call my servant Eliakim, the son of Hilkiah.
[21] I will dress him in your robe
and strengthen him with your belt.
I will put your authority into his hand,
and he will be a father to the people of Jerusalem
and to the house of Judah.
[22] I will lay the key of the house of David on his shoulder.
He will open, and no one will shut.
He will shut, and no one will open.
[23] I will fasten him like a nail in a firm place.
He will be a throne of honor for his father’s house.
[24] They will hang on him all the glory of his father’s house,
the children and the descendants,
all the small vessels,
from the cups to all the jars.
[25] On that day,” says the LORD of Armies,
“the nail that was fastened in a firm place will give way.
It will be cut down and fall.
The load that hung on it will be cut off,
for the LORD has spoken it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Shebna was a high official at the royal court. While the land is in danger, he has a splendid tomb cut into the rock for himself, so that he will be honored after his death. He only thinks of himself.
> Verses 17–19: God will remove him from office and throw him far away like a ball.
> Verses 20–22: In his place comes Eliakim. He is to be “a father” to the people. He receives the key to the palace.
> Verse 22: “He will open, and no one will shut”: In Revelation, this sentence is applied to Jesus (Revelation 3:7).
> Verses 23–25: Eliakim is like a firm nail in the wall. But when the whole family hangs on him and wants to profit from him, even this nail will break out. A warning against favoring your relatives.
