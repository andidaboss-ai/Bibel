# Isaiah – Chapter 32
### The work of righteousness will be peace

---

## A righteous king (Verses 1–8)

[1] Look, a king will reign in righteousness,
and princes will rule with justice.
[2] Each one will be like a hiding place from the wind
and a shelter from the storm,
like streams of water in a dry place,
like the shade of a great rock in a weary land.
[3] The eyes of those who see will not be dim,
and the ears of those who hear will listen.
[4] The heart of the rash will understand knowledge,
and the tongue of those who stammer will be ready to speak clearly.
[5] The fool will no longer be called noble,
and the scoundrel will no longer be highly respected.
[6] For the fool speaks foolishness,
and his heart plans wrong,
to act in a godless way
and to say false things about the LORD,
to leave the hungry empty
and to let the thirsty go without a drink.
[7] The ways of the scoundrel are evil.
He makes wicked plans
to ruin the humble with lying words,
even when the needy are in the right.
[8] But the noble person plans noble things,
and he will stand firm in noble things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: A picture of good government: A king and his ministers rule justly. For the people they are like a shelter from the storm, like water in the desert, like shade on a hot day. Good government protects people.
> Verses 3–4: There will be clarity: People will see, hear and understand.
> Verses 5–8: Things will be called by their right names again. A scoundrel will no longer be celebrated as “noble.” You recognize the scoundrel by the fact that he lets the hungry and thirsty go away empty and cheats the poor with lies. You recognize the noble person by his good deeds.

---

## A warning to the carefree women (Verses 9–14)

[9] Get up, you women who live at ease!
Hear my voice!
You careless daughters, listen to what I say!
[10] In a little more than a year, you will tremble,
you careless women,
for the grape harvest will fail.
The harvest will not come.
[11] Tremble, you women who live at ease!
Be terrified, you careless ones!
Strip, make yourselves bare,
and put sackcloth around your waist!
[12] Beat your breasts for the pleasant fields,
for the fruitful vine.
[13] Thorns and briers will grow on the land of my people,
yes, on all the houses of joy in the cheerful city.
[14] For the palace will be abandoned.
The crowded city will be deserted.
The hill and the watchtower will become caves forever,
a delight for wild donkeys, a pasture for flocks,

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaiah speaks to the rich women of Jerusalem, who live carefree, as if nothing could happen.
> He warns: Soon the harvest will fail. Then the carefree life will be over.
> Verses 11–12: Sackcloth and beating one’s breast were signs of mourning.
> As in chapter 3, this is not a criticism of women in general, but of an upper class that does not see the need in the land. Isaiah criticizes the men in the same way in many other places.
> Verse 14 ends in the middle of a sentence. It continues in verse 15.

---

## Until the Spirit is poured out (Verses 15–20)

[15] until the Spirit is poured out on us from on high,
and the wilderness becomes a fruitful field,
and the fruitful field is thought of as a forest.
[16] Then justice will live in the wilderness,
and righteousness will stay in the fruitful field.
[17] The work of righteousness will be peace,
and the result of righteousness, quietness and confidence forever.
[18] My people will live in a peaceful home,
in safe dwellings,
and in quiet resting places,
[19] even if hail flattens the forest
and the city is leveled completely.
[20] Blessed are you who sow beside all waters,
who let the ox and the donkey roam free.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The turning point comes when God pours out his Spirit. Then the wilderness becomes a garden. The prophets Joel and Ezekiel also speak about this (Joel 2:28; Ezekiel 36:26–27). Christians think of the feast of Pentecost (Acts 2).
> Verse 17 is a wonderful sentence: “The work of righteousness will be peace.” True peace only comes through justice. Without justice, there is no peace. This sentence was the motto of Pope Pius XII and is a guiding word of many peace movements.
> Verse 18: God’s people will live safely and quietly.
> Verse 20: A picture of plenty: Everywhere there is enough water for sowing, and the animals can graze freely.
