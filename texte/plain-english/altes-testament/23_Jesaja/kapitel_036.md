# Isaiah – Chapter 36
### Rabshakeh threatens Jerusalem

---

## Who are you trusting? (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah,
Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, attacked all the fortified cities of Judah
and captured them.
[2] The king of Assyria sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem
to King Hezekiah with a large army.
He stood by the water channel of the upper pool,
on the road to the washer’s field.
[3] Then Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, who was in charge of the palace,
and Shebna the scribe,
and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder, came out to him.
[4] Rabshakeh said to them,
“Tell Hezekiah: ‘This is what the great king, the king of Assyria, says:
“What is this confidence that you are trusting in?
[5] I say: Your advice and your strength for war are only empty words.
Who are you trusting in, that you have rebelled against me?
[6] Look, you are trusting in the staff of this broken reed,
in Egypt.
If anyone leans on it,
it goes into his hand and pierces it.
That is what Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, is like for all who trust in him.
[7] But if you say to me, ‘We trust in the LORD our God,’
isn’t he the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed,
and who has said to Judah and Jerusalem,
‘You shall worship in front of this altar’?”
[8] So now, make a bet with my master, the king of Assyria:
I will give you 2,000 horses
if you can find riders for them on your side.
[9] How then could you drive back even one officer,
the least of my master’s servants?
And you trust in Egypt for chariots and horsemen!
[10] Have I come up against this land without the LORD
to destroy it?
The LORD said to me,
“Go up against this land and destroy it!”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 36–39 tell stories from the time of King Hezekiah. Almost exactly the same stories are also found in 2 Kings 18–20.
> In the year 701 BC, the Assyrian king Sennacherib marches into Judah with a huge army. He captures 46 fortified cities. An Assyrian inscription, the so-called “Sennacherib Prism,” also reports this. Only Jerusalem is left.
> “Rabshakeh” is a title, something like “chief cupbearer,” a high official.
> He stands exactly at the spot where Isaiah once called King Ahaz to trust (Isaiah 7:3).
> His speech is clever war propaganda: Egypt is a broken reed that will hurt you. And your God? Hezekiah tore down his altars! (Hezekiah had closed the many places of worship and brought worship together in Jerusalem.) He even claims that God himself sent him.

## The speech to the people on the wall (Verses 11–22)

[11] Then Eliakim, Shebna and Joah said to Rabshakeh,
“Please speak to your servants in Aramaic, for we understand it.
Do not speak to us in the Jews’ language
in the hearing of the people on the wall.”
[12] But Rabshakeh said,
“Has my master sent me only to your master and to you
to speak these words,
and not to the men sitting on the wall,
who will eat their own dung
and drink their own urine with you?”
[13] Then Rabshakeh stood
and called out with a loud voice in the Jews’ language and said,
“Hear the words of the great king, the king of Assyria!
[14] This is what the king says:
‘Do not let Hezekiah deceive you,
for he will not be able to rescue you.
[15] Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD
when he says, “The LORD will surely rescue us.
This city will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.”’
[16] Do not listen to Hezekiah,
for this is what the king of Assyria says:
‘Make peace with me and come out to me.
Then each of you will eat from his own vine
and each from his own fig tree,
and each of you will drink the water from his own well,
[17] until I come and take you away to a land like your own land,
a land of grain and new wine,
a land of bread and vineyards.
[18] Be careful that Hezekiah does not mislead you
when he says, “The LORD will rescue us.”
Have any of the gods of the nations
rescued their land from the hand of the king of Assyria?
[19] Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad?
Where are the gods of Sepharvaim?
Did they rescue Samaria from my hand?
[20] Which of all the gods of these countries
has rescued its country from my hand,
that the LORD should rescue Jerusalem from my hand?’”
[21] But they kept silent and did not answer him a word,
for the king had commanded, “Do not answer him!”
[22] Then Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, who was in charge of the palace,
and Shebna the scribe,
and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder,
came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn
and told him the words of Rabshakeh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Aramaic was the language of diplomats at that time. “The Jews’ language” means Hebrew. Hezekiah’s officials do not want the ordinary people on the wall to understand everything and become afraid.
> Verses 12–13: But that is exactly what Rabshakeh wants. He speaks to the people in Hebrew on purpose. He threatens them with famine because of the siege.
> Verses 16–17: He tempts them with promises: Surrender, and you will be well off. But in the end, he wants to deport them anyway.
> Verses 18–20: His biggest mistake: He compares the God of Israel with the idols of other nations. By doing this, he insults God himself.
> Verse 21: The people wisely keep silent, as the king had commanded.
> Verse 22: Torn clothes were a sign of great distress and mourning.
