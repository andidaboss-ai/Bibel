# Isaiah – Chapter 57
### Peace to those far away and to those near

---

## The righteous perish (Verses 1–2)

[1] The righteous perish, and no one takes it to heart.
Merciful people are taken away,
and no one considers
that the righteous are taken away from the evil.
[2] They enter into peace.
They rest on their beds,
everyone who has lived uprightly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Good people die, and no one cares. That is a bitter complaint.
> But the prophet also sees a comfort: The righteous person is spared from the coming disaster. He is now at peace and resting.
> If you are mourning someone you love, you can find comfort in this verse: Whoever has lived uprightly is at peace with God. And if you don’t want to be alone in your grief, you can call someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## An accusation against idol worship (Verses 3–13)

[3] “But you, come here,
you sons of a sorceress,
you descendants of adulterers and prostitutes!
[4] Whom are you making fun of?
At whom do you open your mouth wide
and stick out your tongue?
Aren’t you children of disobedience
and descendants of lies,
[5] you who burn with lust among the oaks,
under every green tree,
who kill the children in the valleys,
under the clefts of the rocks?
[6] Among the smooth stones of the valley is your share.
They, they are your lot.
You have even poured out drink offerings to them.
You have brought offerings.
Should I be calm about these things?
[7] On a high and lofty mountain you have made your bed.
You also went up there to offer sacrifices.
[8] Behind the door and the doorposts you have set up your sign.
For you have uncovered yourself to someone other than me
and have gone up.
You have made your bed wide
and made a covenant for yourself with them.
You loved what you saw on their bed.
[9] You went to the king with oil
and used more and more perfumes.
You sent your messengers far away
and lowered yourself even to Sheol, the world of the dead.
[10] You were worn out by your many journeys,
and yet you did not say, ‘It is hopeless.’
You found new strength,
so you did not grow faint.
[11] Whom did you dread and fear,
so that you lie
and have not remembered me
or taken it to heart?
Haven’t I kept silent for a long time,
and so you do not fear me?
[12] I will announce your righteousness,
and as for your works, they will not help you.
[13] When you cry out, let those you have gathered save you!
But the wind will carry them all away.
A breath will take them all.
But whoever takes refuge in me will possess the land
and inherit my holy mountain.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This passage is a harsh accusation against people in the nation who serve other gods.
> Verses 3 and 8: The Bible often describes idol worship with the picture of adultery and prostitution. What is meant: The people have become unfaithful to God, like a spouse who cheats. It is a picture of unfaithfulness to God, not a judgment on people who live in prostitution.
> Verse 5: The worst thing: Some even sacrificed children, in valleys near Jerusalem (compare 2 Kings 23:10). The Bible condemns this as a terrible crime. Harming or killing children can never be justified, in any religion or under any circumstances.
> Verses 6–7: Smooth stones and high mountains were places where foreign gods were worshiped.
> Verse 9: The people sought help from foreign kings and gods and lowered themselves deeper and deeper. “Sheol” is the Hebrew name for the world of the dead.
> Verse 13: The idols are blown away by the wind. But whoever takes refuge in God is kept safe.

## I live with the crushed (Verses 14–21)

[14] He will say, “Build up, build up, prepare the road!
Remove every obstacle from the way of my people!”
[15] For this is what the High and Lofty One says,
who lives forever, whose name is Holy:
“I live in the high and holy place,
and also with the one who is crushed and humble in spirit,
to give new life to the spirit of the humble
and to give new life to the heart of the crushed.
[16] For I will not argue forever,
and I will not always be angry,
for then the spirit would grow faint before me,
and the souls I have made.
[17] Because of the guilt of his greed I was angry
and struck him.
I hid myself and was angry,
and he kept turning away on the path of his own heart.
[18] I have seen his ways,
and I will heal him.
I will also lead him
and give comfort again to him and to those who mourn with him.
[19] I create the fruit of the lips:
Peace, peace, to those far away and to those near,” says the LORD,
“and I will heal them.”
[20] But the wicked are like the stormy sea,
for it cannot rest,
and its waters throw up mud and dirt.
[21] “There is no peace,” says my God,
“for the wicked.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 is a wonderful verse: God is the Most High; he lives in eternity. And at the same time, he lives with those who are crushed and pressed down. It is with them especially that he is, to make them alive again.
> Verse 16: God does not want to be angry forever. He knows that people could not bear it.
> Verse 18: “I have seen his ways, and I will heal him.” God sees the mistakes, and he still wants to heal.
> Verse 19: “Peace, peace, to those far away and to those near.” God’s peace is for everyone, for those who are near to him and for those who are far away. Paul takes up these words and says: Through Christ, Jews and non-Jews are united in one peace (Ephesians 2:17).
> Verses 20–21: Whoever lives against God is restless inside like the stormy sea. The sentence from verse 21 already appeared in chapter 48:22.
