# Isaiah – Chapter 55
### Come to the waters!

---

## An invitation to everyone (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Hey! Come, everyone who is thirsty, to the waters!
Come, you who have no money, buy and eat!
Yes, come, buy wine and milk
without money and without cost!
[2] Why do you spend money on what is not bread,
and your work on what does not satisfy?
Listen carefully to me and eat what is good,
and let your soul enjoy the richest food.
[3] Turn your ear and come to me!
Listen, and your soul will live.
I will make an everlasting covenant with you,
the sure acts of loving kindness promised to David.
[4] Look, I have made him a witness to the peoples,
a leader and commander for the peoples.
[5] Look, you will call a nation you do not know,
and a nation that did not know you will run to you,
because of the LORD your God
and for the sake of the Holy One of Israel,
for he has given you glory.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Like a trader at the market, God calls out: “Hey! Come, everyone!” But with him, you do not have to pay anything. Water, wine and milk are free. God’s gifts are a present.
> Verse 2: A question that still matters today: Why do you spend your money and your strength on things that do not really satisfy you? What truly feeds a person comes from God.
> Verses 3–4: God had promised King David an everlasting covenant (2 Samuel 7). Now this promise is extended to the whole people.
> Verse 5: Foreign nations will come to Israel because they see how God acts toward it.
> Jesus takes up this picture: “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!” (John 7:37). The book of Revelation, too, invites at the end: “Whoever is thirsty should come. Whoever wants it may take the water of life as a free gift” (Revelation 22:17).

---

## My thoughts are not your thoughts (Verses 6–13)

[6] Seek the LORD while he may be found!
Call on him while he is near!
[7] Let the wicked leave his way
and the unrighteous his thoughts.
Let him return to the LORD, and he will have mercy on him,
to our God, for he will forgive generously.
[8] “For my thoughts are not your thoughts,
and your ways are not my ways,” says the LORD.
[9] “For as the heavens are higher than the earth,
so are my ways higher than your ways
and my thoughts than your thoughts.
[10] For as the rain and the snow come down from the sky
and do not return there,
but water the earth
and make it grow and sprout,
and give seed to the sower and bread to the eater,
[11] so is my word that goes out from my mouth:
It will not return to me empty,
but it will accomplish what I please,
and it will succeed in what I sent it to do.
[12] For you will go out with joy
and be led out in peace.
The mountains and the hills will break out into singing before you,
and all the trees of the field will clap their hands.
[13] Instead of the thornbush, the cypress will grow,
and instead of the brier, the myrtle will grow.
This will make a name for the LORD,
an everlasting sign that will never be cut off.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: An urgent invitation: Now is the time to turn back to God! He forgives “generously,” lavishly, without being stingy.
> Verses 8–9: “My thoughts are not your thoughts.” God thinks differently from us humans. He is more generous and more merciful than we can imagine. We might not forgive, but God does.
> Verses 10–11: A beautiful picture: Just as the rain does not fall for nothing but makes the earth fruitful, so God’s word works. It does not come back empty. What God says happens.
> Verse 12: Those returning home leave Babylon with joy. All of nature celebrates with them: The mountains sing, the trees clap their hands.
> Verse 13: Instead of thorns, beautiful trees grow. The world becomes whole again.
> With chapter 55, the “Book of Comfort” (chapters 40–55) ends. It ends the way it began (chapter 40:8): with the promise that God’s word stands and does its work.
