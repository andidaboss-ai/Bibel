# Isaiah – Chapter 10
### Assyria is only the rod in God’s hand

---

## How terrible for those who make unjust laws (Verses 1–4)

[1] How terrible for those who make unjust laws,
and for the writers who write oppressive rules,
[2] to push the needy away from justice
and to rob the poor among my people of their rights,
so that widows become their plunder
and they rob the orphans!
[3] What will you do on the day of punishment,
in the disaster that comes from far away?
To whom will you run for help?
Where will you leave your wealth?
[4] All that is left for them is to sink down among the prisoners
and to fall among the dead.
In spite of all this, his anger has not turned away,
but his hand is still stretched out.

> **What does this mean?**
> An especially sharp accusation: There are people who write laws in such a way that the poor lose their rights. Injustice that is poured into laws is especially bad, because it looks “legal.”
> God sees it. And when judgment comes, their wealth will not help the rich.

---

## How terrible for Assyria, the proud one (Verses 5–19)

[5] How terrible for the Assyrian, the rod of my anger,
the staff in whose hand is my fury!
[6] I will send him against a godless nation,
and against the people who make me angry I will give him orders
to take plunder and to seize spoil,
and to trample them down like mud in the streets.
[7] But that is not what he intends,
and that is not what his heart thinks,
but in his heart is to destroy
and to wipe out many nations.
[8] For he says, “Aren’t all my princes kings?
[9] Isn’t Calno like Carchemish?
Isn’t Hamath like Arpad?
Isn’t Samaria like Damascus?
[10] As my hand has found the kingdoms of the idols,
whose images were more than those of Jerusalem and Samaria,
[11] shall I not do to Jerusalem and her idols
what I have done to Samaria and her idols?”
[12] So it will happen:
When the Lord has finished all his work on Mount Zion and on Jerusalem,
I will punish the fruit of the proud heart of the king of Assyria
and the arrogance of his proud looks.
[13] For he has said,
“By the strength of my hand I have done it,
and by my wisdom, for I am clever.
I have removed the borders of the peoples
and robbed their treasures.
Like a hero I have brought down their rulers.
[14] My hand has found the riches of the peoples like a nest,
and as people gather abandoned eggs,
I have gathered the whole earth.
There was no one who moved a wing,
or opened its beak, or chirped.”
[15] Does the ax brag against the one who chops with it?
Does the saw boast against the one who saws with it?
As if a rod could lift up the one who lifts it,
or as if a staff could lift up someone who is not wood!
[16] So the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
will send a wasting disease among his fat ones,
and under his glory a fire will be lit
like a burning fire.
[17] The Light of Israel will become a fire,
and his Holy One a flame.
It will burn up and eat up his thorns and briers
in a single day.
[18] He will destroy the glory of his forest and of his fertile field,
both soul and body.
It will be as when a flag bearer collapses.
[19] The rest of the trees of his forest will be so few
that a child could write down their number.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: God uses Assyria like a rod to punish his unfaithful people.
> Verses 7–11: But Assyria knows nothing about this. It only wants to conquer and destroy. The king of Assyria brags: I have conquered city after city (Calno, Carchemish, Hamath, Arpad, Damascus, Samaria). Jerusalem is next!
> Verses 13–14: He is proud: I did everything by my own strength and cleverness. To me, the peoples were like a nest full of eggs, I only had to gather them up.
> Verse 15: Isaiah’s answer, with humor: Can an ax show off in front of the person who uses it? Assyria is only a tool. It has no reason to be proud.
> Verses 16–19: So God will judge Assyria too. The mighty empire will be like a forest that burns down. So few trees will be left that a child can count them.
> An important message: No world power lasts forever. Whoever thinks he is all-powerful will fall.

## A remnant will return (Verses 20–27)

[20] On that day it will happen
that the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob
will no longer lean on the one who struck them,
but they will truly lean on the LORD,
the Holy One of Israel.
[21] A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob,
to the Mighty God.
[22] For even if your people Israel are like the sand of the sea,
only a remnant of them will return.
A destruction has been decided,
overflowing with righteousness.
[23] For the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
will make a full end, as decided, in the whole earth.
[24] So the Lord, the LORD of Armies, says:
“My people who live in Zion,
don’t be afraid of the Assyrian,
even if he strikes you with the rod
and raises his staff against you, as Egypt did.
[25] For in just a very little while,
the fury against you will be over,
and my anger will turn to his destruction.”
[26] The LORD of Armies will raise up a whip against him,
as when Midian was struck down at the rock of Oreb.
His rod will be over the sea,
and he will raise it as he did against Egypt.
[27] On that day it will happen
that his burden will be taken from your shoulder
and his yoke from your neck,
and the yoke will be broken because of the anointing oil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–21: “A remnant will return”: That is exactly the name of Isaiah’s son “Shearjashub” (Isaiah 7:3). Only a few will be left. But these few will rely completely on God.
> Verse 21: “Mighty God” is the same title as in chapter 9:6.
> Verses 24–26: A comfort for Jerusalem: Don’t be afraid of Assyria! Soon it will be over. God will set you free as he did back then from Egypt and from the Midianites (Judges 7:25).
> Verse 27: The heavy yoke will be broken. “Because of the anointing oil” is hard to understand in Hebrew. Perhaps it means the anointed king from the house of David, perhaps it is a picture of strength and plenty.

## The enemy marches on Jerusalem (Verses 28–34)

[28] He has come to Aiath.
He has passed through Migron.
At Michmash he stores his baggage.
[29] They have crossed the pass.
At Geba they have set up camp for the night.
Ramah trembles.
Gibeah of Saul has fled.
[30] Cry out loud, daughter of Gallim!
Listen, Laishah! You poor Anathoth!
[31] Madmenah is running away.
The people of Gebim flee to safety.
[32] This very day he will stop at Nob.
He shakes his fist at the mountain of the daughter of Zion,
the hill of Jerusalem.
[33] Look, the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
will cut off the branches with terror.
The tall will be cut down,
and the high will be brought low.
[34] He will cut down the thickets of the forest with iron,
and Lebanon will fall by the Mighty One.

> **What does this mean?**
> An exciting report like a live broadcast: The enemy army marches toward Jerusalem from the north, town by town, closer and closer. The villages flee in panic. Finally the enemy stands at Nob, right in front of Jerusalem, and shakes his fist.
> Verses 33–34: But then the turn: God himself cuts down this mighty “forest” like a lumberjack. The proud enemy falls.
> The next chapter continues: From the cut-down stump of Jesse, a new shoot grows (Isaiah 11:1).
