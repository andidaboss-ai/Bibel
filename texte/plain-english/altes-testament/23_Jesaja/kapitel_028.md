# Isaiah – Chapter 28
### The precious cornerstone

---

## How terrible for the drunkards of Ephraim (Verses 1–6)

[1] How terrible for the proud crown of the drunkards of Ephraim,
and for the fading flower of its glorious beauty,
which is on the head of the fertile valley,
of those who are overcome by wine!
[2] Look, the Lord has someone mighty and strong.
Like a hailstorm, a destroying storm,
like a flood of mighty, overflowing waters,
he will throw them to the ground with his hand.
[3] The proud crown of the drunkards of Ephraim
will be trampled underfoot.
[4] The fading flower of its glorious beauty,
which is on the head of the fertile valley,
will be like the first ripe fig before summer:
Whoever sees it picks it and eats it right away.
[5] On that day, the LORD of Armies
will be a crown of glory and a beautiful diadem
for the remnant of his people,
[6] and a spirit of justice for the one who sits in judgment,
and strength for those who turn back the battle at the gate.
A diadem is a royal headband, like a crown.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The proud crown of Ephraim” is Samaria, the capital of the northern kingdom. It lay on a hill above a fertile valley, like a wreath on a head. But the upper class was drunk and arrogant.
> Verses 2–4: Assyria will come like a hailstorm. Samaria will disappear as quickly as a ripe fig that someone picks and eats right away. That is what happened in the year 722 BC.
> Verses 5–6: Not a city is to be the crown, but God himself. He gives justice to the judges and strength to the soldiers.

---

## Priests and prophets stagger (Verses 7–13)

[7] These also stagger from wine
and reel from strong drink.
The priest and the prophet stagger from strong drink.
They are swallowed up by wine.
They reel from strong drink.
They make mistakes when they see visions.
They stumble when they judge.
[8] For all the tables are completely full of disgusting vomit and filth.
[9] Whom will he teach knowledge?
To whom will he explain the message?
To those who have just been weaned from milk,
who have just been taken from the breast?
[10] For it is: precept on precept, precept on precept,
line on line, line on line,
here a little, there a little.
[11] But he will speak to this people with stammering lips
and in a foreign language,
[12] he who had said to them,
“This is the resting place. Give rest to the weary!”
and, “This is the refreshing!”
But they would not listen.
[13] So the word of the LORD will be to them:
precept on precept, precept on precept,
line on line, line on line,
here a little, there a little,
so that they go, fall backward,
and are broken, snared, and caught.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: Even the priests and prophets in Jerusalem are drunk. Their tables are full of vomit. How are they supposed to understand God’s will like that?
> Verses 9–10: The drunks mock Isaiah: He talks to us as if we were little children! In Hebrew, verse 10 sounds like baby talk: *tsav la-tsav, qav la-qav*. The WEB, our English source text, translates it as “precept on precept, line on line,” and that is how it appears in the text here. The mockers are probably imitating Isaiah’s preaching like baby babble.
> Verses 11–13: Isaiah’s answer: If you don’t listen to me, God will speak to you in a foreign language, that is, through the Assyrian soldiers. Then you will understand only gibberish.
> Verse 12: Yet God had actually offered them rest: “Give rest to the weary!” God’s way would have been simple.

## A covenant with death (Verses 14–22)

[14] So hear the word of the LORD, you mockers
who rule this people in Jerusalem:
[15] “Because you have said,
‘We have made a covenant with death,
and we have an agreement with Sheol, the world of the dead.
When the overflowing whip passes through,
it will not come to us,
for we have made lies our refuge
and have hidden ourselves under falsehood,’”
[16] so the Lord GOD says,
“Look, I am laying in Zion a foundation stone,
a tested stone,
a precious cornerstone, firmly set.
Whoever believes will not be in a hurry.
[17] I will make justice the measuring line
and righteousness the plumb line.
The hail will sweep away the refuge of lies,
and the waters will flood the hiding place.
[18] Your covenant with death will be canceled,
and your agreement with Sheol will not stand.
When the overflowing whip passes through,
you will be trampled down by it.
[19] As often as it passes through, it will seize you,
for morning after morning it will pass through, by day and by night,
and it will be nothing but terror to understand the message.”
[20] For the bed is too short to stretch out on,
and the blanket is too narrow to wrap yourself in.
[21] For the LORD will rise up as on Mount Perazim.
He will be angry as in the Valley of Gibeon,
to do his work, his unusual work,
and to carry out his act, his extraordinary act.
[22] So now, don’t be mockers,
or your chains will be made stronger,
for I have heard from the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
a decision of destruction on the whole earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The leaders of Jerusalem feel safe. They have made alliances, probably with Egypt, and believe nothing can happen to them. Isaiah mockingly calls that a “covenant with death.”
> Verse 16: God’s answer: Alliances do not give security, God himself does. He lays a firm foundation stone in Zion. Whoever trusts in it does not have to panic. The New Testament applies this “cornerstone” to Jesus Christ (Romans 9:33; 1 Peter 2:6).
> Verse 17: God builds with a builder’s tools: Justice is the measuring line, righteousness the plumb line.
> Verse 20: A vivid picture: Their false security is like a bed that is too short and a blanket that is too narrow. You cannot rest in it.
> Verse 21: At Perazim and Gibeon, God once fought for David and Joshua against their enemies (2 Samuel 5:20; Joshua 10). Now he fights against his own people. That is his “unusual,” strange work.

## The parable of the farmer (Verses 23–29)

[23] Listen and hear my voice!
Pay attention and hear what I say!
[24] Does the one who plows to sow plow all day long?
Does he keep turning the soil and breaking up the clods?
[25] When he has leveled the surface,
doesn’t he plant the dill
and scatter the cumin seed
and put the wheat in rows,
the barley in its proper place,
and the spelt in its place?
[26] For his God teaches him the right way
and instructs him.
[27] For dill is not threshed with a sharp threshing sledge,
and a cart wheel is not rolled over the cumin,
but the dill is beaten out with a stick
and the cumin with a rod.
[28] Grain for bread must be ground.
So he does not keep threshing it forever.
Even though he drives the wheel of his threshing cart over it,
his horses do not crush it.
[29] This also comes from the LORD of Armies,
who is wonderful in counsel
and great in wisdom.

> **What does this mean?**
> A little parable from farming: A farmer does not plow forever, but sows afterward. He treats each plant differently: Dill and cumin are beaten out carefully with a stick, grain with the heavy threshing cart. And even the grain is not crushed.
> The message: Just as the farmer knows what each plant needs, God knows what each people needs. His judgment does not last forever, and it has a goal. He does not want to destroy his people, but to make them into “bread.”
