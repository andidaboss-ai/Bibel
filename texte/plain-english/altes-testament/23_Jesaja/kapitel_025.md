# Isaiah – Chapter 25
### God will swallow up death forever

---

## Praise for God’s faithfulness (Verses 1–5)

[1] LORD, you are my God.
I will lift you up!
I will praise your name,
for you have done wonderful things,
plans made long ago,
in complete faithfulness and truth.
[2] For you have made a city into a heap of stones,
a fortified city into a ruin,
a palace of foreigners into no city at all.
It will never be built again.
[3] So a strong people will honor you.
A city of mighty nations will fear you.
[4] For you have been a fortress for the poor,
a fortress for the needy in their distress,
a refuge from the storm,
a shade from the heat,
when the blast of the violent is like a storm against a wall.
[5] Like heat in a dry land,
you quiet the noise of the foreigners.
Like heat by the shade of a cloud,
the victory song of the violent is silenced.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the judgment comes a song of praise. God has faithfully carried out his plans.
> Verse 2: “A city”: This means a city of violence and oppression, without a name. It stands for all powers that are against God and the weak.
> Verse 4: A wonderful verse: God is a fortress for the poor, a refuge in the storm, a shade in the heat. Whoever is weak finds protection with God.

---

## The great feast for all peoples (Verses 6–9)

[6] On this mountain, the LORD of Armies
will prepare a feast for all peoples,
a feast of fine food,
a feast of choice wines,
of fine food full of marrow,
of well-aged, choice wines.
[7] On this mountain he will destroy the covering
that covers all peoples,
and the veil that is spread over all nations.
[8] He has swallowed up death forever!
The Lord GOD will wipe away the tears from all faces.
He will take away the shame of his people from all the earth,
for the LORD has spoken it.
[9] On that day people will say,
“Look, this is our God!
We have waited for him, and he will save us!
This is the LORD!
We have waited for him.
We will be glad and rejoice in his salvation!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most beautiful texts in the whole Bible.
> Verse 6: God invites all peoples to a great feast on Mount Zion. The best food, the best wine. No one is left out. Jesus often speaks of the kingdom of God as a feast (Matthew 8:11; Luke 14:15–24).
> Verse 7: The “covering” and the “veil” are perhaps veils of mourning. God takes the mourning away from all people.
> Verse 8: “He has swallowed up death forever!” Death, which swallows everything, is itself swallowed up. And God himself wipes away every tear, as tenderly as a mother.
> Paul quotes this verse when he speaks of the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:54). And Revelation takes it up: “He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more” (Revelation 21:4). This verse is often read at funerals.
> Verse 9: “This is our God! We have waited for him.” The long wait was worth it.

---

## Moab will be brought low (Verses 10–12)

[10] For the hand of the LORD will rest on this mountain.
Moab will be trampled down where he is,
as straw is trampled down in the water of a manure pile.
[11] He will spread out his hands in the middle of it,
as a swimmer spreads out his hands to swim,
but his pride will be humbled
together with the tricks of his hands.
[12] He has torn down the high fortress of your walls,
brought it low, and brought it to the ground,
down to the dust.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Moab stands as an example of all who are proud. While the peoples celebrate on the mountain, pride sinks into the manure.
> Verse 11: An almost funny picture: Moab tries desperately to swim, but it goes under.
> The message: God’s feast is open to everyone, but not to pride. Whoever raises himself above God shuts himself out.
