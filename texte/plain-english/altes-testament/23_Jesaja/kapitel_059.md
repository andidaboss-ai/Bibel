# Isaiah – Chapter 59
### A Redeemer will come to Zion

---

## Your sins separate you from God (Verses 1–8)

[1] Look, the LORD’s hand is not too short to save,
and his ear is not too dull to hear.
[2] But your wrongdoings have separated you from your God,
and your sins have hidden his face from you,
so that he does not hear.
[3] For your hands are stained with blood
and your fingers with wrongdoing.
Your lips have spoken lies.
Your tongue mutters wickedness.
[4] No one brings a lawsuit in righteousness,
and no one argues a case in truth.
They trust in empty things and speak lies.
They are pregnant with trouble and give birth to wrongdoing.
[5] They hatch the eggs of snakes
and weave spider webs.
Whoever eats their eggs dies,
and when one is crushed, a viper breaks out.
[6] Their webs will not make clothes.
They cannot cover themselves with their works.
Their works are works of wrongdoing,
and acts of violence are in their hands.
[7] Their feet run to evil,
and they hurry to shed innocent blood.
Their thoughts are thoughts of wrongdoing.
Ruin and destruction are on their roads.
[8] They do not know the way of peace,
and there is no justice in their ways.
They have made crooked paths for themselves.
Whoever walks on them does not know peace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: The people ask: Why doesn’t God help? The prophet answers: God’s hand is not too short and his ear is not deaf. But your guilt stands like a wall between you and God.
> Verses 3–8: A list of injustice: violence, lies, unjust courts, murder of innocent people. The pictures are strong: Evil plans are like snake eggs that poison hatches from, and like spider webs that are no good for clothes.
> Verses 7–8: Paul quotes these verses in his letter to the Romans (3:15–17) to show that all people need God’s grace.

---

## We grope like the blind (Verses 9–15)

[9] So justice is far from us,
and righteousness does not reach us.
We look for light, but look, darkness,
for brightness, but we walk in gloom.
[10] We grope for the wall like the blind.
Yes, we grope like those who have no eyes.
We stumble at noon as if it were twilight.
Among the strong we are like the dead.
[11] We all growl like bears
and moan sadly like doves.
We look for justice, but there is none,
for salvation, but it is far from us.
[12] For our rebellious acts are many before you,
and our sins testify against us.
For our rebellious acts are with us,
and our wrongdoings, we know them:
[13] rebelling against the LORD and denying him,
and turning away from following our God,
speaking of oppression and revolt,
thinking up lies in our hearts and speaking them.
[14] Justice is pushed back,
and righteousness stands far away.
For truth has stumbled in the street,
and honesty cannot come in.
[15] Yes, truth is missing,
and whoever turns away from evil becomes prey.
The LORD saw it,
and it displeased him that there was no justice.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the people themselves speak: “we.” They admit their guilt.
> Verses 9–11: We look for light, but it stays dark. We grope like the blind. We growl like bears and moan like doves. An honest prayer in distress.
> Verses 12–13: “Our wrongdoings, we know them.” The people see their guilt. That is the first step toward turning back.
> Verses 14–15: A society where truth lies in the street and no one picks it up. Whoever is honest becomes prey. Sadly, this description fits many times and places. But at the end of verse 15 it says: The LORD saw it, and it displeased him.

---

## God steps in himself (Verses 16–21)

[16] He saw that there was no one,
and he was amazed that no one stepped in.
So his own arm brought him salvation,
and his righteousness held him up.
[17] He put on righteousness like a breastplate
and a helmet of salvation on his head.
He put on garments of vengeance as his clothing
and wrapped himself in zeal like a cloak.
[18] He will repay them according to their deeds, as is fitting:
anger to his opponents, payback to his enemies.
He will repay the islands what they deserve.
[19] So they will fear the name of the LORD from the west
and his glory from the rising of the sun.
For he will come like a rushing stream
driven by the breath of the LORD.
[20] “A Redeemer will come to Zion,
and to those in Jacob who turn away from disobedience,” says the LORD.
[21] “As for me, this is my covenant with them,” says the LORD:
“My Spirit, who is on you,
and my words, which I have put in your mouth,
will not depart from your mouth,
or from the mouth of your descendants,
or from the mouth of your descendants’ descendants,” says the LORD,
“from now on and forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: God sees that there is no justice and that no one steps in. So he steps in himself.
> Verse 17: God puts on “armor”: righteousness as a breastplate, salvation as a helmet. This is a picture. God does not fight with weapons, but with righteousness and rescue. Paul takes up this picture and speaks of the “armor of God” for Christians (Ephesians 6:14–17): truth, righteousness, peace, faith and God’s word. This has nothing to do with real weapons or violence.
> Verse 18: God makes sure that injustice does not go unpunished. Judgment is his business, not the business of people who want revenge.
> Verse 20: “A Redeemer will come to Zion” to those who turn back. Paul quotes this verse in Romans 11:26.
> Verse 21: God’s Spirit and God’s word are to stay with his people, from generation to generation, forever.
