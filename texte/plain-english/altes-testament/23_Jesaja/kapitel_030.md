# Isaiah – Chapter 30
### In quietness and confidence

---

## Useless help from Egypt (Verses 1–7)

[1] “How terrible for the rebellious children,” says the LORD,
“who make plans, but not from me,
and who make an alliance, but not by my Spirit,
so that they pile sin on sin,
[2] who set out to go down to Egypt
without asking my advice,
to make themselves strong in the strength of Pharaoh
and to take refuge in the shadow of Egypt!
[3] So the strength of Pharaoh will be your shame,
and the refuge in the shadow of Egypt your disgrace.
[4] For their princes are at Zoan,
and their messengers have come to Hanes.
[5] They will all be put to shame
because of a people who cannot help them,
who are no help and no use,
but a shame and a disgrace.”
[6] The burden about the animals of the South.
Through the land of trouble and anguish,
of the lioness and the lion,
of the viper and the fiery flying serpent,
they carry their riches on the backs of young donkeys
and their treasures on the humps of camels
to a people who are no use.
[7] For Egypt’s help is useless and empty.
So I have called her “Rahab who sits still.”

> **What does this mean?**
> King Hezekiah and his advisers send messengers to Egypt to make an alliance against Assyria. They do not ask God for advice.
> Verse 6: With great effort they drag gifts through the dangerous desert, full of lions and snakes. All for nothing.
> Verse 7: “Rahab” is a name for a sea monster and for Egypt. Isaiah makes fun of it: Egypt is a monster that only sits still and does nothing.

---

## Just tell us pleasant things (Verses 8–14)

[8] Now go, write it on a tablet in front of them
and write it in a book,
so that it may remain for the time to come,
forever and ever.
[9] For it is a rebellious people,
lying children,
children who will not listen to the teaching of the LORD,
[10] who say to the seers, “Don’t see!”
and to the prophets, “Don’t prophesy to us what is right!
Tell us pleasant things!
Prophesy lies to us!
[11] Get out of the way!
Turn off the path!
Leave us alone with the Holy One of Israel!”
[12] So the Holy One of Israel says,
“Because you reject this word
and trust in oppression and crookedness
and rely on them,
[13] this guilt will be for you
like a crack that threatens to collapse,
a bulge in a high wall,
whose collapse comes suddenly, in an instant.
[14] He will break it as a clay pot is broken,
smashed without mercy,
so that among the pieces there is not one left
to take fire from the hearth
or to scoop water from the cistern.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: The people do not want to hear uncomfortable truth. They say to the prophets: Just tell us pleasant things! Go ahead and lie to us! That is a very human attitude that is still familiar today.
> Verses 13–14: Their guilt is like a crack in a wall. At first you can hardly see it, but suddenly everything collapses. In the end not even one piece is left.

---

## Turning back and rest (Verses 15–17)

[15] For this is what the Lord GOD, the Holy One of Israel, said:
“By turning back and rest you will be saved.
Your strength will be in quietness and confidence.”
But you would not,
[16] but you said, “No, we will flee on horses!”
So you will flee.
And, “We will ride on swift horses!”
So those who chase you will be swift.
[17] A thousand will flee at the threat of one.
At the threat of five you will flee,
until you are left like a pole on a mountaintop
and like a banner on a hill.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 is one of Isaiah’s best-known verses: “Your strength will be in quietness and confidence.” God invites us not to panic, but to turn back, to become calm, and to trust him.
> But the people would rather rely on fast horses, that is, on military strength. The result: They will have to flee.
> A thought that still helps today: In stress and fear, it is often wiser to become quiet and trust God than to act in a rush.

## God waits to be gracious (Verses 18–26)

[18] So the LORD waits to be gracious to you,
and so he rises up to have mercy on you,
for the LORD is a God of justice.
Blessed are all who wait for him.
[19] For the people will live in Zion at Jerusalem.
You will weep no more.
He will surely be gracious to you when you cry for help.
When he hears you, he will answer you.
[20] Even though the Lord gives you the bread of trouble and the water of distress,
your teachers will not hide anymore,
but your eyes will see your teachers,
[21] and when you turn to the right or to the left,
your ears will hear a voice behind you:
“This is the way. Walk in it!”
[22] You will treat as unclean the silver covering of your carved idols
and the gold plating of your cast images.
You will throw them away like something unclean.
You will say to it, “Get out!”
[23] He will give rain for your seed
that you sow in the ground,
and the bread from the produce of the ground will be rich and plentiful.
On that day, your cattle will graze in wide pastures.
[24] The oxen and the young donkeys that work the ground
will eat tasty feed
that has been winnowed with shovel and fork.
[25] On every high mountain and on every lofty hill
there will be brooks and streams of water
on the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall.
[26] The light of the moon will be like the light of the sun,
and the light of the sun will be seven times brighter,
like the light of seven days,
on the day when the LORD binds up the broken bones of his people
and heals the wound they were struck with.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A wonderful thought: God himself is waiting to be able to be gracious. He longs to help his people.
> Verse 19: “You will weep no more.” God hears every cry for help.
> Verses 20–21: A comforting picture: When you are unsure which way to go, you hear a voice behind you: “This is the way. Walk in it!” God goes with you like a good teacher.
> Verses 23–26: A picture of a wonderful future: rain, a rich harvest, water everywhere, bright light. And above all: God binds up the wounds of his people and heals them.

## God’s judgment on Assyria (Verses 27–33)

[27] Look, the name of the LORD comes from far away,
burning with his anger and in thick rising smoke.
His lips are full of fury.
His tongue is like a consuming fire.
[28] His breath is like an overflowing stream that reaches up to the neck,
to sift the nations with the sieve of destruction.
A bridle that leads to ruin will be in the jaws of the peoples.
[29] You will have a song as in the night when a holy feast is kept,
and gladness of heart as when someone goes with a flute
to come to the mountain of the LORD, to the Rock of Israel.
[30] The LORD will make his glorious voice heard
and will show his arm coming down
with the fury of his anger and the flame of a consuming fire,
with cloudburst, storm, and hailstones.
[31] For at the voice of the LORD, the Assyrian will be terrified.
He will strike him with his rod.
[32] Every blow of the rod of punishment that the LORD lays on him
will come to the sound of tambourines and harps.
He will fight against them in battles, swinging his weapon.
[33] For his burning place has long been ready.
Yes, it is prepared for the king.
He has made its fire pit deep and wide,
with fire and plenty of wood.
The breath of the LORD, like a stream of sulfur, sets it on fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> God himself comes like a mighty storm to judge Assyria.
> Verse 29: For God’s people it will be a feast, as in the night of the Passover festival, when people go on pilgrimage to Jerusalem with songs and flutes.
> Verse 33: “The burning place” in Hebrew is “Topheth.” That was a place in the Valley of Hinnom near Jerusalem, where terrible child sacrifices had taken place (2 Kings 23:10). Now the king of Assyria is to meet his end there. From the name of this valley (“Gehinnom”), the word “Gehenna” later came, a picture for hell.
