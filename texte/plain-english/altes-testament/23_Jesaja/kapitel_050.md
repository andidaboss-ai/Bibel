# Isaiah – Chapter 50
### The third song of God’s servant

---

## Is my hand too short? (Verses 1–3)

[1] This is what the LORD says:
“Where is your mother’s certificate of divorce
with which I sent her away?
Or to which of my creditors did I sell you?
Look, you were sold because of your wrongdoings,
and your mother was sent away because of your rebellious acts.
[2] Why was no one there when I came?
Why did no one answer when I called?
Is my hand too short to redeem?
Or do I have no power to rescue?
Look, with my rebuke I dry up the sea.
I turn the rivers into a desert.
Their fish stink because there is no water,
and they die of thirst.
[3] I clothe the heavens in blackness.
I make sackcloth their covering.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The people in exile ask: Has God rejected us for good, like a man sending his wife away with a certificate of divorce? Has he sold us, the way children were sold because of debts? God asks back: Where is the certificate of divorce, then? There is none! The separation came because of your guilt, but it is not final. The relationship continues.
> Verses 2–3: God’s hand is not too short. He can rescue. He has power over the sea and the sky. The problem was that no one listened to him.

---

## I offered my back (Verses 4–9)

[4] The Lord GOD has given me the tongue of those who are taught,
so that I know how to lift up the weary with a word.
He wakes me morning by morning,
he wakes my ear to listen like those who are taught.
[5] The Lord GOD has opened my ear.
I was not rebellious.
I did not turn back.
[6] I offered my back to those who beat me,
and my cheeks to those who pulled out my beard.
I did not hide my face from shame and spitting.
[7] For the Lord GOD will help me.
That is why I have not been disgraced.
That is why I have set my face like flint,
and I know that I will not be put to shame.
[8] The one who declares me right is near.
Who will bring charges against me?
Let us stand up together!
Who is my opponent?
Let him come near to me.
[9] Look, the Lord GOD will help me!
Who is the one who will condemn me?
Look, they will all wear out like a garment.
The moths will eat them up.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the third song of God’s servant. Again the servant himself speaks.
> Verse 4: Every morning he listens to God like a student (a “disciple”) listens to his teacher. And he is given the gift of lifting up tired people with words. A beautiful task for every person.
> Verse 6: The servant is beaten, mocked and spat on. He does not run away. The Gospels tell that Jesus was treated this way (Matthew 26:67; 27:30). That is why Christians see a reference to Jesus here. In the Jewish interpretation, the servant stands for Israel or for the prophet, who have suffered much for their faith.
> Verses 7–9: The servant holds on because he knows: God stands by me. Paul takes up this thought: “Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? It is God who justifies” (Romans 8:33).
> Important: This text describes a person who bears suffering for his task. It does not mean that you should simply put up with violence, for example in your family or relationship. If you are being hit, you may and should get help. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK). US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Whoever walks in the dark (Verses 10–11)

[10] Who among you fears the LORD
and listens to the voice of his servant?
Whoever walks in darkness and has no light,
let him trust in the name of the LORD
and rely on his God.
[11] Look, all of you who light a fire,
who surround yourselves with torches,
walk into the flame of your own fire
and among the torches you have lit.
This is what you will receive from my hand:
You will lie down in pain.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: A comforting verse for everyone who is walking in the dark right now and sees no way forward: Trust in God and hold on to him. Even without light, you are not alone.
> Verse 11: The others try to make their own light, with their own torches, without God. But in the end their own fire burns them.
