# Isaiah – Chapter 38
### Hezekiah’s illness and healing

---

## Set your house in order (Verses 1–8)

[1] In those days, Hezekiah became deathly ill.
The prophet Isaiah, the son of Amoz, came to him
and said to him,
“This is what the LORD says:
‘Set your house in order, for you will die and not live.’”
[2] Then Hezekiah turned his face to the wall
and prayed to the LORD,
[3] and said,
“Please, LORD, remember
how I have lived before you in truth and with an undivided heart
and have done what is good in your eyes.”
And Hezekiah wept bitterly.
[4] Then the word of the LORD came to Isaiah:
[5] “Go and tell Hezekiah:
‘This is what the LORD, the God of your father David, says:
“I have heard your prayer.
I have seen your tears.
Look, I will add fifteen years to your life.
[6] I will rescue you and this city from the hand of the king of Assyria,
and I will defend this city.
[7] And this will be the sign to you from the LORD
that the LORD will do what he has said:
[8] Look, I will make the shadow on the sundial of Ahaz,
which has gone down with the sun,
go back ten steps.”’”
So the sun went back ten steps on the sundial
on which it had gone down.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah becomes seriously ill. Isaiah tells him: You will die. “Set your house in order” means: Put your affairs in order, settle your inheritance.
> Verses 2–3: Hezekiah turns to the wall to be alone with God, and he weeps and prays.
> Verse 5: God answers: “I have heard your prayer. I have seen your tears.” A sentence full of comfort. God sees every tear.
> Verse 8: The “sundial” was probably a staircase on which the shadow moved on step by step. God makes the shadow go back as a sign that he is giving Hezekiah more time to live.
> Not every prayer for healing is answered like this. That is a hard experience. But this story shows: God listens, and praying is not pointless.

## Hezekiah’s song of thanks (Verses 9–20)

[9] The writing of Hezekiah, the king of Judah,
after he had been ill and had recovered from his illness:
[10] I said, “In the middle of my life
I must go through the gates of Sheol, the world of the dead.
The rest of my years is taken from me.”
[11] I said, “I will not see Yah anymore,
Yah in the land of the living.
I will not see people anymore
among those who live in the world.
[12] My home is taken down
and carried away from me like a shepherd’s tent.
I have rolled up my life like a weaver.
He will cut me off from the loom.
From day to night you will make an end of me.
[13] I waited patiently until morning.
Like a lion, he breaks all my bones.
From day to night you will make an end of me.
[14] I chirped like a swallow or a crane.
I moaned like a dove.
My eyes grow weak from looking upward.
Lord, I am in distress. Be my security!”
[15] What shall I say?
He has spoken to me, and he himself has done it.
I will walk carefully all my years
because of the bitterness of my soul.
[16] Lord, people live by these things,
and in all of them my spirit finds life.
You restore me and let me live.
[17] Look, instead of peace I had great bitterness,
but in love you have rescued my soul
from the pit of destruction,
for you have thrown all my sins behind your back.
[18] For Sheol cannot praise you.
Death cannot celebrate you.
Those who go down into the pit
cannot hope for your truth.
[19] The living, the living, they praise you,
as I do today.
The father makes your truth known to the children.
[20] The LORD will save me.
So we will play my songs on stringed instruments
all the days of our life in the house of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> After his healing, Hezekiah writes a song. It honestly describes his fear of death:
> Verses 10–12: In the middle of my life I must die. My life is taken down like a tent, cut off like cloth from the loom. “Sheol” is the Hebrew name for the world of the dead. “Yah” is a short form of God’s name.
> Verse 14: In his weakness, he could only chirp softly like a little bird.
> Verse 17: “You have thrown all my sins behind your back”: a beautiful picture of forgiveness. God no longer looks at them.
> Verses 18–19: Hezekiah says: Only the living can praise God. That is why he is so glad that he may go on living. And he wants to tell his children about God’s faithfulness and truth.

## The fig poultice (Verses 21–22)

[21] For Isaiah had said,
“Let them take a cake of figs
and lay it as a poultice on the boil,
and he will recover.”
[22] And Hezekiah had said,
“What is the sign that I will go up to the house of the LORD?”

> **What does this mean?**
> These two verses are added somewhat later, at the end. In 2 Kings 20:7–8 they are in the middle of the story.
> Interesting: God heals through prayer, but Isaiah also prescribes a remedy, a poultice made of figs. Faith and medicine belong together. If you are sick, you may pray and go to the doctor.
