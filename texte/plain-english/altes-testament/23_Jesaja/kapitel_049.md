# Isaiah – Chapter 49
### I will not forget you

---

## The second song of God’s servant (Verses 1–7)

[1] Listen to me, you islands!
Listen, you peoples far away!
The LORD called me from the womb.
From inside my mother he spoke my name.
[2] He made my mouth like a sharp sword.
He hid me in the shadow of his hand.
He made me into a polished arrow.
He kept me hidden close to him in his quiver.
[3] He said to me, “You are my servant,
Israel, in whom I will show my glory.”
[4] But I said, “I have worked hard in vain.
I have used up my strength for nothing, for no purpose.
Yet surely my right is with the LORD,
and my reward is with my God.”
[5] But now the LORD says,
who formed me from the womb to be his servant,
to bring Jacob back to him
and to gather Israel to him,
for I am honored in the eyes of the LORD,
and my God has become my strength.
[6] Yes, he says:
“It is too small a thing for you to be my servant
to raise up the tribes of Jacob
and to bring back those of Israel who were kept safe.
I will also make you a light for the nations,
so that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth.”
[7] This is what the LORD says, the Redeemer of Israel, his Holy One,
to the one whom people despise,
to the one whom the nation hates,
to a servant of rulers:
“Kings will see it and stand up,
princes, and they will bow down,
because of the LORD, who is faithful,
the Holy One of Israel, who has chosen you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the second song of God’s servant. This time, the servant himself speaks.
> Verses 1–2: God called him even before he was born. His word is as sharp as a sword; he is like an arrow that God keeps ready.
> Verse 3: Here the servant is expressly called “Israel.” This is what the Jewish interpretation is based on, that the servant is the people of Israel.
> Verse 4: The servant feels: Everything was for nothing. Many people know this feeling. But he trusts: My right lies with God.
> Verses 5–6: At the same time, the servant has a task for Israel: to bring the people back to God. That is why some interpreters see the servant as a single person, for example the prophet himself. And the task is even bigger: “a light for the nations,” so that God’s salvation reaches to the end of the earth. The New Testament applies this to Jesus (Luke 2:32) and to the mission of the apostles (Acts 13:47).
> Verse 7: The servant is despised. But in the end, kings will stand up before him.

## The day of salvation (Verses 8–13)

[8] This is what the LORD says:
“At a time of favor I have answered you,
and on a day of salvation I have helped you.
I will protect you
and make you a covenant for the people,
to restore the land,
to give out again the inheritances that lie in ruins,
[9] and to say to the prisoners, ‘Come out!’
and to those in darkness, ‘Show yourselves!’
They will graze along the roads,
and their pasture will be on all the bare heights.
[10] They will not be hungry or thirsty.
Neither heat nor sun will strike them,
for the one who has mercy on them will lead them.
He will guide them to springs of water.
[11] I will turn all my mountains into a road,
and my highways will be raised up.
[12] Look, these will come from far away,
and look, these from the north and from the west,
and these from the land of Sinim.”
[13] Sing, you heavens, and be joyful, earth!
Break out into singing, you mountains!
For the LORD has comforted his people
and will have compassion on his suffering ones.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: “At a time of favor I have answered you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you.” Paul quotes this verse and says: “Look, now is the acceptable time. Look, now is the day of salvation” (2 Corinthians 6:2).
> Verses 9–10: Those returning home will be like a flock led by a good shepherd, without hunger, without thirst, to fresh springs. The book of Revelation takes up verse 10 (7:16–17).
> Verse 12: The “land of Sinim” is not known for certain. Many think of Syene, today’s Aswan in the south of Egypt. A Jewish community lived there later.

## Can a woman forget her child? (Verses 14–21)

[14] But Zion said, “The LORD has abandoned me,
and the Lord has forgotten me.”
[15] “Can a woman forget her nursing child,
so that she has no compassion on the son she gave birth to?
Yes, these may forget,
but I will not forget you!
[16] Look, I have engraved you on the palms of my hands.
Your walls are always before me.
[17] Your children hurry back.
Those who destroyed you and laid you waste are leaving you.
[18] Lift up your eyes and look around:
They are all gathering together and coming to you.
As surely as I live,” says the LORD,
“you will put them all on like jewelry
and adorn yourself with them like a bride.
[19] For your ruins and your deserted places
and your destroyed land,
surely now the land will be too small for those who live there,
and those who swallowed you up will be far away.
[20] The children you thought you had lost
will yet say in your ears,
‘This place is too small for me.
Give me room, so that I can live here.’
[21] Then you will say in your heart,
‘Who has given birth to these for me?
I had lost my children and was alone,
an exile, wandering about.
Who has brought these up?
Look, I was left all alone.
Where have these come from?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Zion, that is, Jerusalem, is pictured as a woman who complains: “The LORD has abandoned me, and the Lord has forgotten me.”
> Verse 15: God’s answer is one of the most tender verses in the Bible: Can a mother forget her baby? Even if that should happen: “But I will not forget you!” God’s love is like a mother’s love, and even more faithful.
> Verse 16: “I have engraved you on the palms of my hands.” As when you write something important on your hand so that you never forget it. God always has his people before his eyes.
> Verses 18–21: The lonely, childless city will suddenly be full of children, so many that there is not enough room. Zion is amazed: Where have they all come from?

## I will save your children (Verses 22–26)

[22] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I will lift up my hand to the nations
and raise my banner to the peoples.
They will bring your sons in their arms,
and your daughters will be carried on their shoulders.
[23] Kings will be your foster fathers,
and their queens your nursing mothers.
They will bow down to you with their faces to the ground
and lick the dust of your feet.
Then you will know that I am the LORD,
and that those who wait for me will not be disappointed.”
[24] Can plunder be taken from the mighty,
or can lawful captives be set free?
[25] But this is what the LORD says:
“Even the captives of the mighty will be taken away,
and the plunder of the violent will be rescued.
For I will fight with the one who fights with you,
and I will save your children.
[26] I will feed your oppressors with their own flesh,
and they will be drunk with their own blood as with sweet wine.
Then all people will know
that I, the LORD, am your Savior
and your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–23: The nations themselves will carry the children of Israel home. Kings and queens will care for them like foster parents. “Licking the dust” is an old picture of complete surrender. What it means is: Things are turned around; the despised people is honored.
> Verses 24–25: Can a mighty man’s captives be taken away from him again? Yes, God can do it. He fights for his people and saves the children.
> Verse 26: A harsh picture. Many interpreters understand it like this: In the end, the oppressors destroy themselves, for example by fighting each other. This is not a call to violence. It describes how violence turns against those who use it. It is God who brings about justice.
