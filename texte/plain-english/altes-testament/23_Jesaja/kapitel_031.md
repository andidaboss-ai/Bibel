# Isaiah – Chapter 31
### The Egyptians are human, not God

---

## How terrible for those who go to Egypt (Verses 1–3)

[1] How terrible for those who go down to Egypt for help
and rely on horses,
and trust in chariots because there are many of them,
and in horsemen because they are very strong,
but do not look to the Holy One of Israel
and do not seek the LORD!
[2] Yet he too is wise.
He will bring disaster
and will not take back his words,
but he will rise up against the house of evildoers
and against the help of those who do wrong.
[3] The Egyptians are human and not God,
and their horses are flesh and not spirit.
When the LORD stretches out his hand,
the helper will stumble,
and the one who is helped will fall,
and they will all perish together.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the warning against the alliance with Egypt. Egypt had many horses and chariots, the most modern military technology of that time.
> Verse 3 is a clear sentence: “The Egyptians are human and not God, and their horses are flesh and not spirit.” Human power is limited. Whoever relies only on it will be disappointed.

---

## God protects Jerusalem like a bird (Verses 4–9)

[4] For this is what the LORD says to me:
“As the lion, the young lion, growls over its prey,
and when a crowd of shepherds is called together against it,
it is not frightened by their shouting
and does not cower at their noise,
so the LORD of Armies will come down
to fight on Mount Zion and on its hill.
[5] Like birds hovering over their nest,
so the LORD of Armies will protect Jerusalem.
He will protect it and rescue it.
He will pass over it and keep it safe.”
[6] Turn back to the one you have turned so far away from,
you people of Israel!
[7] For on that day, everyone will throw away his idols of silver
and his idols of gold,
the sin that your own hands have made for you.
[8] “The Assyrian will fall by a sword that is not a man’s,
and a sword that is not human will eat him up.
He will flee from the sword,
and his young men will be put to forced labor.
[9] His rock will pass away in terror,
and his princes will be afraid of the banner,”
says the LORD, whose fire is in Zion
and whose furnace is in Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: God is like a lion that does not let any noise frighten it.
> Verse 5: And at the same time, God is like a mother bird hovering protectively over her nest. Two pictures: strength and tenderness. “Pass over” reminds us in Hebrew of “Passover,” when God passed over the houses of Israel and spared them.
> Verse 6: The real call: Turn back to God!
> Verse 8: Assyria will not fall by human hands, but because God steps in. That is what chapter 37:36 reports.
