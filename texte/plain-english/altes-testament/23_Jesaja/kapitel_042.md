# Isaiah – Chapter 42
### The first song of God’s servant

---

## My servant brings justice (Verses 1–9)

[1] “Look, my servant, whom I uphold,
my chosen one, in whom my soul delights:
I have put my Spirit on him.
He will bring justice to the nations.
[2] He will not shout or raise his voice
or make it heard in the street.
[3] He will not break a bruised reed,
and he will not put out a dimly burning wick.
He will faithfully bring justice.
[4] He will not grow faint or be discouraged
until he has set up justice on the earth,
and the islands wait for his teaching.”
[5] This is what God, the LORD, says,
who created the heavens and stretched them out,
who spread out the earth and what comes from it,
who gives breath to the people on it
and spirit to those who walk on it:
[6] “I, the LORD, have called you in righteousness.
I will hold your hand.
I will keep you
and make you a covenant for the people,
a light for the nations,
[7] to open the blind eyes,
to bring the prisoners out of the dungeon,
and those who sit in darkness out of the prison.
[8] I am the LORD. That is my name.
I will not give my glory to anyone else,
or my praise to carved idols.
[9] Look, the former things have happened,
and I announce new things.
Before they spring up, I tell you about them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins the first of four songs about the “Servant of God” (the others are in chapters 49, 50 and 52–53).
> Who is this servant? He is chosen by God, God’s Spirit rests on him, and he brings justice to all nations.
> Verses 2–3: He does not do it with noise and violence, but very gently. He does not break off a “bruised reed,” and he does not blow out a wick that has almost gone out. He treats the weak and the hurt with care. A beautiful picture of gentleness.
> Verses 6–7: He becomes “a light for the nations”: He opens blind eyes and sets prisoners free.
> In Judaism, the servant is mostly understood as the people of Israel itself (compare chapter 41:8), which is to be a light for the nations, or also as the prophet. The New Testament connects these words with Jesus (Matthew 12:18–21). At the baptism of Jesus, verse 1 is echoed: “You are my Son. I love you. I am very pleased with you” (Mark 1:11).

---

## A new song (Verses 10–17)

[10] Sing to the LORD a new song,
his praise from the end of the earth,
you who go down to the sea, and everything in it,
you islands and those who live there.
[11] Let the wilderness and its cities raise their voices,
the villages where Kedar lives.
Let the people of Sela sing.
Let them shout for joy from the tops of the mountains!
[12] Let them give glory to the LORD
and announce his praise in the islands.
[13] The LORD goes out like a hero.
He stirs up his zeal like a warrior.
He raises a war cry.
Yes, he shouts aloud.
He wins the victory over his enemies.
[14] “I have been silent for a long time.
I have been quiet and held myself back.
Now I will cry out like a woman giving birth.
I will gasp and pant at the same time.
[15] I will lay waste mountains and hills
and dry up all their plants.
I will turn rivers into islands
and dry up the pools.
[16] I will lead the blind by a way they do not know.
I will guide them on paths they do not know.
I will turn the darkness before them into light
and make the crooked places straight.
These are the things I will do,
and I will not abandon them.
[17] Those who trust in carved idols,
who say to cast images, ‘You are our gods,’
will be turned back.
They will be completely put to shame.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–12: The whole world is to sing a new song to God: from the sea to the desert, from the islands to the mountains.
> Verses 13–14: Two strong pictures of God: a hero going into battle, and a woman in labor who cries out and pants. God has been silent for a long time. Now something new is breaking out like a birth. The Bible sometimes also speaks of God with female pictures.
> Verse 16: A comforting verse: God leads the blind on ways they do not know. He makes the darkness light. He does not abandon them. This is true for everyone who cannot see their way.

---

## The blind and deaf people (Verses 18–25)

[18] “Hear, you deaf,
and look, you blind, so that you may see!
[19] Who is blind but my servant?
Or who is as deaf as my messenger whom I send?
Who is as blind as the one who is at peace,
and as blind as the servant of the LORD?
[20] You see many things, but you do not pay attention.
His ears are open, but he does not listen.
[21] It pleased the LORD, for the sake of his righteousness,
to make the law great and glorious.
[22] But this is a robbed and plundered people.
All of them are trapped in holes
and hidden away in prisons.
They have become captives, and no one rescues them,
plunder, and no one says, ‘Give them back!’
[23] Who among you will listen to this?
Who will pay attention and listen for the time to come?
[24] Who handed Jacob over to be plundered
and Israel to the robbers?
Was it not the LORD, against whom we have sinned?
For they would not walk in his ways,
and they did not obey his law.
[25] So he poured out on him the heat of his anger
and the violence of war.
It set him on fire all around, but he did not understand.
It burned him, but he did not take it to heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Israel is addressed as God’s “servant,” but as a blind and deaf servant. The people were meant to be a light for the nations, but they themselves did not see and hear.
> Verse 22: In exile, the people are like prisoners in holes, robbed, and no one helps.
> Verses 24–25: This happened because the people did not listen to God. And even in their misfortune, they did not understand why.
> This honesty about one’s own guilt is part of the Book of Comfort. But in the next chapter God says: “Do not be afraid, for I have redeemed you!” (chapter 43:1).
