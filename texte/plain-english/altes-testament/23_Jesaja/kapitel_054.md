# Isaiah – Chapter 54
### My loving kindness will not depart from you

---

## Sing, you who are childless! (Verses 1–10)

[1] “Sing, you who are childless, who have not given birth!
Break out into singing and shout for joy, you who have not been in labor!
For the children of the lonely woman are more
than the children of the married woman,” says the LORD.
[2] “Make the place of your tent wider,
and let them stretch out the curtains of your home.
Do not hold back!
Make your ropes longer,
and make your stakes firm.
[3] For you will spread out to the right and to the left,
and your descendants will take possession of nations
and settle in deserted cities.
[4] Do not be afraid, for you will not be put to shame.
Do not be dismayed, for you will not be disappointed.
For you will forget the shame of your youth.
You will no longer remember the disgrace of being a widow.
[5] For your Maker is your husband.
The LORD of Armies is his name.
The Holy One of Israel is your Redeemer.
He will be called the God of the whole earth.
[6] For the LORD has called you
like a wife who was abandoned and is grieved in spirit,
like a wife married young, when she has been rejected,”
says your God.
[7] “For a brief moment I abandoned you,
but with great compassion I will gather you.
[8] In a flood of anger I hid my face from you
for a moment,
but with everlasting loving kindness I will have mercy on you,”
says the LORD, your Redeemer.
[9] “For this is like the waters of Noah to me:
As I swore that the waters of Noah
would never again cover the earth,
so I have sworn that I will no longer be angry with you
and will no longer rebuke you.
[10] For the mountains may depart
and the hills be shaken,
but my loving kindness will not depart from you,
and my covenant of peace will not be shaken,”
says the LORD, who has mercy on you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jerusalem is again described as a woman: She was childless and lonely, like an abandoned wife or a widow. In those days, that was a great disgrace.
> Verses 1–3: Now she is to rejoice, because she will have so many children that the tent has to be made bigger. This means those returning home, who will fill Jerusalem with people again.
> Verse 5: God himself is like a loving husband to his people.
> Verses 7–8: God admits: For a moment I turned away. But my love is everlasting. The anger lasts a moment; the compassion lasts forever.
> Verse 9: Just as God promised after the flood that he would never again flood the earth (Genesis 9:11), he now promises: I will never be so angry with you again.
> Verse 10 is a much-loved verse of comfort: “For the mountains may depart and the hills be shaken, but my loving kindness will not depart from you.” Even when the most solid things collapse, God’s love remains.
> A note: In the Bible, God is compared to a husband who takes his wife back. This says something about God’s faithfulness. It does not mean that people in a marriage must simply put up with rejection, coldness or violence.

## The city made of jewels (Verses 11–17)

[11] “You who are suffering, tossed by storms, not comforted!
Look, I will set your stones in beautiful colors
and lay your foundations with sapphires.
[12] I will make your towers of rubies,
your gates of sparkling jewels,
and all your walls of precious stones.
[13] All your children will be taught by the LORD,
and your children’s peace will be great.
[14] You will be established in righteousness.
You will be far from oppression, for you will not be afraid,
and far from terror, for it will not come near you.
[15] Look, they may gather together, but not because of me.
Whoever gathers against you will fall because of you.
[16] Look, I have created the blacksmith
who fans the coals into flame
and forges a weapon for his work.
And I have created the destroyer to destroy.
[17] No weapon that is made against you will succeed.
And you will condemn every tongue
that rises against you in court.
This is the inheritance of the servants of the LORD,
and their righteousness comes from me,” says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: The destroyed city will be rebuilt more beautifully than ever before, out of sapphires, rubies and jewels. The book of Revelation takes up this picture for the “new Jerusalem” (Isaiah 21:18–21).
> Verse 13: “All your children will be taught by the LORD.” Jesus quotes this verse (John 6:45).
> Verse 14: A city without oppression and without fear: that is God’s goal.
> Verses 16–17: The weapon makers, too, are God’s creatures. He has power over them. That is why no weapon will succeed against his people. This is a promise of protection, not a call to fight.
> Verse 17: From here on, the book no longer speaks of the one “servant,” but of the “servants of the LORD,” that is, of all who are faithful to God.
