# Isaiah – Chapter 7
### The sign of Immanuel

---

## Don’t be afraid! (Verses 1–9)

[1] In the days of Ahaz, the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah,
Rezin, the king of Syria, and Pekah, the son of Remaliah, the king of Israel,
went up to Jerusalem to make war against it,
but they could not defeat it.
[2] The house of David was told, “Syria has joined forces with Ephraim.”
Then his heart trembled, and the heart of his people,
as the trees of the forest tremble in the wind.
[3] Then the LORD said to Isaiah,
“Go out now to meet Ahaz,
you and your son Shearjashub,
at the end of the water channel of the upper pool,
on the road to the field where cloth is washed.
[4] Tell him: ‘Be careful, and stay calm!
Don’t be afraid, and don’t lose heart
because of these two smoking stubs of torches,
because of the fierce anger of Rezin and Syria
and of the son of Remaliah.
[5] Because Syria, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah have planned evil against you,
saying,
[6] “Let’s go up against Judah, tear it apart,
divide it among ourselves,
and set up a king in it, the son of Tabeel,”
[7] this is what the Lord GOD says:
“It will not stand, and it will not happen.”
[8] For the head of Syria is Damascus,
and the head of Damascus is Rezin.
Within 65 years, Ephraim will be broken to pieces,
so that it is no longer a people.
[9] The head of Ephraim is Samaria,
and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah.
If you will not believe, surely you will not be established.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The situation: In the year 734 BC, two neighboring kings attack Jerusalem, the king of Syria and the king of the northern kingdom of Israel (also called “Ephraim”). They want to remove King Ahaz from power. Ahaz and his people tremble with fear.
> Verse 3: Isaiah takes his son with him. His name “Shearjashub” means “A remnant will return.” The name itself is a message.
> Verse 4: “Stay calm! Don’t be afraid!” The two enemies are only “smoking stubs of torches”: They are still smoking, but their fire will soon be out.
> Verse 9: “If you will not believe, surely you will not be established.” In Hebrew, this is a play on words: “believe” and “be established” come from the same word, from which “Amen” also comes. Whoever holds on to God has a firm footing.

---

## The sign: God with us (Verses 10–17)

[10] The LORD spoke to Ahaz again and said,
[11] “Ask for a sign from the LORD your God!
Ask for it from the depths or from the heights above.”
[12] But Ahaz said, “I will not ask.
I will not test the LORD.”
[13] Then he said, “Listen now, you house of David!
Is it not enough for you to try people’s patience,
that you also try the patience of my God?
[14] So the Lord himself will give you a sign:
Look, the virgin will become pregnant
and give birth to a son,
and she will call him Immanuel.
[15] He will eat butter and honey
when he knows how to refuse evil and choose good.
[16] For before the child knows how to refuse evil and choose good,
the land whose two kings you dread
will be abandoned.
[17] The LORD will bring on you, on your people, and on your father’s house
days such as have not come
since the day that Ephraim broke away from Judah:
the king of Assyria.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: God offers Ahaz a sign. Ahaz refuses with a pious excuse. In reality, he has already decided to get help from the king of Assyria instead of from God (2 Kings 16:7).
> Verse 14 is one of the best-known verses of the Bible. “Immanuel” means: “God is with us.”
> In Hebrew, the word “almah” is used here. It means “young woman,” a young woman of an age to marry. The ancient Greek translation translated it with “parthenos,” “virgin.” Our English source text writes “virgin.”
> In Judaism, the verse is usually understood like this: A young woman in Isaiah’s time, perhaps the king’s wife or Isaiah’s wife, has a child. Even before this child is grown, the two enemy kings are defeated. The child is a sign: God is with us.
> The Gospel of Matthew quotes this verse at the birth of Jesus (Matthew 1:22–23). Christians see in it an announcement that in Jesus, God is truly “with us.”
> Verses 15–16: “Butter and honey” is the food of people in a ruined land where farming is no longer possible, only raising animals. Before the child is old enough to tell good from evil, the enemies are gone.
> Verse 17: But then an even greater threat comes: Assyria, the very power from which Ahaz is seeking help.

## The land will be ruined (Verses 18–25)

[18] “On that day, the LORD will whistle for the fly
that is at the farthest end of the rivers of Egypt,
and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.
[19] They will come and all settle
in the ruined valleys, in the cracks of the rocks,
on all the thorn bushes, and on all the pastures.
[20] On that day, the Lord will shave with a razor
hired from beyond the River,
that is, with the king of Assyria:
the head and the hair of the feet,
and it will also take away the beard.
[21] It will happen on that day
that a man will keep alive a young cow and two sheep.
[22] And it will happen that because of the plenty of milk they give,
he will eat butter,
for everyone who is left in the land will eat butter and honey.
[23] It will happen on that day
that every place where there were 1,000 vines,
worth 1,000 silver shekels (about 25 pounds of silver),
will turn into briers and thorns.
[24] People will go there with arrows and bow,
for the whole land will be full of briers and thorns.
[25] And all the hills that were worked with the hoe,
you will no longer go there for fear of briers and thorns,
but they will be used for letting the cattle loose,
and the sheep will trample on them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: The armies of Egypt and Assyria will come like swarms of flies and bees.
> Verse 20: A humiliating picture: The king of Assyria, whom Ahaz himself has “hired,” will shave Judah bald like a razor. Back then, prisoners of war were shaved to humiliate them.
> Verse 23: A shekel is about 0.4 ounces of silver.
> Verses 21–25: The fertile land becomes wilderness. Instead of vineyards, thorns grow. The few survivors keep only a few animals. Where there used to be fields, people only go hunting with bow and arrow.
> The message to Ahaz: Whoever trusts in people instead of God brings disaster on himself in the end.
