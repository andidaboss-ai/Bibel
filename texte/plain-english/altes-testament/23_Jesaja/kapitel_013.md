# Isaiah – Chapter 13
### The judgment on Babylon

---

## The day of the LORD is coming (Verses 1–16)

[1] The burden about Babylon, which Isaiah, the son of Amoz, saw.
[2] Set up a banner on the bare mountain!
Raise your voice to them!
Wave your hand, so that they go in through the gates of the nobles!
[3] I have given orders to my holy ones.
Yes, I have called my mighty men for my anger,
those who proudly rejoice.
[4] The noise of a crowd is on the mountains,
as of a great people.
The noise of the uproar of kingdoms,
of nations gathered together!
The LORD of Armies is gathering the army for battle.
[5] They come from a distant land,
from the farthest end of the sky,
the LORD and the weapons of his fury,
to destroy the whole land.
[6] Wail, for the day of the LORD is near!
It will come like destruction from the Almighty.
[7] So all hands will go limp,
and every person’s heart will melt.
[8] They will be terrified.
Pains and pangs will seize them.
They will writhe like a woman in labor.
They will stare at one another in horror.
Their faces will glow like flames.
[9] Look, the day of the LORD is coming,
cruel, with fury and fierce anger,
to make the land a wasteland
and to destroy its sinners from it.
[10] For the stars of the sky and their constellations
will not give their light.
The sun will be dark when it rises,
and the moon will not let its light shine.
[11] I will punish the world for its evil
and the wicked for their guilt.
I will put an end to the arrogance of the proud
and bring low the pride of the violent.
[12] I will make people rarer than fine gold,
a person rarer than the pure gold of Ophir.
[13] So I will make the heavens tremble,
and the earth will be shaken from its place
by the fury of the LORD of Armies
on the day of his fierce anger.
[14] It will happen: Like a hunted gazelle
and like sheep that no one gathers,
each one will turn to his own people,
and each one will flee to his own land.
[15] Everyone who is found will be stabbed.
Everyone who is caught will fall by the sword.
[16] Their little children will also be smashed before their eyes.
Their houses will be looted, and their wives raped.

> **What does this mean?**
> From chapter 13 on, there are words about foreign peoples (up to chapter 23). The first one is aimed at Babylon, the greatest and proudest empire.
> “The burden”: That is what prophetic words about peoples are called. A heavy message that the prophet must carry.
> Verses 6–13: The “day of the LORD” is described like the end of the world: The sun, the moon and the stars grow dark, the earth shakes. This is picture language for a mighty overthrow. Jesus uses similar pictures (Matthew 24:29).
> Verses 15–16: These verses are horrifying. They describe what happened to the defeated in war back then: Children were killed, women were raped. The prophet makes nothing look better than it is.
> Important: These verses describe the cruelty of war. They are no permission and no approval. Violence against children and rape are always serious crimes, in war as in peace. Today, under international law, they are war crimes.

## The Medes are coming (Verses 17–22)

[17] Look, I will stir up the Medes against them,
who do not care about silver
and take no pleasure in gold.
[18] Their bows will smash the young men.
They will have no pity on the fruit of the womb.
Their eyes will not spare the children.
[19] Babylon, the jewel of kingdoms,
the proud beauty of the Chaldeans,
will be like when God destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah.
[20] It will never be lived in again,
and no one will live there from generation to generation.
The Arab will not pitch a tent there,
and shepherds will not let their flocks rest there.
[21] But wild animals of the desert will rest there,
and their houses will be full of jackals.
Ostriches will live there,
and wild goats will leap about there.
[22] Hyenas will howl in their fortresses
and jackals in the splendid palaces.
Her time is near,
and her days will not be made longer.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: The Medes lived in what is today Iran. Together with the Persians, they conquered Babylon in the year 539 BC. They cannot be bribed with money.
> Verse 18: Again a description of terrible violence in war. Here too: This is no approval, but a description of how cruel wars are.
> Verses 19–22: Splendid Babylon becomes a ruin where only wild animals live. In fact, the city fell apart over the centuries. Today only ruins can be seen in Iraq.
> The message: Even the mightiest empire in the world passes away if it is proud and cruel.
