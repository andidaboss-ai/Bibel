# Isaiah – Chapter 61
### The Spirit of the Lord is on me

---

## Good news for the poor (Verses 1–3)

[1] The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on me.
The LORD has anointed me.
Anointed means: chosen and set apart for a task.
He has chosen me to bring good news to the humble.
He has sent me to bandage those whose hearts are broken.
He has sent me to announce freedom to the captives.
Those who are tied up will be set free.
[2] He has sent me to announce the year of the LORD’s favor.
I am to announce the day when our God takes revenge.
I am to comfort all who mourn.
[3] I am to care for those who mourn in Zion.
I am to give them a garland of flowers instead of ashes.
I am to give them the oil of joy instead of sorrow.
I am to give them clothes of praise instead of a heavy heart.
Then people will call them “trees of righteousness.”
They will call them “the planting of the LORD.”
And the LORD will be honored.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here someone speaks on whom God’s Spirit rests. He is “anointed,” that is, given a task. His task:
> – to bring good news to the poor and humble,
> – to bind up people with broken hearts, the way you bandage a wound,
> – to announce freedom to prisoners,
> – to comfort those who mourn.
> “The year of the LORD’s favor” recalls the Year of Jubilee (Leviticus 25): Every 50 years, debts were to be canceled and slaves set free. A new beginning for everyone.
> Verse 3: Instead of ashes on their heads (a sign of mourning), those who mourn receive a festive garland. Instead of mourning, the oil of joy.
> In the synagogue in Nazareth, Jesus read these verses aloud and said: “Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing” (Luke 4:21). He stopped in the middle of verse 2, after the words about the year of the LORD’s favor, and did not read aloud the words about the day when God takes revenge. In Judaism, the speaker is mostly understood as the prophet himself, who brings this message to Israel.

## You will be called priests of the LORD (Verses 4–9)

[4] They will rebuild the old ruins.
They will rebuild the places that were destroyed long ago.
They will repair the ruined cities.
These cities have been in ruins for many generations.
[5] Strangers will stand and feed your flocks.
Foreigners will work your fields and your vineyards.
[6] But you will be called “priests of the LORD.”
People will call you “servants of our God.”
You will eat the wealth of the nations.
You will boast about their glory.
[7] You had shame.
Instead you will receive twice as much.
You had disgrace.
Instead they will rejoice over their share.
So in their land they will own twice as much.
They will have joy that lasts forever.
[8] “I, the LORD, love justice.
I hate robbery and wrongdoing.
I will give them their reward faithfully.
I will make a covenant with them that lasts forever.
A covenant is a binding agreement.
[9] Their children will be known among the nations.
Their descendants will be known among the peoples.
All who see them will recognize them.
They will see: These are the children the LORD has blessed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Those returning home rebuild the destroyed cities.
> Verses 5–6: All of Israel becomes “priests of the LORD.” Just as the priests were there for the people, Israel is to be there for the whole world and show God to the nations (compare Exodus 19:6). The other nations help it do this.
> Verse 7: For the shame they suffered, there is double joy.
> Verse 8: “I, the LORD, love justice. I hate robbery and wrongdoing.” That is a principle for everything God does.

---

## I rejoice in the LORD (Verses 10–11)

[10] I will be very happy in the LORD!
My soul will be full of joy in my God.
He has dressed me in clothes of salvation.
He has wrapped me in a robe of righteousness.
It is like a bridegroom who puts on a garland of flowers.
It is like a bride who puts on her jewels.
[11] The earth makes its plants sprout.
A garden makes the seeds sown in it spring up.
In the same way, the Lord GOD will make righteousness and praise
spring up in front of all the nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: A song of joy. God has given the speaker new clothes, with salvation and righteousness, as beautiful as a bride and bridegroom on their wedding day. Mary sings in a similar way: “My soul magnifies the Lord. My spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior” (Luke 1:46–47).
> Verse 11: Righteousness grows like a plant in a garden. God makes it spring up, slowly but surely.
