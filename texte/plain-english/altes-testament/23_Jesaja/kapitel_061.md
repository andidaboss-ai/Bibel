# Isaiah – Chapter 61
### The Spirit of the Lord is on me

---

## Good news for the poor (Verses 1–3)

[1] The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on me,
because the LORD has anointed me
to bring good news to the humble.
He has sent me to bind up those whose hearts are broken,
to announce freedom to the captives
and release to those who are bound,
[2] to announce the year of the LORD’s favor
and the day of vengeance of our God,
to comfort all who mourn,
[3] to provide for those who mourn in Zion,
to give them a garland instead of ashes,
the oil of joy instead of mourning,
a garment of praise instead of a heavy spirit,
so that they will be called “trees of righteousness,”
“the planting of the LORD,” so that he may be glorified.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here someone speaks on whom God’s Spirit rests. He is “anointed,” that is, given a task. His task:
> – to bring good news to the poor and humble,
> – to bind up people with broken hearts, the way you bandage a wound,
> – to announce freedom to prisoners,
> – to comfort those who mourn.
> “The year of the LORD’s favor” recalls the Year of Jubilee (Leviticus 25): Every 50 years, debts were to be canceled and slaves set free. A new beginning for everyone.
> Verse 3: Instead of ashes on their heads (a sign of mourning), those who mourn receive a festive garland. Instead of mourning, the oil of joy.
> In the synagogue in Nazareth, Jesus read these verses aloud and said: “Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing” (Luke 4:21). He stopped in the middle of verse 2, after the words about the year of the LORD’s favor, and did not read aloud the words about the day of vengeance. In Judaism, the speaker is mostly understood as the prophet himself, who brings this message to Israel.

## You will be called priests of the LORD (Verses 4–9)

[4] They will rebuild the ancient ruins.
They will raise up the places that were destroyed long ago.
They will renew the ruined cities
that have lain in ruins for many generations.
[5] Strangers will stand and take care of your flocks.
Foreigners will work your fields and your vineyards.
[6] But you will be called “priests of the LORD.”
People will call you “servants of our God.”
You will eat the wealth of the nations.
You will boast in their glory.
[7] Instead of your shame you will receive a double share.
Instead of disgrace they will rejoice in their share.
So in their land they will possess a double portion.
Everlasting joy will be theirs.
[8] “For I, the LORD, love justice.
I hate robbery and wrongdoing.
I will faithfully give them their reward
and make an everlasting covenant with them.
[9] Their descendants will be known among the nations
and their children among the peoples.
All who see them will recognize
that they are the descendants the LORD has blessed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Those returning home rebuild the destroyed cities.
> Verses 5–6: All of Israel becomes “priests of the LORD.” Just as the priests were there for the people, Israel is to be there for the whole world and show God to the nations (compare Exodus 19:6). The other nations help it do this.
> Verse 7: For the shame they suffered, there is double joy.
> Verse 8: “I, the LORD, love justice. I hate robbery and wrongdoing.” That is a principle for everything God does.

---

## I rejoice in the LORD (Verses 10–11)

[10] I will greatly rejoice in the LORD!
My soul will be joyful in my God,
for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation.
He has covered me with the robe of righteousness,
as a bridegroom adorns himself with a garland
and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.
[11] For as the earth brings up its shoots,
and as a garden makes what is sown in it spring up,
so the Lord GOD will make righteousness and praise
spring up before all the nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: A song of joy. God has given the speaker new clothes, with salvation and righteousness, as beautiful as a bride and bridegroom on their wedding day. Mary sings in a similar way: “My soul magnifies the Lord. My spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior” (Luke 1:46–47).
> Verse 11: Righteousness grows like a plant in a garden. God makes it spring up, slowly but surely.
