# Isaiah – Chapter 34
### Judgment on Edom

---

## Judgment on the nations (Verses 1–4)

[1] Come near, you nations, to hear!
Listen, you peoples!
Let the earth hear, and everything that fills it,
the world, and everything that comes from it.
[2] For the LORD is angry with all the nations
and furious with all their armies.
He has completely destroyed them.
He has handed them over to slaughter.
[3] Their dead will be thrown out,
and the stench of their bodies will rise.
The mountains will melt with their blood.
[4] The whole army of the sky will dissolve.
The sky will be rolled up like a scroll,
and all its armies will wither,
as a leaf withers from the vine
or from the fig tree.

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 34 and 35 belong together. They show two sides: judgment on the enemies (chapter 34) and rescue for God’s people (chapter 35).
> Verses 2–3: Terrible pictures of war and death. This is the language of prophecies of judgment, which wants to show: God does not let injustice go unpunished. These pictures are not a call for people to wage war.
> Verse 4: Even the stars fall. The sky is rolled up like a book being closed. The book of Revelation takes up this picture (Revelation 6:13–14).

---

## The sword over Edom (Verses 5–17)

[5] For my sword has drunk its fill in the sky.
Look, it will come down on Edom,
on the people I have put under my curse, for judgment.
[6] The sword of the LORD is full of blood.
It is covered with fat,
with the blood of lambs and goats,
with the kidney fat of rams,
for the LORD has a sacrifice in Bozrah
and a great slaughter in the land of Edom.
[7] The wild oxen will fall with them,
and the young bulls with the mighty bulls.
Their land will be drunk with blood
and their dust greasy with fat.
[8] For the LORD has a day of vengeance,
a year of payback for the cause of Zion.
[9] Edom’s streams will be turned into tar
and its dust into sulfur,
and its land will become burning tar.
[10] It will not be put out, night or day.
Its smoke will rise forever.
From generation to generation it will lie in ruins.
No one will ever pass through it, forever and ever.
[11] But the pelican and the porcupine will own it.
The owl and the raven will live in it.
He will stretch over it the measuring line of confusion
and the plumb line of emptiness.
[12] They will call its nobles to the kingdom,
but no one will be there,
and all its princes will be nothing.
[13] Thorns will grow in its palaces,
nettles and thistles in its fortresses.
It will be a home for jackals,
a yard for ostriches.
[14] The wild animals of the desert will meet with the wolves,
and the wild goat will call to its companion.
Yes, the night creature will settle there
and find herself a place to rest.
[15] The arrow snake will make her nest there,
lay eggs, hatch them, and gather her young in her shade.
Yes, the kites will gather there,
each one with her mate.
[16] Search in the book of the LORD and read:
Not one of these will be missing.
None will be without her mate.
For my mouth has commanded it,
and his Spirit has gathered them.
[17] He has cast the lot for them,
and his hand has divided it for them with a measuring line.
They will own it forever.
From generation to generation they will live in it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Edom was a neighboring people south of the Dead Sea. The Edomites were descended from Esau, Jacob’s brother. When Jerusalem was destroyed later, they rejoiced and helped (Obadiah 10–14; Psalm 137:7). That is why Edom stands here for all the enemies of God and his people.
> Verse 6: The judgment is compared to a sacrifice. Bozrah was the capital of Edom. These pictures are very harsh. They belong to the language of that time and are meant to show: God will make sure there is justice. This is not a command for people to take revenge.
> Verses 9–10: Edom will burn like Sodom and Gomorrah.
> Verses 11–15: The land becomes a wilderness where only animals live. “Measuring line of confusion”: God “measures” the land, but not to build it up, rather to make it empty. The Hebrew word for “night creature” is “Lilith,” a being from ancient legends.
> Verse 16: “Search in the book of the LORD”: God’s words come true exactly.
