# Isaiah – Chapter 40
### Comfort, comfort my people!

---

## Comfort my people (Verses 1–11)

[1] “Comfort, comfort my people,” says your God.
[2] “Speak kindly to Jerusalem,
and call out to her that her hard service is over,
that her guilt is forgiven,
that she has received from the LORD’s hand double
for all her sins.”
[3] A voice calls out,
“Prepare the way of the LORD in the wilderness!
Make a level highway in the desert for our God!
[4] Every valley shall be raised up,
and every mountain and hill shall be made low.
The uneven ground shall be made level,
and the rough places a plain.
[5] The glory of the LORD will be revealed,
and all people together will see it,
for the mouth of the LORD has spoken.”
[6] A voice says, “Call out!”
Someone said, “What shall I call out?”
“All people are like grass,
and all their beauty is like the flower of the field.
[7] The grass withers, the flower fades,
because the breath of the LORD blows on it.
Surely the people are like grass.
[8] The grass withers, the flower fades,
but the word of our God stands forever.”
[9] You who bring good news to Zion,
go up on a high mountain!
You who bring good news to Jerusalem,
lift up your voice with strength!
Lift it up! Do not be afraid!
Say to the cities of Judah, “Look, here is your God!”
[10] Look, the Lord GOD comes as a mighty one,
and his arm rules for him.
Look, his reward is with him,
and his payment goes before him.
[11] He will feed his flock like a shepherd.
He will gather the lambs in his arm
and carry them close to his heart.
He will gently lead the mother sheep.

> **What does this mean?**
> With chapter 40, a new part of the book of Isaiah begins, the “Book of Comfort” (chapters 40–55). It speaks to the people who live in exile in Babylon, about 150 years after Isaiah. Jerusalem is destroyed, the temple burned. Many think: God has forgotten us.
> Verses 1–2: The first words are: “Comfort, comfort my people!” The punishment is over. The guilt is forgiven. God speaks “kindly” again, literally: “to the heart” of Jerusalem.
> Verses 3–5: A road is built through the desert, on which God leads his people home. All obstacles are removed. The Gospels connect this voice with John the Baptist, who prepares the way for Jesus (Mark 1:2–3).
> Verses 6–8: People are as short-lived as grass. But God’s word stands forever. That is the reason for hope. Peter quotes these verses (1 Peter 1:24–25).
> Verse 9: A woman bringing good news is to climb a mountain and call out loudly: “Look, here is your God!”
> Verse 11: A tender picture: God is like a shepherd who takes the little lambs in his arms and gently leads the mother animals. Handel set this verse to music in his “Messiah.”

## Who is like God? (Verses 12–26)

[12] Who has measured the waters in the hollow of his hand
and marked off the sky with the width of his hand,
and held the dust of the earth in a measuring basket,
and weighed the mountains on scales
and the hills in a balance?
[13] Who has directed the Spirit of the LORD
or taught him as his adviser?
[14] Whom did he ask for advice,
and who instructed him
and taught him the path of justice,
and taught him knowledge,
and showed him the way of understanding?
[15] Look, the nations are like a drop in a bucket
and are thought of as a speck of dust on the scales.
Look, he lifts up the islands like a tiny thing.
[16] Lebanon is not enough for firewood,
and its animals are not enough for a burnt offering.
[17] All the nations are like nothing before him.
He regards them as less than nothing, as emptiness.
[18] To whom, then, will you compare God?
Or what image will you set beside him?
[19] A craftsman casts an idol,
and the goldsmith covers it with gold
and casts silver chains for it.
[20] Whoever is too poor for such an offering
chooses wood that will not rot.
He looks for a skilled craftsman
to set up a carved idol that will not wobble.
[21] Don’t you know? Haven’t you heard?
Haven’t you been told from the beginning?
Haven’t you understood since the earth was founded?
[22] He is the one who sits above the circle of the earth,
and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers.
He stretches out the heavens like a curtain
and spreads them out like a tent to live in.
[23] He brings princes to nothing.
He makes the judges of the earth meaningless.
[24] Hardly are they planted,
hardly are they sown,
hardly has their stem taken root in the ground,
when he just blows on them, and they wither,
and the storm carries them away like stubble.
[25] “To whom, then, will you compare me?
Who is my equal?” says the Holy One.
[26] Lift up your eyes on high and see
who has created these.
He brings out their army by number.
He calls them all by name.
Because of his great might, and because he is strong in power,
not one is missing.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people in exile ask: Isn’t our God weaker than the gods of Babylon? Isaiah answers with mighty pictures:
> Verse 12: God measures the oceans in the hollow of his hand and weighs the mountains on scales.
> Verse 15: The great world empires, Babylon too, are like a drop in a bucket to him.
> Verses 18–20: The gods of Babylon are made by craftsmen. They have to be nailed down so that they don’t fall over. How can anyone compare the living God with something like that?
> Verse 22: Seen from above, people are as small as grasshoppers.
> Verse 26: Look at the starry sky! The Babylonians worshiped the stars as gods. Isaiah says: God created them all. He knows every star by name.

## New strength for the tired (Verses 27–31)

[27] Why do you say, Jacob, and why do you speak, Israel:
“My way is hidden from the LORD,
and my God ignores my rights”?
[28] Don’t you know? Haven’t you heard?
The everlasting God, the LORD,
the Creator of the ends of the earth,
does not grow faint or weary.
His understanding cannot be searched out.
[29] He gives power to the weak,
and he increases the strength of those who have no might.
[30] Even young people grow faint and weary,
and young men stumble and fall.
[31] But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength.
They will rise up on wings like eagles.
They will run and not grow weary.
They will walk and not grow faint.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: The people complain: God no longer sees us. We don’t matter to him.
> Verses 28–29: Isaiah’s answer: God never grows tired. And he gives strength to the tired.
> Verse 31 is one of the most loved verses in the Bible. Whoever hopes in God receives new strength, like an eagle that lets the wind carry it upward. Many people have found courage in this verse in hard times.
