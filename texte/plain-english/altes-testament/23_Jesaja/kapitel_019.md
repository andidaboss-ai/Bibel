# Isaiah – Chapter 19
### Blessed be Egypt, my people

---

## Judgment on Egypt (Verses 1–15)

[1] The burden about Egypt.
“Look, the LORD is riding on a swift cloud
and is coming to Egypt.
The idols of Egypt will tremble before him,
and the heart of Egypt will melt within it.
[2] I will stir up Egyptians against Egyptians,
and they will fight, each against his brother
and each against his neighbor,
city against city and kingdom against kingdom.
[3] The spirit of the Egyptians will fail within them.
I will bring their plans to nothing.
They will consult the idols, the magicians,
the spirits of the dead, and the fortune-tellers.
[4] I will hand the Egyptians over to a cruel master.
A fierce king will rule over them,”
says the Lord, the LORD of Armies.
[5] The waters of the sea will fail,
and the river will dry up and be empty.
[6] The rivers will stink.
The streams of Egypt will shrink and dry up.
The reeds and rushes will wither.
[7] The meadows by the Nile, on the bank of the Nile,
and all the sown fields of the Nile will dry up,
blow away, and be gone.
[8] The fishermen will lament,
and all who cast a hook into the Nile will mourn,
and those who spread nets on the water will waste away.
[9] Also those who work with combed flax
and those who weave white cloth will be put to shame.
[10] Its pillars will be broken to pieces.
All who work for wages will be sad.
[11] The princes of Zoan are completely foolish.
The advice of Pharaoh’s wisest advisers has become stupid.
How can you say to Pharaoh,
“I am a son of the wise, a son of ancient kings”?
[12] Where are your wise men, then?
Let them tell you now,
and let them know what the LORD of Armies has planned against Egypt.
[13] The princes of Zoan have become fools.
The princes of Memphis are deceived.
They have led Egypt astray,
they who are the cornerstone of her tribes.
[14] The LORD has mixed a spirit of confusion within her,
and they have led Egypt astray in all it does,
as a drunk man staggers in his vomit.
[15] There will be no work for Egypt
that head or tail, palm branch or reed, can do.

> **What does this mean?**
> Egypt was a great power and again and again a tempting partner for an alliance for Judah. Isaiah says: Egypt itself will fall into chaos.
> Verse 1: God “is riding on a swift cloud.” The gods of Egypt tremble before him.
> Verse 2: Civil war tears the land apart.
> Verses 5–10: The Nile was Egypt’s lifeline. If it dries up, everything is lost: farming, fishing, weaving. The economy collapses.
> Verses 11–14: The famous wise men of Egypt no longer know what to do. Their wisdom is worth nothing without God.

---

## Egypt will come to know the LORD (Verses 16–25)

[16] On that day, the Egyptians will be like women.
They will tremble and be afraid
of the waving of the hand of the LORD of Armies,
which he waves over them.
[17] The land of Judah will be a terror to Egypt.
Everyone who hears about it will be afraid
because of the plan that the LORD of Armies has decided against it.
[18] On that day, there will be five cities in the land of Egypt
that speak the language of Canaan
and swear by the LORD of Armies.
One will be called “The city of destruction.”
[19] On that day, there will be an altar to the LORD
in the middle of the land of Egypt,
and a pillar to the LORD at its border.
[20] It will be a sign and a witness to the LORD of Armies
in the land of Egypt.
For they will cry out to the LORD because of their oppressors,
and he will send them a savior and a defender,
and he will rescue them.
[21] The LORD will make himself known to Egypt,
and the Egyptians will know the LORD on that day.
Yes, they will worship him with sacrifices and offerings,
and they will make vows to the LORD and keep them.
[22] The LORD will strike Egypt, striking and healing.
They will turn back to the LORD,
and he will listen to their prayers and heal them.
[23] On that day, there will be a highway from Egypt to Assyria.
The Assyrian will come to Egypt,
and the Egyptian to Assyria,
and the Egyptians will worship together with the Assyrians.
[24] On that day, Israel will be the third
together with Egypt and with Assyria,
a blessing in the middle of the earth,
[25] because the LORD of Armies has blessed them and said,
“Blessed be Egypt, my people,
Assyria, the work of my hands,
and Israel, my inheritance.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: “Like women”: Back then, women were thought to be weak and fearful. That is a prejudice of that time, not a judgment of the Bible about women. The Bible tells of many brave women.
> Verses 18–25 are among the most amazing verses of the whole Old Testament.
> Of all peoples, Egypt, where Israel lived as slaves, and Assyria, the most brutal enemy, will come to know God and worship him.
> Verse 20: Just as Israel once cried out to God in Egypt, now the Egyptians themselves will cry out to God, and he will send a savior to them too.
> Verse 22: God “strikes and heals.” His judgment has healing as its goal.
> Verse 23: A highway connects the old archenemies. They travel to each other and pray together.
> Verse 25: God calls Egypt “my people” and Assyria “the work of my hands,” titles that otherwise only Israel has. God’s blessing is for all peoples. A vision of peace and reconciliation between enemy nations that still gives hope today.
