# Isaiah – Chapter 16
### Moab looks for refuge

---

## Take in the refugees (Verses 1–5)

[1] Send the lambs for the ruler of the land
from Sela through the wilderness
to the mountain of the daughter of Zion.
[2] For it will be: Like birds fluttering about,
like a nest that has been scattered,
so the daughters of Moab will be at the fords of the Arnon.
[3] Give advice! Do justice!
Make your shade like the night in the middle of the day!
Hide the outcasts!
Don’t betray the refugee!
[4] Let my outcasts live with you!
As for Moab, be a hiding place for him from the destroyer!
For the one who squeezes others is gone.
The destruction stops.
The oppressors have disappeared from the land.
[5] A throne will be set up in loving kindness.
Someone will sit on it in faithfulness,
in the tent of David,
who judges and seeks justice
and is quick to do what is right.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Moabites are fleeing. They are like startled birds at the Arnon River, the border of their land.
> Verse 1: Moab is to send gifts to Jerusalem and ask for protection.
> Verses 3–4: A moving call: Take in the refugees! Give them shade as in the night! Hide them! Don’t betray them! This text is one of the strongest pleas in the Bible for protecting refugees.
> Verse 5: A future king from David’s family will rule with kindness, faithfulness and justice. Foreigners too will find refuge with him.

---

## Lament over Moab’s pride (Verses 6–14)

[6] We have heard of the pride of Moab, that he is very proud,
of his arrogance, his pride, and his anger.
His bragging is nothing.
[7] So Moab will wail for Moab.
Everyone will wail.
You will mourn for the raisin cakes of Kir Hareseth,
completely crushed.
[8] For the fields of Heshbon have withered,
and the vine of Sibmah.
The lords of the nations have broken down its best branches,
which reached as far as Jazer,
which spread into the wilderness.
Its shoots spread out.
They went across the sea.
[9] So I will weep with the weeping of Jazer
for the vine of Sibmah.
I will water you with my tears, Heshbon and Elealeh,
for the battle cry has fallen
on your summer fruits and on your harvest.
[10] Gladness and joy are taken away from the fertile field.
In the vineyards there is no more singing and no shouting for joy.
No one treads out wine in the winepresses anymore.
I have put an end to the shouting.
[11] So my heart sounds like a harp for Moab,
and my inner being for Kir Heres.
[12] It will happen: When Moab appears,
when he wears himself out on the high place
and comes to his sanctuary to pray,
he will get nowhere.
[13] This is the word that the LORD spoke about Moab in the past.
[14] But now the LORD has spoken and said,
“Within three years, as a worker under contract would count them,
the glory of Moab will be despised,
with all his great crowds,
and the rest will be very small and weak.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Moab’s problem was its pride.
> Verses 7–10: Moab was famous for its vineyards. Now everything is destroyed. No more happy songs at the grape harvest.
> Verses 9 and 11: Again the prophet weeps with them. His heart sounds sad like a harp. God’s compassion is also for foreign peoples.
> Verse 14: “As a worker under contract would count them”: A worker counts the days of his contract exactly, not one day more and not one day less. That is how exactly God’s word will come true.
