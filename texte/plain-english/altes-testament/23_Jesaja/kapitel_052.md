# Isaiah – Chapter 52
### How beautiful are the feet of the messenger

---

## Wake up, Zion! (Verses 1–6)

[1] Wake up, wake up!
Put on your strength, Zion!
Put on your beautiful clothes, Jerusalem, the holy city!
For from now on, the uncircumcised and the unclean
will no longer come into you.
[2] Shake off the dust!
Get up, sit up straight, Jerusalem!
Loosen the chains from your neck,
captive daughter of Zion!
[3] For this is what the LORD says:
“You were sold for nothing,
and you will be redeemed without money.”
[4] For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“At first my people went down to Egypt to live there as foreigners,
and the Assyrian oppressed them without cause.
[5] And now, what am I doing here,” says the LORD,
“since my people have been taken away for nothing?
Those who rule over them mock,” says the LORD,
“and my name is insulted constantly, all day long.
[6] Therefore my people will know my name.
Therefore they will know on that day
that I am the one who speaks.
Look, here I am.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: As in chapter 51:17, it says again: “Wake up!” But now Jerusalem is not to stagger anymore, but to put on beautiful clothes, shake off the dust and loosen the chains. The time of mourning is over.
> “The uncircumcised and the unclean” here means the foreign conquerors who had destroyed and dishonored the holy city. This is not about shutting out people of other nations in general. In chapter 56, God expressly invites foreigners into his house.
> Verse 3: Israel was “sold for nothing”: The conquerors paid God nothing for it. That is why God does not have to pay a ransom to bring his people back either.
> Verses 4–5: The people were already oppressed in Egypt and under Assyria. Now again in Babylon. God cannot watch this any longer.

---

## Your God reigns! (Verses 7–12)

[7] How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of the one who brings good news,
who announces peace,
who brings good news,
who announces salvation,
who says to Zion, “Your God reigns!”
[8] Your watchmen lift up their voices.
Together they shout for joy.
For they see with their own eyes
the LORD returning to Zion.
[9] Break out into joy!
Sing together, you ruins of Jerusalem!
For the LORD has comforted his people.
He has redeemed Jerusalem.
[10] The LORD has bared his holy arm
in the sight of all the nations.
All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.
[11] Leave! Leave! Go out from there!
Touch nothing unclean!
Go out from the middle of her!
Make yourselves clean, you who carry the LORD’s vessels!
[12] For you will not go out in a hurry,
and you will not leave in flight.
For the LORD will go before you,
and the God of Israel will be your rear guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: One of the most beautiful verses in the Bible. A messenger runs over the mountains to Jerusalem and brings the good news: Peace! Rescue! “Your God reigns!” People are already glad at the sight of his feet when they see him coming from far away. In the Greek New Testament, the word for “good news” is “gospel.” Paul quotes this verse (Romans 10:15).
> Verse 8: The watchmen on the city wall see with their own eyes how God himself returns home.
> Verse 10: God “has bared his holy arm.” That means: He rolls up his sleeves and shows his power before all nations.
> Verses 11–12: Those returning home are to carry the holy vessels of the temple back to Jerusalem (compare Ezra 1:7–11). Unlike the exodus from Egypt, they do not have to flee in a hurry. God goes before them and also protects them from behind.

## The fourth song of God’s servant begins (Verses 13–15)

[13] Look, my servant will act wisely.
He will be raised and lifted up
and will be very high.
[14] Just as many were horrified at you
(his appearance was so disfigured, more than any man’s,
and his form more than that of any human being),
[15] so he will cleanse many nations.
Kings will shut their mouths because of him.
For they will see what they had not been told,
and they will understand what they had not heard.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins the fourth and best-known song of God’s servant. It goes to the end of chapter 53.
> Verse 13: The end comes right at the beginning: In the end, the servant will be lifted up very high.
> Verse 14: But first he is so disfigured that people are horrified by him. He hardly looks human anymore.
> Verse 15: And then even kings are amazed. They understand something completely new that no one had told them. The WEB translates “he will cleanse”; other translations have “he will startle” or “sprinkle.” The Hebrew word is not completely clear.
