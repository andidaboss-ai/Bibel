# Isaiah – Chapter 63
### You, LORD, are our Father

---

## The one who treads the winepress, coming from Edom (Verses 1–6)

[1] Who is this who comes from Edom,
with garments dyed red from Bozrah?
Who is this, splendid in his clothing,
marching in the greatness of his strength?
“It is I, who speak in righteousness,
mighty to save.”
[2] Why are your clothes red,
and your garments like those of someone who treads the winepress?
[3] “I have trodden the winepress alone.
Of the peoples, no one was with me.
Yes, I trod them in my anger
and trampled them in my fury.
Their lifeblood has splashed on my garments,
and I have stained all my clothing.
[4] For the day of vengeance was in my heart,
and the year of my redeemed has come.
[5] I looked, and there was no one to help.
And I was amazed that there was no one to support me.
So my own arm brought me salvation,
and my fury held me up.
[6] I trampled the peoples in my anger
and made them drunk in my fury.
I poured out their lifeblood on the earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A frightening picture: God comes from Edom (a neighboring people who were often hostile to Israel) like a winegrower treading grapes in the winepress. His clothes are red, not from grape juice, but from the blood of the enemies.
> The picture describes God’s judgment on the powers that oppressed Israel. God acts alone because no human being stepped in (verse 5, as in chapter 59:16).
> Important: Here God acts, not people. The text gives no one the right to use violence or take revenge in God’s name. Judgment and payback are God’s business alone (compare Romans 12:19). At the center stands this sentence: “The year of my redeemed has come” (63:4). It is about setting the oppressed free.
> The book of Revelation takes up this picture (19:13–15). The song *Mine eyes have seen the glory* (the “Battle Hymn of the Republic”) also alludes to it.

## God’s goodness in history (Verses 7–14)

[7] I will tell of the loving kindnesses of the LORD,
the praises of the LORD,
for all that the LORD has done for us,
and the great goodness toward the house of Israel,
which he has shown them in his compassion
and in his many acts of loving kindness.
[8] For he said, “Surely they are my people,
children who will not be false.”
So he became their Savior.
[9] In all their suffering he suffered too,
and the angel of his presence saved them.
In his love and in his pity he redeemed them.
He lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.
[10] But they rebelled
and grieved his Holy Spirit.
So he turned and became their enemy,
and he himself fought against them.
[11] Then he remembered the days of old,
Moses and his people, and said:
“Where is the one who brought them up out of the sea
with the shepherds of his flock?
Where is the one who put his Holy Spirit among them?”
[12] Who made his glorious arm go at the right hand of Moses?
Who divided the waters before them
to make an everlasting name for himself?
[13] Who led them through the depths,
like a horse in the open country,
so that they did not stumble?
[14] Like cattle going down into the valley,
the Spirit of the LORD gave them rest.
That is how you led your people,
to make a glorious name for yourself.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins a long prayer of the people. It goes to the end of chapter 64.
> Verses 7–9: First the people remember God’s goodness. Verse 9 is especially beautiful: “In all their suffering he suffered too.” God suffers with his people. He is not far away, but feels the pain with them.
> Verse 10: But the people have “grieved” God’s Holy Spirit, that is, made him sad. Paul takes up these words (Ephesians 4:30).
> Verses 11–14: The people remember the exodus from Egypt: God led them safely through the sea like horses through flat land, and his Spirit gave them rest.

## Look down from heaven! (Verses 15–19)

[15] Look down from heaven and see,
from your holy and glorious home!
Where are your zeal and your mighty acts?
The longing of your heart and your compassion
are held back from me.
[16] For you are our Father,
even though Abraham does not know us
and Israel does not recognize us.
You, LORD, are our Father.
“Our Redeemer from everlasting” is your name.
[17] LORD, why do you let us wander from your ways
and harden our hearts so that we do not fear you?
Return for the sake of your servants,
the tribes that are your inheritance.
[18] Your holy people possessed it only for a little while.
Our enemies have trampled down your sanctuary.
[19] We have become like those you never ruled over,
like those who were not called by your name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The people cry out: God, look down! Where has your love gone?
> Verse 16: “You, LORD, are our Father.” Even if Abraham and Jacob, the great fathers of the people, no longer recognized them, God remains their Father. This is one of the few places in the Old Testament where God is called “our Father” directly in prayer. Jesus teaches his disciples to pray: “Our Father in heaven” (Matthew 6:9).
> Verse 17: An honest, desperate question: Why do you let us go astray? The Bible allows such questions to God.
> Verses 18–19: The temple is destroyed. The people feel as if they had never belonged to God.
