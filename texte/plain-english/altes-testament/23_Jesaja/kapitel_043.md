# Isaiah – Chapter 43
### I have called you by your name

---

## You are mine (Verses 1–7)

[1] But now this is what the LORD says, who created you, Jacob,
and who formed you, Israel:
“Do not be afraid, for I have redeemed you.
I have called you by your name.
You are mine.
[2] When you pass through the waters, I will be with you,
and through the rivers, they will not sweep over you.
When you walk through the fire, you will not be burned,
and the flame will not scorch you.
[3] For I am the LORD your God,
the Holy One of Israel, your Savior.
I have given Egypt as your ransom,
Ethiopia and Seba in your place.
[4] Because you are precious and honored in my eyes,
and I love you,
I will give people in your place
and nations in exchange for your life.
[5] Do not be afraid, for I am with you.
I will bring your descendants from the east
and gather you from the west.
[6] I will say to the north, ‘Give them up!’
and to the south, ‘Do not hold them back!
Bring my sons from far away
and my daughters from the ends of the earth,
[7] everyone who is called by my name,
whom I have created for my glory,
whom I have formed, yes, whom I have made.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 is one of the best-known verses in the Bible: “Do not be afraid, for I have redeemed you. I have called you by your name. You are mine.” Many people are given it as a Bible verse to take with them at their baptism or confirmation. God knows every person by name.
> Verse 2: God does not promise that there will be no dangers, no water and no fire. But he promises: I am with you, right in the middle of it.
> Verses 3–4: God says to his people: You are so precious to me that I would give up whole countries for you. This is the language of love. The WEB, our English source text, has “Ethiopia” here. It means Cush, the land south of Egypt. Later, the Persians did in fact conquer Egypt after they had set Israel free. This is not about other nations being worth less, but about how valuable Israel is to God.
> Verses 5–7: God will bring his children home from every direction, expressly sons and daughters.

## You are my witnesses (Verses 8–13)

[8] Bring out the people who are blind, even though they have eyes,
and the deaf, even though they have ears.
[9] Let all the nations gather together,
and let the peoples assemble.
Who among them can announce this
and show us the former things?
Let them bring their witnesses to prove they are right,
or let them listen and say, “That is true.”
[10] “You are my witnesses,” says the LORD,
“together with my servant whom I have chosen,
so that you may know and believe me
and understand that I am he.
Before me no god was formed,
and there will be none after me.
[11] I, I am the LORD.
Besides me there is no savior.
[12] I have announced, I have saved, and I have shown it,
and there was no foreign god among you.
So you are my witnesses,” says the LORD,
“and I am God.
[13] Yes, since the first day, I am he.
There is no one who can rescue from my hand.
I act, and who can stop it?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Another court hearing. The nations are to bring witnesses for their gods. God also has witnesses: his people Israel. Even though they are “blind” and “deaf” (verse 8), they can bear witness to what God has done.
> Verses 10–11: “Before me no god was formed, and there will be none after me. … Besides me there is no savior.” Here it is said very clearly: There is only one single God.

## I am doing a new thing (Verses 14–21)

[14] The LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel, says:
“For your sake I have sent to Babylon,
and I will bring all of them down as refugees,
even the Chaldeans, in the ships they were so proud of.
[15] I am the LORD, your Holy One,
the Creator of Israel, your King.”
[16] The LORD, who makes a way in the sea
and a path through the mighty waters,
[17] who brings out chariot and horse,
the army and the warrior
(they lie down together, they will not rise again;
they are snuffed out, put out like a wick), says:
[18] “Do not remember the former things,
and do not keep thinking about the things of the past.
[19] Look, I am doing a new thing.
Now it springs up. Don’t you see it?
I will make a way in the wilderness
and rivers in the desert.
[20] The animals of the field will honor me,
the jackals and the ostriches,
because I give water in the wilderness
and rivers in the desert,
to give drink to my people, my chosen ones,
[21] the people I formed for myself,
so that they may announce my praise.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Babylon will fall. The Chaldeans are the Babylonians. The ships in which they used to sail proudly and happily on the Euphrates become boats for running away.
> Verses 16–17: This recalls the exodus from Egypt: God made a way through the sea, and Pharaoh’s chariots sank.
> Verses 18–19: And yet God says: Do not only think of the old things! “Look, I am doing a new thing.” The return home from Babylon will be a new exodus, with a road through the desert. These verses still give courage today: God can open a new way even in a hopeless situation.

## I blot out your sins (Verses 22–28)

[22] But you have not called on me, Jacob;
instead, you have grown tired of me, Israel.
[23] You have not brought me your sheep for burnt offerings,
and you have not honored me with your sacrifices.
I have not burdened you with offerings
or worn you out with incense.
[24] You have not bought me sweet cane with money,
and you have not filled me with the fat of your sacrifices,
but you have burdened me with your sins.
You have worn me out with your wrongdoings.
[25] I, I am the one who blots out your rebellious acts for my own sake,
and I will not remember your sins.
[26] Remind me! Let us argue the case together!
Present your case, so that you may be proved right.
[27] Your first father sinned,
and your teachers have rebelled against me.
[28] So I will dishonor the princes of the sanctuary,
and I will hand Jacob over to the curse and Israel to scorn.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–24: God complains: You have not honestly sought me. You have not worn me out with sacrifices, but with your sins. God does not ask for much at all, but the people have loaded burdens on him.
> Verse 25: Even so, this is the heart of it: God himself blots out the guilt, “for my own sake.” Not because the people deserved it, but because that is what God is like. And he no longer wants to remember the sins.
> Verse 27: The “first father” probably means Jacob. There was guilt right from the beginning.
> Verse 28: That is why the exile came: The temple was dishonored, the people mocked. But verse 25 is stronger: Forgiveness has the last word.
