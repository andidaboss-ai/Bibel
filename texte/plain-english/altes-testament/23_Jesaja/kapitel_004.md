# Isaiah – Chapter 4
### The Branch of the LORD

---

## Seven women and one man (Verse 1)

[1] On that day, seven women will take hold of one man and say,
“We will eat our own bread
and wear our own clothes.
Just let us be called by your name.
Take away our shame!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This verse still belongs to the end of chapter 3. After the war, so many men have been killed that seven women ask for one man.
> Back then, it was considered a shame for a woman to stay unmarried and without children. Without a husband, women were often unprotected and without rights. This shows how cruel war is and how hard the situation of women was back then.
> Today we know: A woman’s worth does not depend on whether she is married.

---

## New life after judgment (Verses 2–6)

[2] On that day, the Branch of the LORD will be beautiful and glorious,
and the fruit of the land will be the pride and the glory
of the survivors of Israel.
[3] It will happen:
Whoever is left in Zion and whoever remains in Jerusalem
will be called holy,
everyone who is written down among the living in Jerusalem,
[4] when the Lord has washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion
and has cleaned the bloodshed of Jerusalem from its midst
by the spirit of judgment and by the spirit of burning.
[5] Over the whole site of Mount Zion
and over its gatherings,
the LORD will create a cloud and smoke by day
and the shining of a flaming fire by night,
for over all the glory there will be a canopy.
[6] There will be a shelter for shade in the daytime from the heat,
and a refuge and protection from storm and rain.

> **What does this mean?**
> After judgment comes hope. God makes a new beginning.
> Verse 2: “The Branch of the LORD”: a young shoot growing out of the ground. A picture of new life. Later, the “Branch” becomes a name for the coming king from David’s family (Jeremiah 23:5; Zechariah 3:8). Christians see in it a pointer to Christ.
> Verse 3: Those who are left will be called “holy.” They are written in God’s “book of life.”
> Verse 5: A cloud by day and fire by night remind us of the exodus from Egypt, when God led his people through the wilderness this way (Exodus 13:21). God will protect Jerusalem like that again.
> Verse 6: God is like a canopy against heat, storm and rain.
