# Isaiah – Chapter 47
### A song of mourning over Babylon

---

## Come down, daughter of Babylon (Verses 1–7)

[1] “Come down and sit in the dust,
virgin daughter of Babylon!
Sit on the ground without a throne,
daughter of the Chaldeans!
For you will no longer be called tender and delicate.
[2] Take the millstones and grind flour!
Take off your veil, lift up your skirt,
uncover your legs and wade through the rivers!
[3] Your nakedness will be uncovered.
Yes, your shame will be seen.
I will take vengeance and will spare no one.”
[4] Our Redeemer, the LORD of Armies is his name,
is the Holy One of Israel.
[5] “Sit in silence and go into the darkness,
daughter of the Chaldeans!
For you will no longer be called the mistress of kingdoms.
[6] I was angry with my people.
I dishonored my inheritance
and handed them over to you.
You showed them no mercy.
You laid a very heavy yoke on the old.
[7] You said, ‘I will be a princess forever,’
so you did not take these things to heart
and did not think about the consequences.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is a mocking song about the city of Babylon. The city is pictured as a noble young woman, the “daughter of Babylon.” People often spoke of cities in this way at that time.
> Verses 1–3: The proud mistress has to come down from the throne and becomes a slave: She has to grind flour and wade through rivers without a veil. That is what happened to prisoners in those days. The pictures of being exposed describe how the mighty city is humiliated. They are a picture of a city and its power. They never justify exposing women, humiliating them or doing violence to them. For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233.
> Verse 3: “Vengeance” here means that God restores justice. Babylon had treated many nations cruelly.
> Verse 6: God handed his people over to Babylon as a punishment. But Babylon went far beyond every limit: It had no mercy, not even on the old. For this it will be held responsible.

## “I am, and there is no one else”? (Verses 8–15)

[8] But now hear this, you lover of pleasure,
who sit there without a care,
who say in your heart,
‘I am, and there is no one else besides me.
I will not sit as a widow,
and I will not know the loss of children.’
[9] But both these things will come upon you
in a moment, in one day:
the loss of children and widowhood.
They will come upon you in full measure,
in spite of your many sorceries
and the great number of your spells.
[10] For you have trusted in your wickedness.
You have said, ‘No one sees me.’
Your wisdom and your knowledge have led you astray.
You have said in your heart,
‘I am, and there is no one else besides me.’
[11] So disaster will come upon you.
You will not know when it dawns.
Ruin will fall on you.
You will not be able to turn it away.
Destruction will come on you suddenly,
which you do not understand.
[12] Stand up now with your spells
and with your many sorceries,
which you have worked hard at since your youth,
as if you might gain something from them,
as if you might win.
[13] You are worn out by all your many plans.
Now let the astrologers stand up,
the stargazers and those who make predictions every month,
and save you from what is coming upon you.
[14] Look, they are like stubble.
The fire will burn them up.
They will not save themselves from the power of the flame.
It will not be a coal to warm yourself at
or a fire to sit by.
[15] This is how it will be with all you worked so hard for:
Those who have traded with you since your youth
will each wander off on their own way.
There will be no one to save you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8 and 10: Babylon says: “I am, and there is no one else besides me.” That is exactly what God says about himself (Isaiah 45:5–6). Babylon has put itself in God’s place. That is the deepest reason for its fall.
> Verse 9: To become a widow and childless was the worst misfortune for a woman in those days. For the city it means: It loses its king and its inhabitants.
> Verses 12–13: Babylon was famous for astrology and spells. Isaiah mocks: Let the astrologers save you now!
> Verse 14: Their wisdom burns like straw. There is not even a cozy fire left to warm yourself.
> For us today: No person, no company and no state should think: “I am the greatest, and no one sees what I do.” Power without responsibility leads to ruin. In the book of Revelation (chapter 18), “Babylon” becomes a picture of every godless, arrogant power.
