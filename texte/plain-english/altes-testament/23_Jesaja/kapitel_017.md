# Isaiah – Chapter 17
### Damascus and the raging nations

---

## Damascus will become a heap of ruins (Verses 1–6)

[1] The burden about Damascus.
“Look, Damascus will stop being a city
and will become a heap of ruins.
[2] The cities of Aroer are abandoned.
They will belong to the flocks,
which will lie down there, and no one will scare them away.
[3] The fortress will disappear from Ephraim,
and the kingdom from Damascus,
and the rest of Syria.
They will be like the glory of the people of Israel,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[4] “On that day it will happen
that the glory of Jacob will grow thin,
and the fat of his body will grow lean.
[5] It will be like when a harvester gathers the wheat
and cuts the heads of grain with his arm.
Yes, it will be like when someone gathers leftover grain in the Valley of Rephaim.
[6] Yet some leftovers will remain there,
like when an olive tree is beaten:
two or three olives at the very top,
four or five on the outermost branches of the fruitful tree,”
says the LORD, the God of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Damascus was the capital of Syria. Together with the northern kingdom of Israel (Ephraim), it had attacked Jerusalem (chapter 7). Now Isaiah announces: Both will fall. That happened when Assyria conquered Damascus in the year 732 and Samaria in the year 722 BC.
> Verses 5–6: A picture from the harvest: After the harvest, only a few heads of grain are left in the field and a few olives at the very top of the tree. In the same way, only a small remnant will be left. But a remnant will be left.

---

## They will look to their Maker (Verses 7–11)

[7] On that day, people will look to their Maker,
and their eyes will look to the Holy One of Israel.
[8] They will not look to the altars, the work of their hands,
and they will not pay attention to what their fingers have made,
neither the Asherah poles nor the incense altars.
[9] On that day, their strong cities will be
like the abandoned places in the forest and on the mountaintop,
which were abandoned before the people of Israel,
and it will be a wasteland.
[10] For you have forgotten the God of your salvation
and have not remembered the rock of your strength.
So you plant pleasant plants
and set out foreign seedlings.
[11] On the day you plant, you fence them in.
In the morning you make your seed blossom,
but the harvest flees away on the day of grief
and of hopeless sorrow.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: In their distress, people will turn back to their Maker instead of to idols they made themselves. Asherah was a goddess whose sign was a wooden pole.
> Verses 10–11: “Pleasant plants” and “foreign seedlings”: This probably means small gardens for foreign fertility gods. They grow quickly, but bring no harvest. Whoever forgets God sows on sand.

---

## The roaring of the nations (Verses 12–14)

[12] Oh, the roaring of many peoples,
who roar like the roaring of the seas,
and the rushing of nations,
who rush like the rushing of mighty waters!
[13] The nations rush like the rushing of many waters,
but he will rebuke them, and they will flee far away
and be chased like chaff on the mountains before the wind
and like whirling dust before the storm.
[14] In the evening, look, terror!
Before morning, they are gone.
This is the share of those who plunder us,
and the lot of those who rob us.

> **What does this mean?**
> The enemy peoples rage against Jerusalem like a wild sea. But one word from God is enough, and they blow away like chaff in the wind.
> Verse 14: Fear in the evening, and in the morning the enemy has disappeared. That is exactly what happened later, when the Assyrian army in front of Jerusalem was gone overnight (chapter 37:36).
