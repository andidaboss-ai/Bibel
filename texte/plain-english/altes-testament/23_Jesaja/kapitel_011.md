# Isaiah – Chapter 11
### The shoot from the stump of Jesse

---

## A new king (Verses 1–5)

[1] A shoot will come out of the stump of Jesse,
and a branch from his roots will bear fruit.
[2] The Spirit of the LORD will rest on him:
the spirit of wisdom and understanding,
the spirit of counsel and strength,
the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD.
[3] His delight will be in the fear of the LORD.
He will not judge by what his eyes see,
and he will not decide by what his ears hear,
[4] but he will judge the poor with righteousness
and decide fairly for the humble of the earth.
He will strike the earth with the rod of his mouth,
and with the breath of his lips he will kill the wicked.
[5] Righteousness will be the belt around his waist,
and faithfulness the belt around his hips.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Jesse was the father of King David. David’s royal family is like a tree that has been cut down; only a stump is left. But out of this stump, a new, fresh shoot grows.
> A well-known German Christmas carol (in English: “Lo, How a Rose E’er Blooming”) refers to this verse. It sings of a tender root that came from Jesse.
> Verse 2: God’s Spirit rests on this new king with all good gifts: wisdom, understanding, counsel, strength, knowledge, awe. In Christian tradition, people speak of the “gifts of the Holy Spirit.”
> Verses 3–4: He does not judge by outward appearance, but fairly. Above all, he gives justice to the poor and humble.
> “With the rod of his mouth”: He needs no weapons. His word alone is enough to defeat evil.
> Jews expect this king as the Messiah. Christians see Jesus as this shoot from David’s family.

---

## The wolf will live with the lamb (Verses 6–9)

[6] The wolf will live with the lamb,
and the leopard will lie down with the young goat,
the calf and the young lion and the fattened calf together,
and a little child will lead them.
[7] The cow and the bear will graze together.
Their young will lie down together.
The lion will eat straw like the ox.
[8] The nursing baby will play near the hole of the cobra,
and the weaned child will put his hand
into the den of the viper.
[9] They will not hurt or destroy
in all my holy mountain,
for the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD,
as the waters cover the sea.

> **What does this mean?**
> One of the most beautiful pictures in the Bible: paradise on earth. Predators and their prey live together in peace. A little child can play with snakes without fear.
> The hostility between people and the snake that has existed since the fall into sin (Genesis 3:15) is over.
> Verse 9: The reason for this peace: All people know God. Knowing him fills the earth the way water fills the sea.
> This picture stands for a world without violence, without fear, without war. It is a hope that can already encourage us today to stand up for peace.

## God gathers his people (Verses 10–16)

[10] On that day it will happen
that the nations will seek the root of Jesse,
who stands as a banner for the peoples,
and his resting place will be glorious.
[11] On that day it will happen
that the Lord will reach out his hand a second time
to bring back the remnant of his people that is left,
from Assyria, from Egypt, from Pathros, from Cush,
from Elam, from Shinar, from Hamath, and from the islands of the sea.
[12] He will raise a banner for the nations
and gather the outcasts of Israel,
and bring together the scattered people of Judah
from the four corners of the earth.
[13] The jealousy of Ephraim will also go away,
and the enemies of Judah will be cut off.
Ephraim will not be jealous of Judah,
and Judah will not harass Ephraim.
[14] They will swoop down on the shoulders of the Philistines in the west.
Together they will plunder the people of the east.
They will extend their power over Edom and Moab,
and the Ammonites will obey them.
[15] The LORD will completely dry up the tongue of the Egyptian sea.
With his scorching wind, he will wave his hand over the River
and split it into seven streams,
so that people can walk across in sandals.
[16] There will be a highway for the remnant of his people
that is left from Assyria,
as there was for Israel on the day
when it came up out of the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The new king from the “root of Jesse” will be like a flag that draws all peoples. Paul quotes this verse: The nations too will hope in him (Romans 15:12).
> Verses 11–12: God will bring his scattered people back from all countries, “from the four corners of the earth.” Pathros is Upper Egypt, Cush is today’s Sudan, Elam lies in today’s Iran, Shinar is Babylonia.
> Verse 13: The divided people, the northern kingdom (Ephraim) and the southern kingdom (Judah), will be reconciled again.
> Verse 14: Here the language of that time speaks of victory over the neighboring peoples. That is no permission for conquests today. In the context of the chapter, it is about God’s kingdom of peace (Verses 6–9).
> Verses 15–16: As back then at the exodus from Egypt, God will make a way, a new exodus.
