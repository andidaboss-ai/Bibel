# Isaiah – Chapter 48
### Leave Babylon!

---

## You are stubborn (Verses 1–11)

[1] “Hear this, house of Jacob,
you who are called by the name of Israel
and have come from the waters of Judah.
You swear by the name of the LORD
and speak of the God of Israel,
but not in truth and not in righteousness.
[2] For they call themselves citizens of the holy city
and rely on the God of Israel.
The LORD of Armies is his name.
[3] I announced the former things long ago.
Yes, they went out from my mouth, and I made them known.
Suddenly I did them, and they happened.
[4] Because I knew that you are stubborn,
and your neck is an iron sinew,
and your forehead is bronze,
[5] I announced it to you long ago.
Before it happened, I showed it to you,
so that you would not say, ‘My idol did it.
My carved idol and my cast idol commanded it.’
[6] You have heard it. Now look at all of this.
And you, won’t you announce it?
From now on I am showing you new things,
hidden things that you have not known.
[7] They are created now, and not long ago.
Before today you did not hear of them,
so that you cannot say, ‘Look, I already knew them.’
[8] Yes, you did not hear.
Yes, you did not know.
Yes, from long ago your ear was not open,
for I knew that you act very unfaithfully
and were called a rebel from the womb.
[9] For the sake of my name I hold back my anger,
and for the sake of my praise I restrain it for you,
so that I do not cut you off.
[10] Look, I have refined you, but not like silver.
I have chosen you in the furnace of affliction.
[11] For my own sake, for my own sake I will do it.
For how could my name be dishonored?
I will not give my glory to anyone else.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: God speaks honestly with his people: You call yourselves devout and talk about God, but your life does not match it.
> Verses 4–5: Israel is stubborn. God says: I knew “that you are stubborn, and your neck is an iron sinew, and your forehead is bronze.” That is why God announced the events long in advance. So no one could say: “My idol did it.”
> Verses 6–7: But now God is doing something completely new, the liberation from Babylon. No one could have known it beforehand.
> Verse 10: The exile was like a furnace. In a furnace, metal is cleaned of slag. The suffering was hard, but God did not abandon his people in it: “I have chosen you in the furnace of affliction.”
> Verses 9 and 11: God does not save his people because they are so good, but “for my own sake,” because he is true to himself and to his word.

---

## I am the first and the last (Verses 12–16)

[12] Listen to me, Jacob,
and Israel, whom I have called:
I am he. I am the first.
I am also the last.
[13] Yes, my hand laid the foundation of the earth,
and my right hand spread out the heavens.
When I call to them, they all stand up together.
[14] Gather together, all of you, and listen!
Who among them has announced these things?
The one the LORD loves will do what he wants with Babylon,
and his arm will be against the Chaldeans.
[15] I, I have spoken.
Yes, I have called him.
I have brought him, and he will succeed in his way.
[16] Come near to me and hear this:
From the beginning I have not spoken in secret.
From the time it happened, I was there.”
And now the Lord GOD has sent me with his Spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: “The one the LORD loves” is Cyrus again. God has called him to conquer Babylon.
> Verse 16: At the end, suddenly an “I” speaks who is not God himself: “The Lord GOD has sent me with his Spirit.” This is probably the prophet himself, speaking here about his task. Christian interpreters have also seen in this verse a hint of the Father, the Son and the Spirit.

---

## If only you had listened to me! (Verses 17–22)

[17] This is what the LORD says, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel:
“I am the LORD your God,
who teaches you what is good for you,
who leads you on the way you should go.
[18] Oh, if only you had paid attention to my commandments!
Then your peace would have been like a river
and your righteousness like the waves of the sea.
[19] Your descendants would also have been like the sand
and your children like its grains.
Their name would never be cut off or destroyed before me.”
[20] Leave Babylon!
Flee from the Chaldeans!
Announce it with shouts of joy,
make it heard to the end of the earth!
Say, “The LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob!”
[21] They did not go thirsty when he led them through the deserts.
He made water flow from the rock for them.
He split the rock, and the water gushed out.
[22] “There is no peace,” says the LORD, “for the wicked.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A sad and at the same time tender sentence from God: “Oh, if only you had paid attention to my commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river.” God wants good things for his people. His commandments are meant to help them live.
> Verses 20–21: The call to set out: “Leave Babylon!” As at the exodus from Egypt long ago, God will lead his people through the desert and give them water from the rock.
> Verse 22: This sentence closes the first part of the Book of Comfort (it comes again in chapter 57:21). Whoever sets themselves against God finds no inner peace.
