# Isaiah – Chapter 21
### Watchman, what of the night?

---

## Babylon has fallen (Verses 1–10)

[1] The burden about the wilderness of the sea.
As whirlwinds sweep through the South,
so it comes from the wilderness, from a terrible land.
[2] A harsh vision has been shown to me.
The traitor betrays, and the destroyer destroys.
Go up, Elam! Attack!
I have put an end to all the sighing of Media.
[3] So my body is filled with anguish.
Pains have seized me like the pains of a woman in labor.
I am in so much pain that I cannot hear.
I am so shaken that I cannot see.
[4] My heart flutters.
Horror has terrified me.
The twilight I longed for
has turned into trembling for me.
[5] They set the table.
They post the guard.
They eat. They drink.
Get up, you princes, oil the shield!
[6] For the Lord said to me,
“Go, post a watchman.
Let him report what he sees.
[7] When he sees a troop, horsemen in pairs,
a troop of donkeys, a troop of camels,
he must listen carefully, with great attention.”
[8] He cried out like a lion,
“Lord, I stand on the watchtower all day long,
and every night I stay at my post.
[9] Look, here comes a troop of men, horsemen in pairs.”
He answered,
“Fallen, fallen is Babylon,
and all the carved images of her gods are smashed to the ground.
[10] You, my people who were threshed, the grain of my threshing floor!”
What I have heard from the LORD of Armies,
the God of Israel, I have announced to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The wilderness of the sea” means Babylon, which lay on a plain by the Euphrates.
> Verse 2: Elam and Media (in what is today Iran) attack Babylon.
> Verses 3–4: The vision is so terrible that the prophet suffers in his body.
> Verse 5: While the enemy is coming, the princes of Babylon are still celebrating a feast. Daniel 5 tells of such a feast in the night when Babylon fell.
> Verses 6–9: A watchman stands on the tower, day and night. Finally he sees the messengers and cries out: “Fallen, fallen is Babylon!” This cry is taken up again in the Revelation to John (Revelation 14:8; 18:2).
> Verse 10: God comforts his people, who were “threshed” like grain: Your oppressors have fallen.

## Watchman, what of the night? (Verses 11–12)

[11] The burden about Dumah.
Someone calls to me from Seir,
“Watchman, what of the night?
Watchman, what of the night?”
[12] The watchman said,
“The morning is coming, but also the night.
If you want to ask, then ask.
Come back again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A short, mysterious word. Dumah and Seir lie in Edom, south of the Dead Sea.
> Someone calls out in despair in the night: Watchman, how much longer will the night last? How much longer will this hard time last?
> The answer is honest: The morning is coming, but then another night will come too. Good and hard times take turns. Keep asking, come back!
> Many people have asked this question in dark times. To this day, it is a picture of waiting for hope.

## About Arabia (Verses 13–17)

[13] The burden about Arabia.
You will spend the night in the thickets in Arabia,
you caravans of the Dedanites.
[14] They brought water to the thirsty.
The people of the land of Tema met the refugees with bread.
[15] For they have fled from the swords,
from the drawn sword, from the bent bow,
and from the fury of battle.
[16] For this is what the Lord said to me:
“Within a year, as a worker under contract would count it,
all the glory of Kedar will come to an end,
[17] and the rest of the number of archers,
the mighty men of the people of Kedar, will be few,
for the LORD, the God of Israel, has spoken it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Arab tribes (Dedan, Tema, Kedar) are struck by war. The caravans have to hide in the bushes.
> Verse 14: A beautiful detail: The people of Tema bring water and bread to the refugees. Hospitality toward refugees is a sign of humanity that the Bible praises.
> Verse 16: Within a year, Kedar’s power will be broken.
