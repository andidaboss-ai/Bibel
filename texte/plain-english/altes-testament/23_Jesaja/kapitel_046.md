# Isaiah – Chapter 46
### I will carry you into old age

---

## The gods of Babylon have to be carried (Verses 1–7)

[1] Bel bows down. Nebo stoops.
Their idols are loaded on animals and on livestock.
The things you carried around are heavy loads,
a burden for the weary animal.
[2] They stoop and bow down together.
They could not rescue the burden,
but they themselves have gone into captivity.
[3] “Listen to me, house of Jacob,
and all the remnant of the house of Israel,
you who have been carried since your birth,
you who have been carried from the womb.
[4] Even into your old age I am he,
and even when your hair is gray I will carry you.
I have made you, and I will bear you.
Yes, I will carry you and will rescue you.
[5] To whom will you compare me and make me equal,
and measure me, as if we were alike?
[6] Some pour out gold from the bag
and weigh silver on the scales.
They hire a goldsmith, and he makes it into a god.
They fall down, yes, they worship.
[7] They lift it onto their shoulder.
They carry it and set it in its place, and there it stands.
It cannot move from its place.
Yes, someone may cry out to it, but it cannot answer.
It cannot save anyone from their trouble.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Bel (another name for Marduk) was the chief god of Babylon, Nebo (Nabu) his son. Their statues were carried around in processions at great festivals. Isaiah sees a different scene: The idols lie as a heavy load on exhausted pack animals, in flight or as plunder. The gods cannot even save themselves.
> Verses 3–4: The contrast: The idols have to be carried by people. But Israel’s God carries his people, from birth into old age, “even when your hair is gray.” A wonderful comfort for older people: God still carries you even when your strength fades.
> Verse 7: An idol just stands there. People cry out to it, but it does not answer.

---

## My plan will stand (Verses 8–13)

[8] Remember this and show yourselves to be men!
Take it to heart again, you rebels.
[9] Remember the former things of long ago,
for I am God, and there is no other.
I am God, and there is no one like me.
[10] I announce the end from the beginning,
and from ancient times what has not yet been done.
I say: My plan will stand,
and I will do everything I please.
[11] I call a bird of prey from the east,
the man of my plan from a faraway land.
Yes, I have spoken, and I will make it happen.
I have planned it, and I will do it.
[12] Listen to me, you stubborn-hearted,
who are far from righteousness!
[13] I bring my righteousness near.
It is not far away, and my salvation will not be delayed.
I will give salvation to Zion,
my glory to Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: God knows the end from the beginning. He has a plan for history, and this plan will succeed.
> Verse 11: The “bird of prey from the east” is Cyrus again. He swoops down on his prey quickly, like an eagle.
> Verses 12–13: The message also goes out to those who do not want to believe: God’s salvation is very near. It will not come at some point far away, but soon.
