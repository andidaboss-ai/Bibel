# Isaiah – Chapter 18
### A word to Cush

---

## The land of whirring wings (Verses 1–7)

[1] Oh, land of whirring wings,
which lies beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,
[2] which sends messengers by the sea,
in boats of papyrus on the waters, and says,
“Go, you swift messengers,
to a people tall and smooth-skinned,
to a people feared from their beginning on,
a nation that measures out and tramples down,
whose land the rivers divide!”
[3] All you people of the world, you who live on the earth:
When a banner is raised on the mountains, look!
When the trumpet is blown, listen!
[4] For this is what the LORD said to me:
“I will be still and watch from my dwelling place,
like shimmering heat in the sunshine,
like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest.”
[5] For before the harvest, when the blossom is over
and the flower becomes a ripening grape,
he will cut off the shoots with pruning knives
and cut down and take away the spreading branches.
[6] They will all be left to the birds of prey of the mountains
and to the animals of the earth.
The birds of prey will feed on them in the summer,
and all the animals of the earth in the winter.
[7] At that time, a gift will be brought to the LORD of Armies
from a people tall and smooth-skinned,
from a people feared from their beginning on,
a nation that measures out and tramples down,
whose land the rivers divide,
to the place of the name of the LORD of Armies, Mount Zion.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cush lay south of Egypt, in what is today Sudan. The WEB, our English source text, says “Ethiopia” here. At that time, kings from Cush also ruled over Egypt.
> “The land of whirring wings”: Perhaps this means the many insects along the Nile. The people there were tall, proud and famous for their strength.
> Verse 2: The people of Cush send messengers in light papyrus boats to form an alliance against Assyria. But Isaiah says: Don’t rely on alliances.
> Verse 4: At first God watches calmly, like the sun in the midday heat. He has time. But at the right time he steps in, like a vinedresser who prunes the vines (verse 5).
> Verse 7: In the end, even this distant, proud people comes to Jerusalem and brings gifts to God. All peoples will come to God. Acts 8:26–39 tells how a man from Cush (Ethiopia) goes on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem and is baptized.
