# Isaiah – Chapter 39
### The envoys from Babylon

---

## Hezekiah shows everything (Verses 1–8)

[1] At that time, Merodach-baladan the son of Baladan, the king of Babylon,
sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah,
for he had heard that Hezekiah had been ill and had recovered.
[2] Hezekiah was pleased with them
and showed them his treasure house:
the silver, the gold, the spices and the precious oil,
his whole armory,
and everything that was found among his treasures.
There was nothing in his house or in his whole kingdom
that Hezekiah did not show them.
[3] Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and asked him,
“What did these men say?
Where did they come to you from?”
Hezekiah said,
“They have come to me from a faraway country, from Babylon.”
[4] Then he asked,
“What have they seen in your house?”
Hezekiah answered,
“They have seen everything in my house.
There is nothing among my treasures that I have not shown them.”
[5] Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah,
“Hear the word of the LORD of Armies:
[6] ‘Look, the days are coming
when everything in your house,
and everything your ancestors have stored up until today,
will be carried off to Babylon.
Nothing will be left,’ says the LORD.
[7] ‘They will take away your sons, who will come from you,
whom you will father,
and they will be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.’”
[8] Then Hezekiah said to Isaiah,
“The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good.”
And he went on,
“For there will be peace and truth in my days.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The king of Babylon sends a delegation to Hezekiah, supposedly to congratulate him on his recovery. In reality, Babylon was probably looking for allies against Assyria.
> Verse 2: Hezekiah is flattered and proudly shows the foreigners all his treasures and weapons. That was unwise. He trusted in alliances instead of God, and he showed a foreign power all his riches.
> Verses 6–7: Isaiah announces: One day Babylon will take all this away, and descendants of Hezekiah will serve as eunuchs at the court in Babylon. This happened about 100 years later, when Nebuchadnezzar conquered Jerusalem (2 Kings 24–25; Daniel 1:3).
> “Eunuchs”: These were servants and officials at the royal court, who were often castrated. Sometimes the word also simply means high court officials.
> Verse 8: Hezekiah’s answer is shocking: He is relieved that it will only happen after his time. The main thing is that there is peace during my lifetime. A very human but selfish attitude.
> With this look ahead to Babylon, the first part of the book of Isaiah ends. From chapter 40 on, Isaiah speaks to the people who live in exile in Babylon: “Comfort, comfort my people!”
