# Isaiah – Chapter 58
### The fast that pleases God

---

## Why doesn’t God see our fasting? (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Shout out loud! Don’t hold back!
Lift up your voice like a trumpet!
Tell my people about their disobedience
and the house of Jacob about their sins!
[2] Yet they seek me every day
and are glad to know my ways.
Like a nation that has done what is right
and has not abandoned the laws of its God,
they ask me for righteous judgments.
They are glad to come near to God.
[3] ‘Why have we fasted,’ they say, ‘and you don’t see it?
Why have we humbled ourselves, and you don’t notice?’
Look, on the day of your fast, you do whatever you like
and oppress all your workers.
[4] Look, you fast only to quarrel and fight
and to hit with a wicked fist.
The way you fast today
will not make your voice heard on high.
[5] Is this the kind of fast I have chosen?
A day for a person to humble himself?
Is it to bow your head like a reed
and to lie down on sackcloth and ashes?
Will you call that a fast
and a day that pleases the LORD?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3: The people are very devout. They pray, fast and ask about God. And they complain: “Why have we fasted … and you don’t see it?”
> Verses 3–4: God’s answer: You fast, but at the same time you exploit your workers, you quarrel and you hit. That does not fit together.
> Verse 5: Just hanging your head and sitting in ashes, that alone is not what God wants.

---

## Share your bread with the hungry (Verses 6–12)

[6] Isn’t this the kind of fast I have chosen:
to loosen the chains of wickedness,
to untie the straps of the yoke,
to set the oppressed free
and to break every yoke?
[7] Isn’t it to share your bread with the hungry
and to bring the poor who have been thrown out into your house?
When you see someone naked, to clothe him,
and not to hide yourself from your own flesh and blood?
[8] Then your light will break out like the dawn,
and your healing will spring up quickly.
Then your righteousness will go before you,
and the glory of the LORD will be your rear guard.
[9] Then you will call, and the LORD will answer.
You will cry for help, and he will say, ‘Here I am.’
If you take away the yoke from among you,
the pointing of the finger and the wicked talk,
[10] and if you pour out your soul for the hungry
and satisfy the one who is suffering,
then your light will rise in the darkness,
and your gloom will be like the middle of the day.
[11] And the LORD will always guide you
and satisfy your soul in dry places
and make your bones strong.
You will be like a watered garden
and like a spring of water whose waters never fail.
[12] Your descendants will rebuild the ancient ruins.
You will raise up the foundations of many generations.
You will be called Repairer of the Breach,
Restorer of Streets to Live In.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important texts in the Bible about true devotion.
> Verses 6–7: The fast that God wants: setting the oppressed free, sharing bread with the hungry, taking in the homeless, giving clothes to the naked. “Not to hide yourself from your own flesh and blood” means: Do not turn away from people who are just as human as you are.
> Verses 8–10: To whoever acts like this, God promises: Your light will rise like the dawn. When you call, God says: “Here I am.” The “pointing of the finger,” that is, blaming and despising others, is to stop as well.
> Verse 11: Whoever gives becomes like a watered garden themselves, like a spring that never runs dry.
> Verse 12: Such people rebuild what has been destroyed. They “repair the breach”; they mend what is broken, in the city and in the community.
> On the Jewish Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), a day of fasting, this chapter is read in the synagogue. Jesus says something similar: “I was hungry, and you gave me food to eat” (Matthew 25:35).

## The Sabbath as a delight (Verses 13–14)

[13] If you keep your foot from breaking the Sabbath,
from doing whatever you like on my holy day,
and if you call the Sabbath a delight
and the LORD’s holy day honorable,
and if you honor it
by not going your own ways,
not doing whatever you like
and not speaking your own words,
[14] then you will find your delight in the LORD,
and I will make you ride on the heights of the earth
and feed you with the inheritance of your father Jacob.”
For the mouth of the LORD has spoken.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Sabbath is the seventh day, the day of rest. It is not meant to be a burden, but a “delight,” a joy.
> On this day, you are not to go about your business, but to have time for God and for people. This also does the person good.
> For us today: A day of rest each week is a gift. It helps us not to live only for work and money.
