# Isaiah – Chapter 2
### Swords into plowshares

---

## The mountain of the LORD (Verses 1–5)

[1] This is what Isaiah, the son of Amoz, saw about Judah and Jerusalem.
[2] It will happen in the last days
that the mountain of the LORD’s house will stand firm
as the highest of the mountains,
and will be raised above the hills.
And all nations will stream to it.
[3] Many peoples will go and say,
“Come, let’s go up to the mountain of the LORD,
to the house of the God of Jacob!
He will teach us his ways,
and we will walk in his paths.”
For the teaching will go out from Zion,
and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.
[4] He will judge between the nations
and settle matters for many peoples.
They will beat their swords into plowshares
and their spears into pruning hooks.
Nation will not lift up sword against nation,
and they will not learn war anymore.
[5] House of Jacob, come,
and let’s walk in the light of the LORD!

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the greatest visions of peace in the world.
> Verses 2–3: In the future, all peoples will come to Jerusalem, not to conquer, but to learn from God.
> Verse 4: Because God judges fairly between the peoples, no one needs weapons anymore. Swords become plows, spears become tools for the vineyard. No one learns war anymore.
> In East Germany (the GDR), “Swords into plowshares” became the sign of the peace movement in the churches. A statue with this theme stands in front of the United Nations building in New York.
> Almost the same text is also found in the prophet Micah (Micah 4:1–3).
> Verse 5: Isaiah invites his people: Let’s live this way already now, in God’s light.

---

## The day of the LORD against all pride (Verses 6–22)

[6] For you have abandoned your people, the house of Jacob,
because they are full of what comes from the east,
with fortune-tellers like the Philistines,
and they shake hands with the children of foreigners.
[7] Their land is full of silver and gold,
and there is no end to their treasures.
Their land is also full of horses,
and there is no end to their chariots.
[8] Their land is also full of idols.
They worship the work of their own hands,
what their own fingers have made.
[9] People are brought low,
and humanity is humbled.
So do not forgive them.
[10] Go into the rock
and hide in the dust
from the terror of the LORD
and from the glory of his majesty.
[11] The proud looks of people will be brought low,
and the arrogance of people will be bowed down,
and the LORD alone will be lifted up on that day.
[12] For there will be a day of the LORD of Armies
against everything proud and arrogant
and against everything lifted up,
and it will be brought low,
[13] against all the cedars of Lebanon that are high and lifted up,
against all the oaks of Bashan,
[14] against all the high mountains,
against all the hills that are lifted up,
[15] against every high tower,
against every fortified wall,
[16] against all the ships of Tarshish
and against all the beautiful images.
[17] The pride of people will be bowed down,
and the arrogance of people will be brought low,
and the LORD alone will be lifted up on that day.
[18] The idols will disappear completely.
[19] People will go into the caves of the rocks
and into the holes of the earth
from the terror of the LORD
and from the glory of his majesty,
when he rises to shake the earth mightily.
[20] On that day, people will throw away their idols of silver
and their idols of gold,
which they made for themselves to worship,
to the moles and to the bats,
[21] to go into the caves of the rocks
and into the cracks of the jagged rocks
from the terror of the LORD
and from the glory of his majesty,
when he rises to shake the earth mightily.
[22] Stop trusting in people,
who have only a breath in their nostrils!
For what are they worth?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–8: The people have become rich: silver, gold, horses, chariots. But they have forgotten God. They believe in fortune-tellers and worship idols they made themselves.
> Verse 9: “Do not forgive them” is a harsh sentence. Isaiah is so shaken by his people turning away that he asks God not to simply look past it.
> Verses 12–17: The “day of the LORD” is the day on which God steps in and brings low everything high and proud: tall trees, high mountains, high towers, great trading ships. Everything people are proud of loses its shine. Only God remains lifted up.
> Verse 20: An almost funny picture: People throw away their golden idols, to the moles and bats, into the dark holes.
> Verse 22: Don’t put your trust in people. Their life is only one breath.
