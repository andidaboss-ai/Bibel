# Isaiah – Chapter 15
### Lament over Moab

---

## Moab weeps (Verses 1–9)

[1] The burden about Moab.
For in one night, Ar of Moab is ruined and destroyed.
For in one night, Kir of Moab is ruined and destroyed.
[2] They have gone up to Bayith and to Dibon,
to the high places, to weep.
Moab wails over Nebo and over Medeba.
Every head is shaved bald.
Every beard is cut off.
[3] In their streets, they put on sackcloth.
In their streets and on their roofs, everyone wails
and melts into tears.
[4] Heshbon cries out with Elealeh.
Their voice is heard as far as Jahaz.
So the soldiers of Moab cry out loud.
Their souls tremble within them.
[5] My heart cries out for Moab!
Her nobles flee to Zoar, to Eglath Shelishiyah.
For they go up the slope of Luhith weeping.
For on the road to Horonaim,
they raise a cry over the destruction.
[6] For the waters of Nimrim will be a wasteland.
For the grass has dried up, the young grass is gone,
there is nothing green anymore.
[7] So they carry away the riches they have gained
and what they have stored up
across the Brook of the Willows.
[8] For the cry has gone around the borders of Moab.
Its wailing reaches to Eglaim,
and its wailing to Beer Elim.
[9] For the waters of Dimon are full of blood.
For I will bring even more on Dimon:
a lion on those of Moab who escape
and on the rest of the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moab was a neighboring people of Israel, east of the Dead Sea, in what is today Jordan. The Moabites were related to Israel (Genesis 19:37). David’s great-grandmother Ruth was a Moabite.
> One city after another is destroyed in a single night. The people mourn: They shave their hair, cut off their beards, put on mourning clothes.
> Verse 5: What is special: The prophet weeps with them! “My heart cries out for Moab!” Even though Moab was often an enemy, Isaiah feels with the suffering people. God’s prophet does not rejoice over the misfortune of other peoples.
> Verse 9: “The waters of Dimon”: In Hebrew, Dimon sounds like “dam,” blood. A play on words.
