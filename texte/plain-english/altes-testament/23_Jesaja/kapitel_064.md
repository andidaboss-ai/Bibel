# Isaiah – Chapter 64
### Oh, that you would tear open the heavens!

---

## Come down! (Verses 1–7)

[1] Oh, that you would tear open the heavens and come down,
that the mountains would shake before you,
[2] as when fire sets brushwood alight
and the fire makes water boil!
Make your name known to your enemies,
so that the nations tremble before you!
[3] When you did awesome things that we did not expect,
you came down,
and the mountains shook before you.
[4] For since ancient times no one has heard,
no ear has perceived,
and no eye has seen a God besides you,
who acts for the one who waits for him.
[5] You meet the one who rejoices and does what is right,
those who remember you in your ways.
Look, you were angry, and we sinned.
We have been in sin for a long time.
Shall we be saved?
[6] For we have all become like someone who is unclean,
and all our righteousness is like a dirty garment.
We all wither like a leaf,
and our wrongdoings carry us away like the wind.
[7] There is no one who calls on your name,
who stirs himself to take hold of you.
For you have hidden your face from us
and let us waste away because of our wrongdoings.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A passionate cry: “Oh, that you would tear open the heavens and come down!” The people long for God himself to step in. A German Advent hymn (in English: “O Savior, Rend the Heavens Wide”) takes up this verse. Christians see the birth of Jesus as the answer to this cry.
> Verse 4: No other god acts for those who wait for him. Paul quotes this verse freely: “Things which an eye didn’t see … these God has prepared for those who love him” (1 Corinthians 2:9).
> Verse 6: An honest confession: Even our best deeds are like a dirty garment. We wither like a leaf in autumn.
> Verse 7: No one holds on to God anymore. The people feel as if God has turned away.

---

## We are the clay, you are the potter (Verses 8–12)

[8] But now, LORD, you are our Father.
We are the clay, and you are our potter.
We are all the work of your hand.
[9] Do not be so very angry, LORD,
and do not remember our guilt forever.
Please look, we beg you: We are all your people.
[10] Your holy cities have become a wilderness.
Zion has become a wilderness, Jerusalem a ruin.
[11] Our holy and beautiful house,
where our fathers praised you,
has been burned with fire.
Everything we loved lies in ruins.
[12] Will you hold yourself back after all this, LORD?
Will you keep silent and punish us so severely?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: In spite of everything: “You are our Father. We are the clay, and you are our potter.” The people put themselves completely in God’s hands. A potter can reshape a pot that has gone wrong. In the same way, God can make his people new.
> Verse 9: “We are all your people.” An appeal to God’s faithfulness.
> Verses 10–11: The lament over destroyed Jerusalem and the burned temple, “our holy and beautiful house, where our fathers praised you.”
> Verse 12: The prayer ends with an open question: Will you keep silent? In the next chapter, God answers.
