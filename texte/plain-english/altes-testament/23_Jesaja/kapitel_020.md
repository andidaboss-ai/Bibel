# Isaiah – Chapter 20
### Naked and barefoot

---

## A sign against Egypt and Cush (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod,
when Sargon, the king of Assyria, sent him,
and he fought against Ashdod and captured it,
[2] at that time the LORD spoke through Isaiah, the son of Amoz:
“Go and take the sackcloth off your waist,
and take your sandals off your feet.”
He did so, and walked around naked and barefoot.
[3] The LORD said,
“Just as my servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot for three years
as a sign and a wonder about Egypt and about Ethiopia,
[4] so the king of Assyria will lead away the captives of Egypt
and the exiles of Ethiopia,
young and old, naked and barefoot,
with their buttocks uncovered,
to the shame of Egypt.
[5] They will be terrified and put to shame
because of Ethiopia, which they hoped in,
and because of Egypt, which they boasted about.
[6] On that day, the people of this coastland will say,
‘Look, this is what happened to our hope,
to which we ran for help
to be rescued from the king of Assyria.
And we, how will we escape?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the year 711 BC, the Assyrian army commander (“Tartan” is a title) conquered the Philistine city of Ashdod. The Philistines had hoped for help from Egypt.
> Isaiah has to act out a sign: For three years he walks around naked, that is, only in a loincloth, and barefoot like a prisoner of war. For a respected man, that was a great humiliation.
> The message: That is how the Egyptians and the people of Cush will be led away as prisoners. Whoever trusts in Egypt will be disappointed.
> Our English source text says “Ethiopia.” This means Cush, south of Egypt.
> Prophets often did not only speak, but also acted out their message, so that people could not overlook it.
