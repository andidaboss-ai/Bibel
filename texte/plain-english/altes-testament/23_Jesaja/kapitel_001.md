# Isaiah – Chapter 1
### Come, let’s settle this together

---

## Before we begin: Who were the prophets? What is the book of Isaiah?

With Isaiah, the books of the prophets begin.
A prophet is a person through whom God speaks. Prophets do not mainly predict the future. They tell the people of their own time what God wants from them. They criticize injustice, the oppression of the poor and false worship. And they give hope when everything seems lost.
Isaiah lived about 2,700 years ago in Jerusalem, from about 740 to 700 BC. At that time, mighty Assyria was a great threat. Isaiah gave advice to several kings of Judah.
The book of Isaiah has 66 chapters and is often divided into three parts:
– Chapters 1–39: Judgment and hope in Isaiah’s time. Here we also find the promise of “Immanuel” and of the “Prince of Peace.”
– Chapters 40–55: Comfort for the people in exile in Babylon, about 150 years later. “Comfort, comfort my people!” Here are the songs about the “Servant of God,” who suffers for others.
– Chapters 56–66: Hope for the time after the return. A new heaven and a new earth.
Many scholars think that the later parts come from students of Isaiah, who kept on writing in his spirit. Others think Isaiah wrote the whole book.
No book of the prophets is quoted as often in the New Testament as Isaiah. Jews read Isaiah as God’s word to Israel, full of hope for redemption. Christians see in many passages pointers to Jesus Christ.
A name for God that often appears in Isaiah: “the Holy One of Israel.”

## My people do not understand (Verses 1–9)

[1] The vision of Isaiah, the son of Amoz,
which he saw about Judah and Jerusalem
in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah.
[2] Hear, you heavens, and listen, you earth,
for the LORD has spoken:
“I have raised children and brought them up,
but they have rebelled against me.
[3] The ox knows its owner,
and the donkey its master’s feeding trough,
but Israel does not know.
My people do not understand.”
[4] Oh, sinful nation,
a people loaded down with guilt,
children of evildoers,
children who act corruptly!
They have abandoned the LORD.
They have despised the Holy One of Israel.
They have turned away and gone backward.
[5] Why do you want to be beaten even more,
that you rebel more and more?
The whole head is sick,
and the whole heart is weak.
[6] From the sole of the foot to the head, there is nothing healthy in it,
only wounds, welts, and open sores.
They have not been cleaned, not bandaged,
and not soothed with oil.
[7] Your land is ruined.
Your cities are burned with fire.
Strangers eat up your land before your eyes,
and it is ruined, as if destroyed by strangers.
[8] The daughter of Zion is left
like a shelter in a vineyard,
like a hut in a cucumber field,
like a city under siege.
[9] If the LORD of Armies had not left us a very small remnant,
we would have become like Sodom.
We would have been like Gomorrah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Isaiah worked under four kings. That is about 40 years.
> Verses 2–3: God complains like a disappointed father: I raised my children, and they turn away. Even the ox and the donkey know where they belong. But my people do not know. This verse is where the ox and donkey at the manger in many Christmas scenes come from.
> Verses 5–6: The people are like a body full of wounds, from head to toe. They are badly hurt by war and by their own guilt.
> Verse 8: The “daughter of Zion” is Jerusalem. All around the city, everything is ruined. It stands alone like a little hut in an empty field. That happened when the Assyrians laid waste to the land in the year 701 BC.
> Verse 9: God has left a “remnant.” The idea of a “remnant” that is saved is very important in Isaiah.

## God wants justice, not empty sacrifices (Verses 10–17)

[10] Hear the word of the LORD, you rulers of Sodom!
Listen to the teaching of our God, you people of Gomorrah!
[11] “What are your many sacrifices to me?” says the LORD.
“I have had enough of burnt offerings of rams
and the fat of fattened calves.
I take no pleasure in the blood of bulls,
of lambs, or of male goats.
[12] When you come to appear before me,
who asked this of you,
to trample my courtyards?
[13] Bring no more empty offerings!
Incense is disgusting to me.
New moons, Sabbaths, and gatherings:
I cannot stand evil gatherings.
[14] My soul hates your New Moons and your feasts.
They are a burden to me.
I am tired of carrying them.
[15] When you spread out your hands,
I will hide my eyes from you.
Yes, when you pray a lot, I will not listen.
Your hands are full of blood.
[16] Wash yourselves! Make yourselves clean!
Put your evil deeds away from my eyes!
Stop doing evil!
[17] Learn to do good!
Seek justice!
Help the oppressed!
Defend the orphans!
Plead the cause of the widow!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Isaiah calls the rulers of Jerusalem “rulers of Sodom.” That is a shocking provocation. Sodom was the symbol of an evil city.
> Verses 11–15: The people hold many worship services, bring sacrifices and pray a lot. But God does not want this! Why? Because their hands are “full of blood.” They oppress others and do wrong.
> God does not reject worship itself. He rejects worship that goes together with injustice in everyday life. Religion without justice is worthless.
> Verses 16–17: What God really wants: Stop doing evil. Do good. Make sure there is justice. Help the weakest: the oppressed, orphans and widows. This message is still true today for everyone who believes in God.

## Even if your sins are red as blood (Verses 18–20)

[18] “Come now, and let’s settle this together,” says the LORD.
“Though your sins are like scarlet,
they will be as white as snow.
Though they are red like crimson,
they will be like wool.
[19] If you are willing and obey,
you will eat the good things of the land.
[20] But if you refuse and rebel,
you will be eaten up by the sword,
for the mouth of the LORD has spoken it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18 is one of the most beautiful offers in the Bible. God invites: Come, let’s settle the matter! Even if your guilt is as red as blood, I can make it white as snow. There is forgiveness and a new beginning.
> Verses 19–20: But people have to decide: Do I want to turn back or not?

## The faithful city has become unfaithful (Verses 21–31)

[21] How the faithful city has become a prostitute!
She was full of justice.
Righteousness lived in her,
but now murderers live there.
[22] Your silver has become dross,
your wine is mixed with water.
[23] Your princes are rebels and companions of thieves.
Everyone loves bribes and runs after gifts.
They do not defend the orphans,
and the cause of the widow does not come before them.
[24] So the Lord, the LORD of Armies, the Mighty One of Israel, says:
“Oh, I will get relief from my opponents
and take revenge on my enemies.
[25] I will turn my hand against you,
melt away your dross completely,
and remove all your tin.
[26] I will restore your judges as at the first,
and your advisers as at the beginning.
Afterward you will be called
‘The city of righteousness, a faithful city.’
[27] Zion will be redeemed by justice,
and those in her who turn back, by righteousness.
[28] But rebels and sinners will be broken together,
and those who abandon the LORD will be destroyed.
[29] For they will be ashamed of the oaks that you desired,
and you will be put to shame because of the gardens that you chose.
[30] For you will be like an oak whose leaves wither,
and like a garden that has no water.
[31] The strong will be like tinder, and his work like a spark.
Both will burn together,
and no one will put out the fire.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Jerusalem was once a city of justice. Now it has become unfaithful to God, like a woman who cheats on her husband. The prophets often use this picture for being unfaithful to God.
> Verses 22–23: Everything is spoiled: The silver is worthless, the wine watered down, the leaders take bribes. And again: Orphans and widows get no justice.
> Verses 25–26: God wants to clean the city like a smith who frees silver from dross in the fire. After that it will again be called the “city of righteousness.” God’s judgment has a goal: renewal.
> Verse 29: “Oaks” and “gardens” were places where people worshiped foreign gods.
> Verse 31: Whoever relies on his own strength burns up like tinder.
