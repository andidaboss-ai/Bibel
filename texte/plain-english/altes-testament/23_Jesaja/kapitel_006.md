# Isaiah – Chapter 6
### Here I am, send me!

---

## Holy, holy, holy (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord.
He was sitting on a throne.
The throne was high and lifted up.
The edge of his robe filled the temple.
[2] Seraphim stood above him.
Each one had six wings.
With two wings each one covered his face.
With two he covered his feet.
With two he flew.
[3] They called out to each other,
“Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of Armies!
The whole earth is full of his glory!”
[4] The voice of the one who called out
made the doorways shake down to their foundations.
The house filled with smoke.
[5] Then I said, “How terrible for me!
I am lost.
I am a man with unclean lips.
And I live among a people with unclean lips.
Yet my eyes have seen the King, the LORD of Armies!”
[6] Then one of the seraphim flew to me.
He had a burning coal in his hand.
He had taken it from the altar with tongs.
[7] He touched my mouth with it.
He said, “Look, this has touched your lips.
Your guilt is taken away.
Your sin is forgiven.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is Isaiah’s call to be a prophet, in the year 740 BC, when King Uzziah died.
> Isaiah sees God in the temple on a huge throne. Even the edge of his robe fills the whole temple.
> Verse 2: “Seraphim” means “the burning ones.” They are heavenly beings. They cover their faces because even they cannot look at God directly.
> Verse 3: “Holy, holy, holy”: Three times holy means: completely holy, totally different from everything else. This song is still sung today in Jewish prayers (the “Kedushah”) and in Christian worship services (the “Sanctus”).
> Verse 5: Isaiah is terrified. In front of God’s holiness, he realizes how unclean he himself is.
> Verses 6–7: But God makes him clean. The glowing coal touches his lips: His guilt is forgiven. Only then can he pass on God’s word.

---

## Whom shall I send? (Verses 8–13)

[8] I heard the voice of the Lord.
He said, “Whom shall I send? Who will go for us?”
Then I said, “Here I am. Send me!”
[9] He said, “Go and tell this people:
‘You hear and hear, but you do not understand.
You look and look, but you do not see what it means.’
[10] Make the heart of this people dull.
Make their ears deaf.
Close their eyes.
Otherwise they might see with their eyes.
They might hear with their ears.
They might understand with their heart.
Then they would turn back and be healed.”
[11] Then I said, “Lord, how long?”
He answered, “Until the cities lie in ruins and no one lives in them.
Until the houses are empty of people.
Until the land is completely ruined.
[12] Until the LORD has sent the people far away.
Until many places in the land are left empty.
[13] Even if a tenth of the people are still left in the land,
they will be burned up again.
It will be like a terebinth tree or an oak tree.
When they are cut down, a stump is left.
The holy seed is its stump.”
A terebinth is a large tree.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: “Here I am. Send me!” A famous sentence. Isaiah volunteers. Many people have made this sentence their own prayer.
> Verses 9–10: A very hard task: Isaiah is to preach, but the people will not listen. It sounds as if God wants them not to turn back.
> Many interpreters understand it like this: God tells Isaiah in advance how the people will react. His preaching will not soften their hearts; instead, they will become even harder. Jesus quotes these words when many people do not want to understand him (Matthew 13:13–15).
> Verse 11: “Lord, how long?” The answer is hard: until the land is destroyed and the people are carried away.
> Verse 13: But at the end, a sign of hope: When a tree is cut down, a stump remains. And something can grow out of a stump again. This “holy seed” is the remnant from which God will make a new beginning (compare 11:1).
