# Isaiah – Chapter 66
### As a mother comforts her child

---

## Heaven is my throne (Verses 1–6)

[1] This is what the LORD says:
“Heaven is my throne,
and the earth is my footstool.
What kind of house will you build for me?
Where will my resting place be?
[2] For my hand has made all these things,
and so all these things came to be,” says the LORD.
“But this is the one I look to:
the one who is poor and crushed in spirit
and who trembles at my word.
[3] Whoever kills an ox is like someone who kills a person.
Whoever sacrifices a lamb is like someone who breaks a dog’s neck.
Whoever brings an offering is like someone who offers pig’s blood.
Whoever burns incense is like someone who blesses an idol.
Yes, they have chosen their own ways,
and their soul delights in their disgusting things.
[4] So I too will choose their delusions
and bring on them what they fear,
because when I called, no one answered,
and when I spoke, they did not listen,
but they did what is evil in my eyes
and chose what I do not delight in.”
[5] Hear the word of the LORD,
you who tremble at his word:
“Your brothers who hate you,
who throw you out for the sake of my name,
have said, ‘Let the LORD be glorified,
so that we may see your joy!’
But they will be put to shame.
[6] A sound of uproar from the city,
a sound from the temple,
the sound of the LORD repaying his enemies what they deserve.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: God is so great that no building can contain him. Heaven is his throne. But God looks to the poor and humble, to those who take his word seriously. Stephen quotes these verses (Acts 7:49–50).
> Verse 3: A harsh verse: Sacrifices without an honest heart are as bad as crimes to God. Whoever holds worship services but at the same time goes his own evil ways, his worship is worthless.
> Verse 5: Faithful people are shut out and mocked by their own “brothers.” God says: In the end, the mockers will be put to shame.

---

## Rejoice with Jerusalem! (Verses 7–14)

[7] Before she went into labor, she gave birth.
Before her pain came, she gave birth to a son.
[8] Who has ever heard of such a thing?
Who has ever seen such things?
Can a land be born in one day?
Can a nation be born all at once?
For as soon as Zion went into labor, she gave birth to her children.
[9] “Shall I bring a child to the point of birth and not let it be born?” says the LORD.
“Shall I, who cause birth, close the womb?” says your God.
[10] “Rejoice with Jerusalem and be glad for her,
all you who love her!
Rejoice with her in joy,
all you who mourn over her,
[11] so that you may nurse and be satisfied
at her comforting breasts,
so that you may drink deeply and delight
in the abundance of her glory.”
[12] For this is what the LORD says:
“Look, I will make peace flow to her like a river,
and the glory of the nations like an overflowing stream,
and you will nurse.
You will be carried on her hip
and bounced on her knees.
[13] As a mother comforts her child,
so I will comfort you.
You will be comforted in Jerusalem.”
[14] You will see it, and your heart will rejoice,
and your bones will flourish like new grass.
The LORD’s hand will be known among his servants,
and he will be angry with his enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–9: Zion becomes like a mother who has children very suddenly and without pain: a whole people in one day. God finishes what he has begun.
> Verses 10–12: Jerusalem is like a mother who nurses her children, carries them on her hip and bounces them on her knees.
> Verse 13 is one of the most tender verses in the Bible: “As a mother comforts her child, so I will comfort you.” God comforts like a mother. In Germany, this verse was chosen as the Bible verse for the year 2016.
> Verse 14: “Your bones will flourish like new grass”: new life and new strength for everyone who was sad.

## All nations will come (Verses 15–24)

[15] For look, the LORD will come with fire,
and his chariots will be like the whirlwind,
to pay back his anger with fury
and his rebuke with flames of fire.
[16] For the LORD will carry out judgment with fire and with his sword
on all people,
and those killed by the LORD will be many.
[17] “Those who make themselves holy and purify themselves to go into the gardens,
following one in the middle,
eating pork, disgusting things and mice,
will come to an end together,” says the LORD.
[18] “For I know their works and their thoughts.
The time is coming when I will gather all nations and languages,
and they will come and see my glory.
[19] I will set up a sign among them,
and I will send some of those who have escaped to the nations,
to Tarshish, Pul and Lud, who draw the bow,
to Tubal and Javan, to the faraway islands
that have not heard of my fame
and have not seen my glory.
And they will announce my glory among the nations.
[20] They will bring all your brothers out of all the nations
as an offering to the LORD,
on horses, in chariots, in litters, on mules and on camels,
to my holy mountain Jerusalem,” says the LORD,
“just as the people of Israel bring their offering in a clean vessel
into the house of the LORD.
[21] I will also choose some of them to be priests and Levites,”
says the LORD.
[22] “For as the new heavens and the new earth that I will make
will remain before me,” says the LORD,
“so your descendants and your name will remain.
[23] And this is how it will be: From one new moon to the next
and from one Sabbath to the next,
all people will come to worship before me,” says the LORD.
[24] “And they will go out and look at the dead bodies of the people
who have rebelled against me.
For their worm will not die,
and their fire will not be put out,
and they will be disgusting to all people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–17: Once more a picture of judgment: God comes with fire. It strikes those who follow foreign worship. Judgment is God’s business, not the business of people who want to punish others.
> Verses 18–19: Then the great goal: God gathers all nations and languages. Messengers go out into the whole world, to the farthest countries: Tarshish (in the west, perhaps Spain), Lud and Pul (probably in Africa or Asia Minor), Tubal (on the Black Sea) and Javan (Greece).
> Verses 20–21: The nations bring the scattered Israelites home. And the astonishing thing: God even takes people from the nations to be priests and Levites. No one is shut out.
> Verses 22–23: Like the new heavens and the new earth, God’s people will also remain forever. And all people will worship God.
> Verse 24: The book ends with a dark picture: Those who set themselves against God are dead. Jesus takes up this picture when he warns of ruin (Mark 9:48). In the synagogue, verse 23 is read once more after verse 24, so that the reading does not end with judgment, but with hope: All people will come to worship God.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Isaiah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The prophet:** Isaiah lived about 2,700 years ago in Jerusalem. He was a prophet under four kings of Judah. His name means “The LORD saves,” and that is the theme of the whole book.
> **Three parts:** Chapters 1–39 take place in Isaiah’s time, when Assyria was the great threat. Chapters 40–55 speak to the exiles in Babylon (the “Book of Comfort”). Chapters 56–66 speak to those who had returned home to Jerusalem. Many scholars think that later prophets in Isaiah’s tradition wrote parts 2 and 3. Others see the whole book as Isaiah’s work.
> **Holy, holy, holy:** Isaiah sees God as “the Holy One of Israel” (chapter 6). God is high and lifted up, but he lives with the crushed (57:15).
> **Justice:** Isaiah sharply criticizes people who act devout but oppress the poor (chapters 1 and 58). “Learn to do good! Seek justice!” (1:17).
> **The Prince of Peace:** “For a child is born to us” (9:6; in some Bibles 9:5) and the shoot from the stump of Jesse (chapter 11). Wolf and lamb will live together. Christians see Jesus announced here; in Judaism, people expect the Messiah or apply it to King Hezekiah.
> **Swords into plowshares:** All nations come to God’s mountain and no longer learn war (2:4).
> **Comfort my people:** After the judgment comes comfort (chapter 40). “Do not be afraid, for I have redeemed you” (43:1). “But I will not forget you!” (49:15).
> **The Servant of God:** Four songs tell of a servant who suffers in silence and makes many whole (42; 49; 50; 52–53). Jews mostly see Israel in him, Christians above all Jesus.
> **For all nations:** God’s house is “a house of prayer for all peoples” (56:7). In the end, all people will come to worship God (66:23).
> **Everything will be new:** God creates new heavens and a new earth, without weeping and without violence (65:17–25). And he comforts like a mother (66:13).
> **What comes next?** Next comes the book of Jeremiah. Jeremiah lived about 100 years after Isaiah and lived through the destruction of Jerusalem himself.
