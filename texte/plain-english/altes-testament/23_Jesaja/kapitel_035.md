# Isaiah – Chapter 35
### The desert will bloom

---

## Strengthen the tired hands (Verses 1–4)

[1] The wilderness and the dry land will be glad.
The desert will rejoice and bloom like a rose.
[2] It will bloom richly
and rejoice, yes, with joy and singing.
The glory of Lebanon will be given to it,
the splendor of Carmel and Sharon.
They will see the glory of the LORD,
the splendor of our God.
[3] Strengthen the weak hands
and make the shaking knees firm!
[4] Tell those who have a fearful heart:
“Be strong! Do not be afraid!
Look, your God will come with vengeance,
with the payback of God.
He will come and save you.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the dark chapter 34 comes one of the most hopeful chapters in the Bible.
> Verses 1–2: The desert blooms like a rose garden. Where there was only sand before, flowers now grow. The desert receives the beauty of the greenest regions of Israel.
> Verses 3–4: A task for everyone who trusts God: Encourage the tired and the fearful! Tell them: Be strong, do not be afraid! God is coming and will save you.
> The letter to the Hebrews quotes verse 3 as encouragement (Hebrews 12:12).
> If you are afraid or feel you cannot go on, you may hear these words as spoken to you.

---

## The blind see, the lame leap (Verses 5–7)

[5] Then the eyes of the blind will be opened,
and the ears of the deaf will be unblocked.
[6] Then the lame will leap like a deer,
and the tongue of those who cannot speak will sing,
for water will break out in the wilderness
and streams in the desert.
[7] The burning sand will become a pool,
and the thirsty ground springs of water.
Where the jackals used to lie,
grass will grow with reeds and rushes.

> **What does this mean?**
> When God comes, everything will be made whole: The blind see, the deaf hear, the lame leap, those who cannot speak sing.
> When John the Baptist sends to ask whether Jesus is the promised savior, Jesus answers with these words: “The blind see, the lame walk … the deaf hear” (Matthew 11:5).
> Important: These verses are a hope for healing. They do not mean that people with disabilities are worth less. Every person is loved by God just as they are.

---

## The holy way (Verses 8–10)

[8] A highway will be there, a road,
and it will be called “The Holy Way.”
No one who is unclean will travel on it,
but it will be for those who walk on the Way.
Wicked fools will not go there.
[9] No lion will be there,
and no wild beast will go up on it.
They will not be found there,
but the redeemed will walk there.
[10] Then the people the LORD has set free will return
and come to Zion with singing,
and everlasting joy will be on their heads.
They will receive gladness and joy,
and sorrow and sighing will run away.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A road leads through the blooming desert, “The Holy Way.” On it, those who have been set free return home safely. No wild animals, no danger.
> Verse 10: One of the most beautiful verses in the Bible: Those who have been set free come home singing, with everlasting joy like a crown on their heads. Sorrow and sighing run away.
> Johannes Brahms set this verse to music in his “German Requiem.”
> This ends the first large part of the book of Isaiah. Next come four chapters with stories about King Hezekiah (chapters 36–39).
