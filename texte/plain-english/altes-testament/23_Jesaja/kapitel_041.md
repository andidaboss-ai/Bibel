# Isaiah – Chapter 41
### Do not be afraid, for I am with you

---

## Who has stirred up the man from the east? (Verses 1–7)

[1] “Be silent before me, you islands,
and let the peoples renew their strength.
Let them come near, then let them speak.
Let us come together for judgment.
[2] Who has stirred up one from the east?
Who has called him in righteousness to follow him?
He hands nations over to him
and lets him rule over kings.
He makes them like dust before his sword,
like stubble blown away before his bow.
[3] He chases them and passes on safely,
on a road his feet have never walked before.
[4] Who has worked and done this,
calling the generations from the beginning?
I, the LORD, am the first,
and with the last, I am he.”
[5] The islands have seen it and are afraid.
The ends of the earth tremble.
They come near and arrive.
[6] Everyone helps his neighbor.
They say to their brothers, “Be strong!”
[7] So the craftsman encourages the goldsmith.
The one who smooths with the hammer encourages the one who strikes the anvil,
and says of the soldering, “It is good.”
And he fastens it with nails so that it will not wobble.

> **What does this mean?**
> God calls the nations as if to a court hearing.
> Verses 2–3: This is about “one from the east.” That is Cyrus, the king of Persia. He conquered one country after another, and in the year 539 BC he also conquered Babylon. Isaiah says: God has called him.
> Verse 4: God is the first and the last. He guides all of history.
> Verses 6–7: The nations become afraid and encourage each other by quickly building new idols and nailing them down. Isaiah describes this with mockery.

---

## Do not be afraid (Verses 8–16)

[8] “But you, Israel, my servant,
Jacob, whom I have chosen,
descendant of Abraham, my friend,
[9] you whom I have taken hold of from the ends of the earth
and called from its farthest corners,
and to whom I said,
‘You are my servant.
I have chosen you and have not rejected you’:
[10] Do not be afraid, for I am with you.
Do not be discouraged, for I am your God.
I will strengthen you.
Yes, I will help you.
Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness.
[11] Look, all who rage against you
will be disappointed and put to shame.
Those who fight with you will be like nothing and will perish.
[12] You will look for them and not find them,
those who fight with you.
Those who wage war against you will be like nothing,
like something that does not exist.
[13] For I, the LORD your God,
hold your right hand
and say to you, ‘Do not be afraid! I will help you.’
[14] Do not be afraid, you worm Jacob,
and you men of Israel.
I will help you,” says the LORD.
“Your Redeemer is the Holy One of Israel.
[15] Look, I have made you into a new, sharp threshing sledge with teeth.
You will thresh the mountains and crush them
and make the hills like chaff.
[16] You will winnow them, and the wind will carry them away,
and the storm will scatter them.
You will rejoice in the LORD.
You will boast in the Holy One of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: God calls Abraham “my friend.” What an honor! And Israel is his “servant,” whom he has chosen.
> Verse 10 is one of the most comforting verses in the Bible: “Do not be afraid, for I am with you. … I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you.”
> Verse 13: God holds the hand of his people, the way a father or mother holds a child by the hand.
> Verse 14: “You worm Jacob”: In exile, Israel feels as small and weak as a worm. God says: It is you I will help. The Hebrew word for “Redeemer” is “Go’el”: a relative who stands up for a family member in need and buys them free. In the same way, God stands up for his people.
> Verses 15–16: With God’s help, the weak people become as strong as a threshing sledge that crushes mountains. This is a picture of God removing all obstacles.

## Water in the desert (Verses 17–20)

[17] The poor and needy look for water, and there is none.
Their tongue is dry with thirst.
I, the LORD, will answer them.
I, the God of Israel, will not abandon them.
[18] I will open rivers on the bare heights
and springs in the middle of the valleys.
I will make the wilderness a pool of water
and the dry land springs of water.
[19] I will put cedar, acacia, myrtle and oil trees in the wilderness.
I will set cypress, pine and box trees together in the desert,
[20] so that they may see, know, consider and understand together
that the hand of the LORD has done this
and the Holy One of Israel has created it.

> **What does this mean?**
> God takes care of the poor and the thirsty. The desert through which those returning home travel becomes a garden with water and trees.
> This recalls the journey through the wilderness after the exodus from Egypt, when God gave water from the rock.
> Verse 20: Everyone is to recognize from this that God is the Creator.

---

## The idols can do nothing (Verses 21–29)

[21] “Present your case,” says the LORD.
“Bring out your strong arguments!” says the King of Jacob.
[22] “Let them announce and tell us what will happen!
Tell us the former things, what they were,
so that we may think about them and know how they turned out.
Or let us hear the things to come.
[23] Tell us what is going to happen in the future,
so that we may know that you are gods.
Yes, do good or do evil,
so that we may be terrified and see it together.
[24] Look, you are nothing, and your work is nothing.
Whoever chooses you is detestable.
[25] I have stirred up one from the north, and he has come,
from the rising of the sun, one who calls on my name.
He will trample rulers like mortar,
as the potter treads clay.
[26] Who announced it from the beginning, so that we might know,
and beforehand, so that we might say, ‘He is right’?
Surely, there is no one who announced it.
Surely, there is no one who showed it.
Surely, there is no one who hears your words.
[27] I am the first to say to Zion, ‘Look, here they are!’
And I will give Jerusalem a messenger of good news.
[28] When I look, there is no one,
and among them there is no adviser
who can answer a word when I ask.
[29] Look, all their deeds are empty and nothing.
Their cast idols are wind and confusion.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Another court hearing: God challenges the gods of Babylon. If you are gods, then tell us what will happen! Or at least do something, good or evil!
> But the idols are silent. They can do nothing.
> Verse 25: Cyrus comes “from the north” and “from the rising of the sun,” that is, from the northeast. Persia lay to the east, but Cyrus first conquered regions in the north.
> Verse 27: Only the living God can foretell and fulfill what he promises.
