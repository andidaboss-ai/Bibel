# Isaiah – Chapter 26
### Your dead will live

---

## A song about the strong city (Verses 1–6)

[1] On that day, this song will be sung in the land of Judah:
“We have a strong city.
God sets up salvation as its walls and ramparts.
[2] Open the gates,
so that the righteous nation may enter,
the one that keeps faith.
[3] You will keep in perfect peace
the one whose mind is steady,
because he trusts in you.
[4] Trust in the LORD forever,
for in Yah, the LORD, is an everlasting Rock.
[5] For he has brought down those who live on high,
the lofty city.
He brings it low.
He brings it low to the ground.
He brings it down to the dust.
[6] The foot will trample it,
the feet of the poor
and the steps of the needy.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In contrast to the destroyed city of violence (chapter 25:2), here stands God’s strong city. Its walls are not made of stone, but of God’s salvation.
> Verse 2: The gates open for all who are faithful.
> Verse 3 is a well-known verse of comfort: Whoever trusts God finds “perfect peace.” In Hebrew it says “shalom, shalom,” double peace.
> Verse 4: God is a rock that you can rely on forever.
> Verse 6: The poor, who used to be trampled, now walk over the ruins of the proud city. Things are turned around.

---

## At night I long for you (Verses 7–15)

[7] The way of the righteous is straight.
You, Upright One, make the path of the righteous level.
[8] Yes, in the way of your judgments, LORD,
we have waited for you.
Your name and your fame
are the longing of our soul.
[9] With my soul I have longed for you in the night.
Yes, with my spirit within me I will seek you eagerly,
for when your judgments are on the earth,
the people of the world learn righteousness.
[10] If the wicked are shown kindness,
they still do not learn righteousness.
In the land of honesty they do wrong
and do not see the majesty of the LORD.
[11] LORD, your hand is raised,
but they do not see it.
But they will see your zeal for the people and be disappointed.
Yes, fire will burn up your enemies.
[12] LORD, you will give us peace,
for you have also done all our work for us.
[13] LORD our God,
other lords besides you have ruled over us,
but we will only acknowledge your name.
[14] The dead will not live.
The spirits of the dead will not rise.
That is why you have punished and destroyed them
and wiped out all memory of them.
[15] You have made the nation grow, LORD.
You have made the nation grow!
You are glorified!
You have widened all the borders of the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–9: A deep longing for God that can be felt even at night.
> Verse 12: A beautiful thought: All the good things we do are really things God has done in us.
> Verses 13–14: Other lords, that is, foreign rulers, have ruled over the people. But they are dead and forgotten. They will not come back.

---

## Like a woman in labor (Verses 16–19)

[16] LORD, in trouble they looked for you.
They poured out a prayer
when your discipline was on them.
[17] Just as a pregnant woman who is about to give birth
is in pain and cries out in her labor,
so we have been before you, LORD.
[18] We were pregnant. We were in pain.
We gave birth, it seems, only to wind.
We have not brought salvation to the earth,
and the people of the world have not fallen.
[19] Your dead will live.
Their dead bodies will rise.
Wake up and sing for joy, you who live in the dust!
For your dew is like the dew of herbs,
and the earth will give up the spirits of the dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: An honest picture: The people suffered like a woman in labor. But in the end nothing came of it, “only wind.” All the effort seemed to be for nothing.
> Verse 19 is one of the most important verses of the Old Testament: “Your dead will live. Their dead bodies will rise.” Unlike the dead tyrants (verse 14), God’s dead will come back to life.
> As the morning dew makes the plants bloom again, so God’s dew will bring the dead back to life.
> This is one of the first places where the Bible speaks clearly about the resurrection of the dead. Later, Daniel does so (Daniel 12:2). Jews and Christians believe in the resurrection of the dead.

---

## Hide yourself for a moment (Verses 20–21)

[20] Come, my people, go into your rooms
and shut your doors behind you!
Hide yourself for a little moment,
until the anger has passed.
[21] For look, the LORD is coming out of his dwelling place
to punish the people of the earth for their guilt.
The earth will also uncover the blood shed on it
and will no longer hide its dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: As in the night of Passover in Egypt, when Israel stayed in their houses until the disaster had passed (Exodus 12:22–23): Hide yourselves just for a short moment. Then it will be over.
> Verse 21: The earth will uncover all the murders that have happened on it. No injustice stays hidden. Innocent blood that has been shed cries out to God (compare Genesis 4:10).
