# Isaiah – Chapter 56
### A house of prayer for all peoples

---

## Foreigners and eunuchs are welcome (Verses 1–8)

[1] This is what the LORD says:
“Hold on to justice and do what is right,
for my salvation is near,
and my righteousness will soon be revealed.
[2] Blessed is the person who does this,
and the human being who holds on to it,
who keeps the Sabbath and does not dishonor it,
and keeps his hand from doing any evil.”
[3] The foreigner who has joined himself to the LORD must not say,
“The LORD will surely separate me from his people.”
And the eunuch must not say,
“Look, I am a dry tree.”
[4] For this is what the LORD says:
“To the eunuchs who keep my Sabbaths
and choose what pleases me
and hold on to my covenant,
[5] I will give in my house and within my walls
a memorial and a name
better than sons and daughters.
I will give them an everlasting name
that will never be cut off.
[6] And the foreigners who join themselves to the LORD
to serve him and to love the name of the LORD,
to be his servants,
everyone who keeps the Sabbath and does not dishonor it,
and who hold on to my covenant,
[7] these I will bring to my holy mountain
and give them joy in my house of prayer.
Their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted on my altar.
For my house will be called a house of prayer for all peoples.”
[8] The Lord GOD, who gathers the outcasts of Israel, says:
“I will gather still others to him,
in addition to those already gathered.”

> **What does this mean?**
> With chapter 56, the third part of the book of Isaiah begins (chapters 56–66). Many scholars think it comes from the time after the return from exile, when Jerusalem was being rebuilt.
> Verse 3: Two groups were afraid that they did not belong: foreigners who believe in the God of Israel, and eunuchs, that is, men who could not father children, often because they had been mutilated as court officials. According to the law in Deuteronomy 23:1 (in some Bibles 23:2), eunuchs were not allowed to enter the assembly. A eunuch felt like a “dry tree” without a future because he had no children.
> Verses 4–5: God answers surprisingly: Whoever is faithful receives a name in my house that is better than sons and daughters. No one is shut out just because their body is different or because they cannot start a family.
> Verses 6–7: Foreigners who love God are also welcome in the temple. “My house will be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Jesus quotes this verse when he drives the traders out of the temple (Mark 11:17).
> In the book of Acts (8:27–39), a eunuch from Ethiopia is baptized. He is a foreigner and a eunuch at the same time, and he belongs.

## Blind watchmen and mute dogs (Verses 9–12)

[9] All you animals of the field, come and eat,
all you animals in the forest!
[10] Its watchmen are blind.
They all know nothing.
They are all mute dogs.
They cannot bark.
They dream, lie around and love to sleep.
[11] Yes, the dogs are greedy.
They never have enough.
They are shepherds who cannot understand.
They have all turned to their own way,
each one to his own gain, from every side.
[12] “Come,” they say, “I will get wine,
and let us fill ourselves with strong drink.
And tomorrow will be like today, great beyond measure.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes a sharp criticism of the leaders of the people, the “watchmen” and “shepherds.”
> Verse 9: The wild animals are invited: Come, here is easy prey! For the flock is unprotected.
> Verse 10: The watchmen are like guard dogs that do not bark. They sleep instead of warning of danger.
> Verses 11–12: They only think of their own advantage and hold drinking parties. They say: “Tomorrow will be like today, great beyond measure.” They do not notice that the people are in danger.
> For today: Whoever is responsible for others, in politics, church, community or family, should be watchful and think of the weak, not of their own gain.
