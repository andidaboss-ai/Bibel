# Isaiah – Chapter 51
### Wake up, arm of the LORD!

---

## Look to Abraham and Sarah (Verses 1–8)

[1] “Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness,
you who seek the LORD!
Look to the rock you were cut from,
and to the quarry you were dug from.
[2] Look to Abraham, your father,
and to Sarah, who gave birth to you.
For when he was only one, I called him.
I blessed him and made him many.
[3] For the LORD has comforted Zion.
He has comforted all her ruins.
He has made her wilderness like Eden
and her desert like the garden of the LORD.
Joy and gladness will be found in her,
thanksgiving and the sound of songs.
[4] Listen to me, my people,
and hear me, my nation!
For teaching will go out from me,
and I will set up my justice as a light for the peoples.
[5] My righteousness is near.
My salvation has gone out,
and my arms will judge the peoples.
The islands will wait for me,
and they will trust in my arm.
[6] Lift up your eyes to the heavens
and look at the earth below.
For the heavens will vanish like smoke,
and the earth will wear out like a garment.
Its inhabitants will die in the same way.
But my salvation will last forever,
and my righteousness will never come to an end.
[7] Listen to me, you who know righteousness,
you people who have my teaching in your heart!
Do not fear the scorn of people,
and do not be terrified by their insults.
[8] For the moth will eat them up like a garment,
and the worm will eat them like wool.
But my righteousness will last forever
and my salvation for all generations.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: The people in exile feel few and weak. God says: Think of Abraham and Sarah! They were only two old people without children. And yet a great people came from them. In the same way, God can make something great out of you again.
> Verse 3: Destroyed Jerusalem will be like the Garden of Eden again, full of joy and music.
> Verse 6: Even heaven and earth are not eternal. But God’s salvation lasts forever.
> Verses 7–8: Whoever carries God’s teaching in their heart does not need to be afraid of people’s mockery. The mockers pass away; God’s righteousness remains.

---

## The redeemed come home (Verses 9–16)

[9] Wake up, wake up, put on strength, arm of the LORD!
Wake up as in the days of old,
in the generations of long ago!
Wasn’t it you who cut Rahab to pieces,
who pierced the monster?
[10] Wasn’t it you who dried up the sea,
the waters of the great deep,
who made the depths of the sea into a road
for the redeemed to cross over?
[11] Those the LORD has set free will return
and come to Zion with singing.
Everlasting joy will be on their heads.
They will receive gladness and joy.
Sorrow and sighing will run away.
[12] “I, I am the one who comforts you.
Who are you, that you are afraid of a human being who will die,
and of a mortal who will wither like grass?
[13] Have you forgotten the LORD, your Maker,
who stretched out the heavens
and laid the foundations of the earth?
Do you live in constant fear all day long
because of the rage of the oppressor
when he gets ready to destroy?
But where is the rage of the oppressor?
[14] The captive exile will soon be set free.
He will not die and go down into the pit.
He will not run out of bread.
[15] For I am the LORD your God,
who stirs up the sea so that its waves roar.
The LORD of Armies is his name.
[16] I have put my words in your mouth
and covered you in the shadow of my hand,
to plant the heavens
and lay the foundations of the earth
and to say to Zion, ‘You are my people.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: The prophet calls out to God: “Wake up!” Like a person who cannot understand why God has been silent for so long. “Rahab” here is not a person, but a name for a sea monster from ancient stories, a picture of chaos and also of Egypt. God has defeated chaos.
> Verse 10: At the exodus from Egypt, God made a way through the sea.
> Verse 11: That is why those who have been set free will come home rejoicing now, too. “Sorrow and sighing will run away.” (The same verse is already found in chapter 35:10.)
> Verses 12–13: God comforts: Why are you so afraid of mortal people? They wither like grass. Where is the rage of the oppressor? It will soon be over.

---

## The cup of staggering (Verses 17–23)

[17] Wake up, wake up!
Stand up, Jerusalem,
you who have drunk from the LORD’s hand
the cup of his anger!
You have drunk the bowl, the cup of staggering,
and drained it.
[18] There is no one to guide her
among all the sons she has given birth to.
And there is no one to take her by the hand
among all the sons she has brought up.
[19] These two things have happened to you
(who will grieve with you?):
ruin and destruction,
famine and the sword.
How can I comfort you?
[20] Your sons have fainted.
They lie at the corner of every street
like an antelope in a net.
They are full of the LORD’s anger,
the rebuke of your God.
[21] So now hear this, you who are suffering,
you who are drunk, but not with wine:
[22] This is what your Lord, the LORD, says,
your God, who stands up for his people:
“Look, I have taken the cup of staggering out of your hand,
the bowl, the cup of my anger.
You will never drink it again.
[23] I will put it into the hand of those who torment you,
who have said to you,
‘Bow down, so that we can walk over you!’
And you made your back like the ground,
like a street for those who walk over it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Jerusalem is described as a woman who has drunk from a cup and now staggers. The cup is a picture of the disaster that came over the city: destruction, hunger and war (verse 19).
> Verses 18 and 20: No one is there to support her. Her children lie exhausted in the streets.
> Verse 22: But now God takes the cup out of her hand: “You will never drink it again.” God himself stands up for his people, like a lawyer.
> Verse 23: The oppressors had forced Jerusalem to throw herself to the ground so they could walk over her. A picture of the deepest humiliation. That time is over. God is on the side of those who have been humiliated.
