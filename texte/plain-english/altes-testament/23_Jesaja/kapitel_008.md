# Isaiah – Chapter 8
### God is with us

---

## A child with a strange name (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD said to me,
“Take a large tablet and write on it in ordinary letters:
‘For Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz.’
[2] And I will take reliable witnesses for myself to testify:
Uriah the priest, and Zechariah the son of Jeberechiah.”
[3] I went to the prophetess,
and she became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
Then the LORD said to me,
“Name him ‘Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz.’
[4] For before the child knows how to say ‘My father’ and ‘My mother,’
the riches of Damascus and the plunder of Samaria
will be carried away by the king of Assyria.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaiah again has a son with a name that carries a message. “Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz” means: “Quick to the plunder, swift to the spoil.”
> The message: Even before the child can say “Daddy” and “Mommy,” that is, in about a year, the two enemies, Syria (Damascus) and the northern kingdom of Israel (Samaria), will be robbed by Assyria. That is what happened in the year 732 BC.
> Verse 3: “The prophetess” is Isaiah’s wife. She too was a prophet.

---

## The flood from Assyria (Verses 5–10)

[5] The LORD spoke to me again and said,
[6] “Because this people has rejected the gently flowing waters of Shiloah
and rejoices over Rezin and the son of Remaliah,
[7] so, look, the Lord is bringing on them
the mighty floodwaters of the River:
the king of Assyria and all his glory.
It will rise over all its channels
and overflow all its banks.
[8] It will sweep on into Judah.
It will overflow and pass through.
It will reach up to the neck.
Its spreading wings will fill the width of your land,
O Immanuel.
[9] Rage, you peoples, and be broken to pieces!
Listen, all you from distant lands:
Get ready for battle, and be shattered!
Get ready for battle, and be shattered!
[10] Make plans together, and they will come to nothing.
Speak a word, and it will not stand,
for God is with us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Shiloah is a small water channel in Jerusalem. The water there flows calmly and quietly. It is a picture of God’s quiet, reliable help. The people reject it.
> Verses 7–8: Instead, a mighty flood comes: the “River” is the Euphrates, a picture for Assyria. The water will come up to Judah’s neck.
> Verses 8 and 10: Twice we hear an echo of “Immanuel”: “O Immanuel” and “God is with us.” That is the hope in the middle of danger: God is with his people. So the plans of the enemies will fail in the end.

## Fear only God (Verses 11–15)

[11] For this is what the LORD said to me when his hand took strong hold of me,
and he warned me not to walk in the way of this people. He said,
[12] “Don’t call everything a conspiracy that this people calls a conspiracy.
Don’t fear what they fear,
and don’t let yourselves be terrified.
[13] The LORD of Armies is the one you must treat as holy.
He is the one you must fear.
He is the one you must stand in awe of.
[14] He will be a sanctuary,
but for both houses of Israel a stone to stumble over
and a rock that makes them fall.
For the people of Jerusalem he will be a trap and a snare.
[15] Many will stumble over it, fall, be broken,
be snared, and be caught.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Back then, many fears and rumors about conspiracies were spreading. God says to Isaiah: Don’t let yourself be infected by them! That sounds very up to date.
> Verse 13: The only “fear” we should have is awe before God. Whoever takes God seriously does not have to be afraid of anything else.
> Verse 14: For those who trust him, God is a safe place. For those who reject him, he becomes a stone they stumble over. The New Testament takes up this picture (Romans 9:33; 1 Peter 2:8).

---

## Waiting for the LORD (Verses 16–22)

[16] Tie up the testimony!
Seal the teaching among my disciples!
[17] I will wait for the LORD,
who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob,
and I will look for him.
[18] Look, I and the children whom the LORD has given me
are signs and wonders in Israel
from the LORD of Armies, who lives on Mount Zion.
[19] When they say to you,
“Ask the spirits of the dead and the fortune-tellers,
who whisper and mutter,”
shouldn’t a people ask their God?
Should they ask the dead on behalf of the living?
[20] Turn to the teaching and to the testimony!
If they do not speak according to this word,
then there is no morning for them.
[21] They will wander through the land, very troubled and hungry.
And it will happen: When they are hungry,
they will worry and curse their king and their God.
They will turn their faces upward,
[22] then look at the earth
and see distress, darkness, and gloomy anguish.
They will be driven out into thick darkness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Because no one wants to listen, Isaiah has his message written down and sealed. His students are to keep it until the time comes. This is probably how the first parts of the book came about.
> Verse 17: A sentence of trust: Even if God seems hidden right now, I will wait for him.
> Verse 18: Isaiah and his children with their message names are themselves living signs.
> Verse 19: In times of trouble, people run to fortune-tellers and people who call up the dead. Isaiah asks: Why do you ask the dead instead of the living God?
> Verses 21–22: Whoever turns away from God ends up in deep darkness. But right after that comes the great light (chapter 9).
