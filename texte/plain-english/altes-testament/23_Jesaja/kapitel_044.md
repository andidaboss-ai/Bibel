# Isaiah – Chapter 44
### I am the first and the last

---

## My Spirit for your children (Verses 1–8)

[1] But listen now, Jacob, my servant,
and Israel, whom I have chosen.
[2] This is what the LORD says, who made you
and formed you from the womb, who will help you:
“Do not be afraid, Jacob, my servant,
and you, Jeshurun, whom I have chosen.
[3] For I will pour water on the thirsty
and streams on the dry ground.
I will pour my Spirit on your descendants
and my blessing on your children.
[4] They will spring up among the grass
like willows by the streams of water.
[5] One will say, ‘I belong to the LORD.’
Another will call himself by the name of Jacob.
And another will write on his hand, ‘The LORD’s,’
and honor the name of Israel.”
[6] This is what the LORD says, the King of Israel,
and his Redeemer, the LORD of Armies:
“I am the first, and I am the last,
and besides me there is no God.
[7] Who is like me? Who will call it out
and announce it and lay it out before me,
since I established the ancient people?
Let them announce what is coming
and what will happen.
[8] Do not fear, and do not be afraid.
Haven’t I announced it to you long ago and shown it?
You are my witnesses.
Is there a God besides me?
No, there is none.
I do not know any other Rock.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “Jeshurun” is a loving name for Israel. It means something like “the upright one” or “the darling” (as in Deuteronomy 32:15 and 33:5).
> Verses 3–4: God pours out his Spirit like water on dry ground. The children will grow like trees by a stream.
> Verse 5: People joyfully declare that they belong to God: “I belong to the LORD.” Some even write it on their hand, as a sign of belonging.
> Verse 6: “I am the first, and I am the last.” God was there before everything and will be there after everything. The book of Revelation takes up these words (Revelation 1:17 and 22:13).
> Verse 8: God is the only “Rock” you can rely on.

---

## How foolish it is to make an idol! (Verses 9–20)

[9] All who make a carved idol are worthless.
The things they delight in are of no use.
Their own witnesses do not see or know,
so they will be put to shame.
[10] Who has shaped a god
or cast an idol that is good for nothing?
[11] Look, all his companions will be put to shame,
and the craftsmen are only human.
Let them all gather together. Let them stand up.
They will be afraid.
They will be put to shame together.
[12] The blacksmith takes an ax,
works it in the coals,
shapes it with hammers
and works it with his strong arm.
He gets hungry, and his strength fails.
He drinks no water and grows faint.
[13] The carpenter stretches out a measuring line.
He marks it out with a pencil.
He shapes it with planes.
He marks it out with a compass
and shapes it in the form of a man,
with the beauty of a human being,
so that it can live in a house.
[14] He cuts down cedars for himself,
and takes a cypress and an oak,
and lets one grow strong among the trees of the forest.
He plants a cypress tree, and the rain makes it grow.
[15] Then it is used by people for burning.
He takes some of it and warms himself.
Yes, he lights a fire and bakes bread.
Yes, he makes a god from it and worships it.
He makes a carved idol from it and bows down before it.
[16] He burns part of it in the fire.
With part of it he eats meat.
He roasts a roast and is full.
Yes, he warms himself and says, “Aha! I am warm. I have seen the fire.”
[17] The rest of it he makes into a god, his carved idol.
He bows down before it and worships it,
and prays to it and says,
“Save me, for you are my god!”
[18] They do not know and do not understand,
for he has shut their eyes, so that they cannot see,
and their hearts, so that they cannot understand.
[19] No one stops to think,
and there is no knowledge or understanding to say,
“I burned part of it in the fire.
Yes, I also baked bread on its coals.
I roasted meat and ate it.
Shall I make the rest of it into something detestable?
Shall I bow down to a block of wood?”
[20] He feeds on ashes.
A deceived heart has led him astray.
He cannot save his soul
or say, “Isn’t there a lie in my right hand?”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most mocking passages in the Bible. Isaiah describes very precisely how an idol is made:
> Verse 12: The blacksmith works so hard that he gets hungry and thirsty. A god whose maker grows tired!
> Verses 14–17: A man cuts down a tree. With one half he heats his home and roasts his meat. From the other half he carves a god and prays: “Save me!”
> Verses 19–20: Doesn’t he notice that he is bowing down to a block of wood? He is holding a lie in his hand.
> For us today: We probably do not carve gods out of wood. But today, too, people can turn things they have made themselves into their “god,” for example money, success or reputation, and expect to be saved by them.

## I have redeemed you (Verses 21–23)

[21] Remember these things, Jacob and Israel,
for you are my servant.
I have formed you. You are my servant.
Israel, I will not forget you.
[22] I have blotted out your rebellious acts like a thick cloud
and your sins like a cloud.
Return to me, for I have redeemed you.
[23] Sing, you heavens, for the LORD has done it!
Shout, you depths of the earth!
Break out into singing, you mountains,
you forest and all your trees,
for the LORD has redeemed Jacob
and will show his glory in Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: “I will not forget you.” The idols have to be made by people. But Israel is made by God, and he does not forget it.
> Verse 22: The sins are gone like a cloud that disappears. First comes forgiveness, then the invitation: “Return to me!” God does not wait until the people improve.
> Verse 23: The whole creation is to shout for joy about this: heaven, earth, mountains and trees.

## Cyrus, my shepherd (Verses 24–28)

[24] The LORD, your Redeemer,
who formed you from the womb, says:
“I am the LORD, who makes all things,
who alone stretches out the heavens,
who spreads out the earth all by myself,
[25] who makes the signs of liars fail
and makes fools of fortune-tellers,
who turns the wise back
and makes their knowledge foolish,
[26] who confirms the word of his servant
and carries out the plans of his messengers,
who says of Jerusalem, ‘She will be lived in,’
and of the cities of Judah, ‘They will be built,’
and, ‘I will raise up their ruins again,’
[27] who says to the deep, ‘Dry up!’
and, ‘I will dry up your rivers,’
[28] who says of Cyrus, ‘He is my shepherd,
and he will carry out everything I want,’
and says of Jerusalem, ‘She will be built,’
and of the temple, ‘Your foundation will be laid.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: Babylon was famous for its astrologers and fortune-tellers. God says: Their predictions will turn out to be false.
> Verses 26–28: But God keeps his word. Jerusalem and the temple will be rebuilt.
> Verse 28: Here the name is mentioned for the first time: Cyrus, the king of the Persians. God calls him “my shepherd,” even though he is a foreign king who does not know God. In the year 538 BC, Cyrus really did allow the Jews to return home and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1–4).
> Many scholars think that this part of the book of Isaiah was written at the time of Cyrus by a later prophet in Isaiah’s tradition. Others, especially in Jewish and Christian tradition, see here a prediction by Isaiah long before the time of Cyrus.
