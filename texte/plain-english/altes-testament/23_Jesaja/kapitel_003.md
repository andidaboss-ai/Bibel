# Isaiah – Chapter 3
### Judgment on Jerusalem

---

## Chaos among the leaders (Verses 1–12)

[1] For look, the Lord, the LORD of Armies,
is taking away from Jerusalem and from Judah
supply and support,
the whole supply of bread and the whole supply of water,
[2] the hero and the soldier,
the judge and the prophet,
the fortune-teller and the elder,
[3] the captain of fifty and the respected man,
the adviser, the skilled craftsman,
and the clever magician.
[4] “I will give them boys as their princes,
and children will rule over them.
[5] The people will be oppressed, one by another,
and each by his neighbor.
The young will rise up against the old,
and the low against the respected.
[6] Yes, a man will grab his brother in his father’s house and say,
‘You still have a coat, you be our leader,
and let this heap of ruins be in your hands!’
[7] On that day he will cry out,
‘I will not be a healer,
for in my house there is neither bread nor clothing.
Don’t make me leader of the people!’”
[8] For Jerusalem is ruined, and Judah has fallen,
because their tongue and their deeds are against the LORD,
to defy his glorious eyes.
[9] The look on their faces testifies against them.
They show off their sin like Sodom.
They don’t hide it.
How terrible for their souls!
For they have brought disaster on themselves.
[10] Tell the righteous that it will go well with them,
for they will eat the fruit of their deeds.
[11] How terrible for the wicked!
Disaster is coming on them,
for the deeds of their hands will be paid back to them.
[12] As for my people: children are their oppressors,
and women rule over them.
My people, your leaders lead you astray
and destroy the way of your paths.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: God takes away from the land everything it relies on: food, water, soldiers, judges, advisers, craftsmen.
> Verses 4–7: Without good leaders, chaos breaks out. Inexperienced, immature people come to power. No one wants to take responsibility anymore: “I have nothing myself, don’t make me leader!”
> Verses 10–11: A comfort in the middle of judgment: It will go well with the righteous.
> Verse 12: “Children” and “women rule”: Back then, this was seen as a sign that the order of things was upside down, because women usually held no public office. It describes the ideas of that time, not a judgment on whether women can lead well. The Bible also tells of strong women leaders like Deborah (Judges 4–5).
> The real accusation comes at the end: “Your leaders lead you astray.”

---

## God accuses the powerful (Verses 13–15)

[13] The LORD stands up to argue his case,
and he stands to judge the peoples.
[14] The LORD comes to judgment with the elders of his people and with their princes:
“It is you who have eaten up the vineyard.
What you stole from the poor is in your houses.
[15] How dare you crush my people
and grind the faces of the poor?”
says the Lord, the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> God appears like a prosecutor in court. The accused are the powerful, the elders and princes.
> Their crime: They have robbed the poor. What belongs to the poor is now in their houses.
> “Grind the faces of the poor”: a harsh picture for brutal oppression. God calls the poor “my people.” He is on their side.

---

## Against the pride of the daughters of Zion (Verses 16–26)

[16] The LORD also said,
“Because the daughters of Zion are proud
and walk with their necks stretched out,
with flirting eyes,
walking with little steps
and jingling the chains on their feet,
[17] the Lord will cover the heads of the women of Zion with sores,
and the LORD will make their heads bald.”
[18] On that day, the Lord will take away
the finery of their anklets, the headbands, the crescent necklaces,
[19] the earrings, the bracelets, the veils,
[20] the headdresses, the ankle chains, the sashes,
the perfume bottles, the charms,
[21] the signet rings, the nose rings,
[22] the fine robes, the capes, the cloaks, the purses,
[23] the hand mirrors, the fine linen clothes,
the tiaras, and the shawls.
[24] And it will happen:
Instead of sweet scent, there will be rot,
instead of a belt, a rope,
instead of a beautiful hairstyle, baldness,
instead of a fine robe, sackcloth,
and instead of beauty, a brand.
[25] Your men will fall by the sword,
and your heroes in war.
[26] Her gates will lament and mourn.
She will sit on the ground, ruined.

> **What does this mean?**
> Isaiah criticizes the rich women of Jerusalem. They strut through the city with expensive jewelry and clothes while the poor suffer (Verse 15). Their luxury is paid for with the misery of the poor.
> Verses 18–23: A long list of jewelry and clothes, almost like a catalog. This shows how much wealth there was.
> Verse 24: When war comes, they lose everything. Instead of perfume, a stench; instead of jewelry, a rope. “A brand”: Back then, prisoners of war were branded.
> Important: The criticism is not aimed at women or at beauty, but at pride and luxury at the expense of the poor. Isaiah criticizes the men in the same way (Verses 14–15).
> Verses 25–26: War will strike everyone. In the end, Jerusalem sits mourning on the ground like a widow.
