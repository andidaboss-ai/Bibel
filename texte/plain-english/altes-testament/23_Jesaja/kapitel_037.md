# Isaiah – Chapter 37
### Hezekiah’s prayer and the rescue of Jerusalem

---

## Hezekiah sends to Isaiah (Verses 1–7)

[1] When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his clothes,
covered himself with sackcloth,
and went into the house of the LORD.
[2] He sent Eliakim, who was in charge of the palace,
and Shebna the scribe,
and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth,
to the prophet Isaiah, the son of Amoz.
[3] They said to him,
“This is what Hezekiah says:
‘Today is a day of trouble, of rebuke and of disgrace,
for the children have come to the moment of birth,
but there is no strength to give birth.
[4] Perhaps the LORD your God will hear the words of Rabshakeh,
whom his master, the king of Assyria, has sent
to mock the living God,
and will punish the words that the LORD your God has heard.
So lift up your prayer for the remnant that is still left.’”
[5] So the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.
[6] Isaiah said to them,
“Tell your master:
‘This is what the LORD says:
“Do not be afraid of the words you have heard,
with which the servants of the king of Assyria have insulted me.
[7] Look, I will put a spirit in him,
and he will hear news
and return to his own land.
I will make him fall by the sword in his own land.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah reacts very differently from his father Ahaz (chapter 7). Instead of relying on alliances, he goes into the temple and asks Isaiah to pray.
> Verse 3: A powerful picture: like a birth in which the mother has no strength left. The situation seems hopeless.
> Verse 6: God’s answer through Isaiah: “Do not be afraid!”

---

## A threatening letter (Verses 8–13)

[8] Rabshakeh returned
and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah,
for he had heard that the king had left Lachish.
[9] The king heard news about Tirhakah, the king of Ethiopia:
“He has come out to fight against you.”
When he heard this, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,
[10] “This is what you shall say to Hezekiah, the king of Judah:
‘Do not let your God, whom you trust, deceive you
when he says, “Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.”
[11] Look, you have heard what the kings of Assyria
have done to all the lands,
destroying them completely.
And will you be rescued?
[12] Did the gods of the nations rescue them,
the nations my ancestors destroyed:
Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden who were in Telassar?
[13] Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad,
and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena and Ivvah?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Sennacherib has to fight against an army from Cush and Egypt that is coming under King Tirhakah. The WEB, our English source text, says “Ethiopia.” That is why he sends a threatening letter to Jerusalem: Don’t think your God can save you!
> He lists the many cities and kings that Assyria has already defeated. He wants to frighten Hezekiah.

---

## Hezekiah’s prayer (Verses 14–20)

[14] Hezekiah took the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it.
Then Hezekiah went up to the house of the LORD
and spread it out before the LORD.
[15] Hezekiah prayed to the LORD and said,
[16] “LORD of Armies, God of Israel,
you who are enthroned among the cherubim,
you alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth.
You have made heaven and earth.
[17] Turn your ear, LORD, and hear!
Open your eyes, LORD, and see!
Hear all the words of Sennacherib,
who has sent to mock the living God.
[18] It is true, LORD,
the kings of Assyria have destroyed all the countries and their land
[19] and have thrown their gods into the fire,
for they were not gods,
but the work of human hands, wood and stone.
That is why they destroyed them.
[20] So now, LORD our God, save us from his hand,
so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know
that you are the LORD, you alone.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: A moving picture: Hezekiah takes the threatening letter and lays it out in the temple before God. In this way he says: God, look at this! This is an example for prayer: You may “spread out” your worries, even a letter, very concretely before God.
> Verses 16–20: Hezekiah admits that Assyria has defeated many countries. But the gods of these countries were only wood and stone. The God of Israel is the living God, the Creator.
> Verse 20: Hezekiah does not only ask for his own rescue, but that all nations may know God.

## God’s answer to Sennacherib’s pride (Verses 21–35)

[21] Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent a message to Hezekiah:
“This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘Because you have prayed to me about Sennacherib, the king of Assyria,
[22] this is the word the LORD has spoken about him:
The virgin daughter of Zion despises you and laughs at you.
The daughter of Jerusalem shakes her head at you.
[23] Whom have you mocked and insulted?
Against whom have you raised your voice
and lifted your eyes in pride?
Against the Holy One of Israel!
[24] Through your servants you have mocked the Lord
and have said,
“With my many chariots I have climbed to the tops of the mountains,
to the innermost parts of Lebanon.
I will cut down its tall cedars
and its finest cypress trees.
I will enter its farthest height,
the forest of its fruitful field.
[25] I have dug wells and drunk water,
and with the soles of my feet I will dry up all the rivers of Egypt.”
[26] Have you not heard that I did it long ago
and planned it in ancient times?
Now I have let it happen
that you could destroy fortified cities
and turn them into heaps of ruins.
[27] That is why their inhabitants had little strength.
They were dismayed and ashamed.
They were like the grass of the field and like green plants,
like the grass on the rooftops
and like a field before the grain has grown.
[28] But I know when you sit down,
when you go out and when you come in,
and how you rage against me.
[29] Because you rage against me
and your arrogance has reached my ears,
I will put my hook in your nose
and my bridle in your mouth,
and I will turn you back on the road by which you came.
[30] This will be the sign for you:
This year you will eat what grows by itself,
and in the second year what springs up from that.
But in the third year sow and harvest,
plant vineyards and eat their fruit.
[31] The remnant of the house of Judah that has escaped
will again put down roots below
and bear fruit above.
[32] For a remnant will go out from Jerusalem,
and survivors from Mount Zion.
The zeal of the LORD of Armies will do this.’
[33] Therefore this is what the LORD says about the king of Assyria:
‘He will not come into this city
or shoot an arrow there.
He will not come before it with a shield
or build up a siege ramp against it.
[34] He will go back by the road he came,
and he will not come into this city,’
says the LORD.
[35] ‘For I will defend this city to save it,
for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: Jerusalem is pictured as a young woman who shakes her head at the boasting Sennacherib and laughs.
> Verses 24–25: Sennacherib boasts: I have conquered the highest mountains, I even dry up the rivers of Egypt!
> Verses 26–27: God’s answer: You only managed all this because I allowed it. You are only a tool.
> Verse 29: A humiliating picture: God puts a hook in Sennacherib’s nose like an animal and leads him back. The Assyrians themselves often led prisoners away like this.
> Verses 30–32: A sign for Hezekiah: For two years they eat what grows by itself; in the third year, they sow normally again. The remnant of Judah will put down roots again and bear fruit.
> Verses 33–35: Sennacherib will not shoot a single arrow at Jerusalem.

## The rescue of Jerusalem (Verses 36–38)

[36] Then the angel of the LORD went out
and struck down 185,000 men in the camp of the Assyrians.
When people got up early in the morning,
look, they were all dead bodies.
[37] So Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, broke camp,
went away, returned to Nineveh and stayed there.
[38] As he was worshiping in the house of his god Nisroch,
his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer struck him down with the sword.
They escaped into the land of Ararat.
And his son Esar Haddon became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 36: In a single night, a large part of the Assyrian army dies. The Bible says: The angel of the LORD struck them down. Some think of a plague in the camp. The Greek historian Herodotus tells of a sudden disaster in the Assyrian army in Egypt. The Bible sees God stepping in.
> The Assyrian inscriptions report that Sennacherib shut Hezekiah up in Jerusalem “like a bird in a cage.” They do not report a conquest of Jerusalem. Jerusalem was not taken.
> Verse 38: About 20 years later, Sennacherib was murdered by two of his sons, of all times while praying to his god. Assyrian sources also report this. Isaiah’s announcement (verse 7) came true.
> The story shows: God keeps his promises. Whoever trusts him will not be disappointed.
