# Isaiah – Chapter 45
### Cyrus, the LORD’s anointed

---

## I have called you by your name, Cyrus (Verses 1–8)

[1] This is what the LORD says to his anointed, to Cyrus,
whose right hand I have held
to bring nations down before him
and strip kings of their armor,
to open the doors before him,
so that the gates will not stay shut:
[2] “I will go before you
and make the rough places smooth.
I will break the doors of bronze in pieces
and cut through the bars of iron.
[3] I will give you the treasures of darkness
and hidden riches from secret places,
so that you may know that it is I, the LORD,
who calls you by your name, the God of Israel.
[4] For the sake of Jacob, my servant,
and Israel, my chosen one,
I have called you by your name.
I have given you a title of honor,
even though you have not known me.
[5] I am the LORD, and there is no one else.
Besides me there is no God.
I will strengthen you,
even though you have not known me,
[6] so that people may know from the rising of the sun
and from the west that there is no one besides me.
I am the LORD, and there is no one else.
[7] I form the light and create darkness.
I make peace and create disaster.
I am the LORD, who does all these things.
[8] Rain down, you heavens, from above,
and let the skies pour down righteousness.
Let the earth open up so that it produces salvation,
and let it make righteousness spring up with it.
I, the LORD, have created it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Something truly astonishing: God calls the Persian king Cyrus his “anointed.” In Hebrew this is “Mashiach,” the word that “Messiah” comes from. Otherwise, only the kings and priests of Israel were called this. A foreign king who does not know God (verses 4–5) becomes God’s tool to set Israel free.
> Verse 3: “Treasures of darkness” are hidden treasures in treasure chambers. Cyrus conquered rich cities like Sardis and Babylon.
> Verse 7: A difficult verse: God creates light and darkness, peace and disaster. Isaiah wants to say: There is no second god, no god of darkness next to the god of light, as the Persians believed. Everything lies in the hand of the one God. This does not mean that God enjoys misfortune. In the Jewish morning prayer, this verse is prayed in a changed form: God is the one *who makes peace and creates all things*.
> Verse 8: A beautiful picture: Righteousness falls from heaven like rain and grows out of the earth like plants. The old Latin version of this verse (“Rorate caeli,” “Drop down, you heavens”) is sung during Advent.

## The clay and the potter (Verses 9–13)

[9] How terrible for the one who argues with his Maker,
a clay pot among the clay pots of the earth!
Shall the clay say to the one who shapes it, ‘What are you making?’
or your work say, ‘He has no hands’?
[10] How terrible for the one who says to a father, ‘What are you fathering?’
or to a mother, ‘What are you giving birth to?’”
[11] The LORD, the Holy One of Israel and his Maker, says:
“You ask me about the things that are to come,
about my sons,
and you want to give me orders about the work of my hands!
[12] I have made the earth
and created human beings on it.
I, my own hands, stretched out the heavens.
I commanded all their army.
[13] I have stirred him up in righteousness,
and I will make all his ways straight.
He will build my city,
and he will let my exiles go free,
not for a price and not for a reward,”
says the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Some among the people were probably outraged: A foreign king is supposed to be our savior? That cannot be God’s plan!
> Verses 9–10: Isaiah answers with a picture: A clay pot cannot tell the potter what to do. A child cannot blame its parents for having given birth to it.
> Verse 13: Cyrus will have Jerusalem rebuilt and set the exiles free, without a ransom. That is really what happened (Ezra 1).

## Turn to me, all the ends of the earth (Verses 14–25)

[14] This is what the LORD says:
“The produce of Egypt, and the goods of Ethiopia,
and the Sabeans, tall men,
will come over to you, and they will be yours.
They will follow behind you.
They will come over in chains.
They will bow down before you.
They will plead with you:
‘Surely God is with you, and there is no one else.
There is no other god.
[15] Truly, you are a God who hides himself,
God of Israel, the Savior.’”
[16] They will be disappointed, yes, put to shame, all of them.
The makers of idols will go off in disgrace together.
[17] Israel will be saved by the LORD
with an everlasting salvation.
You will not be disappointed or put to shame
forever and ever.
[18] For this is what the LORD says, who created the heavens,
the God who formed the earth and made it,
who established it
and did not create it to be empty,
who formed it to be lived in:
“I am the LORD. There is no other.
[19] I have not spoken in secret,
in some place in a land of darkness.
I did not say to the descendants of Jacob,
‘Seek me in vain!’
I, the LORD, speak righteousness.
I announce what is right.
[20] Gather together and come!
Come near together, you who have escaped from the nations!
Those who carry around the wood of their carved idol have no knowledge,
and they pray to a god that cannot save.
[21] Announce it and present it!
Yes, let them consult with each other.
Who told of this from ancient times?
Who announced it long ago?
Wasn’t it I, the LORD?
There is no other God besides me,
a righteous God and a Savior.
There is no one besides me.
[22] Look to me and be saved,
all the ends of the earth!
For I am God, and there is no other.
[23] I have sworn by myself.
The word has gone out of my mouth in righteousness
and will not be taken back:
To me every knee will bow,
every tongue will swear loyalty.
[24] They will say about me,
‘Only in the LORD is there righteousness and strength.’”
People will come to him.
All who raged against him will be disappointed.
[25] All the descendants of Israel will be declared righteous in the LORD
and will rejoice!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Nations from the south (Egypt, Cush and Sheba, the land of the Sabeans) come to Israel and recognize: The true God is with you. For Cush, the WEB, our English source text, says “Ethiopia” here. The picture with the chains comes from the language of that time, when defeated nations had to hand over prisoners. But the tone of the passage is not violence, but that all nations will recognize the one God. The Bible does not justify oppressing people.
> Verse 15: “You are a God who hides himself.” God is not easy to see or to understand. Many people know this experience.
> Verse 18: God did not create the earth to be an empty desert, but so that people could live there.
> Verse 22: An invitation to the whole world: “Look to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth!” God’s salvation is not only for Israel, but for all nations.
> Verse 23: “To me every knee will bow.” Paul takes up these words in his letter to the Philippians (2:10–11) and applies them to Jesus. In Judaism, the thought that one day all nations will acknowledge the one God is part of the prayer “Aleinu.”
