# Isaiah – Chapter 60
### Arise, shine!

---

## Your light has come (Verses 1–9)

[1] “Arise, shine, for your light has come,
and the glory of the LORD has risen on you!
[2] For look, darkness will cover the earth,
and thick darkness the peoples.
But the LORD will rise on you,
and his glory will be seen on you.
[3] Nations will come to your light,
and kings to the brightness of your rising.
[4] Lift up your eyes and look around:
They all gather together; they come to you.
Your sons will come from far away,
and your daughters will be carried in arms.
[5] Then you will see it and shine with joy,
and your heart will tremble and swell,
because the riches of the sea will be turned to you.
The wealth of the nations will come to you.
[6] Crowds of camels will cover you,
the young camels of Midian and Ephah.
All from Sheba will come.
They will bring gold and frankincense
and will announce the praises of the LORD.
[7] All the flocks of Kedar will be gathered to you.
The rams of Nebaioth will serve you.
They will be accepted as offerings on my altar,
and I will make my glorious house beautiful.
[8] Who are these who fly along like a cloud,
and like doves to their nesting holes?
[9] Surely the islands will wait for me,
and the ships of Tarshish first,
to bring your sons from far away,
their silver and their gold with them,
for the name of the LORD your God
and for the Holy One of Israel,
because he has given you glory.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: A shining beginning: “Arise, shine!” The world lies in darkness, but God’s light rises over Jerusalem like the sun. And the nations come to this light. In many churches, this text is read at the feast of Epiphany (January 6).
> Verse 4: The scattered children come home; the little ones are carried.
> Verse 6: Nations from Arabia bring “gold and frankincense.” This reminds Christians of the wise men from the East who brought gold, frankincense and myrrh to the child Jesus (Matthew 2:11). The tradition that the wise men were kings and came on camels also comes from here and from Psalm 72.
> Verse 8: A beautiful picture: The ships with those returning home look like a cloud of doves flying back to their dovecote.

---

## Your gates are always open (Verses 10–16)

[10] Foreigners will rebuild your walls,
and their kings will serve you.
For in my anger I struck you,
but in my favor I have had mercy on you.
[11] Your gates will always stand open.
They will not be shut by day or by night,
so that people can bring you the wealth of the nations,
with their kings led in procession.
[12] For the nation and the kingdom that will not serve you
will perish.
Yes, those nations will be completely destroyed.
[13] The glory of Lebanon will come to you,
the cypress, the pine and the box tree together,
to make the place of my sanctuary beautiful.
And I will make the place where my feet rest glorious.
[14] The sons of those who oppressed you will come bowing to you,
and all who despised you
will bow down at the soles of your feet.
They will call you “City of the LORD,
Zion of the Holy One of Israel.”
[15] Instead of being abandoned and hated,
so that no one passed through you,
I will make you an everlasting pride,
a joy for many generations.
[16] You will also drink the milk of the nations
and nurse at the breasts of kings.
Then you will know that I, the LORD, am your Savior,
your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: God says: My anger was short; my mercy is great.
> Verse 11: The gates of Jerusalem stand open day and night. No one has to be afraid of enemies anymore. The book of Revelation takes up this picture for the new Jerusalem (21:25–26).
> Verses 12 and 14: Here the language of that time can be heard: The nations that oppressed Israel will now bow before it. Things are turned around. This is a hope of the oppressed for justice, not a justification for ruling over other nations.
> Verse 16: A loving picture: Jerusalem is cared for like a baby nursed by queens.

---

## The LORD is your everlasting light (Verses 17–22)

[17] Instead of bronze I will bring gold,
instead of iron, silver,
instead of wood, bronze,
and instead of stones, iron.
I will also make peace your government
and righteousness your ruler.
[18] Violence will no longer be heard of in your land,
nor ruin or destruction within your borders.
But you will call your walls “Salvation”
and your gates “Praise.”
[19] The sun will no longer be your light by day,
and the brightness of the moon will no longer shine on you.
But the LORD will be your everlasting light,
and your God will be your glory.
[20] Your sun will never set again,
and your moon will no longer fade.
For the LORD will be your everlasting light,
and the days of your mourning will come to an end.
[21] Then your people will all be righteous.
They will inherit the land forever,
the shoot that I planted,
the work of my hands, so that I may be glorified.
[22] The smallest one will become a thousand,
and the least one a strong nation.
I, the LORD, will do it quickly when the time comes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Everything becomes better and more valuable. And the city is not ruled by tyrants, but by “peace” and “righteousness.”
> Verse 18: A world without violence! The walls are called “Salvation” and the gates “Praise.” What a hope for everyone who suffers under war and violence.
> Verses 19–20: God himself will be the light, a light that never sets. “The days of your mourning will come to an end.” The book of Revelation also takes up this picture (21:23; 22:5).
> Verse 22: The small remnant becomes a great people. God does it when the time has come.
