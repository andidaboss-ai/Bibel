# Numbers – Chapter 3
### The Levites and their tasks

---

## The sons of Aaron (Verses 1–4)

[1] This is the history of the descendants of Aaron and Moses,
at the time when the LORD spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai.
[2] These are the names of the sons of Aaron:
Nadab, the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.
[3] These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the priests who were anointed.
Anointed means: oil was poured on them to set them apart for God.
Aaron had made them holy so that they would serve as priests.
[4] Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD
when they brought strange fire before the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai.
They had no children.
Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests at the side of their father Aaron.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the death of Nadab and Abihu is remembered (Leviticus 10). They had no children. That is why all later priests come from Eleazar and Ithamar.

---

## The Levites serve the priests (Verses 5–10)

[5] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[6] “Bring the tribe of Levi here and put them before Aaron the priest.
They shall serve him.
[7] They shall carry out their duties for him and for the whole community in front of the Tent of Meeting.
They shall do the work of the Tabernacle.
[8] They shall take care of all the equipment of the Tent of Meeting.
They shall carry out the duties for the people of Israel
and do the work of the Tabernacle.
[9] You shall give the Levites to Aaron and to his sons.
They are given to him completely, from among the people of Israel.
[10] You shall appoint Aaron and his sons, so that they do their work as priests.
But a stranger who comes near shall be put to death.”

> **What does this mean?**
> There are two groups in the tribe of Levi: Only Aaron and his descendants are priests. They bring the sacrifices at the altar. The other Levites help them. They carry the sanctuary, set it up, take it down and guard it.
> “A stranger” here means: someone who is not a priest and still wants to do the work of a priest.

---

## The Levites instead of the firstborn (Verses 11–13)

[11] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[12] “Look: I have taken the Levites from among the people of Israel
instead of all the firstborn among the people of Israel, who are the first to come from their mother’s womb.
The Levites shall be mine.
[13] For all the firstborn are mine.
On the day when I struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt,
I made all the firstborn in Israel holy for myself, both people and animals.
They shall be mine. I am the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> When God freed Israel from Egypt, he spared the firstborn of Israel (Exodus 12). Since then, they belong to God.
> Now God takes the Levites in their place. Instead of every family giving its oldest son to serve God, a whole tribe serves for everyone.

---

## The census of the Levites (Verses 14–20)

[14] The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai.
He said:
[15] “Count the descendants of Levi by their fathers’ houses and by their families.
You shall count every male from one month old and up.”
[16] Moses counted them by the word of the LORD, as he had been commanded.
[17] These were the sons of Levi by their names: Gershon, Kohath and Merari.
[18] These are the names of the sons of Gershon by their families: Libni and Shimei.
[19] The sons of Kohath by their families: Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel.
[20] The sons of Merari by their families: Mahli and Mushi.
These are the families of the Levites by their fathers’ houses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites are counted differently from the other tribes: not from 20 years old, but already from one month old. For this is not about soldiers. It is about a replacement for all the firstborn.
> Moses and Aaron are descendants of Amram, the son of Kohath (Exodus 6:20).

---

## The Gershonites (Verses 21–26)

[21] From Gershon came the family of the Libnites and the family of the Shimeites.
These are the families of the Gershonites.
[22] Those of them who were counted, by the number of all the males from one month old and up, were 7,500.
[23] The families of the Gershonites shall camp behind the Tabernacle, on the west side.
[24] The leader of the fathers’ house of the Gershonites shall be Eliasaph, the son of Lael.
[25] The duty of the sons of Gershon at the Tent of Meeting shall be:
the Tabernacle, the tent, its covering,
the curtain for the door of the Tent of Meeting,
[26] the hangings of the courtyard,
the curtain for the door of the courtyard, which is around the Tabernacle and the altar,
and its ropes, for all the work on it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Gershonites are responsible for everything made of cloth and leather: the coverings, the curtains and the hangings.

---

## The Kohathites (Verses 27–32)

[27] From Kohath came the family of the Amramites, the family of the Izharites,
the family of the Hebronites and the family of the Uzzielites.
These are the families of the Kohathites.
[28] By the number of all the males from one month old and up, there were 8,600.
They carried out the duties of the sanctuary.
[29] The families of the sons of Kohath shall camp on the south side of the Tabernacle.
[30] The leader of the fathers’ house of the families of the Kohathites shall be Elizaphan, the son of Uzziel.
[31] Their duty shall be:
the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars,
the holy objects of the sanctuary that are used for the service,
the curtain, and all the work on them.
[32] Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, shall be the chief leader of the leaders of the Levites.
He shall be in charge of those who carry out the duties of the sanctuary.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Kohathites have the holiest task: They carry the ark of the covenant and the other holy objects. You can read more about it in Chapter 4.

---

## The Merarites (Verses 33–37)

[33] From Merari came the family of the Mahlites and the family of the Mushites.
These are the families of Merari.
[34] Those of them who were counted, by the number of all the males from one month old and up, were 6,200.
[35] The leader of the fathers’ house of the families of Merari was Zuriel, the son of Abihail.
They shall camp on the north side of the Tabernacle.
[36] The duty of the sons of Merari shall be:
the boards of the Tabernacle, its crossbars, its posts, its bases, all its equipment and all the work on them,
[37] the posts of the courtyard all around, their bases, their tent pegs and their ropes.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Merarites carry the heavy parts: boards, bars, posts and bases. This is the “frame” of the sanctuary.
> Every group has its own task. None is unimportant. Without the boards there is no tent. Without the coverings there is no protection. Without the ark there is no sanctuary.

---

## Moses and Aaron in the east (Verses 38–39)

[38] In front of the Tabernacle, in the east, in front of the Tent of Meeting toward the sunrise,
Moses, Aaron and his sons shall camp.
They shall carry out the duties of the sanctuary, as a service for the people of Israel.
An outsider who comes near shall be put to death.
[39] All the Levites who were counted, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the command of the LORD,
by their families, all the males from one month old and up, were 22,000.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses and the priests camp in the east, right at the entrance of the sanctuary. They guard the most important way in.
> If you add up the numbers (7,500 + 8,600 + 6,200), you get 22,300, not 22,000. Probably a mistake happened when the text was copied. Some scholars think that the Kohathites originally had 8,300 instead of 8,600. Then the total would be exactly right. The English text we follow keeps the Hebrew text with 8,600.

---

## The exchange for the firstborn (Verses 40–51)

[40] The LORD said to Moses:
“Count all the firstborn males of the people of Israel, from one month old and up,
and find out the number of their names.
[41] You shall take the Levites for me – I am the LORD –
instead of all the firstborn among the people of Israel.
And take the livestock of the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the livestock of the people of Israel.”
[42] Moses counted all the firstborn among the people of Israel, as the LORD had commanded him.
[43] All the firstborn males, by the number of names, from one month old and up,
were 22,273.

[44] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[45] “Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the people of Israel,
and the livestock of the Levites instead of their livestock.
The Levites shall be mine. I am the LORD.
[46] There are 273 firstborn of the people of Israel who are more than the number of the Levites.
They must be bought free.
[47] For this, you shall take five shekels for each one.
You shall take them by the shekel of the sanctuary. One shekel is 20 gerahs.
[48] And you shall give the money with which the rest of them are bought free to Aaron and to his sons.”
[49] Moses took the money for buying them free from those who were more than the ones bought free by the Levites.
[50] He took the money from the firstborn of the people of Israel: 1,365 shekels, by the shekel of the sanctuary.
[51] And Moses gave the money for buying them free to Aaron and to his sons,
by the word of the LORD, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> There are 22,273 firstborn, but only 22,000 Levites. For 273 firstborn there is no Levite to take their place. That is why five shekels are paid for each of them, about 2 ounces of silver. Together that is 1,365 shekels (273 × 5).
> Five shekels is exactly the amount with which a firstborn son is still “bought free” in Judaism today. The celebration is called “Pidyon HaBen,” the redemption of the son. It takes place one month after the birth.
> So every firstborn is represented before God. Nobody is forgotten.
