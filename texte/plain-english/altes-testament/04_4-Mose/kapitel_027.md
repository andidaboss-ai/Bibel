# Numbers – Chapter 27
### The daughters of Zelophehad – Joshua becomes Moses’ successor

---

## Five sisters ask for their inheritance (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then the daughters of Zelophehad came forward.
Zelophehad was the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh.
He was from the families of Manasseh, the son of Joseph.
These are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah.
[2] They stood before Moses, before Eleazar the priest,
before the leaders and before the whole community, at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.
They said,
[3] “Our father died in the wilderness.
He was not part of the group that joined together with Korah against the LORD.
He died because of his own sin.
And he had no sons.
[4] Why should our father’s name disappear from his family, just because he had no son?
Give us property among our father’s brothers!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, only sons inherited the family’s land. If a man had no sons, his land went to other relatives. His name and his family disappeared.
> Five sisters do not want to accept that. They bravely step in front of the whole assembly, before Moses, the priest and all the leaders. For women at that time, this was very unusual and brave.
> They argue wisely: Our father was not a rebel like Korah. He died like every one of his generation. He does not deserve to have his name wiped out.

---

## God says they are right (Verses 5–11)

[5] Moses brought their case before the LORD.
[6] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[7] “The daughters of Zelophehad are right.
You must surely give them property as an inheritance among their father’s brothers.
You must let their father’s inheritance pass on to them.
[8] And speak to the people of Israel. Say this:
‘If a man dies and has no son,
then you must let his inheritance pass on to his daughter.
[9] If he has no daughter, then you must give his inheritance to his brothers.
[10] If he has no brothers, then you must give his inheritance to his father’s brothers.
[11] If his father has no brothers,
then you must give his inheritance to his closest relative in his family.
That relative will own it.
This is to be a fixed law and rule for the people of Israel,
as the LORD commanded Moses.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses does not know the answer. He asks God.
> And God says: “The daughters of Zelophehad are right.” God changes the law because of the request of these five women. From now on, daughters may inherit if there are no sons.
> This is an important step for the rights of women. It is not yet full equal rights, but it is a beginning. God listens to those who stand up for justice.
> The story also shows: Laws are not frozen. If a case is unfair, people may ask and request a better solution.
> This story comes up again in Chapter 36.

---

## Moses is to see the land (Verses 12–14)

[12] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go up into this mountain of Abarim.
Look at the land that I have given to the people of Israel.
[13] When you have seen it, you too will be gathered to your people,
just as your brother Aaron was gathered.
[14] For when the community argued in the wilderness of Zin,
you rebelled against my word.
You did not honor me as holy at the waters in front of their eyes.”
These are the waters of Meribah of Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Being gathered to your people means: to die.
> God tells Moses that he will die. Moses may see the land, but not enter it. The reason is Moses’ mistake at the waters of Meribah (Chapter 20).
> His death is told only at the end of the book of Deuteronomy (Chapter 34).

---

## Moses asks for a successor (Verses 15–17)

[15] Moses spoke to the LORD. He said,
[16] “May the LORD, the God of the spirits of all people,
appoint a man over the community.
[17] Let him go out before them and come in before them.
Let him lead them out and bring them in.
Then the community of the LORD will not be like sheep that have no shepherd.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses does not think of himself. He does not complain about his death. His biggest worry is the people. Who will lead them when he is gone?
> “Like sheep that have no shepherd” – Jesus later uses this picture too, when he feels sorry for the people (Mark 6:34). A good leader is like a good shepherd.

---

## Joshua is appointed (Verses 18–23)

[18] The LORD said to Moses,
“Take Joshua, the son of Nun. He is a man in whom the Spirit is.
Lay your hand on him.
[19] Have him stand before Eleazar the priest and before the whole community.
Give him his task in front of their eyes.
[20] Give him authority,
so that the whole community of the people of Israel will obey him.
[21] He is to stand before Eleazar the priest.
Eleazar will ask the LORD for him, using the decision of the Urim.
At his word they will go out,
and at his word they will come in,
he and all the people of Israel with him, the whole community.”

[22] Moses did as the LORD had commanded him.
He took Joshua and had him stand before Eleazar the priest and before the whole community.
[23] He laid his hands on him and gave him his task,
just as the LORD had said through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua had been Moses’ servant and helper for a long time. He was one of the two faithful spies. Now he becomes Moses’ successor.
> Moses lays his hands on him. That is a sign: I am passing on my task and my blessing to you. To this day, churches lay hands on people when they make them pastors or priests.
> In the Hebrew text, Joshua does not get all of Moses’ honor, but only “some of” it. Moses remains unique. Joshua is also to work together with the priest. He does not decide alone.
> The “Urim” were holy lots in the breast pocket of the high priest. People used them to ask what God wanted (Exodus 28:30).
