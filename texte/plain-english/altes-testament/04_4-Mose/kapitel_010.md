# Numbers – Chapter 10
### The silver trumpets and setting out from Sinai

---

## Two trumpets of silver (Verses 1–10)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[2] “Make two trumpets of silver.
Make them of hammered silver.
You shall use them to call the community together and to let the camps set out.
[3] When they blow both of them, the whole community shall gather to you at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[4] If they blow only one, then the leaders shall gather to you, the heads of the thousands of Israel.

[5] “When you blow the alarm, the camps that lie on the east side shall set out.
[6] When you blow the alarm a second time, the camps that lie on the south side shall set out.
They shall blow an alarm when they are to set out.
[7] But when the assembly is to be called together, you shall blow, but you shall not sound the alarm.

[8] “The sons of Aaron, the priests, shall blow the trumpets.
This shall be a statute for you forever, for all your generations.
[9] When you go to war in your land against the enemy who oppresses you,
then you shall sound an alarm with the trumpets.
Then you will be remembered before the LORD your God,
and you will be saved from your enemies.
[10] Also on your days of joy, at your feasts and at the beginnings of your months,
you shall blow the trumpets over your burnt offerings and over your peace offerings.
They shall be a reminder for you before your God.
I am the LORD your God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> With so many people, you need signals that everyone can hear. The trumpets are like a bell or a siren.
> There are different signals: a steady sound to gather and a special signal to set out, maybe short, quick notes.
> The trumpets sound in hard times, in war, and in happy times, at feasts. Both times they say: God, remember us!
> On the Arch of Titus in Rome you can still see today how the Romans carried away such trumpets as loot after they destroyed the temple in AD 70.
> Verse 5: The alarm is a special signal to set out.

## Setting out from Mount Sinai (Verses 11–13)

[11] In the second year, in the second month, on the twentieth day of the month,
the cloud rose up from over the Tabernacle of the covenant.
[12] And the people of Israel set out from the wilderness of Sinai, one stage after another.
And the cloud stopped in the wilderness of Paran.
[13] So they set out for the first time, at the command of the LORD through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel was at Mount Sinai for almost a whole year. There they received the commandments and built the sanctuary. Now the journey goes on, toward the promised land.
> The wilderness of Paran lies north of Sinai, in the south of today’s Israel.

## The order for setting out (Verses 14–28)

[14] First the flag of the camp of the descendants of Judah set out, by their armies.
Nahshon, the son of Amminadab, led its army.
[15] Nethanel, the son of Zuar, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Issachar.
[16] Eliab, the son of Helon, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Zebulun.
[17] Then the Tabernacle was taken down.
And the descendants of Gershon and the descendants of Merari, who carried the Tabernacle, set out.

[18] Then the flag of the camp of Reuben set out, by their armies.
Elizur, the son of Shedeur, led its army.
[19] Shelumiel, the son of Zurishaddai, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Simeon.
[20] Eliasaph, the son of Deuel, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Gad.
[21] Then the Kohathites set out, carrying the holy things.
The others set up the Tabernacle before they arrived.

[22] Then the flag of the camp of the descendants of Ephraim set out, by their armies.
Elishama, the son of Ammihud, led its army.
[23] Gamaliel, the son of Pedahzur, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Manasseh.
[24] Abidan, the son of Gideoni, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Benjamin.

[25] Last of all, the flag of the camp of the descendants of Dan set out, by their armies.
It was the rear guard of all the camps.
Ahiezer, the son of Ammishaddai, led its army.
[26] Pagiel, the son of Ochran, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Asher.
[27] Ahira, the son of Enan, led the army of the tribe of the descendants of Naphtali.
[28] This was the order in which the people of Israel traveled, by their armies.
And they set out.

> **What does this mean?**
> The order is planned wisely: The Gershonites and the Merarites with the boards and coverings leave early. So they can already set up the tent at the new campsite. Then, when the Kohathites arrive with the holy things, the Tabernacle is already standing ready.

## Moses asks Hobab to come along (Verses 29–32)

[29] Moses spoke to Hobab, the son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses’ father-in-law.
He said:
“We are traveling to the place about which the LORD said: ‘I will give it to you.’
Come with us, and we will treat you well.
For the LORD has promised good things to Israel.”
[30] But Hobab answered him:
“I will not go.
I will go back to my own land and to my relatives.”
[31] Moses said:
“Please do not leave us!
For you know where we can camp in the wilderness.
You can be our eyes.
[32] If you go with us – yes, if you do –
then we will do to you all the good that the LORD does to us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Hobab knows the wilderness. He knows where there is water and where it is good to camp. Moses asks him to be their guide.
> It is interesting: God leads through the cloud. Still, Moses gladly accepts the help of an experienced person. Trusting God and human wisdom do not exclude each other.
> It is not completely clear who Hobab is. The sentence can be understood to mean that Reuel is Moses’ father-in-law and Hobab is his son, so Moses’ brother-in-law. In Judges 4:11, Hobab himself is called Moses’ father-in-law. We also know Moses’ father-in-law by the name Jethro (Exodus 3:1).
> We are not told here whether Hobab came along. Later his descendants live in Israel (Judges 1:16). So he probably went with them.

## The ark goes ahead (Verses 33–36)

[33] They set out from the mountain of the LORD and traveled for three days.
The ark of the covenant of the LORD went ahead of them for the three days of travel,
to look for a resting place for them.
[34] And the cloud of the LORD was over them in the daytime when they set out from the camp.
[35] Whenever the ark set out, Moses said:
“Rise up, LORD!
Let your enemies be scattered!
Let those who hate you run away before you!”
[36] And whenever it stopped, he said:
“Come back, LORD, to the many thousands upon thousands of Israel!”

> **What does this mean?**
> The ark of the covenant goes ahead. God himself looks for the way and the resting place for his people. It is like a shepherd who walks ahead of his flock.
> The two prayers of Moses are very old. They are still spoken today in Jewish worship services when the Torah scroll is taken out of its cabinet and put back again. Psalm 68:1 (in some Bibles 68:2) also begins with these words.
> In many Hebrew Bibles there are special marks around verses 35–36: upside-down letters called “nun.” They show that these two verses are something very special.
