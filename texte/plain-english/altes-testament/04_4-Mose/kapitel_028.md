# Numbers – Chapter 28
### The offerings for every day, every Sabbath and the festivals

---

## The daily offering (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Command the people of Israel, and tell them,
‘Be sure to bring me my offering at the right time,
my food for my offerings made by fire, as a pleasant smell to me.’
[3] And tell them,
‘This is the offering made by fire that you must bring to the LORD:
two male lambs one year old without any defect, every day, as a regular burnt offering.
[4] You must offer one lamb in the morning,
and you must offer the other lamb in the evening.
[5] With it, bring about 2 quarts of fine flour as a grain offering,
mixed with about 1 quart of oil from crushed olives.
[6] It is the regular burnt offering that was commanded at Mount Sinai,
as a pleasant smell, an offering made by fire to the LORD.
[7] Its drink offering is to be about 1 quart for each lamb.
In the holy place, you must pour out a drink offering of strong drink to the LORD.
[8] You must offer the other lamb in the evening.
Offer it like the grain offering of the morning and like its drink offering,
as an offering made by fire, a pleasant smell to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: A burnt offering is an offering that is burned completely on the altar.
> Verse 7: A drink offering is wine or another drink that is poured out for God.
> In the Bible the measures are given like this: one tenth of an ephah of flour, and a fourth of a hin of oil and of wine. An ephah is about 6 gallons. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> Chapters 28 and 29 are like a calendar for worship. They say exactly which offerings are brought on which day. We already know the festivals from Leviticus 23. Here it is about the offerings for them.
> The basis is the daily offering: one lamb in the morning, one lamb in the evening. Every day begins and ends with God.
> To this day, Jews pray at these times, in the morning and in the afternoon. Since the temple was destroyed, these prayers take the place of the offerings.

---

## On the Sabbath (Verses 9–10)

[9] “‘On the Sabbath day, you must offer two male lambs one year old without any defect.
With them, bring about 4½ quarts of fine flour as a grain offering, mixed with oil, and its drink offering.
[10] This is the burnt offering for every Sabbath,
in addition to the regular burnt offering and its drink offering.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: two tenths of an ephah of flour.
> On the Sabbath, the daily offering is doubled. The day of rest is a special day for God.

---

## At the beginning of every month (Verses 11–15)

[11] “‘At the beginning of your months, you must bring a burnt offering to the LORD:
two young bulls, one ram and seven male lambs one year old without any defect.
[12] With them, bring about 7 quarts of fine flour mixed with oil as a grain offering for each bull,
and about 4½ quarts of fine flour mixed with oil as a grain offering for the one ram,
[13] and about 2 quarts of fine flour mixed with oil as a grain offering for each lamb.
This is a burnt offering with a pleasant smell, an offering made by fire to the LORD.
[14] Their drink offerings are to be:
about 2 quarts of wine for a bull, a little more than 1 quart for the ram, and about 1 quart for a lamb.
This is the burnt offering for every month, through all the months of the year.
[15] Also, one male goat is to be offered to the LORD as a sin offering,
in addition to the regular burnt offering and its drink offering.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: A sin offering is an offering that takes away guilt.
> In the Bible the measures are given like this: three tenths, two tenths and one tenth of an ephah of flour, and a half, a third and a fourth of a hin of wine.
> The Jewish calendar follows the moon. Every month begins with the new moon. This day is called “Rosh Chodesh,” the head of the month. To this day it is a small holiday.

## Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread (Verses 16–25)

[16] “‘In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, is the LORD’s Passover.
[17] On the fifteenth day of this month there is to be a festival.
For seven days people must eat bread without yeast.
[18] On the first day there is to be a holy assembly.
You must not do any regular work.
[19] Instead, you must bring an offering made by fire, a burnt offering to the LORD:
two young bulls, one ram and seven male lambs one year old.
They must be without any defect.
[20] With them, bring their grain offering, fine flour mixed with oil.
You must offer about 7 quarts for a bull and about 4½ quarts for the ram.
[21] You must offer about 2 quarts for each of the seven lambs.
[22] And bring one male goat as a sin offering, to make atonement for you.
[23] You must offer these in addition to the burnt offering of the morning, which is the regular burnt offering.
[24] In this way you must offer every day, for seven days,
the food of the offering made by fire, a pleasant smell to the LORD.
It is to be offered in addition to the regular burnt offering and its drink offering.
[25] On the seventh day you must have a holy assembly.
You must not do any regular work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: Atonement means: putting things right again between God and people.
> Passover reminds people of how God set them free from Egypt. After it comes the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days, with large offerings every day.

## The Feast of Weeks (Verses 26–31)

[26] “‘Also on the day of the first fruits, when you bring a new grain offering to the LORD at your Feast of Weeks,
you must have a holy assembly.
You must not do any regular work.
[27] Instead, you must bring a burnt offering as a pleasant smell to the LORD:
two young bulls, one ram and seven male lambs one year old.
[28] With them, bring their grain offering, fine flour mixed with oil:
about 7 quarts for each bull, about 4½ quarts for the one ram,
[29] about 2 quarts for each of the seven lambs.
[30] And bring one male goat, to make atonement for you.
[31] You must offer them with their drink offerings,
in addition to the regular burnt offering and its grain offering.
Be sure that they are without any defect.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Feast of Weeks, “Shavuot,” is the festival of the wheat harvest, seven weeks after Passover. In Christianity it became Pentecost (see Leviticus 23:15–22).
