# Numbers – Chapter 12
### Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses

---

## The complaint (Verses 1–3)

[1] Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses
because of the Cushite woman he had married.
For he had married a Cushite woman.
[2] They said,
“Has the LORD really spoken only with Moses?
Hasn’t he also spoken with us?”
And the LORD heard it.
[3] But Moses was a very humble man,
more humble than any other person on the face of the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Miriam and Aaron are the older sister and brother of Moses. Miriam saved him when he was a baby (Exodus 2:4–8). After the crossing of the sea, she started the song of victory (Exodus 15:20–21). Aaron is the high priest.
> “Cush” is the land south of Egypt, today Sudan or Ethiopia. The people there had dark skin. It is not quite clear who this woman is. Maybe it is Zipporah, the first wife of Moses. Maybe Moses married a second wife.
> The complaint about the woman is probably only an excuse. The real reason is jealousy: “We are important too! God speaks with us too!”
> Moses does not defend himself. The Bible says: He was the most humble person on earth. Humble does not mean weak. It means: You do not have to make yourself look big. You leave it to God to defend you.

---

## God defends Moses (Verses 4–9)

[4] Suddenly the LORD said to Moses, to Aaron, and to Miriam,
“You three, come out to the Tent of Meeting!”
So the three of them came out.
[5] The LORD came down in a pillar of cloud
and stood at the door of the tent.
He called Aaron and Miriam, and they both stepped forward.
[6] He said,
“Listen to my words!
If there is a prophet among you,
I, the LORD, make myself known to him in a vision.
I speak with him in a dream.
[7] But it is not like that with my servant Moses.
He is faithful in all my house.
[8] With him I speak mouth to mouth,
clearly and not in riddles.
And he sees the form of the LORD.
So why were you not afraid to speak against my servant, against Moses?”
[9] The LORD became very angry with them, and he left.

> **What does this mean?**
> God himself defends Moses. He explains: Yes, I also speak with other prophets, but in dreams and pictures. With Moses I speak directly, “mouth to mouth,” like with a friend (Exodus 33:11).
> “He is faithful in all my house” – Moses is like a faithful manager who is trusted with the whole house. The Letter to the Hebrews in the New Testament quotes this verse and compares Moses with Jesus (Hebrews 3:2–6).

---

## Miriam gets leprosy (Verses 10–16)

[10] The cloud moved away from over the tent.
Look! Miriam had leprosy. She was as white as snow.
Aaron turned toward Miriam and saw that she had leprosy.
[11] Aaron said to Moses,
“Oh, my lord, please do not hold this sin against us.
We have acted foolishly, and we have sinned.
[12] Please do not let her be like a baby born dead,
whose body is already half eaten away when it comes out of its mother’s womb.”
[13] Then Moses cried out to the LORD,
“Heal her, God, I beg you!”

[14] The LORD said to Moses,
“If her father had only spit in her face,
wouldn’t she be ashamed for seven days?
Let her be shut out of the camp for seven days.
After that she shall be brought back in.”
[15] So Miriam was shut out of the camp for seven days.
And the people did not travel on until Miriam had been brought back in.
[16] After that, the people traveled on from Hazeroth
and set up camp in the wilderness of Paran.

> **What does this mean?**
> Miriam becomes sick, “as white as snow.” This is a kind of skin disease (see Leviticus 13). It is a bitter irony: She complained about a woman with dark skin, and now her own skin becomes white and sick.
> Why is only Miriam punished and not Aaron? The Bible does not say. Maybe because Miriam was the driving force. Her name comes first in verse 1. Maybe Aaron is spared because as high priest he could otherwise not do his service. To us today it still seems unequal.
> The way Moses reacts is wonderful. He is not offended and does not take revenge. He prays for his sister right away. It is one of the shortest prayers in the Bible: “Heal her, God, I beg you!”
> God hears the prayer. Miriam has to stay outside for only seven days. Then she is healed and comes back.
> And the whole people waits for her. No one travels on before Miriam is back. She is not forgotten. She belongs.
