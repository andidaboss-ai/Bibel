# Numbers – Chapter 36
### The daughters of Zelophehad once more

---

## A worry of the family (Verses 1–4)

[1] The heads of the families of the clan of the descendants of Gilead came forward.
Gilead was the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, from the families of the sons of Joseph.
They spoke before Moses and before the leaders, the heads of the families of the people of Israel.
[2] They said,
“The LORD commanded my lord to give the land to the people of Israel as an inheritance by lot.
And my lord was commanded by the LORD
to give the inheritance of Zelophehad, our brother, to his daughters.
[3] But they may marry men from one of the other tribes of the people of Israel.
Then their inheritance will be taken away from the inheritance of our fathers.
It will be added to the inheritance of the tribe they will then belong to.
So it will be taken away from the share of our inheritance.
[4] When the year of jubilee of the people of Israel comes,
their inheritance will be added to the inheritance of the tribe they belong to.
So their inheritance will be taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 27, the five daughters of Zelophehad received the right to inherit their father’s land.
> Now the men from their clan come with a worry: If the daughters marry men from another tribe, the land will later belong to the families of those men. Then the area of the tribe of Manasseh will become smaller.
> The “year of jubilee” came every 50 years. Then land that had been sold was to go back to the family it first belonged to (Leviticus 25). But inherited land like this was not sold. So the year of jubilee would not have changed anything. The loss would be final.

---

## The solution (Verses 5–9)

[5] Moses commanded the people of Israel by the word of the LORD.
He said,
“The tribe of the sons of Joseph is right.
[6] This is what the LORD commands about the daughters of Zelophehad:
‘They may marry whom they think best.
But they must marry into a family of their father’s tribe.
[7] That way no inheritance of the people of Israel will move from tribe to tribe.
For each of the people of Israel is to keep the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers.
[8] Every daughter who owns an inheritance in one of the tribes of the people of Israel
must marry a man from a family of her father’s tribe.
That way each of the people of Israel will own the inheritance of his fathers.
[9] So no inheritance will move from one tribe to another tribe.
For each of the tribes of the people of Israel is to keep its own inheritance.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Once again a question is brought to God. And once again a fair solution is found.
> The daughters keep their inheritance. They may decide for themselves whom they marry: “whom they think best.” That was not a matter of course back then. The only condition is: The man is to be from their own tribe.
> In this way both are protected: the right of the women and the land of the tribe.
> This rule only applied to daughters who had inherited land. Otherwise Israelites could also marry into other tribes.

---

## The daughters obey (Verses 10–12)

[10] The daughters of Zelophehad did as the LORD had commanded Moses.
[11] Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad,
married the sons of their father’s brothers.
[12] They married into the families of the sons of Manasseh, the son of Joseph.
And their inheritance stayed in the tribe of their father’s family.

> **What does this mean?**
> The five sisters marry their cousins. Back then that was common and allowed.
> Their story has a good ending. Later, in the book of Joshua, they really receive their land (Joshua 17:3–6).

---

## The end of the book (Verse 13)

[13] These are the commandments and the rules
that the LORD commanded the people of Israel through Moses,
in the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, at Jericho.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of Numbers ends where the people now stand: by the Jordan, just before the promised land. Across from them is the city of Jericho.
> It is remarkable that the book ends with a story about five women who stood up for their rights. Their courage changed the law of Israel.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Numbers?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of Numbers tells about the journey through the wilderness. It took almost 40 years. The book has three large parts:
> 1. At Mount Sinai (Chapters 1–10): The people are counted and put in order. The Levites receive their tasks. Everything is prepared for setting out. In the middle of it is the blessing that priests still speak today: The LORD bless you and keep you (Numbers 6:24).
> 2. In the wilderness (Chapters 10–21): The people complain again and again. They want to go back to Egypt. They do not believe the two faithful spies. That is why the whole generation must die in the wilderness. Even Moses, Miriam and Aaron may not enter the land. But God still provides for his people every day with manna and water.
> 3. Before the land (Chapters 22–36): The prophet Balaam is supposed to curse Israel, but he has to bless it. A new generation is counted. Joshua becomes Moses’ successor. Everything is prepared for life in the land.
> The book shows honestly how hard it is for people to trust God. And it also shows hard and dark sides, like the war against Midian. These sides must not be made to look nice, and they must never be taken as an example for violence.
> But the most important message is: God stays faithful, even when his people are unfaithful. He leads them step by step to the goal. One generation fails, but the next one now stands by the Jordan, ready for the new land.
> The book of Deuteronomy tells what Moses still says to the people there by the Jordan as a farewell.
