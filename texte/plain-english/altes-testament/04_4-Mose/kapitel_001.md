# Numbers – Chapter 1
### The first census

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Numbers?

The book of Numbers is also called “the Fourth Book of Moses.” Its name comes from the Latin name “Numeri.” That means “numbers.” For at the beginning and near the end of the book, the people are counted.
In Hebrew the book is called “Bemidbar.” That means “In the wilderness.” That name fits even better. For the book tells about Israel’s journey through the wilderness, from Mount Sinai to the border of the promised land.
This journey should have taken only a few weeks. But because the people do not trust God, it takes 40 years. A whole generation dies in the wilderness.
It is a book about complaining and doubt, about rebellions and punishments, but also about God’s patience and faithfulness. The famous blessing is also found here: “The LORD bless you and keep you” (Numbers 6:24–26).

---

## God commands the census (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the Tent of Meeting.
It was on the first day of the second month, in the second year after they had come out of the land of Egypt.
He said:
[2] “Take a census of the whole community of the children of Israel.
A census means: counting all the people.
Count them by their families and by their fathers’ houses,
by the number of the names, every male, one by one,
[3] from 20 years old and up, all in Israel who are able to go to war.
You and Aaron shall count them by their army divisions.
[4] With you there shall be one man from every tribe.
Each one shall be the head of his fathers’ house.

> **What does this mean?**
> A little more than a year has passed since the people left Egypt. At Sinai the people received the commandments and built the sanctuary. Now they shall move on, into the promised land.
> For this, they count how many men can fight. Israel is no longer a group of runaway slaves. It is now an orderly people.
> “By the number of the names”: Everyone is counted with his name. For God, nobody is just a number.

## The helpers from every tribe (Verses 5–16)

[5] “These are the names of the men who shall stand with you and help you:
From the tribe of Reuben: Elizur, the son of Shedeur.
[6] From the tribe of Simeon: Shelumiel, the son of Zurishaddai.
[7] From the tribe of Judah: Nahshon, the son of Amminadab.
[8] From the tribe of Issachar: Nethanel, the son of Zuar.
[9] From the tribe of Zebulun: Eliab, the son of Helon.
[10] From the sons of Joseph:
from the tribe of Ephraim: Elishama, the son of Ammihud;
from the tribe of Manasseh: Gamaliel, the son of Pedahzur.
[11] From the tribe of Benjamin: Abidan, the son of Gideoni.
[12] From the tribe of Dan: Ahiezer, the son of Ammishaddai.
[13] From the tribe of Asher: Pagiel, the son of Ochran.
[14] From the tribe of Gad: Eliasaph, the son of Deuel.
[15] From the tribe of Naphtali: Ahira, the son of Enan.”
[16] These were the men who were called from the community.
They were the leaders of the tribes of their fathers.
They were the heads of the thousands of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every tribe has a leader. These names come up again and again in the book of Numbers, for example in Chapter 7.
> Nahshon from the tribe of Judah is an ancestor of King David, and so also of Jesus (Ruth 4:20–22; Matthew 1:4).
> Joseph gets two tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh. These are the names of his sons (Genesis 48). The tribe of Levi is missing from this list. The end of the chapter explains why.
> Many names contain names for God, for example “El” (God) or “Shaddai” (the Almighty). “Elizur” means “My God is a rock.”

## The census begins (Verses 17–19)

[17] Moses and Aaron took these men who had been named.
[18] On the first day of the second month, they gathered the whole community.
And the people stated their ancestry, by their families and by their fathers’ houses,
by the number of the names, from 20 years old and up, one by one.
[19] Moses counted them in the wilderness of Sinai, just as the LORD had commanded him.

> **What does this mean?**

> Judah is the largest tribe. Later, the kings of Israel come from Judah.

## The total number (Verses 44–46)

[44] These were the ones who were counted.
Moses and Aaron counted them,
together with the twelve leaders of Israel, one for each fathers’ house.
[45] All the children of Israel who were counted by their fathers’ houses, from 20 years old and up,
all in Israel who were able to go to war –
[46] all who were counted were 603,550.

> **What does this mean?**
> 603,550 men, plus women, children and old people. That would be maybe two million people in all. That is a huge number for a journey through the wilderness.
> Some scholars think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” (“elef”) could also mean “clan” or “troop” here. Then there would have been far fewer people. Others take the numbers literally. Above all, the Bible wants to show: God kept his promise to Abraham. One family has become a great people (Genesis 15:5).
> The same number already came up with the offering for the sanctuary (Exodus 38:26).

## The Levites are not counted (Verses 47–54)

[47] But the Levites were not counted with them by the tribe of their fathers.
[48] For the LORD had spoken to Moses.
He had said:
[49] “Only the tribe of Levi you shall not count.
You shall not take a census of them among the children of Israel.
[50] Instead, put the Levites in charge of the Tabernacle of the Testimony,
over all its equipment and over everything that belongs to it.
The Tabernacle is the holy tent where God lives among his people.
They shall carry the Tabernacle and all its equipment.
They shall take care of it and camp all around it.
[51] When the Tabernacle is to move on, the Levites shall take it down.
And when the Tabernacle is to be set up, the Levites shall set it up.
A stranger who comes near it shall be put to death.
[52] The children of Israel shall set up their tents,
every man in his own camp and every man by his own flag, by their army divisions.
[53] But the Levites shall camp all around the Tabernacle of the Testimony,
so that no anger comes on the community of the children of Israel.
The Levites shall be responsible for the Tabernacle of the Testimony.”
[54] The children of Israel did this.
They did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites do not go to war. They have a different task: They take care of the sanctuary. They set it up and take it down, and they carry it on the journey.
> They camp right around the sanctuary, like a protective wall. So nobody comes too close to the holy place who is not called to do so.
> “A stranger” here means: someone who does not belong to the tribe of Levi.
> The sanctuary stands in the middle of the camp. God is the center of the people.
