# Numbers – Chapter 20
### Water from the rock – Moses’ mistake – Aaron’s death

---

## Miriam dies (Verse 1)

[1] The people of Israel, the whole community, came into the wilderness of Zin in the first month.
The people stayed in Kadesh.
Miriam died there, and she was buried there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the story makes a big jump. Almost 40 years have passed since Chapter 14. The old generation has almost all died. Now the new generation is standing at the border of the land again.
> Miriam, the sister of Moses and Aaron, dies. The Bible reports it very briefly. Miriam saved Moses when he was a baby, and after the crossing of the sea she sang and danced.
> Jewish teachers tell this story: As long as Miriam was alive, there was a well that traveled along with the people. That is why right after this it says: “The community had no water.”

---

## No water (Verses 2–6)

[2] The community had no water.
So they gathered together against Moses and against Aaron.
[3] The people argued with Moses and said,
“We wish we had died when our brothers died before the LORD!
[4] Why have you brought the LORD’s assembly into this wilderness,
so that we die here, we and our animals?
[5] Why did you make us come up out of Egypt,
only to bring us to this terrible place?
There is no grain here, no figs, no grapevines, and no pomegranates.
And there is no water to drink.”
[6] Moses and Aaron went away from the assembly to the door of the Tent of Meeting
and fell on their faces.
Then the glory of the LORD appeared to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The new generation complains just like their parents. The words sound almost the same as 40 years before.
> Moses and Aaron do not answer with words. They go to God.

## Moses’ mistake at the rock (Verses 7–13)

[7] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[8] “Take the staff.
Gather the community, you and your brother Aaron.
And in front of their eyes, speak to the rock, so that it gives its water.
That is how you shall bring water out of the rock for them.
That is how you shall give the community and their animals something to drink.”
[9] Moses took the staff from where it lay before the LORD, as the LORD had commanded him.

[10] Moses and Aaron gathered the assembly in front of the rock.
And Moses said to them,
“Listen, you rebels!
Must we bring water out of this rock for you?”
[11] Moses raised his hand and struck the rock twice with his staff.
Then a lot of water came out.
And the community and their animals drank.

[12] The LORD said to Moses and to Aaron,
“You did not believe in me,
and you did not show me as holy in front of the eyes of the people of Israel.
So you will not bring this assembly into the land that I have given them.”
[13] These are the waters of Meribah,
where the people of Israel argued with the LORD,
and where he showed himself holy among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the saddest passages about Moses. God had said: “Speak to the rock.” But Moses strikes it twice with the staff. The water comes anyway, because God does not let his people die of thirst.
> What exactly was the mistake? Jews and Christians have thought a lot about this:
> Moses did not do exactly what God had said. He struck instead of speaking.
> Moses was angry and called the people “rebels.”
> Moses said: “Must we bring water out of this rock for you?” As if he and Aaron were doing the miracle, not God.
> The result is harsh: Moses and Aaron may not enter the promised land. After 40 years of faithful service, that is hard to grasp.
> It shows: Whoever carries a great responsibility is also measured by stricter standards. But God does not leave Moses. He stays his friend to the very end.
> “Meribah” means “arguing.” There was already a place with this name in Exodus 17:7.

## Edom does not let Israel pass through (Verses 14–21)

[14] From Kadesh, Moses sent messengers to the king of Edom to tell him,
“This is what your brother Israel says:
You know all the trouble that has happened to us.
[15] Our fathers went down into Egypt, and we lived in Egypt for a long time.
The Egyptians treated us and our fathers badly.
[16] When we cried out to the LORD, he heard our voice.
He sent an angel and brought us out of Egypt.
Look, now we are in Kadesh, a town at the edge of your land.
[17] “Please let us pass through your land.
We will not go through fields or vineyards,
and we will not drink water from the wells.
We will go along the King’s Highway.
We will not turn off to the right or to the left until we have passed through your land.”
[18] But Edom said to him,
“You shall not pass through my land.
Otherwise I will come out against you with the sword.”
[19] The people of Israel said to him,
“We will go up on the main road.
And if we drink any of your water, I and my animals, I will pay for it.
Just let me pass through on foot, nothing else.”
[20] He said, “You shall not pass through.”
And Edom came out against him with many people and a strong army.
[21] So Edom refused to let Israel pass through his land.
That is why Israel turned away from him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Edomites are descendants of Esau, the twin brother of Jacob (Genesis 36). That is why Israel calls itself “your brother.”
> Israel asks very politely and promises to take nothing and to pay for everything. But Edom says no.
> Israel does not fight against Edom. It takes a detour. God had forbidden Israel to fight against Edom, because they are brothers (Deuteronomy 2:4–5).
> The “King’s Highway” was an important trade road east of the Jordan, from Egypt all the way to Syria.

## Aaron dies (Verses 22–29)

[22] They set out from Kadesh,
and the people of Israel, the whole community, came to Mount Hor.
[23] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron on Mount Hor, at the border of the land of Edom. He said,
[24] “Aaron will be gathered to his people.
For he will not enter the land that I have given to the people of Israel,
because you both rebelled against my command at the waters of Meribah.
[25] Take Aaron and his son Eleazar and bring them up Mount Hor.
[26] Take off Aaron’s clothes and put them on his son Eleazar.
Aaron will be gathered to his people and will die there.”

[27] Moses did as the LORD had commanded.
They went up Mount Hor in front of the whole community.
[28] Moses took off Aaron’s clothes and put them on his son Eleazar.
And Aaron died there on the top of the mountain.
Then Moses and Eleazar came down from the mountain.
[29] When the whole community saw that Aaron had died,
the whole house of Israel wept for Aaron for 30 days.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Being gathered to his people” is a beautiful expression for dying. It means: You go to your ancestors who have already died.
> Aaron’s death is calm and dignified. Before his death, his priestly clothes are taken off him and put on his son Eleazar. That way the office goes on. Aaron can see his son become the one who follows him.
> Moses goes with his brother to the very end. The whole people mourns for 30 days, as later for Moses (Deuteronomy 34:8).
> Aaron had made mistakes, for example with the golden calf. But he had also served faithfully for many years. The people do not forget him.
