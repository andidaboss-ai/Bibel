# Numbers – Chapter 22
### Balak calls Balaam – the talking donkey

---

## Moab is afraid of Israel (Verses 1–4)

[1] The people of Israel traveled on.
They set up camp in the plains of Moab,
on the other side of the Jordan, at Jericho.
[2] Balak, the son of Zippor, saw everything Israel had done to the Amorites.
[3] Moab was very afraid of the people, because there were so many of them.
Moab was filled with dread because of the people of Israel.
[4] Moab said to the elders of Midian,
“Now this crowd will lick up everything around us, like an ox licks up the grass of the field.”
Balak, the son of Zippor, was king of Moab at that time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel is now close to its goal. The people are east of the Jordan, across from the city of Jericho.
> The Moabites are descendants of Lot, Abraham’s nephew (Genesis 19:37). Israel does not want to fight against them at all (Deuteronomy 2:9). But Balak, the king, is afraid.
> Chapters 22 to 24 tell a special story. Israel itself hardly appears in it. The people of Israel do not even know what is being planned against them. But God protects them anyway.

---

## Balak sends for Balaam (Verses 5–14)

[5] Balak sent messengers to Balaam, the son of Beor, in Pethor, which is by the River,
in the land of the people of his own nation.
They were to call him.
Balak had them say,
“Look, a people has come out of Egypt.
Look, they cover the surface of the earth, and they are camping across from me.
[6] So please come now, and curse this people for me.
They are too strong for me.
Then perhaps I will win, so that we can defeat them and I can drive them out of the land.
For I know: The one you bless is blessed, and the one you curse is cursed.”

[7] The elders of Moab and the elders of Midian left.
In their hands they had the payment for fortune-telling.
They came to Balaam and told him Balak’s words.
[8] Balaam said to them,
“Stay here tonight.
Then I will bring you an answer, just as the LORD will speak to me.”
So the leaders of Moab stayed with Balaam.

[9] God came to Balaam and said, “Who are these men with you?”
[10] Balaam said to God,
“Balak, the son of Zippor, king of Moab, has sent me this message:
[11] ‘Look, the people that came out of Egypt covers the surface of the earth.
Now come and curse them for me.
Then perhaps I will be able to fight against them and drive them out.’”
[12] God said to Balaam,
“You must not go with them.
You must not curse the people, because they are blessed.”
[13] Balaam got up in the morning and said to Balak’s leaders,
“Go back to your land. The LORD refuses to let me go with you.”
[14] The leaders of Moab got up and went to Balak.
They said, “Balaam refuses to come with us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Balaam is a famous seer and fortune-teller from Mesopotamia, by the Euphrates River (“the River”). He is not an Israelite. People back then believed that his curses and blessings really worked.
> In Jordan, archaeologists have found an old inscription. It tells about a seer called “Balaam, son of Beor.” So he was also known outside the Bible.
> God’s answer is clear: “You must not curse the people, because they are blessed.” God promised Abraham that he would bless his descendants (Genesis 12:2–3). No curse can change that.

---

## Balak sends messengers again (Verses 15–21)

[15] Then Balak sent leaders again.
There were more of them, and they were more important than the first ones.
[16] They came to Balaam and said to him,
“Balak, the son of Zippor, says this:
‘Please do not let anything stop you from coming to me.
[17] I will give you very great honor.
And I will do whatever you tell me.
So please come, and curse this people for me.’”
[18] Balaam answered Balak’s servants,
“Even if Balak gave me his house full of silver and gold,
I could not go beyond the word of the LORD my God,
to do less or more.
[19] So please stay here tonight as well.
Then I can find out what else the LORD will say to me.”
[20] God came to Balaam at night and said to him,
“If the men have come to call you, get up and go with them.
But you must do only what I tell you.”
[21] Balaam got up in the morning and saddled his donkey.
Then he went with the leaders of Moab.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balaam’s words sound very faithful: Even for a house full of gold, he would not go against God’s word. But then why does he ask God again, when God has already said no?
> Maybe Balaam hopes that God will change his mind, so that he can get the money after all. In the New Testament, Balaam is named as an example. It says that he loved the payment for doing wrong (2 Peter 2:15).
> God lets him go, but only on one condition: He may only say what God tells him.

## The donkey and the angel (Verses 22–30)

[22] But God became angry because Balaam went.
The angel of the LORD stood in the road to block him, like an enemy.
Balaam was riding on his donkey, and his two servants were with him.
[23] The donkey saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road, with a drawn sword in his hand.
So the donkey turned off the road and went into the field.
Balaam hit the donkey to turn her back onto the road.

[24] Then the angel of the LORD stood in a narrow path between the vineyards.
There was a wall on this side and a wall on that side.
[25] The donkey saw the angel of the LORD.
She pressed herself against the wall and crushed Balaam’s foot against the wall.
So he hit her again.

[26] The angel of the LORD went on further.
He stood in a narrow place where there was no room to turn to the right or to the left.
[27] The donkey saw the angel of the LORD, and she lay down under Balaam.
Balaam became angry, and he hit the donkey with his stick.

[28] Then the LORD opened the donkey’s mouth.
She said to Balaam,
“What have I done to you, that you have hit me these three times?”
[29] Balaam said to the donkey,
“Because you have made a fool of me!
I wish I had a sword in my hand. Then I would kill you right now!”
[30] The donkey said to Balaam,
“Am I not your donkey? You have ridden on me all your life until today.
Have I ever done this to you before?”
He said, “No.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the best-known and also funniest stories in the Bible.
> Balaam is a famous seer. People think he can see into the future and into the world of the gods. But he does not see the angel standing right in front of him. His donkey sees him. The animal is wiser than the “great seer.”
> Three times the donkey saves Balaam’s life. And three times he hits her for it.
> Then the donkey speaks. And Balaam is so angry that he is not even surprised that an animal is talking to him. He simply argues with her.
> The donkey speaks very sensibly: “Have I ever done this to you before?” She has always been loyal to him. This is also a reminder to treat animals well.

## Balaam sees the angel (Verses 31–35)

[31] Then the LORD opened Balaam’s eyes.
He saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road, with a drawn sword in his hand.
Balaam bowed his head and fell down with his face to the ground.
[32] The angel of the LORD said to him,
“Why have you hit your donkey these three times?
Look, I have come out to block you, like an enemy, because your way is wrong in my eyes.
[33] The donkey saw me and turned away from me these three times.
If she had not turned away from me,
I would surely have killed you by now, and I would have let her live.”
[34] Balaam said to the angel of the LORD,
“I have sinned.
I did not know that you were standing in the road against me.
So now, if this displeases you, I will go back.”
[35] The angel of the LORD said to Balaam,
“Go with the men.
But you must say only the word that I tell you.”
So Balaam went with Balak’s leaders.

> **What does this mean?**
> God had allowed Balaam to go. Then why is God angry? Maybe because Balaam went with the wrong plans. He was still hoping for the money, and for a way to curse Israel after all.
> Meeting the angel is a warning: Balaam must understand that he is not the one who decides. God decides what he will say.
> Just as God moved the donkey to speak, God will move Balaam to speak.

---

## Balaam comes to Balak (Verses 36–41)

[36] Balak heard that Balaam was coming.
So he went out to meet him at the City of Moab.
It is on the border at the Arnon, at the very edge of the land.
[37] Balak said to Balaam,
“Didn’t I send for you urgently to call you?
Why didn’t you come to me?
Am I not really able to give you honor?”
[38] Balaam said to Balak,
“Look, I have come to you now.
But do I have any power at all to say anything on my own?
I will speak the word that God puts in my mouth.”
[39] Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath Huzoth.
[40] Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep.
He sent some of the meat to Balaam and to the leaders who were with him.
[41] In the morning, Balak took Balaam
and brought him up to the high places of Baal.
From there Balaam saw part of the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balak thinks he can buy Balaam with money and honor. But Balaam warns him right away: He can only say what God puts in his mouth.
> Baal was an important god of the peoples in Canaan. He was a god of the weather and of fertility. From his “high places,” Balaam is supposed to see Israel and curse it. High places were places of worship on hills.
> The next chapter tells how this turns out.
