# Numbers – Chapter 13
### Twelve spies explore the land

---

## Twelve men are chosen (Verses 1–16)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Send out men to explore the land of Canaan, which I am giving to the people of Israel.
From each tribe of their fathers you shall send one man. Each one shall be a leader among them.”
[3] Moses sent them out from the wilderness of Paran, as the LORD had commanded.
All of them were men who were heads among the people of Israel.
[4] These were their names:
From the tribe of Reuben: Shammua the son of Zaccur.
[5] From the tribe of Simeon: Shaphat the son of Hori.
[6] From the tribe of Judah: Caleb the son of Jephunneh.
[7] From the tribe of Issachar: Igal the son of Joseph.
[8] From the tribe of Ephraim: Hoshea the son of Nun.
[9] From the tribe of Benjamin: Palti the son of Raphu.
[10] From the tribe of Zebulun: Gaddiel the son of Sodi.
[11] From the tribe of Joseph, from the tribe of Manasseh: Gaddi the son of Susi.
[12] From the tribe of Dan: Ammiel the son of Gemalli.
[13] From the tribe of Asher: Sethur the son of Michael.
[14] From the tribe of Naphtali: Nahbi the son of Vophsi.
[15] From the tribe of Gad: Geuel the son of Machi.
[16] These are the names of the men whom Moses sent to explore the land.
And Moses called Hoshea the son of Nun Joshua.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people are now at the border of the promised land. Before they go in, twelve spies are to explore the land, one from each tribe.
> Two names are important: Caleb from Judah and Hoshea from Ephraim.
> Moses gives Hoshea a new name: Joshua. “Hoshea” means “rescue.” “Joshua” means “The LORD rescues.” By the way, the Greek name for Joshua is “Jesus.”

---

## The task (Verses 17–20)

[17] Moses sent them to explore the land of Canaan. He said to them,
“Go up this way through the South, and go up into the hill country.
[18] Look at the land and see what it is like.
Look at the people who live in it: whether they are strong or weak, whether they are few or many.
[19] See what the land is like that they live in, whether it is good or bad.
See what the cities are like that they live in, whether they live in open camps or in strong, walled cities.
[20] See what the soil is like, whether it is rich or poor, and whether there are trees in it or not.
Be brave, and bring back some of the fruit of the land.”
It was the time of the first ripe grapes.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The South” is called the “Negev” in Hebrew. That is the dry south of the land.
> The first grape harvest is in July or August.

---

## Exploring the land (Verses 21–25)

[21] So they went up and explored the land,
from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob, up to where the road goes to Hamath.
[22] They went up through the South and came to Hebron.
Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai, the descendants of Anak, lived there.
Hebron had been built seven years before Zoan in Egypt.
[23] They came to the valley of Eshcol.
There they cut off a branch with a single cluster of grapes.
Two men carried it on a pole.
They also brought some pomegranates and figs.
[24] That place was called the valley of Eshcol,
because of the cluster of grapes that the people of Israel cut off there.
[25] After 40 days they came back from exploring the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> The spies go through the whole land, from the south far up into the north.
> Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, and Jacob are buried in Hebron (Genesis 23; 49:29–31).
> The “descendants of Anak,” or “Anakites,” were a people of very tall men.
> “Eshcol” means “cluster of grapes.” The cluster was so big that two men had to carry it. Today this picture is the logo of Israel’s Ministry of Tourism.

---

## The report: a good land, but strong people (Verses 26–29)

[26] They went and came to Moses, to Aaron, and to the whole community of the people of Israel,
in the wilderness of Paran, at Kadesh.
They gave a report to them and to the whole community.
And they showed them the fruit of the land.
[27] They told Moses,
“We came to the land where you sent us.
It really is flowing with milk and honey. And this is its fruit.
[28] But the people who live in the land are strong.
And the cities have strong walls and are very large.
We also saw the descendants of Anak there.
[29] The Amalekites live in the South.
The Hittites, the Jebusites, and the Amorites live in the hill country.
And the Canaanites live by the sea and along the Jordan River.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The report begins well: The land really is as wonderful as God promised.
> But then comes the big “but.” The spies only see the problems now.

---

## Caleb against the others (Verses 30–33)

[30] Caleb calmed the people in front of Moses and said,
“Let’s go up right away and take the land!
We can surely do it!”
[31] But the men who had gone up with him said,
“We cannot go up against these people.
For they are stronger than we are.”
[32] And they spread a bad report among the people of Israel about the land they had explored. They said,
“The land we went through to explore is a land that eats up the people who live in it.
And all the people we saw in it are very tall.
[33] We also saw the Nephilim there, the descendants of Anak, who come from the Nephilim.
We felt like grasshoppers.
And that is how we looked to them too.”

> **What does this mean?**
> All twelve saw the same things. But they come to different conclusions.
> Ten see only the giants and the walls. They forget that God is with them. They even exaggerate: Before, the land was good. Now it “eats up the people who live in it.”
> Caleb sees the same giants. But he trusts that God is stronger.
> The “Nephilim” are legendary giants from ancient times (Genesis 6:4). The spies want to say: We have no chance against these people.
> “We felt like grasshoppers” – that is a sad sentence. Whoever makes himself small also believes that others see him as small. Fear distorts the way we see things.
