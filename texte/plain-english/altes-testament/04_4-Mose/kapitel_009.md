# Numbers – Chapter 9
### The second Passover and the cloud over the sanctuary

---

## The Passover in the wilderness (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai,
in the first month of the second year after they had come out of the land of Egypt.
He said:
[2] “The people of Israel shall keep the Passover at its appointed time.
[3] On the fourteenth day of this month, in the evening, you shall keep it at its appointed time.
You shall keep it by all its statutes and by all its rules.”
[4] Moses told the people of Israel that they should keep the Passover.
[5] And they kept the Passover in the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, in the evening, in the wilderness of Sinai.
The people of Israel did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> One year after leaving Egypt, Israel celebrates the Passover again for the first time. They remember the night when they were set free.
> In time, this comes before Chapter 1. The book of Numbers does not always tell things in their exact order.

---

## When someone cannot keep the Passover (Verses 6–14)

[6] But there were some men who were unclean because they had touched the dead body of a person.
So they could not keep the Passover on that day.
They came to Moses and Aaron on that day.
[7] These men said to him:
“We are unclean because we touched the dead body of a person.
Why should we be kept back?
Why may we not bring the offering of the LORD at its appointed time with the people of Israel?”
[8] Moses answered them:
“Wait. I will hear what the LORD commands about you.”

[9] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[10] “Tell the people of Israel:
‘Someone among you or your descendants may be unclean because he touched a dead body.
Or he may be on a long journey far away.
He shall still keep the Passover for the LORD.
[11] They shall keep it in the second month, on the fourteenth day, in the evening.
They shall eat it with bread made without yeast and with bitter herbs.
[12] They shall leave none of it until the morning.
And they shall not break any of its bones.
They shall keep it by all the statutes of the Passover.
[13] But a man may be clean and not on a journey
and still fail to keep the Passover.
That person shall be cut off from his people.
Because he did not bring the offering of the LORD at its appointed time,
that man shall carry his sin.

[14] “‘A foreigner may live among you and want to keep the Passover for the LORD.
Then he shall keep it by the statute of the Passover and by its rules.
You shall have one and the same statute for the foreigner and for the one who was born in the land.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Some men could not join the celebration. They had touched a dead body, maybe because they had buried a relative. They are sad to be left out. They ask Moses.
> Moses does not know the answer. He says honestly: “Wait. I will hear what the LORD commands about you.” Even a great leader does not have to know everything.
> And God gives a gracious answer: Whoever cannot keep it may keep the Passover one month later. Nobody shall stay left out just because he was prevented. Judaism still has this “second Passover” (Pesach Sheni) today.
> “They shall not break any of its bones” – the Gospel of John remembers this rule when Jesus dies on the cross and none of his bones are broken (John 19:36). Christians see Jesus as the Passover lamb.
> Foreigners may also keep the Passover if they hold on to Israel’s God. The same law applies to them.

## The cloud leads the people (Verses 15–23)

[15] On the day when the Tabernacle was set up,
the cloud covered the Tabernacle, the Tent of the Testimony.
In the evening, something that looked like fire was over the Tabernacle until the morning.
[16] It was always like this:
The cloud covered it, and at night it looked like fire.
[17] Whenever the cloud rose up from over the Tent, the people of Israel set out after it.
And in the place where the cloud stopped, there the people of Israel set up their camp.
[18] At the command of the LORD the people of Israel set out,
and at the command of the LORD they set up camp.
As long as the cloud stayed over the Tabernacle, they stayed in the camp.

[19] When the cloud stayed over the Tabernacle for many days,
the people of Israel kept the LORD’s command and did not set out.
[20] Sometimes the cloud was over the Tabernacle for only a few days.
Then they stayed in the camp at the command of the LORD,
and they set out at the command of the LORD.
[21] Sometimes the cloud was there only from evening until morning.
When the cloud rose up in the morning, they set out.
Or if it stayed a day and a night: When the cloud rose up, they set out.
[22] It could be two days, or a month, or a year that the cloud stayed over the Tabernacle –
the people of Israel stayed in the camp and did not set out.
But when it rose up, they set out.
[23] At the command of the LORD they set up camp,
and at the command of the LORD they set out.
They kept the LORD’s command, at the command of the LORD through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The cloud over the sanctuary is the visible sign that God is there. In the daytime it is a cloud. At night it shines like fire.
> Israel does not make its own travel plans. God decides when they move on and when they stay. Sometimes only one night, sometimes a whole year.
> That was surely not always easy. Nobody knew how long they would stay. They had to be ready every day. It is a practice in trust: God knows the way.
> Seven times these verses say “at the command of the LORD.” The whole life of the people is guided by God.
