# Numbers – Chapter 14
### The people refuse – 40 years in the wilderness

---

## The people want to go back to Egypt (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then the whole community raised their voices and cried out.
And the people wept that night.
[2] All the people of Israel complained against Moses and against Aaron.
The whole community said to them,
“We wish we had died in the land of Egypt!
Or we wish we had died in this wilderness!
[3] Why is the LORD bringing us into this land, only to be killed by the sword?
Our wives and our little children will be captured or killed!
Wouldn’t it be better for us to go back to Egypt?”
[4] And they said to each other,
“Let’s choose a leader and go back to Egypt!”
[5] Then Moses and Aaron fell on their faces in front of the whole assembly of the community of the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the saddest moments in the history of Israel. They are standing right in front of the goal. God has set them free from Egypt, led them through the sea, and fed them in the wilderness. And now they want to go back into slavery.
> They even want to choose a new leader. This is a rebellion against Moses and against God.
> Moses and Aaron throw themselves to the ground. Maybe out of horror, maybe to pray to God.

---

## Joshua and Caleb plead with the people (Verses 6–10)

[6] Joshua the son of Nun and Caleb the son of Jephunneh were among the men who had explored the land.
They tore their clothes.
[7] They said to the whole community of the people of Israel,
“The land we went through to explore is a very, very good land.
[8] If the LORD is pleased with us, he will bring us into this land and give it to us,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
[9] Only do not rebel against the LORD!
And do not be afraid of the people of the land.
For we will eat them up like bread.
Their protection has left them, and the LORD is with us.
Do not be afraid of them!”
[10] But the whole community threatened to stone them with stones.
Then the glory of the LORD appeared at the Tent of Meeting to all the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua and Caleb tear their clothes. That is a sign of great grief and despair.
> Their message is simple: “The LORD is with us. Do not be afraid of them!” What matters is not how strong the enemies are, but how strong God is.
> But the people want to stone the two of them. Sometimes people do not want to hear the truth. Instead they silence the person who tells it.
> At this moment, God steps in.

---

## God wants to destroy the people – Moses pleads (Verses 11–19)

[11] The LORD said to Moses,
“How long will these people treat me with contempt?
How long will they not believe in me,
in spite of all the signs I have done among them?
[12] I will strike them with a deadly disease and reject them.
And I will make you into a nation that is greater and stronger than they are.”

[13] Moses said to the LORD,
“Then the Egyptians will hear about it.
For with your power you brought these people up out of their country.
[14] They will tell it to the people who live in this land.
They have heard that you, LORD, are among these people.
For you, LORD, are seen face to face.
Your cloud stands over them.
And you go ahead of them, in a pillar of cloud by day and in a pillar of fire by night.
[15] Now if you kill these people all at once, like one man,
then the nations that have heard how famous you are will say,
[16] ‘The LORD was not able to bring these people into the land he promised them with an oath.
That is why he slaughtered them in the wilderness.’
[17] Now please let the power of the Lord be shown to be great,
just as you have said:
[18] ‘The LORD is slow to anger and rich in loving kindness.
He forgives wrongdoing and disobedience.
But he will not simply let the guilty go free.
He brings the wrongdoing of the fathers on the children, on the third and on the fourth generation.’
[19] Please forgive the wrongdoing of these people, because your loving kindness is so great,
just as you have forgiven these people from Egypt until now.”

> **What does this mean?**
> As with the golden calf (Exodus 32), God wants to destroy the people and start again with Moses. And as back then, Moses stands up for the people.
> Moses reminds God of his own words: “slow to become angry, and rich in loving kindness and faithfulness … He forgives wrongdoing” (Exodus 34:6–7). Moses prays with God’s own words.
> Interesting: God’s true strength does not show in punishing, but in forgiving (verse 17).

---

## God forgives – but this generation will not come into the land (Verses 20–25)

[20] The LORD said,
“I have forgiven them, as you asked.
[21] But as surely as I live, and as surely as the whole earth will be filled with the glory of the LORD:
[22] All these men have seen my glory and my signs,
which I did in Egypt and in the wilderness.
And yet they have tested me these ten times
and have not listened to my voice.
[23] So they will surely not see the land that I promised their fathers with an oath.
None of those who treated me with contempt will see it.
[24] But my servant Caleb has a different spirit in him,
and he has followed me completely.
So I will bring him into the land where he went.
And his descendants will own it.
[25] The Amalekites and the Canaanites live in the valley.
So tomorrow turn around and go into the wilderness, on the road to the Red Sea.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God forgives. The people are not destroyed. But forgiveness does not mean that there are no consequences. Whoever does not trust God cannot go into a land that can only be won with trust.
> “Ten times” means: again and again. The number ten stands for completeness.
> Caleb has “a different spirit.” He sees the same things as the others, but he has trust instead of fear.
> “Red Sea”: That is what our English source text says. In Hebrew it says “Sea of Reeds.” Here it probably means the Gulf of Aqaba.

## The punishment: 40 years in the wilderness (Verses 26–35)

[26] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[27] “How long must I put up with this evil community that complains against me?
I have heard the complaints that the people of Israel make against me.
[28] Tell them,
‘As surely as I live, says the LORD:
Just as you have spoken in my ears, so I will do to you.
[29] Your dead bodies will fall in this wilderness:
all of you who were counted, your whole number,
everyone 20 years old and older who has complained against me.
[30] You will surely not come into the land
where I promised with an oath to let you live,
except Caleb the son of Jephunneh and Joshua the son of Nun.
[31] But your little children, who you said would be captured or killed,
I will bring them in.
They will get to know the land that you have rejected.
[32] But as for you, your dead bodies will fall in this wilderness.
[33] Your children will wander in the wilderness for 40 years.
They will carry the results of your unfaithfulness, which was like prostitution,
until your dead bodies have all come to an end in the wilderness.
[34] You explored the land for 40 days.
For each day there will be one year.
So you will carry your guilt for 40 years.
And you will know what it means when I turn away from you.’
[35] I, the LORD, have spoken.
I will surely do this to this whole evil community that has gathered together against me.
In this wilderness they will come to an end, and there they will die.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The punishment is bitter. But it is exactly what the people themselves said. They had cried out: “we wish we had died in this wilderness!” (verse 2). Now God says: “Just as you have spoken in my ears, so I will do to you” (verse 28).
> And they had said that their children would die. God says: No, your children are the very ones who will receive the land. The ones you were afraid for will be saved.
> 40 days of exploring, 40 years in the wilderness. A whole generation dies in the wilderness. Only the new generation will go into the land.
> “Unfaithfulness, which was like prostitution” – that is often how the Bible describes being unfaithful to God. God and his people are like a married couple.
> The New Testament tells this story as a warning: “don’t harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness” (Hebrews 3:7–19).

---

## The ten spies die (Verses 36–38)

[36] Moses had sent men to explore the land.
They came back and made the whole community complain against him,
because they spread a bad report about the land.
[37] These men who spread the bad report about the land
died by a plague before the LORD.
[38] But of the men who went to explore the land,
Joshua the son of Nun and Caleb the son of Jephunneh stayed alive.

> **What does this mean?**
> The ten spies carry a special responsibility. They infected the whole people with their fear.
> Joshua and Caleb survive. They will be the only ones of their generation to enter the land.

---

## Too late: the failed attack (Verses 39–45)

[39] Moses told these words to all the people of Israel.
And the people mourned greatly.
[40] They got up early in the morning and went up to the top of the mountain. They said,
“Here we are!
We will go up to the place the LORD has promised.
For we have sinned.”
[41] Moses said,
“Why are you now disobeying the LORD’s command?
It will not succeed.
[42] Do not go up, for the LORD is not among you.
Otherwise your enemies will defeat you.
[43] For the Amalekites and the Canaanites are there in front of you.
And you will be killed by the sword.
Because you have turned away from following the LORD,
the LORD will not be with you.”
[44] But they dared to go up to the top of the mountain anyway.
But the ark of the LORD’s covenant and Moses did not leave the camp.
[45] Then the Amalekites and the Canaanites who lived on that mountain came down.
They attacked them and beat them, all the way to Hormah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now that it is too late, they want to go up after all. But this is not real trust in God. It is stubbornness. Yesterday God said, “Go!” and they did not want to. Today God says, “Stay!” and they go anyway.
> They set out without God and without the ark of the covenant. And they lose.
> The story shows: It is not only about doing the right thing. It is about doing it at the right time and with God.
