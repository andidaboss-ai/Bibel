# Numbers – Chapter 33
### The stops on the journey through the wilderness

---

## Moses writes down the way (Verses 1–4)

[1] These are the journeys of the people of Israel
when they went out of the land of Egypt in their armies,
led by Moses and Aaron.
[2] At the LORD’s command, Moses wrote down the starting points of their journeys.
These are their journeys, by their starting points.

[3] They set out from Rameses in the first month, on the fifteenth day of the first month.
On the day after the Passover, the people of Israel went out with a raised hand,
in front of the eyes of all the Egyptians.
[4] At that time the Egyptians were burying all their firstborn, whom the LORD had struck down among them.
The LORD had also brought judgment on their gods.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is like a travel diary. It lists all the places where Israel camped on the way from Egypt to the Jordan. Moses himself wrote this list.
> “With a raised hand” means: freely, bravely, without hiding. The slaves do not leave in secret. They go out as free people.
> “Judgment on their gods”: The plagues in Egypt showed that the gods of Egypt have no power against the LORD.

---

## From Egypt to Mount Sinai (Verses 5–15)

[5] The people of Israel set out from Rameses and camped in Succoth.
[6] They set out from Succoth and camped in Etham, which is at the edge of the wilderness.
[7] They set out from Etham and turned back to Pihahiroth, which is across from Baal Zephon.
And they camped across from Migdol.
[8] They set out from Hahiroth and went through the middle of the sea into the wilderness.
They traveled three days in the wilderness of Etham and camped in Marah.
[9] They set out from Marah and came to Elim.
In Elim there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees. They camped there.
[10] They set out from Elim and camped by the Red Sea.
[11] They set out from the Red Sea and camped in the wilderness of Sin.
[12] They set out from the wilderness of Sin and camped in Dophkah.
[13] They set out from Dophkah and camped in Alush.
[14] They set out from Alush and camped in Rephidim.
There was no water there for the people to drink.
[15] They set out from Rephidim and camped in the wilderness of Sinai.

> **What does this mean?**
> We know many of these places from the book of Exodus: the way through the sea (Chapter 14), the bitter water of Marah (Chapter 15), the oasis of Elim with its palm trees, the water from the rock at Rephidim (Chapter 17), and finally Mount Sinai.
> “Red Sea”: That is what the English source text, the World English Bible, says. The Hebrew says “Yam Suf.” That literally means “Sea of Reeds.”

---

## From Sinai to Kadesh (Verses 16–36)

[16] They set out from the wilderness of Sinai and camped in Kibroth Hattaavah.
[17] They set out from Kibroth Hattaavah and camped in Hazeroth.
[18] They set out from Hazeroth and camped in Rithmah.
[19] They set out from Rithmah and camped in Rimmon Perez.
[20] They set out from Rimmon Perez and camped in Libnah.
[21] They set out from Libnah and camped in Rissah.
[22] They set out from Rissah and camped in Kehelathah.
[23] They set out from Kehelathah and camped at Mount Shepher.
[24] They set out from Mount Shepher and camped in Haradah.
[25] They set out from Haradah and camped in Makheloth.
[26] They set out from Makheloth and camped in Tahath.
[27] They set out from Tahath and camped in Terah.
[28] They set out from Terah and camped in Mithkah.
[29] They set out from Mithkah and camped in Hashmonah.
[30] They set out from Hashmonah and camped in Moseroth.
[31] They set out from Moseroth and camped in Bene Jaakan.
[32] They set out from Bene Jaakan and camped in Hor Haggidgad.
[33] They set out from Hor Haggidgad and camped in Jotbathah.
[34] They set out from Jotbathah and camped in Abronah.
[35] They set out from Abronah and camped in Ezion Geber.
[36] They set out from Ezion Geber and camped at Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Kibroth Hattaavah means “graves of craving.” People died there after they greedily ate quails (Chapter 11). At Hazeroth, Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses (Chapter 12).
> Most places in this list are not mentioned anywhere else. Today people often no longer know where they were. These are the many years in the wilderness that the Bible otherwise tells little about.
> Ezion Geber is on the Gulf of Aqaba, on the Red Sea. Later King Solomon had a harbor there (1 Kings 9:26).

---

## Aaron’s death and the way to Moab (Verses 37–49)

[37] They set out from Kadesh and camped at Mount Hor, at the edge of the land of Edom.
[38] At the LORD’s command, Aaron the priest went up Mount Hor and died there.
That was in the fortieth year after the people of Israel had come out of the land of Egypt,
in the fifth month, on the first day of the month.
[39] Aaron was 123 years old when he died on Mount Hor.
[40] The Canaanite king of Arad, who lived in the South in the land of Canaan,
heard that the people of Israel were coming.

[41] They set out from Mount Hor and camped in Zalmonah.
[42] They set out from Zalmonah and camped in Punon.
[43] They set out from Punon and camped in Oboth.
[44] They set out from Oboth and camped in Iye Abarim, at the border of Moab.
[45] They set out from Iyim and camped in Dibon Gad.
[46] They set out from Dibon Gad and camped in Almon Diblathaim.
[47] They set out from Almon Diblathaim and camped in the mountains of Abarim, across from Nebo.
[48] They set out from the mountains of Abarim and camped in the plains of Moab,
by the Jordan, at Jericho.
[49] They camped by the Jordan, from Beth Jeshimoth all the way to Abel Shittim, in the plains of Moab.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only here does the Bible say exactly when Aaron died and how old he was. His death was told in Chapter 20.
> The king of Arad is mentioned briefly. He had attacked Israel (Numbers 21:1–3).
> “Iyim” is a short form of Iye Abarim. It is the same place.
> If you count all the camping places, with Rameses at the beginning, you get 42 stops. Jewish teachers have said: God saw every step of his people. No stop was forgotten. It is the same in the life of every person: God knows every stage, even the hard ones.

---

## The task for the land of Canaan (Verses 50–56)

[50] The LORD spoke to Moses in the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, at Jericho.
He said,
[51] “Speak to the people of Israel, and tell them,
‘When you cross the Jordan into the land of Canaan,
[52] you must drive out all the people who live in the land before you.
Destroy all their idols of stone.
Destroy all their images made of melted metal.
And tear down all their high places.
[53] You must take the land as your own and live in it.
For I have given you the land, so that you may own it.
[54] You must divide the land by lot as an inheritance, by your families.
To the larger groups you must give a larger inheritance,
and to the smaller ones a smaller inheritance.
Wherever the lot falls for someone, that will be his.
You will inherit by the tribes of your fathers.
[55] But if you do not drive out the people who live in the land before you,
then those of them you let stay will be like splinters in your eyes
and like thorns in your sides.
They will cause you trouble in the land where you live.
[56] And this will happen:
What I planned to do to them, I will do to you.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> “High places” were places on hills where people brought offerings to foreign gods.
> God commands them to drive out the people who live there and to destroy their idols. The reason is in verse 55: Israel must not be led into worshiping idols. Later in Israel’s history, that really did happen again and again.
> Verse 56 is a serious warning: If Israel becomes like the nations, then Israel will fare like them. Israel can lose the land again too. Later that happened in the exile in Babylon.
> This passage belongs to a certain time and situation. It is not a task for today. Nobody may use it to drive people off their land.
