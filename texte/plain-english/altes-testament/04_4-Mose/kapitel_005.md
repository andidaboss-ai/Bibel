# Numbers – Chapter 5
### Purity in the camp, making things right, suspicion of adultery

---

## The unclean outside the camp (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[2] “Command the people of Israel to send out of the camp
everyone who has leprosy, everyone who has a discharge,
and everyone who has become unclean by touching a dead body.
Leprosy here means: a serious skin disease.
[3] You shall send out both men and women. Send them outside the camp,
so that they do not make their camp unclean. I live in the middle of it.”
[4] The people of Israel did this and sent them outside the camp.
As the LORD had told Moses, so the people of Israel did.

> **What does this mean?**
> God lives in the middle of the camp. That is why the camp shall be clean. Whoever is unclean has to stay outside for a while, until he is clean again.
> This also protected people from diseases that spread easily. But for those who were affected, it was a hard time. The rules for skin diseases and discharges are in Leviticus 13–15.

---

## Confessing and making things right (Verses 5–10)

[5] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[6] “Speak to the people of Israel:
‘A man or a woman may commit any sin that people commit
and so be unfaithful to the LORD.
Then that person is guilty.
[7] He shall confess the sin that he has done.
And he shall pay back the damage in full
and add one fifth to it.
He shall give it to the person he has wronged.
[8] But the person may have no relative to whom the damage can be paid back.
Then the payment for the guilt belongs to the LORD, that means: to the priest.
This is in addition to the ram of atonement, with which atonement is made for the guilty person.
Atonement means: the guilt is taken away, and there is peace with God again.
[9] Every offering that is lifted up from all the holy gifts that the people of Israel bring to the priest shall be his.
[10] The holy gifts of every person shall belong to the priest.
Whatever anyone gives to the priest shall be his.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This adds to Leviticus 6:1–7. Whoever has harmed someone else shall openly confess his guilt, pay back the damage and add 20 percent.
> What is new here: What if the person who was harmed has died and has no relatives? Then the priest gets the money. Making things right does not stop just because the person is no longer there.
> Whoever harms a person is also “unfaithful to the LORD.” Wrong done to people is also wrong done to God.

## When a man suspects his wife (Verses 11–15)

[11] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[12] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them:
‘A man’s wife may go astray and be unfaithful to him.
[13] Another man may sleep with her.
It may be hidden from the eyes of her husband and kept secret.
She has made herself unclean,
but there is no witness against her,
and she was not caught in the act.
[14] And a spirit of jealousy comes over her husband,
and he is jealous of his wife, and she really has made herself unclean.
Or a spirit of jealousy comes over him,
and he is jealous of his wife, but she has not made herself unclean.
[15] Then the man shall bring his wife to the priest.
And he shall bring her offering for her:
about 2 quarts of barley flour.
He shall not pour any oil on it or put any frankincense on it.
Frankincense is a sweet-smelling resin that is burned.
For it is a grain offering of jealousy,
a grain offering of remembering that brings guilt to remembrance.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: one tenth of an ephah of barley flour.
> This is about a case that is hard for us to read today. A man suspects that his wife has cheated on him. But there is no proof and there are no witnesses.
> Verse 14 says clearly: Maybe she really has committed adultery. But maybe the man is only jealous without a reason. Both are possible.
> Back then, a jealous man could cause his wife a lot of suffering. He could send her away or even use violence. This procedure takes the matter out of his hands. He may not judge her himself. He must bring the woman to the priest. There, God decides.

## The bitter water (Verses 16–28)

[16] “‘The priest shall bring her near and set her before the LORD.
[17] The priest shall take holy water in a clay pot.
And the priest shall take some of the dust that is on the floor of the Tabernacle
and put it into the water.
[18] The priest shall set the woman before the LORD
and let the hair of her head hang loose.
He shall put the grain offering of remembering in her hands, the grain offering of jealousy.
And the priest shall hold in his hand the bitter water that brings a curse.

[19] “‘The priest shall make the woman take an oath and say to her:
“If no man has slept with you,
and if you have not gone astray and made yourself unclean
while you were under your husband’s care,
then may this bitter water that brings a curse not harm you.
[20] But you may have gone astray
while you were under your husband’s care,
and you may have made yourself unclean,
and a man other than your husband may have slept with you –”
[21] then the priest shall make the woman swear the oath with the curse.
And the priest shall say to the woman:
“May the LORD make you a curse and an oath among your people,
when the LORD makes your thigh waste away and your belly swell.
[22] May this water that brings a curse go into your body
and make your belly swell and your thigh waste away.”
And the woman shall say: “Amen, Amen.”

[23] “‘The priest shall write these curses in a book.
And he shall wash them off into the bitter water.
[24] He shall make the woman drink the bitter water that brings the curse.
And the water that brings the curse shall go into her and become bitter.
[25] The priest shall take the grain offering of jealousy out of the woman’s hand.
He shall wave the grain offering before the LORD and bring it to the altar.
[26] The priest shall take a handful of the grain offering as its memorial part
and burn it on the altar.
After that he shall make the woman drink the water.

[27] “‘When he has made her drink the water, then this will happen:
If she has made herself unclean and has been unfaithful to her husband,
then the water that brings the curse will go into her and become bitter.
Her belly will swell, and her thigh will waste away.
And the woman will become a curse among her people.
[28] But if the woman has not made herself unclean, but is clean,
then she shall be free and shall be able to have children.

> **What does this mean?**
> The water is made of holy water, dust from the floor of the sanctuary and the ink of the curses that was washed off. Of course this water is not poisonous. If something happens, then only through God himself.
> Nobody knows for sure what “thigh waste away and belly swell” exactly means. Maybe it means that the woman cannot have children or has a miscarriage. “Thigh” here is probably a polite word for the sexual organs.
> “Amen” means: “So be it.” The woman herself agrees to the oath.
> If the woman is innocent, she is declared innocent in public. Then nobody may blame her for anything anymore. This protected the woman against false suspicion.

---

## Summary (Verses 29–31)

[29] “‘This is the law for jealousy,
when a wife who is under her husband’s care goes astray and makes herself unclean,
[30] or when a spirit of jealousy comes over a man and he is jealous of his wife.
Then he shall set the woman before the LORD,
and the priest shall carry out this whole law on her.
[31] The man shall be free from guilt.
But that woman shall carry her guilt.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> For us today this procedure is very hard to bear. It is one-sided: Only the woman is tested, not the man. There is no such procedure for a man whose wife suspects him. This shows the unequal position of men and women at that time.
> The Jewish teachers also saw this procedure critically. They limited it with many conditions. In the first century AD, Rabbi Yohanan ben Zakkai ended it completely. He said: When there were more and more unfaithful men, the water could no longer be used on the women (Mishnah Sotah 9:9).
> Jesus took women and men equally seriously. He said that a man who desires another woman also already commits adultery (Matthew 5:28). And he saved a woman who had been caught in adultery from being stoned to death (John 8:1–11).
