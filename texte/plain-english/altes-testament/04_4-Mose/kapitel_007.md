# Numbers – Chapter 7
### The gifts of the 12 tribal leaders

---

## Wagons and oxen for the Levites (Verses 1–9)

[1] Moses had finished setting up the Tabernacle.
He had anointed it and made it holy, together with all its furniture.
And he had also anointed and made holy the altar with all its equipment.
[2] On that day the leaders of Israel brought gifts. They were the heads of their fathers’ houses.
They were the leaders of the tribes. They were in charge of those who had been counted.
[3] They brought their offering before the LORD:
6 covered wagons and 12 oxen,
a wagon for every 2 leaders and an ox for each leader.
They brought them in front of the Tabernacle.

[4] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[5] “Accept these from them.
They shall be used for the work at the Tent of Meeting.
Give them to the Levites, to every man according to his work.”
[6] Moses took the wagons and the oxen and gave them to the Levites.
[7] He gave two wagons and four oxen to the descendants of Gershon, according to their work.
[8] He gave four wagons and eight oxen to the descendants of Merari, according to their work,
under the direction of Ithamar, the son of Aaron the priest.
[9] But to the descendants of Kohath he gave none.
For their work was with the holy things. They carried them on their shoulders.

> **What does this mean?**
> The chapter goes back in time a little: to the day when the sanctuary was finished (Exodus 40).
> The wagons help with transport. The Merarites get the most wagons, because they carry the heavy boards and posts. The Gershonites get two wagons for the coverings and curtains.
> The Kohathites get no wagon. The holy things, like the ark of the covenant, must be carried on the shoulders. Holy things are not simply loaded up like luggage.

## 12 days, 12 gifts (Verses 10–11)

[10] The leaders brought gifts for the dedication of the altar on the day it was anointed.
Dedication means: the altar is given to God and used for the first time.
The leaders brought their offerings in front of the altar.
[11] The LORD said to Moses:
“They shall bring their offering, each leader on his own day, for the dedication of the altar.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the 12 tribal leaders bring their gifts, on 12 days one after the other. Each one brings exactly the same thing. And the Bible writes down each gift separately and completely.
> When you read it, it can seem boring. But it has a purpose: Every tribe is equally important. The small tribe gets just as much room as the big one. None is cut short. God sees every single gift.
> The gifts are: a silver plate of 130 shekels, a silver bowl of 70 shekels, both full of flour with oil, and a gold dish of 10 shekels, full of incense. Besides that, many animals for the different offerings. A shekel is about 0.4 ounces. In the text, the weight in pounds and ounces is given in brackets.

## The first day: Nahshon from the tribe of Judah (Verses 12–17)

[12] On the first day, Nahshon, the son of Amminadab, from the tribe of Judah, brought his offering.
[13] His offering was:
a silver plate that weighed 130 shekels (about 3 pounds),
and a silver bowl of 70 shekels (about 1.7 pounds), by the shekel of the sanctuary,
both of them full of fine flour mixed with oil, as a grain offering;
[14] a gold dish of 10 shekels (about 4 ounces), full of incense;
[15] one young bull, one ram and one male lamb one year old, as a burnt offering;
[16] one male goat as a sin offering;
[17] and as a peace offering: two head of cattle, five rams, five male goats and five male lambs one year old.
This was the offering of Nahshon, the son of Amminadab.

## The total of all the gifts (Verses 84–88)

[84] This was the gift from the leaders of Israel for the dedication of the altar, on the day when it was anointed:
12 silver plates, 12 silver bowls, 12 gold dishes.
[85] Each silver plate weighed 130 shekels (about 3 pounds), and each bowl 70 shekels (about 1.7 pounds).
All the silver of the dishes weighed 2,400 shekels (about 60 pounds), by the shekel of the sanctuary.
[86] The 12 gold dishes, full of incense, weighed 10 shekels (about 4 ounces) each, by the shekel of the sanctuary.
All the gold of the dishes weighed 120 shekels (about 3 pounds).
[87] All the cattle for the burnt offering were 12 bulls,
with 12 rams and 12 male lambs one year old, and their grain offering,
and 12 male goats as a sin offering.
[88] And all the cattle for the peace offering were 24 bulls,
with 60 rams, 60 male goats and 60 male lambs one year old.
This was the gift for the dedication of the altar, after it had been anointed.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible gives the weights like this: 130 shekels for each silver plate, 70 shekels for each silver bowl, together 2,400 shekels of silver. 10 shekels for each gold dish, together 120 shekels of gold.
> The math is right: 12 × 130 + 12 × 70 = 2,400. And 12 × 10 = 120.
> Altogether there were 252 animals. It was a big dedication celebration. The whole people gave together, tribe by tribe.

## God speaks to Moses (Verse 89)

[89] Moses went into the Tent of Meeting to speak with the LORD.
Then he heard the voice speaking to him
from above the mercy seat on the ark of the Testimony, from between the two cherubim.
The mercy seat is the gold lid on top of the ark. Cherubim are heavenly beings.
And the LORD spoke to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the goal of everything: The sanctuary is finished and dedicated. And now God speaks with Moses there, just as he had promised (Exodus 25:22).
> All the gifts, all the offerings, all the effort had this one purpose: that God lives among his people and speaks with them.
