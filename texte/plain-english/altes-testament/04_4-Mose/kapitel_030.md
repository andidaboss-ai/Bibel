# Numbers – Chapter 30
### Promises made to God

---

## A man must keep his promise (Verses 1–2)

[1] Moses spoke to the heads of the tribes of the people of Israel.
He said, “This is what the LORD has commanded:
[2] When a man makes a vow to the LORD,
or swears an oath to bind himself to something,
he must not break his word.
He must do everything that has come out of his mouth.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, the verses of this chapter are counted one number higher: Verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 17. Here we follow the verse numbers of the WEB, our English source text.
> A “vow” is a solemn promise to God. For example: “I will bring an offering,” or “I will fast for a while.”
> The rule is clear: What you promise to God, you must keep. Words have weight. Jesus later says: “Let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No.’” (Matthew 5:37).

---

## A young woman in her father’s house (Verses 3–5)

[3] “Also, when a woman makes a vow to the LORD and binds herself to something,
while she is still young and lives in her father’s house,
[4] and her father hears her vow and her promise with which she has bound herself,
and her father says nothing to her,
then all her vows will stand.
And every promise with which she has bound herself will stand.
[5] But if her father forbids it on the day he hears about it,
then none of her vows and none of her promises with which she has bound herself will stand.
The LORD will forgive her, because her father has forbidden it.

---

## A married woman (Verses 6–8)

[6] “If she gets married while her vows are on her,
or a hasty word from her lips with which she has bound herself,
[7] and her husband hears it and says nothing to her on the day he hears it,
then her vows will stand.
And her promises with which she has bound herself will stand.
[8] But if her husband forbids it on the day he hears it,
then he cancels the vow that is on her,
and the hasty word from her lips with which she has bound herself.
The LORD will forgive her.

---

## A widow or a divorced woman (Verse 9)

[9] “But the vow of a widow or of a divorced woman,
everything with which she has bound herself, will stand for her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Widows and divorced women are independent. They decide about their vows themselves, just like men.

---

## A vow in marriage (Verses 10–16)

[10] “If she made a vow in her husband’s house,
or bound herself to something with an oath,
[11] and her husband heard it and said nothing to her and did not forbid it,
then all her vows will stand.
And every promise with which she bound herself will stand.
[12] But if her husband completely canceled them on the day he heard them,
then nothing that came from her lips will stand,
not her vows and not her promise.
Her husband has canceled them. The LORD will forgive her.
[13] Every vow and every oath that binds her to humble herself,
her husband may confirm it, or her husband may cancel it.
[14] But if her husband says nothing to her from one day to the next,
then he confirms all her vows and all her promises that are on her.
He has confirmed them, because he said nothing to her on the day he heard them.
[15] But if he cancels them later, after he has heard them,
then he will carry her guilt.”

[16] These are the rules that the LORD commanded Moses,
between a man and his wife,
and between a father and his daughter, while she is young and lives in her father’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Humble herself here means: to fast or to give up something.
> These rules show how the family was organized back then. The father or the husband was responsible for the household. That is why he could cancel a vow of his daughter or his wife. For example, if she had promised to offer something valuable from the family’s property.
> From today’s point of view, this is unequal. Men could decide for themselves, women could not always do so. Here the Bible describes the order of its own time. Today men and women have equal rights. That fits the dignity that God gives every person (Genesis 1:27; Galatians 3:28).
> But there is also protection for the woman: The man must react right away, on the same day. He cannot cancel a vow later just as he likes. If he does it anyway, he carries the guilt, not the woman.
> And when a vow is canceled, God forgives the woman. She is not guilty.
