# Numbers – Chapter 11
### The people complain – meat, quails and God’s Spirit

---

## Fire at the edge of the camp (Verses 1–3)

[1] The people complained loudly in the ears of the LORD.
When the LORD heard it, he became angry.
The fire of the LORD burned among them
and burned up some of the edge of the camp.
[2] Then the people cried out to Moses.
And Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire died down.
[3] That place was named Taberah,
because the fire of the LORD had burned among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people have been traveling for only three days, and already they begin to complain. We are not told exactly what about.
> Here a sad series of stories begins: Again and again the people grumble against God and against Moses. This “grumbling” goes through the whole book of Numbers.
> “Taberah” means “burning.”
> Moses prays for the people, even though they complain. He speaks up for them before God, as he already did with the golden calf (Exodus 32).

---

## “Who will give us meat?” (Verses 4–9)

[4] The mixed crowd that was among them was filled with greedy craving.
And the children of Israel also cried again and said:
“Who will give us meat to eat?
[5] We remember the fish that we ate in Egypt for free,
the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions and the garlic.
[6] But now we have lost our appetite.
We see nothing at all except this manna.”

[7] The manna was like coriander seed.
And it looked like bdellium.
[8] The people went around and gathered it.
They ground it in hand mills or crushed it in mortars.
They boiled it in pots and made flat cakes from it.
It tasted like fresh oil.
[9] When the dew fell on the camp at night, the manna fell on it too.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “mixed crowd” are people who were not Israelites but had left Egypt together with them (Exodus 12:38). They start, and the Israelites join in.
> It is amazing: They remember the good food in Egypt that they got “for free.” They have forgotten that they were slaves, had to work hard, and that their children were killed. The memory of the past becomes nicer than it really was.
> God gives them manna every day, bread from heaven. But they are tired of it. They no longer see the miracle, only the same thing every day.
> Bdellium is a clear, yellowish resin.

## Moses cannot go on (Verses 10–15)

[10] Moses heard the people crying, family by family, every man at the door of his tent.
Then the LORD became very angry.
And Moses was upset too.
[11] Moses said to the LORD:
“Why have you treated your servant so badly?
Why have I not found favor in your eyes,
that you put the burden of this whole people on me?
[12] Did I conceive this whole people?
Did I give birth to them,
that you tell me: ‘Carry them in your arms, as a nurse carries a baby,
to the land that you swore to give to their fathers’?
[13] Where can I get meat to give to this whole people?
For they cry to me and say: ‘Give us meat to eat!’
[14] I cannot carry this whole people alone.
It is too heavy for me.
[15] If you treat me like this,
then please kill me right now, if I have found favor in your eyes.
Then I will not have to see my misery anymore.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the Bible shows a great leader at the end of his strength. Moses is exhausted and desperate. He even wishes he were dead.
> Moses speaks quite openly with God, even with complaints. And God does not punish him for it. He listens and helps. You may tell God everything, even your despair.
> The picture is strong: Moses feels like a mother who has to carry a screaming baby. But he asks: “Did I give birth to them?” In this way he is saying indirectly: You, God, gave birth to this people. You must carry them.
> Other great people in the Bible were also so exhausted that they wanted to die, for example Elijah (1 Kings 19:4) and Jonah (Jonah 4:3).
> Whoever feels like this today is not alone. It is good to talk to someone about it and to look for help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## God promises help and meat (Verses 16–23)

[16] The LORD said to Moses:
“Gather for me 70 men from the elders of Israel,
men you know to be elders of the people and officers over them.
Elders are older men who lead the people.
Bring them to the Tent of Meeting. Let them stand there with you.
[17] I will come down and speak with you there.
I will take some of the Spirit that is on you and put it on them.
Then they shall carry the burden of the people with you,
so that you do not have to carry it alone.

[18] “And say to the people:
‘Make yourselves holy for tomorrow. Then you will eat meat.
For you have cried in the ears of the LORD and said:
“Who will give us meat to eat? We were well off in Egypt.”
So the LORD will give you meat, and you will eat.
[19] You will not eat it for only one day, or two days, or five days, or ten days, or twenty days,
[20] but for a whole month,
until it comes out of your nostrils and you are sick of it.
For you have rejected the LORD, who is among you,
and you have cried before him and said: “Why did we ever come out of Egypt?”’”

[21] Moses said:
“The people I live among are 600,000 men on foot.
And you say: ‘I will give them meat, so that they can eat for a whole month.’
[22] Shall flocks and herds be slaughtered for them, so that it is enough for them?
Or shall all the fish in the sea be caught for them, so that it is enough for them?”
[23] The LORD said to Moses:
“Has the LORD’s hand become too short?
Now you will see whether my word comes true for you or not.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers two problems: Moses gets helpers, and the people get meat.
> The helpers get some of the Spirit that is on Moses. Moses does not lose anything by it. God’s Spirit is shared, but it does not become less. It is like a candle that is used to light other candles.
> But the meat is not simply a gift. “Until it comes out of your nostrils” – God gives them what they want, far too much of it. Sometimes what we want so badly is not good for us.
> Even Moses doubts whether God can do this. God’s answer: “Has the LORD’s hand become too short?” That means: Is anything too hard for me?

## The Spirit comes on the 70 elders (Verses 24–30)

[24] Moses went out and told the people the words of the LORD.
He gathered 70 men from the elders of the people
and placed them all around the Tent.
[25] The LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him.
He took some of the Spirit that was on him and put it on the 70 elders.
When the Spirit rested on them, they prophesied.
To prophesy means here: to speak words given by God’s Spirit.
But after that they did not do it again.

[26] But two men had stayed in the camp.
One was named Eldad, and the other was named Medad.
The Spirit rested on them too.
They were among those whose names had been written down,
but they had not gone out to the Tent.
And they prophesied in the camp.
[27] Then a young man ran and told Moses:
“Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp!”
[28] Joshua, the son of Nun, answered. He was Moses’ helper and one of his chosen men.
He said: “My lord Moses, stop them!”
[29] Moses said to him:
“Are you jealous for my sake?
I wish that all the LORD’s people were prophets,
and that the LORD would put his Spirit on them all!”
[30] Then Moses went back into the camp, he and the elders of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Prophesying” here probably means: They were seized by God’s Spirit and spoke with great excitement. Maybe they praised God loudly. It was a visible sign that God’s Spirit was on them.
> Eldad and Medad are not in the right place. Still, God’s Spirit comes on them. God cannot be locked in, not even in his own sanctuary.
> Joshua wants to protect Moses. He is afraid that Moses will lose respect. But Moses is generous: “I wish that all the LORD’s people were prophets!” Moses does not want power for himself alone. He is glad when others receive God’s Spirit too.
> This wish of Moses comes true later. Through the prophet Joel, God announces: After this I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh (Joel 2:28; in some Bibles 3:1). “All flesh” means: all people. And at Pentecost, Peter says that this has now happened (Acts 2:17).

## The quails and the punishment (Verses 31–35)

[31] Then a wind went out from the LORD
and brought quails from the sea.
Quails are small birds.
It let them fall all around the camp,
about a day’s journey on one side and a day’s journey on the other side,
and about 3 feet above the ground.
[32] The people got up and gathered the quails all that day, all that night and all the next day.
Whoever gathered the least had about 580 gallons.
And they spread them out for themselves all around the camp.
[33] The meat was still between their teeth, before it was even chewed,
when the LORD became angry with the people.
And the LORD struck the people with a very terrible plague.
[34] That place was named Kibroth Hattaavah,
because there they buried the people who had been greedy.
[35] From Kibroth Hattaavah the people traveled on to Hazeroth.
And they stayed at Hazeroth.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: two cubits high and ten homers. A cubit is about 18 inches. A homer is about 58 gallons.
> Quails are small birds that fly over this area in large flocks twice a year. When they are tired, they fly very low, and you can catch them easily. God sends a wind that brings them right to the camp.
> “Two cubits high” can mean that the birds lay on top of each other that high. Or that they flew so low that people could catch them with their hands.
> The people gather as if they were drunk, for two days and a night. Their greed is the problem, not the meat.
> “Kibroth Hattaavah” means “graves of craving.” The place reminds us that greed can kill.
> Psalm 106:15 says about this: God gave them what they asked for, but he sent a wasting disease into their souls. That means: They got what they wanted. But it was not good for them.
