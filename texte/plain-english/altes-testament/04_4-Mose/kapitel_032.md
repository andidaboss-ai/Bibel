# Numbers – Chapter 32
### Reuben and Gad want to stay east of the Jordan

---

## The request of Reuben and Gad (Verses 1–5)

[1] The people of Reuben and the people of Gad had a very large number of livestock.
They saw the land of Jazer and the land of Gilead.
They saw that the area was a good place for livestock.
[2] So the people of Gad and the people of Reuben came.
They spoke to Moses, to Eleazar the priest and to the leaders of the community.
They said,
[3] “Ataroth, Dibon, Jazer, Nimrah, Heshbon, Elealeh, Sebam, Nebo and Beon,
[4] the land that the LORD defeated before the community of Israel,
is a land for livestock.
And your servants have livestock.”
[5] They said,
“If we have found favor in your eyes,
let this land be given to your servants as their property.
Do not take us across the Jordan.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people are still camping east of the Jordan. Israel has just conquered this land from the kings Sihon and Og (Chapter 21).
> The tribes of Reuben and Gad raise livestock. They see that the land here has good pastures. They want to stay. They do not want to move into the actual land of Canaan on the other side of the river.

---

## Moses becomes angry (Verses 6–15)

[6] Moses said to the people of Gad and to the people of Reuben,
“Should your brothers go to war while you sit here?
[7] Why are you taking away the courage of the people of Israel,
so that they will not go over into the land that the LORD has given them?
[8] That is what your fathers did
when I sent them from Kadesh Barnea to look at the land.
[9] They went up to the valley of Eshcol and saw the land.
Then they took away the courage of the people of Israel,
so that they would not go into the land that the LORD had given them.
[10] On that day the LORD’s anger burned, and he swore,
[11] ‘None of the men who came up out of Egypt,
20 years old and older,
will see the land that I promised with an oath to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob.
For they have not followed me with their whole heart.
[12] Only Caleb, the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite, and Joshua, the son of Nun, will see it,
for they have followed the LORD completely.’
[13] The LORD’s anger burned against Israel.
He made them wander back and forth in the wilderness for 40 years,
until the whole generation that had done evil in the LORD’s eyes was gone.
[14] Look, now you have taken your fathers’ place,
a new crowd of sinful men,
to make the LORD’s burning anger against Israel even greater.
[15] For if you turn away from following him,
he will leave the people in the wilderness again.
And you will destroy this whole people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is afraid: It could become like it was 38 years ago. Back then, ten spies took away the people’s courage (Chapters 13 and 14). That is why the people had to stay in the wilderness for 40 years.
> If two tribes now say, “We are staying here,” then the others might think, “Then why should we fight?” Moses sees the danger: The unity of the people could break apart.
> Moses says it very harshly: You are like your fathers. You are putting the whole people in danger.

---

## Reuben and Gad make an offer (Verses 16–19)

[16] They came up to him and said,
“We will build pens for our livestock here, and cities for our little children.
[17] But we ourselves will be armed and ready to go ahead of the people of Israel,
until we have brought them to their place.
Our little children will live in the cities with walls,
to be safe from the people who live in the land.
[18] We will not go back to our homes
until all the people of Israel have received their inheritance.
[19] For we will not inherit with them on the other side of the Jordan and beyond.
Our inheritance has come to us on this side of the Jordan, to the east.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Reuben and Gad understand Moses’ worry. They make a fair offer: Our families stay here in safe cities. But our men will fight together with the other tribes. They will even go first. Only when everyone has their land will they come home.
> They do not leave their brothers alone.

---

## Moses accepts the offer (Verses 20–24)

[20] Moses said to them,
“If you do this,
if you arm yourselves to go to war before the LORD,
[21] and every armed man of yours crosses the Jordan before the LORD,
until he has driven out his enemies before him,
[22] and the land is conquered before the LORD,
then afterward you may go back.
Then you will be free of your duty to the LORD and to Israel.
And this land will be your property before the LORD.
[23] But if you do not do this,
look, then you have sinned against the LORD.
And be sure that your sin will find you out.
[24] Build cities for your little children and pens for your sheep.
And do what you have promised.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Your sin will find you out” has become a well-known saying. It means: You cannot run away from your guilt. Someone who breaks a promise will feel the results sooner or later.
> Moses reminds them: Promises must be kept. Chapter 30 was about this too.

---

## The agreement is confirmed (Verses 25–32)

[25] The people of Gad and the people of Reuben spoke to Moses. They said,
“Your servants will do what my lord commands.
[26] Our little children, our wives, our flocks and all our livestock
will stay there in the cities of Gilead.
[27] But your servants will cross over,
every man who is armed for war, before the LORD into battle,
just as my lord says.”

[28] So Moses gave orders about them to Eleazar the priest,
to Joshua, the son of Nun,
and to the heads of the families of the tribes of the people of Israel.
[29] Moses said to them,
“The people of Gad and the people of Reuben may cross the Jordan with you,
every man armed for battle before the LORD,
and the land may be conquered before you.
If so, you must give them the land of Gilead as their property.
[30] But if they do not cross over with you armed,
they must receive property among you in the land of Canaan.”

[31] The people of Gad and the people of Reuben answered,
“We will do what the LORD has said to your servants.
[32] We will cross over armed before the LORD into the land of Canaan.
But the property of our inheritance will stay ours on the other side of the Jordan.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses knows that he will die soon. That is why he passes on the agreement to Eleazar and Joshua. Later they are to make sure that it is kept.
> Reuben and Gad kept their word. In the book of Joshua, their men fight together with the others. Only after that does Joshua send them home, and he praises them for their faithfulness (Joshua 22:1–4).

---

## The land east of the Jordan is divided (Verses 33–42)

[33] Moses gave them, the people of Gad, the people of Reuben
and the half-tribe of Manasseh, the son of Joseph,
the kingdom of Sihon, king of the Amorites,
and the kingdom of Og, king of Bashan.
He gave them the land with its cities and borders, the cities of the land all around.

[34] The people of Gad built Dibon, Ataroth, Aroer,
[35] Atroth-shophan, Jazer, Jogbehah,
[36] Beth Nimrah and Beth Haran: cities with walls, and pens for sheep.
[37] The people of Reuben built Heshbon, Elealeh, Kiriathaim,
[38] Nebo and Baal Meon – their names were changed – and Sibmah.
They gave other names to the cities that they built.

[39] The people of Machir, the son of Manasseh, went to Gilead.
They took it and drove out the Amorites who lived there.
[40] Moses gave Gilead to Machir, the son of Manasseh.
And he lived there.
[41] Jair, the son of Manasseh, went and took their villages.
He called them Havvoth Jair.
[42] Nobah went and took Kenath and its villages.
He called it Nobah, after his own name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now, suddenly, the half-tribe of Manasseh appears too. Some of the families of Manasseh also get land east of the Jordan, in the north. The other part later lives west of the Jordan.
> So two and a half tribes live east of the Jordan, and nine and a half tribes live in the land of Canaan.
> “Havvoth Jair” means “the tent villages of Jair.”
> Nebo and Baal Meon were names of foreign gods. That is probably why the names were changed.
