# Numbers – Chapter 25
### Israel worships Baal Peor – Phinehas steps in

---

## The people turn to foreign gods (Verses 1–5)

[1] Israel stayed in Shittim.
There the people began to sleep with the daughters of Moab, like with prostitutes.
[2] For the women invited the people to the sacrifices for their gods.
The people ate with them, and they bowed down to their gods.
[3] So Israel joined itself to Baal Peor.
Then the LORD became angry with Israel.
[4] The LORD said to Moses,
“Take all the leaders of the people, and hang them up before the LORD, in the light of the sun,
so that the burning anger of the LORD will turn away from Israel.”
[5] Moses said to the judges of Israel,
“Each of you must kill those of his men who have joined themselves to Baal Peor.”

> **What does this mean?**
> What Balaam could not do with curses now happens in another way. The women from Moab invite the Israelites to their sacrifice festivals. There is food and celebration there, and probably also sex as part of the worship of the gods.
> Later the Bible says that Balaam thought up this plan (Numbers 31:16; Revelation 2:14). He could not curse Israel, but he could lead it astray.
> “Baal Peor” is the god Baal, as he was worshiped at Mount Peor.
> This is a terrible step backward. Almost right after God’s blessing through Balaam, Israel breaks the covenant. A covenant is a binding agreement.
> The punishment is shocking for us today. The Bible tells honestly how harshly people back then dealt with turning away from God. For today, this is true: Nobody may kill people in God’s name.

---

## Phinehas steps in (Verses 6–9)

[6] Then a man from the people of Israel came.
He brought a Midianite woman to his brothers.
He did it in front of Moses and in front of the whole community of Israel,
while they were weeping at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.
[7] Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, saw it.
He got up from the middle of the community and took a spear in his hand.
[8] He went after the man of Israel into the tent.
He pierced both of them, the man of Israel and the woman, through her body.
Then the plague among the people of Israel stopped.
[9] In the plague, 24,000 people died.

> **What does this mean?**
> The whole people is weeping at the holy tent and is sorry. But one man openly brings a woman from Midian into the camp. He does it in front of Moses, as if he wanted to mock God and the people.
> Phinehas, Aaron’s grandson, kills the two. With that, a plague ends that has already killed many people.
> This act is very hard for us to understand today. It is violence. In Judaism, people discussed it a lot. The teachers said: This was a one-time exception. Nobody may point to it as a reason to use violence in God’s name themselves. They made strict rules so that nobody would copy it.
> Sadly, extremists have pointed to Phinehas again and again in history. That is a misuse of this story.

## God’s covenant with Phinehas (Verses 10–15)

[10] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[11] “Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest,
has turned my anger away from the people of Israel.
He was jealous with my jealousy among them.
So I did not destroy the people of Israel in my jealousy.
[12] So say this:
‘Look, I give him my covenant of peace.
[13] For him and for his descendants after him,
it will be a covenant of a priesthood that lasts forever.
For he was jealous for his God,
and he made atonement for the people of Israel.’”
Atonement means: putting things right again between God and people.

[14] The man of Israel who was killed with the Midianite woman
was named Zimri, the son of Salu. He was a leader of a family among the Simeonites.
[15] The Midianite woman who was killed was named Cozbi, the daughter of Zur.
He was the head of the people of a family in Midian.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Jealous here means: full of burning zeal for God.
> God gives Phinehas a “covenant of peace.” After an act of violence, that sounds strange. But it means: Phinehas stopped the plague. In this way, he brought back peace between God and the people.
> His descendants will be the high priests. Zadok, who later was high priest under King Solomon, was one of his descendants.
> The names are given because both came from important families. It was not a small matter. It was a public scandal caused by leading people.

---

## War against Midian (Verses 16–18)

[16] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[17] “Attack the Midianites, and strike them!
[18] For they attacked you with their tricks.
They led you astray in the matter of Peor,
and in the matter of Cozbi, the daughter of a leader of Midian, their sister.
She was killed on the day of the plague because of Peor.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Midianites led Israel astray on purpose, to take it away from God. That is why Israel is to fight against them. Chapter 31 tells about this.
> Interesting: Moses himself was married to a Midianite woman, Zipporah. And his father-in-law Jethro was a Midianite who helped Israel. Not all Midianites were enemies.
