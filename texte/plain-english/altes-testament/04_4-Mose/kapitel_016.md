# Numbers – Chapter 16
### The rebellion of Korah

---

## Korah and his people rebel (Verses 1–3)

[1] Korah was the son of Izhar, the son of Kohath, the son of Levi.
He gathered some men,
together with Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab,
and On, the son of Peleth. They were from the tribe of Reuben.
[2] They rose up against Moses,
together with 250 men from the people of Israel.
They were leaders of the community, men called to the assembly, well-known men.
[3] They gathered together against Moses and against Aaron and said to them,
“You go too far!
The whole community is holy, every one of them,
and the LORD is among them.
So why do you put yourselves above the LORD’s assembly?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the biggest rebellion against Moses and Aaron.
> Korah is a Levite from the family of Kohath, a cousin of Moses and Aaron. He already has an important service at the holy tent. But he wants more: He wants to be a priest like Aaron.
> Dathan, Abiram, and On come from the tribe of Reuben. Reuben was Jacob’s firstborn son. Maybe they felt pushed aside and wanted to lead.
> Their argument sounds good: “The whole community is holy!” That is even true (Exodus 19:6). But they use this sentence to get power for themselves. They do not care about God, only about themselves.

---

## Moses answers Korah (Verses 4–11)

[4] When Moses heard it, he fell on his face.
[5] He said to Korah and to his whole group,
“In the morning the LORD will show who belongs to him and who is holy.
He will let that person come near to him.
Whoever he chooses, he will let come near to him.
[6] Do this:
Korah and his whole group shall take fire pans for incense.
[7] Tomorrow put fire in them and put incense on them before the LORD.
The man whom the LORD chooses is the one who is holy.
You go too far, you sons of Levi!”

[8] Moses said to Korah,
“Listen, you sons of Levi!
[9] Is it too small a thing for you that the God of Israel has set you apart from the community of Israel?
He has brought you near to himself,
so that you do the work at the LORD’s tabernacle
and stand before the community to serve them.
[10] He has brought you near, and all your brothers, the sons of Levi, with you.
And now you want to be priests as well?
[11] That is why you and your whole group have gathered together against the LORD!
Who is Aaron, that you complain against him?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses does not answer with violence. He leaves the decision to God. God is to show whom he has chosen.
> He reminds Korah of what he already has: an honorable service. But Korah is not satisfied. Envy never sees what you have, only what others have.
> “Who is Aaron?” – Aaron did not take his office for himself. God gave it to him. Whoever rebels against Aaron is really rebelling against God.

---

## Dathan and Abiram refuse (Verses 12–15)

[12] Moses sent for Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab.
But they said, “We will not come up!
[13] Is it too small a thing that you have brought us up out of a land flowing with milk and honey,
to kill us in the wilderness?
Do you also have to make yourself a ruler over us?
[14] You have not brought us into a land flowing with milk and honey.
You have not given us fields and vineyards as our own.
Do you want to put out the eyes of these men?
We will not come up!”
[15] Then Moses became very angry and said to the LORD,
“Do not accept their offering!
I have not taken a single donkey from them,
and I have not harmed any of them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Dathan and Abiram turn the truth upside down. They call Egypt, the land of slavery, “a land flowing with milk and honey.” And they blame Moses that they are not in the promised land. But it was the people themselves who did not want to go in (Chapter 14).
> “Do you want to put out the eyes of these men?” means something like: Do you think we are stupid? We can see what is going on.
> Moses is rightly hurt. He never made himself rich at their expense.

---

## The test with the fire pans (Verses 16–22)

[16] Moses said to Korah,
“You and your whole group, come before the LORD tomorrow:
you, they, and Aaron.
[17] Each man shall take his fire pan and put incense on it.
And each man shall bring his fire pan before the LORD, 250 fire pans.
You and Aaron too, each with his fire pan.”
[18] So each man took his fire pan, put fire in it, and put incense on it.
And they stood at the door of the Tent of Meeting with Moses and Aaron.
[19] Korah gathered the whole community against them at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
Then the glory of the LORD appeared to the whole community.

[20] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[21] “Separate yourselves from this community, so that I can destroy them in a moment!”
[22] Then they fell on their faces and said,
“God, the God of the spirits of all people!
One man sins, and you want to be angry with the whole community?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Korah has stirred up the whole community. God wants to destroy them all.
> Once again Moses and Aaron plead for the people. They call God “the God of the spirits of all people.” That means: God has given every person life, and he knows each one.
> Their argument: It is not fair to punish everyone for the guilt of one person. That is a reminder of Abraham, who pleaded for Sodom (Genesis 18:23–25).

---

## The earth opens up (Verses 23–35)

[23] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[24] “Speak to the community and say,
‘Get away from around the tents of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram!’”
[25] Moses got up and went to Dathan and Abiram.
And the elders of Israel followed him.
[26] He said to the community,
“Please go away from the tents of these wicked men,
and do not touch anything that belongs to them,
so that you are not destroyed because of all their sins!”
[27] So they went away from the tents of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram on every side.
Dathan and Abiram came out and stood at the door of their tents
with their wives, their sons, and their little children.

[28] Moses said,
“This is how you will know that the LORD has sent me to do all these things,
and that they do not come from my own mind:
[29] If these men die the way all people die,
or if what happens to all people happens to them,
then the LORD has not sent me.
[30] But the LORD may do something new.
The ground may open its mouth and swallow them up with everything that belongs to them,
and they may go down alive into Sheol.
Then you will understand that these men have treated the LORD with contempt.”

[31] As soon as he had finished saying all these words, the ground under them split apart.
[32] The earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up,
with their households, all of Korah’s men, and all their belongings.
[33] They went down alive into Sheol, they and everything that belonged to them.
The earth closed over them.
And they disappeared from the assembly.
[34] All Israel around them ran away when they heard them scream.
For they said, “The earth might swallow us up too!”
[35] And fire came out from the LORD and burned up the 250 men who had offered the incense.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a terrible story. God shows in a frightening way whom he has chosen.
> “Sheol” is the Hebrew word for the world of the dead. In the old way of thinking, it is the place where all the dead go, deep under the earth.
> It is especially hard that the women and children die too. To us today this is almost unbearable. Back then, the family was seen as one unit. The Bible tells this honestly, without making it look better than it is.
> But not all of Korah’s children died. Later it says clearly: “But the sons of Korah did not die” (Numbers 26:11). Their descendants became singers in the temple. Eleven psalms have the heading “by the sons of Korah” (Psalm 42; 44–49; 84–85; 87–88). Psalm 42, for example, begins with a deer that longs for streams of water. The child of a rebel became a singer of praise to God.

## The fire pans become a warning sign (Verses 36–40)

[36] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[37] “Tell Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest
to take the fire pans out of the fire.
And he shall scatter the fire far away.
For the pans are holy,
[38] the fire pans of these men who sinned and lost their own lives.
Let them be hammered into thin plates as a covering for the altar.
For they brought them before the LORD, so they are holy.
They shall be a sign for the people of Israel.”
[39] So Eleazar the priest took the bronze fire pans
that the men who were burned had offered,
and they hammered them into a covering for the altar.
[40] This was to remind the people of Israel
that no outsider who is not a descendant of Aaron
may come near to burn incense before the LORD.
Otherwise it will happen to him as it happened to Korah and his group,
just as the LORD had told Eleazar through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, Chapter 17 already begins here. There, verses 36–50 are numbered 17:1–15. We follow the numbering of our English source text.
> The fire pans are not thrown away. They are made into a covering for the altar. Everyone who sees the altar is to remember: Only those whom God calls may do this service.

---

## The people complain again – Aaron stands between the dead and the living (Verses 41–50)

[41] But on the next day, the whole community of the people of Israel complained against Moses and Aaron. They said,
“You have killed the LORD’s people!”
[42] When the community gathered against Moses and Aaron, they looked toward the Tent of Meeting.
Look! The cloud covered it, and the glory of the LORD appeared.
[43] Moses and Aaron went to the front of the Tent of Meeting.
[44] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[45] “Get away from this community, so that I can destroy them in a moment!”
Then they fell on their faces.

[46] Moses said to Aaron,
“Take your fire pan, put fire from the altar in it, and put incense on it.
Take it quickly to the community and make atonement for them!
For anger has gone out from the LORD. The plague has begun.”
[47] Aaron did what Moses had said and ran into the middle of the assembly.
The plague had already begun among the people.
He put on the incense and made atonement for the people.
[48] He stood between the dead and the living.
And the plague was held back.
[49] The people who died in the plague were 14,700,
in addition to those who died because of Korah.
[50] Aaron went back to Moses at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
And the plague had stopped.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unbelievable: The very next day, the people complain again. They blame Moses and Aaron for the death of Korah.
> But then something wonderful happens. Aaron, the man everyone complained about, runs into the middle of the plague to save his people. He uses the same fire pan that the fight was about.
> “He stood between the dead and the living.” That is a powerful picture. The priest puts himself in danger to protect others. That is how God shows who the true priest is: not the one who wants power, but the one who risks his life for others.
> Christians see this as a picture of Jesus, who stands between God and people to save them.
