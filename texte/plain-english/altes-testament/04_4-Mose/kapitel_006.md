# Numbers – Chapter 6
### The vow of the Nazirites – the blessing of Aaron

---

## Whoever gives himself to God in a special way (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[2] “Speak to the children of Israel and tell them:
‘A man or a woman may make a special vow,
the vow of a Nazirite, to set himself apart for the LORD.
A vow is a solemn promise to God.
[3] Then he shall stay away from wine and strong drink.
He shall not drink vinegar made from wine or vinegar made from strong drink.
He shall not drink any grape juice, and he shall not eat fresh or dried grapes.
[4] As long as he is set apart, he shall eat nothing that comes from the grapevine,
from the seeds to the skins.

[5] “‘As long as his vow to be set apart lasts, no razor shall touch his head,
until the days are over in which he sets himself apart for the LORD.
He shall be holy.
He shall let the locks of the hair on his head grow long.

[6] “‘As long as he sets himself apart for the LORD, he shall not go near a dead body.
[7] He shall not make himself unclean, not even for his father, his mother, his brother or his sister when they die.
For the sign that he is set apart for God is on his head.
[8] As long as he is set apart, he is holy to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A “Nazirite” is a person who gives himself completely to God for a certain time. The word comes from the Hebrew word “nazir”: set apart, dedicated.
> Important: This is true for men and for women. Anyone could make this vow, not only priests.
> A Nazirite has three signs:
> 1. No wine and nothing from the grapevine. Wine stands for parties and for an easy life in the land.
> 2. The hair is not cut. The long hair is a sign that everyone can see: This person belongs completely to God right now.
> 3. No contact with the dead, not even with his own parents. In this he is even stricter than an ordinary priest, like the high priest (Leviticus 21:11).
> Famous Nazirites in the Bible are Samson (Judges 13), Samuel (1 Samuel 1:11) and probably John the Baptist (Luke 1:15). Paul also made such a vow (Acts 18:18; 21:23–26).

## When someone suddenly dies beside him (Verses 9–12)

[9] “‘Someone may die very suddenly beside him.
And so he makes his head that is set apart unclean.
Then he shall shave his head on the day he is made clean.
He shall shave it on the seventh day.
[10] On the eighth day he shall bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the priest,
to the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[11] The priest shall offer one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.
He shall make atonement for him,
because he became guilty through the dead body.
And the priest shall make his head holy again on that same day.
[12] He shall set himself apart again for the LORD for the days of his vow.
And he shall bring a male lamb one year old as a guilt offering.
But the earlier days no longer count,
because his time of being set apart was made unclean.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sometimes something happens that is nobody’s fault: Someone suddenly dies beside you. Then the Nazirite has to start over. The time before no longer counts.
> This shows how serious the vow is. But it also shows: You can start again.

---

## The end of the time of being set apart (Verses 13–21)

[13] “‘This is the law for the Nazirite:
When the days of his being set apart are over,
he shall be brought to the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[14] And he shall bring his offering to the LORD:
a male lamb one year old without any defect as a burnt offering,
a female lamb one year old without any defect as a sin offering,
a ram without any defect as a peace offering,
[15] a basket of bread made without yeast, cakes of fine flour mixed with oil,
and thin wafers made without yeast and spread with oil,
together with their grain offering and their drink offerings.

[16] “‘The priest shall bring them before the LORD.
And he shall offer the man’s sin offering and his burnt offering.
[17] He shall offer the ram to the LORD as a peace offering,
together with the basket of bread made without yeast.
The priest shall also offer its grain offering and its drink offering.

[18] “‘The Nazirite shall shave his head that was set apart at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
He shall take the hair of his head that was set apart
and put it into the fire that burns under the peace offering.
[19] The priest shall take the boiled shoulder of the ram,
one cake without yeast from the basket and one wafer without yeast.
He shall put them into the hands of the Nazirite after he has shaved his head that was set apart.
[20] And the priest shall wave them before the LORD as a wave offering.
They are holy and belong to the priest,
together with the breast that is waved and the thigh that is offered.
After that, the Nazirite may drink wine again.

[21] “‘This is the law for the Nazirite who makes a vow,
and for his offering to the LORD for his being set apart,
in addition to whatever else he can afford.
He must do just as he has promised in his vow, by the law for his being set apart.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of the time of being set apart, the Nazirite brings many offerings: burnt offerings, sin offerings, peace offerings. It is a big celebration.
> His long hair is cut off and burned. It was the sign that he was set apart. Now it belongs completely to God.
> After that he may drink wine again and live a normal life.
> Whoever wants to give more may give more. But what you have promised, you must also keep.

## The blessing of Aaron (Verses 22–27)

[22] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said:
[23] “Speak to Aaron and to his sons and tell them:
‘This is how you shall bless the children of Israel.’
Tell them:
[24] ‘The LORD bless you and keep you.
[25] The LORD make his face shine on you and be gracious to you.
[26] The LORD lift up his face toward you and give you peace.’
[27] So they shall put my name on the children of Israel.
And I will bless them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the best-known and most beautiful texts in the whole Bible. It is called “the Aaronic blessing” or “the priestly blessing.”
> It is still spoken in synagogues today. And in very many churches, every worship service ends with exactly these words.
> The name of the LORD comes three times. Each line gets a little longer, like a blessing that keeps on growing.
> “Bless” means: God gives good things, life and success. “Keep” means: God protects.
> “Make his face shine on you” is a picture: God looks at you kindly, the way a father or a mother smiles lovingly at their child.
> “Peace” in Hebrew is “shalom.” That is more than “no war.” It means: Everything is whole and good, with God, with other people and with yourself.
> Verse 27 is important: The priests speak the words. But God himself gives the blessing: “And I will bless them.”
> Archaeologists found two small silver scrolls in Jerusalem with this blessing written on them. They are about 2,600 years old. They are among the oldest known Bible texts in the world.
