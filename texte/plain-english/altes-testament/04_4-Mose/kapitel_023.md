# Numbers – Chapter 23
### Balaam is supposed to curse – and he blesses

---

## Seven altars (Verses 1–6)

[1] Balaam said to Balak,
“Build seven altars for me here.
And get seven bulls and seven rams ready for me here.”
[2] Balak did what Balaam had said.
Balak and Balaam offered a bull and a ram on every altar.
[3] Balaam said to Balak,
“Stay here by your burnt offering, and I will go.
Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me.
I will tell you whatever he shows me.”
Then he went to a bare hilltop.
[4] God met Balaam.
Balaam said to him,
“I have prepared the seven altars.
And I have offered a bull and a ram on every altar.”
[5] The LORD put a word in Balaam’s mouth and said,
“Go back to Balak, and speak like this.”
[6] Balaam went back to him.
Balak was standing by his burnt offering, he and all the leaders of Moab.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balaam tries to win God’s favor with many sacrifices. That is what fortune-tellers did back then. But God cannot be bought, and he does not change his mind that way.

---

## The first saying: a people that lives alone (Verses 7–12)

[7] Then Balaam began his saying.
He said,
“Balak has brought me from Aram,
the king of Moab from the mountains of the East.
He said, ‘Come, curse Jacob for me.
Come, speak against Israel.’
[8] How can I curse those whom God has not cursed?
How can I speak against those whom the LORD has not spoken against?
[9] From the top of the rocks I see them.
From the hills I look at them.
Look, it is a people that lives alone.
It will not be counted among the nations.
[10] Who can count the dust of Jacob?
Who can count even a fourth of Israel?
Let me die the death of the righteous!
Let my end be like theirs!”

[11] Balak said to Balaam,
“What have you done to me?
I brought you here to curse my enemies.
But look, you have done nothing but bless them!”
[12] Balaam answered,
“Must I not be careful to say what the LORD puts in my mouth?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Instead of cursing, Balaam has to bless. No person can curse what God has blessed.
> “It is a people that lives alone”: Israel is different from the other nations, because it belongs to God.
> “The dust of Jacob” is a reminder of God’s promise to Abraham: Your descendants will be as many as the dust of the earth (Genesis 13:16).
> Balaam even wishes to die like an Israelite, as a righteous person. But strangely, he later dies in a battle against Israel (Numbers 31:8).

---

## The second try (Verses 13–17)

[13] Balak said to him,
“Please come with me to another place, where you can see them.
You will see only part of them. You will not see all of them.
Curse them for me from there.”
[14] He took him to the field of Zophim, to the top of Pisgah.
Zophim means: watchmen.
There Balak built seven altars, and he offered a bull and a ram on every altar.
[15] Balaam said to Balak,
“Stay here by your burnt offering, while I meet God over there.”
[16] The LORD met Balaam and put a word in his mouth.
He said, “Go back to Balak, and say this.”
[17] Balaam came to him.
Balak was standing by his burnt offering, and the leaders of Moab were with him.
Balak asked him, “What has the LORD said?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Balak thinks the curse might work better if Balaam sees only part of the people. He simply tries it at another place. As if God’s will depended on where you stand.

---

## The second saying: God does not lie (Verses 18–24)

[18] Then Balaam began his saying.
He said,
“Get up, Balak, and hear!
Listen to me, you son of Zippor!
[19] God is not a man, that he should lie.
He is not a human being, that he should change his mind.
Would he say something and not do it?
Would he promise something and not keep it?
[20] Look, I have been given the command to bless.
He has blessed, and I cannot undo it.
[21] He has not seen evil in Jacob.
He has not seen wickedness in Israel.
The LORD his God is with him.
The shout of a king is among them.
[22] God brings them out of Egypt.
He has strength like a wild ox.
[23] There is no magic spell against Jacob.
There is no fortune-telling against Israel.
Now people will say about Jacob and about Israel,
‘Look what God has done!’
[24] Look, a people rises up like a lioness.
Like a lion it stands up.
It will not lie down until it has eaten its prey
and drunk the blood of those it has killed.”

[25] Balak said to Balaam,
“Then do not curse them at all, but do not bless them at all either!”
[26] But Balaam answered Balak,
“Didn’t I tell you, ‘I must do everything the LORD says’?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19 is one of the most famous verses about God: “God is not a man, that he should lie.” People change their minds, break promises and take bribes. God does not. What he promises, he keeps.
> “He has not seen evil in Jacob” – that is amazing. We have seen how often Israel complained and sinned. But God has forgiven. He sees his people with the eyes of love.
> The lion is a picture of strength. The picture is warlike. It belongs to the language of that time.
> Balak becomes desperate. Now he would rather have Balaam say nothing at all.

---

## The third try (Verses 27–30)

[27] Balak said to Balaam,
“Come now, I will take you to another place.
Perhaps God will be pleased to let you curse them for me from there.”
[28] Balak took Balaam to the top of Peor, which looks down on the desert.
[29] Balaam said to Balak,
“Build seven altars for me here.
And get seven bulls and seven rams ready for me here.”
[30] Balak did what Balaam had said.
He offered a bull and a ram on every altar.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balak does not give up. A third place, seven altars again. But God’s blessing cannot be turned around.
