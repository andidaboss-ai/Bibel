# Numbers – Chapter 19
### The red cow and the water for cleansing

---

## The ashes of the red cow (Verses 1–10)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron. He said,
[2] “This is the rule of the law that the LORD has commanded:
Tell the people of Israel to bring you a red young cow,
without spot and without defect, which has never worn a yoke.
[3] You shall give her to Eleazar the priest.
He shall lead her outside the camp.
And she shall be killed in front of him.
[4] Eleazar the priest shall take some of her blood with his finger
and sprinkle her blood seven times toward the front of the Tent of Meeting.
[5] The cow shall be burned in front of him:
her skin, her meat, and her blood, together with her dung, shall be burned.
[6] The priest shall take cedar wood, hyssop, and red yarn
and throw them into the middle of the fire where the cow is burning.

[7] “Then the priest shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water.
After that he may come into the camp.
But the priest is unclean until the evening.
[8] The one who burns the cow shall wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water.
He is unclean until the evening.

[9] “A man who is clean shall gather up the ashes of the cow
and put them outside the camp in a clean place.
They shall be kept for the community of the people of Israel,
for the water that is used to cleanse people from uncleanness.
It is a sin offering.
[10] The one who gathers the ashes of the cow shall wash his clothes and is unclean until the evening.
This shall be a rule forever for the people of Israel
and for the foreigner who lives among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: A yoke is a wooden frame that animals wear on their necks when they pull a plow.
> Verse 6: Hyssop is a small plant with many thin branches.
> Whoever touches a dead person becomes unclean. That happened all the time in the wilderness, because many people died there. That is why a way of cleansing is needed.
> A red young cow without defect is burned. Her ashes are mixed with water. With this water, people who have touched a dead person are cleansed.
> Red is the color of blood and of life. The cedar wood and the red yarn also stand for life. The remedy against death is a sign of life.
> What is strange: Whoever makes the cleansing ashes becomes unclean himself. The people who help others become clean take uncleanness on themselves. Jewish teachers have said that even wise King Solomon did not understand this riddle.
> The Letter to the Hebrews recalls the ashes of the cow. It says: If even they could cleanse, how much more does the blood of Jesus cleanse us (Hebrews 9:13–14).

## Whoever touches a dead person (Verses 11–16)

[11] “Whoever touches the dead body of any person is unclean for seven days.
[12] He shall cleanse himself with the water on the third day.
Then he will be clean on the seventh day.
But if he does not cleanse himself on the third day, he will not be clean on the seventh day.
[13] Whoever touches a dead person, the body of someone who has died,
and does not cleanse himself,
makes the LORD’s tabernacle unclean.
That person shall be cut off from Israel.
Because the water for cleansing was not sprinkled on him, he is unclean.
His uncleanness is still on him.

[14] “This is the law when a person dies in a tent:
Everyone who comes into the tent and everyone who is in the tent is unclean for seven days.
[15] Every open container that does not have a lid tied on it is unclean.
[16] “Someone out in the open field may touch a person who was killed with a sword,
or a dead body, or a human bone, or a grave.
He is unclean for seven days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Death is seen as the most unclean thing of all. It is the opposite of God, who is life.
> Whoever touches a dead person may not go into the holy tent for a week. That is not a punishment. People buried their dead with love, and that was good and right. But afterward a person needs time and a cleansing before coming before God again.
> Even an open container in a tent with a dead person becomes unclean. But one with a lid stays clean. That is also a good rule for health.

---

## How the cleansing happens (Verses 17–22)

[17] “For the unclean person, they shall take some of the ashes of the burned sin offering
and pour running water on them in a container.
[18] A clean person shall take hyssop, dip it in the water,
and sprinkle it on the tent, on all the containers, on the people who were there,
and on the one who touched the bone, the person who was killed, the dead body, or the grave.
[19] The clean person shall sprinkle the unclean person on the third day and on the seventh day.
On the seventh day he shall cleanse him.
Then that person shall wash his clothes and bathe in water.
In the evening he is clean.

[20] “But someone may be unclean and not cleanse himself.
That person shall be cut off from the assembly,
because he has made the LORD’s holy place unclean.
The water for cleansing has not been sprinkled on him. He is unclean.
[21] This shall be a rule for them forever.
The one who sprinkles the water for cleansing shall wash his clothes.
And the one who touches the water for cleansing is unclean until the evening.
[22] “Whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean.
And the person who touches it is unclean until the evening.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The cleansing takes time: on the third and on the seventh day. There is no quick way back, but there is a sure way.
> In Psalm 51:7 (in some Bibles 51:9), David prays that God would cleanse him with hyssop, so that he becomes clean. He is probably thinking of this cleansing. But he means his heart, which is to become clean from guilt.
> After the temple was destroyed, this law could no longer be carried out. That is why devout Jews today see themselves as “unclean through the dead.” They do not step onto the place of the former Most Holy Place in Jerusalem.
