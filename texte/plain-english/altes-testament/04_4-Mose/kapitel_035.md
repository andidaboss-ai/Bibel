# Numbers – Chapter 35
### Cities for the Levites – cities of refuge

---

## 48 cities for the Levites (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses in the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, at Jericho.
He said,
[2] “Command the people of Israel to give the Levites cities to live in, from their inheritance.
You must also give the Levites pasture land around the cities.
[3] They will have the cities to live in.
And their pasture land will be for their livestock, for their goods and for all their animals.
[4] “The pasture land of the cities that you give to the Levites
is to reach out from the city wall about 1,500 feet, all around.
[5] Outside the city you must measure
about 3,000 feet on the east side,
about 3,000 feet on the south side,
about 3,000 feet on the west side
and about 3,000 feet on the north side.
The city will be in the middle.
This will be the pasture land of their cities.
[6] “Among the cities that you give to the Levites will be the six cities of refuge.
You must give them so that someone who has killed a person can flee there.
Besides them, you must give 42 cities.
[7] All the cities that you give to the Levites will be 48 cities together, with their pasture land.
[8] As for the cities that you give from the property of the people of Israel:
From the large tribes you must take many, and from the small ones you must take few.
Each tribe must give some of its cities to the Levites, according to the inheritance it receives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: A city of refuge is a city where someone can flee and be safe.
> The Bible says: 1,000 cubits and 2,000 cubits. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The numbers in verses 4 and 5 are hard to understand. Bible teachers explain them in different ways. One explanation is: The 1,000 cubits go out from the wall. The 2,000 cubits are the length of each side of the whole pasture land. Another explanation is: The first 1,000 cubits are pasture land, and the next 1,000 cubits are fields and vineyards.
> The tribe of Levi does not get its own area. The Levites are there for the worship of God. That is why they live spread out in cities all over the land. That way they are close to the people everywhere and can teach them about faith.

---

## Six cities of refuge (Verses 9–15)

[9] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[10] “Speak to the people of Israel, and tell them,
‘When you cross the Jordan into the land of Canaan,
[11] you must choose cities for yourselves to be cities of refuge for you.
Someone who has killed a person without meaning to may flee there.
[12] The cities will give you refuge from the avenger,
so that the one who has killed does not die
before he has stood trial before the community.
[13] The cities that you give will be six cities of refuge for you.
[14] You must give three cities on the other side of the Jordan,
and you must give three cities in the land of Canaan.
They will be cities of refuge.
[15] These six cities will be a refuge for the people of Israel,
for the stranger and for the foreigner living among them.
Everyone who has killed a person without meaning to may flee there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then there was no police. If someone was killed, a close relative had the duty to take revenge. He was called the “avenger of blood.”
> But what if it was an accident? Then revenge would be unjust. That is why God creates the cities of refuge. Anyone who has killed without meaning to can flee there. There he is safe until a court has decided.
> This is a big step forward: Revenge does not decide, but a just court does.
> Strangers and foreigners also have this right. The law is the same for everyone.

## What is murder? (Verses 16–21)

[16] “‘But if he hit him with an object made of iron, so that he died,
he is a murderer. The murderer must surely be put to death.
[17] If he hit him with a stone in his hand that can kill a person,
and he died, he is a murderer.
The murderer must surely be put to death.
[18] Or if he hit him with a wooden weapon in his hand that can kill a person,
and he died, he is a murderer.
The murderer must surely be put to death.
[19] The avenger of blood himself must put the murderer to death.
When he meets him, he must put him to death.
[20] If he pushed him out of hate,
or threw something at him while lying in wait, so that he died,
[21] or if he hit him with his hand because he was his enemy, so that he died,
then the one who hit him must surely be put to death.
He is a murderer.
The avenger of blood must put the murderer to death when he meets him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a difference is made: When is it murder, and when is it an accident?
> It is murder when someone uses a dangerous weapon. Or when he acts out of hate. Or when he hides and lies in wait for the other person. Then he wanted to kill.
> Back then, the punishment for murder was death. Today many countries have ended the death penalty, and many Jews and Christians work for this. But the thought behind it stays important: A human life is endlessly precious. Anyone who takes it on purpose does a very serious wrong.

## What is an accident? (Verses 22–29)

[22] “‘But he may have pushed him suddenly, without being his enemy,
or thrown something at him without lying in wait,
[23] or dropped a stone on him that can kill a person,
without seeing him, so that he died.
And he was not his enemy and did not want to harm him.
[24] Then the community must judge between the one who hit him and the avenger of blood,
by these rules.
[25] The community must save the one who killed from the hand of the avenger of blood.
And the community must bring him back to his city of refuge, where he had fled.
He must stay there until the death of the high priest,
who was anointed with the holy oil.

[26] “‘But the one who killed may at some time go outside the border of his city of refuge, where he fled.
[27] And the avenger of blood may find him outside the border of his city of refuge
and kill him.
Then the avenger of blood is not guilty of blood.
[28] For the one who killed should have stayed in his city of refuge until the death of the high priest.
But after the death of the high priest, he may go back to the land that is his property.
[29] “‘These things are to be a fixed law and rule for you,
for all your generations, everywhere you live.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: Anointed means: Oil was poured on his head as a sign that God chose him.
> Someone who has killed another person by accident is not a murderer. But he is not simply free either. He must stay in the city of refuge. He is protected there, but he cannot go home. This shows: Even an accident is something serious. A life has been lost.
> He may only go home when the high priest dies. With the death of the high priest, a new time begins. Jewish teachers have said: The death of the high priest brings a kind of atonement.
> Christians see in this a picture of Jesus. In the letter to the Hebrews, Jesus is called the great high priest (Hebrews 4:14). Through his death, people become free. And the letter to the Hebrews speaks of people who flee to God for refuge, to hold on to the hope he gives (Hebrews 6:18).

## No ransom for murder (Verses 30–34)

[30] “‘Whoever kills a person: The murderer must be put to death based on what witnesses say.
But one witness alone may not speak against a person, so that he dies.
[31] “‘You must also not take a ransom for the life of a murderer who is guilty and must die.
He must surely be put to death.
[32] “‘And you must not take a ransom for someone who has fled to his city of refuge,
so that he can come back and live in the land before the death of the priest.
[33] “‘So you must not pollute the land where you live.
For blood pollutes the land.
No atonement can be made for the land for the blood that was shed in it,
except by the blood of the one who shed it.
[34] You must not make the land unclean, the land where you live and where I live.
For I, the LORD, live among the people of Israel.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: A ransom is money paid to set someone free.
> An important protection: Nobody may be condemned because of what only one witness says. There must be at least two. That is meant to prevent false accusations. This rule is still part of many legal systems today.
> No ransom: A rich person is not to be able to buy his way free. A human life cannot be paid for with money. Poor and rich are equal before the law.
> The last verse explains the deepest reason: God lives among his people. That is why the land is holy. Murder is not only a crime against people, but also against God, who lives there.
