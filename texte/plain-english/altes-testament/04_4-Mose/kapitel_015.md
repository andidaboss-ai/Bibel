# Numbers – Chapter 15
### Rules for life in the land – the fringes on the clothes

---

## Grain offerings and drink offerings with the animal offerings (Verses 1–12)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘You will come into the land where you will live, which I am giving you.
[3] There you will bring an offering by fire to the LORD:
a burnt offering or a sacrifice, to keep a promise,
or as a free-will gift, or at your feasts,
to make a pleasant smell for the LORD,
from the cattle or from the sheep and goats.
[4] Then the one who brings his offering shall bring a grain offering to the LORD:
about 2 quarts of fine flour mixed with about 1 quart of oil.
[5] And you shall prepare about 1 quart of wine as a drink offering,
with the burnt offering or with the sacrifice, for each lamb.

[6] “‘For a ram you shall prepare a grain offering:
about 4½ quarts of fine flour mixed with a little more than 1 quart of oil.
[7] And as a drink offering you shall bring a little more than 1 quart of wine,
as a pleasant smell for the LORD.
[8] When you prepare a young bull as a burnt offering or as a sacrifice,
to keep a promise, or as a peace offering for the LORD.
[9] Then with the bull a grain offering shall be brought:
about 7 quarts of fine flour mixed with about 2 quarts of oil.
[10] And as a drink offering you shall bring about 2 quarts of wine,
as an offering by fire, a pleasant smell for the LORD.
[11] This is how it shall be done for each bull, for each ram,
for each male lamb or young goat.
[12] However many animals you prepare,
you shall do this for each one of them, according to their number.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the measures are given like this: one tenth, two tenths, or three tenths of an ephah of flour, and a fourth, a third, or half a hin of oil and wine. An ephah is about 6 gallons. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> Right after the story of the spies, God says: “You will come into the land …” That is a sign of hope. The punishment is not the last word. Israel will come into the land, with fields for flour, olive trees, and vineyards.
> Flour, oil, and wine belong with every animal offering. The bigger the animal, the more there is. It is like a complete festive meal.

## The same law for foreigners (Verses 13–16)

[13] “‘Everyone born in Israel shall do these things in this way
when they bring an offering by fire, a pleasant smell for the LORD.
[14] Suppose a foreigner lives with you,
or someone else is among you in your generations.
If he wants to bring an offering by fire, a pleasant smell for the LORD,
he shall do it just as you do.
[15] For the assembly there shall be one rule,
for you and for the foreigner who lives with you.
It is a rule forever, for all your generations.
As you are, so the foreigner shall be before the LORD.
[16] One law and one rule shall apply
to you and to the foreigner who lives with you.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Four times it is stressed here: The same applies to the foreigner as to the Israelite. “As you are, so the foreigner shall be before the LORD.”
> Before God there are no first-class and second-class people. Everyone may come and bring God his gift.

## The first bread for God (Verses 17–21)

[17] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[18] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘You will come into the land where I am bringing you.
[19] When you eat from the bread of the land,
you shall give a wave offering to the LORD.
[20] From the first of your dough you shall give a flat loaf as a wave offering.
You shall lift it up just like the wave offering from the threshing floor.
A threshing floor is the place where the grain is separated from the stalks.
[21] From the first of your dough you shall give a wave offering to the LORD, for all your generations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every time people bake bread, they are to give a piece of the dough to God. That way, even with their daily bread, they think of the one who gives it.
> To this day, many Jewish women take off a small piece of dough when they bake bread and burn it. This custom is called “taking challah.” That is also where the name “challah” for the Sabbath bread comes from.
> In the Lord’s Prayer, Christians pray: “Give us today our daily bread” (Matthew 6:11).

## When the community sins without meaning to (Verses 22–26)

[22] “‘Suppose you make a mistake
and do not keep all these commandments that the LORD has spoken to Moses,
[23] everything the LORD has commanded you through Moses,
from the day the LORD gave the commandments and on through all your generations.
[24] If it happened without meaning to, without the community knowing about it,
then the whole community shall offer one young bull as a burnt offering,
as a pleasant smell for the LORD,
with its grain offering and its drink offering, as the rule says,
and one male goat as a sin offering.
[25] The priest shall make atonement for the whole community of the people of Israel,
and they will be forgiven.
For it was a mistake.
And they have brought their offering, an offering by fire to the LORD,
and their sin offering before the LORD, because of their mistake.
[26] The whole community of the people of Israel will be forgiven,
and also the foreigner who lives among them.
For the whole people did it without meaning to.

## When one person sins without meaning to (Verses 27–29)

[27] “‘If one person sins without meaning to,
he shall bring a one-year-old female goat as a sin offering.
[28] The priest shall make atonement before the LORD for the person who made the mistake,
because he sinned without meaning to.
He shall make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.
[29] You shall have one and the same law for anyone who does something without meaning to,
for the one born among the people of Israel and for the foreigner who lives among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> For mistakes made without meaning to, there is always a way to forgiveness. We already know this from Leviticus 4.

## Whoever sins with a raised hand (Verses 30–31)

[30] “‘But suppose someone does something with a raised hand,
whether he was born in Israel or is a foreigner.
He insults the LORD.
That person shall be cut off from his people.
[31] For he has treated the word of the LORD with contempt and has broken his commandment.
That person shall be completely cut off.
His guilt is on him.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> “With a raised hand” means: on purpose, boldly, in defiance. Someone knows exactly that something is wrong and does it anyway, to mock God.
> There is no offering for something like this. Offerings only help when a person is sorry. Whoever despises God on purpose and without regret shuts himself out.
> But the Bible shows in many places: When a person really turns back and is sorry, God is ready to forgive even then (for example King David in Psalm 51).

## A man gathers wood on the Sabbath (Verses 32–36)

[32] While the people of Israel were in the wilderness,
they found a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath day.
[33] Those who found him gathering sticks
brought him to Moses and Aaron and to the whole community.
[34] They kept him under guard,
because it had not yet been made clear what should be done to him.
[35] The LORD said to Moses,
“The man must be put to death.
The whole community shall stone him with stones outside the camp.”
[36] So the whole community brought him outside the camp,
and they stoned him to death with stones, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is probably here as an example of a sin “with a raised hand.” The man knew that no work was to be done on the Sabbath (Exodus 35:2–3). Still, he did it openly.
> To us today, the punishment is shockingly harsh. The Bible tells it without making it look better than it is. It shows how seriously the Sabbath was taken back then. It was the sign of the covenant between God and Israel (Exodus 31:13–17).
> Later, Jesus stressed the meaning of the Sabbath in a new way: “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath” (Mark 2:27). And he healed people on the Sabbath.
> Here, too: The large Jewish and Christian communities today reject such punishments.

## The fringes on the clothes (Verses 37–41)

[37] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[38] “Speak to the people of Israel and tell them
that they shall make fringes on the corners of their clothes, for all their generations.
And on the fringe of each corner they shall put a blue cord.
[39] It shall be a fringe for you.
When you look at it, you shall remember all the LORD’s commandments and do them.
And you shall not follow your own heart and your own eyes,
which you used to chase after like a prostitute.
[40] That way you shall remember all my commandments and do them,
and be holy to your God.
[41] I am the LORD your God,
who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God.
I am the LORD your God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The fringes are called “tzitzit” in Hebrew. They are like a string tied around your finger: When you see them, you remember God’s commandments.
> The blue color was very expensive. It is a reminder of the sky and of the clothes of the priests. Every Israelite, not only the priest, wears something blue as a sign: I belong to God.
> “Not following your own heart and your own eyes” – people are easily led by what they see and want to have. The fringes are meant to help them look to God instead.
> To this day, devout Jews wear these fringes on a prayer shawl (tallit) or on an undergarment. Jesus also wore fringes like this. The sick woman touched “the fringe of his garment” (Matthew 9:20).
> Verses 37–41 are part of the “Shema Israel,” the most important Jewish prayer, which is said in the morning and in the evening.
