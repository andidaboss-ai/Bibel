# Numbers – Chapter 31
### The war against Midian

---

## 12,000 men go to war (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[2] “Take revenge for the people of Israel on the Midianites.
After that you will be gathered to your people.”
Being gathered to your people means: to die.
[3] Moses spoke to the people. He said,
“Arm men from among you for war.
They are to go against Midian, to carry out the LORD’s revenge on Midian.
[4] From every tribe, from all the tribes of Israel, you must send 1,000 men to the war.”
[5] So from the thousands of Israel, 1,000 men were chosen from every tribe,
12,000 men armed for war.
[6] Moses sent them to the war, 1,000 from every tribe,
together with Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest.
Phinehas had the holy objects and the trumpets for the alarm signal in his hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is one of the hardest in the whole Bible. It tells about a war with great cruelty.
> The reason for the war is told in Chapter 25: The Midianites had led Israel into worshiping idols on purpose. Because of this, 24,000 Israelites died in a plague.
> It is Moses’ last task before his death.

---

## The victory (Verses 7–12)

[7] They fought against Midian, as the LORD had commanded Moses.
They killed every male.
[8] Together with the others they killed, they also killed the kings of Midian:
Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur and Reba, the five kings of Midian.
They also killed Balaam, the son of Beor, with the sword.
[9] The people of Israel took the women of Midian captive, together with their little children.
They took all their livestock, all their flocks and all their goods as plunder.
[10] They burned all their cities where they lived and all their camps with fire.
[11] They took all the captives and all the plunder, people and animals.
[12] They brought the captives, the plunder and the goods they had taken to Moses,
to Eleazar the priest and to the community of the people of Israel.
They brought them to the camp in the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, at Jericho.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balaam is also among the dead. He had blessed Israel. But later he had advised the Midianites to lead Israel astray (verse 16). So his life ends in the fight against Israel.
> Zur is the father of Cozbi, the woman from Chapter 25.
> It is not certain whether all of Midian was really wiped out. Later in the Bible there are many Midianites again (Judges 6). It was probably about one particular group of Midianites in this area.

---

## Moses’ terrible order (Verses 13–18)

[13] Moses, Eleazar the priest and all the leaders of the community went out to meet them outside the camp.
[14] Moses was angry with the officers of the army,
the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds, who came back from the war.
[15] Moses said to them,
“Have you let all the women live?
[16] Look, it was these women who, on Balaam’s advice, caused the people of Israel
to be unfaithful to the LORD in the matter of Peor.
That is why the plague came on the community of the LORD.
[17] So now kill every boy among the children.
And kill every woman who has slept with a man.
[18] But keep alive for yourselves all the girls who have not slept with a man.

> **What does this mean?**
> Something horrible is ordered here: Boys and women are to be killed. Only the young girls stay alive, as captives of the Israelites.
> We must say it honestly: This is almost unbearable for us. Children and women are killed. Girls are made captives. By our understanding today, these are serious war crimes.
> How can something like this be in the Bible? Jews and Christians have given different answers:
> Some say: The Bible honestly tells how people fought wars back then. All the nations of that time did such things. The Bible does not hide this, but it does not make it an example for all times.
> Others say: These stories show how seriously turning away from God was taken back then. The people were to be protected from foreign gods.
> Many point out: You have to read the whole Bible. Later God forbids that children be punished for the guilt of their parents (Deuteronomy 24:16). The prophets dream of a peace in which swords will be made into plows (Isaiah 2:4). Jesus says: “Love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44).
> One thing is completely certain: Nobody may use this passage to justify violence against people. That would be a serious misuse of the Bible.

## Cleansing after the war (Verses 19–24)

[19] “Camp outside the camp for seven days.
Everyone who has killed a person, and everyone who has touched someone who was killed,
must purify himself on the third day and on the seventh day, you and your captives.
[20] You must purify every piece of clothing,
everything made of leather, everything made of goats’ hair,
and all things made of wood.”

[21] Eleazar the priest said to the soldiers who had gone into the battle,
“This is the rule of the law that the LORD has commanded Moses:
[22] The gold, the silver, the bronze, the iron, the tin and the lead,
[23] everything that can stand the fire, you must pass through the fire. Then it will be clean.
But it must also be purified with the water for cleansing.
And everything that cannot stand the fire, you must pass through the water.
[24] On the seventh day you must wash your clothes, and then you will be clean.
After that you may come into the camp.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: To purify means: to make clean before God.
> Even someone who has killed in war becomes unclean. He may not simply go back into the camp and before God. He needs seven days and a cleansing.
> This shows: Even a war that was thought to be necessary is not something that simply passes. Killing leaves marks. Later King David was not allowed to build the temple, because he had shed so much blood (1 Chronicles 22:8).
> Metal is cleansed by fire, other things by water.

## The plunder is divided (Verses 25–47)

[25] The LORD spoke to Moses. He said,
[26] “Count the plunder that was taken, people and animals.
Do it together with Eleazar the priest and the heads of the families of the community.
[27] Divide the plunder into two halves:
one for the soldiers who went into battle,
and one for the whole community.
[28] From the soldiers who went into battle, collect a tax for the LORD:
1 out of every 500, from the people, from the cattle, from the donkeys and from the flocks.
[29] Take it from their half and give it to Eleazar the priest, as a wave offering for the LORD.
[30] From the half of the people of Israel, you must take 1 out of every 50,
from the people, from the cattle, from the donkeys, from the flocks, from all the livestock.
Give them to the Levites, who do the work at the LORD’s tabernacle.”
The tabernacle is the holy tent where God lives among his people.
[31] Moses and Eleazar the priest did as the LORD had commanded Moses.

[32] The plunder, not counting what the soldiers had taken for themselves,
was 675,000 sheep,
[33] 72,000 cattle,
[34] 61,000 donkeys,
[35] and 32,000 people in all, girls who had not slept with a man.

[36] The half that was the share of the soldiers was 337,500 sheep.
[37] The LORD’s tax from the sheep was 675.
[38] The cattle were 36,000. The LORD’s tax from them was 72.
[39] The donkeys were 30,500. The LORD’s tax from them was 61.
[40] The people were 16,000. The LORD’s tax from them was 32 people.
[41] Moses gave the tax, the LORD’s wave offering, to Eleazar the priest,
as the LORD had commanded Moses.

[42] From the half of the people of Israel, which Moses had separated from the half of the soldiers –
[43] the half of the community was 337,500 sheep,
[44] 36,000 cattle,
[45] 30,500 donkeys
[46] and 16,000 people –
[47] from the half of the people of Israel, Moses took 1 out of every 50, people and animals.
He gave them to the Levites, who did the work at the LORD’s tabernacle,
as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29: A wave offering is a gift that is lifted up before God.
> The plunder is divided fairly: The 12,000 soldiers get half, and the whole people get the other half. That way, those who stayed at home also get something.
> From both halves, a part goes to God, that is, to the priests and Levites.
> The numbers are very exact. You can check them: 337,500 divided by 500 is exactly 675, and so on. The math works out.
> It is especially hard that people are also counted as “plunder,” the girls who were taken captive. 32 of them went to the priests, probably as servants at the holy tent. This shows a world in which people were seen as property. Today we know: No person may be the property of another.
> Later there were rules to protect the girls. An Israelite could not simply use or sell a woman taken captive. He had to give her time to mourn and treat her like a wife (Deuteronomy 21:10–14). By today’s standards, this is still not a just situation.

## The officers’ thank offering (Verses 48–54)

[48] The officers over the thousands of the army,
the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds, came to Moses.
[49] They said to Moses,
“Your servants have counted the soldiers who are under our command.
Not a single man of us is missing.
[50] So we have brought an offering for the LORD, what each man found:
gold jewelry, armbands, bracelets, signet rings, earrings and necklaces.
We bring it to make atonement for ourselves before the LORD.”
Atonement means: putting things right again between God and people.
[51] Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold from them, all of it finely made jewelry.
[52] All the gold of the wave offering that they gave to the LORD,
from the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds,
was 16,750 shekels (about 420 pounds).
[53] The soldiers had taken plunder, each man for himself.
[54] Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of the commanders of thousands and of hundreds.
They brought it into the Tent of Meeting,
as a reminder for the people of Israel before the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 50: A signet ring is a ring with a seal that is used like a signature.
> A shekel is about 0.4 ounces. So 16,750 shekels of gold weighed about 420 pounds.
> Not a single soldier died in the war. The officers see this as a miracle and thank God with a large gift.
> “To make atonement for ourselves”: Maybe they feel that they have taken guilt on themselves by killing. They want to be made clean again before God.
