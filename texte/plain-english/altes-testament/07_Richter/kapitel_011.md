# Judges – Chapter 11
### Jephthah and his vow

---

## The son who was driven out (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty warrior.
He was the son of a prostitute.
Gilead was Jephthah’s father.
[2] Gilead’s wife also had sons by him.
When his wife’s sons grew up, they drove Jephthah out and said to him,
“You will not inherit anything in our father’s house,
because you are the son of another woman.”
[3] So Jephthah fled from his brothers and lived in the land of Tob.
Outlaws gathered around Jephthah, and they went out with him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jephthah has a hard start in life: He is the child of a prostitute. His half-brothers drive him away so that he does not inherit anything. He becomes an outsider and lives together with other outcasts.
> God often uses exactly the people whom others look down on.

---

## The elders bring Jephthah back (Verses 4–11)

[4] After a while, the children of Ammon made war against Israel.
[5] When the children of Ammon made war against Israel,
the elders of Gilead went to bring Jephthah back from the land of Tob.
[6] They said to Jephthah,
“Come and be our commander, so that we can fight against the children of Ammon.”
[7] Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead,
“Didn’t you hate me and drive me out of my father’s house?
Why have you come to me now, when you are in trouble?”
[8] The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah,
“That is why we have come back to you now.
Go with us and fight against the children of Ammon.
Then you will be our head over all the people of Gilead.”
[9] Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead,
“If you bring me back home to fight against the children of Ammon,
and the LORD gives them to me, will I be your head?”
[10] The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah,
“The LORD will be witness between us.
We will surely do what you say.”
[11] So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead.
And the people made him head and commander over them.
And Jephthah spoke all his words in front of the LORD in Mizpah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now, in their need, they need the outcast. Jephthah says honestly: You drove me away, and now you come crawling back?
> He bargains wisely and has the promise confirmed in front of God. He knows how quickly people change their minds.

---

## Jephthah negotiates with the king of Ammon (Verses 12–28)

[12] Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the children of Ammon, saying,
“What do you have against me, that you have come to me to fight against my land?”
[13] The king of the children of Ammon answered Jephthah’s messengers,
“Because Israel took away my land when he came up out of Egypt,
from the Arnon to the Jabbok and to the Jordan.
So now give it back peacefully.”
[14] Jephthah sent messengers again to the king of the children of Ammon
[15] and said to him,
“This is what Jephthah says:
Israel did not take away the land of Moab or the land of the children of Ammon.
[16] But when they came up from Egypt,
Israel went through the wilderness to the Red Sea and came to Kadesh.
[17] Then Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying,
‘Please let me pass through your land.’
But the king of Edom did not listen.
In the same way, Israel sent to the king of Moab, but he refused.
So Israel stayed in Kadesh.
[18] Then they went through the wilderness and went around the land of Edom and the land of Moab.
They came to the east side of the land of Moab and camped on the other side of the Arnon.
But they did not go into the territory of Moab, for the Arnon was the border of Moab.
[19] Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, the king of Heshbon.
And Israel said to him,
‘Please let us pass through your land to our place.’
[20] But Sihon did not trust Israel to only pass through his territory.
Instead, Sihon gathered all his people, camped in Jahaz, and fought against Israel.
[21] And the LORD, the God of Israel, gave Sihon and all his people into the hand of Israel,
and they struck them.
So Israel took all the land of the Amorites who lived in that country.
[22] They took the whole territory of the Amorites,
from the Arnon to the Jabbok, and from the wilderness to the Jordan.
[23] So now the LORD, the God of Israel, has driven out the Amorites in front of his people Israel.
And now you want to take it?
[24] Don’t you take what Chemosh your god gives you to own?
So we also will take everything that the LORD our God has driven out in front of us.
[25] Now are you any better than Balak the son of Zippor, the king of Moab?
Did he ever quarrel with Israel, or did he ever fight against them?
[26] Israel has lived in Heshbon and its towns,
in Aroer and its towns,
and in all the cities along the Arnon for three hundred years!
Why didn’t you take them back during that time?
[27] I have not sinned against you.
But you are doing me wrong by making war against me.
May the LORD, the Judge, judge today between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon.”
[28] But the king of the children of Ammon did not listen to the words that Jephthah sent him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before Jephthah fights, he tries with words. He sends messengers twice and explains the history in detail, just as it is told in Numbers 20–21. This is one of the first diplomatic negotiations recorded in the Bible.
> His arguments: Israel did not take the land from the children of Ammon, but from King Sihon, who had attacked Israel. And Israel has lived there for 300 years. Why do you come only now?
> “Chemosh” was actually the god of the Moabites. Maybe Jephthah mixes up the peoples here, or Ammon and Moab were closely connected at that time. Jephthah speaks in the language of his opponent here, without saying that Chemosh is a real god.
> “The LORD, the Judge”: In the end, Jephthah leaves the decision to God. He is the real judge.
> “Red Sea”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew says “Sea of Reeds.”

## The terrible vow (Verses 29–33)

[29] Then the Spirit of the LORD came on Jephthah.
He passed through Gilead and Manasseh, he passed through Mizpah of Gilead,
and from Mizpah of Gilead he went on to the children of Ammon.
[30] And Jephthah made a vow to the LORD and said,
“If you will really give the children of Ammon into my hand,
[31] then whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me
when I come back in peace from the children of Ammon
shall belong to the LORD,
and I will offer it up as a burnt offering.”
[32] So Jephthah went over to the children of Ammon to fight against them.
And the LORD gave them into his hand.
[33] He struck them from Aroer to Minnith, twenty cities, and as far as Abelcheramim,
with a very great slaughter.
So the children of Ammon were brought down in front of the children of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> A vow is a solemn promise made to God.
> Even though the Spirit of the LORD has already come on Jephthah, he makes a vow. He wants to promise God something so that he will win. This was not necessary. God had already equipped him.
> The vow is careless and terrible: “whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me.” At that time, animals often lived on the ground floor of houses. Maybe he was thinking of an animal. But he also had to expect that a person might come out.
> God had clearly forbidden human sacrifice (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 12:31). Jephthah obviously did not know God’s commandments well. He thought like the peoples around him, who sacrificed people to their gods.

## Jephthah’s daughter (Verses 34–40)

[34] When Jephthah came to his house in Mizpah,
his daughter came out to meet him, with tambourines and dancing.
She was his only child.
Besides her, he had no son or daughter.
[35] When he saw her, he tore his clothes and said,
“Oh, my daughter! You have brought me very low,
and you are one of those who trouble me.
For I have opened my mouth to the LORD, and I cannot take it back.”
[36] She said to him,
“My father, you have opened your mouth to the LORD.
Do to me what has come out of your mouth,
since the LORD has taken revenge for you on your enemies, the children of Ammon.”
[37] And she said to her father,
“Let just this one thing be done for me:
Leave me alone for two months,
so that I may go away and go down to the mountains
and weep over my virginity, I and my friends.”
[38] He said, “Go.”
And he sent her away for two months.
She went with her friends and mourned her virginity on the mountains.
[39] At the end of the two months, she came back to her father.
And he did with her according to his vow that he had made.
She had never been with a man.
And it became a custom in Israel
[40] that the daughters of Israel go every year to celebrate the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite, four days a year.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the saddest and most terrible stories in the Bible.
> His daughter, his only child, comes out happily to meet him, with music and dancing, to celebrate the victory. And Jephthah says: “You are one of those who trouble me.” He thinks of himself first, not of her.
> The daughter is braver and has more dignity than her father. She does not even have a name in the story. She only asks for two months to mourn with her friends that she will never marry and never have children.
> What exactly happened then? The Bible only says: “He did with her according to his vow.” There are two ways to understand it:
> Many, including most of the old Jewish and Christian Bible teachers, understand that Jephthah really sacrificed his daughter. That was a horrible crime against God’s commandment.
> Others think he dedicated her to God, so that she was never allowed to marry and lived her whole life in God’s service. One hint for this could be that she weeps over her “virginity,” not over her death.
> Jewish scholars have said: Jephthah should not have kept his vow. A vow that goes against God’s commandment does not count. He could have gone to the priest and had it released (Leviticus 27).
> The Bible does not praise Jephthah for this vow. God is silent in this story. It is a warning: against careless promises and against treating God like a business partner. And it shows how far Israel was from God at this time.
> The women of Israel did not forget Jephthah’s daughter. Every year they remembered her. In this way, the victim without a name at least gets a memorial.
