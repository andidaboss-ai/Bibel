# Judges – Chapter 13
### An angel announces Samson’s birth

---

## The Philistines rule over Israel (Verse 1)

[1] The children of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
And the LORD gave them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the story begins as it so often does in the book of Judges: Israel turns away from God, and a foreign people rules over the land.
> The Philistines lived on the coast in the southwest, by the Mediterranean Sea. They were a strong people with weapons made of iron. Later the land got the name “Palestine” from them.
> This time nothing is said about the people crying out to God. Still, God sends a rescuer. God acts before the people even ask him.

---

## The angel appears to the woman (Verses 2–7)

[2] There was a man from Zorah, from the family of the Danites.
His name was Manoah.
His wife could not have children and was childless.
[3] The angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and said to her,
“You cannot have children and are childless.
But you will become pregnant and have a son.
[4] So now please be careful:
Do not drink wine or strong drink,
and do not eat anything unclean.
[5] For you will become pregnant and have a son.
No razor shall come on his head.
For the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb.
He will begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines.”

[6] Then the woman went to her husband and told him,
“A man of God came to me.
His face was like the face of the angel of God, very awesome.
I did not ask him where he came from,
and he did not tell me his name.
[7] But he said to me,
‘You will become pregnant and have a son.
And now do not drink wine or strong drink.
Do not eat anything unclean.
For the child shall be a Nazirite to God
from the womb to the day of his death.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Zorah was in the hill country west of Jerusalem, close to the land of the Philistines.
> A woman who cannot have children does have a son because God steps in. This happens more often in the Bible: with Sarah, the mother of Isaac, with Rebekah, with Rachel, later with Hannah, the mother of Samuel, and in the New Testament with Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist. It shows: This child is a gift from God and has a special task.
> The woman has no name in the story. But she is the one to whom the angel appears first.
> A “Nazirite” is a person who is set apart completely for God. He drinks no wine, does not cut his hair, and does not touch dead bodies (Numbers 6). Normally people made such a vow for a certain time. But Samson is to be a Nazirite his whole life. Even his mother is not to drink wine while she is pregnant.
> “He will begin to save Israel”: Samson will not defeat the Philistines completely. He only makes a beginning. Only much later, under King David, will Israel be completely free from the Philistines.

---

## Manoah asks for a second visit (Verses 8–14)

[8] Then Manoah prayed to the LORD and said,
“Oh, Lord, please let the man of God whom you sent come to us again.
Let him teach us what we should do with the child who will be born.”
[9] God listened to Manoah’s voice.
The angel of God came again to the woman while she was sitting in the field.
But Manoah, her husband, was not with her.
[10] The woman quickly ran and told her husband.
She said to him,
“The man who came to me the other day has appeared to me.”
[11] Manoah got up and followed his wife.
He came to the man and said to him,
“Are you the man who spoke to my wife?”
He said, “I am.”
[12] Manoah said,
“Now let what you have said happen.
How shall the child live, and what shall his task be?”
[13] The angel of the LORD said to Manoah,
“The woman must be careful about everything I told her.
[14] She must not eat anything that comes from the vine.
She must not drink wine or strong drink,
and she must not eat anything unclean.
She must keep everything I commanded her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Manoah wants to do everything right. He prays, and God hears him. But again the angel comes first to the woman, not to him.
> Manoah asks for rules for the child. The angel does not answer with new rules. He only says: What I told the woman, that counts. The woman had already understood everything correctly.
> “Strong drink” means other drinks that make you drunk, for example beer.

---

## The offering and the angel’s name (Verses 15–23)

[15] Manoah said to the angel of the LORD,
“Please stay with us.
Then we will prepare a young goat for you.”
[16] The angel of the LORD said to Manoah,
“Even if you keep me here, I will not eat your bread.
If you want to prepare a burnt offering, you must offer it to the LORD.”
For Manoah did not know that he was the angel of the LORD.
[17] Manoah said to the angel of the LORD,
“What is your name?
When your words come true, we want to honor you.”
[18] The angel of the LORD said to him,
“Why do you ask about my name? It is incomprehensible.”

[19] So Manoah took the young goat with the grain offering
and offered it on the rock to the LORD.
Then the angel did an amazing thing while Manoah and his wife watched.
[20] For when the flame went up from the altar toward the sky,
the angel of the LORD went up in the flame of the altar.
Manoah and his wife saw it,
and they fell on their faces to the ground.
[21] But the angel of the LORD did not appear to Manoah and his wife again.
Then Manoah knew that he was the angel of the LORD.
[22] Manoah said to his wife,
“We will surely die, because we have seen God.”
[23] But his wife said to him,
“If the LORD had wanted to kill us,
he would not have accepted a burnt offering and a grain offering from our hands.
He would not have shown us all these things.
And he would not have told us such things now.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Manoah wants to serve the guest a meal, as hospitality requires. He thinks he is a human being, a prophet. The angel turns him away from himself: If you want to give something, give it to God.
> “Incomprehensible” means: too great to understand. This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew word means “wonderful.” The angel’s name is too great for human beings. Later the prophet Isaiah uses the same word about the promised child: He will be called “Wonderful” (Isaiah 9:6). In verse 19 there is a related word: The angel does “an amazing thing.”
> Manoah becomes afraid. At that time, people believed: Whoever sees God must die. But his wife thinks clearly and calmly: God has accepted our offering. He has promised us a child. He does not want to kill us, he wants to bless us. In this story, the woman is the wiser one.

---

## Samson is born (Verses 24–25)

[24] The woman had a son and named him Samson.
The child grew up, and the LORD blessed him.
[25] The Spirit of the LORD began to stir him in Mahaneh Dan,
between Zorah and Eshtaol.

> **What does this mean?**
> The name Samson (in Hebrew “Shimshon”) is probably connected with the Hebrew word for “sun.”
> “Mahaneh Dan” means “camp of Dan.” At that time, the tribe of Dan still lived in this area, before part of it moved north (Chapter 18).
> The Spirit of God comes on Samson. That is the source of his strength, not his muscles. The next chapters tell about this.
