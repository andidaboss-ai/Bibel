# Judges – Chapter 2
### A new generation that does not know God

---

## The angel of the LORD at Bochim (Verses 1–5)

[1] The angel of the LORD came up from Gilgal to Bochim and said,
“I brought you up out of Egypt
and brought you into the land that I swore to give to your ancestors.
I said, ‘I will never break my covenant with you.
[2] But you must not make a covenant with the people who live in this land.
You must tear down their altars.’
But you have not listened to my voice.
Why have you done this?
[3] So I have also said,
‘I will not drive them out in front of you.
They will be like a pain in your sides,
and their gods will be a trap for you.’”
[4] When the angel of the LORD said these words to all the people of Israel,
the people cried out loud and wept.
[5] They named that place Bochim.
And they offered sacrifices there to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Bochim” means “those who weep.”
> The angel of the LORD speaks as if he were God himself: “I brought you up out of Egypt.” In the Bible, the angel of the LORD is often a messenger through whom God himself speaks.
> God stays faithful: “I will never break my covenant with you.” But Israel has not listened. That is why the nations will stay in the land, with all their temptations.
> The people weep. But the question is: Are they weeping because they are truly sorry, or only because they are sad about what is coming now?

---

## Looking back at Joshua’s death (Verses 6–10)

[6] When Joshua had sent the people away,
the people of Israel each went to his own inheritance to take the land.
[7] The people served the LORD as long as Joshua lived,
and as long as the elders lived who outlived Joshua
and who had seen all the great work that the LORD had done for Israel.
[8] Joshua, the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of 110.
[9] They buried him in the area of his inheritance, in Timnath Heres in the hill country of Ephraim, north of the mountain of Gaash.
[10] That whole generation also died and was gathered to their ancestors.
And after them another generation grew up
that did not know the LORD
or the work he had done for Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses repeat almost word for word the end of the book of Joshua (Joshua 24:28–31). They remind us how the new situation came about.
> “Timnath Heres”: In Joshua 24:30 the place was called “Timnathserah.” The letters are switched around. “Heres” means “sun.”
> Verse 10 is one of the saddest sentences in the Bible: A new generation grows up “that did not know the LORD.” The parents saw God’s deeds, but they did not pass it on to their children. And yet Moses had said so often: Tell your children! (Deuteronomy 6:7).
> Faith is not inherited. Every generation must get to know God for itself.

## The cycle of the time of the judges (Verses 11–19)

[11] The people of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD,
and they served the Baals.
[12] They left the LORD, the God of their ancestors,
who had brought them out of the land of Egypt,
and they followed other gods, the gods of the peoples who lived around them.
They bowed down to them and made the LORD angry.
[13] They left the LORD and served Baal and the Ashtaroth.
[14] So the anger of the LORD burned against Israel.
He gave them into the hands of raiders, who robbed them.
He sold them into the hands of their enemies all around,
so that they could no longer stand against their enemies.
[15] Wherever they went out, the hand of the LORD was against them, to bring them harm,
as the LORD had said and as the LORD had sworn to them.
And they were in great distress.
[16] Then the LORD raised up judges,
who saved them from the hand of those who robbed them.
[17] But they did not listen to their judges either.
For they were unfaithful like prostitutes and went after other gods and bowed down to them.
They quickly turned away from the way their ancestors had walked,
who had obeyed the commandments of the LORD.
They did not do so.
[18] When the LORD raised up judges for them,
the LORD was with the judge
and saved them from the hand of their enemies as long as the judge lived.
For the LORD felt sorry for them when they groaned
because of those who oppressed them and treated them badly.
[19] But when the judge died,
they turned back and acted even worse than their ancestors.
They followed other gods, to serve them and bow down to them.
They did not stop what they were doing or give up their stubborn ways.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the pattern of the whole book is explained. It is like a circle that repeats again and again:
> 1. Israel leaves God and serves foreign gods.
> 2. God lets enemies oppress Israel.
> 3. Israel groans and cries out in its need.
> 4. God has pity and sends a judge who rescues the people.
> 5. As long as the judge lives, there is peace.
> 6. After his death, Israel falls away again, even worse than before.
> “Baal” was a god of the Canaanites. People believed he brought rain and fertility. “Ashtaroth” is the plural of Ashtoreth, a goddess of love and fertility. For farmers, the temptation was great: They wanted to be sure that the harvest would be good.
> Verse 18 shows God’s heart: “The LORD felt sorry for them when they groaned.” God suffers with his people when they suffer, even when it is their own fault.

---

## God tests Israel (Verses 20–23)

[20] So the anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he said,
“This nation has broken my covenant, which I commanded their ancestors,
and has not listened to my voice.
[21] So I too will no longer drive out in front of them any of the nations
that Joshua left when he died.
[22] Through them I will test Israel,
to see whether they will keep to the way of the LORD and walk in it, as their ancestors did, or not.”
[23] So the LORD let those nations stay and did not drive them out quickly.
He had not given them into Joshua’s hand either.

> **What does this mean?**
> Why did the other nations stay in the land? The Bible gives several answers. Here it says: They are a test. God wants to see whether Israel will stay faithful to him, even when temptation lives right next door.
> This is true for us too: Faithfulness to God does not show when everything is easy, but when there are temptations.
