# Judges – Chapter 7
### Gideon’s three hundred men

---

## Too many warriors (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, and all the people who were with him got up early
and camped beside the spring of Harod.
The camp of Midian was north of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.
[2] The LORD said to Gideon,
“There are too many people with you for me to give Midian into their hand.
Otherwise Israel will brag against me and say, ‘My own hand has saved me.’
[3] So now announce so that all the people can hear:
‘Whoever is afraid and trembling, let him turn back and leave Mount Gilead.’”
So 22,000 of the people turned back, and 10,000 remained.
[4] The LORD said to Gideon,
“There are still too many people.
Bring them down to the water, and I will test them for you there.
When I tell you, ‘This one shall go with you,’ he shall go with you.
And when I tell you, ‘This one shall not go with you,’ he shall not go.”
[5] So he brought the people down to the water.
And the LORD said to Gideon,
“Everyone who laps the water with his tongue, as a dog laps, put him by himself.
Do the same with everyone who gets down on his knees to drink.”
[6] The number of those who lapped, putting their hand to their mouth, was three hundred men.
But all the rest of the people got down on their knees to drink water.
[7] The LORD said to Gideon,
“With the three hundred men who lapped I will save you
and give Midian into your hand.
Let all the other people go, each to his own place.”
[8] So the people took food in their hands, and their trumpets.
And he sent all the other men of Israel to their tents.
He kept only the three hundred men with him.
And the camp of Midian lay below him in the valley.

> **What does this mean?**
> Normally, before a war, you want as many soldiers as possible. God does the opposite: He sends almost everyone home. Of 32,000, only 300 remain. Why? So that Israel cannot say: We won by our own strength. It should be clear: God has given the victory.
> Whoever is afraid may go home. This was also written in the law for war (Deuteronomy 20:8).
> It is not completely clear how the test at the water worked. Probably the 300 scooped up water with their hand and lapped it from their hand. The others knelt down and drank straight from the water. Many Bible teachers think the choice had nothing to do with who could fight better. God simply wanted a small number.
> Later Paul writes what the Lord said to him: God’s power is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9). This story shows that very clearly.

## A dream in the enemy camp (Verses 9–15)

[9] That same night the LORD said to him,
“Get up, go down into the camp, for I have given it into your hand.
[10] But if you are afraid to go down,
go down to the camp with your servant Purah.
[11] You will hear what they are saying.
After that your hands will be strong enough to go down into the camp.”
So he went down with his servant Purah to the edge of the armed men in the camp.
[12] The Midianites, the Amalekites, and all the people of the east lay in the valley,
as many as locusts.
And their camels could not be counted. They were as many as the sand on the seashore.
[13] When Gideon got there,
a man was just telling his friend a dream.
He said, “I had a dream.
A loaf of barley bread came rolling into the camp of Midian.
It came to the tent and hit it so that it fell over.
It turned it upside down, so that the tent lay flat.”
[14] His friend answered,
“This is nothing else than the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel.
God has given Midian and the whole army into his hand.”
[15] When Gideon heard the dream and what it meant, he worshiped God.
Then he went back to the camp of Israel and said,
“Get up! For the LORD has given the army of Midian into your hand!”

> **What does this mean?**
> God knows Gideon’s fear. He does not say: Don’t be afraid! Instead he says: If you are afraid, take your servant with you. God lovingly meets Gideon in his weakness.
> Barley bread was the bread of poor people. Small, poor Israel is like a loaf of bread that knocks over the big tent of the enemies.
> The enemies themselves are already afraid of Gideon. This gives Gideon courage. Before he does anything else, he worships God.

## Jars, torches, and trumpets (Verses 16–22)

[16] He divided the three hundred men into three groups.
He put trumpets into all their hands, and empty jars with torches inside the jars.
[17] He said to them,
“Watch me and do the same.
When I come to the edge of the camp, do just as I do.
[18] When I blow the trumpet, I and all who are with me,
then you also blow the trumpets all around the camp and shout,
‘For the LORD and for Gideon!’”
[19] So Gideon and the hundred men who were with him came to the edge of the camp
at the beginning of the middle watch of the night, just after the guards had been changed.
They blew the trumpets and smashed the jars that were in their hands.
[20] The three groups blew the trumpets and broke the jars.
They held the torches in their left hands and the trumpets in their right hands to blow them.
And they shouted, “The sword of the LORD and of Gideon!”
[21] Each man stood in his place around the camp.
Then the whole army ran around in confusion.
They cried out and fled.
[22] The three hundred blew their trumpets.
And the LORD turned every man’s sword against his friend, throughout the whole army.
And the army fled as far as Beth Shittah toward Zererah, as far as the border of Abel Meholah, near Tabbath.

> **What does this mean?**
> What strange weapons: trumpets, jars, and torches. Not a single sword is mentioned!
> The plan: In the middle of the night, just as the guards had changed, the men smash the jars. Suddenly three hundred torches light up all around. Three hundred trumpets sound. Normally only one leader had a trumpet, for a hundred soldiers. So the enemies thought: There is a huge army!
> In the darkness and panic, the enemies fight against each other. God confuses them, and they flee.
> The “middle watch” was around midnight. At that time, the night was divided into three watches.
> Many Christians see a beautiful picture in this: Only when the jar breaks can the light shine (see also 2 Corinthians 4:6–7).

## The chase (Verses 23–25)

[23] The men of Israel from Naphtali, from Asher, and from all Manasseh were called together,
and they chased Midian.
[24] Gideon sent messengers throughout the whole hill country of Ephraim and said,
“Come down against Midian,
and take the waters ahead of them as far as Beth Barah, and also the Jordan!”
So all the men of Ephraim were called together,
and they took the waters as far as Beth Barah and the Jordan.
[25] They captured the two princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb.
They killed Oreb at Oreb’s rock, and they killed Zeeb at Zeeb’s winepress,
while they chased Midian.
And they brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side of the Jordan.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now many men do join in after all, to chase the fleeing enemies. But the real victory had already been given.
> “Oreb” means “raven,” and “Zeeb” means “wolf.” Later the places were named after them.
> Cutting off the heads is cruel. At that time it was proof of victory. Later the prophet Isaiah remembers this day as a great example of God’s rescue (Isaiah 9:3; 10:26).
