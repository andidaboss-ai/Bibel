# Judges – Chapter 16
### Samson and Delilah

---

## Samson in Gaza (Verses 1–3)

[1] Samson went to Gaza.
There he saw a prostitute and went in to her.
[2] The people of Gaza were told, “Samson is here!”
They surrounded him and lay in wait for him all night in the city gate.
They kept quiet all night and said,
“Wait until morning light. Then we will kill him.”
[3] Samson lay there until midnight.
At midnight he got up.
He took hold of the doors of the city gate with the two posts
and pulled them up, bar and all.
He put them on his shoulders
and carried them up to the top of the mountain that faces Hebron.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gaza was one of the five great cities of the Philistines, by the sea in the south. Samson goes right into the land of his enemies.
> The Bible tells openly that Samson goes to a prostitute. Again he follows what his eyes see. God’s law rejected this.
> A city gate was the most important part of a city wall. It was heavy and strong. Samson carries it away. For the Philistines this is a great humiliation: Their city stands open, without protection.
> Hebron is about 37 miles from Gaza, in the mountains of Judah. Maybe a mountain in the direction of Hebron is meant.

---

## The Philistines bribe Delilah (Verses 4–5)

[4] After this, he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek.
Her name was Delilah.
[5] The lords of the Philistines came up to her and said to her,
“Talk him into it and find out where his great strength comes from
and how we can overpower him.
Then we will tie him up and torment him.
And each of us will give you 1,100 pieces of silver.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The valley of Sorek is near Samson’s home town of Zorah, at the border of the land of the Philistines. The Bible does not say whether Delilah was a Philistine or an Israelite.
> Here, for the first time, it says that Samson “loves” a woman. But nowhere does it say that Delilah loves him.
> The “lords of the Philistines” were the rulers of the five Philistine cities. If each paid 1,100 pieces of silver, that came to 5,500 altogether. A piece of silver was probably a shekel, a little under half an ounce. So 1,100 pieces of silver were about 27 pounds of silver. That was a huge fortune.

## Three false answers (Verses 6–14)

[6] Delilah said to Samson,
“Please tell me where your great strength comes from
and what you could be tied up with, so that someone could torment you.”
[7] Samson said to her,
“If they tie me up with seven green cords that have never been dried,
then I will become weak and be like any other man.”
[8] So the lords of the Philistines brought her seven green cords
that had not been dried.
And she tied him up with them.
[9] She had men lying in wait in the inner room.
She said to him, “The Philistines are on you, Samson!”
Then he broke the cords, as a flax thread breaks when it comes too close to the fire.
So the secret of his strength was not found out.

[10] Delilah said to Samson,
“You have made a fool of me and told me lies.
Now please tell me how you could be tied up.”
[11] He said to her,
“If they just tie me up with new ropes that have never been used for work,
then I will become weak and be like any other man.”
[12] So Delilah took new ropes and tied him up with them.
Then she said to him, “The Philistines are on you, Samson!”
The men lying in wait were in the inner room.
He snapped the ropes off his arms like a thread.

[13] Delilah said to Samson,
“Until now you have made a fool of me and told me lies.
Tell me what you could be tied up with.”
He said to her,
“If you weave the seven braids of my head into the cloth on the loom.”
[14] She fastened it with the pin
and said to him, “The Philistines are on you, Samson!”
He woke up from his sleep
and pulled out the pin of the loom and the cloth.

> **What does this mean?**
> The WEB, our English source text, says “green cords.” This probably means fresh, damp cords, for example made from animal gut. When they dry, they become hard.
> A loom is a frame for weaving cloth.
> Delilah asks quite openly: What can you be tied up with, so that someone can torment you? Each time, Samson notices that she wants to betray him. Still, he stays with her. He is playing with danger.
> With each answer he comes closer to his real secret: The third answer is already about his hair.
> Verses 13 and 14 are short in the Hebrew text. The old Greek translation tells in a little more detail how Delilah weaves his hair while Samson sleeps. The WEB follows the Hebrew text.

## Samson gives away his secret (Verses 15–20)

[15] She said to him,
“How can you say, ‘I love you,’
when your heart is not with me?
You have made a fool of me three times now
and have not told me where your great strength comes from.”
[16] She pressed him with her words every day and kept urging him.
Then his soul was troubled to death.
[17] He opened his whole heart to her and said to her,
“No razor has ever come on my head.
For I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother’s womb.
If I am shaved, my strength will leave me.
Then I will become weak and be like any other man.”

[18] When Delilah saw that he had opened his whole heart to her,
she sent for the lords of the Philistines and said,
“Come up this one more time.
For he has opened his whole heart to me.”
Then the lords of the Philistines came up to her
and brought the money in their hands.
[19] She made him fall asleep on her knees.
She called for a man and had him shave off the seven braids of his head.
She began to torment him,
and his strength left him.
[20] She said, “The Philistines are on you, Samson!”
He woke up from his sleep and said,
“I will go out as at other times and shake myself free.”
But he did not know that the LORD had left him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Delilah uses the same trick as the woman from Timnah did then (Chapter 14): If you love me, then tell me. And again Samson gives in.
> It is important to know: The strength was not in the hair itself. The hair was the sign that Samson belonged completely to God. Samson had already broken many rules. Now he also gives up the last sign of his Nazirite vow.
> Verse 20 is one of the saddest sentences in the Bible: “He did not know that the LORD had left him.” Samson had become so used to his strength that he had forgotten where it came from.

## Samson in prison (Verses 21–22)

[21] The Philistines grabbed him and gouged out his eyes.
They brought him down to Gaza and tied him up with chains of bronze.
And he had to grind grain at the mill in the prison.
[22] But the hair on his head began to grow again
after it had been shaved.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samson had always done what pleased his eyes. Now he loses his eyes. The strong hero becomes a slave. Grinding grain at a hand mill was work for slaves and women at that time, and often a donkey was used for it. It was a great humiliation.
> He is brought to Gaza, the city whose gate he had carried away.
> Verse 22 is a small sign of hope: The hair grows back. God is not finished with Samson yet.

---

## The feast for the god Dagon (Verses 23–27)

[23] The lords of the Philistines gathered together
to offer a great sacrifice to their god Dagon and to celebrate.
For they said,
“Our god has given Samson, our enemy, into our hand.”
[24] When the people saw him, they praised their god.
For they said,
“Our god has given our enemy into our hand,
the one who destroyed our land and killed many of us.”
[25] When their hearts were happy, they said,
“Call Samson, so that he can entertain us.”
They called Samson out of the prison,
and he had to perform in front of them.
They made him stand between the pillars.
[26] Samson said to the boy who was holding him by the hand,
“Let me feel the pillars that hold up the house,
so that I can lean on them.”
[27] The house was full of men and women.
All the lords of the Philistines were there.
And on the roof there were about 3,000 men and women
who were watching Samson perform.

> **What does this mean?**
> Dagon was a god who was worshiped in that whole region, probably a god of grain. The Philistines believe: Our god is stronger than the God of Israel. So now it is no longer only about Samson. It is about the question: Who is the true God?
> The blind Samson has to entertain the crowd. He is mocked and put on show.
> Archaeologists have dug up temples of the Philistines whose roofs rested on two wooden pillars that stood close together. This fits this story.

---

## Samson’s death (Verses 28–31)

[28] Samson called to the LORD and said,
“Lord GOD, please remember me.
Please give me strength just this one more time, O God,
so that I can take revenge on the Philistines with one blow for my two eyes.”
[29] Samson took hold of the two middle pillars that held up the house
and leaned against them,
one with his right hand and the other with his left.
[30] Samson said, “Let me die with the Philistines!”
He pushed with all his strength.
Then the house fell on the lords and on all the people who were in it.
So the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those he had killed in his life.
[31] Then his brothers and his father’s whole family came down.
They took him, brought him up,
and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol, in the tomb of his father Manoah.
He had judged Israel for 20 years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samson prays for the second time in his life. Blind and imprisoned, he asks God for help, and God answers. Even after great failure, a person can come back to God.
> But his prayer is not holy and unselfish. He asks for revenge for his eyes. Right to the end, Samson remains a person with great faults.
> Samson’s death took thousands of people with him, women too. This is a terrible story from a violent time. The Bible tells it as the LORD’s victory over the god Dagon. It must never be used as an example for killing yourself and others. Whoever uses violence in God’s name today misuses the Bible.
> In the New Testament, Samson is counted among the people who had faith (Hebrews 11:32). This does not mean that everything he did was good. It means: God can do something even with weak people who make mistakes, if in the end they trust him.
> Samson is the last judge the book tells about. The remaining chapters show how bad things became in Israel without good leadership.
