# Judges – Chapter 4
### Deborah, Barak, and Jael

---

## Israel is oppressed by Jabin (Verses 1–3)

[1] After Ehud had died, the people of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
[2] So the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin, the king of Canaan, who ruled in Hazor.
The commander of his army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth of the Gentiles.
[3] Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD.
For he had nine hundred chariots of iron,
and he cruelly oppressed the people of Israel for twenty years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the cycle: Ehud dies, Israel falls away, an enemy comes.
> Nine hundred chariots of iron were a huge army at that time. Israel had no such weapons.
> In Hebrew, “Harosheth of the Gentiles” is called “Harosheth Haggoyim.” “Gentiles” means peoples who did not belong to Israel.

---

## The prophetess Deborah (Verses 4–10)

[4] At that time Deborah judged Israel. She was a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth.
[5] She sat under Deborah’s palm tree between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim.
And the people of Israel came up to her to have their cases judged.
[6] She sent for Barak the son of Abinoam, from Kedesh in Naphtali,
and said to him,
“Hasn’t the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded you:
‘Go and lead the way to Mount Tabor,
and take with you 10,000 men from the people of Naphtali and the people of Zebulun.
[7] I will draw Sisera, the commander of Jabin’s army, with his chariots and his crowd of soldiers,
to you at the river Kishon,
and I will give him into your hand’?”
[8] Barak said to her,
“If you go with me, then I will go.
But if you don’t go with me, I will not go.”
[9] She said,
“I will surely go with you.
But the honor on this journey you are taking will not be yours.
For the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman.”
Then Deborah got up and went with Barak to Kedesh.
[10] Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together to Kedesh.
And 10,000 men followed him.
And Deborah went up with him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Deborah is a very special woman. She is a prophetess, a judge, and the leader of the whole people. At a time when women usually had no public offices, God speaks through her. People come to her to get justice.
> She is the only person in the book of Judges who is also described as a judge in today’s sense: She judges cases under a palm tree.
> Barak does not dare to go alone. He says: Only if you come with me. Deborah comes along, but she says ahead of time: A woman will get the honor. The reader may think: Deborah. But it will be another woman.
> “Lappidoth” means “torches.” So some Bible teachers translate “a woman of fire” instead of “the wife of Lappidoth.”

---

## Heber the Kenite (Verse 11)

[11] Heber the Kenite had separated himself from the Kenites, the descendants of Hobab, Moses’ brother-in-law.
He had set up his tent as far away as the oak in Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> This verse seems to interrupt the story. But it is important for later: Heber is a Kenite, so he is not an Israelite. He lives near the battlefield. And he is at peace with King Jabin (Verse 17).
> “Brother-in-law”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew word usually means “father-in-law” (see Chapter 1:16).

---

## The victory at the river Kishon (Verses 12–16)

[12] Sisera was told that Barak the son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor.
[13] So Sisera gathered all his chariots, nine hundred chariots of iron,
and all the people who were with him, from Harosheth of the Gentiles to the river Kishon.
[14] Deborah said to Barak,
“Go! For this is the day on which the LORD has given Sisera into your hand.
Hasn’t the LORD gone out ahead of you?”
So Barak went down from Mount Tabor, with 10,000 men behind him.
[15] And the LORD threw Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army into confusion
in front of Barak with the edge of the sword.
Sisera left his chariot and ran away on foot.
[16] Barak chased the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth of the Gentiles.
And the whole army of Sisera fell by the edge of the sword.
Not one man was left.

> **What does this mean?**
> The chariots were very strong on the plain. But God threw them into confusion. In the song of Deborah (Chapter 5:21) we learn more: The river Kishon flooded its banks. The heavy chariots got stuck in the mud.
> Deborah gives the signal: This is the day! She tells Barak when to start.

---

## Jael and Sisera (Verses 17–22)

[17] But Sisera ran away on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite.
For there was peace between Jabin, the king of Hazor, and the house of Heber the Kenite.
[18] Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him,
“Come in, my lord, come in to me. Don’t be afraid.”
He came into the tent with her, and she covered him with a rug.
[19] He said to her,
“Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty.”
She opened a container of milk, gave him a drink, and covered him.
[20] He said to her,
“Stand in the door of the tent.
If anyone comes and asks you, ‘Is there a man here?’
you must say, ‘No.’”
[21] Then Jael, Heber’s wife, took a tent peg
and took a hammer in her hand.
She went quietly to him
and drove the peg through his temple, so that it went into the ground.
For he was in a deep sleep and worn out.
And he died.
[22] Just then, as Barak was chasing Sisera, Jael came out to meet him and said to him,
“Come, and I will show you the man you are looking for.”
He went in with her,
and there lay Sisera, dead, with the tent peg in his temple.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Deborah’s word comes true: A woman gets the honor. But it is not Deborah, it is Jael, a woman from a foreign people.
> This story is very hard to judge. Jael invites Sisera in as a guest. At that time, hospitality was holy: A guest was protected. Jael breaks this law and kills him in his sleep.
> Why does she do it? The Bible does not say. Maybe she was on Israel’s side, against her own husband. Maybe she was afraid of what Sisera would do to her. In war, women had hardly any way to protect themselves. Tent peg and hammer were her tools, because at that time women put up the tents.
> In the song of Deborah, Jael is praised (Chapter 5:24). For Israel she was a hero who defeated a cruel oppressor. But Jews and Christians have also asked again and again whether her betrayal of a guest was right. The Bible tells the story without judging everything.
> Sisera asks for water, and Jael gives him milk. Milk makes you sleepy. Maybe she wanted him to fall asleep quickly.

---

## Jabin is defeated (Verses 23–24)

[23] So on that day God brought down Jabin, the king of Canaan, in front of the people of Israel.
[24] And the hand of the people of Israel pressed harder and harder on Jabin, the king of Canaan,
until they had destroyed Jabin, the king of Canaan.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the end, it was not Barak, not Deborah, and not Jael who won. “God brought down Jabin.” People are tools in God’s hand.
