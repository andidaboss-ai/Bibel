# Judges – Chapter 8
### Gideon’s victory and his end

---

## Gideon calms Ephraim down (Verses 1–3)

[1] The men of Ephraim said to him,
“Why have you treated us like this? Why didn’t you call us when you went to fight against Midian?”
And they argued with him sharply.
[2] He said to them,
“What have I done now compared to you?
Aren’t the grapes left over in Ephraim better than the whole grape harvest of Abiezer?
[3] God has given the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb, into your hand!
What was I able to do compared to you?”
When he had said this, their anger against him calmed down.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ephraim was a large, proud tribe. It feels left out. Gideon answers wisely and humbly: You have achieved much more than I have! Even your leftover grapes, the little that is left after the harvest, are worth more than our whole harvest.
> A proverb says that a gentle answer turns away anger (Proverbs 15:1). In this way, Gideon prevents a fight between brothers.

---

## Succoth and Penuel refuse to help (Verses 4–9)

[4] Gideon came to the Jordan and crossed over,
he and the three hundred men who were with him.
They were worn out, but they kept chasing the enemies.
[5] He said to the men of Succoth,
“Please give loaves of bread to the people who follow me.
For they are worn out, and I am chasing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian.”
[6] The leaders of Succoth said,
“Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your hand,
that we should give bread to your army?”
[7] Gideon said,
“Because of this, when the LORD has given Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand,
I will tear your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers.”
[8] From there he went up to Penuel and spoke to them in the same way.
And the men of Penuel answered him just as the men of Succoth had answered.
[9] So he also said to the men of Penuel,
“When I come back in peace, I will tear down this tower.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Succoth and Penuel were Israelite cities east of the Jordan. Still, they refuse to give bread to their worn-out brothers. They are afraid: If Gideon loses, the Midianites will take revenge on them.
> “Are the hands … already in your hand”: At that time, people sometimes cut off the hands of defeated enemies as proof. So the leaders of Succoth are mocking him: Have you already defeated them?
> Gideon becomes angry. The humble Gideon from before is changing.

---

## The kings of Midian are captured (Verses 10–12)

[10] Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their armies with them,
about 15,000 men, all who were left of the whole army of the people of the east.
For 120,000 men who carried swords had fallen.
[11] Gideon went up by the road of the tent dwellers, east of Nobah and Jogbehah,
and struck the army while it felt safe.
[12] Zebah and Zalmunna fled, and he chased them.
He captured the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna,
and threw the whole army into panic.

---

## Gideon’s revenge (Verses 13–21)

[13] Gideon the son of Joash came back from the battle by the ascent of Heres.
[14] He caught a young man from the men of Succoth and questioned him.
The young man wrote down for him the names of the leaders of Succoth and its elders, 77 men.
[15] He came to the men of Succoth and said,
“Here are Zebah and Zalmunna. You mocked me because of them and said,
‘Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your hand,
that we should give bread to your worn-out men?’”
[16] He took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers,
and with them he taught the men of Succoth a lesson.
[17] And he tore down the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city.
[18] Then he said to Zebah and Zalmunna,
“What kind of men were they whom you killed at Tabor?”
They answered,
“They were like you. Each one looked like a king’s son.”
[19] He said,
“They were my brothers, the sons of my mother.
As surely as the LORD lives: If you had let them live, I would not kill you.”
[20] He said to Jether, his firstborn son,
“Get up and kill them!”
But the boy did not draw his sword.
For he was afraid, because he was still young.
[21] Then Zebah and Zalmunna said,
“Get up yourself and strike us down.
For as the man is, so is his strength.”
So Gideon got up and killed Zebah and Zalmunna.
And he took the crescents that hung on the necks of their camels.

> **What does this mean?**
> An interesting detail: An ordinary young man was able to write down the names. So even at that time, not only priests and scholars could write.
> “He taught them a lesson”: This is close to what the WEB says. Other old manuscripts say: “he threshed them.” It means: He punished them harshly with thorns.
> Now it becomes clear: Gideon is no longer fighting only for Israel, but also out of personal revenge. The kings had killed his brothers. And he uses great violence against his own countrymen in Succoth and Penuel. The hero of Chapter 7 shows a dark side here. The Bible does not hide this.
> Gideon wants to make his young son kill. But the boy is afraid. This shows: Violence is not something we should teach children.
> The “crescents” were jewelry in the shape of a crescent moon. They often had a religious meaning, because the moon was worshiped as a god.

---

## Gideon refuses the crown (Verses 22–23)

[22] Then the men of Israel said to Gideon,
“Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson too.
For you have saved us from the hand of Midian.”
[23] Gideon said to them,
“I will not rule over you,
and my son will not rule over you either.
The LORD shall rule over you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people want to make Gideon king, with his whole family. But Gideon says a wonderful sentence: “The LORD shall rule over you.”
> He has understood: It was not he who saved the people, but God. God alone is the true king of Israel.
> Sadly, right after this, his actions do not match his words.

---

## The golden ephod (Verses 24–28)

[24] Gideon said to them,
“I have a request for you:
Each of you, give me the earrings from his plunder.”
(For the enemies had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)
[25] They answered, “We will gladly give them.”
They spread out a garment,
and each man threw the earrings from his plunder onto it.
[26] The weight of the golden earrings that he asked for was about 42 pounds of gold.
Besides this, there were the crescents, the pendants, and the purple clothes that the kings of Midian had worn,
and also the chains on the necks of their camels.
[27] Gideon made an ephod out of it and set it up in his city, Ophrah.
And all Israel was unfaithful to God with it there, like a prostitute.
And it became a trap for Gideon and his family.
[28] So Midian was brought down in front of the people of Israel,
and they did not lift up their heads anymore.
And the land had rest for forty years in the days of Gideon.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: 1,700 shekels of gold. A shekel is a little under half an ounce.
> Here the Midianites are called “Ishmaelites.” Both peoples were nomads from the desert. Sometimes the Bible uses both names for the same people (see Genesis 37:25–28).
> An “ephod” was a piece of clothing worn by the high priest. It was used to ask for God’s will (Exodus 28). Gideon made himself his own ephod out of gold. Maybe he wanted to honor God. But it became an idol. People worshiped it instead of God.
> This is Gideon’s tragedy: He said, “The LORD shall rule over you,” but he himself made something that led people away from God. Meaning well is not always the same as doing well.
> Still: forty years of peace. God really did save Israel through Gideon.

## Gideon’s death (Verses 29–35)

[29] Jerubbaal the son of Joash went and lived in his own house.
[30] Gideon had seventy sons of his own, for he had many wives.
[31] His concubine, who lived in Shechem, also had a son by him.
And he named him Abimelech.
[32] Gideon the son of Joash died at a good old age.
He was buried in the tomb of his father Joash, in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.
[33] As soon as Gideon was dead, the people of Israel turned away again.
They were unfaithful like prostitutes and followed the Baals,
and they made Baal Berith their god.
[34] The people of Israel no longer remembered the LORD their God,
who had rescued them from the hand of all their enemies around them.
[35] And they did not show kindness to the family of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon,
for all the good he had done for Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gideon refused the crown, but he lived like a king: many wives, seventy sons, great wealth. And he named a son “Abimelech,” which means “my father is king.” What a name!
> A “concubine” was a woman who lived with a man but had fewer rights than a wife.
> As soon as Gideon is dead, the cycle starts all over again. “Baal Berith” means “lord of the covenant.” The people make a covenant with a false god instead of with the true God.
> The next chapter tells what becomes of Abimelech.
