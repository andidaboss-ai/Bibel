# Judges – Chapter 18
### The tribe of Dan looks for new land

---

## The spies at Micah’s house (Verses 1–6)

[1] In those days there was no king in Israel.
In those days the tribe of the Danites was looking for an inheritance to live in.
For until that day, no inheritance had fallen to them among the tribes of Israel.
[2] The children of Dan sent five men from their family, from their whole number,
brave men, from Zorah and from Eshtaol,
to spy out the land and explore it.
They said to them, “Go, explore the land!”
They came to the hill country of Ephraim, to Micah’s house, and spent the night there.
[3] When they were near Micah’s house,
they recognized the voice of the young man, the Levite.
They went over there and said to him,
“Who brought you here?
What are you doing in this place?
What do you have here?”
[4] He said to them,
“Micah has done this and that for me.
He has hired me, and I have become his priest.”
[5] They said to him,
“Please ask God for advice, so that we may know
whether the journey we are on will be successful.”
[6] The priest said to them,
“Go in peace.
The journey you are on is in front of the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The tribe of Dan had actually been given a territory (Joshua 19:40–48). But it could not conquer it. The Amorites forced the Danites back into the hill country (Judges 1:34). That is why the tribe is now looking for land somewhere else.
> “No inheritance had fallen to them” means: They had not yet really taken their land.
> The spies recognize the voice of the Levite, maybe by his southern accent.
> The priest’s answer sounds good, but it can mean two things. It can mean: God protects your journey. But it can also simply mean: God sees what you are doing.

---

## The land of Laish (Verses 7–10)

[7] Then the five men went on and came to Laish.
They saw the people who lived there:
They lived in safety, in the way of the Sidonians, quiet and carefree.
For there was no one in the land with power who could put them to shame in anything.
And they were far away from the Sidonians
and had nothing to do with anyone else.
[8] They came back to their brothers at Zorah and Eshtaol.
Their brothers asked them, “What do you say?”
[9] They said,
“Come on, let’s go up against them.
For we have seen the land, and it is very good.
And you are just sitting still?
Don’t be too lazy to go and enter and take the land.
[10] When you go, you will come to a people who feel safe.
And the land is large.
For God has given it into your hand.
It is a place where nothing on earth is lacking.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Laish was in the far north, at the foot of Mount Hermon, by the springs of the Jordan. It is green there and has lots of water.
> The Sidonians were a people of traders on the coast, in what is Lebanon today. The people of Laish lived peacefully like them. They had no army and no allies.
> The spies say: “God has given it into your hand.” But God had not said that. Laish was not part of the land that God had given to the Danites. They use God’s name for their own plans.
> This story reminds us of Moses’ spies (Numbers 13). But that was God’s command. Here it is only the plan of one tribe.

---

## The Danites steal Micah’s idols (Verses 11–20)

[11] Then six hundred men from the family of the Danites set out from there, from Zorah and Eshtaol,
armed with weapons of war.
[12] They went up and camped at Kiriath Jearim in Judah.
That is why that place is called Mahaneh Dan to this day.
It is behind Kiriath Jearim.
[13] From there they went on to the hill country of Ephraim
and came to Micah’s house.

[14] Then the five men spoke up
who had gone to spy out the land of Laish.
They said to their brothers,
“Do you know that in these houses there is an ephod, and teraphim,
and a carved image, and a cast metal image?
So now think about what you want to do.”
[15] They went over there and came to the house of the young Levite, to Micah’s house,
and asked him how he was doing.
[16] The six hundred men of the children of Dan, armed with their weapons of war,
stood at the entrance of the gate.
[17] The five men who had gone to spy out the land went up.
They went in there and took the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the cast metal image.
And the priest stood at the entrance of the gate with the six hundred men armed with weapons of war.
[18] When these men went into Micah’s house
and took the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the cast metal image,
the priest said to them, “What are you doing?”
[19] They said to him,
“Be quiet, put your hand over your mouth, and come with us.
Be a father and a priest to us.
Is it better for you to be priest for the house of one man,
or priest for a tribe and a family in Israel?”
[20] Then the priest’s heart was glad.
He took the ephod, the teraphim, and the carved image
and went with the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Six hundred warriors set out with their families. This was probably only a part of the tribe of Dan.
> “Mahaneh Dan” means “camp of Dan.” This place was already mentioned in the story of Samson (Chapter 13:25).
> The Danites steal the idols. Micah had paid for them with stolen silver, and now they themselves are stolen. No one asks: Does God even want such images?
> The Levite is not loyal. For a better job, he leaves Micah right away, who had taken him in like a son. He is even “glad.” He cares about honor and advantage, not about God.

## Micah is left with empty hands (Verses 21–26)

[21] So they turned and went away.
They put the little children, the livestock, and their belongings in front of them.
[22] When they were already a good distance from Micah’s house,
the men from the houses near Micah’s house gathered together
and caught up with the children of Dan.
[23] They called out after the children of Dan.
They turned around and said to Micah,
“What is wrong with you, that you come with such a crowd?”
[24] He said,
“You have taken away my gods that I made, and the priest,
and you have gone away!
What do I have left?
How can you ask me, ‘What is wrong with you?’”
[25] The children of Dan said to him,
“Don’t let us hear your voice.
Otherwise angry men will attack you,
and you will lose your life and the lives of your family.”
[26] The children of Dan went on their way.
When Micah saw that they were too strong for him,
he turned and went back to his house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Micah’s complaint is sad and at the same time almost funny: “You have taken away my gods that I made.” A god that you have made yourself and that others can simply carry away cannot help. Later the prophets often mocked such gods (for example Isaiah 44:9–20).
> Here only the law of the stronger counts. No one protects Micah. There is no king and no judge.

---

## The Danites conquer Laish (Verses 27–31)

[27] They took what Micah had made, and the priest he had.
They came to Laish, to a people who lived quietly and carefree.
They struck them with the edge of the sword
and burned the city with fire.
[28] There was no one to rescue them, because the city was far from Sidon,
and they had nothing to do with anyone else.
It was in the valley near Beth Rehob.
The Danites rebuilt the city and lived in it.
[29] They named the city Dan,
after the name of their forefather Dan, who was born to Israel.
But the city used to be called Laish.

[30] The children of Dan set up the carved image for themselves.
And Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Moses,
he and his sons were priests for the tribe of the Danites
until the day the land was taken into captivity.
[31] So they set up for themselves the carved image that Micah had made.
It stayed there the whole time that the house of God was in Shiloh.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Danites attack a peaceful people without defense, who had done nothing to anyone. The Bible tells this without praise. God had not given them this task. This story shows how deeply Israel had sunk. It is not an example to follow, but a warning.
> “Israel” in verse 29 is another name for Jacob. Dan was one of his twelve sons.
> The city of Dan later became the northernmost city of Israel. People spoke of the land from Dan to Beersheba and meant the whole land from north to south. Later King Jeroboam set up a golden calf there (1 Kings 12:29). Dan remained a place of false worship.
> Now we learn the Levite’s name: Jonathan, a grandson of Moses. It is shocking: Even the grandson of the great Moses serves idols.
> In the Hebrew text, a small letter “n” is written above the line here. This turns the name “Moses” into “Manasseh.” Jewish Bible teachers have said: This was done to protect the honor of Moses. The WEB has “Moses.”
> “The captivity of the land” probably means the time when the Assyrians conquered the north of Israel and took people away (2 Kings 15:29; 17:6).
> Shiloh was the place where God’s tent stood, the proper place of worship (Joshua 18:1). So the Danites had their own worship, separate from the house of God.
