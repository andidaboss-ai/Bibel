# Judges – Chapter 6
### Gideon is called

---

## The Midianites come like locusts (Verses 1–6)

[1] The children of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
So the LORD gave them into the hand of Midian for seven years.
[2] The hand of Midian pressed hard on Israel.
Because of Midian, the children of Israel made hiding places for themselves in the mountains, caves, and strongholds.
[3] Whenever Israel had sown its seed,
the Midianites, the Amalekites, and the people of the east came up against them.
[4] They camped against them and destroyed the crops of the land as far as Gaza.
They left nothing to live on in Israel, no sheep, no ox, and no donkey.
[5] For they came up with their livestock and their tents.
They came in as many as locusts.
They and their camels could not be counted.
And they came into the land to destroy it.
[6] So Israel became very poor because of Midian.
And the children of Israel cried out to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Midianites were nomads from the desert. Every year at harvest time they came with their herds and camels and took everything. The Israelites had nothing left to eat. They had to hide in caves.
> At that time, camels were a new and feared “weapon.” With them, the robbers could travel long distances through the desert and attack quickly.

---

## A prophet reminds them of God’s deeds (Verses 7–10)

[7] When the children of Israel cried out to the LORD because of Midian,
[8] the LORD sent a prophet to the children of Israel.
He said to them,
“This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘I brought you up from Egypt.
I brought you out of the house of slavery.
[9] I rescued you from the hand of the Egyptians
and from the hand of all who oppressed you.
I drove them out in front of you and gave you their land.
[10] And I said to you,
“I am the LORD your God.
Do not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you live.”
But you have not listened to my voice.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before God helps, he sends a prophet. The people are to understand why they are in trouble. It is not only about being set free from the enemies, but about turning back to God.

---

## The angel visits Gideon (Verses 11–16)

[11] The angel of the LORD came and sat under the oak in Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite.
His son Gideon was threshing wheat in the winepress,
to hide it from the Midianites.
[12] The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said to him,
“The LORD is with you, you mighty hero!”
[13] Gideon said to him,
“Oh, my lord, if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us?
Where are all his wonderful works that our fathers told us about, when they said,
‘Didn’t the LORD bring us up from Egypt?’
But now the LORD has thrown us away and given us into the hand of Midian.”
[14] The LORD looked at him and said,
“Go in this strength of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian.
Haven’t I sent you?”
[15] He said to him,
“Oh, Lord, how can I save Israel?
My family is the poorest in Manasseh,
and I am the least in my father’s house.”
[16] The LORD said to him,
“I will be with you.
And you will strike the Midianites as if they were one man.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Normally, grain is threshed in an open place in the wind. Gideon threshes in secret in a winepress, a pit in the rock. He is afraid.
> To thresh means: to beat the grain so that the seeds come loose.
> And it is to this frightened man that the angel says: “You mighty hero!” It sounds almost like a joke. But God sees in Gideon what he can become.
> Gideon asks an honest question that many people know: If God is with us, why are things so bad for us? God does not answer the question directly. But he sends Gideon to change something.
> Gideon refuses: I am the least, from the poorest family. This reminds us of Moses, who did not want to go either (Exodus 3–4). God gives him the same promise as he gave Moses then: I will be with you. It does not depend on Gideon’s strength, but on God being with him.
> Here, the angel of the LORD is also simply called “the LORD.” God himself speaks through the angel.

## A sign: fire from the rock (Verses 17–24)

[17] He said to him,
“If I have found favor in your eyes,
then give me a sign that it is really you who is talking with me.
[18] Please don’t go away from here until I come to you
and bring out my gift and set it in front of you.”
He said, “I will wait until you come back.”
[19] Gideon went in and prepared a young goat
and bread without yeast from about 6 gallons of flour.
He put the meat in a basket and poured the broth into a pot.
He brought it out to him under the oak and offered it to him.
[20] The angel of God said to him,
“Take the meat and the bread without yeast, lay them on this rock,
and pour out the broth.”
And he did so.
[21] Then the angel of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff that was in his hand
and touched the meat and the bread without yeast.
Fire went up out of the rock and burned up the meat and the bread without yeast.
And the angel of the LORD disappeared from his sight.
[22] Then Gideon realized that it was the angel of the LORD.
And Gideon said,
“Oh no, Lord GOD!
For I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!”
[23] The LORD said to him,
“Peace be with you! Don’t be afraid. You will not die.”
[24] Then Gideon built an altar there to the LORD and called it “The LORD is Peace.”
To this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: an ephah of flour. An ephah is about 6 gallons. That was a lot, a generous gift at a time of hunger.
> Gideon asks for a sign. He wants to be sure. God is patient with him.
> At that time, people believed: Whoever sees God must die. That is why Gideon is frightened. But God says: “Peace be with you!” In Hebrew: “Shalom.”
> The altar is called “The LORD is Peace” (in Hebrew: “Yahweh Shalom”). In the middle of trouble, in the middle of war, Gideon learns: God is peace.

---

## Gideon tears down the altar of Baal (Verses 25–32)

[25] That same night the LORD said to him,
“Take your father’s bull, the second bull, which is seven years old.
Tear down the altar of Baal that belongs to your father,
and cut down the Asherah pole that is next to it.
[26] Then build an altar to the LORD your God on top of this stronghold, carefully laid out.
And take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering,
with the wood of the Asherah pole that you cut down.”
[27] So Gideon took ten men from his servants
and did as the LORD had told him.
Because he was afraid of his father’s family and of the men of the city,
he could not do it by day, so he did it at night.
[28] When the men of the city got up early in the morning,
the altar of Baal was torn down,
the Asherah pole next to it was cut down,
and the second bull had been offered on the newly built altar.
[29] They said to one another, “Who has done this?”
They searched and asked around.
Then they said, “Gideon the son of Joash has done this.”
[30] Then the men of the city said to Joash,
“Bring out your son so that he may die!
For he has torn down the altar of Baal
and cut down the Asherah pole next to it.”
[31] Joash said to all who stood against him,
“Will you fight for Baal? Or will you save him?
Whoever fights for him shall be put to death by morning!
If he is a god, let him fight for himself,
because someone has torn down his altar!”
[32] So on that day Gideon was named Jerub-Baal,
because people said, “Let Baal fight against him, because he has torn down his altar.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before Gideon fights against the Midianites, he has to clean up at home. His own father had an altar for Baal. The worship of idols was right in the middle of Israel, even in Gideon’s family.
> Gideon obeys, but at night, because he is afraid. God accepts him anyway. You do not have to be fearless to obey God.
> The men of the city want to kill Gideon because he has insulted Baal. This shows how far Israel had moved away from God. But Gideon’s father defends him with a wise sentence: If Baal is a god, let him defend himself. A god who has to be protected is not a god.
> “Jerub-Baal” means “let Baal fight.” The name becomes a way of mocking Baal, because Baal did nothing.

---

## The army gathers (Verses 33–35)

[33] Then all Midian, Amalek, and the people of the east gathered together.
They crossed over and camped in the valley of Jezreel.
[34] But the Spirit of the LORD came on Gideon, and he blew a trumpet.
The Abiezrites were called together to follow him.
[35] He sent messengers throughout all Manasseh,
and they too were called together to follow him.
He sent messengers to Asher, to Zebulun, and to Naphtali.
And they came up to meet them.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The Spirit of the LORD came on Gideon”: The Hebrew literally says that the Spirit of the LORD put on Gideon like a piece of clothing. God wraps him up completely. The frightened farmer becomes a leader.

---

## The sign with the fleece (Verses 36–40)

[36] Gideon said to God,
“If you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said,
[37] then I will put a fleece of wool on the threshing floor.
If there is dew only on the fleece and all the ground stays dry,
then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said.”
[38] And that is what happened.
When he got up early the next morning, he squeezed the fleece
and wrung the dew out of the fleece, a whole bowl full of water.
[39] Gideon said to God,
“Don’t let your anger burn against me, and I will speak just this once more.
Please let me make a test with the fleece just this once more:
Let only the fleece be dry now, and let there be dew on all the ground.”
[40] And God did so that night.
Only the fleece was dry, and there was dew on all the ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> A fleece is the wool of a sheep in one piece.
> Gideon has already received a sign (Verse 21). Still, he doubts. He asks twice for a sign with the fleece. And God is patient with him and gives him both signs.
> This is comforting: God does not look down on those who doubt. He meets them in their weakness.
> This story gave us the saying “to put out a fleece”: to ask God for a sign. But many Bible teachers also say: Gideon did not really need it. God had already spoken clearly. The story is not a model for testing God all the time (Deuteronomy 6:16).
