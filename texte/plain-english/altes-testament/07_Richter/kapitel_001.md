# Judges – Chapter 1
### The conquest stays incomplete

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Judges?

The book of Judges tells about the time after Joshua, before Israel had a king. This time may have lasted 200 to 300 years.
The “judges” were not judges in a court, as we know them today. They were leaders and rescuers whom God called in times of need. They set the people free from enemies. Well-known judges are Deborah, a woman, Gideon, and Samson.
The book has a fixed pattern that repeats again and again: Israel leaves God and serves foreign gods. Then enemies oppress it. The people cry out to God. God sends a rescuer. There is peace. And then everything starts over again.
Each time it gets worse. At the end of the book, the people have sunk into chaos and violence. The last sentence is: “In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes” (Judges 21:25).
The book of Judges is honest. It shows the dark sides of God’s people, including terrible violence, especially against women. We leave nothing out, but we explain it. The Bible does not tell these stories as examples to follow but as a warning: This is what it looks like when people forget God.
And still the book shows: God does not give up on his people. Again and again he hears the people’s cry and sends rescue.

## Judah goes into battle first (Verses 1–7)

[1] After Joshua died, the people of Israel asked the LORD,
“Which of us should go up first against the Canaanites to fight against them?”
[2] The LORD said,
“Judah will go up.
Look, I have given the land into his hand.”
[3] Judah said to his brother Simeon,
“Come up with me into my territory, so that we can fight against the Canaanites.
Then I will also go with you into your territory.”
So Simeon went with him.
[4] Judah went up, and the LORD gave the Canaanites and the Perizzites into their hand.
They struck 10,000 men at Bezek.
[5] At Bezek they found Adoni-Bezek and fought against him.
They struck the Canaanites and the Perizzites.
[6] But Adoni-Bezek ran away.
They chased him, caught him,
and cut off his thumbs and his big toes.
[7] Adoni-Bezek said,
“70 kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off
have looked for scraps of food under my table.
As I have done, so God has paid me back.”
They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

> **What does this mean?**
> After Joshua’s death, there is no single leader anymore. The tribes ask God himself. Judah, the largest tribe, is to begin.
> “Adoni-Bezek” means “lord of Bezek.”
> The punishment is cruel: Without thumbs, you cannot hold a sword. Without big toes, you cannot run well. This was how warriors were made unable to fight. Adoni-Bezek himself had done this to 70 kings. He realizes: What I did to others has now happened to me.
> This is not a call to cut off parts of people’s bodies. The Bible is telling how cruel the time was.

## Jerusalem, Hebron, and Debir (Verses 8–15)

[8] The people of Judah fought against Jerusalem.
They took it, struck it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire.
[9] After that, the people of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites
who lived in the hill country, in the South, and in the lowland.
[10] Judah went against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron.
(The name of Hebron used to be Kiriath Arba.)
They struck Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai.
[11] From there he went against the people who lived in Debir.
(The name of Debir used to be Kiriath Sepher.)
[12] Caleb said,
“I will give my daughter Achsah as a wife to the man who strikes Kiriath Sepher and takes it.”
[13] Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took it.
So Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah as his wife.
[14] When she came to him, she got him to ask her father for a field.
She got off her donkey, and Caleb asked her, “What would you like?”
[15] She said to him,
“Give me a blessing.
Since you have given me land in the South, give me springs of water too.”
So Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Jerusalem is taken and burned. But verse 21 says that the Jebusites went on living there. Maybe only part of the city was destroyed, or the Jebusites came back. Only David took Jerusalem for good (2 Samuel 5).
> We already know the story of Caleb, Othniel, and Achsah from Joshua 15:13–19. Here it is told again almost word for word. Othniel will soon become the first judge (Chapter 3).

## Successes in the south (Verses 16–20)

[16] The descendants of the Kenite, Moses’ brother-in-law,
went up with the people of Judah from the city of palm trees into the wilderness of Judah, which is south of Arad.
They went and lived with the people.
[17] Judah went with his brother Simeon.
They struck the Canaanites who lived in Zephath
and completely destroyed the city.
So the city was named Hormah.
[18] Judah also took Gaza with its territory,
Ashkelon with its territory, and Ekron with its territory.
[19] The LORD was with Judah, and Judah drove out the people of the hill country.
But it could not drive out the people of the valley,
because they had chariots of iron.
[20] They gave Hebron to Caleb, as Moses had said.
And he drove out the three sons of Anak from there.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Brother-in-law”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew word usually means “father-in-law.” The Kenites were the family of Moses’ father-in-law Jethro (Exodus 18). They did not belong to Israel, but they lived together with Israel. The city of palm trees (verse 16) is Jericho.
> “Hormah” means “devoted to destruction.”
> Verse 18 says that Judah took the Philistine cities of Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ekron. But later these cities belong to the Philistines again. The Greek translation of the Bible even says the opposite here: Judah did not take them.
> Verse 19 is remarkable: “The LORD was with Judah,” and still they could not conquer the valley because of the iron chariots. The narrator does not ask why. But the question remains: Was it really the strength of the enemies, or did Judah lack trust?

## The other tribes do not drive out the Canaanites (Verses 21–36)

[21] The people of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites who lived in Jerusalem.
So the Jebusites live with the people of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day.
[22] The house of Joseph also went up against Bethel, and the LORD was with them.
[23] The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel.
(The name of the city used to be Luz.)
[24] The watchmen saw a man coming out of the city and said to him,
“Please show us the way into the city, and we will be kind to you.”
[25] He showed them the way into the city.
They struck the city with the edge of the sword.
But they let the man and his whole family go.
[26] The man went into the land of the Hittites, built a city, and named it Luz.
That is its name to this day.
[27] Manasseh did not drive out the people of Beth Shean and its towns,
or Taanach and its towns, or the people of Dor and its towns,
or the people of Ibleam and its towns,
or the people of Megiddo and its towns.
The Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.
[28] When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor.
But they did not drive them out completely.
[29] Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer.
The Canaanites lived among them in Gezer.
[30] Zebulun did not drive out the people of Kitron or the people of Nahalol.
The Canaanites lived among them and had to do forced labor.
[31] Asher did not drive out the people of Acco,
or the people of Sidon, Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik, or Rehob.
[32] The Asherites lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land,
for they did not drive them out.
[33] Naphtali did not drive out the people of Beth Shemesh or the people of Beth Anath.
He lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land.
But the people of Beth Shemesh and Beth Anath had to do forced labor.
[34] The Amorites forced the people of Dan back into the hill country.
They did not let them come down into the valley.
[35] The Amorites were determined to stay at Mount Heres, in Aijalon, and in Shaalbim.
But the hand of the house of Joseph grew strong against them, so that they had to do forced labor.
[36] The border of the Amorites went from the ascent of Akrabbim, from the rock, and on upward.

> **What does this mean?**
> It is like a long list of failures: Tribe after tribe does not drive out the Canaanites. With some tribes, the Canaanites live among Israel. With Asher and Naphtali, it is the other way around: Israel lives among the Canaanites. And Dan is even driven out itself.
> The problem was not the people themselves, but their gods. As Joshua had warned (Joshua 23:12–13), the nations became a trap for Israel. Chapter 2 tells about this.
> Forced labor for people of another nation does not fit our understanding of human dignity today. But the narrator does not criticize it because of human dignity. He criticizes it because Israel did not do what God had said.
> A man from Bethel betrays his city and is spared. This is like Rahab. But unlike Rahab, he does not become part of Israel. He goes away and builds a new city.
