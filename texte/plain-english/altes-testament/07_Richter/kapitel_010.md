# Judges – Chapter 10
### Tola, Jair, and Israel’s repentance

---

## Tola and Jair (Verses 1–5)

[1] After Abimelech, Tola came forward to save Israel.
He was the son of Puah and the grandson of Dodo.
He was a man from the tribe of Issachar.
He lived in Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim.
[2] Tola was judge over Israel for 23 years.
Then he died and was buried in Shamir.
[3] After him came Jair from Gilead.
He was judge over Israel for 22 years.
[4] Jair had 30 sons.
They rode on 30 young donkeys.
They had 30 cities in the land of Gilead.
These cities are still called Havvoth Jair today.
[5] Jair died and was buried in Kamon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only a little is told about Tola and Jair. They are called the “minor judges.” They led Israel in calmer times.
> Thirty sons on thirty donkeys: This shows that Jair was rich and powerful. Whoever rode on a donkey was an important man.
> “Havvoth Jair” means “tent villages of Jair” (see also Numbers 32:41).

---

## Israel serves many gods (Verses 6–9)

[6] Once again the people of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
They served the Baals and the Ashtaroth.
They served the gods of Syria, the gods of Sidon, and the gods of Moab.
They served the gods of the people of Ammon and the gods of the Philistines.
They turned away from the LORD and did not serve him.
[7] So the LORD became very angry with Israel.
He handed them over to the Philistines and to the people of Ammon.
[8] That year these enemies crushed the people of Israel and treated them harshly.
For 18 years they oppressed all the people of Israel on the other side of the Jordan.
That was in the land of the Amorites, in Gilead.
[9] The people of Ammon even crossed the Jordan.
They fought against Judah, against Benjamin, and against the tribe of Ephraim.
So Israel was in great trouble.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it gets worse than ever before: Israel serves not just one foreign god, but the gods of seven different peoples. And they leave the LORD completely.
> Two enemies come at the same time: the Philistines in the west and the people of Ammon in the east. The story of Samson later tells about the Philistines. The story of Jephthah tells about the people of Ammon.

---

## God is tired of it (Verses 10–16)

[10] Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD.
They said,
“We have sinned against you.
We have turned away from our God and served the Baals.”
[11] The LORD said to the people of Israel,
“I saved you from the Egyptians and from the Amorites.
I saved you from the people of Ammon and from the Philistines.
Didn’t I?
[12] The Sidonians, the Amalekites, and the Maonites also oppressed you.
You cried out to me, and I saved you from their power.
[13] But you have turned away from me and served other gods.
So I will not save you anymore.
[14] Go and cry out to the gods you have chosen.
Let them save you when you are in trouble!”
[15] The people of Israel said to the LORD,
“We have sinned!
Do to us whatever you think is good.
Only please save us today!”
[16] They got rid of the foreign gods among them,
and they served the LORD.
Then the LORD was deeply grieved by Israel’s misery.

> **What does this mean?**
> This time, God first says no. The people have begged so often and then fallen away again anyway. God says: Go and call on your other gods! This is bitter mockery, but also pain. God is like a father who is disappointed by his children again and again.
> But then the people do something they had not done before: They really put away the foreign gods. They do not only ask, they turn back.
> And then comes one of the most beautiful sentences in the book: “The LORD was deeply grieved by Israel’s misery.” God cannot just watch his people suffer. His love is stronger than his anger.
> “Maonites”: It is not certain who is meant. The Greek translation has “Midianites” here.

---

## Who will lead us? (Verses 17–18)

[17] Then the people of Ammon gathered their army.
They set up camp in Gilead.
The people of Israel also came together.
They set up camp in Mizpah.
[18] The people and the leaders of Gilead said to one another,
“Who will be the first to fight against the people of Ammon?
That man will be the head over everyone who lives in Gilead.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The armies stand facing each other. But Israel has no leader. No one wants to make the first move. The next chapter tells who this man will be.
