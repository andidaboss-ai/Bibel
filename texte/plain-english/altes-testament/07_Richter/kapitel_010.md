# Judges – Chapter 10
### Tola, Jair, and Israel’s repentance

---

## Tola and Jair (Verses 1–5)

[1] After Abimelech, Tola rose up to save Israel.
He was the son of Puah, the son of Dodo, a man from Issachar.
He lived in Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim.
[2] He judged Israel for 23 years.
Then he died and was buried in Shamir.
[3] After him Jair the Gileadite rose up.
He judged Israel for 22 years.
[4] He had thirty sons who rode on thirty young donkeys.
They had thirty cities, which are called Havvoth Jair to this day.
They are in the land of Gilead.
[5] Jair died and was buried in Kamon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only a little is told about Tola and Jair. They are called the “minor judges.” They led Israel in calmer times.
> Thirty sons on thirty donkeys: This shows that Jair was rich and powerful. Whoever rode on a donkey was an important man.
> “Havvoth Jair” means “tent villages of Jair” (see also Numbers 32:41).

---

## Israel serves many gods (Verses 6–9)

[6] The children of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
They served the Baals and the Ashtaroth,
the gods of Syria, the gods of Sidon, the gods of Moab,
the gods of the children of Ammon, and the gods of the Philistines.
They left the LORD and did not serve him.
[7] So the anger of the LORD burned against Israel,
and he sold them into the hand of the Philistines and into the hand of the children of Ammon.
[8] They troubled and oppressed the children of Israel that year.
For eighteen years they oppressed all the children of Israel who lived on the other side of the Jordan,
in the land of the Amorites, which is in Gilead.
[9] The children of Ammon even crossed the Jordan
to fight against Judah, against Benjamin, and against the house of Ephraim too.
And Israel was in great distress.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it gets worse than ever before: Israel serves not just one foreign god, but the gods of seven different peoples. And they leave the LORD completely.
> Two enemies come at the same time: the Philistines in the west and the children of Ammon in the east. The story of Samson later tells about the Philistines. The story of Jephthah tells about the children of Ammon.

---

## God is tired of it (Verses 10–16)

[10] Then the children of Israel cried out to the LORD and said,
“We have sinned against you,
because we have left our God and served the Baals.”
[11] The LORD said to the children of Israel,
“Didn’t I save you from the Egyptians, from the Amorites, from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines?
[12] The Sidonians, the Amalekites, and the Maonites also oppressed you.
And you cried out to me, and I saved you from their hand.
[13] But you have left me and served other gods.
So I will not save you anymore.
[14] Go and cry out to the gods you have chosen.
Let them save you in your time of distress!”
[15] The children of Israel said to the LORD,
“We have sinned!
Do to us whatever seems good to you.
Only please save us today!”
[16] And they put away the foreign gods from among them and served the LORD.
Then his soul could no longer bear the misery of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> This time, God first says no. The people have begged so often and then fallen away again anyway. God says: Go and call on your other gods! This is bitter mockery, but also pain. God is like a father who is disappointed by his children again and again.
> But then the people do something they had not done before: They really put away the foreign gods. They do not only ask, they turn back.
> And then comes one of the most beautiful sentences in the book: “His soul could no longer bear the misery of Israel.” God cannot just watch his people suffer. His love is stronger than his anger.
> “Maonites”: It is not certain who is meant. The Greek translation has “Midianites” here.

---

## Who will lead us? (Verses 17–18)

[17] Then the children of Ammon gathered and camped in Gilead.
And the children of Israel gathered and camped in Mizpah.
[18] The people, the leaders of Gilead, said to one another,
“Who is the man who will start the fight against the children of Ammon?
He shall be head over all the people who live in Gilead.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The armies stand facing each other. But Israel has no leader. No one wants to make the first move. The next chapter tells who this man will be.
