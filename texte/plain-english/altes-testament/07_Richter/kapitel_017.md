# Judges – Chapter 17
### Micah and his idol

---

## The stolen silver (Verses 1–6)

[1] There was a man from the hill country of Ephraim.
His name was Micah.
[2] He said to his mother,
“The eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you –
you spoke a curse about it, and you said it so that I heard it too –
look, the silver is with me. I took it.”
His mother said, “May the LORD bless my son!”
[3] He gave the eleven hundred pieces of silver back to his mother.
Then his mother said,
“I truly dedicate the silver to the LORD, from my hand for my son,
to make a carved image and a cast metal image.
So now I will give it back to you.”
[4] When he gave the money back to his mother,
his mother took two hundred pieces of silver and gave them to a silversmith.
He made a carved image and a cast metal image out of it.
It was in Micah’s house.
[5] The man Micah had a house of gods.
He made an ephod and teraphim
and dedicated one of his sons, who became his priest.
[6] In those days there was no king in Israel.
Everyone did what was right in his own eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> With this chapter, the last part of the book begins. It no longer tells about judges, but about how Israel falls apart on the inside.
> Micah steals from his own mother. Only when she curses the thief does he become afraid and give the money back. The mother blesses him right away to cancel the curse.
> 1,100 pieces of silver are about 27 pounds of silver, and 200 pieces of silver are about 5 pounds. A piece of silver was probably a shekel, a little under half an ounce. Interesting: In Chapter 16, Delilah got exactly this much from each of the lords.
> The mother wants to dedicate the silver “to the LORD,” and then she makes idols out of it. This is exactly what God had forbidden: You shall not make an idol for yourself (Exodus 20:4). She means well, but she does the opposite of God’s will. Besides, out of 1,100 pieces of silver, she gives only 200 for it.
> An “ephod” here was probably an object used to ask God questions. “Teraphim” were household gods, small figures (see Genesis 31:19).
> Micah makes his own son a priest. But only the descendants of Aaron were allowed to be priests.
> Verse 6 is the key to the last chapters of the book: Everyone makes his own religion and his own rules. At the end of the book, this sentence comes again.

## A Levite becomes Micah’s priest (Verses 7–13)

[7] There was a young man from Bethlehem in Judah, from the family of Judah.
He was a Levite, and he lived there.
[8] The man left the city, Bethlehem in Judah,
to live wherever he could find a place.
On his journey he came to the hill country of Ephraim, to Micah’s house.
[9] Micah asked him, “Where do you come from?”
He said to him,
“I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah.
I am looking for a place to live.”
[10] Micah said to him,
“Live with me.
Be a father and a priest to me.
I will give you ten pieces of silver a year, a set of clothes, and your food.”
So the Levite went in.
[11] The Levite was happy to live with the man.
And the young man was like one of his sons to him.
[12] Micah dedicated the Levite.
The young man became his priest and lived in Micah’s house.
[13] Then Micah said,
“Now I know that the LORD will do good to me,
because I have a Levite as my priest.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites had no land of their own. They were to live in the Levite cities and live from the tithes of the people (Joshua 21). That this Levite is wandering around looking for work shows: The people no longer took care of the Levites.
> A tithe is a tenth of what someone earns or harvests.
> Ten pieces of silver a year are about 4 ounces of silver.
> Micah thinks: Now I have a “real” priest, so God has to bless me. He thinks that you could buy God’s blessing with the right things and the right people. But he has idols in his house. God does not let himself be used like that.
> “Father” was a title of honor for a spiritual teacher.
