# Judges – Chapter 14
### The lion, the riddle, and the wedding

---

## Samson wants to marry a Philistine woman (Verses 1–4)

[1] Samson went down to Timnah.
In Timnah he saw a woman, one of the daughters of the Philistines.
[2] He came back up and told his father and his mother.
He said,
“I have seen a woman in Timnah, one of the daughters of the Philistines.
Now get her for me as my wife.”
[3] Then his father and his mother said to him,
“Isn’t there a woman among the daughters of your relatives
or among all my people,
that you have to go and take a wife from the uncircumcised Philistines?”
Samson said to his father,
“Get her for me, for she pleases me well.”
[4] But his father and his mother did not know that this came from the LORD.
For he was looking for a reason to act against the Philistines.
At that time the Philistines ruled over Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time, parents chose the husbands and wives for their children. But Samson decides for himself. He wants a woman from the people who are oppressing Israel.
> “Uncircumcised”: Circumcision was the sign of the covenant with God. The Philistines did not have this sign. The parents are worried: A marriage with a woman who does not know the LORD can lead away from faith (Deuteronomy 7:3–4).
> “She pleases me well” literally means in Hebrew: “She is right in my eyes.” This fits the big theme at the end of the book: “Everyone did what was right in his own eyes” (Judges 17:6; 21:25).
> Verse 4 is hard: God uses even Samson’s stubborn wish for his plan. This does not mean that God likes everything Samson does. But God can also act through people who make mistakes.

## Samson and the lion (Verses 5–9)

[5] Then Samson went down to Timnah with his father and his mother.
They came to the vineyards of Timnah.
Suddenly a young lion roared at him.
[6] Then the Spirit of the LORD came on him with power.
He tore the lion apart with his bare hands,
as you would tear apart a young goat.
But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done.
[7] He went down and talked with the woman.
And Samson liked her very much.

[8] After a while he came back to take her as his wife.
He turned off the road to look at the dead body of the lion.
And there was a swarm of bees in the body of the lion, and honey.
[9] He took the honey into his hands
and went on, eating as he walked.
He came to his father and mother and gave them some, and they ate.
But he did not tell them that he had taken the honey out of the lion’s body.

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time there were still lions in Israel. Samson kills one without a weapon. He does not do this by his own strength: The Spirit of the LORD comes on him.
> Samson is in the vineyards. For a Nazirite this is a dangerous place, because he is not allowed to eat anything from the vine.
> Then Samson takes honey out of the dead lion. A Nazirite was not allowed to touch dead bodies (Numbers 6:6). There the law speaks about dead people. But a dead animal made every Israelite unclean (Leviticus 11:27). Samson does not take the rules seriously. That is probably why he keeps it secret from his parents. He even gives them some of the honey without telling them.

## The wedding feast and the riddle (Verses 10–14)

[10] His father went down to the woman.
And Samson held a feast there,
for that is what young men used to do.
[11] When the people saw him, they brought 30 companions to be with him.
[12] Samson said to them,
“Let me tell you a riddle now.
If you can tell me the answer within the seven days of the feast and find it out,
then I will give you 30 linen shirts and 30 changes of clothing.
[13] But if you cannot tell me the answer,
then you must give me 30 linen shirts and 30 changes of clothing.”
They said to him,
“Tell us your riddle, so that we can hear it.”
[14] He said to them,
“Out of the eater came food.
Out of the strong came sweetness.”
For three days they could not solve the riddle.

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time, a wedding was celebrated for seven days. In Hebrew, the word for “feast” actually means “drinking party.” The text does not say whether Samson, as a Nazirite, drank wine there.
> The 30 companions are Philistines. They are supposed to keep Samson company, but maybe also to watch him.
> A “change of clothing” (the words in the WEB, our English source text) was a set of fine festive clothes. Clothing was very expensive at that time. So the bet was about a lot.
> The riddle is actually unfair. No one could know the answer, because no one had seen what happened with the lion.

## The woman gives away the riddle (Verses 15–18)

[15] On the seventh day they said to Samson’s wife,
“Talk your husband into telling us the riddle.
Otherwise we will burn you and your father’s house with fire.
Did you invite us here to make us poor? Isn’t that so?”
[16] Samson’s wife cried in front of him and said,
“You just hate me and don’t love me.
You have told a riddle to the people of my nation,
and you haven’t told me the answer.”
He said to her,
“I haven’t even told my father or my mother.
Why should I tell you?”
[17] She cried in front of him for the seven days while their feast lasted.
On the seventh day he told her, because she pressed him so hard.
And she told the riddle to the people of her nation.
[18] On the seventh day, before the sun went down, the men of the city said to him,
“What is sweeter than honey?
What is stronger than a lion?”
He said to them,
“If you had not plowed with my heifer,
you would not have found out my riddle.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Philistines threaten the young woman with death. She is in a terrible situation: She is caught between her husband and her people.
> Verse 15 says “on the seventh day,” but verse 17 says she cried “for the seven days.” This does not fit together completely. The old Greek translation has “on the fourth day” in verse 15. Maybe the woman had already been crying the whole week, and on the seventh day the threat was added.
> A heifer is a young cow. “Plowed with my heifer” is a crude picture. Samson means: You used my wife to get the secret. This is not a respectful way to talk about your own wife.
> This scene will happen again later: Once again a woman will press Samson until he gives away his secret (Chapter 16).

## Samson takes revenge in Ashkelon (Verses 19–20)

[19] Then the Spirit of the LORD came on him with power.
He went down to Ashkelon and struck down 30 of their men.
He took what he could from them as plunder
and gave the changes of clothing to those who had solved the riddle.
His anger burned, and he went up to his father’s house.
[20] But Samson’s wife was given to his companion,
who had been his friend.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ashkelon was one of the five great cities of the Philistines, by the sea.
> Samson kills 30 innocent men just to pay what he owes from the bet. This is a horrible deed. The Bible says that the Spirit of the LORD came on him. This means: Through Samson, God begins the fight against Israel’s oppressors. But the Bible does not say that Samson’s anger and revenge are good. Samson is a hero with great weaknesses.
> Jewish and Christian Bible teachers have often seen Samson critically: He was gifted by God, but he lived by his own wishes. This story must never be used as an example to justify revenge or violence.
> In the end, Samson loses his wife. Her father gives her to another man. The wedding ends in disaster.
