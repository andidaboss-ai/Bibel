# Judges – Chapter 15
### The foxes and the donkey’s jawbone

---

## Samson wants to see his wife (Verses 1–3)

[1] But after a while, at the time of the wheat harvest,
Samson visited his wife and brought a young goat with him.
He said, “I will go in to my wife in her room.”
But her father would not let him go in.
[2] Her father said,
“I was sure that you completely hated her.
So I gave her to your companion.
Isn’t her younger sister more beautiful than she is?
Please take her instead.”
[3] Samson said to them,
“This time I will be without blame toward the Philistines
when I do them harm.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The wheat harvest was in early summer, around May or June.
> Samson comes back as if nothing had happened. At that time, a young goat was a gift that people brought to make peace.
> Meanwhile the father has given his daughter to another man. Now he offers Samson the younger sister, as if women were goods that can be traded. That is how it often was at that time. The Bible tells it, but it does not praise it.

---

## The foxes with the torches (Verses 4–6)

[4] Samson went and caught three hundred foxes.
He took torches and tied the foxes together by their tails, two by two.
Between each pair of tails he put a torch.
[5] He set the torches on fire
and let the foxes run into the standing grain of the Philistines.
So he burned up the bundles of grain and the standing grain,
and also the olive groves.
[6] Then the Philistines asked, “Who has done this?”
People said, “Samson, the son-in-law of the man from Timnah.
For he took his wife away and gave her to his companion.”
So the Philistines went up
and burned her and her father with fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> The word for “foxes” can also mean jackals. Jackals live in packs and are easier to catch in large numbers.
> At harvest time the grain was dry. A fire destroyed the food for a whole year. This was a heavy blow for the Philistines.
> Then something terrible happens: The Philistines burn the woman and her father. This is exactly what they had threatened her with (Chapter 14:15). The woman had given away Samson’s secret so that she would not be burned. Now she is burned anyway. She is the innocent victim of the violence between the men.

---

## Revenge and more revenge (Verses 7–8)

[7] Samson said to them,
“If you act like this,
I will surely take revenge on you,
and after that I will stop.”
[8] He struck them hip and thigh, with a great slaughter.
And he went down and lived in the cave in the rock of Etam.

> **What does this mean?**
> “To strike hip and thigh” is an old saying. It means: He beat them completely.
> Here you can see how revenge always creates new revenge: Samson burns the fields, the Philistines burn the woman, Samson kills many Philistines, and the Philistines march against Judah. Samson says: “After that I will stop.” But the violence does not stop.
> Here the Bible shows honestly where revenge leads. Later the Bible teaches another way: You shall not take revenge (Leviticus 19:18), and Jesus says: Love your enemies (Matthew 5:44).

---

## The men of Judah hand Samson over (Verses 9–13)

[9] Then the Philistines went up,
camped in Judah, and spread out in Lehi.
[10] The men of Judah asked, “Why have you come up against us?”
They said,
“We have come up to tie up Samson.
We want to do to him as he has done to us.”
[11] Then three thousand men of Judah went down to the cave in the rock of Etam.
They said to Samson,
“Don’t you know that the Philistines rule over us?
What is this that you have done to us?”
He said to them,
“As they did to me, so I have done to them.”
[12] They said to him,
“We have come down to tie you up
and hand you over to the Philistines.”
Samson said to them,
“Swear to me that you will not attack me yourselves.”
[13] They said to him,
“No, we will only tie you up tightly and hand you over to them.
But we will surely not kill you.”
They tied him up with two new ropes
and brought him up from the rock.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a sad scene: The men of Judah do not fight against the oppressors. They have accepted the rule of the Philistines. They would rather hand over their own countryman than get into trouble. Three thousand men come to tie up one single man.
> Earlier, Judah had been the first tribe to fight against the Canaanites (Chapter 1). Now Judah only wants peace and quiet.
> Samson does not fight back against his own people. He lets himself be tied up.
> In Hebrew, “Lehi” means “jawbone.” This fits what comes next.

---

## The donkey’s jawbone (Verses 14–17)

[14] When he came to Lehi, the Philistines came shouting to meet him.
Then the Spirit of the LORD came on him with power.
The ropes on his arms became like flax that has been burned in the fire.
And the ropes fell off his hands.
[15] He found a fresh jawbone of a donkey.
He reached out his hand, took it, and struck down a thousand men with it.
[16] Samson said,
“With the jawbone of a donkey, heaps on heaps.
With the jawbone of a donkey I have struck a thousand men.”
[17] When he had finished speaking, he threw the jawbone out of his hand.
And that place was called Ramath Lehi.

> **What does this mean?**
> Flax is a plant from which linen thread is made. When it burns, it falls apart easily.
> A “fresh” jawbone was not yet dried out and brittle, but hard and heavy.
> Once again Samson touches something dead. For a Nazirite this is actually forbidden.
> Samson’s saying is a play on words: In Hebrew, “donkey” and “heap” sound the same: “chamor.” It is like a little victory song.
> “Ramath Lehi” means “hill of the jawbone.”
> Samson says: “I have struck.” He only speaks about himself, not about God. But the Bible makes clear: The strength came from the Spirit of the LORD.

---

## God gives water (Verses 18–20)

[18] He was very thirsty.
He called on the LORD and said,
“You have given this great rescue through the hand of your servant.
And now shall I die of thirst
and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?”
[19] But God split open the hollow place that is in Lehi,
and water came out of it.
When he had drunk, his strength came back, and he came back to life.
That is why the spring was named En Hakkore.
It is in Lehi to this day.
[20] He judged Israel for twenty years, in the days of the Philistines.

> **What does this mean?**
> For the first time, Samson prays. He realizes: Without God he is only a weak human being who can die of thirst. The strong hero needs help.
> God answers right away. He lets water come out of the ground, just as he did for Israel in the wilderness long ago.
> “En Hakkore” means “spring of the one who called.” The name reminds us: Samson called to God, and God answered.
> Verse 20 says that Samson judged Israel for twenty years. He did not drive out the Philistines. They stayed in power. But he was a constant danger for them. This sentence stands here like an ending, and it is repeated once more at the end of Chapter 16.
