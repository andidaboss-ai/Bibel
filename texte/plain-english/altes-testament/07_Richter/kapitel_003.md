# Judges – Chapter 3
### The first judges: Othniel, Ehud, and Shamgar

---

## The nations that stayed in the land (Verses 1–6)

[1] These are the nations that the LORD left, to test Israel by them,
all those who had not been through any of the wars of Canaan.
[2] It was only so that the generations of the people of Israel would learn,
so that they would be taught war, at least those who had not known it before:
[3] the five lords of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians,
and the Hivites who lived on Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal Hermon to the entrance of Hamath.
[4] They were left to test Israel by them,
to find out whether they would listen to the commandments of the LORD,
which he had commanded their fathers through Moses.
[5] So the people of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites,
the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.
[6] They took their daughters as wives
and gave their own daughters to their sons
and served their gods.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here another reason is given why nations stayed in the land: The new generation was to learn to defend itself and to trust God while doing so.
> Verse 6 shows what Joshua had feared: Through the marriages, Israel took on the foreign gods. It was not about where people came from, but about faith.

---

## Othniel, the first judge (Verses 7–11)

[7] The people of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asheroth.
[8] So the anger of the LORD burned against Israel,
and he sold them into the hand of Cushan Rishathaim, the king of Mesopotamia.
And the people of Israel served Cushan Rishathaim for eight years.
[9] Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD.
And the LORD raised up a savior for the people of Israel, who saved them:
Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother.
[10] The Spirit of the LORD came on him, and he judged Israel.
He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan Rishathaim, the king of Mesopotamia, into his hand.
His hand grew strong against Cushan Rishathaim.
[11] And the land had rest for forty years.
Then Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here, for the first time, we see the whole cycle from Chapter 2: falling away, oppression, crying out, rescue, rest.
> “Cushan Rishathaim” means something like “Cushan, the doubly wicked.” It sounds like a mocking name.
> We already know Othniel: He took Debir and married Achsah (Chapter 1). He comes from the family of Caleb, the faithful spy.
> “The Spirit of the LORD came on him”: The judges were not strong by their own power. God’s Spirit gave them courage and strength for their task.
> In the Bible, “forty years” often means a whole generation, a long time.

## Ehud and King Eglon (Verses 12–26)

[12] The people of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
So the LORD made Eglon, the king of Moab, strong against Israel,
because they had done what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
[13] He gathered the people of Ammon and Amalek to himself.
He went and struck Israel, and they took the city of palm trees.
[14] The people of Israel served Eglon, the king of Moab, for eighteen years.
[15] Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD.
And the LORD raised up a savior for them:
Ehud the son of Gera, the Benjamite, a left-handed man.
The people of Israel sent tribute through him to Eglon, the king of Moab.
[16] Ehud made himself a sword that had two edges and was about 18 inches long.
He wore it under his clothing on his right thigh.
[17] He brought the tribute to Eglon, the king of Moab.
Now Eglon was a very fat man.
[18] When Ehud had finished bringing the tribute,
he sent away the people who had carried the tribute.
[19] But he himself turned back at the stone idols that were near Gilgal and said,
“I have a secret message for you, king.”
The king said, “Quiet!”
And all who stood by him left him.
[20] Ehud came to him.
He was sitting all alone in his cool upper room.
Ehud said, “I have a message from God for you.”
Then he got up from his seat.
[21] Ehud reached out his left hand,
took the sword from his right thigh,
and thrust it into his belly.
[22] The handle also went in after the blade,
and the fat closed over the blade.
For he did not pull the sword out of his belly.
And it came out at the back.
[23] Then Ehud went out onto the porch,
shut the doors of the upper room behind him, and locked them.
[24] After he had gone out, the king’s servants came.
They saw that the doors of the upper room were locked.
They said, “Surely he is covering his feet in the upper room.”
[25] They waited until they were ashamed.
But he did not open the doors of the upper room.
So they took the key and opened them.
And there was their lord, lying dead on the floor.
[26] But Ehud had escaped while they waited.
He had gone past the stone idols and escaped to Seirah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says: a cubit long. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> Moab, Ammon, and Amalek attack together. They take Jericho, the “city of palm trees.” For eighteen years, Israel has to pay tribute, which means payments to the foreign king.
> Ehud was left-handed. This is important for the story: The guards probably looked for a sword on the left side, where right-handed men wear it. That is why they did not find it. By the way, the name “Benjamin” means “son of the right hand.” A left-handed man from the tribe of the “right hand” is a little joke in the story.
> The story is told very vividly, almost like a crime story, and with rough humor: The king is so fat that the fat swallows the sword. The servants think he is sitting on the toilet. “Covering his feet” is a polite way of saying this. That is why they wait so long, “until they were ashamed.”
> Ehud uses a trick and kills a king. Today we would call this an assassination. The Bible tells it as freedom from oppression. But it is not a call to murder rulers.

## Moab is defeated (Verses 27–30)

[27] When he got there, he blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim.
Then the people of Israel went down with him from the hill country, and he led them.
[28] He said to them,
“Follow me! For the LORD has given your enemies, the Moabites, into your hand.”
They followed him and took the fords of the Jordan against the Moabites.
And they did not let anyone cross over.
[29] At that time they struck about ten thousand men of Moab, all strong and brave men.
No one escaped.
[30] So that day Moab was brought down under the hand of Israel.
And the land had rest for eighty years.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Moabites wanted to flee across the Jordan to their home. But Israel took the fords.
> Fords are the shallow places where you can walk across a river.
> Eighty years of rest: That is two generations, the longest time of peace in the whole book.

---

## Shamgar (Verse 31)

[31] After him came Shamgar the son of Anath.
He struck six hundred men of the Philistines with an ox goad.
He also saved Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only a single sentence is told about Shamgar. An ox goad was a long stick with a metal point. It was used to drive cattle while plowing. Shamgar had no real weapon, only a farm tool. Still, he saved Israel.
> “Anath” was the name of a Canaanite goddess. Maybe Shamgar was not an Israelite at all. Still, God used him.
> Shamgar is also mentioned in the song of Deborah (Chapter 5:6).
