# Judges – Chapter 20
### The war against Benjamin

---

## Israel gathers at Mizpah (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then all the people of Israel went out.
The congregation gathered together as one man,
from Dan to Beersheba, with the land of Gilead,
to the LORD at Mizpah.
[2] The leaders of all the people, of all the tribes of Israel,
took their places in the assembly of the people of God:
400,000 foot soldiers who fought with the sword.
[3] (The people of Benjamin heard that the people of Israel had gone up to Mizpah.)
The people of Israel said,
“Tell us, how did this evil thing happen?”
[4] The Levite, the husband of the woman who had been murdered, answered,
“I came to Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin, I and my concubine,
to spend the night there.
[5] The men of Gibeah rose up against me
and surrounded the house at night.
They intended to kill me.
And they raped my concubine, and she is dead.
[6] So I took my concubine, cut her into pieces,
and sent her throughout the whole land of Israel’s inheritance.
For they have done a shameful and foolish thing in Israel.
[7] Now, you people of Israel, all of you:
Give your advice and your opinion here.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “From Dan to Beersheba” means: from the whole land, from the far north to the far south. For the first time in the book of Judges, all of Israel acts together. But not against a foreign enemy, but against one of its own tribes.
> Mizpah was in the territory of Benjamin, north of Jerusalem.
> 400,000 men is a very large number. Some Bible teachers think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” could also mean “troop” or “unit” here. Then there would have been far fewer men.
> The Levite tells the story in a way that makes him look good. He does not say that he himself pushed his wife outside. He says: “They intended to kill me,” but in Chapter 19 the men wanted to rape him. He keeps quiet about his own guilt.

## The Benjamites do not hand over the criminals (Verses 8–17)

[8] All the people stood up as one man and said,
“None of us will go to his tent,
and none of us will go back to his house.
[9] But this is what we will do to Gibeah now:
We will go up against it as the lot decides.
[10] From all the tribes of Israel we will take 10 men out of 100,
100 out of 1,000, and 1,000 out of 10,000,
to get food for the people.
Then, when they come to Gibeah in Benjamin,
they will do what is right for all the shameful things the men of Gibeah have done in Israel.”
[11] So all the men of Israel gathered against the city,
joined together as one man.

[12] The tribes of Israel sent men through the whole tribe of Benjamin, saying,
“What is this evil thing that has happened among you?
[13] Now hand over the men, the wicked men who are in Gibeah.
Then we will put them to death and remove the evil from Israel.”
But the people of Benjamin would not listen to the voice of their brothers, the people of Israel.
[14] The people of Benjamin gathered from the cities to Gibeah,
to go out to battle against the people of Israel.
[15] On that day the people of Benjamin were counted:
from the cities, 26,000 men who fought with the sword,
besides the people of Gibeah. Of them, 700 chosen men were counted.
[16] Among all these soldiers there were 700 chosen men who were left-handed.
Each one of them could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.
[17] The men of Israel, not counting Benjamin, were counted:
400,000 men who fought with the sword.
All of them were men of war.

> **What does this mean?**
> At first Israel acts rightly: It only demands that the criminals be handed over. Only the guilty are to be punished, not a whole tribe.
> But Benjamin refuses. The tribe sides with the criminals only because they belong to their own family. In this way, the whole tribe shares in the guilt. This is a warning: Whoever covers for criminals because they are “our people” becomes guilty too.
> The left-handed men remind us of Ehud, the judge from the tribe of Benjamin, who was also left-handed (Chapter 3). The sling was a dangerous weapon. Well-trained slingers could hit things very exactly.
> In verse 15, some old manuscripts of the Greek translation have 25,000 instead of 26,000.

## Two defeats for Israel (Verses 18–28)

[18] The people of Israel got up, went up to Bethel, and asked God for advice.
They asked,
“Which of us shall go up first to battle against the people of Benjamin?”
The LORD said, “Judah first.”
[19] The people of Israel got up in the morning and camped across from Gibeah.
[20] The men of Israel went out to battle against Benjamin.
The men of Israel lined up for battle against them at Gibeah.
[21] The people of Benjamin came out of Gibeah
and on that day struck down 22,000 men of Israel.
[22] The people, the men of Israel, took courage
and lined up for battle again in the same place where they had lined up on the first day.
[23] The people of Israel went up and wept in front of the LORD until evening.
They asked the LORD,
“Shall I go into battle again against my brother Benjamin?”
The LORD said, “Go up against him.”
[24] On the second day the people of Israel came near to the people of Benjamin.
[25] On the second day Benjamin went out against them from Gibeah
and struck down another 18,000 men of the people of Israel.
All of them fought with the sword.

[26] Then all the people of Israel and all the people went up and came to Bethel.
They wept and sat there in front of the LORD.
They fasted that day until evening.
Then they offered burnt offerings and peace offerings in front of the LORD.
[27] The people of Israel asked the LORD
– for the ark of the covenant of God was there in those days,
[28] and Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron, served before it in those days –
and they asked,
“Shall I go out again to battle against my brother Benjamin,
or shall I stop?”
The LORD said,
“Go up. For tomorrow I will give him into your hand.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The question in verse 18 reminds us of the beginning of the book. There, Israel asked: Who should fight first against the Canaanites? And God said: Judah (Judges 1:1–2). Now Judah does not fight against foreign enemies, but against its brother tribe. The book ends where it began, but everything is turned upside down.
> Israel first asks God who should fight first, not whether they should fight at all. They have already made up their minds.
> Twice Israel loses, even though God says: Go up. This is hard to understand. Many Bible teachers think: In this way God was also making Israel humble. Only the third time do they weep, fast, and offer sacrifices. Only now do they ask: Or shall I stop? And only now does God promise victory.
> At that time, the ark of the covenant was in Bethel. Phinehas, Aaron’s grandson, was priest. He was already alive in the time of Moses (Numbers 25). This shows: This story takes place at the beginning of the time of the judges, not at the end, even though it is told last in the book.
> Losses in verses 21 and 25: 22,000 and 18,000 men, together 40,000.

## The ambush (Verses 29–36)

[29] Israel set men in ambush all around Gibeah.
[30] On the third day the people of Israel went up against the people of Benjamin
and lined up against Gibeah as at the other times.
[31] The people of Benjamin went out against the people and were drawn away from the city.
They began to strike down and kill some of the people as at the other times,
on the highways, one of which goes up to Bethel and the other to Gibeah,
in the open field, about 30 men of Israel.
[32] The people of Benjamin said,
“They are being struck down in front of us, as at the first.”
But the people of Israel said,
“Let’s run away and draw them away from the city onto the highways.”
[33] All the men of Israel got up from their place
and lined up at Baal Tamar.
And the men of Israel who were lying in ambush broke out of their hiding place,
out of Maareh Geba.
[34] 10,000 chosen men from all Israel came against Gibeah.
The battle was hard.
But the Benjamites did not know that disaster was close to them.
[35] The LORD struck Benjamin in front of Israel.
And on that day the people of Israel killed 25,100 men of Benjamin.
All of them fought with the sword.
[36] So the people of Benjamin saw that they were beaten.
For the men of Israel had given ground to Benjamin,
because they were relying on the men in ambush whom they had set against Gibeah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel uses the same trick that Joshua used at the city of Ai (Joshua 8): Part of the army pretends to run away. The defenders chase it and leave their city without protection. Then the men from the hiding place attack the city.
> Verse 35 sums up the end of the battle ahead of time. From verse 36 on, it is told in more detail how it came to that.
> “The LORD struck Benjamin”: The Bible says that here God is judging a tribe that covered up a serious crime.

## Gibeah burns (Verses 37–48)

[37] The men in ambush hurried and rushed at Gibeah.
The men in ambush spread out
and struck the whole city with the edge of the sword.
[38] The men of Israel and the men in ambush had agreed on a sign:
They were to make a great cloud of smoke rise up out of the city.
[39] The men of Israel turned around in the battle.
And Benjamin began to strike down and kill about 30 of the men of Israel.
For they said,
“They are surely being struck down in front of us, as in the first battle.”
[40] But when the cloud began to rise out of the city like a pillar of smoke,
the Benjamites looked behind them.
And the whole city was going up in smoke to the sky.
[41] The men of Israel turned around,
and the men of Benjamin were terrified.
For they saw that disaster had come on them.
[42] So they turned their backs to the men of Israel
and fled toward the wilderness.
But the battle stayed close behind them.
And those who came out of the cities killed them in the middle of it.
[43] They surrounded the Benjamites, chased them,
and trampled them down at their resting place,
as far as near Gibeah, toward the east.
[44] 18,000 men of Benjamin fell.
All of them were brave men.
[45] They turned and fled toward the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon.
On the highways the Israelites gleaned 5,000 men from them.
They chased them as far as Gidom and killed 2,000 more men of them.
[46] So all of Benjamin who fell that day were 25,000 men
who fought with the sword.
All of them were brave men.
[47] But 600 men turned and fled toward the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon.
They stayed at the rock of Rimmon for four months.
[48] The men of Israel turned against the people of Benjamin again
and struck them with the edge of the sword,
the whole city, the livestock, and everything they found.
They also set on fire all the cities they found.

> **What does this mean?**
> “To glean” is a picture from the harvest: You gather up what is still left. Here it is a cruel picture for killing those who were running away.
> The numbers: 18,000 + 5,000 + 2,000 = 25,000. Verse 35 is more exact: 25,100. Verse 46 gives a round number.
> According to verse 15, Benjamin had 26,700 men. If 25,100 fall and 600 survive, about 1,000 are still missing. Maybe they had already fallen in the first two battles. The Bible does not say.
> What happens in verse 48 is horrible: Israel kills not only the soldiers, but also women, children, and animals in the cities of Benjamin. A just punishment for a crime turns into revenge without limits. A whole tribe is almost wiped out.
> The Bible does not tell this as an example to follow. In these chapters, the book of Judges shows step by step: This is where it leads when everyone does what is right in his own eyes. No one may use this story to justify violence against a whole group.
