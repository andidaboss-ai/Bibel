# Proverbs – Chapter 7
### The young man and the seductive woman

---

## Call wisdom your sister (Verses 1–5)

[1] My son, hold on to my words.
Keep my commandments safe inside you.
[2] Keep my commandments, and you will live.
Guard my teaching as carefully as the pupil of your eye.
[3] Tie them on your fingers.
Write them on your heart, as if it were a tablet.
[4] Say to wisdom, “You are my sister.”
Call understanding your close relative.
[5] Then they will protect you from the woman who is not your wife,
from the foreign woman with her flattering words.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “As carefully as the pupil of your eye”: The pupil is the dark center of the eye. You protect it very carefully.
> Verse 3: “Tie them on your fingers”: In Judaism, people tie small boxes with Bible words to their arm and head when they pray (tefillin). This recalls verses like these (compare Deuteronomy 6:8).
> Verse 4: Wisdom should be as close to you as a sister.

## A story at the window (Verses 6–23)

[6] Once I was at the window of my house.
I looked out through the lattice.
[7] I saw some young people who had no experience.
Among them I noticed a young man who had no sense.
[8] He walked along the street near her corner.
He went on the road to her house.
[9] It was at twilight, in the evening.
It was in the middle of the night, in the dark.
[10] Look, a woman came to meet him.
She was dressed like a prostitute, and she had a sly plan.
[11] She is loud and rebellious.
She never stays at home.
[12] Sometimes she is in the streets, sometimes in the squares.
She lies in wait at every corner.
[13] She grabbed him and kissed him.
Without any shame she said to him:
[14] “I have peace offerings at home.
Today I have paid what I promised to God.
[15] That is why I came out to meet you.
I was eager to find you, and now I have found you.
[16] I have spread colorful covers on my couch,
striped sheets made of yarn from Egypt.
[17] I have sprinkled my bed
with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.
[18] Come, let’s make love until the morning.
Let’s enjoy each other’s love.
[19] My husband is not at home.
He has gone on a long trip.
[20] He took a bag of money with him.
He will not come home until the full moon.”
[21] She talked him into it with her persuasive words.
She seduced him with her flattering lips.
[22] He followed her right away,
like an ox that goes to be slaughtered,
like a fool who steps into a noose.
[23] In the end an arrow pierces his liver.
He is like a bird that rushes into a trap.
It does not know that it will cost its life.

> **What does this mean?**
> The father tells a story that he watched from the window. In the evening, a young man without experience goes on purpose to the area where the woman lives. He is looking for temptation.
> Verse 14: With a peace offering, people got meat back, which had to be eaten at home. So the woman is saying: I have a good meal at home. She even uses religion for her seduction.
> Verse 17: Myrrh, aloes and cinnamon are sweet-smelling spices.
> Verses 19–20: Her husband is away, no one will notice. Temptation often sounds like this: No one will find out.
> Verses 22–23: The young man does not notice that he is walking into a trap, like an animal going to the butcher.
> The story warns against unfaithfulness and against thinking “just this once.”

## Her house is the way to death (Verses 24–27)

[24] So now, my sons, listen to me.
Pay attention to what I say.
[25] Don’t let your heart turn toward her ways.
Don’t wander onto her paths.
[26] She has brought down many victims.
She has killed a huge number of people.
[27] Her house is the road to Sheol (the world of the dead).
Her house leads down to the rooms of death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unfaithfulness has already destroyed many lives: marriages, families, children, trust.
> The father’s advice: Don’t even let your heart go in this direction. The decision is made in the heart, long before anything happens (compare Proverbs 4:23).
