# Proverbs – Chapter 7
### The young man and the seductive woman

---

## Call wisdom your sister (Verses 1–5)

[1] My son, keep my words!
Store up my commandments within you!
[2] Keep my commandments, and you will live!
Guard my teaching like the apple of your eye!
[3] Tie them on your fingers!
Write them on the tablet of your heart!
[4] Say to wisdom, “You are my sister!”
Call understanding your relative,
[5] so that they keep you from the strange woman,
from the foreign woman who flatters with her words.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “Like the apple of your eye”: as carefully as you protect your eye.
> Verse 3: “Tie them on your fingers”: In Judaism, people tie small boxes with Bible words to their arm and head when they pray (tefillin). This recalls verses like these (compare Deuteronomy 6:8).
> Verse 4: Wisdom should be as close to you as a sister.

---

## A story at the window (Verses 6–23)

[6] For at the window of my house,
I looked out through my lattice.
[7] I saw among the simple ones,
I noticed among the young people
a young man without sense.
[8] He was walking along the street near her corner.
He took the way to her house,
[9] in the twilight, in the evening of the day,
in the middle of the night and in the darkness.
[10] Look, a woman came to meet him,
dressed like a prostitute and with sly plans.
[11] She is loud and stubborn.
Her feet don’t stay in her house.
[12] Now she is in the streets, now in the squares,
and she lies in wait at every corner.
[13] She grabbed him and kissed him.
With a bold face she said to him:
[14] “I had peace offerings to bring.
Today I have kept my vows.
[15] So I came out to meet you,
to look eagerly for your face, and I have found you.
[16] I have spread my couch with covers of tapestry,
with striped cloths of yarn from Egypt.
[17] I have perfumed my bed
with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.
[18] Come, let’s take our fill of love until the morning!
Let’s enjoy ourselves with love!
[19] For my husband isn’t at home.
He has gone on a long journey.
[20] He has taken a bag of money with him.
He will come home at the full moon.”
[21] With persuasive words she led him astray.
With the flattery of her lips she seduced him.
[22] He followed her at once,
as an ox goes to the slaughter,
as a fool steps into a noose,
[23] until an arrow strikes through his liver,
like a bird that hurries into the trap
and does not know that it will cost its life.

> **What does this mean?**
> The father tells a story that he watched from the window. A young man without experience goes into the area where the woman lives in the evening on purpose. He is looking for temptation.
> Verse 14: With a peace offering, people got meat back, which had to be eaten at home. So the woman is saying: I have a good meal at home. She even uses religion for her seduction.
> Verses 19–20: Her husband is away, no one will notice. Temptation often sounds like this: No one will find out.
> Verses 22–23: The young man does not notice that he is walking into a trap, like an animal going to the butcher.
> The story warns against unfaithfulness and against thinking “just this once.”

---

## Her house is the way to death (Verses 24–27)

[24] So now, sons, listen to me!
Pay attention to the words of my mouth!
[25] Don’t let your heart turn to her ways.
Don’t go astray on her paths,
[26] for she has brought down many wounded.
Yes, all those she has killed are a mighty army.
[27] Her house is the way to Sheol, the world of the dead,
going down to the rooms of death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unfaithfulness has already destroyed many lives: marriages, families, children, trust.
> The father’s advice: Don’t even let your heart go in this direction. The decision is made in the heart, long before anything happens (compare Proverbs 4:23).
