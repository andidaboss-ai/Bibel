# Proverbs – Chapter 13
### Hope and fulfillment

---

## Guard your mouth (Verses 1–6)

[1] A wise son listens to his father’s instruction,
but a mocker does not listen to correction.
[2] By the fruit of his lips a person enjoys good things,
but the unfaithful crave violence.
[3] Whoever guards his mouth guards his life.
Whoever opens his lips wide comes to ruin.
[4] The soul of the lazy person wants things and gets nothing,
but the wish of hard workers will be fully satisfied.
[5] A righteous person hates lies,
but a wicked person brings shame and disgrace.
[6] Righteousness guards the way of honesty,
but wickedness overthrows the sinner.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Whoever is careful about what he says protects himself. Whoever blurts everything out gets into trouble. This is also true for what we write on the internet.
> Verse 4: The lazy person wishes for a lot, but he does nothing for it. Wishing alone is not enough.

---

## Wealth and poverty (Verses 7–11)

[7] Some people pretend to be rich and yet have nothing.
Some people pretend to be poor and yet have great wealth.
[8] The ransom for a man’s life is his riches,
but the poor person hears no threats.
[9] The light of the righteous shines brightly,
but the lamp of the wicked goes out.
[10] Pride only produces quarrels,
but wisdom is with those who take advice.
[11] Wealth gained dishonestly dwindles away,
but whoever gathers it by his own hand makes it grow.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Appearances can fool you. Some show off even though they have nothing. Others are modest even though they have a lot.
> Verse 8: A surprising thought: Rich people can be blackmailed or kidnapped and then have to pay a ransom. No one threatens the poor person for that reason. Wealth brings worries too.
> Verse 11: Quick, dishonest money disappears quickly. What is earned honestly and slowly grows.

---

## Hope that is put off (Verses 12–19)

[12] Hope that is put off makes the heart sick,
but when a longing is fulfilled, it is a tree of life.
[13] Whoever despises instruction will pay for it,
but whoever respects a command will be rewarded.
[14] The teaching of the wise is a spring of life,
to turn away from the traps of death.
[15] Good understanding wins favor,
but the way of the unfaithful is hard.
[16] Every wise person acts with knowledge,
but a fool shows off his foolishness.
[17] A wicked messenger falls into trouble,
but a trustworthy messenger brings healing.
[18] Poverty and shame come to the one who refuses instruction,
but whoever pays attention to correction will be honored.
[19] A longing that is fulfilled is sweet to the soul,
but fools hate to turn away from evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 is a very human sentence. Whoever waits and hopes for something for a long time becomes tired and sad: for healing, for a partner, for a child, for work, for peace. The Bible understands this pain. And when the longing is finally fulfilled, it is like new life.
> If disappointed hopes leave you not knowing how to go on: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 17: A good messenger brings healing, because he passes on messages faithfully and makes peace.

---

## Good friends and a good inheritance (Verses 20–25)

[20] Whoever walks with wise people becomes wise,
but whoever makes friends with fools will be harmed.
[21] Misfortune chases sinners,
but good things reward the righteous.
[22] A good person leaves an inheritance to his grandchildren,
but the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous.
[23] Plenty of food grows in the fields of the poor,
but injustice sweeps it away.
[24] Whoever spares the rod hates his son,
but whoever loves him is careful to discipline him.
[25] The righteous person eats until his soul is satisfied,
but the belly of the wicked goes hungry.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: Tell me who your friends are, and I will tell you who you are. Friends shape us.
> Verse 22: A good inheritance is not only money, but also a good example, values and faith.
> Verse 23: An important sentence about justice: Poverty often does not come from laziness, but from injustice. The poor work and harvest, but others take it away from them. That still happens today.
> Verse 24: For a long time, this verse was used to justify hitting children. Back then, that was normal. The message of the verse is: Love shows itself when parents bring up their children and set limits for them, instead of letting everything go. Today we know: Hitting harms children, and in many countries, including Germany, it is against the law. Good parenting works without violence.
> If you experience violence in your own family: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673.
