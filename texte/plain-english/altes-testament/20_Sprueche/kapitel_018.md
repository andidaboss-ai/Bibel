# Proverbs – Chapter 18
### The name of the LORD is a strong tower

---

## First listen, then speak (Verses 1–9)

[1] Whoever cuts himself off from others is looking only for himself
and fights against all good judgment.
[2] A fool has no joy in understanding,
but only in telling his own opinion.
[3] When wickedness comes, contempt comes too,
and with shame comes disgrace.
[4] The words of a person’s mouth are like deep waters.
The spring of wisdom is like a flowing stream.
[5] It is not good to favor the wicked
and to take away justice from the innocent.
[6] A fool’s lips get into quarrels,
and his mouth asks for a beating.
[7] A fool’s mouth is his ruin,
and his lips are a trap for his life.
[8] The words of a gossip are like tasty treats.
They go down into a person’s innermost being.
[9] Whoever is careless in his work
is a brother of the one who destroys.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Whoever withdraws from everyone in order to think only of himself loses sight of what is right. We need other people.
> But whoever withdraws because of sadness or fear is not selfish, but needs help and care.
> Verse 2: A fool does not want to understand, but only to say his opinion. We know this from many discussions on the internet too.
> Verse 8: Gossip tastes like a treat. People like to hear it, and it sticks. But it poisons the heart.
> Verse 9: Whoever works sloppily causes just as much damage in the end as someone who destroys on purpose.

## The strong tower (Verses 10–15)

[10] The name of the LORD is a strong tower.
The righteous person runs to him and is safe.
[11] The rich man’s wealth is his strong city,
like a wall no one can climb, in his own imagination.
[12] Before destruction a person’s heart is proud,
but before honor comes humility.
[13] Whoever answers before he has listened,
that is foolishness and shame for him.
[14] A person’s spirit will hold him up in sickness,
but a crushed spirit, who can bear it?
[15] The heart of the person with understanding gets knowledge.
The ear of the wise looks for knowledge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: A comparison: The righteous person finds protection with God, as in a strong tower. The rich man believes that his money is a safe wall. But that is only his imagination.
> Verse 13: Whoever interrupts someone and answers before he has really listened makes himself look foolish. First listen, then speak!
> Verse 14: A strong will to live helps you bear sickness. But when the soul is crushed, that is harder than any sickness. Distress of the soul is serious and needs help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Hearing both sides (Verses 16–21)

[16] A person’s gift makes room for him
and brings him before important people.
[17] Whoever presents his case first seems right,
until another comes and questions him.
[18] The lot settles disputes
and keeps strong opponents apart.
[19] A brother who has been hurt is harder to win than a fortified city.
Disputes are like the bars of a fortress.
[20] A person’s stomach is filled with the fruit of his mouth.
He is satisfied with the harvest of his lips.
[21] Death and life are in the power of the tongue.
Those who love it will eat its fruit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: A gift can open doors. This is an observation, not advice to bribe.
> Verse 17: A very wise sentence: Whoever hears only one side believes it at once. But you must always hear both sides before you judge. This is true in court, in a quarrel between friends and with the news.
> Verse 19: A hurt brother is harder to win back than a fortress. Quarrels in the family go deep. So: Don’t hurt others carelessly!
> Verse 21: Words have power over life and death. With words you can build up or destroy, encourage or crush. This is especially true for bullying.

## A friend who sticks closer than a brother (Verses 22–24)

[22] Whoever finds a wife finds a good thing
and receives favor from the LORD.
[23] The poor beg for mercy,
but the rich answer harshly.
[24] A man with many companions may be ruined,
but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: A good marriage is a gift from God.
> Verse 23: A sad observation: The poor have to beg, and the rich often answer harshly. It should not be like this.
> Verse 24: Having many people you know does not mean having real friends. One single faithful friend is worth more. Christians see in Jesus this friend “who sticks closer than a brother.” Jesus calls his disciples his friends (compare John 15:13–15).
