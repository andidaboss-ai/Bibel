# Proverbs – Chapter 22
### A good name is better than riches

---

## God made the rich and the poor (Verses 1–9)

[1] A good name is more desirable than great riches,
and favor is better than silver and gold.
[2] The rich and the poor have this in common:
The LORD made them all.
[3] A wise person sees danger and hides,
but the simple go on and suffer for it.
[4] The reward of humility and of the fear of the LORD
is wealth, honor, and life.
[5] Thorns and traps are on the path of the wicked.
Whoever guards his life stays far away from them.
[6] Train up a child in the way he should go,
and when he is old he will not turn away from it.
[7] The rich rule over the poor.
The borrower is servant to the lender.
[8] Whoever sows wickedness reaps trouble,
and the rod of his anger will be broken.
[9] Whoever has a generous eye will be blessed,
for he shares his food with the poor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A good reputation and the trust of others are worth more than money.
> Verse 2: An important sentence: Before God, rich and poor are equal. He created both. No one is worth more than the other.
> Verse 3: A wise person sees dangers early and gets out of the way.
> Verse 6 is a well-known saying about bringing up children: What children learn early shapes them for their whole life. That is why good parenting is so important. But it is no guarantee: Children who were brought up well can also go their own way. Parents are not to blame for everything.
> Verse 7: Debts make you dependent. Whoever borrows money is no longer free. Good advice: Be careful with loans.
> Verse 9: “A generous eye”: Whoever sees the need of others and shares will be blessed.

## The lazy person and the lion (Verses 10–16)

[10] Drive out the mocker, and the quarrel will go out.
Yes, arguments and insults will stop.
[11] Whoever loves a pure heart and speaks kindly
is the king’s friend.
[12] The LORD’s eyes watch over knowledge,
but he ruins the words of the unfaithful.
[13] The lazy person says, “There is a lion outside!
I will be killed in the streets!”
[14] The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit.
Whoever is under the LORD’s anger will fall into it.
[15] Foolishness is tied up in the heart of a child.
The rod of discipline drives it far from him.
[16] Whoever oppresses the poor to make himself richer,
and whoever gives to the rich, both end up in poverty.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: A funny proverb: The lazy person makes up the craziest excuses so that he does not have to go to work: “There is a lion outside!”
> Verse 15: Children have to learn many things first. Back then, people believed that this included hitting. Today we know: Hitting harms children, and in many countries, including Germany, it is against the law. Children need love, clear rules and patience. If you need help with parenting: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673.
> Verse 16: Whoever takes advantage of the poor, or flatters the rich in order to become rich himself, loses in the end.

## The words of the wise begin (Verses 17–21)

[17] Turn your ear and listen to the words of the wise!
Set your heart on my teaching!
[18] For it is a pleasant thing if you keep them within you,
if all of them are ready on your lips.
[19] I teach you today, yes, you,
so that your trust may rest in the LORD.
[20] Haven’t I written to you 30 excellent sayings,
full of advice and knowledge,
[21] to teach you truth, reliable words,
so that you can give good answers to those who sent you?

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a new part of the book begins: “The words of the wise” (22:17 to 24:22).
> Verse 20: “30 excellent sayings”: This collection has 30 sections. It is very similar to an old Egyptian book of wisdom, the “Instruction of Amenemope,” which also has 30 chapters. So Israel also took over wisdom from neighboring peoples and connected it with faith in the LORD.
> Verse 19: The goal of all wisdom: that you trust in God.

## Don’t take advantage of the poor (Verses 22–29)

[22] Don’t take advantage of the poor because he is poor,
and don’t crush the needy in court!
[23] For the LORD will plead their case
and take the life of those who rob them.
[24] Don’t make friends with a hot-tempered person!
Don’t spend time with someone who carries anger inside him,
[25] so that you don’t learn his ways
and set a trap for your own life.
[26] Don’t be one of those who shake hands on a deal,
one of those who guarantee debts!
[27] If you have nothing to pay with,
why should your bed be taken away from under you?
[28] Don’t move the ancient boundary stone
that your ancestors set up!
[29] Do you see a person who is skilled in his work?
He will serve kings.
He will not serve unknown people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–23: Whoever takes advantage of the poor because they cannot defend themselves will have to deal with God. God himself is the lawyer of the poor.
> Verses 24–25: Anger is catching. Whoever spends a lot of time with hot-tempered people easily becomes like them.
> Verse 27: Whoever guarantees a debt and cannot pay loses even his bed.
> Verse 28: Boundary stones marked which land belonged to whom. Whoever secretly moved them stole land. This usually hurt the weak.
> Verse 29: Whoever does his work well is valued and goes far.
