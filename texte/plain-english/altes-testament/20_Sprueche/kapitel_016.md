# Proverbs – Chapter 16
### People make plans, God directs

---

## Commit your deeds to the LORD (Verses 1–9)

[1] The plans of the heart belong to people,
but the answer of the tongue comes from the LORD.
[2] All of a person’s ways are pure in his own eyes,
but the LORD weighs the motives.
[3] Commit your deeds to the LORD,
and your plans will succeed.
[4] The LORD has made everything for its own purpose,
yes, even the wicked for the day of disaster.
[5] Everyone who is proud in heart is disgusting to the LORD.
They will certainly not go unpunished.
[6] By kindness and truth guilt is atoned for.
By the fear of the LORD people turn away from evil.
[7] When a person’s ways please the LORD,
he makes even his enemies be at peace with him.
[8] Better a little with righteousness
than a big income with injustice.
[9] A person’s heart plans his way,
but the LORD directs his steps.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1 and 9 are the basis of a well-known saying: People make plans, but God directs. We may make plans. But in the end, God has the last word.
> Verse 2: We often think of ourselves as good. God looks deeper, at our real motives.
> Verse 3: “Commit your deeds to the LORD” means: Put everything you do into God’s hands.
> Verse 4: This verse is difficult. It does not mean that God made people evil. It says: Nothing is outside of God’s plan. In the end, evil too will not escape his judgment.
> Verse 6: Guilt is not taken away by sacrifices alone, but by kindness and truth, by God’s grace and by a changed life.

---

## The just king (Verses 10–15)

[10] Decisions inspired by God are on the lips of the king.
His mouth must not be unfaithful when he judges.
[11] Honest scales and balances belong to the LORD.
All the weights in the bag are his work.
[12] It is disgusting for kings to do wrong,
for the throne is made firm by righteousness.
[13] Righteous lips please kings.
They value the one who speaks the truth.
[14] The king’s anger is a messenger of death,
but a wise man will calm it.
[15] In the light of the king’s face is life.
His favor is like a cloud with spring rain.

> **What does this mean?**
> These proverbs are about the king, about those who have power. A good ruler should judge justly and value honest people.
> Verse 11: Honest measures and weights are God’s business too. Justice in trade matters to him.
> Verse 12: Power only lasts if it is just. To this day, that is a standard for every government.
> Verse 15: In the land of Israel, the spring rain was important for the harvest. A just ruler is just as vital.

---

## Pride comes before a fall (Verses 16–24)

[16] How much better it is to get wisdom than gold!
Yes, getting understanding is better than silver.
[17] The highway of the upright is to turn away from evil.
Whoever watches his way keeps his life safe.
[18] Pride goes before destruction,
and a proud spirit before a fall.
[19] It is better to be humble with the poor
than to share plunder with the proud.
[20] Whoever pays attention to the word finds good things.
Happy is the one who trusts in the LORD.
[21] Whoever is wise in heart will be called understanding.
Kind lips help teaching.
[22] Understanding is a spring of life to the one who has it,
but the punishment of fools is their foolishness.
[23] The heart of the wise teaches his mouth
and adds learning to his lips.
[24] Kind words are a honeycomb,
sweet to the soul and health to the bones.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: “Pride goes before destruction, and a proud spirit before a fall.” This is where the well-known saying comes from: Pride comes before a fall.
> Verse 19: Better to be humble among the poor than rich among the show-offs.
> Verse 21: People like to listen to someone who teaches kindly.
> Verse 24: Kind words are sweet as honey. They do the soul good, and even the body.

---

## Being patient is better than being a hero (Verses 25–33)

[25] There is a way that seems right to a person,
but in the end it leads to death.
[26] The hunger of the worker works for him,
for his mouth drives him on.
[27] A worthless person digs up trouble.
His speech is like a burning fire.
[28] A twisted person stirs up quarrels.
A gossip separates close friends.
[29] A violent person lures his neighbor
and leads him on a way that is not good.
[30] Whoever winks his eyes to plan twisted things,
whoever presses his lips together, is set on evil.
[31] Gray hair is a glorious crown.
It is gained by a life of righteousness.
[32] Whoever is slow to anger is better than a mighty hero,
whoever controls himself is better than someone who captures a city.
[33] The lot is thrown into the lap,
but every decision comes from the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25 repeats Proverbs 14:12. Some proverbs appear twice, because they are especially important.
> Verse 26: Hunger drives a person to work.
> Verse 28: Whoever spreads rumors and gossip can even split up best friends.
> Verse 31: Old people deserve respect. Their gray hair is like a crown.
> Verse 32: Real strength is self-control. Whoever has his anger under control is stronger than a war hero.
> Verse 33: Back then, some decisions were made by casting lots, a kind of throwing dice. The proverb says: Even what looks like chance is in God’s hand.
