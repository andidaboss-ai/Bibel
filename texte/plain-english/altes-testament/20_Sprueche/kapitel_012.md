# Proverbs – Chapter 12
### Whoever loves correction

---

## Learning and family (Verses 1–8)

[1] Whoever loves correction loves knowledge,
but whoever hates being corrected is stupid.
[2] A good person gets favor from the LORD,
but he condemns a person with wicked plans.
[3] A person is not made secure by wickedness,
but the root of the righteous will not be moved.
[4] A capable woman is the crown of her husband,
but a wife who brings shame is like rot in his bones.
[5] The thoughts of the righteous are just,
but the advice of the wicked is deceitful.
[6] The words of the wicked lie in wait for blood,
but the speech of the upright rescues them.
[7] The wicked are overthrown and are no more,
but the house of the righteous will stand.
[8] A person is praised for his wisdom,
but whoever has a twisted mind will be despised.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: An honest sentence: Whoever does not want to be criticized stays stupid. Whoever wants to learn from mistakes becomes wise.
> Verse 3: A tree with deep roots does not fall in a storm. It is the same with righteous people.
> Verse 4: A good wife is like a crown, a great treasure. A partner who brings shame makes life hard. This is true for both sides of a marriage.

---

## Hard work and kindness to animals (Verses 9–14)

[9] Better is someone who is little known and has a servant
than someone who thinks he is important and has no bread.
[10] A righteous person cares about the life of his animal,
but even the kindness of the wicked is cruel.
[11] Whoever works his land will have plenty of bread,
but whoever chases fantasies has no sense.
[12] The wicked person wants the plunder of evil men,
but the root of the righteous flourishes.
[13] An evil person is trapped by the sin of his lips,
but the righteous gets out of trouble.
[14] A person is filled with good things by the fruit of his mouth.
What a person’s hands do will be paid back to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Better to be modest and have enough than to show off and go hungry.
> Verse 10: A beautiful verse about protecting animals: A righteous person takes care of his animals. Whoever torments animals shows what is in his heart. The laws of Moses already protected animals (for example Deuteronomy 25:4).
> Verse 11: Whoever works hard has food to eat. Whoever only chases dreams and quick riches ends up with nothing.

---

## Words that heal (Verses 15–23)

[15] The way of a fool is right in his own eyes,
but whoever is wise listens to advice.
[16] A fool shows his anger the same day,
but whoever overlooks an insult is wise.
[17] Whoever is truthful gives honest testimony,
but a false witness lies.
[18] Some people speak without thinking, and it is like the stabbing of a sword,
but the tongue of the wise heals.
[19] Truthful lips will last forever,
but a lying tongue lasts only for a moment.
[20] Deceit is in the heart of those who plan evil,
but those who encourage peace have joy.
[21] No harm will happen to the righteous,
but the wicked will be filled with evil.
[22] Lying lips are disgusting to the LORD,
but those who do the truth please him.
[23] A wise person keeps his knowledge to himself,
but the heart of fools announces foolishness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: A fool always thinks he is right. A wise person asks others too.
> Verse 16: Whoever strikes back at once when he is insulted makes everything worse. Whoever stays calm is wise.
> Verse 18: Words can hurt like a sword. But words can also heal. Everyone has the choice.
> Verse 19: Lies do not get far. The truth lasts.
> Verse 20: Whoever makes peace has joy (compare Matthew 5:9: “Blessed are those who make peace”).
> Verse 21: This is no guarantee that good people never suffer. It means: In the end, evil will not defeat the righteous.

---

## A kind word (Verses 24–28)

[24] The hands of hard workers will rule,
but laziness ends in forced labor.
[25] Worry in a person’s heart weighs it down,
but a kind word makes it glad.
[26] A righteous person is careful in friendship,
but the way of the wicked leads them astray.
[27] The lazy person does not roast his game,
but the possessions of hard-working people are valuable.
[28] In the way of righteousness is life.
On its path there is no death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: A beautiful, simple sentence: Worries weigh us down. But a kind word can make a person glad again. Everyone can say a word like that today.
> Verse 26: Choose your friends carefully. Bad friends can lead you astray.
> Verse 27: A funny picture: The lazy person has hunted an animal, but he is too lazy to roast it. He never finishes anything.
> Verse 28: The way of righteousness leads to life. Some Jewish and Christian interpreters already see here a hope beyond death.
