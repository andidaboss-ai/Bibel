# Proverbs – Chapter 20
### Who can say: My heart is pure?

---

## Wine and quarrels (Verses 1–7)

[1] Wine is a mocker, and beer is a brawler.
Whoever is led astray by them is not wise.
[2] The terror of a king is like the roaring of a lion.
Whoever makes him angry puts his own life at risk.
[3] It is an honor for a person to stay away from quarrels,
but every fool starts a quarrel.
[4] The lazy person does not plow because of the winter.
So he begs at harvest time and has nothing.
[5] The plan in a person’s heart is like deep water,
but a man of understanding will draw it out.
[6] Many people claim to be full of unfailing love,
but who can find a faithful person?
[7] A righteous person lives with integrity.
Happy are his children after him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Alcohol turns people into mockers and fighters. Whoever lets alcohol control him is not wise. Whoever has a problem with alcohol can find help, for example at addiction counseling services or with Alcoholics Anonymous. US: SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357.
> Verse 3: It is not a weakness to avoid a quarrel. It is an honor.
> Verse 4: In Israel, people plowed in fall and winter. Whoever is too lazy then has no harvest in the summer.
> Verse 5: What a person really thinks is often hidden deep inside. A wise person can help bring it to light by asking good questions.
> Verse 6: Many talk about love and faithfulness. But truly faithful people are rare.

---

## No one is without sin (Verses 8–15)

[8] A king who sits on the judgment seat
scatters away all evil with his eyes.
[9] Who can say, “I have made my heart pure.
I am clean and without sin”?
[10] Two different weights and two different measures,
both of them are disgusting to the LORD.
[11] Even a child shows by his actions
whether what he does is pure and whether it is right.
[12] The hearing ear and the seeing eye,
the LORD has made both of them.
[13] Don’t love sleep, or you will become poor.
Open your eyes, and you will have plenty of bread.
[14] “It’s no good, it’s no good!” says the buyer.
But when he has gone on his way, he boasts.
[15] There is gold and plenty of rubies,
but lips full of knowledge are a rare jewel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: An honest question: No one can say about himself that he is without sin (compare 1 John 1:8). Everyone needs God’s forgiveness.
> Verse 10: Two different measures: buying with one weight and selling with another. God hates cheating. In German, the expression for a double standard, measuring with two different measures, comes from here.
> Verse 11: Even with children, you can see from their behavior what they are like.
> Verse 14: A funny proverb about bargaining at the market: The buyer says, “It’s no good, it’s no good!” In this way he wants to push the price down. Later he proudly tells what a good deal he made.
> Verse 15: There is a lot of gold. But wise words are rare and precious.

## Wait for the LORD (Verses 16–23)

[16] Take the coat of the one who guarantees a loan for a stranger.
Hold him as security for an unfaithful woman.
[17] Food gained by cheating tastes sweet to a person,
but afterward his mouth is full of gravel.
[18] Plans are made firm by advice.
Make war with wise guidance!
[19] Whoever goes around as a gossip gives away secrets.
So don’t get involved with someone who opens his lips wide.
[20] Whoever curses his father or his mother,
his lamp will go out in the deepest darkness.
[21] An inheritance gained quickly at the beginning
will not be blessed in the end.
[22] Don’t say, “I will pay back evil!”
Wait for the LORD, and he will save you.
[23] The LORD hates two different weights,
and dishonest scales do not please him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Whoever carelessly guarantees a loan for strangers has only himself to blame when his things are taken as security. Hard advice to be careful (compare Chapter 6:1–5).
> Verse 17: What you get by cheating tastes sweet at first. But later it is like gravel in your mouth.
> Verse 18: Even in war, you need good advice. Whoever makes big decisions should think them through well.
> Verse 21: Whoever grabs an inheritance early (compare Luke 15:12) will not become happy with it.
> Verse 22: An important sentence: Don’t take revenge yourself! Leave it to God. Paul says the same thing (Romans 12:17–19).

## The lamp of the LORD (Verses 24–30)

[24] A man’s steps come from the LORD.
How then can a person understand his way?
[25] It is a trap for a person to dedicate something to God too quickly
and only think about his vows afterward.
[26] A wise king sifts out the wicked
and drives the threshing wheel over them.
[27] The human spirit is the LORD’s lamp,
searching all his innermost parts.
[28] Love and faithfulness keep the king safe.
His throne is held up by love.
[29] The glory of young men is their strength.
The beauty of old people is their gray hair.
[30] Wounding blows clean away evil,
and beatings clean the innermost parts.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: We often do not understand the way our life takes. But God leads our steps.
> Verse 25: Be careful with quick promises to God. First think, then promise (compare Ecclesiastes 5:4–5).
> Verse 26: In threshing, people drove a wheel over the grain to separate the grains from the chaff. A just king separates the wicked from the good.
> Verse 27: A beautiful thought: Our conscience is like a lamp from God that shines into our inner being.
> Verse 28: A good ruler does not rule with violence, but with love and faithfulness.
> Verse 29: Young and old each have their own beauty: the young their strength, the old their experience.
> Verse 30: Back then, people believed that physical punishment could make a person better. Today we know: Violence does not make people better, it hurts them. At its heart, the meaning is: Painful experiences can make a person think.
