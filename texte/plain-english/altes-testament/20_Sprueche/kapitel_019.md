# Proverbs – Chapter 19
### Whoever has pity on the poor lends to the LORD

---

## Poor but honest (Verses 1–7)

[1] Better is a poor person who lives with integrity
than someone with twisted lips who is a fool.
[2] It is not good to have eagerness without knowledge,
or to hurry with your feet and miss the way.
[3] A person’s foolishness ruins his way,
and his heart rages against the LORD.
[4] Wealth brings many friends,
but the poor person is separated from his friend.
[5] A false witness will not go unpunished.
Whoever pours out lies will not go free.
[6] Many try to win the favor of a ruler,
and everyone is a friend to the one who gives gifts.
[7] All the relatives of the poor person avoid him.
How much more do his friends stay away from him!
He runs after them with requests, but they are gone.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Honesty is worth more than money.
> Verse 2: Eagerness alone is not enough. Whoever rushes off without thinking gets lost.
> Verse 3: A person makes mistakes through his own foolishness, and then he blames God. We know this.
> Verses 4–7: A bitter truth: Whoever is rich has many “friends.” Whoever becomes poor is left by everyone, even by his own family. The Bible describes this honestly, so that we do better and do not leave the poor alone.

---

## Patience and forgiveness (Verses 8–14)

[8] Whoever gets wisdom loves his own life.
Whoever keeps understanding will find good things.
[9] A false witness will not go unpunished.
Whoever tells lies will perish.
[10] A life of luxury does not fit a fool,
much less does it fit a servant to rule over princes.
[11] A person’s good sense makes him slow to anger.
It is his glory to overlook a wrong.
[12] The king’s anger is like the roaring of a lion,
but his favor is like dew on the grass.
[13] A foolish son is a disaster for his father.
A wife’s quarrels are like constant dripping.
[14] House and riches are an inheritance from fathers,
but a wise wife is from the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Whoever is wise does not get upset right away. It is an honor to forgive someone something instead of holding it against him.
> Verse 13: Constant quarreling in a marriage is like a dripping faucet that drives you crazy. Of course this is also true for quarrelsome men.
> Verse 14: You can inherit a house. But you cannot buy a good, wise marriage partner. That is a gift from God.

---

## Lending to God (Verses 15–21)

[15] Laziness makes you fall into a deep sleep.
The lazy soul will go hungry.
[16] Whoever keeps the commandment keeps his life safe,
but whoever despises his ways will die.
[17] Whoever has pity on the poor lends to the LORD.
He will pay him back.
[18] Discipline your son while there is still hope.
Don’t share the guilt for his death.
[19] A hot-tempered man must pay the penalty.
For if you rescue him, you will have to do it again.
[20] Listen to advice and accept instruction,
so that you will be wise in the end.
[21] There are many plans in a person’s heart,
but the LORD’s plan will stand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17 is a wonderful thought: Whoever helps a poor person is really giving to God. And God stays in no one’s debt. Jesus says something similar (Matthew 25:40).
> Verse 18: Parents should bring up their children in time, while they can still be shaped. Whoever sets no limits at all lets the child run into disaster. Bringing up children does not mean violence.
> Verse 19: Whoever has a hot temper gets into trouble again and again. If you always get him out of it, he learns nothing.
> Verse 21: Again the thought: People make plans, but God directs (compare Proverbs 16:9).

---

## What makes a person lovable (Verses 22–29)

[22] What makes a person desirable is his kindness.
A poor person is better than a liar.
[23] The fear of the LORD leads to life, and then to contentment.
You rest and are not touched by trouble.
[24] The lazy person puts his hand in the dish
and does not even bring it back to his mouth.
[25] Whip a mocker, and the simple will learn good sense.
Correct a person with understanding, and he will gain knowledge.
[26] Whoever robs his father and drives away his mother
is a son who brings shame and disgrace.
[27] If you stop listening to instruction, my son,
you will wander away from the words of knowledge.
[28] A corrupt witness mocks justice,
and the mouth of the wicked gulps down wrongdoing.
[29] Punishments are ready for mockers,
and beatings for the backs of fools.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: What really makes a person lovable? Not money or looks, but kindness.
> Verse 24: A funny picture: The lazy person is even too lazy to lift his hand from the plate to his mouth.
> Verses 25 and 29: Back then, punishment by beating was normal. The proverb wants to say: Whoever does not want to listen must feel the consequences, and others learn from it. Today, in most countries, there is no more punishment by beating, and that is a good thing. The heart of it stays: A wise person learns from a single word.
> Verse 26: Treating your parents badly in their old age is a great shame. The commandment “Honor your father and your mother” (Exodus 20:12) is also for grown-up children.
