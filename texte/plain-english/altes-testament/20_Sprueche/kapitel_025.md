# Proverbs – Chapter 25
### Apples of gold in settings of silver

---

## The men of Hezekiah (Verses 1–7)

[1] These also are proverbs of Solomon,
which the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah, copied out.
[2] It is the glory of God to hide a thing,
but the glory of kings is to search out a matter.
[3] As the heavens are high and the earth is deep,
so the hearts of kings cannot be searched out.
[4] Take away the waste from the silver,
and material comes out for the silversmith.
[5] Take away the wicked from the king’s presence,
and his throne will be made firm by righteousness.
[6] Don’t make yourself important before the king,
and don’t claim a place among great people,
[7] for it is better that someone says to you, “Come up here!”
than that you are put lower in front of the prince
whom your eyes have seen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Here a new part begins (Chapters 25–29). King Hezekiah lived about 250 years after Solomon. His writers collected old proverbs of Solomon and copied them out. That is how they were kept for us.
> Verse 2: God has hidden many things in the world. People may and should search them out. Many scientists have understood this verse as encouragement.
> Verses 6–7: Don’t sit down in the place of honor on your own. It is better to be asked to come forward than to be sent away. Jesus tells almost exactly this parable (Luke 14:7–11).

---

## A word at the right time (Verses 8–15)

[8] Don’t be quick to bring charges to court.
What will you do in the end when your neighbor puts you to shame?
[9] Settle your quarrel with your neighbor himself,
but don’t give away someone else’s secret,
[10] or the one who hears it may put you to shame,
and your bad reputation will never go away.
[11] A word spoken at the right time
is like apples of gold in settings of silver.
[12] Like an earring of gold and jewelry of fine gold,
so is a wise person who corrects to an ear that listens.
[13] Like the coolness of snow at harvest time,
so is a faithful messenger to those who send him,
for he refreshes the soul of his masters.
[14] Like clouds and wind without rain,
so is someone who boasts of gifts he does not give.
[15] By patience a ruler can be persuaded.
A gentle tongue breaks bones.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–10: A quarrel should be settled directly with the other person, not right away in court and not behind his back. Jesus gives similar advice (Matthew 18:15).
> Verse 11: A wonderful picture: The right word at the right time is as precious and beautiful as a work of art made of gold and silver.
> Verse 13: In the hot harvest time, something cool feels good. That is how refreshing a reliable person is.
> Verse 14: Whoever promises a lot and keeps nothing is like clouds that bring no rain. Disappointing.
> Verse 15: “A gentle tongue breaks bones”: Patience and kind words often achieve more than violence.

---

## Don’t overdo it (Verses 16–20)

[16] Have you found honey? Eat only as much as is enough for you,
so that you don’t eat too much and throw it up.
[17] Set your foot in your neighbor’s house only rarely,
so that he does not get tired of you and hate you.
[18] A person who gives false testimony against his neighbor
is like a club, a sword, or a sharp arrow.
[19] Trusting an unfaithful person in a time of trouble
is like a bad tooth or a lame foot.
[20] Like someone who takes away a coat on a cold day,
or like vinegar on baking soda,
so is someone who sings songs to a sad heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: Good things too should be enjoyed in moderation: honey and visits to the neighbors. Too much of a good thing spoils everything.
> Verse 19: Whoever relies on an unreliable person in a time of need is like someone with a toothache who tries to bite.
> Verse 20: A sensitive proverb: Cheerful songs do not help someone who is sad. When vinegar gets on baking soda, it foams up. That is how a sad person reacts to forced cheerfulness. It is better to weep with those who weep (Romans 12:15).

---

## When your enemy is hungry (Verses 21–28)

[21] If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat.
If he is thirsty, give him water to drink,
[22] for in this way you will heap burning coals on his head,
and the LORD will reward you.
[23] The north wind brings rain,
and in the same way a slandering tongue brings an angry face.
[24] It is better to live in a corner of the roof
than to share a house with a quarrelsome woman.
[25] Like cold water to a thirsty soul,
so is good news from a faraway country.
[26] Like a muddy spring and a dirty well,
so is a righteous person who gives in before the wicked.
[27] It is not good to eat much honey,
and it is not honorable to look for your own honor.
[28] Like a city that is broken down and has no walls,
so is a person who does not control his spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–22 are among the greatest sentences of the Old Testament: Be good to your enemy! Give him food and drink. The “burning coals” probably mean: He will be ashamed and maybe turn around. Kindness can turn an enemy into a friend. Paul quotes these verses (Romans 12:20–21): “Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.”
> Verse 24 repeats Proverbs 21:9.
> Verse 25: Good news from far away, for example from someone you love, is as refreshing as cold water.
> Verse 26: When good people give in to evil, it is like a dirty well: You can no longer rely on them.
> Verse 28: Whoever cannot control himself is as unprotected as a city without walls.
