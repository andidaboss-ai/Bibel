# Proverbs – Chapter 24
### Rescue those who are being led away to death

---

## A house is built by wisdom (Verses 1–10)

[1] Don’t envy evil people,
and don’t wish to be with them,
[2] for their hearts plan violence,
and their lips talk about harm.
[3] Through wisdom a house is built.
By understanding it is made firm.
[4] By knowledge the rooms are filled
with all kinds of rare and beautiful treasures.
[5] A wise man has great power.
A man with knowledge increases his strength,
[6] for with wise guidance you fight your war,
and victory comes through many advisers.
[7] Wisdom is too high for a fool.
He does not open his mouth in the gate.
[8] Whoever plans to do evil
will be called a schemer.
[9] The plans of foolishness are sin.
People hate the mocker.
[10] If you give up in the time of trouble,
your strength is small.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 3–4: A house, a family, a life is not built only with stones, but with wisdom. And the most beautiful treasures in it are not furniture, but knowledge and love.
> Verse 7: Court cases were held and decisions were made in the city gate. There the fool has nothing wise to say.
> Verse 10: Real strength only shows in hard times.

---

## Don’t look away (Verses 11–12)

[11] Rescue those who are being led away to death!
Yes, hold back those who are staggering to the slaughter!
[12] If you say, “Look, we didn’t know this,”
doesn’t the one who weighs the hearts notice it?
Doesn’t the one who protects your life know it?
Won’t he pay back everyone according to what he has done?

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most urgent calls in the Bible: When people are in danger of death, step in! Don’t look away!
> Verse 12: The excuse “We didn’t know this” does not count before God. He knows what we knew.
> After the Holocaust, many people in Germany said, “We didn’t know anything.” This verse is a lasting warning not to overlook injustice. It is still true today, everywhere people are persecuted, abused or killed.

---

## Don’t be glad when your enemy falls (Verses 13–22)

[13] My son, eat honey, for it is good,
honey from the honeycomb, which is sweet to your taste.
[14] In the same way you will know that wisdom is for your soul.
If you have found it, there will be a reward:
Your hope will not be cut off.
[15] Don’t lie in wait, you wicked man, against the home of the righteous!
Don’t destroy his resting place!
[16] For a righteous person falls seven times and gets up again,
but the wicked are brought down by disaster.
[17] Don’t be glad when your enemy falls!
Don’t let your heart rejoice when he is brought down,
[18] or the LORD will see it, and it will displease him,
and he will turn his anger away from him.
[19] Don’t get upset because of evildoers,
and don’t envy the wicked,
[20] for the evil person has no future.
The lamp of the wicked will go out.
[21] My son, fear the LORD and the king!
Don’t join those who rebel,
[22] for their disaster will come suddenly.
Who knows what ruin may come from them both?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: Wisdom is sweet like honey. It does the soul good.
> Verse 16: An encouraging sentence: Good people fall too, even often, “seven times.” But they get up again. Falling down is no shame. Staying down is.
> Verses 17–18: Don’t be glad about your enemy’s misfortune! God is not pleased when we enjoy someone else’s harm. This fits with Jesus’ command to love our enemies.
> Verses 19–20: Don’t get upset about evil people who are successful. Their success does not last (compare Psalm 37).

## More words of the wise (Verses 23–29)

[23] These also are sayings of the wise:
Taking sides in court is not good.
[24] Whoever says to the wicked, “You are righteous,”
peoples will curse him, and nations will hate him.
[25] But it will go well with those who prove the guilty wrong,
and a rich blessing will come on them.
[26] An honest answer
is like a kiss on the lips.
[27] Prepare your work outside,
and get your fields ready.
After that, build your house.
[28] Don’t be a witness against your neighbor without a reason!
Don’t deceive with your lips!
[29] Don’t say, “I will do to him as he has done to me.
I will pay the man back for what he has done.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–25: A judge must be fair and must not favor anyone. Whoever lets the guilty go free loses everyone’s trust.
> Verse 26: A beautiful picture: An honest answer is a sign of friendship, like a kiss.
> Verse 27: First lay the foundation, then build. First take care of your income, then build a house or start a family.
> Verse 29: “I will do to him as he has done to me”: That is not a good rule. Revenge is not our business (compare Proverbs 20:22).

## The vineyard of the lazy person (Verses 30–34)

[30] I went past the field of the lazy person,
past the vineyard of the man without sense.
[31] Look, it was all grown over with thorns.
Its ground was covered with nettles,
and its stone wall was broken down.
[32] Then I saw it and thought about it carefully.
I saw it and learned a lesson:
[33] A little sleep, a little slumber,
a little folding of the hands to sleep,
[34] and your poverty will come like a robber
and your need like an armed man.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wise man tells what he has seen: A vineyard that no one takes care of grows wild. Weeds grow, and the wall falls down.
> He learns from it: Decay does not come suddenly, but slowly, through many small things left undone. That is also true for friendships, marriages and faith.
> Verses 33–34 repeat Proverbs 6:10–11.
> This ends the part called “The words of the wise.”
