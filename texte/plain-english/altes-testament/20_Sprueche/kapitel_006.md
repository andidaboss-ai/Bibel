# Proverbs – Chapter 6
### Go to the ant!

---

## Guaranteeing a debt: Free yourself quickly (Verses 1–5)

[1] My son, if you have guaranteed a loan for your neighbor,
if you have shaken hands on a promise for a stranger,
[2] then you are trapped by the words of your mouth.
You are caught by the words of your mouth.
[3] Do this now, my son, and save yourself,
since you have come into the hand of your neighbor:
Go, humble yourself!
Press your request with your neighbor!
[4] Give no sleep to your eyes
and no slumber to your eyelids!
[5] Free yourself like a gazelle from the hand of the hunter,
like a bird from the trap of the bird catcher!

> **What does this mean?**
> To “guarantee a loan” means: I promise to pay someone else’s debts if he cannot. Back then, people sealed this with a handshake.
> The advice: If you have given such a guarantee without thinking, try to get out of it quickly. Otherwise you can lose everything.
> This advice is still relevant today. Many people get into great debt by guaranteeing loans for friends or relatives. That does not mean that we should not help. But we should not promise anything that we cannot carry.
> If you have debts and need help: In many countries, nonprofit debt counseling services help for free.

## Learn from the ant (Verses 6–11)

[6] Go to the ant, you lazy person!
Look at her ways, and become wise!
[7] She has no leader,
no overseer and no ruler.
[8] Still, she gets her bread ready in the summer
and gathers her food at harvest time.
[9] How long will you sleep, you lazy person?
When will you get up from your sleep?
[10] A little sleep, a little slumber,
a little folding of the hands to sleep,
[11] and your poverty will come like a robber
and your need like an armed man.

> **What does this mean?**
> The little ant is an example: No one has to drive her on. Still, she works hard and gets ready for the winter.
> Verses 10–11: Laziness comes in small steps: “A little sleep, a little slumber …” Today people say: Just five more minutes. And suddenly need is there, like a robber who comes by surprise.
> Important: This is about laziness, not about people who are sick, worn out or unemployed. Poverty often has very different causes. The Bible calls many times for helping the poor.

## The troublemaker (Verses 12–15)

[12] A worthless person, a man of wrongdoing,
is the one who goes around with a twisted mouth,
[13] who winks with his eyes,
who gives signals with his feet,
who points with his fingers,
[14] in whose heart is twistedness,
who plans evil all the time,
who always sows quarrels.
[15] So his disaster will come suddenly.
He will be broken suddenly, and there is no healing.

> **What does this mean?**
> A picture of a sneaky person: He speaks falsely and gives secret signals with his eyes, feet and fingers. In this way he makes plans with others against someone.
> He keeps stirring up quarrels. But in the end, his behavior falls back on himself.

## Seven things that God hates (Verses 16–19)

[16] There are six things that the LORD hates,
yes, seven that are disgusting to him:
[17] proud eyes, a lying tongue,
hands that shed innocent blood,
[18] a heart that makes wicked plans,
feet that run quickly to do harm,
[19] a false witness who tells lies,
and the one who sows quarrels among brothers.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Six, yes seven”: a typical way of counting that puts special weight on the last one. The seventh is the worst: sowing quarrels between people who belong together.
> The list goes through the whole body: eyes, tongue, hands, heart, feet. Evil can be done with everything we have.
> God does not hate the people, but these deeds, because they hurt others and destroy community.

## The commandment is a lamp (Verses 20–35)

[20] My son, keep your father’s commandment,
and don’t leave your mother’s teaching!
[21] Tie them on your heart always!
Tie them around your neck!
[22] When you walk, it will lead you.
When you sleep, it will watch over you.
When you wake up, it will talk with you.
[23] For the commandment is a lamp, and the law is light.
The correction that comes with instruction is the way of life,
[24] to keep you from the immoral woman,
from the flattering tongue of the unfaithful wife.
[25] Don’t desire her beauty in your heart,
and don’t let her catch you with her eyelids!
[26] For a prostitute brings you down to a piece of bread.
The adulteress hunts for your precious life.
[27] Can a man scoop fire into his lap
and his clothes not be burned?
[28] Or can someone walk on hot coals
and his feet not be burned?
[29] So it is with the one who goes in to his neighbor’s wife.
Whoever touches her will not go unpunished.
[30] People don’t despise a thief
if he steals to fill himself when he is hungry.
[31] But if he is caught, he must pay back seven times as much.
He must give all the wealth of his house.
[32] Whoever commits adultery with a woman has no sense.
Whoever does it destroys his own life.
[33] He will get wounds and dishonor.
His shame will not be wiped away.
[34] For jealousy wakes up the husband’s anger.
He will not hold back on the day of revenge.
[35] He will not accept any ransom.
He will not be satisfied, even if you give many gifts.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The good teachings of parents go with you through your whole life: during the day, at night and in the morning.
> Verse 23: “The commandment is a lamp”: like Psalm 119:105.
> Verses 27–28: Whoever cheats is playing with fire. You cannot walk on hot coals without getting burned.
> Verses 30–35: A comparison: A thief who steals because he is hungry can still be understood. He can make up for the damage. But adultery cannot be made good with money. The hurt husband cannot be bought.
> Verse 34: This describes how a betrayed man may react. It is not permission for revenge or violence. It only shows how deep the wound of unfaithfulness goes.
