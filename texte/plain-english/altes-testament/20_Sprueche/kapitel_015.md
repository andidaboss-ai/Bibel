# Proverbs – Chapter 15
### A gentle answer

---

## Gentle and harsh words (Verses 1–7)

[1] A gentle answer turns away anger,
but a harsh word stirs up anger.
[2] The tongue of the wise makes knowledge attractive,
but the mouth of fools pours out foolishness.
[3] The LORD’s eyes are everywhere.
They keep watch over the evil and the good.
[4] A gentle tongue is a tree of life,
but deceit in it crushes the spirit.
[5] A fool despises his father’s correction,
but whoever pays attention to correction shows good sense.
[6] In the house of the righteous is much treasure,
but the income of the wicked brings trouble.
[7] The lips of the wise spread knowledge,
but not so the heart of fools.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 is one of the best-known proverbs: Whoever answers calmly and kindly can end a quarrel. Whoever answers harshly pours oil on the fire. This works in the family, at work and on the internet.
> Verse 3: God sees everything. For the evil this is a warning, for the good it is a comfort.
> Verse 4: Kind words are like a tree that gives life. Lies and meanness can break a person inside.

---

## What pleases God (Verses 8–15)

[8] The sacrifice of the wicked is disgusting to the LORD,
but the prayer of the upright pleases him.
[9] The way of the wicked is disgusting to the LORD,
but he loves the one who chases after righteousness.
[10] Hard discipline is for the one who leaves the way.
Whoever hates correction will die.
[11] Sheol (the world of the dead) and Abaddon lie open before the LORD.
How much more the hearts of the human race!
[12] A mocker does not love to be corrected.
He will not go to the wise.
[13] A glad heart makes a cheerful face,
but an aching heart breaks the spirit.
[14] The heart of the one who has understanding looks for knowledge,
but the mouth of fools feeds on foolishness.
[15] All the days of the suffering are miserable,
but whoever has a cheerful heart has a feast all the time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: God cannot be bribed with sacrifices when someone’s life is evil. But the honest prayer of an upright person makes him glad, even without a sacrifice.
> Verse 11: “Abaddon” is a Hebrew word for the deep pit, another word for Sheol, the world of the dead. God sees even into the deepest depths. All the more he sees into our hearts.
> Verses 13 and 15: Our mood inside shows on our face and shapes our whole life. A glad heart makes every day a feast.
> If someone is sad for a very long time and cannot become happy again, it can be depression. That is an illness and not a weakness. Doctors and therapists can help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Better a little with love (Verses 16–23)

[16] Better a little with the fear of the LORD
than great treasure with trouble.
[17] Better a meal of vegetables where there is love
than a fattened calf with hatred.
[18] An angry person stirs up quarrels,
but whoever is slow to anger calms the quarrel.
[19] The way of the lazy person is like a thorn hedge,
but the path of the upright is a highway.
[20] A wise son makes his father glad,
but a foolish person despises his mother.
[21] Foolishness is a joy to the one who has no wisdom,
but a man of understanding keeps his way straight.
[22] Where there is no advice, plans fail,
but where there are many advisers, they succeed.
[23] A person is glad about the answer of his mouth.
How good is a word at the right time!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: What really makes people happy? Not expensive food or a lot of money. A simple meal with people who love each other is better than a feast full of quarrels.
> Verse 19: For the lazy person everything is hard, as if there were thorns in the way. Whoever lives uprightly makes good progress.
> Verse 22: Big decisions should not be made alone. Get good advice!
> Verse 23: The right word at the right moment is a great gift.

## God hears prayer (Verses 24–33)

[24] The path of life leads upward for the wise,
so that he does not go down to Sheol.
[25] The LORD tears down the house of the proud,
but he keeps the widow’s borders safe.
[26] The LORD hates the thoughts of the wicked,
but the thoughts of the pure please him.
[27] Whoever is greedy for gain brings trouble on his own house,
but whoever hates bribes will live.
[28] The heart of the righteous thinks about how to answer,
but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil.
[29] The LORD is far from the wicked,
but he hears the prayer of the righteous.
[30] The light of the eyes makes the heart glad.
Good news gives health to the bones.
[31] The ear that listens to correction lives
and will be at home among the wise.
[32] Whoever refuses instruction despises his own life,
but whoever listens to correction gains understanding.
[33] The fear of the LORD teaches wisdom.
Before honor comes humility.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: Back then, widows often had no protection. Powerful people tried to take land from them by moving the boundary stones. God himself protects the widow’s border. He is on the side of the weak.
> Verse 27: Bribery destroys. Whoever rejects it lives well.
> Verse 28: Good advice: Think first, then speak.
> Verse 30: A shining face and good news do body and soul good.
> Verse 33: “Before honor comes humility”: Whoever does not take himself too seriously will be honored in the end.
