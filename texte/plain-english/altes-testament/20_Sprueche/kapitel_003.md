# Proverbs – Chapter 3
### Trust in the LORD with all your heart

---

## Kindness and truth (Verses 1–4)

[1] My son, don’t forget my teaching,
but let your heart keep my commandments,
[2] for they will add to your life many days,
years of life, and peace.
[3] Don’t let kindness and truth leave you!
Tie them around your neck!
Write them on the tablet of your heart!
[4] So you will find favor and good understanding
in the eyes of God and people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Kindness and truth should be as close to you as a chain around your neck. And they should be deep inside you, as if written on your heart.
> Verse 4: Whoever is kind and faithful is valued by God and people. Something similar is said later about Jesus as a child (Luke 2:52).

---

## Don’t rely on your own understanding (Verses 5–12)

[5] Trust in the LORD with all your heart,
and don’t lean on your own understanding!
[6] In all your ways acknowledge him,
and he will make your paths straight.
[7] Don’t be wise in your own eyes!
Fear the LORD, and turn away from evil!
[8] It will be health for your body
and strength for your bones.
[9] Honor the LORD with your wealth,
with the first fruits of all your harvest!
[10] So your barns will be filled with plenty,
and your wine vats will overflow with new wine.
[11] My son, don’t despise the LORD’s discipline,
and don’t grow tired of his correction!
[12] For the LORD corrects the one he loves,
just as a father corrects the son he delights in.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6 are among the best-known verses of the Bible. Our understanding is good, but it has limits. Whoever trusts God and includes him in all decisions finds the right way.
> Verse 7: Whoever thinks he already knows everything cannot learn anything anymore.
> Verses 9–10: The “first fruits” are the first and best part of the harvest. People gave it to God as thanks. The principle is still true today: God first, not last. But it is not a trick to get rich.
> Verses 11–12: When God corrects us, it is a sign of his love, as with good parents. The letter to the Hebrews quotes these verses (Hebrews 12:5–6). “Correction” here means bringing up a child, not violence.

---

## Wisdom is worth more than gold (Verses 13–20)

[13] Happy is the person who finds wisdom,
the person who gets understanding.
[14] For what she earns is better than earning silver,
and what she brings in is better than fine gold.
[15] She is more precious than rubies.
Nothing you can wish for compares to her.
[16] Long life is in her right hand.
In her left hand are riches and honor.
[17] Her ways are pleasant ways.
All her paths are peace.
[18] She is a tree of life to those who take hold of her.
Happy is everyone who holds on to her.
[19] By wisdom the LORD founded the earth.
By understanding he set the heavens in place.
[20] By his knowledge the deep waters were broken open,
and the skies drop down the dew.

> **What does this mean?**
> Wisdom is worth more than gold, silver and precious stones. For with wisdom you can live well. Money cannot promise that.
> Verse 18: “A tree of life” reminds us of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9). Whoever holds on to wisdom finds real life. In Judaism, people sing this verse when the Torah scroll is put back into its cabinet.
> Verses 19–20: God made the whole world with wisdom. Whoever lives wisely lives in harmony with creation.

---

## Then you can sleep in peace (Verses 21–26)

[21] My son, don’t let them out of your sight!
Keep sound wisdom and good judgment,
[22] so they will be life for your soul
and jewelry for your neck.
[23] Then you will walk safely on your way.
Your foot will not stumble.
[24] When you lie down, you will not be afraid.
Yes, you will lie down, and your sleep will be sweet.
[25] Don’t be afraid of sudden terror,
or of the ruin of the wicked when it comes,
[26] for the LORD will be your confidence
and will keep your foot from being caught.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever lives wisely has a good conscience and can sleep in peace.
> Verse 26: The real reason for this peace is not our cleverness, but God: He is our confidence.

---

## Do good when you can (Verses 27–35)

[27] Don’t hold back good from those who deserve it,
when it is in the power of your hand to do it.
[28] Don’t say to your neighbor, “Go, and come back again;
tomorrow I will give it to you,”
when you have it with you.
[29] Don’t plan evil against your neighbor,
while he lives trustingly beside you.
[30] Don’t quarrel with anyone without a reason,
if he has done you no harm.
[31] Don’t envy the violent man,
and choose none of his ways!
[32] For the twisted person is disgusting to the LORD,
but he is a close friend to the upright.
[33] The LORD’s curse is in the house of the wicked,
but he blesses the home of the righteous.
[34] Surely he mocks the mockers,
but he gives grace to the humble.
[35] The wise will inherit honor,
but shame will be the promotion of fools.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 27–28: Very practical: If you can help someone and he needs it, do it now. Don’t put it off until tomorrow. This is especially true for wages, debts or help for the poor.
> Verses 29–30: Being a good neighbor: Don’t take advantage of other people’s trust, and don’t start pointless quarrels.
> Verse 31: Violent people sometimes seem successful. But don’t envy them.
> Verse 34: This verse is quoted in the New Testament: “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble” (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5).
> Verse 35: “Shame will be the promotion of fools”: Whoever lives foolishly only seems to rise. In reality, his way leads to shame.
