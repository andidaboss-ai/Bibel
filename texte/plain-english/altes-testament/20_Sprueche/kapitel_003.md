# Proverbs – Chapter 3
### Trust in the LORD with all your heart

---

## Kindness and truth (Verses 1–4)

[1] My son, don’t forget what I teach you.
Keep my commandments in your heart.
[2] They will give you a long life,
many years of life, and peace.
[3] Don’t let kindness and truth leave you.
Tie them around your neck.
Write them on your heart, as if it were a tablet.
[4] Then God and people will like you.
They will think well of you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Kindness and truth should be as close to you as a chain around your neck. And they should be deep inside you, as if written on your heart.
> Verse 4: Whoever is kind and faithful is valued by God and people. Something similar is said later about Jesus as a child (Luke 2:52).

---

## Don’t rely on your own understanding (Verses 5–12)

[5] Trust in the LORD with all your heart.
Don’t depend on your own understanding.
[6] Think of him in everything you do.
Then he will make your paths straight.
[7] Don’t think that you are wise.
Fear the LORD, and turn away from evil.
To fear the LORD means: to honor him deeply and to obey him.
[8] That will be healing for your body
and strength for your bones.
[9] Honor the LORD with what you own.
Give him the first fruits of all your harvest.
[10] Then your barns will be full,
and your wine vats will overflow with new wine.
[11] My son, don’t reject the LORD’s discipline.
Don’t be annoyed when he corrects you.
[12] The LORD corrects the one he loves,
just as a father corrects the son he takes delight in.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6 are among the best-known verses of the Bible. Our understanding is good, but it has limits. Whoever trusts God and includes him in all decisions finds the right way.
> Verse 7: Whoever thinks he already knows everything cannot learn anything anymore.
> Verses 9–10: The “first fruits” are the first and best part of the harvest. People gave it to God as thanks. The principle is still true today: God first, not last. But it is not a trick to get rich.
> Verses 11–12: When God corrects us, it is a sign of his love, as with good parents. The letter to the Hebrews quotes these verses (Hebrews 12:5–6). “Correct” here means bringing up a child, not violence.

---

## Wisdom is worth more than gold (Verses 13–20)

[13] Happy is the person who finds wisdom,
the person who gains understanding.
[14] Wisdom brings more profit than silver.
What she gives you is better than fine gold.
[15] She is worth more than rubies.
Nothing you could wish for is as valuable as she is.
[16] In her right hand she holds a long life.
In her left hand she holds riches and honor.
[17] Her ways are pleasant ways.
All her paths lead to peace.
[18] She is a tree of life for those who take hold of her.
Happy is everyone who holds on to her.
[19] The LORD made the earth with wisdom.
He set the heavens in place with understanding.
[20] With his knowledge he broke open the deep waters.
With his knowledge the skies let the dew fall.

> **What does this mean?**
> Wisdom is worth more than gold, silver and precious stones. For with wisdom you can live well. Money cannot promise that.
> Verse 18: “A tree of life” reminds us of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9). Whoever holds on to wisdom finds real life. In Judaism, people sing this verse when the Torah scroll is put back into its cabinet.
> Verses 19–20: God made the whole world with wisdom. Whoever lives wisely lives in harmony with creation.

---

## Then you can sleep in peace (Verses 21–26)

[21] My son, keep these things always in sight.
Hold on to good wisdom and careful thinking.
[22] They will give life to your soul.
They will be like jewelry around your neck.
[23] Then you will walk safely on your way.
You will not trip.
[24] When you lie down, you will not be afraid.
You will lie down, and you will sleep well.
[25] Don’t be afraid of sudden terror.
Don’t be afraid when ruin comes on the wicked.
[26] The LORD will be your confidence.
He will keep your foot from being caught in a trap.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever lives wisely has a good conscience and can sleep in peace.
> Verse 26: The real reason for this peace is not our cleverness, but God: He is our confidence.

---

## Do good when you can (Verses 27–35)

[27] Do good to those who need it.
Don’t hold it back when you are able to do it.
[28] You have something with you that your neighbor needs.
Then don’t say to him, “Go away and come back later.
I will give it to you tomorrow.”
[29] Don’t plan evil against your neighbor.
He lives next to you and trusts you.
[30] Don’t argue with anyone without a reason
if he has not harmed you.
[31] Don’t envy a violent man.
Don’t choose any of his ways.
[32] The LORD hates people who are crooked.
But he is a close friend to honest people.
[33] The LORD’s curse is on the house of the wicked.
But he blesses the home of those who do right.
[34] He mocks those who mock others.
But he is gracious to humble people.
[35] Wise people will receive honor.
But fools will only be raised up to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 27–28: Very practical: If you can help someone and he needs it, do it now. Don’t put it off until tomorrow. This is especially true for wages, debts or help for the poor.
> Verses 29–30: Being a good neighbor: Don’t take advantage of other people’s trust, and don’t start pointless quarrels.
> Verse 31: Violent people sometimes seem successful. But don’t envy them.
> Verse 34: This verse is quoted in the New Testament: “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble” (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5).
> Verse 35: “Fools will only be raised up to shame”: Whoever lives foolishly only seems to rise. In reality, his way leads to shame.
