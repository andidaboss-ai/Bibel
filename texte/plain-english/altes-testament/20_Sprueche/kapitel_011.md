# Proverbs – Chapter 11
### Honest scales and generous hearts

---

## Honesty and humility (Verses 1–8)

[1] A false scale is disgusting to the LORD,
but accurate weights please him.
[2] When pride comes, then comes shame,
but with humility comes wisdom.
[3] The honesty of the upright guides them,
but the twistedness of the unfaithful destroys them.
[4] Riches are no use on the day of anger,
but righteousness saves from death.
[5] The righteousness of the blameless makes his way straight,
but the wicked falls by his own wickedness.
[6] The righteousness of the upright saves them,
but the unfaithful are trapped by their evil desires.
[7] When a wicked man dies, his hope is lost,
and the expectation of power comes to nothing.
[8] A righteous person is saved out of trouble,
and the wicked takes his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: In trade, people used scales with stone weights. Some traders cheated with false weights. God hates cheating in business. That is just as true today: false bills, hidden costs, lies in advertising.
> Verse 2: “When pride comes, then comes shame.” From verses like this came a well-known saying: Pride comes before a fall.
> Verse 4: In the end, what counts is not how much money someone has, but how he has lived.

---

## The city and the neighbor (Verses 9–15)

[9] With his mouth the godless man destroys his neighbor,
but the righteous are saved through knowledge.
[10] When things go well for the righteous, the city is glad.
When the wicked die, there is shouting for joy.
[11] By the blessing of the upright, the city is lifted up,
but by the mouth of the wicked it is torn down.
[12] Whoever looks down on his neighbor has no wisdom,
but a man of understanding keeps quiet.
[13] Whoever spreads gossip gives away secrets,
but whoever has a faithful spirit keeps a secret.
[14] Where there is no wise guidance, a nation falls,
but where there are many advisers, there is victory.
[15] Whoever guarantees a loan for a stranger will suffer for it,
but whoever refuses to guarantee loans is safe.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: Good and honest people are a blessing for the whole city, for society. Evil words can destroy a city.
> Verse 13: Whoever passes on secrets destroys trust. A faithful friend can keep quiet.
> Verse 14: Good decisions need many wise advisers. No one should decide alone, least of all someone who has power.
> Verse 15: Again the warning against guaranteeing loans (compare Chapter 6:1–5).

---

## Generosity pays off (Verses 16–26)

[16] A gracious woman gains honor,
but violent men gain riches.
[17] The merciful person does good to himself,
but the cruel person harms his own flesh.
[18] The wicked earn wages that deceive,
but whoever sows righteousness reaps a sure reward.
[19] Whoever is truly righteous gets life.
Whoever chases after evil gets death.
[20] Those who have a twisted heart are disgusting to the LORD,
but those whose ways are blameless please him.
[21] Most certainly, the evil person will not go unpunished,
but the descendants of the righteous will be saved.
[22] Like a gold ring in a pig’s snout,
so is a beautiful woman who has no sense.
[23] The wish of the righteous is only good.
What the wicked can expect is anger.
[24] One person gives freely and gets more and more.
Another holds back more than is right, and still becomes poor.
[25] The generous soul will be richly filled.
Whoever gives others water will get water himself.
[26] People curse someone who holds back grain,
but blessing comes on the head of the one who sells it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: You gain honor by being kind. Violent people may get money, but no real honor.
> Verse 17: Whoever is merciful does himself good. Whoever is cruel harms himself too in the end.
> Verse 22: A strong picture: Beauty without sense is like gold jewelry on a pig that digs around in the dirt. Of course this is just as true for men.
> Verses 24–25: A surprising principle: Whoever gives generously does not become poorer, but richer, in joy, in friends and often in blessing too. Whoever is stingy gains nothing by it.
> Verse 26: Whoever stores up grain during a famine to drive up the price is acting wrongly. This is still true today for speculating with food.

---

## The tree of life (Verses 27–31)

[27] Whoever eagerly looks for good looks for favor,
but whoever searches for evil, it will come to him.
[28] Whoever trusts in his riches will fall,
but the righteous will flourish like green leaves.
[29] Whoever brings trouble on his own house will inherit the wind.
The fool will be servant to the one who is wise in heart.
[30] The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life.
Whoever is wise wins people.
[31] Look, the righteous are paid back on the earth.
How much more the wicked and the sinner!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 28: Money is no safe support. Righteous people are like a tree with fresh leaves (compare Psalm 1:3).
> Verse 29: Whoever brings quarrels into his own family is left with empty hands in the end. He “will inherit the wind.”
> Verse 30: “Whoever is wise wins people”: A wise person draws others to him and can help them.
> Verse 31: Everyone feels the results of his actions already here on earth. Peter quotes this verse in its Greek form (1 Peter 4:18).
