# Proverbs – Chapter 28
### Whoever confesses his sins finds mercy

---

## Bold as a lion (Verses 1–7)

[1] The wicked run away when no one is chasing them,
but the righteous are as bold as a lion.
[2] When a land rebels, it has many rulers,
but order is kept by a man of understanding and knowledge.
[3] A needy person who oppresses the poor
is like a pounding rain that leaves no crops.
[4] Those who leave the law praise the wicked,
but those who keep the law fight against them.
[5] Evil people don’t understand justice,
but those who seek the LORD understand it fully.
[6] Better is a poor person who lives with integrity
than someone who goes crooked ways and is rich.
[7] Whoever keeps the law is a wise son,
but whoever spends time with gluttons brings shame on his father.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Whoever has a guilty conscience is always afraid, even without a reason. Whoever has a good conscience can be bold.
> Verse 2: When a land turns away from God, there is chaos and constantly new people in power.
> Verse 3: It is especially bad when someone who was poor himself takes advantage of other poor people.
> Verse 5: Whoever seeks God gets a sense for justice.

---

## Excessive interest and honest confession (Verses 8–14)

[8] Whoever increases his wealth by excessive interest
gathers it for the one who has pity on the poor.
[9] Whoever turns his ear away from hearing the law,
even his prayer is disgusting.
[10] Whoever leads the upright astray onto an evil way
will fall into his own trap,
but the blameless will inherit good things.
[11] The rich person is wise in his own eyes,
but the poor person who has understanding sees through him.
[12] When the righteous win, there is great glory,
but when the wicked rise to power, people hide.
[13] Whoever hides his sins will not succeed,
but whoever confesses them and gives them up finds mercy.
[14] Happy is the person who always has reverence,
but whoever hardens his heart falls into trouble.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Whoever gets rich with excessive interest does not keep the money in the end. God makes sure that it comes to those who help the poor. The law of Moses forbade taking interest from poor Israelites (Exodus 22:25).
> Verse 9: Whoever does not want to listen to God, his prayers are empty.
> Verse 12: When good people govern, the land is glad. When evil people come to power, people hide in fear. This has happened again and again in dictatorships.
> Verse 13 is one of the most comforting verses of the book: Whoever hides his guilt does not become happy. But whoever honestly admits it and stops doing it finds God’s mercy. Compare Psalm 32:5 and 1 John 1:9.

## The cruel ruler (Verses 15–22)

[15] Like a roaring lion or a charging bear,
so is a wicked ruler over helpless people.
[16] A cruel ruler has no sense.
Whoever hates dishonest gain will live long.
[17] A person who is tormented by the guilt of bloodshed
will be a fugitive until death. No one will support him.
[18] Whoever lives blamelessly is kept safe,
but whoever goes crooked ways will fall suddenly.
[19] Whoever works his land will have plenty of food,
but whoever chases fantasies will have plenty of poverty.
[20] A faithful person is rich with blessings,
but whoever is eager to get rich will not go unpunished.
[21] Taking sides is not good,
and yet a person will do wrong for a piece of bread.
[22] A stingy person hurries after riches
and does not know that poverty is waiting for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–16: A cruel ruler is like a wild animal for his people. Cruel, unjust rule is not strength, but foolishness.
> Verse 17: Whoever has killed a person finds no peace. His conscience torments him.
> Verse 20: “Getting rich quick” is dangerous. This is also true for gambling and dishonest investments. If you are addicted to gambling, you can find help. US: SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 21: Some people let themselves be bribed even for a small thing.

## Whoever gives to the poor (Verses 23–28)

[23] Whoever corrects a person will later find more favor
than the one who flatters with his tongue.
[24] Whoever robs his father or his mother and says, “It’s not wrong,”
is a partner of a destroyer.
[25] Whoever is greedy stirs up quarrels,
but whoever trusts in the LORD will do well.
[26] Whoever trusts in himself is a fool,
but whoever lives in wisdom is kept safe.
[27] Whoever gives to the poor will lack nothing,
but whoever closes his eyes will get many curses.
[28] When the wicked rise to power, people hide,
but when they perish, the righteous do well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: In the end, honest criticism is valued more than flattery.
> Verse 24: Grown-up children too must not take advantage of their parents, for example by taking their money or grabbing their inheritance early. That is wrong, even if you try to make it sound good.
> Verse 26: Whoever trusts only in himself is relying on shaky ground.
> Verse 27: Whoever gives to the poor does not become poorer. Whoever looks away becomes guilty.
