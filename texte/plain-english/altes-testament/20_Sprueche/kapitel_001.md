# Proverbs – Chapter 1
### The beginning of wisdom

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Proverbs?

The book of Proverbs is a book full of wisdom for life.
It has short, wise sayings for everyday life: about work and laziness, friendship and quarrels, money and poverty, words and silence, family and neighbors.
Many proverbs are linked to King Solomon. He was famous for his wisdom (1 Kings 4:29–34). But other wise people also added proverbs, for example Agur and King Lemuel (Chapters 30 and 31).
The book has several parts:
– Chapters 1–9: longer speeches. A father (and a mother) teach their son. Wisdom appears like a woman who calls out in the street.
– Chapters 10–29: many single, short proverbs. Often they are two lines that show a contrast, like this: “The wise … but the fool …”
– Chapters 30–31: words of Agur and Lemuel, and at the end the praise of a capable woman.
The motto of the whole book is in Chapter 1, verse 7: “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge.”
Important: Proverbs are not promises that always come true for everyone. They describe how life usually works. The book of Job and the book of Ecclesiastes show that there are exceptions too.
The book often speaks to “my son,” because back then it was written for young men. But its wisdom is for all people, for women and men, young and old.

## What this book is for (Verses 1–7)

[1] The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel:
[2] to know wisdom and instruction,
to understand words of understanding,
[3] to receive instruction in wise dealing,
in righteousness, justice, and fairness,
[4] to give good sense to the simple,
knowledge and good judgment to the young man,
[5] so that the wise man may hear and grow in learning,
and the man of understanding may gain good advice,
[6] to understand proverbs and parables,
the words of the wise and their riddles.
[7] The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge.
But the foolish despise wisdom and instruction.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–6 name the purpose of the book: It wants to make people wise for life. Not only the “simple,” that is, people without experience, but also those who are already wise. You never stop learning.
> Verse 3: “Righteousness” comes up very often in this book. Righteous means: living right before God.
> Verse 7 is the most important sentence of the book. “The fear of the LORD” does not mean being afraid. It means: taking God seriously, respecting him, trusting him. Whoever lives like this has the foundation for all wisdom.
> In the book of Proverbs, the “fool” is not simply someone who knows little. It is someone who does not want to learn and does not take God seriously.

## Don’t let yourself be led astray (Verses 8–19)

[8] My son, listen to your father’s instruction,
and don’t leave your mother’s teaching!
[9] For they will be a beautiful wreath for your head
and chains around your neck.
[10] My son, if sinners try to lure you, don’t agree!
[11] If they say, “Come with us!
Let’s lie in wait for blood!
Let’s secretly lie in wait for the innocent without any reason!
[12] Let’s swallow them up alive like Sheol (the world of the dead),
whole, like those who go down into the pit!
[13] We’ll find all kinds of valuable wealth.
We’ll fill our houses with plunder.
[14] You shall cast your lot with us.
We’ll all have one purse.”
[15] My son, don’t walk on the path with them!
Keep your foot away from their path!
[16] For their feet run to evil.
They hurry to shed blood.
[17] For the net is spread in vain
in the sight of any bird.
[18] But these men lie in wait for their own blood.
They secretly lie in wait for their own lives.
[19] Such are the ways of everyone who is greedy for gain.
Greed takes away the life of its owners.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Father and mother both teach. The mother is named on purpose.
> Verse 9: Good teaching is like jewelry. It makes a person beautiful.
> Verses 10–14: A gang tries to win over a young man: “Come with us! … We’ll all have one purse.” That sounds like belonging and quick money. This kind of temptation still exists today: gangs, crime, fraud.
> Verses 17–18: A bird that sees the net does not fly into it. But these men do not see their own trap. Whoever harms others harms themselves in the end.
> Verse 19: Greed destroys the life of the one who is greedy.

## Wisdom calls out in the street (Verses 20–33)

[20] Wisdom calls aloud in the street.
She raises her voice in the public squares.
[21] She calls out at the noisiest places.
At the entrance of the city gates, she speaks her words:
[22] “How long, you simple ones, will you love being simple?
How long will mockers enjoy mocking,
and how long will fools hate knowledge?
[23] Turn back when I correct you!
Look, I will pour out my spirit on you.
I will make my words known to you.
[24] Because I have called, and you have refused,
because I have stretched out my hand, and no one has paid attention,
[25] but you have ignored all my advice
and wanted none of my correction,
[26] I also will laugh at your disaster.
I will mock when trouble comes over you,
[27] when trouble comes over you like a storm,
when your disaster comes like a whirlwind,
when distress and fear come on you.
[28] Then they will call to me, but I will not answer.
They will search for me eagerly, but they will not find me,
[29] because they hated knowledge
and did not choose the fear of the LORD.
[30] They wanted none of my advice.
They despised all my correction.
[31] So they will eat the fruit of their own way
and be filled with their own plans.
[32] For the turning away of the simple will kill them.
The careless ease of fools will destroy them.
[33] But whoever listens to me will live safely.
He will be at peace, without fear of harm.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here wisdom is described like a woman who calls out loudly in the middle of the city: in the market square, at the city gate, where everyone passes by. Wisdom is not hidden. Everyone can hear it.
> Verse 23: She invites: Turn back! I want to give you my spirit.
> Verses 24–32: Hard words for those who do not want to listen. Whoever ignores every warning must live with the consequences. “They will eat the fruit of their own way”: You reap what you sow.
> Verse 26: “I also will laugh”: This is picture language that is made sharp on purpose. It does not mean that God is happy about suffering. It shows: Whoever rejects wisdom cannot suddenly rely on it in a disaster.
> Verse 33: The goal is good: Whoever listens to wisdom lives safely and without fear.
