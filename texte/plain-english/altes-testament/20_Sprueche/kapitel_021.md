# Proverbs – Chapter 21
### The king’s heart in God’s hand

---

## Doing right is better than sacrifice (Verses 1–8)

[1] The king’s heart is in the LORD’s hand like streams of water.
He turns it wherever he wants.
[2] Every way of a person is right in his own eyes,
but the LORD weighs the hearts.
[3] To do righteousness and justice
pleases the LORD more than sacrifice.
[4] Proud eyes and an arrogant heart,
the lamp of the wicked, are sin.
[5] The plans of hard workers surely lead to profit,
but everyone who is hasty surely rushes into poverty.
[6] Getting treasures with a lying tongue
is a breath that quickly passes, for those who look for death.
[7] The violence of the wicked will sweep them away,
because they refuse to do what is right.
[8] The way of the guilty is crooked,
but the behavior of the innocent is upright.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A farmer guides water through small channels onto his field, wherever he wants. In the same way, God guides even the heart of the powerful. Kings and presidents too are not outside of God’s power.
> Verse 3: God would rather have us act justly than carry out religious rituals. The prophets say this too (Hosea 6:6; Micah 6:8). Jesus quotes this thought (Matthew 9:13).
> Verse 5: Whoever plans well and works hard gets further than someone who wants to do everything in a rush.

---

## Better to live on the roof (Verses 9–19)

[9] It is better to live in a corner of the roof
than to share a house with a quarrelsome woman.
[10] The soul of the wicked wants evil.
His neighbor finds no mercy in his eyes.
[11] When the mocker is punished, the simple become wise.
When the wise is taught, he receives knowledge.
[12] The Righteous One looks at the house of the wicked
and brings the wicked to ruin.
[13] Whoever shuts his ears to the cry of the poor
will cry out himself and not be heard.
[14] A secret gift calms anger,
and a bribe hidden in the coat calms strong rage.
[15] For the righteous, it is a joy to do justice,
but for those who do wrong, it is a terror.
[16] A person who wanders away from the way of understanding
will rest in the assembly of the dead.
[17] Whoever loves pleasure will be poor.
Whoever loves wine and oil will not become rich.
[18] The wicked is a ransom for the righteous,
the unfaithful for the upright.
[19] It is better to live in a desert land
than with a quarrelsome and irritable woman.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9 and 19: Said with humor: Better to live alone in a small corner on the flat roof, or even in the desert, than in a house full of quarrels. The proverbs are written for men. The same is true for women with a quarrelsome husband.
> If you experience violence in your own relationship, you can get help day and night. US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 12: “The Righteous One” can mean God himself here, the one who is righteous above all. Our English source, the World English Bible, writes it with capital letters and understands it this way.
> Verse 13: Whoever ignores the need of the poor will one day stand without help himself.
> Verse 14: An observation of how bribery works. It is not advice.
> Verse 18: A difficult verse: The disaster that would really hit the righteous hits the wicked in the end.

## Victory comes from the LORD (Verses 20–31)

[20] Precious treasure and oil are in the home of the wise,
but a foolish person swallows it up.
[21] Whoever chases after righteousness and kindness
finds life, righteousness, and honor.
[22] A wise person climbs into the city of the strong
and tears down the fortress it trusts in.
[23] Whoever guards his mouth and his tongue
keeps his life safe from troubles.
[24] The proud and arrogant person, “Mocker” is his name,
acts with overflowing pride.
[25] The craving of the lazy person kills him,
for his hands refuse to work.
[26] Some people greedily want more all day long,
but the righteous give and do not hold back.
[27] The sacrifice of the wicked is disgusting.
How much more when he brings it with evil intentions!
[28] A false witness will perish.
A person who listens will speak forever.
[29] A wicked person makes his face hard,
but the upright makes his ways firm.
[30] There is no wisdom, no understanding and no advice
against the LORD.
[31] The horse is made ready for the day of battle,
but victory comes from the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: The wise person saves and plans ahead. The fool uses everything up at once.
> Verse 22: Wisdom is stronger than walls and weapons.
> Verse 26: The greedy person always wants more. The righteous person likes to give.
> Verse 28: Whoever listens well, his word lasts.
> Verses 30–31: People can prepare well and should do so. But the result is in God’s hand. No human plan can stand against God.
