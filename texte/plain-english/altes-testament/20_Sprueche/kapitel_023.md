# Proverbs – Chapter 23
### Give me your heart

---

## At the table of a powerful man (Verses 1–8)

[1] When you sit down to eat with a ruler,
think carefully about what is in front of you.
[2] If you are a greedy eater,
put a knife to your throat.
[3] Don’t long for his fine food.
That food can fool you.
[4] Don’t wear yourself out trying to get rich.
Be wise and hold back.
[5] Why do you look at something that will soon be gone?
Riches surely grow wings like an eagle
and fly away into the sky.
[6] Don’t eat the food of a stingy man.
Don’t long for his fine food.
[7] In his heart he is always counting the cost.
“Eat and drink!” he says to you.
But he does not really mean it.
[8] You will throw up the bites you have eaten.
And your kind words will be wasted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: When a powerful person invites you, be careful. Maybe he wants to bribe you with good food or get information out of you. “Putting a knife to your throat” is a strong picture for: Control yourself!
> Verses 4–5: Wise advice: Riches do not last. They can fly away like an eagle. That is why we should not spend our whole life chasing them.
> Verses 6–8: A stingy person invites you and says in a friendly way, “Eat and drink!” But in his thoughts he counts every bite. With someone like that, no food tastes good.

## The strong Defender of orphans (Verses 9–14)

[9] Don’t talk to a fool.
He will look down on your wise words.
[10] Don’t move an old boundary stone.
Don’t take over the fields of orphans.
[11] Their Defender is strong.
He will take their side against you in court.
[12] Open your heart to teaching.
Open your ears to words of knowledge.
[13] Don’t hold back correction from a child.
If you punish him with a rod, he will not die.
[14] Punish him with a rod,
and you will save his life from Sheol, the world of the dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: Orphans had no one to defend them. But God himself is their strong Defender. Whoever takes something away from them will have to deal with him.
> Verses 13–14: These verses reflect the way children were brought up back then, when hitting was normal. Their message is: Parents should not leave children to themselves, but bring them up with love and clear limits, so that they do not get onto a path that leads to ruin.
> Today we know that hitting harms children in body and soul. In Germany, children have had a right to be brought up without violence since the year 2000. This verse is no permission to hit children.
> Help for children and young people: In the US, call Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111. Parents who need support can also call Childhelp, or in the UK the NSPCC at 0808 800 5000.

## My son, if your heart is wise (Verses 15–25)

[15] My son, if your heart is wise,
then my heart will be glad too.
[16] Deep inside I will be full of joy
when you say what is right.
[17] Don’t let your heart envy sinners.
Instead, fear the LORD all day long.
[18] Then you will surely have a future.
Your hope will not be destroyed.
[19] Listen, my son, and be wise.
Keep your heart on the right path.
[20] Don’t spend time with people who drink too much wine,
or with people who stuff themselves with meat.
[21] Drunkards and people who eat too much will become poor.
Too much sleep will dress them in rags.
[22] Listen to your father, who gave you life.
Don’t look down on your mother when she is old.
[23] Buy the truth, and don’t sell it.
Get wisdom, discipline, and understanding.
[24] The father of a righteous child is full of joy.
A man who has a wise child is happy because of him.
[25] Let your father and your mother be glad.
Let your mother, who gave birth to you, be full of joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: Sometimes people who do evil seem to be doing well. Don’t envy them! Whoever trusts God has a future and a hope.
> Verses 20–21: Too much alcohol and too much food make people poor and sluggish.
> Verse 22: A beautiful verse: Don’t despise your mother when she is old. Old parents deserve respect and care.
> Verse 23: “Buy the truth, and don’t sell it”: Truth is so valuable that you should give everything for it. And you should not give it up for anything in the world.

## Give me your heart (Verses 26–28)

[26] My son, give me your heart.
Let your eyes follow my ways.
[27] A prostitute is like a deep pit.
A woman who is unfaithful to her husband is like a narrow well.
[28] She lies in wait like a robber.
She makes more and more men unfaithful.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 26: “Give me your heart”: The father does not only want obedience, but trust and love. Many people also hear the voice of God in this sentence.
> Verses 27–28: Again the warning against unfaithfulness. Whoever falls into this “pit” has a hard time getting out again.

## Who has woe? (Verses 29–35)

[29] Who has pain? Who has sorrow?
Who has fights? Who has complaints?
Who has bruises for no reason?
Who has red eyes?
[30] People who sit for a long time over their wine,
people who go to try out mixed wine.
[31] Don’t look at the wine when it is red,
when it sparkles in the cup,
when it goes down smoothly.
[32] In the end it bites like a snake.
It poisons like a viper.
[33] Your eyes will see strange things.
Your mind will think up confused things.
[34] You will be like someone who lies down in the middle of the sea,
like someone who lies on top of a ship’s mast.
[35] “They hit me, but I was not hurt!
They beat me, but I don’t feel it!
When will I wake up?
I can do it again. I will look for more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an amazingly exact description of alcohol addiction, almost 3,000 years old.
> Verses 29–30: Whoever drinks too much has quarrels, injuries that he cannot explain, and red eyes.
> Verses 31–32: At the beginning, wine seems beautiful and pleasant. But in the end it bites like a snake.
> Verses 33–34: Drunk people see things that are not there. They feel dizzy, as on a rocking ship.
> Verse 35: The saddest thing: The drunk person does not even notice that he was hurt. And as soon as he wakes up, he wants to drink again. That is addiction.
> If you or someone in your family is struggling with alcohol: Addiction is an illness, and there is help. US: SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357. Alcoholics Anonymous and local addiction counseling services also help.
