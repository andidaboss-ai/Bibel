# Proverbs – Chapter 27
### Iron sharpens iron

---

## Don’t boast about tomorrow (Verses 1–6)

[1] Don’t boast about tomorrow,
for you don’t know what a day may bring.
[2] Let someone else praise you, and not your own mouth,
a stranger, and not your own lips.
[3] A stone is heavy, and sand is a burden,
but the trouble a fool causes is heavier than both.
[4] Anger is cruel, and rage is overwhelming,
but who can stand before jealousy?
[5] Better open correction
than hidden love.
[6] The wounds a friend gives are faithful,
but the kisses of an enemy are many.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: No one knows what tomorrow will bring. That is why we should not boast about what we will do tomorrow. James says the same thing (James 4:13–15).
> Verse 2: Praising yourself makes a bad impression. It is better when others praise you.
> Verse 4: Jealousy is even more destructive than anger.
> Verses 5–6: A real friend also tells you unpleasant truths, even when it hurts. A false friend only flatters you. Judas betrayed Jesus with a kiss (Matthew 26:48–49).

## A neighbor nearby (Verses 7–14)

[7] A full soul refuses even honey,
but to a hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.
[8] Like a bird that flutters away from its nest,
so is a person who wanders away from his home.
[9] Perfume and incense make the heart glad,
and so does the honest advice of a friend.
[10] Don’t leave your friend and your father’s friend!
Don’t go to your brother’s house on the day of your disaster.
A neighbor who is near is better than a brother who is far away.
[11] Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad,
then I can answer the one who mocks me.
[12] A wise person sees danger and takes shelter,
but the simple go on and suffer for it.
[13] Take his coat when he guarantees a loan for a stranger!
Hold it as security for an unfaithful woman!
[14] Whoever blesses his neighbor with a loud voice early in the morning,
it will be counted as a curse to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Whoever is full does not even value the best things. Whoever is hungry is glad about everything. Thankfulness often depends on whether you know what it means to lack something.
> Verse 8: Whoever has to leave his home is like a bird without a nest. Many refugees know this feeling.
> Verse 10: Old friendships are precious. And in times of need, a neighbor often helps faster than relatives who live far away.
> Verses 12–13 repeat Proverbs 22:3 and 20:16.
> Verse 14: A funny proverb: Whoever shouts a blessing loudly early in the morning, while the neighbor still wants to sleep, does not make himself popular. The right moment matters.

## Iron sharpens iron (Verses 15–22)

[15] A constant dripping on a rainy day
and a quarrelsome wife are alike:
[16] holding her back is like holding back the wind
or like grabbing oil with your right hand.
[17] Iron sharpens iron,
so a person sharpens his friend’s face.
[18] Whoever takes care of the fig tree will eat its fruit.
Whoever looks after his master will be honored.
[19] As water reflects a face,
so a person’s heart reflects the person.
[20] Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied,
and a person’s eyes are never satisfied.
[21] The melting pot is for silver and the furnace for gold,
but a person is tested by the praise he receives.
[22] Even if you crush a fool in a mortar with a pestle
together with the grain,
his foolishness will not leave him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–16: Like a leaking roof on a rainy day. This is true for quarrelsome partners, whether men or women.
> Verse 17 is a famous proverb: As two pieces of iron sharpen each other, so good friends make each other better, through conversations, discussions and also disagreement.
> Verse 19: When you look into water, you see your face. When you look into your heart, you see who you really are.
> Verse 20: People always want more. Their eyes are never satisfied. That is a fitting description of greed and consumerism.
> Verse 21: How someone deals with praise shows his character. Does he become proud, or does he stay humble?
> Verse 22: Some people never change, no matter what happens.

## Take good care of your flock (Verses 23–27)

[23] Know well the condition of your sheep,
and pay attention to your herds,
[24] for riches do not last forever,
and the crown does not last for all generations.
[25] The hay is brought in, and the new grass appears,
and the plants of the hills are gathered in.
[26] The lambs are for your clothing,
and the goats are the price of a field.
[27] There will be plenty of goats’ milk as food for you,
as food for your family,
and to feed your servant girls.

> **What does this mean?**
> A little song about good farming: Take good care of what has been entrusted to you. Then you will be provided for.
> Verse 24: Money and power do not last. But whoever works carefully and takes care of living things has a new harvest every year.
> Today we can apply this to everything that has been entrusted to us: work, family, employees, creation.
