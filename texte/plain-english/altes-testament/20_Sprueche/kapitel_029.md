# Proverbs – Chapter 29
### The fear of people is a trap

---

## Righteous rulers (Verses 1–7)

[1] Whoever is often corrected and still makes his neck stiff
will be broken suddenly, and there is no healing.
[2] When the righteous do well, the people are glad,
but when the wicked rule, the people groan.
[3] Whoever loves wisdom makes his father glad,
but whoever spends time with prostitutes wastes his wealth.
[4] The king makes the land stable by justice,
but whoever takes bribes tears it down.
[5] A person who flatters his neighbor
spreads a net for his feet.
[6] An evil person is trapped by his sin,
but the righteous can sing and be glad.
[7] The righteous care about justice for the poor.
The wicked do not care about knowledge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Whoever is warned again and again and still stays stubborn will one day fall hard.
> Verse 2: Good governments make a people glad. Bad governments make them groan.
> Verse 4: Corruption destroys a country. Justice gives it stability.
> Verse 7: One mark of righteous people: They work to make sure that the poor get justice.

---

## The wise person controls himself (Verses 8–14)

[8] Mockers stir up a city,
but wise people turn away anger.
[9] If a wise person goes to court with a fool,
the fool rages or mocks, and there is no peace.
[10] The bloodthirsty hate a person of integrity,
and they try to kill the upright.
[11] A fool lets out all of his anger,
but a wise person keeps himself under control.
[12] If a ruler listens to lies,
all his officials become wicked.
[13] The poor person and the oppressor have this in common:
The LORD gives light to the eyes of both.
[14] The king who judges the poor fairly,
his throne will stand forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Whoever spreads hate stirs up a whole city. Wise people calm things down and bring people together.
> Verse 11: Simply letting out your anger is not wise. Whoever controls himself is wise.
> Verse 12: When a boss or ruler likes to hear lies, everyone around him becomes a liar.
> Verse 13: The oppressor and the oppressed have the same eyesight from God. Both are God’s creatures. This gives the poor person his dignity and reminds the powerful of his responsibility.

---

## Bringing up children and revelation (Verses 15–21)

[15] The rod of correction gives wisdom,
but a child left to himself brings shame to his mother.
[16] When the wicked increase, sin increases,
but the righteous will see their downfall.
[17] Correct your son, and he will give you peace.
Yes, he will bring joy to your soul.
[18] Where there is no revelation, the people throw off all limits,
but happy is the one who keeps the law.
[19] A servant cannot be corrected by words.
Even if he understands, he will not respond.
[20] Do you see a person who is hasty with his words?
There is more hope for a fool than for him.
[21] Whoever spoils his servant from his youth
will have him become like a son in the end.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15 and 17: Children need to be brought up and need limits. A child who is completely left to himself is lost. The “rod” was normal back then. Today we know: Good parenting works without hitting. What matters is loving consistency.
> Verse 18: A well-known verse: When a people no longer hears God’s word, it loses its measure and its direction. Where God’s teaching counts, there is something to hold on to.
> Verses 19 and 21 describe the relationship with servants as it was normal back then. Slavery and oppression are wrong. In many places, the Bible tells how God sets people free from slavery.
> Verse 20: Whoever speaks before he thinks is even worse off than a fool.

---

## Whoever trusts in the LORD is safe (Verses 22–27)

[22] An angry person stirs up quarrels,
and a furious person commits many sins.
[23] A person’s pride brings him low,
but whoever is humble gains honor.
[24] Whoever is the partner of a thief is an enemy of his own life.
He takes an oath, but does not dare to testify.
[25] The fear of people is a trap,
but whoever trusts in the LORD is kept safe.
[26] Many seek the favor of the ruler,
but a person’s justice comes from the LORD.
[27] A dishonest person hates the righteous,
and those who are upright in their ways hate the wicked.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Whoever takes part in a theft and then keeps silent in court, even though he would have to testify under oath, harms himself.
> Verse 25 is a freeing sentence: Whoever is always afraid of what others think is caught as in a trap. Whoever trusts in God becomes free.
> Verse 26: Many try to look good in the eyes of the powerful. But in the end, justice comes from God.
> This ends the proverbs that the men of Hezekiah collected. Next come the words of Agur (Chapter 30) and Lemuel (Chapter 31).
