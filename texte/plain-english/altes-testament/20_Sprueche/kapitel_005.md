# Proverbs – Chapter 5
### Stay faithful to your wife

---

## Sweet as honey, bitter as wormwood (Verses 1–14)

[1] My son, pay attention to my wisdom!
Turn your ear to my understanding,
[2] so that you keep good judgment
and your lips protect knowledge.
[3] For the lips of an adulteress drip honey.
Her mouth is smoother than oil.
[4] But in the end she is as bitter as wormwood
and as sharp as a two-edged sword.
[5] Her feet go down to death.
Her steps lead straight to Sheol (the world of the dead).
[6] She does not think about the way of life.
Her ways are crooked, and she does not know it.
[7] So now, my sons, listen to me!
Don’t turn away from the words of my mouth!
[8] Keep your way far from her!
Don’t come near the door of her house,
[9] so that you don’t give your honor to others
and your years to the cruel one,
[10] so that strangers don’t feast on your wealth
and your hard work does not make another man’s house rich.
[11] You will groan at the end,
when your flesh and your body are worn out,
[12] and you will say, “How I hated instruction,
and my heart despised correction!
[13] I did not obey the voice of my teachers.
I did not turn my ear to those who taught me!
[14] I have almost come to complete ruin,
in the middle of the gathered assembly.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The father warns his son against adultery. At the beginning, the temptation seems sweet as honey. But in the end it is as bitter as wormwood, a very bitter plant.
> Verses 9–10: Whoever cheats loses a lot: honor, years of life, money. Back then, adultery could cost heavy punishments and payments of compensation.
> Verses 12–14: In the end, the man is sorry: If only I had listened to my teachers!
> As in Chapter 2: The book speaks to young men. The warning against unfaithfulness is for everyone, for men and for women.

## Drink from your own well (Verses 15–23)

[15] Drink water out of your own cistern,
running water out of your own well!
[16] Should your springs overflow into the streets,
streams of water into the public squares?
[17] Let them belong to you alone,
not to strangers with you.
[18] Let your spring be blessed!
Be happy with the wife of your youth,
[19] a loving doe, a graceful deer!
Let her breasts satisfy you at all times.
Let yourself always be captivated by her love.
[20] For why, my son, should you be captivated by an adulteress?
Why should you embrace the breast of another woman?
[21] For a person’s ways are before the LORD’s eyes.
He examines all his paths.
[22] The evil deeds of the wicked catch him.
The ropes of his sin hold him tight.
[23] He will die because he has no instruction.
In the greatness of his foolishness, he will go astray.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the Bible speaks openly and positively about love and sexuality in marriage.
> Verses 15–17: Your “own well” is a picture for your own wife. In a dry land, having your own well was a great treasure. You do not share it with strangers.
> Verses 18–19: Be happy with your wife! Let yourself be captivated by her love again and again, even after many years. The Bible sees physical love in marriage as something beautiful and good (compare the Song of Songs).
> Verse 21: God sees everything, even what happens in secret.
> Verse 22: Sin is like a rope that you get caught in yourself.
