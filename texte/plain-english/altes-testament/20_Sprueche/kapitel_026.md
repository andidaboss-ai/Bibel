# Proverbs – Chapter 26
### About fools, lazy people and liars

---

## How to deal with fools (Verses 1–12)

[1] Like snow in summer and like rain at harvest time,
so honor does not fit a fool.
[2] Like a fluttering sparrow, like a darting swallow,
so a curse that is not deserved does not land.
[3] A whip for the horse, a bridle for the donkey,
and a rod for the back of fools!
[4] Don’t answer a fool according to his foolishness,
or you will become like him.
[5] Answer a fool according to his foolishness,
or he will think he is wise.
[6] Whoever sends a message by the hand of a fool
is cutting off his own feet and drinking violence.
[7] Like the legs of the lame that hang down limp,
so is a proverb in the mouth of fools.
[8] Like someone who ties a stone into a sling,
so is the one who gives honor to a fool.
[9] Like a thorn bush that gets into the hand of a drunk person,
so is a proverb in the mouth of fools.
[10] Like an archer who wounds everyone,
so is the one who hires a fool or hires those who just happen to pass by.
[11] Like a dog that goes back to its vomit,
so is a fool who repeats his foolishness.
[12] Do you see a person who is wise in his own eyes?
There is more hope for a fool than for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: A comforting sentence: When someone curses you without a reason, it has no power over you. The curse flies past like a bird.
> Verse 3: A comparison from that time. It means: Whoever does not listen to words must feel the consequences. It is not a call to violence.
> Verses 4–5 seem to contradict each other. That is on purpose! Sometimes it is better not to respond to foolish talk at all, so that you don’t become foolish yourself. Sometimes you have to disagree, so that the other person does not think he is right. Wisdom means: seeing what is right in which situation.
> Verses 7 and 9: A fool may be able to repeat wise sayings, but he does not understand them and uses them wrongly.
> Verse 11: A strong picture: The fool makes the same mistakes again and again. Peter quotes this verse (2 Peter 2:22).
> Verse 12: Whoever thinks he is wise and does not want to learn anymore is even worse off than a fool.

## The lazy person and his excuses (Verses 13–16)

[13] The lazy person says, “There is a lion in the road!
A fierce lion is roaming the streets!”
[14] As the door turns on its hinges,
so the lazy person turns on his bed.
[15] The lazy person puts his hand in the dish.
He is too lazy to bring it back to his mouth.
[16] The lazy person is wiser in his own eyes
than seven people who answer with good sense.

> **What does this mean?**
> Four funny proverbs about the lazy person:
> He makes up excuses (verse 13). He turns back and forth in bed like a door on its hinges, but he does not get anywhere (verse 14). He is even too lazy to eat (verse 15). And still he thinks he is smarter than everyone else (verse 16).

## Quarrels, gossip and lies (Verses 17–28)

[17] Like someone who grabs a dog by the ears
is someone who passes by and meddles in a quarrel that is not his own.
[18] Like a madman who shoots burning arrows, arrows, and death,
[19] so is the man who deceives his neighbor
and says, “Wasn’t I only joking?”
[20] When there is no wood, the fire goes out.
Without gossip, a quarrel dies down.
[21] As coals are to hot embers and wood to fire,
so is a quarrelsome person for starting quarrels.
[22] The words of a gossip are like tasty treats.
They go down into the innermost being.
[23] Like silver waste coated on a clay pot,
so are burning lips with an evil heart.
[24] A spiteful person hides what he is with his lips,
but in his heart he carries deceit.
[25] When his speech is charming, don’t believe him,
for there are seven disgusting things in his heart.
[26] His spite may be hidden by deception,
but his wickedness will be uncovered before the assembly.
[27] Whoever digs a pit will fall into it.
Whoever rolls a stone, it will roll back on him.
[28] A lying tongue hates those it hurts,
and a flattering mouth causes ruin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Whoever grabs a strange dog by the ears gets bitten. Whoever meddles in someone else’s quarrel gets into trouble himself.
> Verses 18–19: Whoever hurts others and then says, “Wasn’t I only joking?” is as dangerous as someone who shoots arrows wildly in all directions. This is also true for bullying that is disguised as a joke.
> Verses 20–21: Gossip is like wood for a fire. Without gossip, the quarrel goes out.
> Verse 23: A cheap clay pot coated with a silver shine looks valuable, but it is not. That is what beautiful words from an evil heart are like.
> Verse 27: “Whoever digs a pit will fall into it.” This is where a well-known saying comes from: Whoever digs a pit for others will fall into it himself.
