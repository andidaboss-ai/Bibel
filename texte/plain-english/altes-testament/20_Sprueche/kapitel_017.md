# Proverbs – Chapter 17
### A friend loves at all times

---

## Better dry bread in peace (Verses 1–6)

[1] Better a dry piece of bread with quietness
than a house full of feasting with quarrels.
[2] A servant who acts wisely will rule over a son who brings shame,
and will get a share of the inheritance among the brothers.
[3] The melting pot is for silver and the furnace for gold,
but the LORD tests the hearts.
[4] An evildoer listens to wicked lips.
A liar lends his ear to a harmful tongue.
[5] Whoever mocks the poor insults his Maker.
Whoever is glad about a disaster will not go unpunished.
[6] Grandchildren are the crown of the old,
and the pride of children is their parents.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Peace is more important than luxury (compare Proverbs 15:17).
> Verse 2: Wisdom counts for more than family background. A wise servant can get further than a son who is good for nothing.
> Verse 3: Metal is tested in the fire to see if it is real. In the same way, God tests what is in our hearts.
> Verse 5: Whoever laughs at the poor, or is glad when things go badly for others, insults God, who created every person.
> Verse 6: Grandparents are happy about their grandchildren, and children are proud of their parents. A beautiful picture of several generations.

---

## Love covers wrongs (Verses 7–14)

[7] Fine speech does not fit a fool,
much less do lying lips fit a prince.
[8] A bribe is a precious stone in the eyes of the one who gives it.
Wherever he turns, he succeeds.
[9] Whoever covers a wrong encourages love,
but whoever keeps bringing a matter up separates best friends.
[10] A correction goes deeper into a person with understanding
than 100 blows into a fool.
[11] An evil person looks only for rebellion.
So a cruel messenger will be sent against him.
[12] Better to meet a mother bear robbed of her cubs
than a fool in his foolishness.
[13] Whoever pays back evil for good,
evil will not leave his house.
[14] The beginning of a quarrel is like a dam breaking.
So stop the argument before the quarrel breaks out.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: A sober observation: Whoever bribes thinks that he can get anything with money. The proverb does not praise this. In other places, bribery is clearly condemned (verse 23).
> Verse 9: Whoever forgives a friend for a mistake and does not keep bringing it up keeps the friendship.
> Verse 10: A wise person learns from a single word. A fool does not even learn from hard consequences.
> Verse 12: A funny, strong comparison: An angry mother bear is less dangerous than a fool who is doing something stupid.
> Verse 14: A quarrel is like a small hole in a dam. At the beginning, you can still plug it. Later everything breaks through. So: Stop arguing early!

## A friend loves at all times (Verses 15–22)

[15] Whoever lets the guilty go free and whoever condemns the righteous,
both of them are disgusting to the LORD.
[16] Why does a fool have money in his hand to buy wisdom,
when he has no sense?
[17] A friend loves at all times,
and a brother is born for times of trouble.
[18] A person without sense shakes hands on a deal
and guarantees a loan in front of his neighbor.
[19] Whoever loves disobedience loves quarrels.
Whoever builds his gate high is asking for destruction.
[20] Whoever has a twisted heart does not find good things,
and whoever has a lying tongue falls into trouble.
[21] Whoever has a fool for a son has grief.
The father of a fool has no joy.
[22] A cheerful heart is good medicine,
but a crushed spirit dries up the bones.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: A sentence for every court: Condemning the innocent and letting the guilty go free are equally bad before God.
> Verse 16: You cannot buy wisdom if you do not want to learn.
> Verse 17 is a wonderful sentence about friendship: A real friend is always there, not only in good times. And brothers and sisters are there for each other especially in times of need.
> Verse 19: Whoever “builds his gate high” shows off and puts himself above others. That leads to a fall.
> Verse 22: “A cheerful heart is good medicine”: Medicine knows today that joy and laughter are good for your health.

---

## Keeping silent is wise (Verses 23–28)

[23] A wicked person secretly accepts a bribe
to twist the ways of justice.
[24] The person with understanding keeps wisdom before his eyes,
but the eyes of a fool wander to the ends of the earth.
[25] A foolish son brings grief to his father
and bitterness to the mother who gave birth to him.
[26] It is also not good to punish the righteous,
or to whip officials for being honest.
[27] Whoever holds back his words has knowledge.
Whoever has a calm spirit is a man of understanding.
[28] Even a fool is thought wise when he keeps silent.
When he shuts his lips, people think he has understanding.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Bribery twists justice. That is corruption.
> Verse 24: The wise person looks at what is in front of him. The fool dreams of faraway things and does not see what is close by.
> Verse 26: Whoever is honest must not be punished for it. This is also true for people who uncover wrongdoing.
> Verse 28: A funny ending: Whoever keeps silent seems wise, even if he may not be. Sometimes it is better to say nothing.
