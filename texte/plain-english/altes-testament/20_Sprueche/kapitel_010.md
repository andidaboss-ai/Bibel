# Proverbs – Chapter 10
### The proverbs of Solomon begin

---

## Hard work and righteousness (Verses 1–7)

[1] The proverbs of Solomon.
A wise son makes his father glad,
but a foolish son brings grief to his mother.
[2] Treasures gained by wickedness are no use,
but righteousness saves from death.
[3] The LORD will not let the soul of the righteous go hungry,
but he pushes away the craving of the wicked.
[4] Whoever works with a lazy hand becomes poor,
but the hand of the hard worker brings wealth.
[5] Whoever gathers in summer is a wise son,
but whoever sleeps during the harvest is a son who brings shame.
[6] Blessings are on the head of the righteous,
but violence covers the mouth of the wicked.
[7] The memory of the righteous is a blessing,
but the name of the wicked will rot.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the second big part of the book begins: the short proverbs of Solomon (Proverbs 10:1 to 22:16).
> Almost every proverb has two lines. Often the second line shows the opposite of the first, like this: “The wise … but the fool …” In this way, you learn by comparing.
> The proverbs mostly stand alone. You can read each one by itself and think about it. The headings in this edition only give a rough summary.
> Verse 1: What children do affects their parents too. Here father and mother are named.
> Verses 4–5: Hard work usually pays off. But as the introduction already said: Proverbs describe what is normal, not every single case. Not every poor person is lazy.
> Verse 7: Good people are remembered well: “The memory of the righteous is a blessing.” To this day, Jews say about someone who has died: May his memory be a blessing.

## The power of words (Verses 8–21)

[8] Whoever is wise in heart accepts commandments,
but a chattering fool will fall.
[9] Whoever lives blamelessly walks safely,
but whoever goes crooked ways will be found out.
[10] Whoever winks with the eye causes sorrow,
but a chattering fool will fall.
[11] The mouth of the righteous is a spring of life,
but violence covers the mouth of the wicked.
[12] Hatred stirs up quarrels,
but love covers all wrongs.
[13] Wisdom is found on the lips of the one who has understanding,
but a rod is for the back of the one who has no sense.
[14] Wise people store up knowledge,
but the mouth of the fool is close to ruin.
[15] The rich man’s wealth is his strong city.
The ruin of the poor is their poverty.
[16] The work of the righteous leads to life.
The gain of the wicked leads to sin.
[17] Whoever pays attention to correction is on the way of life,
but whoever rejects correction leads others astray.
[18] Whoever hides hatred has lying lips.
Whoever spreads slander is a fool.
[19] Where there are many words, there is no lack of disobedience,
but whoever holds back his lips acts wisely.
[20] The tongue of the righteous is like the best silver.
The heart of the wicked is worth little.
[21] The lips of the righteous feed many,
but fools die for lack of understanding.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many proverbs are about our words. Words can give life (verse 11) or cause harm.
> Verse 12: A famous sentence: “Love covers all wrongs.” Whoever loves does not expose the mistakes of others, but forgives. The New Testament quotes this sentence (1 Peter 4:8; compare James 5:20).
> Verse 13: Back then, the “rod” was a normal way to bring up children. Today we know: Violence against children does harm, and in many countries, including Germany, it is against the law. The message of the proverb stays: Whoever does not want to listen must bear the consequences.
> Verse 15: A sober look: Money protects. Poverty makes people vulnerable. This is an observation, not an excuse. In other places, the book calls for helping the poor.
> Verse 19: Whoever talks a lot also says a lot that is wrong. Sometimes silence is wiser.

## The blessing of the LORD (Verses 22–32)

[22] The LORD’s blessing brings wealth,
and he adds no trouble to it.
[23] For a fool it is fun to do wickedness,
but for a man of understanding wisdom is a joy.
[24] What the wicked fear will catch up with them,
but the wish of the righteous will be granted.
[25] When the whirlwind passes, the wicked is gone,
but the righteous stand firm forever.
[26] Like vinegar to the teeth and like smoke to the eyes,
so is the lazy person to those who send him.
[27] The fear of the LORD makes life longer,
but the years of the wicked will be shortened.
[28] What the righteous look forward to is joy,
but the hope of the wicked will come to nothing.
[29] The way of the LORD is a fortress for the upright,
but ruin for those who do wrong.
[30] The righteous will never be shaken,
but the wicked will not live in the land.
[31] The mouth of the righteous brings forth wisdom,
but the twisted tongue will be cut off.
[32] The lips of the righteous know what is pleasing,
but the mouth of the wicked is twisted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: What God gives does not bring sorrow with it. Wealth gained through wrongdoing, on the other hand, brings worries.
> Verse 25: When a storm comes, it becomes clear who stands on firm ground. Jesus tells a similar parable about the house on the rock (Matthew 7:24–27).
> Verse 26: A funny comparison: A lazy messenger is as unpleasant as vinegar on your teeth or smoke in your eyes.
> Verse 27: This is a general experience, not a guarantee. Good people sometimes die young too. The book of Job and Ecclesiastes speak openly about this.
