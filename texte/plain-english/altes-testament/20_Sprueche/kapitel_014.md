# Proverbs – Chapter 14
### Righteousness lifts up a nation

---

## The wise woman builds her house (Verses 1–9)

[1] Every wise woman builds her house,
but the foolish one tears it down with her own hands.
[2] Whoever lives in his uprightness fears the LORD,
but whoever goes crooked ways despises him.
[3] The fool’s talk brings a rod to his back,
but the lips of the wise protect them.
[4] Where there are no oxen, the feeding trough is clean,
but a rich harvest comes through the strength of the ox.
[5] A truthful witness does not lie,
but a false witness pours out lies.
[6] A mocker looks for wisdom and does not find it,
but knowledge comes easily to a person with understanding.
[7] Stay away from a foolish person,
for you will not find knowledge on his lips.
[8] The wisdom of the wise is to think about his way,
but the foolishness of fools is deceit.
[9] Fools mock at making atonement for sins,
but among the upright there is goodwill.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A wise woman builds up her house: her family, her home, her community. Foolishness, on the other hand, destroys what you have. That is true for every person.
> Verse 4: A picture from farming: Without oxen, the barn stays clean, but you also have no harvest. Whoever wants to achieve something must also accept work and mess.
> Verse 6: Whoever mocks cannot learn. Whoever is open learns easily.

## The heart knows its own sorrow (Verses 10–17)

[10] The heart knows its own bitterness and joy.
A stranger cannot share them with it.
[11] The house of the wicked will be overthrown,
but the tent of the upright will flourish.
[12] There is a way that seems right to a person,
but in the end it leads to death.
[13] Even in laughter the heart may be sad,
and joy may end in sorrow.
[14] The unfaithful will be paid back for his ways,
and in the same way a good person will be rewarded for his ways.
[15] A simple person believes everything,
but the wise person thinks carefully about his steps.
[16] A wise person fears evil and stays away from it,
but the fool is hotheaded and careless.
[17] Whoever gets angry quickly does foolish things,
and a sly person is hated.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Every person has feelings that others cannot fully understand. Only God knows our heart completely.
> Verse 12: A serious sentence: Not everything that feels right is right. That is why we need God’s teaching and good advice.
> Verse 13: Some people laugh on the outside, but inside they are sad. You cannot see it by looking at them. It is worth looking closely and asking.
> Verse 15: Whoever believes everything he hears or reads is easily fooled. Today this is especially true for fake news on the internet. Check before you believe and pass it on!

## Poor and rich (Verses 18–24)

[18] The simple inherit foolishness,
but the wise are crowned with knowledge.
[19] The evil bow down before the good,
and the wicked at the gates of the righteous.
[20] The poor person is avoided even by his own neighbor,
but the rich person has many friends.
[21] Whoever looks down on his neighbor sins,
but happy is the one who has pity on the poor.
[22] Don’t those who plan evil go astray?
But love and faithfulness belong to those who plan good.
[23] In all hard work there is profit,
but mere talk leads only to poverty.
[24] The crown of the wise is their riches,
but the foolishness of fools crowns them with foolishness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–21: A sad observation: Rich people have many “friends,” poor people are avoided. But God sees it differently: Whoever looks down on the poor sins. Whoever helps them is happy.
> Verse 23: Working gets you somewhere. Only talking and making plans gets you nothing.

---

## The fear of the LORD is a spring of life (Verses 25–35)

[25] A truthful witness saves lives,
but a false witness is deceitful.
[26] In the fear of the LORD is a secure fortress,
and he will be a refuge for his children.
[27] The fear of the LORD is a spring of life,
turning people away from the traps of death.
[28] In a great number of people is the king’s glory,
but when the people are missing, it is the ruin of the prince.
[29] Whoever is slow to anger has great understanding,
but whoever has a quick temper shows foolishness.
[30] A heart at peace is life for the body,
but envy makes the bones rot.
[31] Whoever oppresses the poor shows contempt for his Maker,
but whoever is kind to the needy honors him.
[32] The wicked is brought down in his disaster,
but in death the righteous has a refuge.
[33] Wisdom rests in the heart of the one who has understanding,
and it is made known even in the inner being of fools.
[34] Righteousness lifts up a nation,
but sin is a disgrace to any people.
[35] The king shows favor to a servant who acts wisely,
but his anger falls on the one who brings shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29: Patience is a sign of wisdom. A quick temper makes you foolish.
> Verse 30: A modern thought: Inner peace is good for your health. Envy makes you sick.
> Verse 31 is one of the most important sentences of the book: Every poor person is created by God. Whoever treats a poor person badly insults God himself. Whoever helps him honors God. Jesus says something similar: “Whatever you did for one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you did for me” (Matthew 25:40).
> Verse 32: The righteous has a refuge even in death. Here we can hear a hope beyond death.
> Verse 34: “Righteousness lifts up a nation”: A country is not great because of power or wealth, but because of justice.
