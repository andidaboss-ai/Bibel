# Proverbs – Chapter 8
### Wisdom speaks

---

## Wisdom calls all people (Verses 1–11)

[1] Doesn’t wisdom call out?
Doesn’t understanding raise her voice?
[2] On top of the high places by the way,
where the paths meet, there she stands.
[3] Beside the gates, at the entrance of the city,
at the entrance doors, she calls aloud:
[4] “I call to you men!
My voice goes out to all people.
[5] You simple ones, understand good sense!
You fools, get an understanding heart!
[6] Listen, for I will speak excellent things.
What my lips say is right.
[7] For my mouth speaks truth.
Wickedness is disgusting to my lips.
[8] All the words of my mouth are righteous.
There is nothing crooked or twisted in them.
[9] They are all clear to the one who understands,
right to those who find knowledge.
[10] Receive my instruction rather than silver,
knowledge rather than the best gold!
[11] For wisdom is better than rubies.
Nothing you can wish for compares to it.

> **What does this mean?**
> As in Chapter 1, wisdom calls out in public, where everyone passes by: at crossroads and at the city gates. Her invitation is for all people.
> Her words are true, righteous and clear. They are worth more than gold and precious stones.

---

## Kings reign by me (Verses 12–21)

[12] “I, wisdom, live with good sense.
Find knowledge and good judgment!
[13] The fear of the LORD means: hating evil.
I hate pride, arrogance, the evil way, and the twisted mouth.
[14] Advice and sound knowledge are mine.
I have understanding and power.
[15] By me kings reign,
and rulers decide what is just.
[16] By me princes and nobles rule,
all the righteous rulers of the earth.
[17] I love those who love me.
Those who search for me eagerly will find me.
[18] With me are riches and honor,
lasting wealth and success.
[19] My fruit is better than gold, yes, than fine gold,
what I bring in is better than the best silver.
[20] I walk in the way of righteousness,
in the middle of the paths of justice,
[21] so that I may give wealth to those who love me.
I fill their treasuries.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Fear of God shows itself in a very practical way: You hate evil, especially pride and lies.
> Verses 15–16: Good politics needs wisdom. Rulers should govern justly. That is still true today for everyone who carries responsibility.
> Verse 17: A beautiful promise: Whoever searches for wisdom will find it.

---

## I was there when God made the world (Verses 22–31)

[22] “The LORD had me at the beginning of his work,
before his deeds of long ago.
[23] I was set up from everlasting,
from the beginning, before the earth existed.
[24] When there were no depths, I was born,
when there were no springs full of water.
[25] Before the mountains were set in their place,
before the hills, I was born,
[26] when he had not yet made the earth,
or the fields,
or the first dust of the world.
[27] When he set up the heavens, I was there.
When he drew a circle on the surface of the deep,
[28] when he made the clouds above firm,
when the springs of the deep became strong,
[29] when he gave the sea its boundary,
so that the waters would not go beyond his command,
when he marked out the foundations of the earth,
[30] then I was the master builder at his side.
Day by day I was his delight,
always rejoicing before him.
[31] I rejoiced in his whole world.
My delight was with the human race.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most beautiful texts in the book. Wisdom tells: Before there were mountains, seas and earth, I was already with God. I was there when he created everything.
> Verse 30: Wisdom was like a master builder at God’s side. Or, as the Hebrew word can also be translated: like a child who plays before God and is full of joy. Creation is full of joy.
> Verse 31: Wisdom especially likes to be with people.
> Jews often see wisdom as the Torah, God’s teaching, which was with God even before the world. Christians see in this text a pointer to Christ. The Gospel of John says: “In the beginning was the Word. And the Word was with God. … All things were made through him” (John 1:1–3). Paul calls Christ “the wisdom of God” (1 Corinthians 1:24).

## Whoever finds me finds life (Verses 32–36)

[32] “So now, my sons, listen to me,
for happy are those who keep my ways.
[33] Listen to instruction, and be wise!
Don’t refuse it!
[34] Happy is the person who listens to me,
watching at my gates every day,
waiting at the posts of my door.
[35] For whoever finds me finds life
and will receive favor from the LORD.
[36] But whoever sins against me harms his own soul.
All those who hate me love death.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 34: A picture like someone in love who waits every day in front of the door of the person they love. That is how longingly we should search for wisdom.
> Verses 35–36: It is about life or death. Whoever finds wisdom finds life. Whoever rejects it harms himself.
