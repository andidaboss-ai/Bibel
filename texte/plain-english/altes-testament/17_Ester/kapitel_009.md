# Esther – Chapter 9
### The 13th of Adar and the feast of Purim

---

## The day of decision (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then came the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month Adar.
On that day the king’s command and his order were to be carried out.
On that day the enemies of the Jews had hoped to defeat them.
But it turned out that the opposite happened:
The Jews defeated those who hated them.
[2] The Jews gathered together in their cities
in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.
They wanted to attack those who wanted to harm them.
No one could stand against them.
All the peoples had become afraid of them.
[3] All the princes of the provinces, the local governors, the governors,
and the king’s officials helped the Jews.
They had become afraid of Mordecai.
[4] Mordecai was powerful in the king’s palace.
People in all the provinces heard about him.
Mordecai became more and more powerful.

> **What does this mean?**
> The day comes. The day on which the Jews were to be destroyed. But “it turned out that the opposite happened”. That is the key word of the whole book: everything turns into its opposite.
> Important: the Jews only attack “those who wanted to harm them”. It was about self-defense against people who wanted to kill them. For Haman’s order was still valid.

---

## The fighting in Susa (Verses 5–15)

[5] The Jews struck down all their enemies with the sword.
They killed and destroyed them.
They did what they wanted with those who hated them.
[6] In the fortress of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed 500 men.
[7] They killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,
[8] Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,
[9] Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha,
[10] They were the ten sons of Haman, the son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.
But they did not touch the plunder.
[11] On that day the king was told how many people had been killed in the fortress of Susa.
[12] The king said to Queen Esther,
“The Jews have killed and destroyed 500 men in the fortress of Susa,
and also the ten sons of Haman.
What must they have done in the rest of the king’s provinces!
Now what do you wish for? I will give it to you.
What else do you ask for? It will be done.”
[13] Esther said,
“If it pleases the king,
let the Jews in Susa do the same tomorrow.
Let them act by today’s order again.
And let Haman’s ten sons be hanged on the gallows.”
[14] The king commanded that this be done.
An order was given out in Susa.
And Haman’s ten sons were hanged.
[15] The Jews in Susa gathered together again
on the fourteenth day of the month Adar.
They killed 300 men in Susa.
But they did not touch the plunder.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are hard to read. Many people die.
> We have to see the context: there were still many enemies who wanted to carry out the first order and kill the Jews. The Jews defend their lives.
> Three times it is stressed: “They did not touch the plunder.” That was allowed to them (Chapter 8:11). But they wanted to show: it was not about getting rich, but about surviving. That recalls Saul, who took plunder from the Amalekites back then and was rebuked for it (1 Samuel 15).
> The ten sons of Haman were probably already dead (verse 10). They were hanged up in public as a warning.
> Why does Esther ask for a second day in Susa? The text does not say. Maybe there were especially many enemies left in the capital. This passage is hard to understand, and many readers still find it hard today.
> Jews and Christians do not read this story as a call to revenge or violence. They read it as the story of a rescue from a planned genocide.

---

## In the provinces (Verses 16–19)

[16] The other Jews in the king’s provinces also gathered together.
They defended their lives
and got rest from their enemies.
They killed 75,000 of those who hated them.
But they did not touch the plunder.
[17] This happened on the thirteenth day of the month Adar.
On the fourteenth day of that month they rested.
They made it a day of feasting and joy.
[18] But the Jews in Susa gathered together
on the thirteenth and the fourteenth day of the month.
On the fifteenth day of that month they rested.
They made it a day of feasting and joy.
[19] That is why the Jews in the villages, who live in towns without walls,
celebrate the fourteenth day of the month Adar.
It is a day of joy and feasting, a holiday.
On this day they send gifts of food to one another.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number 75,000 is very high. The Greek version of the book has 15,000. As with many large numbers in the Bible, it is uncertain how exactly it is meant.
> “They defended their lives”: again it is stressed that it was about self-defense.
> Because there was fighting in Susa for two days, the Jews in walled cities celebrate Purim one day later. To this day Jews in Jerusalem celebrate Purim on the 15th of Adar (“Shushan Purim”) and in other places on the 14th of Adar.

---

## Mordecai establishes the feast of Purim (Verses 20–28)

[20] Mordecai wrote down these things.
He sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus,
both near and far.
[21] He told them to celebrate the fourteenth and fifteenth days of the month Adar every year.
[22] On these days the Jews had gotten rest from their enemies.
In this month their sorrow had been turned into joy,
and their mourning into a holiday.
They should make them days of feasting and joy.
They should send gifts of food to one another
and give gifts to the poor.
[23] The Jews had already begun to celebrate.
They accepted it as a custom, as Mordecai had written to them.
[24] Haman, the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews,
had made plans against the Jews to destroy them.
He had cast “Pur”, that is, the lot,
to crush them and to destroy them.
[25] But when the king learned about it,
he gave orders in letters:
The evil plan that Haman had made against the Jews
should fall back on his own head.
He and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.
[26] That is why these days were called “Purim”, after the word “Pur.”
This letter said all these things.
And the Jews had seen these things themselves and had lived through them.
[27] So the Jews made a rule for themselves,
for their descendants, and for everyone who would join them:
Every year, without fail, they would celebrate these two days,
as it was written and at the set time.
[28] These days should be remembered and celebrated
in every generation, in every family, in every province, and in every city.
These days of Purim should never stop among the Jews.
Their descendants should never forget them.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is how the feast of Purim began. “Purim” is the plural of “Pur”, lot. Haman had cast the lot to find a day for the destruction. Now this day is a day of joy.
> How is Purim celebrated? To this day just as it is written here:
> – with a feast and much joy,
> – people send each other food (“Mishloach Manot”),
> – people give gifts to the poor (“Matanot la-Evyonim”).
> In addition, the book of Esther is read aloud in the synagogue. Every time the name Haman is said, the children make noise with rattles, to drown out his name. Many people dress up in costumes.
> Important: thinking of the poor is part of the feast. Whoever has been saved should give gifts to others.
> Purim does not celebrate the death of the enemies. It celebrates the rescue, the rest and the turning of sorrow into joy.

## The second letter of Purim (Verses 29–32)

[29] Then Queen Esther, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew
wrote with full authority.
They wanted to confirm this second letter about Purim.
[30] He sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus.
They had words of peace and truth.
[31] The letters confirmed these days of Purim at their set times.
Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had ordered it.
The Jews had made it a rule for themselves and for their descendants,
together with the times of fasting and mourning.
[32] Esther’s command confirmed these rules about Purim.
It was written in the book.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther herself confirms the feast with her royal authority. She is no longer only the young orphan who does what she is told. She is a leader of her people.
> “Words of peace and truth”: the feast is meant to bring peace.
> “The times of fasting and mourning”: to this day Jews fast on the day before Purim, in memory of Esther’s fasting (the “Fast of Esther”, Chapter 4:16).
