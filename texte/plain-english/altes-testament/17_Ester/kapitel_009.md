# Esther – Chapter 9
### The 13th of Adar and the feast of Purim

---

## The day of decision (Verses 1–4)

[1] In the twelfth month, the month Adar,
on the thirteenth day of the month,
the king’s command and his decree were to be carried out.
On that day the enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them.
But it turned out that the opposite happened:
the Jews overpowered those who hated them.
[2] The Jews gathered together in their cities
throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus,
to lay hands on those who wanted to harm them.
No one could stand against them,
because the fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.
[3] All the princes of the provinces, the local governors, the governors,
and those who did the king’s business helped the Jews,
because the fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.
[4] For Mordecai was great in the king’s house,
and his fame went out throughout all the provinces.
The man Mordecai became greater and greater.

> **What does this mean?**
> The day comes. The day on which the Jews were to be destroyed. But “it turned out that the opposite happened”. That is the key word of the whole book: everything turns into its opposite.
> Important: the Jews only attack “those who wanted to harm them”. It was about self-defense against people who wanted to kill them. For Haman’s order was still valid.

---

## The fighting in Susa (Verses 5–15)

[5] The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword,
with killing and destruction,
and did what they wanted to those who hated them.
[6] In the citadel of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed 500 men.
[7] They killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,
[8] Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,
[9] Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha,
[10] the ten sons of Haman the son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.
But they didn’t lay their hand on the plunder.
[11] On that day the number of those killed in the citadel of Susa
was brought before the king.
[12] The king said to Queen Esther,
“The Jews have killed and destroyed 500 men in the citadel of Susa,
including the ten sons of Haman.
What then have they done in the rest of the king’s provinces!
Now what do you wish? It shall be given you.
What else do you ask for? It shall be done.”
[13] Then Esther said,
“If it pleases the king,
let the Jews who are in Susa be allowed
to do tomorrow also according to today’s decree,
and let Haman’s ten sons be hanged on the gallows.”
[14] The king commanded this to be done.
A decree was given out in Susa,
and they hanged Haman’s ten sons.
[15] The Jews who were in Susa gathered together
on the fourteenth day of the month Adar too,
and killed 300 men in Susa.
But they didn’t lay their hand on the plunder.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are hard to read. Many people die.
> We have to see the context: there were still many enemies who wanted to carry out the first order and kill the Jews. The Jews defend their lives.
> Three times it is stressed: “They didn’t lay their hand on the plunder.” That was allowed to them (Chapter 8:11). But they wanted to show: it was not about getting rich, but about surviving. That recalls Saul, who took plunder from the Amalekites back then and was rebuked for it (1 Samuel 15).
> The ten sons of Haman were probably already dead (verse 10). They were hanged up in public as a warning.
> Why does Esther ask for a second day in Susa? The text does not say. Maybe there were especially many enemies left in the capital. This passage is hard to understand, and many readers still find it hard today.
> Jews and Christians do not read this story as a call to revenge or violence. They read it as the story of a rescue from a planned genocide.

## In the provinces (Verses 16–19)

[16] The other Jews who were in the king’s provinces gathered together,
defended their lives, and had rest from their enemies.
They killed 75,000 of those who hated them.
But they didn’t lay their hand on the plunder.
[17] This was done on the thirteenth day of the month Adar.
And on the fourteenth day of that month they rested
and made it a day of feasting and gladness.
[18] But the Jews who were in Susa gathered together
on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth day of the month.
And on the fifteenth day of that month they rested
and made it a day of feasting and gladness.
[19] That is why the Jews of the villages, who live in the towns without walls,
make the fourteenth day of the month Adar a day of gladness and feasting,
a holiday, and a day of sending gifts of food to one another.

> **What does this mean?**
> The number 75,000 is very high. The Greek version of the book has 15,000. As with many large numbers in the Bible, it is uncertain how exactly it is meant.
> “They defended their lives”: again it is stressed that it was about self-defense.
> Because there was fighting in Susa for two days, the Jews in walled cities celebrate Purim one day later. To this day Jews in Jerusalem celebrate Purim on the 15th of Adar (“Shushan Purim”) and in other places on the 14th of Adar.

## Mordecai establishes the feast of Purim (Verses 20–28)

[20] Mordecai wrote these things down
and sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus,
both near and far,
[21] to tell them to keep the fourteenth and fifteenth days of the month Adar every year,
[22] as the days on which the Jews had rest from their enemies,
and as the month which had been turned for them from sorrow to gladness,
and from mourning into a holiday.
They should make them days of feasting and gladness,
and of sending gifts of food to one another, and gifts to the needy.
[23] The Jews accepted the custom that they had begun,
as Mordecai had written to them.
[24] For Haman the son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews,
had plotted against the Jews to destroy them,
and had cast “Pur”, that is, the lot,
to crush them and to destroy them.
[25] But when this became known to the king,
he commanded by letters
that the wicked plan which Haman had planned against the Jews
should come back on his own head,
and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.
[26] That is why they called these days “Purim”, from the word “Pur.”
So because of all the words of this letter,
and because of what they had seen concerning this matter,
and what had happened to them,
[27] the Jews made it a rule for themselves,
for their descendants, and for all who joined them,
that without fail they would keep these two days
as it was written and at its set time every year,
[28] and that these days should be remembered and kept
in every generation, every family, every province, and every city,
and that these days of Purim should never disappear among the Jews,
and that the memory of them should never die out among their descendants.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is how the feast of Purim began. “Purim” is the plural of “Pur”, lot. Haman had cast the lot to find a day for the destruction. Now this day is a day of joy.
> How is Purim celebrated? To this day just as it is written here:
> – with a feast and much joy,
> – people send each other food (“Mishloach Manot”),
> – people give gifts to the poor (“Matanot la-Evyonim”).
> In addition, the book of Esther is read aloud in the synagogue. Every time the name Haman is said, the children make noise with rattles, to drown out his name. Many people dress up in costumes.
> Important: thinking of the poor is part of the feast. Whoever has been saved should give gifts to others.
> Purim does not celebrate the death of the enemies. It celebrates the rescue, the rest and the turning of sorrow into joy.

## The second letter of Purim (Verses 29–32)

[29] Then Queen Esther, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew
wrote with full authority,
to confirm this second letter of Purim.
[30] He sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus,
with words of peace and truth,
[31] to confirm these days of Purim at their set times,
as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had ordered,
and as they had made it a rule for themselves and their descendants
in the matter of the fastings and their mourning.
[32] The command of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim,
and it was written in the book.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther herself confirms the feast with her royal authority. She is no longer only the young orphan who does what she is told. She is a leader of her people.
> “Words of peace and truth”: the feast is meant to bring peace.
> “The fastings and their mourning”: to this day Jews fast on the day before Purim, in memory of Esther’s fasting (the “Fast of Esther”, Chapter 4:16).
