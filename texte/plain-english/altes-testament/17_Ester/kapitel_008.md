# Esther – Chapter 8
### The counter-decree

---

## Mordecai receives the ring (Verses 1–2)

[1] On that day King Ahasuerus gave the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews,
to Queen Esther.
Mordecai came before the king,
for Esther had told him what Mordecai was to her.
[2] The king took off his ring, which he had taken back from Haman,
and gave it to Mordecai.
And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the king learns that Mordecai is Esther’s foster father.
> The signet ring with which Haman had sealed the order to murder now belongs to Mordecai. And all of Haman’s property goes to Esther. Everything has been turned around.

---

## Esther pleads a second time (Verses 3–8)

[3] Esther spoke once more before the king.
She fell down at his feet and begged him with tears
to put an end to the evil of Haman the Agagite,
and to the plan that he had made against the Jews.
[4] Then the king held out the golden scepter to Esther.
So Esther got up and stood before the king.
[5] She said,
“If it pleases the king,
and if I have found favor in his sight,
and the thing seems right to the king,
and I am pleasing in his eyes,
let it be written to cancel the letters made up by Haman,
the son of Hammedatha the Agagite,
which he wrote to destroy the Jews who are in all the king’s provinces.
[6] For how can I bear to see the disaster that would come to my people?
How can I bear to see the destruction of my relatives?”
[7] Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew,
“Look, I have given Esther the house of Haman,
and they have hanged him on the gallows,
because he laid his hand on the Jews.
[8] Now you write to the Jews as you think best,
in the king’s name,
and seal it with the king’s ring.
For what is written in the king’s name and sealed with the king’s ring
cannot be canceled by anyone.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Haman is dead, but his order is still valid. On the 13th of Adar all enemies may attack the Jews.
> Esther goes to the king once more, risking her life. She weeps and pleads. She does not only think of herself, but of her whole people.
> The problem: a Persian law cannot be taken back (Chapter 1:19; Daniel 6:8). Even the king cannot cancel his own order.
> The solution: a new order that works against the old one.

---

## The new decree (Verses 9–14)

[9] Then the king’s scribes were called at that time,
on the twenty-third day of the third month, the month Sivan.
Everything that Mordecai commanded was written
to the Jews, to the local governors, to the governors and the princes of the provinces
from India to Ethiopia, 127 provinces,
to every province in its own writing,
and to every people in their own language,
and to the Jews in their writing and in their language.
[10] He wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus
and sealed it with the king’s ring.
He sent letters by messengers on horseback,
riding on royal horses bred from fast horses.
[11] In these letters the king allowed the Jews in every city
to gather together and to defend their lives:
to destroy, to kill, and to wipe out all the forces of any people or province
that would attack them,
their little ones and women,
and to take their possessions as plunder,
[12] on one day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus,
on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month Adar.
[13] A copy of the letter was made public to all the peoples,
so that the decree would be given out in every province,
and the Jews would be ready on that day
to take revenge on their enemies.
[14] So the messengers who rode on royal horses went out in a hurry,
driven on by the king’s command.
And the decree was given out in the citadel of Susa.

> **What does this mean?**
> The new decree comes two months after the first one. There are still nine months until the 13th of Adar.
> The new decree is almost word for word the same as Haman’s order to murder (Chapter 3:12–14), only the other way around. Now the Jews may defend themselves.
> Important: this is about defense. The Jews may only act against those “that would attack them”.
> The words “their little ones and women” are hard to read. They repeat Haman’s words from Chapter 3:13. Chapter 9 does not say whether this was really carried out. There only killed men are mentioned. And the Jews took no plunder (Chapter 9:10, 15, 16), although it was allowed.
> “To take revenge” here means: to defend themselves against the attackers and to get justice.

## Joy instead of mourning (Verses 15–17)

[15] Mordecai went out from the presence of the king
in royal clothing of blue and white,
with a great crown of gold,
and with a robe of fine linen and purple.
And the city of Susa shouted for joy and was glad.
[16] The Jews had light, gladness, joy, and honor.
[17] In every province and in every city,
wherever the king’s command and his decree came,
the Jews had gladness and joy, a feast and a holiday.
Many from among the peoples of the land became Jews,
for the fear of the Jews had fallen on them.

> **What does this mean?**
> What a contrast to Chapter 4: there Mordecai wore sackcloth and ashes. Now he wears royal clothes. There was mourning in all the provinces. Now there is joy everywhere.
> “Light, gladness, joy, and honor”: to this day this verse is spoken in Jewish families at the end of the Sabbath.
> At the end of Chapter 3 the city of Susa was upset. Now it shouts for joy. So many people in Susa were glad that the Jews were saved.
> “Many … became Jews”: some joined the Jewish people. The text does not say exactly whether out of fear or out of conviction.
