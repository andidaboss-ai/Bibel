# Esther – Chapter 8
### The counter-decree

---

## Mordecai receives the ring (Verses 1–2)

[1] On that same day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews.
Esther had told the king how Mordecai was related to her.
So Mordecai came before the king.
[2] The king had taken back his signet ring from Haman.
Now he took it off and gave it to Mordecai.
Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman’s house.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the king learns that Mordecai is Esther’s foster father.
> The signet ring with which Haman had sealed the order to murder now belongs to Mordecai. And all of Haman’s property goes to Esther. Everything has been turned around.

---

## Esther pleads a second time (Verses 3–8)

[3] Then Esther spoke to the king once more.
She fell down at his feet.
She cried and begged him to stop the evil plan of Haman the Agagite.
Haman had made this plan against the Jews.
[4] The king held out the golden scepter to Esther.
So Esther got up and stood in front of the king.
[5] She said,
“If it pleases the king,
and if he is pleased with me,
and if the king thinks it is right,
and if he likes me,
then let an order be written.
It should cancel the letters that Haman thought up.
Haman, the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, wrote them
to destroy the Jews in all the king’s provinces.
[6] How can I stand to watch the disaster that would come on my people?
How can I stand to watch my relatives be destroyed?”
[7] Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew,
“Look, I have given Esther the house of Haman.
And Haman was hanged on the gallows,
because he attacked the Jews.
[8] Now you may also write to the Jews, as you think best.
Write it in the king’s name.
Seal it with the king’s ring.
No one can cancel a letter that is written in the king’s name
and sealed with the king’s ring.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Haman is dead, but his order is still valid. On the 13th of Adar all enemies may attack the Jews.
> Esther goes to the king once more, risking her life. She weeps and pleads. She does not only think of herself, but of her whole people.
> The problem: a Persian law cannot be taken back (Esther 1:19; Daniel 6:8). Even the king cannot cancel his own order.
> The solution: a new order that works against the old one.

---

## The new decree (Verses 9–14)

[9] So the king’s scribes were called at once.
It was the twenty-third day of the third month, the month Sivan.
They wrote down everything that Mordecai commanded.
They wrote to the Jews, to the local governors, and to the governors and princes of the provinces.
There were 127 provinces, from India to Ethiopia.
They wrote to every province in its own writing
and to every people in its own language.
They also wrote to the Jews in their own writing and their own language.
[10] He wrote the letters in the name of King Ahasuerus.
He sealed them with the king’s ring.
He sent them with messengers on horseback.
They rode fast royal horses that were bred from the best horses.
[11] In these letters the king gave the Jews in every city the right
to gather together and to defend their lives.
They were allowed to destroy, kill, and wipe out every army of any people or province
that would attack them,
together with their little ones and women.
And they were allowed to take what their enemies owned.
[12] All this was to happen on one day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus:
on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month Adar.
[13] A copy of the letter was made public to all the peoples.
The order was to be given out as law in every province.
So the Jews would be ready on that day
to take revenge on their enemies.
[14] The king commanded the messengers to hurry.
So they rode out quickly on the royal horses.
The order was also given out in the fortress of Susa.

> **What does this mean?**
> The new decree comes two months after the first one. There are still nine months until the 13th of Adar.
> The new decree is almost word for word the same as Haman’s order to murder (Esther 3:12–14), only the other way around. Now the Jews may defend themselves.
> Important: this is about defense. The Jews may only act against those “that would attack them”.
> The words “their little ones and women” are hard to read. They repeat Haman’s words from Esther 3:13. Chapter 9 does not say whether this was really carried out. There only killed men are mentioned. And the Jews took no plunder (Esther 9:10, 15, 16), although it was allowed.
> “To take revenge” here means: to defend themselves against the attackers and to get justice.

## Joy instead of mourning (Verses 15–17)

[15] Mordecai left the king.
He wore royal clothes of blue and white.
He wore a large golden crown
and a robe of fine linen and purple.
The city of Susa shouted for joy and celebrated.
[16] For the Jews it was a time of light, gladness, joy, and honor.
[17] The king’s command and his order reached every province and every city.
Wherever it came, the Jews were glad and full of joy.
They had a feast and a holiday.
Many people from the other peoples of the land became Jews.
They had become afraid of the Jews.

> **What does this mean?**
> What a contrast to Chapter 4: there Mordecai wore sackcloth and ashes. Now he wears royal clothes. There was mourning in all the provinces. Now there is joy everywhere.
> “Light, gladness, joy, and honor”: to this day this verse is spoken in Jewish families at the end of the Sabbath.
> At the end of Chapter 3 the city of Susa was upset. Now it shouts for joy. So many people in Susa were glad that the Jews were saved.
> “Many … became Jews”: some joined the Jewish people. The text does not say exactly whether out of fear or out of conviction.
