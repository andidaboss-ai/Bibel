# Esther – Chapter 6
### A sleepless night

---

## The king cannot sleep (Verses 1–3)

[1] That night the king couldn’t sleep.
He ordered the book of records, the chronicles, to be brought,
and they were read to the king.
[2] It was found written there that Mordecai had reported Bigthana and Teresh,
two of the king’s eunuchs who guarded the door,
who had tried to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.
[3] The king said,
“What honor and dignity has been given to Mordecai for this?”
The king’s servants who attended him said,
“Nothing has been done for him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the turning point of the whole story. And it begins with something very small: the king cannot sleep.
> Why exactly on this night? Why does he have exactly this book read to him? Why exactly this passage? The Bible does not say. But the reader notices: this is no coincidence. Here God is guiding things in secret.
> Jewish interpreters say: it was not only the king who could not sleep, but “the King of the world”, that is God, who “will not doze and will not sleep” (Psalm 121:4).
> Mordecai’s good deed from Chapter 2 seemed forgotten. Now, at exactly the right moment, it comes to light.

---

## Haman honors Mordecai (Verses 4–11)

[4] The king said, “Who is in the court?”
Haman had just come into the outer court of the king’s house,
to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him.
[5] The king’s servants said to him,
“Look, Haman is standing in the court.”
The king said, “Let him come in.”
[6] So Haman came in.
The king said to him,
“What shall be done for the man whom the king delights to honor?”
Haman said in his heart,
“Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?”
[7] Haman said to the king,
“For the man whom the king delights to honor,
[8] let royal clothing be brought which the king himself has worn,
and the horse that the king rides on,
on whose head a royal crown is set.
[9] Let the clothing and the horse be handed over to one of the king’s most noble princes,
so that they dress the man whom the king delights to honor,
and have him ride on horseback through the city square,
and call out before him,
‘Thus it shall be done to the man whom the king delights to honor!’”
[10] Then the king said to Haman,
“Hurry and take the clothing and the horse, as you have said,
and do this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king’s gate.
Don’t leave out anything of all that you have said.”
[11] Then Haman took the clothing and the horse
and dressed Mordecai,
and had him ride through the city square,
and called out before him,
“Thus it shall be done to the man whom the king delights to honor!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most wonderful scenes of the Bible, full of irony.
> Haman comes in the middle of the night or early in the morning, to demand Mordecai’s death. The king asks him how someone should be honored. Haman thinks that he himself is meant, and thinks up the greatest honor.
> Then the shock: he himself has to show this honor to his archenemy! He has to lead Mordecai through the city and call out: “Thus it shall be done to the man whom the king delights to honor!”
> Here we see what Jesus says later: “Everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled. And whoever humbles himself will be lifted up” (Luke 14:11).

---

## Haman’s fall begins (Verses 12–14)

[12] Mordecai came back to the king’s gate.
But Haman hurried to his house,
mourning, with his head covered.
[13] Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends everything that had happened to him.
Then his wise men and Zeresh his wife said to him,
“Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is a Jew.
If so, you will not win against him,
but you will surely fall before him.”
[14] While they were still talking with him,
the king’s eunuchs came
and hurried to bring Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai goes back quietly to his work. He does not let himself be blinded by the honor.
> Haman, on the other hand, goes home like a mourner.
> Astonishing: even Haman’s wife and his advisers now see that you cannot win against the Jewish people. They sense that a higher power is at work.
> Haman has no time to collect himself. He is fetched to the banquet at once.
