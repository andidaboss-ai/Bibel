# Esther – Chapter 6
### A sleepless night

---

## The king cannot sleep (Verses 1–3)

[1] That night the king could not sleep.
So he ordered his servants to bring the book of records, the chronicles.
They read from it to the king.
[2] There they found a report about Mordecai.
Mordecai had told about Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s eunuchs who guarded the door.
These two had tried to attack King Ahasuerus.
[3] The king asked,
“How has Mordecai been honored and rewarded for this?”
The king’s servants who served him answered,
“Nothing has been done for him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the turning point of the whole story. And it begins with something very small: the king cannot sleep.
> Why exactly on this night? Why does he have exactly this book read to him? Why exactly this passage? The Bible does not say. But the reader notices: this is no coincidence. Here God is guiding things in secret.
> Jewish interpreters say: it was not only the king who could not sleep, but “the King of the world”, that is God, who “will not doze and will not sleep” (Psalm 121:4).
> Mordecai’s good deed from Chapter 2 seemed forgotten. Now, at exactly the right moment, it comes to light.

---

## Haman honors Mordecai (Verses 4–11)

[4] The king asked, “Who is in the courtyard?”
At that moment Haman had just come into the outer courtyard of the king’s palace.
He wanted to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows that he had set up for him.
[5] The king’s servants said to him,
“Look, Haman is standing in the courtyard.”
The king said, “Let him come in.”
[6] So Haman came in.
The king asked him,
“What should be done for the man the king wants to honor?”
Haman thought to himself,
“Whom would the king want to honor more than me?”
[7] So Haman said to the king,
“This is what should be done for the man the king wants to honor:
[8] Let servants bring royal clothes that the king has worn.
Let them bring a horse that the king rides,
with a royal crown on its head.
[9] Let them give the clothes and the horse to one of the king’s most important princes.
He should dress the man the king wants to honor.
He should lead him on the horse through the city square.
And he should call out in front of him,
‘This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor!’”
[10] Then the king said to Haman,
“Hurry! Take the clothes and the horse, as you have said.
Do all this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king’s gate.
Don’t leave out anything you have said.”
[11] So Haman took the clothes and the horse.
He dressed Mordecai.
He led him on the horse through the city square.
And he called out in front of him,
“This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most wonderful scenes of the Bible, full of irony.
> Haman comes in the middle of the night or early in the morning, to demand Mordecai’s death. The king asks him how someone should be honored. Haman thinks that he himself is meant, and thinks up the greatest honor.
> Then the shock: he himself has to show this honor to his archenemy! He has to lead Mordecai through the city and call out: “This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor!”
> Here we see what Jesus says later: “Everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled. And whoever humbles himself will be lifted up” (Luke 14:11).

---

## Haman’s fall begins (Verses 12–14)

[12] Then Mordecai went back to the king’s gate.
But Haman hurried home.
He was full of grief and had covered his head.
[13] Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened to him.
Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said to him,
“You have begun to fall before Mordecai.
If he is a Jew, you will not win against him.
You will surely fall before him.”
[14] While they were still talking with him,
the king’s eunuchs arrived.
They quickly took Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai goes back quietly to his work. He does not let himself be blinded by the honor.
> Haman, on the other hand, goes home like a mourner.
> Astonishing: even Haman’s wife and his advisers now see that you cannot win against the Jewish people. They sense that a higher power is at work.
> Haman has no time to collect himself. He is fetched to the banquet at once.
