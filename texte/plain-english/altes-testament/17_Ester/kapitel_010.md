# Esther – Chapter 10
### The greatness of Mordecai

---

## Mordecai seeks the good of his people (Verses 1–3)

[1] King Ahasuerus laid a tax on the land and on the islands of the sea.
[2] All the acts of his power and of his might,
and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai,
to which the king had raised him:
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia?
[3] For Mordecai the Jew was second only to King Ahasuerus,
and great among the Jews,
and accepted by the multitude of his brothers,
seeking the good of his people
and speaking peace to all his descendants.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book ends briefly. Mordecai is now the second most powerful man in the empire, exactly where Haman stood before.
> But Mordecai is completely different from Haman. Haman sought his own honor and the death of others. Mordecai was “seeking the good of his people and speaking peace”.
> That recalls Joseph in Egypt, who also, as a foreigner, became the second man after the king and saved his people (Genesis 41:40).
> Mordecai is called “accepted by the multitude of his brothers”. Word for word it says “by the majority”. An honest hint: not everyone agreed with him. Even a good leader cannot please everyone.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Esther?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The story:** A Jewish orphan, Esther, becomes queen of Persia. Haman, the most powerful official, plans to kill all the Jews. Esther risks her life to save her people. Haman is exposed and falls into the pit he dug himself. The Jews are saved.
> **The hidden God:** In the Hebrew book of Esther, God is not named a single time. And yet he is everywhere: in the “coincidences”, in the timing of the sleepless night, in the delay of the banquet, in the lot that falls on a day eleven months later. The book shows: even when we do not see God, he is at work.
> **Everything turns around:** That is the great theme: sorrow becomes joy, fasting becomes feasting, the gallows strikes the one who built it, the enemy is brought low, the despised one is honored.
> **Courage:** Esther is an example of courage. She is afraid, but she acts: “If I perish, I perish.” And Mordecai’s question still applies to every person today: who knows if you are not in your place for such a time as this?
> **A warning against hatred:** The book shows how hatred of Jews arises: from wounded pride, from lies and from the indifference of the powerful. Sadly this warning has stayed important through all of history until today.
> **Purim:** The book explains why Jews still celebrate Purim today, with joy, gifts for friends and gifts for the poor.
> **Hard passages:** The fighting in Chapter 9 is hard to read. It is told as self-defense against a planned genocide. It is no permission for revenge.
> **What comes next?** With the book of Esther the history books of the Old Testament end. Next come the books of wisdom and poetry. The first is the book of Job, which asks the question: why do good people have to suffer?
