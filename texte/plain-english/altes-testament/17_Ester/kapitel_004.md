# Esther – Chapter 4
### “For a time like this”

---

## Mourning and weeping (Verses 1–3)

[1] Mordecai found out about everything that had been done.
He tore his clothes.
He put on sackcloth and put ashes on himself.
Sackcloth is rough cloth that people wore when they mourned.
He went out into the middle of the city.
He cried out loudly and bitterly.
[2] He went as far as the king’s gate, but no farther.
No one was allowed to go through the king’s gate dressed in sackcloth.
[3] The king’s command and his order reached every province.
Wherever it came, the Jews mourned deeply.
They fasted, cried, and wailed.
Many of them lay in sackcloth and ashes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai mourns in public. Sackcloth and ashes were signs of the deepest mourning and repentance.
> No one was allowed to appear sad before the king (compare Nehemiah 2:2). That is why Mordecai stays in front of the gate.
> In the whole empire the Jews fast and weep. In the Bible fasting was always connected with prayer. Even if God’s name is not mentioned: the people turn to God.

---

## Esther finds out (Verses 4–9)

[4] Esther’s young women and her eunuchs came and told her about it.
The queen was very upset.
She sent clothes to Mordecai for him to wear instead of his sackcloth.
But he did not accept them.
[5] Then Esther called Hathach.
He was one of the king’s eunuchs.
The king had chosen him to serve her.
She ordered him to go to Mordecai.
He was to find out what was wrong and why.
[6] So Hathach went out to Mordecai.
Mordecai was in the city square in front of the king’s gate.
[7] Mordecai told him everything that had happened to him.
He told him the exact amount of money that Haman had promised to pay into the king’s treasury
for destroying the Jews.
[8] He also gave Hathach a copy of the written order.
It had been given out in Susa, and it said to destroy the Jews.
Hathach was to show it to Esther and explain it to her.
Mordecai told him to urge her to go to the king.
She should beg the king for mercy
and plead with him for her people.
[9] Hathach came back and told Esther what Mordecai had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther did not yet know anything about the order. In the palace she was cut off from the outside world.
> At first she only wants to comfort Mordecai, with new clothes. But Mordecai does not want comfort. He wants her to act.
> Mordecai gives her the proof: the copy of the decree. And he asks her: go to the king and plead for your people!

---

## Esther’s fear (Verses 10–12)

[10] Then Esther spoke to Hathach.
She gave him a message for Mordecai:
[11] “All the king’s servants and the people in the king’s provinces know this:
There is one law for every man or woman
who goes to the king in the inner courtyard without being called.
That person must be put to death.
Only if the king holds out his golden scepter to that person will he live.
But the king has not called me to come to him for 30 days now.”
[12] Esther’s words were told to Mordecai.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther is afraid, and rightly so. Going to the king without being called could mean death.
> The king has not called her for 30 days. Maybe he has lost interest in her. That makes the situation even more dangerous.

---

## Mordecai’s famous answer (Verses 13–14)

[13] Then Mordecai sent this answer back to Esther:
“Don’t think that you will escape just because you are in the king’s palace.
You will not be safer than all the other Jews.
[14] If you stay silent now,
help and rescue will come to the Jews from another place.
But you and your father’s family will die.
Who knows? Maybe you have become queen for a time like this.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai’s answer is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible.
> He says three things:
> – Even in the palace you are not safe. You are Jewish too.
> – If you stay silent, rescue will come “from another place”. Many interpreters see this as a hidden hint at God. Mordecai is sure: God will not let his people perish.
> – “Who knows? Maybe you have become queen for a time like this.” Maybe everything Esther has gone through had a meaning. Maybe she is in this place exactly for this.
> This sentence still encourages people today: where you stand right now, you can do something good. Maybe that is exactly why you are there.

---

## Esther’s decision (Verses 15–17)

[15] Then Esther sent this answer to Mordecai:
[16] “Go and gather all the Jews who are in Susa.
Fast for me.
Don’t eat or drink anything for three days, night or day.
My young women and I will fast in the same way.
Then I will go to the king, even though it is against the law.
And if I die, I die.”
[17] So Mordecai went away.
He did everything Esther had commanded him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther makes her decision. The quiet young woman who always listened to others becomes a brave leader. Now she gives Mordecai instructions.
> She asks everyone to fast for her. In the Bible fasting always means asking God for help. Even though God is not named, it is clear: Esther seeks God’s help.
> “If I die, I die.” Esther risks her life for her people. That is true courage: you are afraid and still do what is right.
