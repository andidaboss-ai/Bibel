# Esther – Chapter 4
### “For such a time as this”

---

## Mourning and weeping (Verses 1–3)

[1] When Mordecai found out all that had been done,
he tore his clothes and put on sackcloth with ashes.
He went out into the middle of the city
and cried out loudly and bitterly.
[2] He came as far as the king’s gate,
for no one was allowed to go inside the king’s gate dressed in sackcloth.
[3] In every province, wherever the king’s command and his decree came,
there was great mourning among the Jews,
with fasting, weeping, and wailing.
And many lay in sackcloth and ashes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai mourns in public. Sackcloth and ashes were signs of the deepest mourning and repentance.
> No one was allowed to appear sad before the king (compare Nehemiah 2:2). That is why Mordecai stays in front of the gate.
> In the whole empire the Jews fast and weep. In the Bible fasting was always connected with prayer. Even if God’s name is not mentioned: the people turn to God.

---

## Esther finds out (Verses 4–9)

[4] Esther’s young women and her eunuchs came and told her this,
and the queen was very upset.
She sent clothing to Mordecai, to put on instead of his sackcloth,
but he didn’t accept it.
[5] Then Esther called for Hathach,
one of the king’s eunuchs, whom the king had appointed to serve her.
She ordered him to go to Mordecai
to find out what this was and why it was.
[6] So Hathach went out to Mordecai,
to the city square in front of the king’s gate.
[7] Mordecai told him all that had happened to him,
and the exact amount of money that Haman had promised to pay into the king’s treasuries
for the destruction of the Jews.
[8] He also gave him the copy of the written decree
that had been given out in Susa to destroy them,
to show it to Esther and to explain it to her.
And he told him to urge her to go in to the king,
to beg him for mercy,
and to plead with him for her people.
[9] Hathach came and told Esther the words of Mordecai.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther did not yet know anything about the order. In the palace she was cut off from the outside world.
> At first she only wants to comfort Mordecai, with new clothes. But Mordecai does not want comfort. He wants her to act.
> Mordecai gives her the proof: the copy of the decree. And he asks her: go to the king and plead for your people!

---

## Esther’s fear (Verses 10–12)

[10] Then Esther spoke to Hathach
and gave him a message for Mordecai:
[11] “All the king’s servants and the people of the king’s provinces know this:
whoever comes to the king into the inner court without being called,
whether man or woman,
there is one law for him: that he be put to death.
Only the one to whom the king holds out the golden scepter may live.
And I have not been called to come in to the king for these 30 days.”
[12] They told Esther’s words to Mordecai.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther is afraid, and rightly so. Going to the king without being called could mean death.
> The king has not called her for 30 days. Maybe he has lost interest in her. That makes the situation even more dangerous.

---

## Mordecai’s famous answer (Verses 13–14)

[13] Then Mordecai asked them to give this answer to Esther:
“Don’t think that you will escape in the king’s house
any more than all the other Jews.
[14] For if you stay silent now,
then relief and rescue will come to the Jews from another place,
but you and your father’s house will perish.
Who knows if you haven’t come to the kingdom for such a time as this?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai’s answer is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible.
> He says three things:
> – Even in the palace you are not safe. You are Jewish too.
> – If you stay silent, rescue will come “from another place”. Many interpreters see this as a hidden hint at God. Mordecai is sure: God will not let his people perish.
> – “Who knows if you haven’t come to the kingdom for such a time as this?” Maybe everything Esther has gone through had a meaning. Maybe she is in this place exactly for this.
> This sentence still encourages people today: where you stand right now, you can do something good. Maybe that is exactly why you are there.

---

## Esther’s decision (Verses 15–17)

[15] Then Esther asked them to answer Mordecai:
[16] “Go, gather together all the Jews who are in Susa,
and fast for me.
Don’t eat or drink for three days, night or day.
I and my young women will also fast in the same way.
Then I will go in to the king, which is against the law.
And if I perish, I perish.”
[17] So Mordecai went his way
and did all that Esther had commanded him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther makes her decision. The quiet young woman who always listened to others becomes a brave leader. Now she gives Mordecai instructions.
> She asks everyone to fast for her. In the Bible fasting always means asking God for help. Even though God is not named, it is clear: Esther seeks God’s help.
> “If I perish, I perish.” Esther risks her life for her people. That is true courage: you are afraid and still do what is right.
