# Esther – Chapter 5
### Esther goes to the king

---

## The golden scepter (Verses 1–4)

[1] On the third day Esther put on her royal clothes.
She stood in the inner courtyard of the king’s palace,
across from the king’s hall.
The king sat on his royal throne in the royal hall,
across from the entrance.
[2] The king saw Queen Esther standing in the courtyard.
He was pleased with her.
He held out to Esther the golden scepter that was in his hand.
So Esther came closer and touched the top of the scepter.
[3] Then the king asked her,
“What do you want, Queen Esther? What do you ask for?
I will give it to you, even up to half of the kingdom.”
[4] Esther said,
“If the king agrees,
let the king and Haman come today
to the banquet that I have prepared for the king.”

> **What does this mean?**
> After three days of fasting Esther takes the step. She puts on her royal clothes and comes before the king, without being called, risking her life.
> The king holds out the scepter to her. She may live! The fasting and praying have been answered. Here too God is not named, but the reader feels who is behind it.
> “Even up to half of the kingdom” was a common saying. It means: I am very well disposed toward you (compare Mark 6:23).
> Esther is wise. She does not say her real request at once. First she invites them to a banquet.

---

## The first banquet (Verses 5–8)

[5] Then the king said,
“Bring Haman quickly.
Then we can do what Esther has asked.”
So the king and Haman went to the banquet that Esther had prepared.
[6] While they were drinking wine at the banquet, the king said to Esther,
“What do you wish for? I will give it to you.
What do you ask for? I will do it, even up to half of the kingdom.”
[7] Esther answered,
“This is my wish and my request:
[8] Maybe the king is pleased with me.
Maybe the king is willing to give me what I wish and to do what I ask.
If so, then let the king and Haman come to another banquet that I will prepare for them.
Tomorrow I will do what the king has said.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther puts off her request once again. Why? Maybe she is waiting for the right moment. Maybe she wants to make the king curious.
> This delay is decisive. For in the night in between something unexpected happens (Chapter 6). What looks like hesitating is part of a greater plan.

---

## Haman’s pride and hatred (Verses 9–14)

[9] That day Haman went out happy and in a good mood.
But then Haman saw Mordecai in the king’s gate.
Mordecai did not stand up for him.
He did not even move.
Then Haman was filled with anger against Mordecai.
[10] But Haman controlled himself and went home.
There he called his friends and his wife Zeresh.
[11] Haman bragged to them about his great riches
and about his many sons.
He told them about all the ways the king had honored him.
He told them how the king had raised him above the other princes and servants of the king.
[12] Haman also said,
“And that is not all.
Queen Esther invited no one except me to come with the king to the banquet she had prepared.
And tomorrow she has invited me again, together with the king.
[13] But all this means nothing to me
as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king’s gate.”
[14] Then his wife Zeresh and all his friends said to him,
“Have a gallows built, 50 cubits high.
In the morning, ask the king to have Mordecai hanged on it.
Then go happily with the king to the banquet.”
50 cubits is about 75 feet.
Haman liked this idea, so he had the gallows built.

> **What does this mean?**
> Haman has everything: wealth, many sons, power, honor. But one single man who does not bow before him spoils everything for him. That is the nature of pride and hatred: they never let a person be content.
> “Pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). Haman is at the peak of his pride. Soon he will fall.
> The Bible says “50 cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches. That is as high as a house with seven floors. The whole city was to see how Mordecai is punished. Maybe the number is also exaggerated in the telling, to show Haman’s boundless hatred.
