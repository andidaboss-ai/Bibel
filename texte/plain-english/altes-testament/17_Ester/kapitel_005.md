# Esther – Chapter 5
### Esther goes to the king

---

## The golden scepter (Verses 1–4)

[1] On the third day Esther put on her royal clothing
and stood in the inner court of the king’s house,
across from the king’s house.
The king sat on his royal throne in the royal house,
across from the entrance of the house.
[2] When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court,
she won favor in his sight.
The king held out to Esther the golden scepter that was in his hand.
So Esther came near and touched the top of the scepter.
[3] Then the king asked her,
“What would you like, Queen Esther? What do you ask for?
It shall be given you, even to the half of the kingdom.”
[4] Esther said,
“If it seems good to the king,
let the king and Haman come today
to the banquet that I have prepared for him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> After three days of fasting Esther takes the step. She puts on her royal clothes and comes before the king, without being called, risking her life.
> The king holds out the scepter to her. She may live! The fasting and praying have been answered. Here too God is not named, but the reader feels who is behind it.
> “Even to the half of the kingdom” was a common saying. It means: I am very well disposed toward you (compare Mark 6:23).
> Esther is wise. She does not say her real request at once. First she invites them to a banquet.

---

## The first banquet (Verses 5–8)

[5] Then the king said,
“Bring Haman quickly,
so that it may be done as Esther has said.”
So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.
[6] While they were drinking wine at the banquet, the king said to Esther,
“What do you wish? It shall be given you.
What do you ask for? Even to the half of the kingdom, it shall be done.”
[7] Then Esther answered,
“My wish and my request is this:
[8] If I have found favor in the sight of the king,
and if it pleases the king to grant my wish and to do what I ask,
let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare for them.
And tomorrow I will do as the king has said.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther puts off her request once again. Why? Maybe she is waiting for the right moment. Maybe she wants to make the king curious.
> This delay is decisive. For in the night in between something unexpected happens (Chapter 6). What looks like hesitating is part of a greater plan.

---

## Haman’s pride and hatred (Verses 9–14)

[9] Haman went out that day full of joy and glad in his heart.
But when Haman saw Mordecai in the king’s gate,
and Mordecai didn’t stand up or move for him,
he was filled with anger against Mordecai.
[10] Yet Haman held himself back and went home.
There he sent for his friends and for Zeresh his wife.
[11] Haman told them about the glory of his riches,
the great number of his children,
all the ways in which the king had promoted him,
and how he had raised him above the princes and servants of the king.
[12] Haman also said,
“Yes, Queen Esther let no one come in with the king to the banquet that she had prepared
except me.
And tomorrow I am invited by her again, together with the king.
[13] Yet all this is worth nothing to me
as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king’s gate.”
[14] Then Zeresh his wife and all his friends said to him,
“Let a gallows be made, 50 cubits high, about 75 feet.
And in the morning speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on it.
Then go in happily with the king to the banquet.”
This pleased Haman, so he had the gallows made.

> **What does this mean?**
> Haman has everything: wealth, many sons, power, honor. But one single man who does not bow before him spoils everything for him. That is the nature of pride and hatred: they never let a person be content.
> “Pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). Haman is at the peak of his pride. Soon he will fall.
> The Bible says “50 cubits”. A cubit is about 18 inches. That is as high as a house with seven floors. The whole city was to see how Mordecai is punished. Maybe the number is also exaggerated in the telling, to show Haman’s boundless hatred.
