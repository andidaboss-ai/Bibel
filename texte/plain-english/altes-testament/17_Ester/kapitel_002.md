# Esther – Chapter 2
### Esther becomes queen

---

## The search for a new queen (Verses 1–4)

[1] Some time later King Ahasuerus was no longer angry.
Then he remembered Vashti.
He remembered what she had done
and what had been decided against her.
[2] The king’s servants who served him said,
“Let people look for beautiful young virgins for the king.
[3] The king should choose officers in all the provinces of his kingdom.
They should gather all the beautiful young virgins
and bring them to the fortress of Susa, to the women’s house.
There Hegai will take care of them.
He is the king’s eunuch who is in charge of the women.
The young women should receive beauty treatments.
[4] Then the young woman who pleases the king the most
should become queen instead of Vashti.”
The king liked this idea, and he did it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king’s anger has calmed down. Maybe he even misses Vashti. But the law cannot be changed (Esther 1:19).
> The servants suggest a kind of “beauty contest”. For the young women this was no game. They were taken from their families and had no choice. They came into the king’s harem. Most of them saw the king only once and afterward lived shut away in the women’s house.
> The Bible describes these conditions without approving of them.

---

## Mordecai and Esther (Verses 5–7)

[5] In the fortress of Susa there was a Jew named Mordecai.
He was the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish.
He was from the tribe of Benjamin.
[6] He had been taken away from Jerusalem as a prisoner.
He was among the prisoners who were taken away with Jeconiah, the king of Judah.
Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon, had taken them away.
[7] Mordecai had raised Hadassah, that is, Esther.
She was the daughter of his uncle.
She had no father and no mother.
The young woman had a lovely figure and was beautiful.
When her father and mother died,
Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai comes from the tribe of Benjamin, like King Saul, whose father was also called Kish. That becomes important later (Esther 3:1).
> Verse 6 probably means: his family (maybe his great-grandfather Kish) had been carried away in 597 BC with King Jeconiah (Jehoiachin). Otherwise Mordecai himself would have been more than 100 years old.
> Esther has two names: Hadassah is Hebrew and means “myrtle”. Esther is Persian and probably means “star”.
> Esther is an orphan. Her cousin Mordecai has brought her up like a daughter. He is a loving foster father.

---

## Esther in the women’s house (Verses 8–11)

[8] The king’s command and his order were made known.
Many young women were brought to the fortress of Susa.
Hegai took care of them.
Esther was also taken into the king’s palace.
Hegai, who was in charge of the women, took care of her.
[9] Hegai liked the young woman, and she won his kindness.
He quickly gave her the beauty treatments and her special food.
He gave her seven young women who were chosen for her from the king’s palace.
He moved her and her young women to the best place in the women’s house.
[10] Esther had not told anyone who her people and her relatives were.
Mordecai had told her not to tell anyone.
[11] Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the courtyard of the women’s house.
He wanted to find out how Esther was doing
and what would happen to her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther “was taken”. She had no choice.
> Esther wins the favor of Hegai. The book says this again and again: Esther finds favor with everyone. That recalls Joseph, who also found favor in a foreign land (Genesis 39:4, 21).
> Why was Esther to keep it secret that she was Jewish? Maybe there were already prejudices against Jews back then. Mordecai wanted to protect her.
> Mordecai worries every day about his foster daughter. He cannot see her, but he comes every day to ask about her.

---

## A year of preparation (Verses 12–14)

[12] Each young woman had to go through 12 months of beauty treatment.
Then her turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus.
The treatment took this long:
6 months with oil of myrrh,
and 6 months with sweet perfumes and other beauty treatments for women.
[13] Then the young woman went to the king.
She was given whatever she asked for
to take with her from the women’s house to the king’s palace.
[14] She went in the evening.
The next morning she came back to the second women’s house.
There Shaashgaz took care of her.
He was the king’s eunuch who was in charge of the concubines.
A concubine is a wife of lower rank.
She did not go to the king again,
unless the king liked her very much and called for her by name.

> **What does this mean?**
> A whole year of beauty treatment for one single night. After that each woman became a “concubine” of the king and had to live in the second women’s house. A concubine is a wife of lower rank. If the king did not call her again, she never saw him again.
> That was a sad fate for many young women. The Bible tells it soberly. It shows the power and the arbitrariness of a king who treats people like property.

---

## Esther becomes queen (Verses 15–18)

[15] Then Esther’s turn came to go in to the king.
Esther was the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai’s uncle.
Mordecai had taken her as his daughter.
She did not ask for anything except what Hegai suggested.
Hegai was the king’s eunuch who was in charge of the women.
Everyone who saw Esther liked her.
[16] So Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus in his royal palace.
It was in the tenth month, the month Tebeth,
in the seventh year of his rule.
[17] The king loved Esther more than all the other women.
She won his favor and kindness more than all the other virgins.
So he put the royal crown on her head.
He made her queen instead of Vashti.
[18] Then the king gave a great feast for all his princes and his servants.
It was Esther’s feast.
He declared a holiday in the provinces.
He gave gifts, as generously as a king can.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther is modest. She asks for nothing special, but listens to Hegai’s advice.
> The month Tebeth is December/January. The seventh year of the king is about 479 BC. So four years lay between Chapter 1 and Chapter 2. In this time Xerxes fought his war against Greece.
> Esther becomes queen. A Jewish orphan now sits on the throne of the greatest empire in the world. No one knows yet why this is so important.
> “Holiday”: the Hebrew word can also mean “tax relief”. It means a release from work or from taxes.

---

## Mordecai saves the king (Verses 19–23)

[19] The virgins were gathered together a second time.
At that time Mordecai was sitting in the king’s gate.
[20] Esther still had not told anyone who her relatives and her people were.
Mordecai had commanded her not to.
Esther obeyed Mordecai,
just as she had when he was raising her.
[21] In those days Mordecai was sitting in the king’s gate.
Two of the king’s eunuchs, Bigthan and Teresh, were angry.
They guarded the door.
They tried to attack King Ahasuerus.
[22] Mordecai found out about it.
He told Queen Esther.
Esther told the king and said that the news came from Mordecai.
[23] The matter was investigated.
It was true.
So both men were hanged on a gallows.
All this was written in the book of the chronicles while the king watched.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Sitting in the king’s gate” probably means: Mordecai had an office at court. The gate was the place where officials worked and where justice was done.
> Esther is queen now, but she still listens to her foster father. She is faithful to him.
> Mordecai saves the king’s life. But he gets no reward. The matter is only written in a book and seems forgotten. But in Chapter 6 exactly this book will play a big role. In the story of Esther nothing happens by chance.
> “Gallows”: word for word it says “wood” or “stake”. Among the Persians, condemned people were often impaled on a stake or hanged on it.
> Xerxes really was murdered later, in the year 465 BC, by court officials.
