# Esther – Chapter 2
### Esther becomes queen

---

## The search for a new queen (Verses 1–4)

[1] After these things, when the anger of King Ahasuerus had calmed down,
he remembered Vashti, and what she had done,
and what had been decided against her.
[2] Then the king’s servants who served him said,
“Let beautiful young virgins be looked for, for the king.
[3] Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom,
so that they gather together all the beautiful young virgins
to the citadel of Susa, to the women’s house,
into the care of Hegai the king’s eunuch, the keeper of the women.
Let them be given beauty treatments.
[4] And let the young woman who pleases the king
be queen instead of Vashti.”
This pleased the king, and he did so.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king’s anger has calmed down. Maybe he even misses Vashti. But the law cannot be changed (Chapter 1:19).
> The servants suggest a kind of “beauty contest”. For the young women this was no game. They were taken from their families and had no choice. They came into the king’s harem. Most of them saw the king only once and afterward lived shut away in the women’s house.
> The Bible describes these conditions without approving of them.

---

## Mordecai and Esther (Verses 5–7)

[5] There was a certain Jew in the citadel of Susa whose name was Mordecai,
the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, a Benjamite.
[6] He had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captives
who had been carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah,
whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away.
[7] He had brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle’s daughter,
for she had neither father nor mother.
The young woman was lovely and beautiful.
When her father and mother had died,
Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mordecai comes from the tribe of Benjamin, like King Saul, whose father was also called Kish. That becomes important later (Chapter 3:1).
> Verse 6 probably means: his family (maybe his great-grandfather Kish) had been carried away in 597 before Christ with King Jeconiah (Jehoiachin). Otherwise Mordecai himself would have been more than 100 years old.
> Esther has two names: Hadassah is Hebrew and means “myrtle”. Esther is Persian and probably means “star”.
> Esther is an orphan. Her cousin Mordecai has brought her up like a daughter. He is a loving foster father.

---

## Esther in the women’s house (Verses 8–11)

[8] When the king’s command and his decree were made known,
many young women were gathered together to the citadel of Susa,
into the care of Hegai.
Esther was taken into the king’s house,
into the care of Hegai, the keeper of the women.
[9] The young woman pleased him, and she won his kindness.
He quickly gave her the beauty treatments and her portions of food,
and the seven chosen young women who were to be given her from the king’s house.
He moved her and her young women to the best place in the women’s house.
[10] Esther had not told anyone about her people or her relatives,
because Mordecai had told her not to make it known.
[11] Every day Mordecai walked in front of the court of the women’s house,
to find out how Esther was doing,
and what would become of her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther “was taken”. She had no choice.
> Esther wins the favor of Hegai. The book says this again and again: Esther finds favor with everyone. That recalls Joseph, who also found favor in a foreign land (Genesis 39:4, 21).
> Why was Esther to keep it secret that she was Jewish? Maybe there were already prejudices against Jews back then. Mordecai wanted to protect her.
> Mordecai worries every day about his foster daughter. He cannot see her, but he comes every day to ask about her.

---

## A year of preparation (Verses 12–14)

[12] Each young woman’s turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus
after her beauty treatment for twelve months.
For that was how long their beauty treatment took:
six months with oil of myrrh,
and six months with sweet perfumes and with other treatments for women.
[13] Then the young woman came to the king like this:
whatever she asked for was given her
to take with her from the women’s house to the king’s house.
[14] In the evening she went,
and the next morning she returned to the second women’s house,
into the care of Shaashgaz, the king’s eunuch, who kept the concubines.
She did not come in to the king again,
unless the king delighted in her and she was called by name.

> **What does this mean?**
> A whole year of beauty treatment for one single night. After that each woman became a “concubine” of the king and had to live in the second women’s house. A concubine is a wife of lower rank. If the king did not call her again, she never saw him again.
> That was a sad fate for many young women. The Bible tells it soberly. It shows the power and the arbitrariness of a king who treats people like property.

---

## Esther becomes queen (Verses 15–18)

[15] Now the turn came for Esther to go in to the king.
She was the daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai,
who had taken her as his daughter.
She asked for nothing except what Hegai the king’s eunuch, the keeper of the women, advised.
And Esther won favor in the sight of all who looked at her.
[16] So Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus into his royal house
in the tenth month, the month Tebeth,
in the seventh year of his reign.
[17] The king loved Esther more than all the women,
and she won his favor and kindness more than all the virgins.
So he set the royal crown on her head
and made her queen instead of Vashti.
[18] Then the king gave a great feast for all his princes and his servants,
Esther’s feast.
He announced a holiday in the provinces
and gave gifts, as only a king can.

> **What does this mean?**
> Esther is modest. She asks for nothing special, but listens to Hegai’s advice.
> The month Tebeth is December/January. The seventh year of the king is about 479 before Christ. So four years lay between Chapter 1 and Chapter 2. In this time Xerxes fought his war against Greece.
> Esther becomes queen. A Jewish orphan now sits on the throne of the greatest empire in the world. No one knows yet why this is so important.
> “Holiday”: the Hebrew word can also mean “tax relief”. It means a release from work or from taxes.

---

## Mordecai saves the king (Verses 19–23)

[19] When the virgins were gathered together the second time,
Mordecai was sitting in the king’s gate.
[20] Esther had not yet told anyone about her relatives or her people,
as Mordecai had commanded her.
For Esther obeyed Mordecai,
just as she had when he was bringing her up.
[21] In those days, while Mordecai was sitting in the king’s gate,
two of the king’s eunuchs, Bigthan and Teresh, who guarded the door,
were angry and tried to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.
[22] Mordecai found out about it and told Queen Esther.
And Esther told the king in Mordecai’s name.
[23] When the matter was looked into and found to be true,
both of them were hanged on a gallows.
And it was written in the book of the chronicles in the king’s presence.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Sitting in the king’s gate” probably means: Mordecai had an office at court. The gate was the place where officials worked and where justice was done.
> Esther is queen now, but she still listens to her foster father. She is faithful to him.
> Mordecai saves the king’s life. But he gets no reward. The matter is only written in a book and seems forgotten. But in Chapter 6 exactly this book will play a big role. In the story of Esther nothing happens by chance.
> “Gallows”: word for word it says “wood” or “stake”. Among the Persians, condemned people were often impaled on a stake or hanged on it.
> Xerxes really was murdered later, in the year 465 before Christ, by court officials.
