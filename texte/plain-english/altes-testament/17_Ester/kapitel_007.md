# Esther – Chapter 7
### Haman is exposed

---

## Esther’s request (Verses 1–4)

[1] So the king and Haman came to the banquet with Queen Esther.
[2] On the second day, while they were drinking wine at the banquet,
the king said again to Esther,
“What do you wish, Queen Esther? It shall be given you.
What do you ask for? Even to the half of the kingdom, it shall be done.”
[3] Then Queen Esther answered,
“If I have found favor in your sight, O king,
and if it pleases the king,
let my life be given me as my wish,
and my people as my request.
[4] For we have been sold, I and my people,
to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish.
If we had only been sold as male and female slaves,
I would have kept quiet,
although the enemy could never have made up for the king’s loss.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At last Esther speaks. And she speaks very wisely:
> – First she asks for her own life. The king loves her. That touches him personally.
> – Then she asks for her people. For the first time she makes known that she is Jewish.
> – She uses the words “to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish”, exactly the words of the order to murder (Esther 3:13).
> – “We have been sold”: she reminds him of the money that Haman had offered.
> The last sentence is hard to understand. It probably means: slavery would have been bad, but I would not have bothered the king for that. But here it is about life and death.

---

## “This wicked Haman!” (Verses 5–6)

[5] Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther,
“Who is he, and where is he,
who has dared in his heart to do such a thing?”
[6] Esther said,
“An enemy and a foe:
this wicked Haman!”
Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king had sealed the order himself, but he had not even asked which people was meant (Esther 3:10–11). Now he learns: it is the people of his own queen!
> Esther points to Haman. The mighty man is suddenly exposed.

---

## Haman’s end (Verses 7–10)

[7] The king got up in his anger from the banquet of wine
and went into the palace garden.
Haman stayed to beg Queen Esther for his life,
for he saw that the king had decided evil against him.
[8] Then the king returned from the palace garden into the room of the banquet of wine.
Haman had fallen on the couch where Esther was.
Then the king said,
“Will he even attack the queen in front of me, in my own house?”
As soon as the word left the king’s mouth,
they covered Haman’s face.
[9] Then Harbonah, one of the eunuchs who were with the king, said,
“Look, the gallows 50 cubits high, about 75 feet,
which Haman has made for Mordecai,
who spoke up for the good of the king,
is standing at Haman’s house.”
The king said, “Hang him on it!”
[10] So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai.
Then the king’s anger calmed down.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king goes out. Maybe he has to think. After all, he gave the order himself.
> Haman falls down before Esther and begs for mercy. At that time people lay on couches while eating. When the king comes back, he misreads the scene, or he wants to misread it. That gives him a reason to condemn Haman.
> “They covered Haman’s face”: this was probably a sign that someone had been condemned to death.
> The eunuch Harbonah points to the gallows (he was already named in Esther 1:10).
> The great irony: Haman dies on the gallows that he had built for Mordecai. “Whoever digs a pit will fall into it” (Proverbs 26:27; Psalm 7:15).
> But the danger is not over yet. The order to destroy the Jews is still valid. And a Persian law cannot be taken back (Chapter 8).
