# Hosea – Chapter 8
### Whoever sows the wind will reap the whirlwind

---

## An eagle over the house of the LORD (Verses 1–6)

[1] “Put the trumpet to your lips!
Something like an eagle is over the house of the LORD,
because they have broken my covenant
and rebelled against my law.
[2] They cry out to me,
‘My God, we, Israel, know you!’
[3] Israel has thrown away what is good.
The enemy will chase him.
[4] They have set up kings, but not by my choice.
They have made leaders, and I did not approve.
With their silver and their gold
they have made idols for themselves,
so that they will be destroyed.
[5] Samaria, throw out your calf idol!
My anger burns against them!
How long will it be until they can be pure?
[6] For this calf comes from Israel!
A craftsman made it,
and it is not God.
Yes, the calf of Samaria will be broken to pieces.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Alarm! Like a bird of prey, the enemy (Assyria) is already circling over the land.
> Verse 2: The people say piously: “My God, we, Israel, know you!” But in verse 3, God says: “Israel has thrown away what is good.” Pious words are not the same as really knowing God.
> Verse 4: In the northern kingdom, kings often came to power by murder, not by God’s will.
> Verses 5–6: The “calf of Samaria”: King Jeroboam I had set up golden bull statues in the northern kingdom (1 Kings 12:28–29). Hosea mocks: “A craftsman made it, and it is not God” (Hosea 8:6). How can something that a human being made be a god?

---

## Sowing the wind, reaping the whirlwind (Verses 7–10)

[7] “For they sow the wind,
and they will reap the whirlwind.
A whirlwind is a violent storm.
The grain has no stalks standing.
The stalks will produce no flour.
And even if they produce some,
strangers will swallow it up.
[8] Israel is swallowed up.
Now they are among the nations like something worthless.
[9] For they have gone up to Assyria,
like a wild donkey wandering alone.
Ephraim has paid lovers for himself.
[10] But even though they sell themselves among the nations,
I will now gather them together.
And they will begin to waste away
under the heavy load of the king of mighty rulers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: “They sow the wind, and they will reap the whirlwind.” This became a famous saying. It is still used today. It means: Whoever does bad things will in the end get back something much worse. Small wrong decisions can have big, terrible consequences.
> Verse 9: A wild donkey wanders around alone. Israel is alone and lonely because it has separated itself from God. And it even has to pay its “lovers” (its allies), with high tribute payments to Assyria (compare 2 Kings 15:19–20). Tribute is money that a weaker country must pay to a stronger one.
> Verse 10: The “king of mighty rulers” is the king of Assyria. The burden of tribute will crush Israel.

---

## Many altars, much sin (Verses 11–14)

[11] “Ephraim has built many altars for sinning.
So they have become altars for sinning for him.
[12] I wrote down for him the many things of my law,
but they were seen as something strange.
[13] As for the sacrifices they offer me,
they offer meat and eat it.
But the LORD does not accept them.
Now he will remember their guilt
and punish their sins.
They will go back to Egypt.
[14] For Israel has forgotten his Maker
and has built palaces.
And Judah has built many cities with strong walls.
But I will send fire on his cities,
and it will burn up their fortresses.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: More altars do not mean more faith. On the contrary: They became places of sin.
> Verse 12: God has written down his commandments, but the people treat them like something strange that has nothing to do with them.
> Verse 13: The sacrifices are just barbecues now. They eat the meat, but God does not accept the sacrifices, because there is no real faith behind them.
> “They will go back to Egypt” – a picture that they will fall back into the slavery from which God once set them free.
> Verse 14: “Israel has forgotten his Maker and has built palaces.” They trust in wealth and fortresses instead of God. But walls and palaces do not protect you if you forget God.
