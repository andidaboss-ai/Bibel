# Hosea – Chapter 14
### I will love them freely

---

## Return, Israel! (Verses 1–3)

[1] Israel, return to the LORD your God.
For you have fallen because of your sin.
[2] Take words with you
and return to the LORD.
Say to him,
“Forgive all our sins,
and accept what is good.
Then we will offer you bulls,
as we promised with our lips.
[3] Assyria cannot save us.
We will not ride on horses anymore.
And we will never again say to the work of our hands,
‘Our gods!’
For with you, the orphan finds mercy.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, the verse numbers in this chapter are one higher (14:2–10).
> After all the words of judgment, the book ends with a great call to turn back.
> Verse 2: “Take words with you.” God does not ask for expensive sacrifices. He wants honest words, a prayer from the heart. Hosea even gives the people the prayer they should say.
> Verse 3: The people are to give up three things: trusting in Assyria (political alliances), in horses (military power), and in gods they made themselves.
> “With you, the orphan finds mercy.” A beautiful sentence: Israel is like an orphan who has no one anymore. But with God, it finds mercy.
> In Judaism, this passage is read on “Shabbat Shuvah,” the Sabbath of Return between the Jewish New Year and the Day of Atonement.

---

## I will be like the dew (Verses 4–8)

[4] “I will heal their unfaithfulness.
I will love them freely,
for my anger has turned away from them.
[5] I will be like the dew to Israel.
He will bloom like the lily.
He will send down his roots like Lebanon.
[6] His branches will spread out.
His beauty will be like the olive tree,
and his fragrance like Lebanon.
[7] People will live in his shade.
They will come back to life like the grain
and bloom like the grapevine.
Their fragrance will be like the wine of Lebanon.
[8] Ephraim, what more do I have to do with idols?
I answer him, and I will take care of him.
I am like a green cypress tree.
Your fruit comes from me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s answer is beautiful:
> Verse 4: “I will heal their unfaithfulness. I will love them freely.” Unfaithfulness means: The people turned away from God. They were not faithful to him. God does not love because the people deserved it, but freely, out of pure grace. And he heals their unfaithfulness the way an illness is healed.
> Verse 5: “I will be like the dew to Israel.” In Hosea 6:4, the people’s love was “like the dew that disappears early.” Now God himself is the dew that comes new every morning and refreshes the land. In Israel’s dry summer, the dew is vital.
> Verses 5–7: Many pictures of life and beauty: blooming lilies, deep roots, spreading branches, the olive tree, fragrance, grain, the grapevine.
> Verse 8: God compares himself with an evergreen tree that bears fruit. This is a very rare picture in the Bible. The fruitfulness that Israel looked for in Baal really comes from God: “Your fruit comes from me.”
> Lebanon was famous for its great cedar trees with deep roots.

## Whoever is wise, let him understand (Verse 9)

[9] Who is wise enough to understand these things?
Who has enough good sense to know them?
For the ways of the LORD are right.
Those who do right walk in them.
But the rebels stumble in them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The last verse is like an afterword for the readers: Whoever is wise should think about this book. God’s ways are good and straight. Some walk on them and find life. Others stumble over them.
> The book is an offer. Every reader must decide for themselves how to answer it.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Hosea?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A prophet who feels God’s pain:** Hosea married Gomer, who became unfaithful to him. So he felt in his own life what God feels when his people leave him (Chapters 1–3).
> **Children with message names:** “No mercy” and “Not my people.” But in the end the names are turned around: “mercy” and “my people” (1:10; 2:23).
> **God tries to win his people back:** “So I will win her back with love. I will bring her into the wilderness and speak tenderly to her” (2:14). God wants a relationship of love, not of power.
> **The charge:** “There is no truth, no goodness, and no knowledge of God in the land” (4:1). Lies, violence, idol worship, and false trust in great powers.
> **Mercy instead of sacrifice:** “For I desire mercy, not sacrifice” (6:6). Jesus quoted this sentence twice.
> **Wind and whirlwind:** “For they sow the wind, and they will reap the whirlwind” (8:7).
> **God’s mother’s heart:** “It was I who taught Ephraim to walk … with ties of love … I bent down to him” (Chapter 11).
> **Love that does not give up:** “How can I give you up, Ephraim? … For I am God, and not a human being” (11:8–9).
> **Healing:** “I will heal their unfaithfulness. I will love them freely … I will be like the dew to Israel” (14:4–5).
> **Hard pictures:** The pictures of the unfaithful wife come from a time when men had all the power. They never justify violence in relationships. And the cruelties of war that the book describes are wrong.
> **For today:** Hosea shows that God is not cold and does not stop caring. He loves, he suffers, he struggles with himself, and in the end his love wins. It is never too late to turn back: “Take words with you and return to the LORD.”
> **What comes next?** Next comes the prophet Joel. A terrible plague of locusts becomes his reason to call the people to turn back. And he promises: “I will pour out my Spirit on all people.”
