# Hosea – Chapter 12
### Jacob, who struggled with God

---

## Ephraim chases the wind (Verses 1–2)

[1] Ephraim feeds on wind.
He chases the east wind.
He keeps making more and more lies and ruin.
They make a covenant with Assyria,
and oil is carried to Egypt.
[2] The LORD also has a charge against Judah.
He will punish Jacob for the way he lives.
He will pay him back for what he has done.

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, the verse numbers in this chapter are one higher (12:2–15).
> Verse 1: “Feeding on wind” and “chasing the east wind” – that is pointless. That is how pointless it is to rely on Assyria and Egypt. Israel made agreements with both great powers at the same time and sent oil to Egypt as a gift. A dangerous double game.

---

## Jacob, the fighter (Verses 3–6)

[3] In the womb, he took hold of his brother’s heel.
And as a grown man, he struggled with God.
[4] Yes, he struggled with the angel and won.
He wept and begged him for a blessing.
He found God at Bethel,
and there God spoke with us –
[5] the LORD, the God of Armies.
The LORD is his famous name!
[6] So turn back to your God!
Hold on to kindness and justice,
and always wait for your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hosea reminds the people of Jacob, the father of the people of Israel (Israel is Jacob’s second name):
> – Even in the womb, he held on to the heel of his twin brother Esau (Genesis 25:26). The name “Jacob” sounds like “heel-holder,” and also like “cheater.”
> – Later, he struggled with God a whole night by the river Jabbok and received the name “Israel” (“he struggles with God,” Genesis 32:22–30).
> – At Bethel, God met him in the dream of the stairway to heaven (Genesis 28:10–22).
> Verse 4: “He wept and begged him for a blessing” – this is a detail that is not written like this in Genesis. Jacob did not only fight. He also wept and asked for a blessing.
> The message: Jacob was not a perfect person either. He was a cheater. But he struggled with God and met him. In the same way, the people should turn back to God.
> Verse 6: The way back is simple to say: “Hold on to kindness and justice, and always wait for your God.” That is a good summary of faith, for today too.

## Dishonest scales and false pride (Verses 7–11)

[7] A merchant has dishonest scales in his hand.
He loves to cheat people.
[8] Ephraim said,
“Surely I have become rich.
I have gained wealth for myself.
In all my wealth, they will not find any guilt in me
that would be sin.”
[9] “But I am the LORD your God
ever since the land of Egypt.
I will again make you live in tents,
as in the days of the holy feast.
[10] I have also spoken to the prophets.
I have given them many visions.
And through the prophets, I have spoken in parables.
Parables are stories or pictures that teach something.
[11] If Gilead is evil,
then they are surely worthless.
In Gilgal they sacrifice bulls.
Yes, their altars are like piles of stones
in the rows of a plowed field.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: Israel is like a dishonest merchant with false scales. And then he says proudly: “Surely I have become rich … In all my wealth, they will not find any guilt in me.” He means: No one can blame me for anything! Wealth through cheating, and then self-righteous on top of it.
> Verse 9: God reminds them: “I am the LORD your God ever since the land of Egypt.” He will make them live in tents again, as at the Feast of Tabernacles (also called the Feast of Booths), when people remembered the time in the wilderness. This can mean punishment (losing their houses) or also a new beginning like back then in the wilderness (compare Hosea 2:14).
> Verse 10: God spoke through prophets again and again, in pictures and parables. He was not silent.

## Through a prophet (Verses 12–14)

[12] Jacob fled to the country of Aram.
Israel worked to get a wife.
To get a wife, he took care of flocks and herds.
[13] Through a prophet, the LORD brought Israel up out of Egypt.
And through a prophet, Israel was kept safe.
[14] Ephraim has made God bitterly angry.
So the guilt of his bloodshed will stay on him.
And his Lord will pay him back for his insults.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Jacob had to flee from Esau. For many years he worked as a shepherd for Laban, so that he could marry Rachel (Genesis 29). A picture of how hard the way of the father of Israel was.
> Verse 13: The “prophet” through whom God led Israel out of Egypt is Moses. God has always led and protected his people through prophets. Hosea places himself in this line.
> Verse 14: Ephraim has bitterly disappointed God. It must bear the consequences of what it has done.
