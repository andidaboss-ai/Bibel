# Hosea – Chapter 1
### Hosea’s marriage and his children

---

## Before we begin: The twelve Minor Prophets and Hosea

Now the twelve “Minor Prophets” begin: Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi. They are called “minor” (small) because their books are shorter than those of Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Ezekiel, not because they are less important. In Judaism, they form one single book together, the “Book of the Twelve.”
Hosea lived in the 8th century BC in the northern kingdom of Israel, at about the same time as Amos, Isaiah, and Micah. It was a time when many people were doing well. But the people mixed the God of Israel with Baal, a god of fertility. Shortly afterward, in the year 722 BC, the northern kingdom was destroyed by the Assyrians.
His name means “The LORD saves,” very similar to “Joshua” and “Jesus.”
What is special about Hosea: He feels God’s pain in his own life. His own marriage to an unfaithful wife becomes a picture of God’s relationship with his unfaithful people.
A word first: Hosea uses the picture of an unfaithful wife for the people. This picture comes from a time when men had all the power. It is a picture of unfaithfulness toward God, not a statement about women. And it never excuses violence in a marriage.
The great message of Hosea: God’s love does not give up. “How can I give you up?” (Hosea 11:8). The book has 14 chapters.

## Hosea marries Gomer (Verses 1–3)

[1] This is the word of the LORD that came to Hosea, the son of Beeri.
It came in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah,
and in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash, king of Israel.
[2] When the LORD first spoke through Hosea,
the LORD said to Hosea,
“Go, marry a woman who is a prostitute,
and have children who come from unfaithfulness.
For the land is like an unfaithful wife.
It has left the LORD.”
[3] So he went and married Gomer, the daughter of Diblaim.
She became pregnant and gave birth to a son for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> God asks Hosea to do something shocking: He is to marry a woman who will be unfaithful to him. That way, Hosea is to feel in his own life what God feels when his people leave him and run after other gods.
> Interpreters disagree about whether Gomer already lived like this before the marriage or only became unfaithful later. Some also think that she belonged to a pagan cult, a religion of other gods.
> Important: Here, Gomer is a picture of the whole people, especially of the men who ruled the land and served idols. The text is not a statement about what women are like.

## Three children with names that are signs (Verses 4–9)

[4] The LORD said to him,
“Name him Jezreel.
For in just a little while,
I will punish the house of Jehu
for the blood it shed at Jezreel.
And I will put an end to the kingdom of the house of Israel.
[5] On that day,
I will break the bow of Israel in the valley of Jezreel.”
The bow stands for the power of the army.
[6] She became pregnant again and gave birth to a daughter.
Then the LORD said to Hosea,
“Name her Lo-Ruhamah.
For I will no longer have mercy on the house of Israel.
I will not forgive them in any way.
[7] But I will have mercy on the house of Judah.
I will save them by the LORD their God.
I will not save them by bow, sword, battle,
horses, or horsemen.”
[8] After she had weaned Lo-Ruhamah,
she became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
To wean means: The child stops drinking its mother’s milk.
[9] The LORD said,
“Name him Lo-Ammi.
For you are not my people,
and I will not be yours.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The three children get names that are a message, like living posters:
> – Jezreel (“God sows”): In the valley of Jezreel, King Jehu had killed many people when he came to power (2 Kings 9–10). God has not forgotten this violence.
> – Lo-Ruhamah (“No mercy”): God will no longer have mercy.
> – Lo-Ammi (“Not my people”): The relationship between God and Israel is broken. God had told Moses his name: “I AM” (Exodus 3:14). Now God says: “I will not be yours” (Hosea 1:9). A shocking sentence.
> Imagine what it means for children to carry names like these. But the story goes on: In Hosea 1:10 and 2:23, the names are turned around!
> Verse 7: Judah will be saved, but not by weapons. God himself will save them.

## But then: children of the living God (Verses 10–11)

[10] Yet the number of the people of Israel will be like the sand of the sea,
which cannot be measured or counted.
And this will happen:
In the place where people said to them,
‘You are not my people,’
they will be called ‘children of the living God.’
[11] The people of Judah and the people of Israel
will be gathered together.
They will choose one leader for themselves.
And they will go up out of the land.
For the day of Jezreel will be great.

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, these two verses are 2:1–2. That is why the verse numbers in Chapter 2 of the WEB are two lower than in many other Bibles.
> In the middle of judgment, there is suddenly a great hope! Where it said “Not my people,” it will say “children of the living God.” God’s last word is not separation, but love.
> Verse 10: This reminds us of the promise to Abraham: descendants like the sand of the sea (Genesis 22:17).
> Verse 11: The divided people (the northern kingdom and the southern kingdom) will become one again.
> “The day of Jezreel”: Now the name gets a good meaning, “God sows.” God will sow his people again, like seed that comes up.
> Paul quotes this verse in his letter to the Romans (Romans 9:25–26): Now people from the nations who were not God’s people may also be called children of the living God.
