# Hosea – Chapter 5
### God draws back

---

## A trap and a net (Verses 1–7)

[1] “Listen to this, you priests!
Pay attention, house of Israel!
Listen, house of the king!
For the judgment is against you.
You have been a trap at Mizpah
and a net spread out on Tabor.
[2] The rebels are deep in killing,
but I will correct all of them.
[3] I know Ephraim,
and Israel is not hidden from me.
For now, Ephraim, you have acted like a prostitute.
Israel has made itself unclean.
[4] Their deeds do not let them turn back to their God.
For a spirit of prostitution is in them,
and they do not know the LORD.
[5] The pride of Israel speaks against him to his face.
So Israel and Ephraim will stumble in their guilt.
Judah will also stumble with them.
[6] They will go with their flocks and their herds
to look for the LORD,
but they will not find him.
He has drawn back from them.
[7] They have been unfaithful to the LORD.
They have had children who are not his.
Now the New Moon festival will eat them up, together with their fields.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The priests, the people, and the royal family are all spoken to. The leaders are like traps and nets for the people: Instead of protecting them, they lead them into ruin. Mizpah and Tabor were places with shrines.
> Verse 4: “Their deeds do not let them turn back to their God.” A frightening insight: Whoever does wrong for a long time finds it harder and harder to get free of it. Bad habits can hold people captive.
> Verse 6: They bring many animals for sacrifice and want to look for God, but they do not find him. God cannot be “bought” with sacrifices when the heart is not in it.
> Verse 7: “Children who are not his” – children who are no longer raised in faith in God. The WEB says “illegitimate children.” A whole generation has become strangers to God.

## Alarm in Benjamin (Verses 8–12)

[8] “Blow the horn in Gibeah,
and the trumpet in Ramah!
Shout a battle cry at Beth Aven:
‘Behind you, Benjamin!’
[9] Ephraim will become a ruin on the day of punishment.
Among the tribes of Israel,
I have told what will surely happen.
[10] The leaders of Judah are like people who move boundary stones.
I will pour out my anger on them like water.
[11] Ephraim is oppressed.
He is crushed in judgment,
because he was determined to run after idols.
[12] So I am like a moth to Ephraim,
and like rot to the house of Judah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Alarm! Gibeah, Ramah, and Beth Aven are in the land of Benjamin, at the border between the northern and the southern kingdom. This probably points to a war between Israel and Judah (compare 2 Kings 16:5).
> Verse 10: “Moving boundary stones” was a serious crime (Deuteronomy 19:14). In this way, people stole land from their neighbors. The leaders of Judah use the war to take land for themselves.
> Verse 12: God compares himself to a moth and to rot. Not a dramatic judgment, but a slow, quiet falling apart from the inside.

---

## False help from Assyria (Verses 13–15)

[13] “When Ephraim saw his sickness,
and Judah saw his wound,
Ephraim went to Assyria
and sent to King Jareb.
But he cannot heal you.
He cannot cure your wound.
[14] For I will be like a lion to Ephraim,
and like a young lion to the house of Judah.
I myself will tear them to pieces and go away.
I will carry them off, and no one will rescue them.
[15] I will go back to my place
until they admit their guilt
and look for my face.
In their trouble, they will look for me with all their heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Israel notices that it is “sick,” that is, in great trouble. But instead of coming to God, it looks for help from the king of Assyria. “Jareb” means “the one who loves to fight,” probably a mocking name for the Assyrian king. He cannot heal. On the contrary: Assyria later destroyed the northern kingdom.
> Verse 14: A frightening picture: God like a lion. He is not harmless.
> Verse 15: But the goal is not destruction. God draws back and waits “until they admit their guilt and look for my face.” God hopes that his people will find their way back to him in their trouble.
