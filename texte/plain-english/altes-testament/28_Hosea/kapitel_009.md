# Hosea – Chapter 9
### The days of reckoning

---

## No more joy (Verses 1–6)

[1] Do not rejoice, Israel.
Do not celebrate like the nations.
For you have been unfaithful to your God.
You love the pay of a prostitute on every threshing floor.
A threshing floor is a flat place where grain is separated from the straw.
[2] The threshing floor and the winepress will not feed them.
The new wine will fail them.
[3] They will not stay in the LORD’s land.
Ephraim will go back to Egypt,
and they will eat unclean food in Assyria.
[4] They will not pour out wine offerings to the LORD.
Their sacrifices will not please him.
Their sacrifices will be to them like the bread of mourners.
All who eat it will become unclean.
For their bread will only be for their own hunger.
It will not come into the house of the LORD.
[5] What will you do on the day of the holy gathering,
on the day of the LORD’s feast?
[6] For when they run away from destruction,
Egypt will gather them up.
Memphis will bury them.
Weeds will take over their precious things of silver.
Thorns will grow in their tents.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The harvest festival is being celebrated, but Hosea calls out: “Do not rejoice!” On the threshing floors, the people thanked Baal, the god of fertility, for the harvest. Hosea calls this “the pay of a prostitute”: They believe the harvest is Baal’s payment to them.
> Verses 3–4: In the exile (in Assyria), they will no longer be able to celebrate feasts for God. They will have to eat unclean food. Their bread will be like “the bread of mourners”: Bread in a house where someone had died was seen as unclean.
> Verse 5: A sad question: “What will you do on the day of the holy gathering?” Without the temple and without the land, there are no more feasts.
> Verse 6: Memphis in Egypt was known for its large burial grounds. Whoever flees to Egypt will be buried there. The abandoned land will be covered with weeds and thorns.

## The prophet is a fool? (Verses 7–9)

[7] The days of punishment have come.
The days of reckoning have come.
Reckoning means: Everyone must answer for what they have done.
Israel will think that the prophet is a fool,
and that the man filled with the Spirit is crazy.
This is because your sins are so many,
and because your hatred is so great.
[8] A prophet keeps watch over Ephraim together with my God.
But a bird catcher’s trap is set on all his paths,
and there is hatred in the house of his God.
[9] They have sunk deep into evil,
as in the days of Gibeah.
God will remember their guilt.
He will punish them for their sins.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The people laugh at Hosea: “Israel will think that the prophet is a fool, and that the man filled with the Spirit is crazy.” People who say uncomfortable truths are often made fun of.
> Verse 8: The prophet is like a watchman (compare Ezekiel 3:17). But people set traps for him. Even in the temple, he meets hatred.
> Verse 9: “As in the days of Gibeah” is a reminder of a terrible crime in Gibeah: There a woman suffered cruel sexual violence that led to her death (Judges 19–21). Hosea says: That is how low the people have sunk.

## Like grapes in the wilderness (Verses 10–14)

[10] “I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness.
I saw your fathers
like the first ripe figs on a fig tree in its first season.
But they came to Baal Peor.
They gave themselves to that shameful god.
And they became as disgusting as the thing they loved.
[11] As for Ephraim, their glory will fly away like a bird.
There will be no birth,
no pregnancy,
and no conceiving of children.
[12] Even if they bring up their children,
I will take them away,
so that not one is left.
Yes, how terrible for them when I turn away from them!
[13] I have seen Ephraim like Tyre,
planted in a pleasant place.
But Ephraim will lead his children out to the killer.”
[14] Give them – LORD, what will you give them?
Give them wombs that lose their babies
and breasts that are dry.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: God remembers the beginning tenderly: When he found Israel, it was as precious as grapes in the wilderness or as the very first figs. Love at first sight!
> But at Baal Peor, Israel already worshiped the god Baal in the wilderness (Numbers 25). God says: “They became as disgusting as the thing they loved” (Hosea 9:10). This means: We become like what we love and worship.
> Verses 11–14: These verses are very hard. Baal, the god of fertility, was supposed to give children. Hosea says: Exactly the opposite will happen. Idol worship brings childlessness and death.
> Verse 14: Hosea himself breaks off and hesitates: “Give them – LORD, what will you give them?” You can feel his pain. He does not know what to pray.
> Very important: These verses describe a judgment on a whole people at a certain time. They do not mean in any way that a miscarriage, a stillbirth, or not being able to have children is God’s punishment for a person. Anyone who has lost a child is not to blame. ## Wanderers among the nations (Verses 15–17)

[15] “All their evil is in Gilgal.
That is where I began to hate them.
Because of the evil things they have done,
I will drive them out of my house.
I will not love them anymore.
All their leaders are rebels.
[16] Ephraim has been struck down.
Their root has dried up.
They will bear no fruit.
Even if they give birth,
I will kill the dear children they bear.”
[17] My God will reject them,
because they did not listen to him.
And they will be wanderers among the nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: In Gilgal, Israel had once made Saul its first king (1 Samuel 11:15), even though God himself wanted to be their king. Later there was also idol worship there.
> “I will not love them anymore.” That is a terrible sentence. But you have to read the whole book: In Chapters 11 and 14, God says exactly the opposite: “How can I give you up?” and “I will love them freely” (14:4). God’s anger is real, but his love has the last word.
> Verse 16: A play on words: “Ephraim” sounds like “fruitful.” But now Ephraim will bear no more fruit.
> Verse 17: “Wanderers among the nations.” That came true: After 722 before Christ, the Israelites of the northern kingdom were taken away by the Assyrians and scattered among the nations.
> This verse is not permission for violence against children either. In the Bible, children always deserve protection (compare Mark 10:14: “Let the little children come to me!”).
