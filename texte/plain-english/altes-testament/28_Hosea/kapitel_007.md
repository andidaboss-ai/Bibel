# Hosea – Chapter 7
### A flat cake that was not turned over

---

## Burning like an oven (Verses 1–7)

[1] “When I want to heal Israel,
then the guilt of Ephraim comes to light,
and the evil of Samaria too.
For they cheat and lie.
The thief breaks in,
and gangs of robbers rob people out on the streets.
[2] They do not think in their hearts
that I remember all their evil.
Now their own deeds surround them.
They are right in front of my face.
[3] They make the king glad with their evil,
and the leaders with their lies.
[4] They are all unfaithful, like people who commit adultery.
They are burning like an oven.
The baker does not even need to stir the fire
from the time he kneads the dough
until it has risen.
[5] On the day of our king’s celebration,
the leaders made themselves sick with the heat of wine.
The king joined hands with people who mock.
[6] For they have made their hearts ready like an oven,
while they lie in wait.
Their anger smolders all night.
In the morning it blazes up like a flaming fire.
[7] They are all as hot as an oven.
They devour their judges.
All their kings have fallen.
Not one of them calls to me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A sad sentence: God wants to heal. But the more closely he looks, the more evil comes to light.
> Verses 3–7: Hosea describes the political situation in the northern kingdom. In his time, several kings were murdered one after the other (compare 2 Kings 15). The plotters flatter the king, get drunk with him at the feast, and then they strike.
> The picture of the oven: Their passion and their desire to kill glow in secret like an oven overnight. And in the morning they break out like fire.
> Verse 7: “Not one of them calls to me.” In all the chaos, no one asks about God.

---

## Ephraim does not notice (Verses 8–10)

[8] “Ephraim mixes himself with the nations.
Ephraim is a flat cake that was not turned over.
[9] Strangers have eaten up his strength,
and he does not notice it.
Yes, gray hairs have appeared here and there on him,
and he does not notice it.
[10] The pride of Israel speaks against him to his face.
And still, in spite of all this, they have not returned to the LORD their God.
They have not looked for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: A vivid picture from the kitchen: a flat bread that someone forgot to turn over on the hot stone. It is burned on one side and raw on the other. Half this way, half that way. Israel is neither fully with God nor fully with the nations. It is useless.
> Verse 9: A sad picture: Israel grows old and weak without noticing it. Gray hairs appear, but Israel thinks it is still young and strong. The foreign powers have eaten up its strength.
> For today: Sometimes we do not notice how things slowly weaken us, until it is almost too late.

## A silly dove (Verses 11–16)

[11] “Ephraim is like a dove that is easily fooled
and has no sense.
They call to Egypt.
They go to Assyria.
[12] When they go,
I will spread my net over them.
I will bring them down like the birds of the sky.
I will punish them,
as their community has been told.
[13] How terrible for them!
For they have wandered away from me.
Destruction will come on them!
For they have sinned against me.
I wanted to set them free,
but they have spoken lies against me.
[14] They have not cried out to me from their hearts.
Instead, they howl on their beds.
They gather together for grain and new wine.
They turn away from me.
[15] I trained them and made their arms strong,
and yet they plan evil against me.
[16] They turn back, but not to the Most High.
They are like a bow that does not shoot straight.
Their leaders will fall by the sword
because of their angry words.
For this they will be laughed at in the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Israel is like a confused dove that flutters back and forth: now to Egypt, now to Assyria. It looks for protection from the great powers, but not from God.
> Verse 13: “I wanted to set them free.” God was ready to help. But they turned away.
> Verse 14: They howl and complain on their beds, but only because they are hungry (grain and new wine). They do not cry out to God “from their hearts.” They care about food, not about their relationship with God.
> Verse 16: “They turn back, but not to the Most High.” They change direction, but toward the wrong thing. Like a bow that does not shoot straight and misses.
