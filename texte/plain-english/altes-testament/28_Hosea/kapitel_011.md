# Hosea – Chapter 11
### How can I give you up?

---

## When Israel was young, I loved him (Verses 1–4)

[1] “When Israel was a child, I loved him.
And I called my son out of Egypt.
[2] The more they were called,
the more they went away from them.
They offered sacrifices to the Baals
and burned incense to carved idols.
[3] And yet it was I who taught Ephraim to walk.
I took them by their arms.
But they did not know that it was I who healed them.
[4] I led them with human ropes,
with ties of love.
I was to them like someone who lifts the yoke off their necks.
I bent down to him
and fed him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most tender texts in the whole Bible. God speaks like a father or a mother about a child.
> Verse 1: “When Israel was a child, I loved him.” God remembers the beginning, when he set Israel free from Egypt.
> The Gospel writer Matthew quotes this verse about Jesus, who fled to Egypt as a child with Mary and Joseph and came back: “Out of Egypt I called my son” (Matthew 2:15).
> Verse 3: “And yet it was I who taught Ephraim to walk.” Just as parents hold a child’s hands when it takes its first steps. “I took them by their arms.” But the child did not notice who had helped it.
> Verse 4: “With ties of love.” God did not pull with force, but with love. “I bent down to him and fed him.” Like a mother who bends down to her little child to feed it.
> This picture shows: God also has motherly sides.

---

## They do not want to turn back (Verses 5–7)

[5] “They will not go back to the land of Egypt.
But the Assyrian will be their king,
because they refused to turn back to me.
[6] The sword will fall on their cities.
It will destroy the bars of their gates
and put an end to their plans.
[7] My people are determined to turn away from me.
Even if they call to the Most High,
he will certainly not lift them up.

> **What does this mean?**
> The beloved child has turned away. And that has consequences: Assyria will rule over them. The sword will come.
> Here you can feel God’s pain: like parents whose child is going down a dangerous road and no longer listens to them.

---

## My heart is turned within me (Verses 8–11)

[8] “How can I give you up, Ephraim?
How can I hand you over, Israel?
How can I make you like Admah?
How can I make you like Zeboiim?
My heart is turned within me.
My compassion is stirred up.
[9] I will not act in my fierce anger.
I will not come back to destroy Ephraim.
For I am God, and not a human being.
I am the Holy One among you.
I will not come in anger.
[10] They will follow the LORD.
He will roar like a lion.
When he roars,
his children will come trembling from the west.
[11] They will come trembling like a bird out of Egypt,
and like a dove out of the land of Assyria.
And I will let them live in their homes,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point of the whole book!
> Verse 8: God struggles with himself: “How can I give you up, Ephraim?” Admah and Zeboiim were cities that were destroyed together with Sodom and Gomorrah (Deuteronomy 29:23). God says: I cannot do that to my beloved child!
> “My heart is turned within me.” God’s love is stronger than his anger. Compassion wins.
> Verse 9: The most important reason: “For I am God, and not a human being.” A human being might say: “That is enough now. You have only got what you deserve.” But God is different. Precisely because he is holy, precisely because he is God, he can forgive where people would give up.
> Verses 10–11: God roars like a lion, but not to attack. He roars to call his children. And they come trembling, like birds, from everywhere, back home.
> These verses show God’s heart. Jesus tells the same message in the parable of the lost son: The father sees his son from far away, is moved with compassion, runs to meet him, and hugs him (Luke 15:20).

---

## Lies and deceit (Verse 12)

[12] Ephraim surrounds me with lies,
and the house of Israel with deceit.
Judah still wanders away from God
and is unfaithful to the Holy One.

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, this verse is the first verse of Chapter 12 (12:1). That is why the verse numbers in Chapter 12 of the WEB are one lower than in many other Bibles.
> After God’s wonderful confession of love comes reality: The people go on lying and deceiving. God’s love is there, but people also have to accept it.
> The meaning of the second part is not quite clear in Hebrew. Some translations understand it in a positive way (Judah still holds on to God). The WEB understands it in a negative way.
