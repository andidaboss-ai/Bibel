# Hosea – Chapter 3
### Love that does not give up

---

## Go again and love her (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD said to me,
“Go again.
Love a woman who is loved by another man
and who is unfaithful to her husband.
Love her just as the LORD loves the people of Israel,
even though they turn to other gods
and love raisin cakes.”
[2] So I bought her for myself
for 15 pieces of silver
and a homer and a half of barley.
That is about 9 bushels of barley.
[3] I said to her,
“You must stay with me for many days.
You must not act like a prostitute.
You must not be with any other man.
And I will act the same way toward you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gomer has left Hosea and is living with another man. Maybe she has even fallen into debt or into someone else’s power. God says to Hosea: “Go again. Love a woman …”
> That is unbelievable: After all the hurt, Hosea is to bring his wife back, “just as the LORD loves the people of Israel, even though they turn to other gods.”
> Verse 1: “Raisin cakes” were eaten at festivals for foreign gods.
> Verse 2: Hosea has to buy her freedom. The price (15 pieces of silver and barley) was about the price of a female slave. Maybe Gomer had become a slave because of debt. A homer is about 6 bushels.
> Verse 3: Hosea promises to be faithful to her: “And I will act the same way toward you.” It is a new beginning, with a time of quiet and of thinking things over.
> Christians see here a picture of God’s love in Jesus Christ: God “buys free” (redeems) those who have turned away from him.
> Important for today: This chapter is not advice to stay in a relationship where you are hurt, cheated on, or abused. It describes God’s love. If you live in a relationship that harms you, you may get help and protect yourself.

## Many days without a king (Verses 4–5)

[4] For the people of Israel will live for many days
without a king and without a leader,
without sacrifice and without a sacred stone,
without an ephod and without idols.
An ephod is a special garment of the priest, used to ask God for answers.
[5] After that, the people of Israel will return.
They will look for the LORD their God
and for David their king.
They will come trembling to the LORD
and to his goodness
in the last days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Just as Gomer has to live apart for a while, Israel will also be without a king, without temple worship, and without idols for a long time. That happened in the exile.
> But after that comes the return: They will look for God and for “David their king,” that is, a king from the family of David. Jews expect the Messiah from David’s family. Christians see this fulfilled in Jesus, the “Son of David.”
> “Come trembling to the LORD and to his goodness”: not out of fear of punishment, but full of awe at a goodness that they have not deserved.
> The message of the first three chapters: God’s love is stronger than people’s unfaithfulness. He does not give up.
