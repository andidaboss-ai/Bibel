# Hosea – Chapter 2
### I will speak tenderly to her

---

## The unfaithful wife (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Say to your brothers, ‘My people!’
And say to your sisters, ‘My loved one!’
[2] Bring charges against your mother!
Bring charges, for she is not my wife,
and I am not her husband.
Let her put away the signs of her prostitution from her face,
and her unfaithfulness from between her breasts.
[3] If she does not,
I will strip her naked.
I will make her as bare as on the day she was born.
I will make her like a wilderness.
I will make her like a dry land,
and I will let her die of thirst.
[4] I will have no mercy on her children,
for they are children of unfaithfulness.
[5] For their mother has acted like a prostitute.
The woman who became pregnant with them has acted shamefully.
For she said,
‘I will go after my lovers.
They give me my bread and my water,
my wool and my linen,
my oil and my drinks.’

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, the verses of this chapter are two higher (2:3–25).
> Verse 1: The names of the children are turned around: “Lo-Ammi” (not my people) becomes “Ammi” (my people), and “Lo-Ruhamah” (no mercy) becomes “Ruhamah” (loved one, who finds mercy).
> Verses 2–5: The “mother” is the land of Israel as a whole. She is unfaithful to her husband (God). Her “lovers” are the Baal gods. The people believed that Baal gave them rain, bread, wine, and wool. They had forgotten that everything comes from God.
> Verse 3: At that time, stripping a woman naked was a punishment for adultery. Adultery means: A married person has sex with someone else. Here it is a picture of what will happen to the land: It will become bare and empty like a desert.
> Very important: This picture never justifies a man humiliating, beating, or shaming his wife. Behavior like that is violence and is wrong. If you experience violence in a relationship: For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## She did not know that it was I (Verses 6–13)

[6] “So I will block her road with thorns.
I will build a wall against her,
so that she cannot find her way.
[7] She will run after her lovers,
but she will not catch up with them.
She will look for them,
but she will not find them.
Then she will say,
‘I will go back to my first husband,
for back then it was better for me than now.’
[8] For she did not know
that it was I who gave her the grain, the new wine, and the oil.
I gave her more and more silver and gold,
and they used it for Baal.
[9] “So I will take back my grain at harvest time,
and my new wine when it is ready.
I will take away my wool and my linen,
which were meant to cover her naked body.
[10] Now I will show her shame
in front of her lovers.
And no one will rescue her from my hand.
[11] I will also put an end to all her celebrations:
her feasts, her New Moon festivals, her Sabbaths,
and all her holy gatherings.
[12] I will ruin her grapevines and her fig trees.
She said about them,
‘These are my pay that my lovers have given me.’
I will turn them into a thicket,
and the animals of the field will eat them.
[13] I will punish her for the days of the Baals,
when she burned incense to them.
She put on her earrings and her jewelry.
She went after her lovers,
and she forgot me,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: God “blocks the road.” He lets the people notice that the false gods do not help. That sounds hard, but it has a goal: The people are to come back. “Back then it was better for me.” Like the lost son who remembers his father’s house (Luke 15:17).
> Verse 8: The heart of the problem: “She did not know that it was I who gave her the grain, the new wine, and the oil.” The people gave the credit for God’s gifts to the false gods.
> Verse 13: “And she forgot me.” That is God’s real pain: being forgotten by the one you love.
> For today: We too often forget where the good things in our lives come from. And we thank the wrong one, for example only our own skill or money.

## I will lead her into the wilderness (Verses 14–17)

[14] “So I will win her back with love.
I will bring her into the wilderness
and speak tenderly to her.
[15] From there I will give her back her vineyards.
I will make the valley of Achor a door of hope.
There she will answer me as in the days when she was young,
as on the day when she came up out of the land of Egypt.
[16] On that day,” says the LORD,
“you will call me ‘my husband.’
You will no longer call me ‘my master.’
[17] For I will take the names of the Baals out of her mouth.
Their names will no longer be spoken.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most tender texts in the Bible! After all the threats, there is suddenly a “So” that no one expects: Not “So I will punish her,” but “So I will win her back with love … and speak tenderly to her.” God tries to win his people’s love, like someone in love.
> The wilderness is a reminder of the first love between God and Israel, when they came out of Egypt (compare Jeremiah 2:2). There, where there is nothing to distract, the relationship can begin again.
> Verse 15: The valley of Achor (“valley of trouble”) was a place of disaster (Joshua 7:24–26). Now it becomes a “door of hope.” God can turn places of trouble into doors of hope.
> Verse 16: A play on words: The WEB translates “my master.” In Hebrew, the word there is “Baali,” that is, “my Baal.” In Hebrew, “Baal” also means “lord” or “owner,” and it was used for a husband. God does not want to be called “my Baal” (my owner), but “my husband” (Hebrew “Ishi”). God wants a relationship of love, not of power.

## I will make you my bride forever (Verses 18–23)

[18] “On that day, I will make a covenant for them
with the animals of the field,
with the birds of the sky,
and with the creatures that crawl on the ground.
A covenant is a binding agreement.
I will break the bow and the sword and put an end to war in the land.
I will let them lie down in safety.
[19] I will make you my bride forever.
Yes, I will make you my bride
with righteousness and justice,
with loving kindness and compassion.
[20] I will make you my bride with faithfulness,
and you will know the LORD.
[21] “On that day,
I will answer,” says the LORD.
“I will answer the heavens,
and they will answer the earth.
[22] The earth will answer the grain, the new wine, and the oil,
and they will answer Jezreel.
[23] I will sow her for myself in the land, like seed.
I will have mercy on her who had not received mercy.
I will say to those who were not my people, ‘You are my people.’
And they will say, ‘You are my God!’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A covenant of peace with all of creation. No more war. A picture of paradise.
> Verses 19–20: God makes a new engagement, and he brings the “wedding gifts” himself: righteousness, justice, loving kindness, compassion, and faithfulness. In Judaism, these words are spoken every morning when the prayer straps (tefillin) are wound around the finger.
> “You will know the LORD.” In Hebrew, “knowing” does not only mean knowledge, but a deep, personal relationship.
> Verses 21–22: A chain of blessing: God answers the heavens, the heavens answer the earth (with rain), the earth answers the grain, and all together answer the people.
> Verse 23: The names of the children are finally turned around. “No mercy” becomes “mercy.” “Not my people” becomes “my people.” And the people answer: “You are my God!” The relationship is restored.
> 1 Peter 2:10 also quotes this verse: “In the past you were not a people, but now you are God’s people.”
