# Hosea – Chapter 10
### Break up your unplowed ground!

---

## A rich vine with a divided heart (Verses 1–8)

[1] Israel is a rich, spreading vine
that produces his fruit.
The more fruit he had,
the more altars he built.
The better things went for his land,
the more beautifully they decorated their sacred stones.
[2] Their heart is divided.
Now they will be found guilty.
God will tear down their altars.
He will destroy their sacred stones.
[3] Now they will surely say,
“We have no king,
because we do not fear the LORD.
And the king, what could he do for us?”
[4] They make promises.
They swear falsely when they make agreements.
So judgment springs up like poisonous weeds
in the rows of a plowed field.
[5] The people who live in Samaria will be terrified
about the calves of Beth Aven.
Its people will mourn over it,
together with its priests who used to celebrate over it,
over its glory,
because the glory has left it.
[6] It too will be carried to Assyria
as a gift for a great king.
Ephraim will be put to shame.
Israel will be ashamed of his own plans.
[7] Samaria and her king will float away
like a twig on the water.
[8] The high places of Aven, the sin of Israel,
will also be destroyed.
High places were places of worship on hills.
Thorns and thistles will grow up on their altars.
They will say to the mountains, “Cover us!”
and to the hills, “Fall on us!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A sad contradiction: The richer Israel became, the more altars it built for foreign gods. Wealth did not lead to thankfulness toward God, but to more idol worship.
> Verse 2: “Their heart is divided.” They want both: a little bit of God and a little bit of Baal. But a divided heart does not hold together.
> Verses 5–6: The golden calf in Bethel (mockingly called “Beth Aven” = house of evil) is carried away by the Assyrians as loot. The god they trusted is itself dragged away!
> Verse 7: A strong picture: The king floats helplessly like a twig on the water.
> Verse 8: “They will say to the mountains, ‘Cover us!’ and to the hills, ‘Fall on us!’” That is how great their despair is. Jesus says these words on the way to the cross (Luke 23:30). They also appear in Revelation (Revelation 6:16).

---

## Break up your unplowed ground (Verses 9–15)

[9] “Israel, you have sinned since the days of Gibeah.
They have stayed there.
The battle against the wicked people
does not reach them in Gibeah.
[10] When I want to, I will punish them.
The nations will gather against them
when they are tied to their two sins.
[11] Ephraim is a trained young cow
that loves to thresh grain.
So I will put a yoke on her beautiful neck.
I will put a rider on Ephraim.
Judah will plow.
Jacob will break up the clods of earth.
[12] Sow righteousness for yourselves.
Then you will reap the fruit of kindness.
Break up your unplowed ground,
for it is time to look for the LORD,
until he comes and rains righteousness on you.
[13] You have plowed evil.
You have reaped wrongdoing.
You have eaten the fruit of lies.
For you trusted in your own way
and in your many strong soldiers.
[14] So the roar of battle will rise up among your people.
All your fortresses will be destroyed,
just as Shalman destroyed Beth Arbel on the day of battle.
There the mother was smashed to pieces together with her children.
[15] That is what Bethel will do to you
because of your great evil.
At dawn, the king of Israel will be completely destroyed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Israel was like a young cow that likes to thresh grain. When threshing, the cow was allowed to eat (Deuteronomy 25:4). It was pleasant work. Now it has to carry the heavy yoke of the plow. A yoke is a wooden bar put on the neck of animals that pull a plow.
> Verse 12: A beautiful call: “Sow righteousness for yourselves. Then you will reap the fruit of kindness. Break up your unplowed ground.” Righteousness means: living right before God. Unplowed ground is a field that has not been used for a long time and has become hard. You have to break it up so that something can grow again. In the same way, the people should break up their hard hearts and start over. Compare Jeremiah 4:3.
> “It is time to look for the LORD.” It is never too late for a new beginning.
> Verse 13: The opposite: Whoever plows evil reaps wrongdoing. Whoever trusts in their own strength and soldiers eats “the fruit of lies.”
> Verse 14: Beth Arbel was a city that was cruelly destroyed. No one knows for sure who “Shalman” was. The sentence “There the mother was smashed to pieces together with her children” describes the cruelty of war. The Bible does not keep quiet about what war does to women and children.
