# Hosea – Chapter 6
### I desire mercy, not sacrifice

---

## Come, let us return! (Verses 1–3)

[1] “Come! Let us return to the LORD.
For he has torn us to pieces,
and he will heal us.
He has wounded us,
and he will bandage our wounds.
[2] After two days he will bring us back to life.
On the third day he will raise us up,
and we will live in his presence.
[3] Let us know the LORD.
Let us try hard to know the LORD.
As surely as the sun rises,
the LORD will appear.
He will come to us like the rain,
like the spring rain that waters the earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This sounds like a beautiful song about turning back to God. The people say: “Come! Let us return to the LORD. He has wounded us, and he will heal us.”
> Verse 2: “On the third day he will raise us up.” Christians have often connected this verse with the resurrection of Jesus on the third day (compare 1 Corinthians 15:4: “He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures”). In its original setting, it means: God will quickly raise his people up again.
> But be careful: The verses that follow show that God does not fully trust this song. Does this turning back sound too easy, as if it were a matter of course? As if you only had to say a few pious words, and God would make everything all right again?

---

## Your love is like the morning dew (Verses 4–6)

[4] “Ephraim, what should I do with you?
Judah, what should I do with you?
For your love is like a morning cloud,
like the dew that disappears early.
[5] That is why I have cut them to pieces through the prophets.
I have killed them with the words of my mouth.
My judgments against you flash out like lightning.
[6] For I desire mercy, not sacrifice.
I desire the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Here God sounds like desperate parents: “Ephraim, what should I do with you?” The people’s love is like the morning dew: In the morning it is there, but as soon as the sun shines, it is gone. It does not last.
> Verse 6: One of the most important sentences in the whole Bible: “For I desire mercy, not sacrifice. I desire the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”
> The Hebrew word “chesed” means goodness, faithfulness, and loving mercy all at once. God does not want religious rituals without the heart. He wants real love, faithfulness, and mercy.
> Jesus quotes this verse twice: when people blame him for eating with sinners (Matthew 9:13), and when people criticize his disciples because of the Sabbath (Matthew 12:7). “But go and learn what this means: ‘I desire mercy, and not sacrifice’” (Matthew 9:13).
> This verse is also very important in Judaism. After the temple was destroyed, Rabbi Johanan ben Zakkai taught with this verse: Acts of love now make up for sin, as the sacrifices did before.

## They have broken the covenant (Verses 7–11)

[7] “But like Adam, they have broken the covenant.
There they were unfaithful to me.
[8] Gilead is a city of people who do evil.
It is stained with blood.
[9] Like gangs of robbers who lie in wait for a man,
so the priests join together and murder on the road to Shechem.
They commit shameful crimes.
[10] In the house of Israel I have seen something horrible.
There Ephraim acts like a prostitute.
Israel has made itself unclean.
[11] “And for you too, Judah, a harvest has been set,
when I turn things around for my people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: “Like Adam” – this can mean: like the first human being in paradise, who broke God’s command. Or “Adam” here is the name of a place (a town by the Jordan, compare Joshua 3:16). The word “there” points to this. Both are possible.
> Verses 8–9: Terrible conditions: violence and murder, even by priests. The people who should serve God have become robbers.
> Verse 11: Judah too will get its “harvest,” that is, the result of what it has done. But the verse ends with hope: “when I turn things around for my people.” In the WEB, the words are: when I restore the fortunes of my people.
