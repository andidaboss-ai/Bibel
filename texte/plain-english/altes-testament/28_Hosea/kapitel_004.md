# Hosea – Chapter 4
### No faithfulness, no love, no knowledge of God

---

## God brings charges against his people (Verses 1–3)

[1] Listen to the word of the LORD, you people of Israel.
For the LORD has a charge against the people who live in the land:
“There is no truth,
no goodness,
and no knowledge of God in the land.
[2] There is cursing, lying, murder, stealing, and adultery.
People break through every limit.
One killing follows another.
[3] That is why the land will mourn.
Everyone who lives in it will waste away,
together with all living things in it,
with the animals of the field and the birds of the sky.
Yes, even the fish in the sea are dying.

> **What does this mean?**
> God brings “charges” against his people, as in a court of law.
> Verse 1: Three things are missing in the land: truth (faithfulness, being someone others can count on), goodness (love, mercy), and knowledge of God. These are the foundations of every good community.
> Verse 2: Instead, there is cursing, lying, murder, stealing, and adultery. That is almost exactly the Ten Commandments, only turned around (compare Exodus 20:7, 13–16). When people forget God, their life together also breaks apart.
> Verse 3: Even nature suffers: Animals, birds, and fish die. The wrong that people do has consequences for all of creation. A thought that is very relevant today, when we see the destruction of the environment and the loss of many kinds of animals and plants.

---

## The guilt of the priests (Verses 4–10)

[4] “But let no one bring charges,
and let no one blame others.
For your people are like those who bring charges against a priest.
[5] You will stumble in the daytime,
and the prophet will also stumble with you at night.
And I will destroy your mother.
[6] My people are destroyed because they lack knowledge.
Because you have rejected knowledge,
I will also reject you.
You will no longer be a priest for me.
Because you have forgotten the law of your God,
I will also forget your children.
[7] The more of them there were,
the more they sinned against me.
I will turn their glory into shame.
[8] They feed on the sin of my people.
They are eager for them to do more wrong.
[9] It will be the same for the people and for the priest.
I will punish them for the way they live.
I will pay them back for what they have done.
[10] They will eat, but they will not have enough.
They will act like prostitutes, but they will not have more children.
For they have stopped listening to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God speaks directly to the priests. They should have taught the people who God is and what he wants. But they did not do it.
> Verse 6: A famous sentence: “My people are destroyed because they lack knowledge.” The people no longer know God, because no one has taught them. The priests are to blame.
> Verse 8: A heavy accusation: The priests “feed on the sin” of the people. They received a share of the sin offerings. The more the people sinned, the more the priests earned. Instead of calling the people to turn back, they profited from their guilt.
> Verse 9: “The same for the people and for the priest.” The spiritual leaders are no better than the others.
> For today: Whoever is responsible for others (in church, at school, or in the family) should pass on knowledge and values and must not take advantage of others.

## Idol worship under the trees (Verses 11–14)

[11] Prostitution, wine, and new wine take away people’s good sense.
[12] My people ask their wooden idol for advice.
They expect a stick of wood to give them answers.
For a spirit of prostitution has led them astray.
They have been unfaithful to their God.
[13] They offer sacrifices on the tops of the mountains.
They burn incense on the hills,
under oaks, poplars, and terebinths,
because their shade is good.
That is why your daughters act like prostitutes,
and your brides are unfaithful to their husbands.
[14] I will not punish your daughters when they act like prostitutes,
or your brides when they are unfaithful.
For the men themselves go off with prostitutes.
They offer sacrifices with the shrine prostitutes.
So a people without understanding will come to ruin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The people ask a piece of wood (an idol or a stick used to tell the future) instead of asking God.
> Verse 13: Under shady trees on the hills, people celebrated fertility cults, often with sexual rituals. A terebinth is a large tree with wide branches.
> Verse 14: A remarkable verse! God says: I will not punish the young women. For the men themselves go to the prostitutes and take part in the cults. God stands against the double standard where women are punished while men do the same thing. The main responsibility belongs to the men, who set the example and have the power.
> The WEB speaks of “shrine prostitutes.” Scholars disagree about whether there really was “holy prostitution” in Israel. What is sure is this: The women in these cults were often exploited.

## Ephraim is joined to idols (Verses 15–19)

[15] “Israel, even if you act like a prostitute,
do not let Judah become guilty!
Do not go to Gilgal.
Do not go up to Beth Aven.
And do not swear, ‘As surely as the LORD lives.’
[16] For Israel has been very stubborn, like a stubborn young cow.
How then can the LORD feed them like a lamb in a meadow?
[17] Ephraim has joined himself to idols.
Leave him alone!
[18] Their drink has turned sour.
They act like prostitutes all the time.
Their rulers dearly love their shameful ways.
[19] The wind has wrapped her up in its wings.
They will be put to shame because of their sacrifices.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: Gilgal and Beth Aven (“house of evil”) were places of worship in the northern kingdom. “Beth Aven” is a mocking name for Bethel (“house of God”). The house of God has become a house of evil.
> Judah (the southern kingdom) should not make the same mistakes.
> Verse 16: A stubborn cow that does not want to be led. God wants to feed his people like a shepherd feeds his lambs, but they do not let themselves be led.
> Verse 17: “Ephraim” is another name for the northern kingdom of Israel (after its largest tribe). “Leave him alone!” – a sad sentence. Sometimes you have to let people go their own way.
