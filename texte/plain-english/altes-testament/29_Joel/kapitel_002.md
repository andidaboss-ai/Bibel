# Joel – Chapter 2
### Tear your hearts

---

## The day of the LORD is coming (Verses 1–11)

[1] Blow the trumpet in Zion!
Sound the alarm on my holy mountain!
Let all the people who live in the land tremble,
for the day of the LORD is coming.
It is very near.
[2] It is a day of darkness and gloom,
a day of clouds and thick darkness.
Like the dawn spreading over the mountains,
a great and strong people comes.
There has never been anything like it,
and there will never be anything like it again,
not for many generations.
[3] A fire burns up everything in front of them.
Behind them, a flame burns.
In front of them, the land is like the garden of Eden.
Behind them, it is an empty wilderness.
Yes, no one has escaped them.
[4] They look like horses,
and they run like horsemen.
[5] They leap on the tops of the mountains
with a noise like chariots,
with a noise like flames of fire burning up dry straw,
like a strong army lined up for battle.
[6] When the peoples see them, they are in agony.
Every face turns pale.
[7] They run like mighty soldiers.
They climb the wall like warriors.
Each one marches in his line.
They do not leave their path.
[8] They do not push each other.
Each one marches in his own track.
They break through the defenses
and do not break their ranks.
[9] They rush into the city.
They run on the wall.
They climb up into the houses.
They come in through the windows like thieves.
[10] The earth shakes in front of them.
The heavens tremble.
The sun and the moon grow dark,
and the stars stop shining.
[11] The LORD thunders with his voice in front of his army.
For his army is very great.
The one who carries out his command is strong.
For the day of the LORD is great and very terrible.
Who can live through it?

> **What does this mean?**
> Joel describes the locusts like a huge, perfectly organized army. Anyone who has ever seen a swarm of locusts knows this: The animals darken the sky, they eat everything, and they even get into the houses through the windows.
> Verse 3: Before, the land is like paradise. Afterward, it is a desert.
> Verse 4: The head of a locust really does look a little like a horse’s head. In Italian, the locust is even called “cavalletta,” “little horse.”
> Verse 11: And the frightening part: This army is God’s army. The “day of the LORD” is a day of judgment. “Who can live through it?”

---

## Turn back with all your heart (Verses 12–14)

[12] “But even now,” says the LORD,
“turn back to me with all your heart,
with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning.”
[13] Tear your hearts, not your clothes,
and turn back to the LORD your God.
For he is gracious and merciful.
He is slow to become angry, and he is full of loving kindness.
He is ready to change his mind about sending disaster.
[14] Who knows?
Maybe he will turn and change his mind.
Maybe he will leave a blessing behind him,
a grain offering and a drink offering
for the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “But even now!” In the middle of the disaster, it is not too late. God invites the people to turn back.
> Verse 13: A famous sentence: “Tear your hearts, not your clothes.” At that time, people tore their clothes as a sign of grief. But God does not want outward signs. He wants a real change in the heart.
> “He is gracious and merciful. He is slow to become angry, and he is full of loving kindness” – that is how God described himself to Moses (Exodus 34:6). It is one of the most important statements about God in the Old Testament.
> Verse 14: “Who knows?” Joel does not promise anything. God is free. But we may hope.
> This passage is read in many churches on Ash Wednesday, at the beginning of Lent, the time of fasting before Easter.

## Gather everyone! (Verses 15–17)

[15] Blow the trumpet in Zion!
Set apart a holy time of fasting!
Call a holy gathering!
[16] Gather the people!
Make the gathering holy!
Bring the elders together!
Gather the children
and the babies who are still nursing!
Let the bridegroom come out of his room
and the bride out of her room.
[17] Let the priests, who serve the LORD,
weep between the porch of the temple and the altar.
Let them say,
“Spare your people, LORD!
Do not let your own people be put to shame,
with the nations ruling over them.
Why should they say among the peoples,
‘Where is their God?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Everyone should come: old people and children, even the babies, even the bride and groom who are just getting married. No one is left out. In times of trouble, the whole community stands together before God.
> Verse 17: The priests pray: “Spare your people, LORD!” And they have a strong argument: What will the other nations think? “Where is their God?” God’s honor is at stake.

---

## God answers (Verses 18–20)

[18] Then the LORD became zealous for his land.
He had pity on his people.
[19] The LORD answered his people,
“I will send you grain, new wine, and oil.
You will have enough of them and be satisfied.
I will never again make you a joke among the nations.
[20] I will drive the army from the north far away from you.
I will push it into a dry and empty land,
its front into the eastern sea,
and its back into the western sea.
Its stink will rise up,
and its bad smell will go up.”
Surely he has done great things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is the turning point of the book: God has pity. He answers the prayer of the people.
> Verse 20: The “army from the north” is probably the locusts (or an enemy that they stand for). God drives them into the sea. Swarms of locusts really are sometimes blown into the sea by the wind and die there. Then the dead animals stink terribly on the coast.
> The “eastern sea” is the Dead Sea. The “western sea” is the Mediterranean Sea.

## Rejoice! (Verses 21–27)

[21] Land, do not be afraid!
Be glad and rejoice,
for the LORD has done great things.
[22] Do not be afraid, you animals of the field!
For the pastures of the wilderness are turning green.
The trees are bearing their fruit.
The fig tree and the grapevine give their full harvest.
[23] “So be glad, you people of Zion!
Rejoice in the LORD your God!
For he gives you the early rain in the right amount.
He makes the rain come down for you,
the early rain and the late rain, as before.
[24] The threshing floors will be full of wheat.
The tanks will overflow with new wine and oil.
[25] I will give you back what you lost in the years
that the swarming locust has eaten,
the great locust, the grasshopper, and the caterpillar,
my great army that I sent among you.
[26] You will have plenty to eat and be satisfied.
You will praise the name of the LORD your God,
who has done wonderful things for you.
And my people will never again be put to shame.
[27] You will know that I am in the middle of Israel,
and that I am the LORD your God, and there is no other.
And my people will never again be put to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the great joy. The land, the animals, and the people should not be afraid anymore. God sends rain at the right time: the early rain in the fall (for sowing) and the late rain in the spring (for ripening).
> Verse 25: A wonderful promise: “I will give you back what you lost in the years that the swarming locust has eaten.” The swarming locust is a kind of locust. God can make up for lost time. Many people have found comfort in this verse when they felt they had lost years of their lives through illness, war, addiction, or guilt.
> Verse 27: “You will know that I am in the middle of Israel.” That is the greatest gift: not only grain and wine, but God’s nearness.

## I will pour out my Spirit (Verses 28–32)

[28] “And afterward, this will happen:
I will pour out my Spirit on all people.
Your sons and your daughters will prophesy.
To prophesy means: to speak a message from God.
Your old men will dream dreams.
Your young men will see visions.
[29] In those days, I will pour out my Spirit
even on the male and female servants.
[30] I will show wonders in the sky and on the earth:
blood, fire, and columns of smoke.
[31] The sun will turn dark,
and the moon will turn to blood,
before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.
[32] And this will happen:
Whoever calls on the name of the LORD will be saved.
For on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem
there will be people who escape,
as the LORD has said.
Among those who are left, there will be those whom the LORD calls.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, these verses form their own Chapter 3 (Joel 3:1–5).
> This is one of the greatest promises in the Bible. Before, God’s Spirit came only on individual people: prophets, kings, judges. Now God says: I will pour out my Spirit “on all people”! The WEB says: “on all flesh.”
> And on all of them, without any difference:
> – sons and daughters (men and women),
> – old and young,
> – even male and female servants (slaves, who were at the very bottom of society).
> God’s Spirit makes no difference between people because of whether they are men or women, old or young, or rich or poor. Moses already had this wish: “I wish that all the LORD’s people were prophets” (Numbers 11:29).
> On the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came on the disciples of Jesus, Peter quotes exactly these verses: “But this is what has been spoken through the prophet Joel” (Acts 2:16–21).
> Verse 32: “Whoever calls on the name of the LORD will be saved.” Paul also quotes this sentence (Romans 10:13). Salvation is open to everyone who turns to God.
