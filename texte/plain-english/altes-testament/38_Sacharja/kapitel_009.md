# Zechariah – Chapter 9
### Your king is coming to you

---

## Judgment on the neighboring nations (Verses 1–8)

[1] A message from God.
The word of the LORD is against the land of Hadrach,
and it will come to rest on Damascus.
For the eyes of people
and of all the tribes of Israel are turned toward the LORD.
[2] It is also against Hamath, which borders on it,
and against Tyre and Sidon,
because they are very wise.
[3] Tyre built herself a fortress.
She piled up silver like dust
and fine gold like the mud in the streets.
[4] Look, the Lord will take away what she owns.
He will strike down her power on the sea,
and she will be eaten up by fire.
[5] Ashkelon will see it and be afraid.
Gaza too will twist in pain,
and so will Ekron, for her hope will come to nothing.
The king will disappear from Gaza,
and no one will live in Ashkelon.
[6] Foreigners will live in Ashdod,
and I will cut off the pride of the Philistines.
[7] I will take the blood out of their mouths
and the disgusting food from between their teeth.
They too will be left over for our God.
They will be like a clan leader in Judah,
and Ekron will be like a Jebusite.
[8] I will camp around my house to guard it against the army,
so that no one passes through or comes back.
No oppressor will pass through them anymore,
for now I have seen it with my own eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the second part of the book of Zechariah begins (Chapters 9–14). It sounds different from the first part and talks more about the far future.
> Verses 1–6: Judgment on the neighboring nations in the north (Hadrach, Damascus, Hamath, Tyre, Sidon) and in the west (the cities of the Philistines). Tyre was a rich trading city on an island, and people thought that no one could conquer it. Many interpreters think that these verses are about the war of Alexander the Great (333–332 BC). He really did conquer Tyre.
> Verse 7: A surprising turn: The Philistines are not only punished, they are made clean. They no longer have to eat blood and unclean sacrifices (the WEB says “abominations”). And then they too belong to God’s people, like a tribe in Judah. The Jebusites were the first people who lived in Jerusalem. Later they became part of Israel.
> Verse 8: God himself protects his temple.

---

## The king on the donkey (Verses 9–10)

[9] Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion!
Shout for joy, daughter of Jerusalem!
Look, your King is coming to you!
He is righteous, and he brings salvation.
He is humble, and riding on a donkey,
on a colt, the young one of a donkey.
[10] I will cut off the chariots from Ephraim
and the war horses from Jerusalem.
The bow for battle will be cut off.
And he will speak peace to the nations.
He will rule from sea to sea,
and from the River to the ends of the earth.
“The River” means the great river Euphrates.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most famous verses in the Bible.
> Verse 9: A king comes to Jerusalem. But he does not come on a war horse or in a chariot like other kings. He rides on a donkey, the animal of ordinary people and of peace. He is righteous, he brings salvation, and he is humble.
> Verse 10: This king does away with weapons: no more chariots, no more war horses, no more bows. He brings peace to the nations. His kingdom covers the whole world. This is a reminder of Psalm 72:8.
> All four Gospels tell how Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey. Matthew and John quote this verse directly (Matthew 21:4–5; John 12:14–15). Christians remember this on Palm Sunday.
> In Judaism, this verse is applied to the Messiah who is coming.

## Prisoners of hope (Verses 11–17)

[11] And as for you,
because of the blood of your covenant,
I have set your prisoners free from the pit
that has no water in it.
[12] Turn back to the fortress,
you prisoners of hope!
Even today I announce
that I will give you back twice as much.
[13] For I bend Judah like a bow for me.
I have put Ephraim on it like an arrow.
I will stir up your sons, Zion,
against your sons, Greece.
I will make you like the sword of a strong warrior.
[14] The LORD will appear over them.
His arrow will flash like lightning.
The Lord GOD will blow the trumpet
and will go out in the storms from the south.
[15] The LORD of Armies will defend them.
They will destroy and win with sling stones.
They will drink and roar as if from wine.
They will be full like bowls,
like the corners of the altar.
[16] The LORD their God will save them on that day,
like a flock, because they are his people.
For they are like the jewels of a crown,
shining high over his land.
[17] For how great is his goodness,
and how great is his beauty!
Grain will make the young men grow strong,
and new wine the young women.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: “Because of the blood of your covenant”: The covenant between God and Israel was sealed with blood at Mount Sinai (Exodus 24:8). Because of this covenant, God frees the prisoners from a pit without water, a picture of hopeless trouble (compare Joseph in Genesis 37:24; Jeremiah in Jeremiah 38:6). At the Last Supper, Jesus speaks of “my blood of the new covenant” (Mark 14:24).
> Verse 12: “Prisoners of hope” is a beautiful phrase. They are still prisoners, but they have hope. God promises to give them back twice as much as they have lost.
> Verses 13–15: A picture of battle: God uses his people like a bow and arrow against “Greece.” In Hebrew it is “Javan,” that is, the Greeks. Maybe the text is thinking of the Greek rulers after Alexander the Great. These pictures describe God’s protection for his people when they are in danger. They are not a call for people to make war. After all, the king from verse 10 is the one who brings peace.
> Verses 16–17: God saves his people like a shepherd saves his flock. They are as precious to him as jewels in a crown. At the end there is plenty: enough grain and wine for the young people.
