# Zechariah – Chapter 13
### A fountain to wash away sin and uncleanness

---

## A fountain is opened (Verses 1–6)

[1] “On that day a fountain will be opened
for the house of David and for the people of Jerusalem,
to wash away sin and uncleanness.
[2] On that day,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“I will cut off the names of the idols from the land,
and no one will remember them anymore.
I will also remove the prophets and the unclean spirit
from the land.
[3] And it will happen:
If anyone still prophesies,
his father and his mother, who gave birth to him, will say to him,
‘You must die,
because you speak lies in the name of the LORD.’
And his father and his mother, who gave birth to him,
will stab him when he prophesies.
[4] On that day
the prophets will be ashamed,
each one of his vision, when he prophesies.
They will no longer wear a coat of hair to fool people.
[5] Instead, he will say,
‘I am not a prophet.
I am a farmer.
I have been made a servant since I was young.’
[6] And someone will ask him,
‘What are these wounds between your arms?’
Then he will answer,
‘I got them in the house of my friends.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A wonderful picture: A fountain is opened that washes people clean from sin and uncleanness. It follows the mourning for the one who was pierced (12:10). Christians see in it a picture of forgiveness through Jesus. A well-known hymn by William Cowper begins with this picture: “There is a fountain filled with blood.”
> Verse 2: God will remove all idols and all false prophets from the land. This is about lying prophets who tell lies in God’s name (compare 10:2).
> Verse 3: To prophesy means to speak as a prophet. This is a very hard verse. It uses strong words to say: False prophecy will be taken so seriously that not even a person’s own parents will put up with it. This takes up the law in Deuteronomy 13:6–10 and 18:20.
> Important: This describes a future in which lies about God are no longer accepted. It is never a command for parents to hurt or kill their children. Violence in the family is wrong. If you experience violence at home, get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453. For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verses 4–6: The false prophets will be ashamed. They will no longer wear the “coat of hair,” the mark of prophets like Elijah (2 Kings 1:8). They will deny that they were ever prophets.
> The “wounds between the arms” (that is, on the chest or the back) were maybe cuts like the ones the prophets of idols made on themselves (1 Kings 18:28). The man makes an excuse: “I got them in the house of my friends” (Zechariah 13:6).
> Some Christians have applied verse 6 to the wounds of Jesus. But in this passage it is about a false prophet.

## Strike the shepherd! (Verses 7–9)

[7] “Wake up, sword, against my shepherd,
and against the man who is close to me,”
says the LORD of Armies.
“Strike the shepherd,
and the sheep will be scattered.
And I will turn my hand against the little ones.
[8] And in all the land,”
says the LORD,
“two parts of it will be cut off and die,
but the third part will be left in it.
[9] I will bring the third part into the fire.
I will make them pure, as silver is made pure,
and I will test them, as gold is tested.
They will call on my name,
and I will answer them.
I will say, ‘They are my people.’
And they will say, ‘The LORD is my God.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: God calls the sword against “my shepherd,” the “man who is close to me.” This shepherd is clearly a good shepherd who is very close to God. When he is struck, the sheep are scattered.
> Jesus quotes this verse in the night before his death: “Tonight all of you will fall away because of me. For it is written, ‘I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered’” (Mark 14:27; Matthew 26:31). To fall away means: to doubt him and leave him. Soon after that, all the disciples run away.
> Verses 8–9: A hard time of testing. Only a third is left. The numbers are a picture, not an exact count.
> But the goal is not destruction but making pure: As silver is cleaned of waste in the fire, God will make his people clean.
> At the end there are again the words of the covenant: “They are my people.” “The LORD is my God.” God and his people belong completely together again.
