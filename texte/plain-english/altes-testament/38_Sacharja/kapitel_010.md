# Zechariah – Chapter 10
### God gathers his scattered people

---

## Sheep without a shepherd (Verses 1–5)

[1] Ask the LORD for rain in the springtime,
the LORD who makes the storm clouds.
He gives showers of rain to everyone,
for the plants in the field.
[2] For the teraphim have said empty things.
Teraphim are small figures of household gods.
And the fortune-tellers have seen lies.
They have told false dreams.
Their comfort is empty.
That is why the people wander like sheep.
They suffer because there is no shepherd.
[3] My anger burns against the shepherds,
and I will punish the male goats.
For the LORD of Armies has taken care of his flock,
the house of Judah.
He will make them like his proud horse in battle.
[4] From Judah will come the cornerstone,
from him the tent peg,
from him the bow for battle,
from him every ruler together.
[5] They will be like strong warriors
who trample down the mud of the streets in battle.
They will fight, because the LORD is with them.
And the riders on horses will be put to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Ask God for rain! He alone gives what people need to live, not the idols.
> Verse 2: The “teraphim” (small figures of gods) and the fortune-tellers have comforted the people with lies. That is why the people wander around “like sheep without a shepherd.” Jesus uses the same picture when he feels pity for the crowds (Matthew 9:36).
> Verse 3: God is angry with the bad “shepherds,” that is, the leaders who have let their people down. The “male goats” are the powerful people who push their way through at the cost of others (compare Ezekiel 34:17).
> Verse 4: New, good leaders will come out of Judah itself. The “cornerstone” holds up the whole house. The “tent peg” holds the tent in place. Christians often apply the “cornerstone” to Jesus (compare Psalm 118:22; Ephesians 2:20).

---

## I will bring them home (Verses 6–12)

[6] “I will make the house of Judah strong,
and I will save the house of Joseph.
I will bring them back,
because I have mercy on them.
They will be as if I had never rejected them,
for I am the LORD their God,
and I will answer them.
[7] Ephraim will be like a strong warrior,
and their hearts will be happy as if from wine.
Yes, their children will see it and be glad.
Their hearts will rejoice in the LORD.
[8] I will whistle for them and gather them,
for I have set them free.
They will become as many as they were before.
[9] I will scatter them like seed among the peoples.
In far countries they will remember me.
They will live with their children and will come back.
[10] I will also bring them back from the land of Egypt
and gather them from Assyria.
I will bring them into the land of Gilead and Lebanon,
and there will not be enough room for them.
[11] He will pass through the sea of trouble
and strike the waves in the sea.
All the depths of the Nile will dry up.
The pride of Assyria will be brought down,
and the power of Egypt will go away.
[12] I will make them strong in the LORD.
They will walk here and there in his name,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: God will save not only Judah (the southern kingdom) but also “the house of Joseph,” that is, the northern kingdom of Israel, which had already been destroyed in 722 BC. All Israel is to be united again.
> “As if I had never rejected them”: God’s forgiveness is complete. He makes a new start, as if nothing had happened.
> Verse 8: God “whistles” for his people like a shepherd for his flock. And they come.
> Verse 9: A beautiful picture: God has “scattered” his people among the nations like seed. Even far away, they will remember God and grow.
> Verses 10–11: As when the people left Egypt, God will lead his people through the sea. Egypt and Assyria stand for all the countries where Israel was scattered.
