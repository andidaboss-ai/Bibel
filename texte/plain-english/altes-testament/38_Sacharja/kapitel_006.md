# Zechariah – Chapter 6
### The four chariots and the crown

---

## The eighth vision: four chariots (Verses 1–8)

[1] Again I looked up and saw
four chariots coming out
from between two mountains.
The mountains were mountains of bronze.
[2] The first chariot had red horses.
The second chariot had black horses.
[3] The third chariot had white horses.
The fourth chariot had spotted horses.
All of them were strong.
[4] Then I asked the angel who talked with me,
“What are these, my lord?”
[5] The angel answered me,
“These are the four winds of the sky.
They go out after standing before the Lord of all the earth.
[6] The chariot with the black horses goes out toward the land of the north.
The white horses went out after them.
And the spotted horses went out toward the land of the south.”
[7] The strong horses went out
and wanted to go back and forth through the earth.
He said, “Go, go back and forth through the earth!”
So they went back and forth through the earth.
[8] Then he called to me and said to me,
“Look, those who go toward the land of the north
have given my Spirit rest in the land of the north.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The last of the eight visions is a reminder of the first one (1:8–11): Again it is about horses of different colors. This time they pull chariots.
> The two mountains of bronze are maybe the gate to God’s home in heaven.
> Verse 5: The chariots are “the four winds of the sky,” that is, God’s messengers who go out in all directions. God rules over the whole world.
> Verse 8: In the “land of the north,” that is, in Babylon, where God’s people were held captive for so long, God has now “given his Spirit rest.” That means: His anger against Babylon is calmed. The judgment has been carried out.
> Here the eight visions of the night end. They have shown: God is at work. He punishes the enemies, makes his people clean, and builds Jerusalem again.

## Joshua is crowned (Verses 9–15)

[9] The word of the LORD came to me.
It said,
[10] “Take gifts from those who were captives,
from Heldai, from Tobijah, and from Jedaiah.
They have come from Babylon.
Go on the same day
into the house of Josiah the son of Zephaniah.
[11] Yes, take silver and gold,
make crowns,
and set them on the head of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest.
[12] Speak to him and say,
‘This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“Look, the man whose name is the Branch!
He will grow up out of his place,
and he will build the LORD’s temple.
[13] He will build the LORD’s temple.
He will carry royal honor,
and he will sit and rule on his throne.
He will be a priest on his throne.
And there will be peace and agreement between the two of them.
[14] The crowns will be for Helem, for Tobijah, for Jedaiah,
and for Hen the son of Zephaniah.
They will be kept in the LORD’s temple so that people remember.
[15] Those who are far away will come
and build in the LORD’s temple.
Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you.
This will happen
if you truly listen to the voice of the LORD your God.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> After the visions comes an action with a meaning in the real world:
> Verses 10–11: Three men had just come from Babylon and had brought silver and gold, probably as a gift for the temple. Zechariah is to make crowns from it and to put them on the head of Joshua the high priest.
> Verse 12: “The man whose name is the Branch”: The “Branch” is the coming king from the house of David (compare 3:8). He will build the temple. At that time, people may have thought of Zerubbabel, who really did build the temple.
> Verse 13: Something special: The Branch will be king and priest at the same time. And there will be peace between the two offices. In Israel, king and priest were normally separate. In the New Testament, Jesus is described as king and high priest at the same time (Hebrews 7).
> Why is Joshua crowned and not Zerubbabel? Interpreters disagree about this. Maybe it was too dangerous to crown a descendant of David in public, because the Persians could have seen it as a rebellion.
> Verse 14: The crowns are to stay in the temple, to remind people of this promise. Helem and Hen are probably other names for Heldai and Josiah from verse 10.
> Verse 15: People from far away will also come and help. But there is one condition: “This will happen if you truly listen to the voice of the LORD your God.”
