# Zechariah – Chapter 12
### They will look at the one they have pierced

---

## Jerusalem, a heavy stone for the nations (Verses 1–9)

[1] A message, the word of the LORD about Israel.
This is what the LORD says,
who stretches out the sky
and lays the foundation of the earth
and forms the human spirit inside a person:
[2] “Look, I will make Jerusalem a cup
that makes all the peoples around it stagger.
And it will also come on Judah
when Jerusalem is under siege.
[3] On that day
I will make Jerusalem a heavy stone for all the peoples.
All who try to lift it will be badly hurt.
And all the nations of the earth will gather together against it.
[4] On that day,” says the LORD,
“I will strike every horse with terror
and its rider with madness.
But I will keep my eyes open over the house of Judah,
and I will strike every horse of the peoples with blindness.
[5] The clan leaders of Judah will say in their hearts,
‘The people of Jerusalem are my strength
through the LORD of Armies, their God.’
[6] On that day I will make the clan leaders of Judah
like a pan of fire among wood,
and like a burning torch among bundles of grain.
They will eat up all the peoples around them,
on the right and on the left.
And the people of Jerusalem will again live in their own place, in Jerusalem.
[7] The LORD will also save the tents of Judah first,
so that the glory of the house of David
and the glory of the people of Jerusalem
will not be made greater than Judah.
[8] On that day the LORD will defend the people of Jerusalem.
Whoever is weak among them on that day
will be like David.
And the house of David will be like God,
like the angel of the LORD going before them.
[9] On that day
I will set out to destroy all the nations
that come against Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> The last three chapters speak about the end times, about “that day.” These words come up very often in Chapters 12–14.
> Verse 1: God introduces himself as the Creator of the sky, the earth, and the human spirit. He has the power to do what follows.
> Verses 2–3: Many nations attack Jerusalem. But for them the city becomes like a “cup that makes all the peoples around it stagger” (they will stagger as if they were drunk). And it becomes like a stone that is too heavy, so that whoever lifts it gets hurt.
> Verses 4–6: God fights for Jerusalem. These pictures describe God’s protection for a city in danger. They are not a command for people to make war against other nations.
> Verse 7: Interesting: God first saves the ordinary people in the countryside (“the tents of Judah”), so that the capital city and the royal family do not think too highly of themselves. God does not favor the powerful.
> Verse 8: Even the weakest will be as strong as the hero David.

## Mourning for the one who was pierced (Verses 10–14)

[10] “I will pour out on the house of David
and on the people of Jerusalem
a spirit of grace and of prayer for mercy.
They will look at me, the one they have pierced.
They will mourn for him
as people mourn for an only son.
They will weep bitterly for him
as people weep for a firstborn son.
[11] On that day the mourning in Jerusalem will be great,
like the mourning of Hadadrimmon in the valley of Megiddo.
[12] The land will mourn,
each family by itself:
the family of the house of David by itself, and their wives by themselves;
the family of the house of Nathan by itself, and their wives by themselves;
[13] the family of the house of Levi by itself, and their wives by themselves;
the family of the Shimeites by itself, and their wives by themselves;
[14] all the families that are left,
each family by itself, and their wives by themselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: This is one of the most mysterious and most important verses of the Old Testament. God pours out a “spirit of grace and of prayer for mercy.” The people see their guilt. They look at “me, the one they have pierced,” and they mourn for him as for an only child.
> Who is the one who was pierced? The Hebrew text says “at me,” that is, at God himself, and then “for him.” This is hard to understand. Some old manuscripts and translations read “at the one whom they have pierced.”
> The Gospel of John quotes this verse when a soldier sticks a spear into the side of Jesus on the cross: “They will look on him whom they pierced” (John 19:37; compare Revelation 1:7).
> In the Jewish tradition, the verse is explained in different ways, for example as being about a Messiah who falls in battle, or about a martyr.
> Verse 11: Hadadrimmon was probably a place near Megiddo. Maybe the verse is a reminder of the great mourning for the good King Josiah, who was killed in battle near Megiddo (2 Chronicles 35:22–25).
> Verses 12–14: The whole people mourns, each family by itself, the men and the women apart, as was the custom at funerals at that time. Everyone is affected: the royal family (David, Nathan) and the families of priests (Levi, Shimei).
