# Zechariah – Chapter 14
### The LORD will be King over the whole earth

---

## The day of the LORD is coming (Verses 1–5)

[1] Look, a day of the LORD is coming,
when the things taken from you will be divided up among you.
[2] For I will gather all nations against Jerusalem to fight.
The city will be taken,
the houses will be robbed,
and the women will be raped.
Half of the city will be taken away as captives,
but the rest of the people will not be cut off from the city.
[3] Then the LORD will go out
and fight against those nations,
as he fights on a day of battle.
[4] On that day his feet will stand on the Mount of Olives,
which lies to the east of Jerusalem.
And the Mount of Olives will split in two,
from east to west,
so that a very large valley is formed.
Half of the mountain will move toward the north,
and the other half toward the south.
[5] You will flee through the valley of my mountains,
for the valley of the mountains will reach to Azel.
Yes, you will flee,
just as you fled from the earthquake
in the days of Uzziah king of Judah.
And the LORD my God will come,
and all the holy ones with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The last chapter describes the great “day of the LORD,” the end of history.
> Verse 2: First, terrible things happen: Jerusalem is conquered and robbed, and women are raped. The Bible does not keep silent about what happens in war, especially what is done to women. This is a grave wrong, and the guilt lies only with those who do it. If you have experienced sexual violence, you can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673. For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233.
> Verse 3: But then God himself steps in and fights against the attackers.
> Verse 4: God stands on the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem. The mountain splits, and a large valley is formed, a way of escape for the people of the city.
> The Mount of Olives also plays an important role in the New Testament: There Jesus prayed in the night before his death (in the garden of Gethsemane at the foot of the mountain), and from there he was taken up into heaven (Acts 1:9–12). There the angels say: “This Jesus … will come back in the same way.”
> Verse 5: The earthquake in the time of King Uzziah was so strong that people still remembered it centuries later (compare Amos 1:1). We do not know where Azel was.
> “All the holy ones” are probably the angels who come with God.

## Living water and one God (Verses 6–11)

[6] On that day
there will be no light,
no cold, and no frost.
[7] It will be a day like no other,
a day that the LORD knows,
neither day nor night.
But it will happen:
In the evening there will be light.
[8] On that day
living water will flow out from Jerusalem,
half of it toward the eastern sea
and half of it toward the western sea.
It will be like this in summer and in winter.
“Living water” means fresh, flowing water.
[9] The LORD will be King over the whole earth.
On that day the LORD will be one,
and his name one.
[10] The whole land will be made flat like the Arabah,
from Geba to Rimmon, south of Jerusalem.
The Arabah is the low, flat valley of the Jordan River.
But Jerusalem will be lifted up and will stay in her place,
from Benjamin’s Gate to the place of the First Gate,
to the Corner Gate,
and from the Tower of Hananel to the king’s winepresses.
[11] People will live in it,
and there will be no more curse.
Jerusalem will live in safety.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: A completely new day, different from all the days before. Day and night no longer take turns. “In the evening there will be light”: When people expect it to get dark, it becomes bright. The book of Revelation says about the new Jerusalem: “There will be no night” (Revelation 22:5).
> Verse 8: Living, fresh water flows out of Jerusalem, east to the Dead Sea and west to the Mediterranean Sea, all year long. This is a reminder of the stream from the temple in Ezekiel (Ezekiel 47) and of the river of life in Revelation 22:1. Jesus speaks of the “living water” that he gives (John 4:10–14; 7:37–38).
> Verse 9: The high point of the book: “The LORD will be King over the whole earth. On that day the LORD will be one, and his name one.” This is a reminder of the basic Jewish confession of faith, the “Hear, Israel”: “The LORD is our God. The LORD is one” (Deuteronomy 6:4). In the end, all people will know the one God. In the Jewish prayer “Aleinu,” this verse is still prayed every day.
> Verses 10–11: The land becomes flat, and only Jerusalem is lifted up. People will live in the city again within its old borders. And there will be “no more curse,” no more destruction. At last Jerusalem will live in safety.

## The end of the attackers (Verses 12–15)

[12] This will be the plague
with which the LORD will strike all the peoples
who have fought against Jerusalem:
Their bodies will waste away while they are still standing on their feet.
Their eyes will waste away in their sockets,
and their tongues will waste away in their mouths.
A plague is a terrible sickness or disaster.
[13] On that day
a great panic from the LORD will come over them.
Each one will grab the hand of his neighbor,
and each one will raise his hand against his neighbor.
[14] Judah too will fight at Jerusalem.
And the riches of all the nations around will be gathered together:
gold, silver, and clothes, in great amounts.
[15] The same kind of plague will also strike
the horses, the mules, the camels, the donkeys,
and all the animals that are in those camps.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are hard to read. They use strong pictures to describe God’s judgment on the nations that attacked Jerusalem and did the horrible things from verse 2.
> Verse 13: The attackers become confused and fight against each other, as in earlier stories in the Bible (Judges 7:22; 2 Chronicles 20:23).
> Verse 14: The treasures that the nations had robbed come back.
> Important: It is God who judges here. No person has the right to use such pictures to justify violence against others. And the chapter does not end with destruction, but with an invitation to the nations. That comes now.

## All nations celebrate the Feast of Booths (Verses 16–21)

[16] And it will happen:
Everyone who is left of all the nations
that came against Jerusalem
will go up year after year
to worship the King, the LORD of Armies,
and to celebrate the Feast of Booths.
Booths are simple huts made of branches.
[17] And it will happen:
If any of the families of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem
to worship the King, the LORD of Armies,
no rain will fall on them.
[18] If the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come,
no rain will fall on them either.
This will be the plague
with which the LORD will strike the nations
that do not go up to celebrate the Feast of Booths.
[19] This will be the punishment of Egypt
and the punishment of all the nations
that do not go up to celebrate the Feast of Booths.
[20] On that day these words will be written on the bells of the horses:
“HOLY TO THE LORD.”
And the pots in the LORD’s house
will be like the bowls in front of the altar.
[21] Yes, every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah
will be holy to the LORD of Armies.
All who offer sacrifices will come and take some of them and cook in them.
On that day there will no longer be a Canaanite in the house of the LORD of Armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: An amazing turn: The nations that fought against Jerusalem will now come to Jerusalem every year to worship God. Enemies become guests at the feast.
> The Feast of Booths reminded people of the journey through the desert, when God took care of his people while they lived in tents. It was also a thanksgiving feast for the harvest. Now all nations celebrate together that God takes care of them.
> Verses 17–19: Whoever does not come gets no rain. This makes sense: Whoever rejects the God who gives rain cuts himself off from the source of life. Egypt is named especially, because it lived from the Nile River and did not depend on rain. Even there it will have results.
> Verses 20–21: In the end, everything is holy. Before, the words “HOLY TO THE LORD” were written only on the gold plate on the high priest’s turban (Exodus 28:36). Now they are written even on the bells of the horses! And every cooking pot is as holy as the dishes in the temple. There is no longer a difference between holy and everyday things. All of life belongs to God.
> “No longer a Canaanite”: The Hebrew word can also mean “trader.” Then it means: No more business will be done in the temple. Jesus drove the traders out of the temple (John 2:16).

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Zechariah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Return to me:** “Return to me … and I will return to you” (1:3).
> **Eight visions of the night:** riders, horns, the measuring line, Joshua in new clothes, the golden lampstand, the flying scroll, the basket, and the four chariots. All of them show: God is with his people again. He forgives guilt and builds Jerusalem again.
> **By my Spirit:** “Not by might, and not by power, but by my Spirit” (4:6). “Who looks down on the day of small things?” (4:10).
> **What God wants:** “Don’t treat the widow, the orphan, the foreigner, or the poor harshly” (7:10).
> **Peace in the city:** Old people in the streets, children playing in the streets (8:4–5).
> **The king on the donkey:** “Look, your King is coming to you … humble, and riding on a donkey” (9:9).
> **The good shepherd:** He is rejected and paid off with 30 pieces of silver (11:12–13). “Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered” (13:7).
> **The one who was pierced:** “They will look at me, the one they have pierced” (12:10).
> **God is King over all:** “The LORD will be King over the whole earth. On that day the LORD will be one, and his name one” (14:9).
> **Zechariah and Jesus:** No other book of the prophets is quoted as often as Zechariah in the reports about the last days of Jesus.
> **For today:** God begins with small beginnings, works through his Spirit, and has a great goal: that all people know him and live in peace.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Malachi, the last book of the Old Testament. It ends with the announcement that God will send a messenger, the prophet Elijah, before the great day of the LORD comes.
