# Zechariah – Chapter 4
### Not by might or power, but by my Spirit

---

## The fifth vision: the golden lampstand (Verses 1–5)

[1] The angel who talked with me came again
and woke me up,
like a man who is woken up from his sleep.
[2] He said to me,
“What do you see?”
I said,
“I have seen a lampstand made all of gold.
There is a bowl on top of it,
and seven lamps are on it.
There are seven pipes to each of the lamps on top of it.
[3] And there are two olive trees by it,
one on the right side of the bowl
and the other on its left side.”
[4] I answered and said to the angel who talked with me,
“What are these, my lord?”
[5] Then the angel who talked with me answered me,
“Don’t you know what these are?”
I said, “No, my lord.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Zechariah sees a golden lampstand, like the lampstand in the temple (the menorah, Exodus 25:31–40). On top there is a bowl with oil. From the bowl, pipes lead to seven lamps. Next to it stand two olive trees, which always give new oil.
> The special thing is: No one has to fill up the oil. It flows by itself from the trees. The light never goes out.
> The exact form is not quite clear in Hebrew. People understand the number of pipes in different ways.
> Zechariah honestly asks what this means. It is okay not to understand everything right away and to ask.

## By my Spirit (Verses 6–10)

[6] Then he answered and said to me,
“This is the word of the LORD to Zerubbabel:
‘Not by might, and not by power,
but by my Spirit,’
says the LORD of Armies.
[7] Who are you, great mountain?
Before Zerubbabel you will become flat land.
And he will bring out the top stone
while people shout, ‘Grace, grace to it!’”
The top stone is the last stone that finishes a building.
[8] Then the word of the LORD came to me.
It said,
[9] “The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house.
His hands will also finish it.
Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you.
[10] Yes, who looks down on the day of small things?
For these seven will rejoice
when they see the plumb line in the hand of Zerubbabel.
These are the eyes of the LORD,
which go back and forth through the whole earth.”
A plumb line is a string with a weight. Builders use it to make walls straight.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: This is one of the best-known verses in the Bible: “Not by might, and not by power, but by my Spirit.” Zerubbabel had no army, no money, no power. Building the temple seemed impossible. But God says: It does not depend on your strength, but on my Spirit. Just as the oil in the lampstand flows by itself, God’s Spirit is at work.
> Verse 7: All obstacles, as big as a mountain, will become as flat as a plain. And at the end the top stone will be set in place with shouts of joy: “Grace, grace!” Everything is God’s gift.
> Verse 9: Zerubbabel has laid the foundation, and he will also finish the building. This happened in the year 515 BC (Ezra 6:15).
> Verse 10: “Who looks down on the day of small things?” Many were disappointed because the new temple was so small and plain (compare Haggai 2:3). But God says: Don’t look down on small beginnings! Great things often begin small.
> “These seven” are the seven lamps. They are “the eyes of the LORD,” which look over the whole earth. God sees everything, even the small beginnings, and he is glad about them.
> For today: Sometimes we feel small and weak. But with God, what counts is not how strong we are, but that his Spirit is at work.

## The two olive trees (Verses 11–14)

[11] Then I asked him,
“What are these two olive trees
on the right side of the lampstand and on its left side?”
[12] And I asked him a second time,
“What are these two olive branches
beside the two golden pipes,
which pour out the golden oil?”
[13] He answered me,
“Don’t you know what these are?”
I said, “No, my lord.”
[14] Then he said,
“These are the two anointed ones
who stand by the Lord of the whole earth.”
Anointed means: oil was poured on them to set them apart for a task.

> **What does this mean?**
> The two olive trees are “the two anointed ones.” Word for word, the Hebrew says “the two sons of oil.” This probably means the two leaders of the people: Zerubbabel, the governor from the royal family, and Joshua, the high priest. Kings and priests were anointed with oil when they began their service.
> Through them, God lets his “oil” flow to the people, that is, his Spirit and his blessing. The political leader and the spiritual leader work together.
> In the Revelation to John, “two witnesses” are compared with these two olive trees (Revelation 11:4).
