# Zechariah – Chapter 2
### A city without walls

---

## The third vision: the man with the measuring line (Verses 1–5)

[1] I looked up and saw
a man with a measuring line in his hand.
[2] Then I asked,
“Where are you going?”
He said to me,
“To measure Jerusalem,
to see how wide and how long it is.”
[3] Then the angel who talked with me went out,
and another angel went out to meet him.
[4] He said to him,
“Run and tell this young man,
‘Jerusalem will be lived in like villages without walls,
because there will be so many people and animals in it.
[5] For I,’ says the LORD,
‘will be a wall of fire all around her,
and I will be the glory in the middle of her.’

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, the verses of this chapter are usually moved by four (WEB 2:1 = there 2:5, and so on), because there the last four verses of Chapter 1 already belong to Chapter 2.
> A young man wants to measure Jerusalem, probably to plan the new city walls.
> But an angel says: Stop! Jerusalem will become so big that no wall can go around it. So many people and animals will live there.
> Verse 5: And Jerusalem does not need walls of stone. God himself will be the wall, a wall of fire. This is a reminder of the pillar of fire when the people left Egypt (Exodus 13:21). God protects his city, and he lives in the middle of it.

---

## Come home from Babylon! (Verses 6–9)

[6] ‘Come! Come! Flee from the land of the north,’
says the LORD.
‘For I have scattered you like the four winds of the sky,’
says the LORD.
[7] ‘Come, Zion!
Escape, you who live with the daughter of Babylon!’
[8] For the LORD of Armies says:
‘For the sake of honor he has sent me to the nations
that robbed you.
For whoever touches you
touches the apple of his eye.
[9] For look, I will shake my hand over them,
and they will be robbed by those who served them.
Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: This is a call to the Jews who still lived in Babylon (the “land of the north”): Come home! Not all of them had gone back with the first group. Many had built a new life in Babylon.
> Babylon really lay in the east. But the road from there came into Israel from the north. That is why it is called the “land of the north.”
> “The daughter of Babylon” means the city of Babylon and its people.
> Verse 8: A famous picture: “Whoever touches you touches the apple of his eye.” The “apple of the eye” is the pupil, the dark center of the eye. It is the most sensitive part of the body. People protect it without thinking. That is how precious and how protected God’s people are to him (compare Deuteronomy 32:10; Psalm 17:8).
> “The apple of his eye”: In Hebrew it is not quite clear whose eye is meant. The usual understanding: God’s own eye.
> Who is speaking here (“he has sent me”)? Maybe the prophet, maybe the angel of the LORD. Interpreters disagree about this.

---

## Many nations will come to God (Verses 10–13)

[10] Sing and rejoice, daughter of Zion!
For look, I am coming, and I will live among you,’
says the LORD.
[11] On that day many nations will join themselves to the LORD,
and they will be my people.
I will live among you,
and you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you.
[12] The LORD will take Judah as his own share in the holy land,
and he will again choose Jerusalem.
[13] Be silent, all people, before the LORD,
for he has risen up from his holy home!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: God himself comes and lives among his people. The Gospel of John says about Jesus: “The Word became flesh and lived among us” (John 1:14).
> Verse 11: A great view of the future: Many nations will join themselves to God and be “my people.” God’s people will be bigger than Israel alone. Other nations are not shut out but invited.
> Verse 12: The words “holy land” are very rare in the Old Testament. Many interpreters think that this is the only place where they appear.
> Verse 13: The WEB says “all flesh,” that means: all people. God has risen up to act (compare Habakkuk 2:20).
