# Zechariah – Chapter 3
### New clothes for the high priest

---

## The fourth vision: Joshua on trial (Verses 1–5)

[1] He showed me Joshua the high priest
standing before the angel of the LORD.
And Satan was standing at his right side to accuse him.
[2] The LORD said to Satan,
“May the LORD rebuke you, Satan!
Yes, may the LORD, who has chosen Jerusalem, rebuke you!
Isn’t this man a burning stick pulled out of the fire?”
To rebuke means to speak sharply against someone and stop him.
[3] Now Joshua was dressed in filthy clothes,
and he was standing before the angel.
[4] The angel answered and said to those who stood before him,
“Take the filthy clothes off him.”
And to Joshua he said,
“Look, I have taken your guilt away from you,
and I will dress you in fine clothes.”
[5] I said,
“Let them put a clean turban on his head.”
So they put a clean turban on his head
and dressed him in clothes.
And the angel of the LORD was standing nearby.

> **What does this mean?**
> A court scene in heaven. The one on trial is Joshua, the high priest. He stands for the whole people before God.
> In Hebrew, “Satan” means “accuser” or “enemy.” Here he is like a prosecutor who accuses Joshua (compare Job 1–2). He stands “at his right side,” where the accuser stood in court.
> Verse 2: But God himself rejects the accuser! Joshua is like a burning stick that was pulled out of the fire just in time. The people barely survived the fire of the exile. God has saved them, and he will not let them fall again.
> Verse 3: Joshua is wearing filthy clothes. This is a picture of guilt and uncleanness. Normally a priest was allowed to come before God only in clean clothes.
> Verses 4–5: God takes the guilt away. He gives Joshua new, festive clothes and a clean turban (the turban of the high priest).
> This is a wonderful picture of forgiveness: God not only takes our guilt away, he also gives us something new. The New Testament says something similar: We may “put on” Christ like new clothes (Galatians 3:27). And in the parable of the lost son, Jesus tells how the father has the best robe put on the son who came home (Luke 15:22).

---

## The Branch and the stone (Verses 6–10)

[6] The angel of the LORD made a solemn promise to Joshua.
He said,
[7] “This is what the LORD of Armies says:
‘If you walk in my ways
and if you follow my instructions,
then you will also rule my house
and take care of my courtyards.
And I will let you come and go among these who are standing here.
[8] Listen now, Joshua the high priest,
you and your companions who sit before you.
They are men who are a sign.
For look, I will bring my servant, the Branch.
[9] For look at the stone that I have set before Joshua.
On this one stone are seven eyes.
Look, I will carve words into it,’
says the LORD of Armies,
‘and I will take away the guilt of this land in one day.
[10] On that day,’ says the LORD of Armies,
‘each of you will invite his neighbor
to sit under the vine and under the fig tree.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: If Joshua stays faithful to God, he may lead the temple. And he will be allowed into God’s heavenly council, “among these who are standing here,” that is, among the angels.
> Verse 8: God announces his servant, the “Branch.” This is a title for the coming king from the house of David (compare Isaiah 11:1; Jeremiah 23:5; 33:15). A new shoot can grow out of a tree stump that was cut down. In the same way, new life will grow out of David’s royal family, which had been destroyed. At that time, people may have thought of Zerubbabel (compare Zechariah 6:12). Christians also see in it a hint of Jesus.
> Verse 9: A mysterious stone with seven eyes. Maybe it is a foundation stone for the temple or a precious stone on the clothes of the high priest. The seven eyes stand for God’s eyes, which see everything (compare 4:10). The exact meaning is not certain.
> God will take away the guilt of the land “in one day.” Christians think of the day when Jesus died on the cross.
> Verse 10: A picture of peace and friendship: People invite their neighbors to sit in the shade of their own vine and fig tree (compare Micah 4:4).
