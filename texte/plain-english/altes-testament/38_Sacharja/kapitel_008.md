# Zechariah – Chapter 8
### Children play in the streets of Jerusalem

---

## God returns to Zion (Verses 1–8)

[1] The word of the LORD of Armies came to me.
[2] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“I am jealous for Zion with a great jealousy.
I am jealous for her with great anger.”
[3] This is what the LORD says:
“I have come back to Zion,
and I will live in the middle of Jerusalem.
Jerusalem will be called ‘The City of Truth,’
and the mountain of the LORD of Armies will be called ‘The Holy Mountain.’”
[4] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“Old men and old women will again live
in the streets of Jerusalem,
each one with a walking stick in his hand
because they are so old.
[5] And the streets of the city will be full of boys and girls
playing in its streets.”
[6] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“In those days this may seem too wonderful
to those who are left of this people.
But should it also seem too wonderful to me?”
says the LORD of Armies.
[7] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“Look, I will save my people
from the land in the east
and from the land in the west.
[8] I will bring them home,
and they will live in Jerusalem.
They will be my people,
and I will be their God,
in faithfulness and in righteousness.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapter 8 is full of promises. It says “This is what the LORD says” 10 times.
> Verse 3: Jerusalem gets a new name: “The City of Truth.” The Hebrew word also means “faithfulness”: a city where people can rely on each other.
> Verses 4–5: This is one of the most beautiful pictures in the Bible: Old people sit in peace in the streets, and children play there. It is a picture of peace and safety. In war, the weak die first, the old people and the children. When they can live in peace, there really is peace.
> Verse 6: For the small, poor community in the ruins, this sounds unbelievable. But God says: For me, nothing is impossible.
> Verses 7–8: God will bring his people home from east and west, from the whole world. “They will be my people, and I will be their God.” These are the words of the covenant (compare Jeremiah 31:33).

---

## Be strong! (Verses 9–17)

[9] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“Let your hands be strong,
you who hear these words in these days
from the mouth of the prophets.
These prophets were there on the day when the foundation
of the house of the LORD of Armies was laid,
the temple, so that it could be built.
[10] For before those days there were no wages for people
and no wages for animals.
And no one who went out or came in
had peace from the enemy.
For I turned all people against each other,
everyone against his neighbor.
[11] But now I will not treat those who are left of this people
as I did in the earlier days,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[12] “For there will be a seed of peace.
The vine will give its fruit,
the ground will give its crops,
and the sky will give its dew.
I will give all these things
to those who are left of this people as their own.
[13] And it will happen:
Just as you were a curse among the nations,
house of Judah and house of Israel,
so I will save you,
and you will be a blessing.
Don’t be afraid.
Let your hands be strong.”
[14] For this is what the LORD of Armies says:
“When your ancestors made me angry,
I decided to do evil to you,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“and I did not change my mind.
[15] In the same way, in these days I have decided again
to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah.
Don’t be afraid.
[16] These are the things you are to do:
Each of you, speak the truth with his neighbor.
At your city gates, judge in a way that is true and brings peace.
[17] None of you should plan evil in his heart against his neighbor.
And don’t love false oaths.
For all these are things that I hate,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–12: Just like in Haggai: Since the building of the temple started again, everything is turning to good. Before there were no wages and no peace, only fighting. Now “a seed of peace” is growing.
> Verse 13: A great turning point: Until now, Israel was an example of curse and misfortune among the nations. Now it is to become a blessing. This is a reminder of the promise to Abraham: “You will be a blessing” (Genesis 12:2).
> “Don’t be afraid. Let your hands be strong.” This is the encouragement.
> Verse 16: In those days, people met at the city gates to judge cases.
> Verses 16–17: And again the commands for everyday life: speak the truth, judge fairly, don’t plan evil against your neighbor, no false oaths. The letter to the Ephesians takes up verse 16: “Speak truth, each one with his neighbor” (Ephesians 4:25).

## Days of fasting become feasts of joy (Verses 18–19)

[18] The word of the LORD of Armies came to me.
[19] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“The fasts of the fourth, the fifth, the seventh, and the tenth months
will become joy and gladness for the house of Judah,
and happy feasts.
So love truth and peace!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now at last comes the answer to the question of the people from Bethel (7:3): The days of fasting will become feasts of joy!
> The four days of fasting reminded people of the disaster of Jerusalem:
> In the fourth month: The wall was broken through (Jeremiah 39:2).
> In the fifth month: The temple was burned (2 Kings 25:8–9).
> In the seventh month: Gedaliah was murdered (2 Kings 25:25).
> In the tenth month: The siege began (2 Kings 25:1).
> In Judaism, some of these days of fasting are still kept today, especially Tisha B’Av (the ninth of Av), to remember the destruction of the temple.
> “So love truth and peace!” That is the condition.

## The nations come to Jerusalem (Verses 20–23)

[20] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“Many peoples will still come,
and people who live in many cities.
[21] The people of one city will go to another and say,
‘Let’s go quickly
to ask the LORD for his favor
and to seek the LORD of Armies.
I will go too.’
[22] Yes, many peoples and strong nations will come
to seek the LORD of Armies in Jerusalem
and to ask the LORD for his favor.”
[23] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“In those days, 10 men
from all the languages of the nations
will take hold of the edge of the robe of a Jewish man and say,
‘We will go with you,
for we have heard that God is with you.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of the first part of the book there is a great view of the future: People from many nations will come to seek God. They invite each other: “Come, let’s go! I will go too!”
> Verse 23: A strong picture: 10 people from different nations hold on to the edge of a Jewish man’s robe and say: “We will go with you, for we have heard that God is with you.”
> The Jewish people are to become a signpost to God for the world. For Christians, this came true because many people from all nations found the God of Israel through the Jew Jesus and his Jewish apostles.
> This verse is also a warning against every form of hatred of Jews: The nations are to look at the Jewish people with respect and love.
