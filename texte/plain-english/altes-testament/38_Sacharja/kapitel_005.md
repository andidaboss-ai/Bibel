# Zechariah – Chapter 5
### The flying scroll and the basket

---

## The sixth vision: the flying scroll (Verses 1–4)

[1] Again I looked up and saw
a flying scroll.
[2] He said to me,
“What do you see?”
I answered,
“I see a flying scroll.
It is twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide.”
That is about 30 feet long and about 15 feet wide.
[3] Then he said to me,
“This is the curse
that goes out over the whole land.
For everyone who steals
will be cut off, as it says on one side.
And everyone who swears falsely
will be cut off, as it says on the other side.
To swear falsely means to make a promise before God and lie.
[4] I will send it out,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“and it will go into the house of the thief
and into the house of the one who swears falsely by my name.
It will stay in the middle of his house
and destroy it, with its wood and its stones.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A huge scroll flies through the air: twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide, that is, about 30 by 15 feet, as big as a large billboard. A cubit is about 18 inches. Everyone can see it.
> On the scroll is a curse against two kinds of wrong: stealing and swearing falsely (lying under oath). This is a reminder of the Ten Commandments: “You shall not steal” and “You shall not misuse the name of the LORD.”
> Verse 4: The curse goes into the houses of those who do these things and destroys them from the inside. In the end, wrong destroys the life of the one who does it.
> The message: In the new Jerusalem that God is building, there should be no more wrong. God is cleaning up.

---

## The seventh vision: the woman in the basket (Verses 5–11)

[5] Then the angel who talked with me came forward
and said to me,
“Look up now
and see what is coming out.”
[6] I said,
“What is it?”
He said,
“This is an ephah basket that is coming out.”
He also said,
“This is what they look like in all the land.
[7] Look, a lid made of lead that weighed one talent was lifted up.
And there was a woman sitting inside the ephah basket.”
[8] He said,
“This is Wickedness.”
He threw her back down into the ephah basket
and threw the lead weight on its opening.
[9] Then I looked up and saw
two women.
The wind was in their wings.
They had wings like the wings of a stork.
They lifted up the ephah basket between the earth and the sky.
[10] Then I said to the angel who talked with me,
“Where are they taking the ephah basket?”
[11] He said to me,
“To build a house for her in the land of Shinar.
When it is ready,
she will be set there in her own place.”

> **What does this mean?**
> An ephah was a measure for grain, about 5 to 10 gallons. Here it is a large basket or barrel with a heavy lid made of lead. A talent weighed about 66 pounds.
> Verse 6: Word for word, the WEB says “their appearance.” Many translations follow a small change in the text here and read “their guilt.” It probably means: The basket shows how things stand with the guilt in the whole land.
> Verses 7–8: A woman sits in the basket. The angel says: “This is Wickedness.” In Hebrew, the word for “wickedness” is feminine. That is why it is shown as a woman. This is a picture. It says nothing about women. Men and women can both be wicked.
> Verse 9: Notice this: Two other women, with wings like storks, carry the basket away. They are serving God. So in this vision, women are also God’s helpers.
> Verse 11: The basket is taken to “Shinar.” That is Babylonia (compare Genesis 11:2, the tower of Babel). There Wickedness gets a “temple.” The message: God removes evil from his land and takes it where it belongs, far away.
