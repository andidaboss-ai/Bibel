# Zechariah – Chapter 1
### Return to me!

---

## Before we begin: Who was Zechariah?

Zechariah lived at the same time as Haggai, from the year 520 BC on, after the return from the exile in Babylon. Both of them encouraged the people to rebuild the temple (Ezra 5:1; 6:14).
Zechariah probably came from a family of priests (compare Nehemiah 12:16). His name means: The LORD has remembered.
Zechariah is the longest book of the 12 minor prophets. It has two parts:
Chapters 1–8: Eight visions in the night, with angels, horses, horns, a lampstand, a flying scroll, and other puzzling pictures. An angel explains to Zechariah what they mean. The message: God is with his people again. Jerusalem will be built again.
Chapters 9–14: Words about the future, about a king who is coming, a good shepherd, and the day when God will be King over the whole earth. Many scholars think that this second part was written later.
No other book of the prophets is quoted as often as Zechariah in the reports about the last days of Jesus: the king on the donkey (9:9), the 30 pieces of silver (11:12–13), “They will look at me, the one they have pierced” (12:10), and the shepherd who is struck (13:7).
A note: In some Bibles, the verses at the end of Chapter 1 (verses 18–21) are the beginning of Chapter 2 (there 2:1–4). The book has 14 chapters.

## Return to me! (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius,
the word of the LORD came to the prophet Zechariah,
the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo.
It said,
[2] “The LORD was very angry with your ancestors.
[3] So tell the people:
This is what the LORD of Armies says:
‘Return to me,’ says the LORD of Armies,
‘and I will return to you,’
says the LORD of Armies.
[4] Don’t be like your ancestors.
The earlier prophets called out to them:
‘This is what the LORD of Armies says:
Turn back now from your evil ways and from your evil deeds!’
But they did not hear and did not listen to me,
says the LORD.
[5] Your ancestors, where are they?
And the prophets, do they live forever?
[6] But my words and my rules,
which I commanded my servants the prophets,
didn’t they catch up with your ancestors?
Then they turned back and said,
‘The LORD of Armies has dealt with us
just as he decided to do to us,
because of our ways and because of our deeds.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This was about October/November 520 BC, that is, between Haggai’s second and third messages.
> Verse 3: The heart of it: “Return to me … and I will return to you.” God is waiting for his people to come back to him. And then he is there at once. The letter of James says something similar: “Come near to God, and he will come near to you” (James 4:8).
> Verses 4–6: Zechariah reminds the people of their history: The ancestors did not listen to the prophets. The ancestors are dead, and the prophets have died too. But God’s word has stayed, and it came true: The exile came. In the end, the ancestors understood.
> The message: Learn from history! Don’t make the same mistakes.

## The first vision: Riders among the myrtle trees (Verses 7–17)

[7] On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month,
that is the month Shebat,
in the second year of Darius,
the word of the LORD came to the prophet Zechariah,
the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo.
It said,
[8] “I had a vision in the night.
I saw a man riding on a red horse.
He stood among the myrtle trees in a deep valley.
And behind him were red, brown, and white horses.
[9] Then I asked,
‘My lord, what are these?’”
The angel who talked with me said to me,
“I will show you what these are.”
[10] The man who stood among the myrtle trees answered,
“They are the ones the LORD has sent
to go back and forth through the earth.”
[11] They reported to the angel of the LORD
who stood among the myrtle trees. They said,
“We have gone back and forth through the earth.
Look, the whole earth is at rest and in peace.”
[12] Then the angel of the LORD answered,
“LORD of Armies, how long will you not show mercy
to Jerusalem and to the cities of Judah?
You have been angry with them for these 70 years.”
[13] The LORD answered the angel who talked with me
with kind words, with comforting words.
[14] So the angel who talked with me said to me,
“Call out and say,
‘This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“I am jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion with a great jealousy.
[15] I am very angry with the nations that live without a care.
For I was only a little angry,
but they made the disaster even worse.”
[16] So the LORD says:
“I have come back to Jerusalem with mercy.
My house will be built in it,”
says the LORD of Armies,
“and a measuring line will be stretched out over Jerusalem.”’
[17] Call out again and say,
‘This is what the LORD of Armies says:
“My cities will again overflow with good things.
The LORD will again comfort Zion
and will again choose Jerusalem.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: This was about the middle of February 519 BC. In this night Zechariah has eight visions.
> Verse 8: Myrtle trees are bushes with a sweet smell.
> Verses 8–11: Riders on horses of different colors. They are like scouts that God has sent into the whole world. Their report: “The whole earth is at rest and in peace.” After some rebellions, the Persian Empire had just found peace again.
> But for Jerusalem this “peace” is not good news: The nations live without a care, while Jerusalem still lies in ruins.
> Verse 12: The angel prays for Jerusalem: “How long?” The “70 years” are a reminder of Jeremiah’s prophecy (Jeremiah 25:11; 29:10). From the burning of the first temple to its rebuilding, it really was about 70 years (587/586 to 515 BC).
> Verses 13–17: God answers with comforting words: I love Jerusalem with a deep and passionate love. I am angry with the nations that went too far when they punished my people. I am coming back with mercy. My temple will be built. The city will be measured and built again.

## The second vision: Four horns and four craftsmen (Verses 18–21)

[18] I looked up and saw
four horns.
[19] I asked the angel who talked with me,
“What are these?”
He answered me,
“These are the horns that have scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem.”
[20] The LORD showed me four craftsmen.
[21] Then I asked,
“What are these coming to do?”
He said,
“These are the horns that scattered Judah,
so that no one could lift up his head.
But these craftsmen have come to terrify them,
to throw down the horns of the nations
that lifted up their horn against the land of Judah to scatter it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, these verses are at the beginning of Chapter 2 (there verses 1–4).
> In the Bible, a horn is a picture of power and strength, like the horns of a bull. The four horns are the world powers that destroyed and scattered Israel and Judah. “Four” can stand for all four directions, that is, for all the enemies all around.
> The four “craftsmen” (workers skilled with their hands) come to knock off the horns. For every power that oppresses his people, God has a power against it.
> The message: Those who oppress others will not rule forever.
