# Zechariah – Chapter 7
### Fasting that pleases God

---

## A question about fasting (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the fourth year of King Darius,
the word of the LORD came to Zechariah
on the fourth day of the ninth month, the month of Chislev.
[2] The people of Bethel had sent Sharezer and Regem Melech and their men
to ask the LORD for his favor.
[3] They were also to speak to the priests of the house of the LORD of Armies
and to the prophets. They asked,
“Should I weep in the fifth month and keep away from food,
as I have done for so many years?”
To fast means to go without food for a time, to pray or to mourn.
[4] Then the word of the LORD of Armies came to me.
It said,
[5] “Speak to all the people of the land and to the priests. Say,
‘When you fasted and mourned in the fifth and in the seventh month
for these 70 years,
did you really fast for me, really for me?
[6] And when you eat and when you drink,
don’t you eat for yourselves
and drink for yourselves?
[7] Aren’t these the words
that the LORD announced through the earlier prophets?
That was when people lived in Jerusalem and it did well,
together with its cities all around,
and when people lived in the South and in the low hills.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This was about two years after the visions of the night, in December 518 BC.
> Verses 2–3: A group from Bethel asks a practical question: Since the destruction of the temple (in the fifth month) we have fasted and mourned every year. Now the temple is being built again. Should we go on fasting?
> Verse 5: God answers with a question of his own: Did you fast for me at all? Or was it only a habit, a ritual for yourselves?
> In the seventh month, people probably remembered the murder of the governor Gedaliah (2 Kings 25:25; Jeremiah 41).
> Verse 6: When you ate and when you fasted, you only thought of yourselves.
> Verse 7: The earlier prophets already said what really matters. That comes now.

---

## What God really wants (Verses 8–14)

[8] The word of the LORD came to Zechariah.
It said,
[9] “This is what the LORD of Armies has said:
‘Judge in a way that is true and fair.
Show kindness and mercy to one another,
each of you to his brother.
[10] Don’t treat the widow, the orphan,
the foreigner, or the poor harshly.
And none of you should plan evil in his heart against his brother.’
[11] But they refused to listen.
They stubbornly turned their backs
and closed their ears, so that they would not hear.
[12] Yes, they made their hearts as hard as flint,
so that they would not hear the law
and the words that the LORD of Armies had sent by his Spirit
through the earlier prophets.
That is why great anger came from the LORD of Armies.
[13] And so it happened:
As he called and they refused to listen,
so they will call, and I will not listen,”
says the LORD of Armies.
[14] “I will scatter them as with a storm wind
among all the nations they did not know.
So the land behind them lay in ruins,
so that no one traveled through it or came back.
For they made the lovely land a ruin.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: This is what God really wants, more important than fasting:
> Judge in a fair way.
> Show kindness and mercy.
> Do not treat the weakest people harshly: widows, orphans, foreigners, and the poor.
> Do not even plan evil against others in your heart.
> This is a reminder of Isaiah 58:6–7. There God says: The fast that I want is this: to break the chains of wickedness … and to share your bread with the hungry. And it is a reminder of Micah 6:8.
> Verses 11–12: The ancestors did not want to hear this. They made their hearts “as hard as flint” (others translate “as hard as a diamond”). Flint is a very hard stone.
> Verses 13–14: That is why the exile came. Whoever does not listen to God when he calls will find that God is silent when he himself calls.
> For today: How do we treat foreigners, poor people, and people who are alone? To God, this is more important than any religious practice.
