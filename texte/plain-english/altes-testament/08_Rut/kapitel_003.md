# Ruth – Chapter 3
### Ruth at night on the threshing floor

---

## Naomi’s plan (Verses 1–5)

[1] Naomi, her mother-in-law, said to her,
“My daughter, shouldn’t I look for rest for you,
so that things will go well for you?
[2] Isn’t Boaz our relative, whose young women you were with?
Look, tonight he is winnowing barley on the threshing floor.
[3] So wash yourself, put on perfumed oil, get dressed,
and go down to the threshing floor.
But don’t let the man know you are there
until he has finished eating and drinking.
[4] When he lies down, notice the place where he is lying.
Then go in, uncover his feet, and lie down.
Then he will tell you what to do.”
[5] She said to her,
“I will do everything you say.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 1, Naomi had wished that her daughters-in-law would find “rest” in the house of a husband. Now she herself wants to make sure that Ruth gets a safe home.
> “Winnowing”: After threshing, people threw the grain into the air with a fork. The evening wind carried away the light chaff, and the heavy grains fell back down. The “threshing floor” was a hard, flat place where this was done. At harvest time, the men slept there to guard the grain.
> Naomi’s plan is brave and also risky. A young woman goes to a man at night. That could easily have been misunderstood. “Uncovering his feet” was probably a sign: Ruth lies down at his feet and in this way silently asks him for protection. Some interpreters think the expression also has a hidden double meaning. But the text clearly shows: Nothing dishonorable happens that night.

---

## Ruth asks Boaz (Verses 6–9)

[6] She went down to the threshing floor
and did everything her mother-in-law had told her.
[7] When Boaz had eaten and drunk and his heart was happy,
he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain.
She came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.
[8] At midnight the man woke up with a start and turned over.
Look! A woman was lying at his feet.
[9] He said, “Who are you?”
She answered,
“I am Ruth, your servant.
Spread the corner of your garment over your servant,
for you are a family redeemer.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ruth does what Naomi said. But then she herself says something that Naomi had not said: She asks Boaz quite directly.
> “Spread the corner of your garment over me” was a request for marriage. It means: Take me under your protection. Take me as your wife (compare Ezekiel 16:8).
> The Hebrew word for “corner” is the same word as “wing.” In Chapter 2:12, Boaz had said: You have come to take refuge under the wings of the LORD. Now Ruth says: Then you be the answer to your own prayer. Spread your wings over me. God often helps through people.
> “You are a family redeemer”: The Hebrew word here is “go’el.” Ruth reminds Boaz of his responsibility for the family of Elimelech.

---

## Boaz promises to help (Verses 10–13)

[10] He said,
“May the LORD bless you, my daughter.
You have shown even more kindness now at the end than at the beginning.
For you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich.
[11] And now, my daughter, don’t be afraid.
I will do for you everything you say.
For the whole town of my people knows that you are a woman of noble character.
[12] It is true that I am a family redeemer.
But there is another redeemer who is a closer relative than I am.
[13] Stay here tonight.
In the morning, if he will do the duty of a redeemer for you, good.
Let him do the redeemer’s duty.
But if he does not want to do the duty of a redeemer for you,
then I will do the duty of a redeemer for you,
as surely as the LORD lives.
Lie down until morning.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Boaz is probably much older than Ruth. He praises her: You could have married a young man. But you are thinking of the family of your dead husband and of Naomi. That is even greater kindness (chesed) than your faithfulness at the beginning.
> “A woman of noble character” is the same expression in Hebrew as in Proverbs 31:10, which asks who can find such a woman. In the Hebrew Bible, the book of Ruth often comes right after the book of Proverbs. Ruth is like a living example of this woman. And in Chapter 2:1, Boaz was described with a similar word. The two are a good match.
> Boaz is honest: There is another man who has the first right. Boaz does not want to do anything behind his back. He wants to settle everything properly and openly.

---

## Ruth returns to Naomi (Verses 14–18)

[14] She lay at his feet until morning.
Then she got up before anyone could recognize another person.
For he said,
“No one must know that the woman came to the threshing floor.”
[15] He said,
“Bring the shawl you are wearing and hold it out.”
She held it out.
He measured out six measures of barley and put it on her back.
Then he went into the town.
[16] When she came to her mother-in-law, Naomi said,
“How did it go, my daughter?”
She told her everything the man had done for her.
[17] She said,
“He gave me these six measures of barley.
For he said,
‘Don’t go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’”
[18] Then Naomi said,
“Wait, my daughter, until you know how things will turn out.
For the man will not rest until he has settled this today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ruth leaves in the dark, so that no false gossip about her arises. Boaz protects her good name.
> The text does not say how big a “measure” was. It was probably an amount that a woman could still carry.
> “Don’t go back … empty-handed”: In Chapter 1:21, Naomi had said: “The LORD has brought me home again empty.” Now the emptiness is being filled step by step. The barley is like a promise: Soon more will come.
> At the end, Ruth has to wait. Sometimes you yourself cannot do anything more, only trust.
