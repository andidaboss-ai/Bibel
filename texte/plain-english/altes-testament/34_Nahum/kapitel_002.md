# Nahum – Chapter 2
### The fall of Nineveh

---

## The attack on Nineveh (Verses 1–5)

[1] The one who smashes things to pieces has come up against you.
Guard the fortress!
Watch the road!
Get ready for battle!
Make your strength as great as you can!
[2] For the LORD will bring back the glory of Jacob,
like the glory of Israel.
For destroyers have destroyed them
and ruined their vines.
[3] The shields of his mighty warriors are red.
The brave men are dressed in scarlet.
Scarlet is a bright red color.
The chariots flash with steel
on the day he gets ready for battle.
The spears of pine wood are waved in the air.
[4] The chariots race wildly in the streets.
They rush back and forth in the squares.
They look like torches.
They run like lightning.
[5] He calls for his best troops.
They stumble as they go.
They hurry to the city wall,
and the protective shield is set up.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles the verses of this chapter are moved by one (there they are verses 2–14).
> Verse 1: Nahum mocks Nineveh: Get ready to defend yourself! It will not help you.
> Verse 2: The reason for all this: God wants to restore his people, who were laid waste by the Assyrians.
> Verses 3–5: A lively description of the attack: red shields, soldiers in scarlet, flashing chariots that race through the streets like torches. The “protective shield” was a kind of roof for soldiers who attacked a wall.

---

## The city falls (Verses 6–10)

[6] The gates of the rivers are opened,
and the palace melts away.
[7] It has been decided:
She is stripped naked, she is carried away.
Her female servants moan like doves
and beat their breasts.
[8] Nineveh has been like a pool of water since long ago.
But now they are running away.
“Stop! Stop!” people cry,
but no one looks back.
[9] Take the silver as plunder!
Take the gold as plunder!
For there is no end to the treasure,
plenty of every kind of precious thing.
[10] She is empty, bare, and ruined.
Hearts melt with fear, knees knock together.
Their bodies and faces have turned pale.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Nineveh lay on the Tigris River and had canals and floodgates. When the “gates of the rivers” are opened, the water floods the city. An ancient writer of history reports a flood that helped when the city was conquered. Historians argue about whether it really happened like that.
> Verse 7: The city is described as a woman who is “stripped naked” and carried away. This is a picture for the humiliation of a conquered city. The women and female servants mourn and cry. In war, women suffer especially. That is injustice, no matter who does it.
> Verse 8: Nineveh was like a large pool in which wealth and people from all over the world gathered. Now everything runs out like water from a broken pool. Everyone is running away.
> Verse 9: The treasures that Assyria had stolen from other peoples are now stolen from Assyria itself.

## Where is the den of the lions? (Verses 11–13)

[11] Where now is the den of the lions,
the place where the young lions were fed?
There the lion and the lioness walked
with the lion cubs,
and no one made them afraid.
[12] The lion tore up enough food for his cubs.
He killed prey for his lionesses.
He filled his caves with the animals he killed,
and his dens with prey.
[13] “Look, I am against you,”
says the LORD of Armies.
“I will burn up her chariots in smoke,
and the sword will kill your young lions.
I will cut off your prey from the earth.
And the voice of your messengers will no longer be heard.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The lion was the symbol of the kings of Assyria. They liked to have themselves shown hunting lions.
> Nineveh was like a lions’ den: The kings robbed the other peoples and filled their palaces with plunder. No one could stop them.
> Verse 13: But now God says: “Look, I am against you.” The den will be empty. The “messengers” of Assyria, who brought threats and orders to other peoples (compare 2 Kings 18:17–35), will fall silent.
