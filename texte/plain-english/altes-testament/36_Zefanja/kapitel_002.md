# Zephaniah – Chapter 2
### Seek the LORD!

---

## A call to turn back (Verses 1–3)

[1] Gather together, yes, gather together,
you nation that has no shame,
[2] before the set time comes,
when the day blows away like chaff,
before the burning anger of the LORD comes on you,
before the day of the LORD’s anger comes on you.
Chaff is the dry skin of grain. The wind blows it away.
[3] Seek the LORD, all you humble people of the land,
who have kept his rules.
Seek what is right.
Seek humility.
Maybe you will be hidden
on the day of the LORD’s anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the announcement of judgment there is a call: It is not too late yet! Gather together, turn back, before the day comes.
> Verse 3: This is a key verse of the book. It speaks to the “humble,” the “poor” in the land. They are told: Seek the LORD, seek what is right, seek humility.
> “Maybe you will be hidden”: This is a play on words with the name Zephaniah (“The LORD has hidden”). God protects those who seek him. The word “maybe” shows: No one can control God. But there is hope.

---

## Judgment on the Philistines in the west (Verses 4–7)

[4] For Gaza will be left empty,
and Ashkelon will lie in ruins.
Ashdod will be driven out at noon,
and Ekron will be pulled up by the roots.
[5] How terrible it will be for those who live by the sea coast,
the nation of the Cherethites!
The word of the LORD is against you,
Canaan, land of the Philistines.
“I will destroy you until no one is left living there.”
[6] The sea coast will become pastures,
with huts for shepherds and pens for flocks.
[7] The coast will belong to those who are left of the house of Judah.
They will find pasture there.
In the houses of Ashkelon they will lie down in the evening,
for the LORD their God will take care of them
and bring them back to good times.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the view turns to the neighboring nations in all four directions: west (the Philistines), east (Moab and Ammon), south (Cush), and north (Assyria).
> Verse 4: In Hebrew these are plays on words: “Gaza” sounds like the word for “left empty,” and “Ekron” sounds like the word for “pulled up by the roots.”
> Verse 5: The Philistines lived on the coast of the Mediterranean Sea. They probably came at first from the area of the Aegean Sea, maybe from Crete. That is why they are called “Cherethites” here. The word probably means “Cretans.”
> Verse 7: Those who are left of Judah will one day live there in peace. God will “bring them back to good times,” that is, make everything good again.

---

## Judgment on Moab and Ammon in the east (Verses 8–11)

[8] “I have heard how Moab mocked
and how the people of Ammon insulted,
how they mocked my people
and made themselves great against their land.
[9] So as surely as I live,”
says the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel,
“Moab will surely become like Sodom,
and the people of Ammon like Gomorrah:
a place that belongs to nettles and salt pits,
a land of ruins forever.
Those who are left of my people will rob them.
Those of my nation who survive will take their land.”
Nettles are weeds that sting.
[10] This will happen to them because of their pride,
because they have mocked
and made themselves great against the people of the LORD of Armies.
[11] The LORD will be terrifying to them,
for he will starve all the gods of the land.
People will worship him,
everyone from his own place,
all the shores of the nations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–10: Moab and Ammon lay east of the Jordan River. They had mocked Israel and threatened its land. Their pride will be punished. Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19) were cities by the Dead Sea. Their destruction became a saying.
> Verse 11: A surprising view of the future: God will “starve” the false gods, that is, take all their power away. And then people of all nations will worship God, “everyone from his own place.” No one has to come to Jerusalem. God can be worshiped everywhere in the world.

---

## Judgment on Cush and Assyria (Verses 12–15)

[12] “You Cushites too,
you will be killed by my sword.”
[13] He will stretch out his hand against the north
and destroy Assyria.
He will make Nineveh a land of ruins,
as dry as the desert.
[14] Herds will lie down in the middle of her,
all kinds of animals.
The pelican and the porcupine will spend the night
on the tops of her pillars.
Their calls will echo through the windows.
Ruin will be on the doorsteps,
for he has stripped bare the cedar beams.
[15] This is the happy city that lived without a care,
that said in her heart,
“I am, and there is no one besides me.”
How she has become a ruin,
a place where animals lie down!
Everyone who passes by her
will hiss and shake their fists.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Cush lay south of Egypt. The Cushites are the people of Cush. For a time, kings from Cush also ruled over Egypt. This is about the political empire, not about the skin color or the origin of people. In Chapter 3:10, Zephaniah says that people from Cush will worship God. God loves all nations the same (compare Amos 9:7).
> Verses 13–15: Nineveh, the capital of Assyria, will become a ruin where wild animals live. This happened in the year 612 BC (see the book of Nahum).
> Which animals exactly are meant (pelican, porcupine, owl, bittern) is not certain. The meaning is: Where people used to celebrate, now only wild animals live.
> Verse 15: Nineveh said: “I am, and there is no one besides me.” This is pride: putting yourself in the place of God (compare Isaiah 47:8). Such pride leads to a fall.
