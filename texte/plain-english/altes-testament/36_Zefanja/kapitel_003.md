# Zephaniah – Chapter 3
### God rejoices over you with singing

---

## How terrible for the city that does not listen (Verses 1–7)

[1] How terrible it will be for her who rebels and is unclean,
the city that treats people harshly!
[2] She did not listen to the voice.
She did not accept correction.
She did not trust in the LORD.
She did not come close to her God.
[3] Her princes inside her are roaring lions.
Her judges are wolves in the evening.
They leave nothing until the next day.
[4] Her prophets are proud and cannot be trusted.
Her priests have made the holy place unholy.
They have done violence to the law.
[5] The LORD inside her is righteous.
He does no wrong.
Every morning he brings his justice to light.
He does not fail.
But those who do wrong know no shame.
[6] “I have cut off nations.
Their towers lie in ruins.
I have made their streets empty,
so that no one walks through them.
Their cities are destroyed,
so that no one is left,
no one lives there.
[7] I said,
‘Just fear me.
Accept correction.’
Then her home would not be cut off,
with all that I had decided for her.
But they got up early
and made all their deeds evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it is Jerusalem’s own turn. After the neighboring nations, God’s own city is no better.
> Verse 2: She has failed in four ways: She did not listen, she did not let herself be corrected, she did not trust, and she did not come close to God.
> Verses 3–4: All the leaders fail: the princes are like wild animals, the judges like hungry wolves, the prophets are proud, the priests make the holy place unholy.
> Verse 5: The contrast: God is in the middle of the city, and he is righteous. Every morning, as faithfully as the sun rises, he shows his justice. But those who do wrong are not ashamed.
> Verse 7: God hoped: When they see what happens to other nations, they will turn back. But they even “got up early” to do evil.

---

## God will make the nations pure (Verses 8–10)

[8] “So wait for me,” says the LORD,
“until the day when I rise up to take the prey.
For I have decided to gather the nations,
to bring the kingdoms together,
to pour out my fury on them,
all my burning anger.
For the whole earth will be eaten up
by the fire of my jealousy.
[9] For then I will make the lips of the peoples pure,
so that they all call on the name of the LORD
and serve him shoulder to shoulder.
[10] From beyond the rivers of Cush,
my worshipers, the daughter of my scattered people,
will bring me my offering.
“Daughter” here is a picture word for a people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Once more the judgment on the whole earth.
> Verse 9: But then the great turning point! The goal of the judgment is not destruction but cleansing. God makes the lips of the nations “pure.” They will all call on God together, “shoulder to shoulder,” like people who carry a heavy load together.
> This is a reminder of the tower of Babel (Genesis 11): There the languages were mixed up, and the people were scattered. Here the nations come together again.
> Verse 10: Even from far-away Cush, south of Egypt, people will come to worship God. In the New Testament, a man from Cush (Ethiopia), a court official, is baptized (Acts 8:26–39).

---

## A humble people (Verses 11–13)

[11] On that day you will not need to be ashamed
of all your deeds
by which you have sinned against me.
For then I will take away from among you
those who are proud and boast.
And you will no longer be proud
on my holy mountain.
[12] But I will leave among you a humble and poor people.
They will find safety in the name of the LORD.
[13] Those who are left of Israel will not do wrong
and will not tell lies.
No lying tongue will be found in their mouths.
For they will eat and lie down like sheep,
and no one will make them afraid.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: God takes the shame away. But he removes the proud.
> Verse 12: What is left is “a humble and poor people.” It is not the powerful and rich, but the small ones who find safety with God. With them God makes a new beginning.
> This is a reminder of Jesus: “Blessed are those who are poor in spirit … Blessed are the gentle” (Matthew 5:3, 5).
> Verse 13: This new people lives honestly, without lies and tricks. And it lives in peace, like a flock that no one frightens.

---

## Rejoice, daughter of Zion! (Verses 14–20)

[14] Sing, daughter of Zion!
Shout for joy, Israel!
Be glad and rejoice with all your heart,
daughter of Jerusalem!
[15] The LORD has taken away your punishments.
He has thrown out your enemy.
The King of Israel, the LORD, is among you.
You will not be afraid of evil anymore.
[16] On that day people will say to Jerusalem,
“Don’t be afraid, Zion!
Don’t let your hands grow weak!”
[17] The LORD your God is among you,
a mighty one who saves.
He will rejoice over you with joy.
He will calm you in his love.
He will rejoice over you with singing.
[18] “I will take away from you
those who are sad about the set feasts.
They are a burden and a shame to you.
[19] Look, at that time I will deal with all
who make you suffer.
I will save those who limp
and gather those who were driven away.
I will give them praise and honor,
those whose shame was known in all the earth.
[20] At that time I will bring you home.
At that time I will gather you.
For I will give you honor and praise
among all the peoples of the earth,
when I bring you back to good times before your eyes,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book that began with the “day of anger” ends with a song of joy!
> Verses 14–15: Jerusalem is to sing and shout for joy. The punishment is over. God himself is in the city as King.
> Verse 16: “Don’t be afraid, Zion! Don’t let your hands grow weak!” A word of encouragement.
> Verse 17: This is one of the most beautiful verses in the Bible. Not only do the people rejoice over God, but God rejoices over his people! He shouts for joy and sings over the people he loves. He is like parents who are happy about their child and sing a song for it.
> “He will calm you in his love”: Others also translate “he will be silent in his love,” that is, so full of love that he needs no more words.
> Verse 18: The verse is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: God also gathers those who were sad because they could not celebrate the feasts in Jerusalem while they were in exile. And he takes away their shame.
> Verses 19–20: God gathers the weak, those who limp and those who were driven away. Those who were looked down on everywhere receive honor and praise.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Zephaniah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The day of the LORD:** Zephaniah announces a “day of anger” on Judah and on all nations (1:14–18). The famous “Dies irae” came from this.
> **Against idols and not caring:** Judgment comes on those who worship Baal, who do not want to decide, and who say in their hearts, “The LORD will not do good, and he will not do evil” (1:4–6, 12). That means: God does nothing anyway.
> **Seek the LORD!** “Seek what is right. Seek humility. Maybe you will be hidden on the day of the LORD’s anger” (2:3).
> **Judgment on all nations:** In all directions. And yet in the end people of all nations will worship God (2:11).
> **Pure lips for all nations:** “So that they all call on the name of the LORD and serve him shoulder to shoulder” (3:9).
> **A humble and poor people:** God makes a new beginning with the small ones who find safety with him (3:12).
> **God’s joy:** “The LORD your God is among you … He will rejoice over you with singing” (3:17).
> **For today:** God does not just shrug. He takes wrong seriously. But his last word is love and joy, over you too.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Haggai. He lived after the return from exile. He encouraged the people to rebuild the temple that had been destroyed.
