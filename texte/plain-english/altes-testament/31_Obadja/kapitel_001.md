# Obadiah – Chapter 1
### Do not be glad about your brother’s disaster

---

## Before we begin: Who was Obadiah?

Obadiah is the shortest book of the Old Testament. It has only one single chapter with 21 verses.
We know almost nothing about the prophet himself. His name means “servant of the LORD.”
The book speaks against Edom. Edom was a neighboring people southeast of the Dead Sea, in a rocky mountain land. The people of Edom were seen as the descendants of Esau, the twin brother of Jacob (Genesis 25–36). So Israel and Edom were “brother peoples.”
The book is probably about the time when the Babylonians conquered Jerusalem in the 6th century BC (around 587/586 BC). At that time the people of Edom did not help. Just the opposite: They were glad. They joined in the robbing, and they handed over refugees (compare Psalm 137:7; Ezekiel 35).
The message: Whoever is glad about the disaster of others and takes advantage of the weak will be judged too. “As you have done, it will be done to you” (verse 15).

---

## The pride of Edom (Verses 1–4)

[1] This is the vision of Obadiah.
This is what the Lord GOD says about Edom:
We have heard news from the LORD.
A messenger has been sent among the nations.
He says,
“Get up! Let’s go to battle against her!”
[2] “Look, I have made you small among the nations.
People look down on you very much.
[3] The pride of your heart has fooled you.
You live in the cracks of the rock.
Your home is high up.
You say in your heart,
‘Who will bring me down to the ground?’
[4] Even if you fly up high like the eagle,
even if your nest is set among the stars,
I will bring you down from there,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Edom lived in a mountain land with steep rocks. The cities were high up. People thought no one could conquer them. (The famous rock city of Petra lies in this area. But it was built up only later by the Nabataeans.)
> That is why Edom felt completely safe: “Who will bring me down to the ground?”
> God answers: Your pride has fooled you. Even if you build your nest among the stars, I will bring you down.
> For today: Whoever thinks no one can touch him easily becomes proud. Safety does not come from walls, wealth, or power.

---

## Robbed and betrayed by friends (Verses 5–9)

[5] “If thieves came to you,
if robbers came by night,
oh, what disaster is waiting for you!
Wouldn’t they steal only until they had enough?
If grape pickers came to you,
wouldn’t they leave some grapes behind?
[6] How Esau will be searched through!
How his hidden treasures will be found!
[7] All the men you made agreements with
have sent you away, as far as the border.
The men who lived in peace with you
have fooled you and defeated you.
Friends who eat your bread
set a trap for you.
He has no understanding.”
[8] “On that day,” says the LORD,
“won’t I destroy the wise men of Edom,
and understanding from the mountain of Esau?
[9] Your mighty warriors, Teman, will be terrified.
And so everyone on the mountain of Esau will be cut off and killed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: Even thieves take only as much as they can carry. And grape pickers always leave a few grapes hanging for the poor. But Edom will be robbed completely. Nothing will be left.
> Verse 7: Of all people, the allies and friends who sat at the table with Edom will betray it. This reminds us of Psalm 41:9 (in some Bibles 41:10).
> Verse 8: Edom was famous for its wisdom (compare Jeremiah 49:7). Job’s friend Eliphaz came from Teman (Job 2:11). But this wisdom will not help.
> Teman is a town or an area in Edom. Here it stands for the whole land.

---

## What Edom did to its brother (Verses 10–14)

[10] “Because of the violence you did to your brother Jacob,
shame will cover you.
And you will be cut off forever.
[11] On the day you stood by and watched,
on the day strangers carried away his goods,
and foreigners went in through his gates
and threw lots for Jerusalem,
you were like one of them.
[12] But do not look down on your brother
on the day of his disaster.
Do not be glad about the people of Judah
on the day of their ruin.
Do not talk big
on the day of trouble.
[13] Do not go in through the gate of my people
on the day of their disaster.
Do not look down on their suffering
on the day of their disaster.
Do not grab their wealth
on the day of their disaster.
[14] Do not stand at the crossroads
to kill those of his people who escape.
Do not hand over those who are left
on the day of trouble.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the heart of the book. What did Edom do wrong?
> Verse 11: It watched when the enemies robbed Jerusalem. It “stood by and watched,” and so it was “like one of them.” Whoever watches injustice and does nothing shares the guilt.
> Verses 12–14: And it got worse and worse: First Edom looked down. Then it was glad. Then it bragged. Then it joined in the robbing. And in the end it waited for refugees at the crossroads and handed them over to the enemies.
> What makes it special: Edom was the “brother people.” Here we see the old quarrel between the twins Jacob and Esau (Genesis 27).
> For today: Being glad about another person’s trouble, looking away, turning refugees away or handing them over, making a profit from the disaster of others. Obadiah shows how seriously God takes this.

---

## The day of the LORD for all nations (Verses 15–16)

[15] “For the day of the LORD is near for all the nations!
As you have done, it will be done to you.
What you have done will come back on your own head.
[16] For as you have drunk on my holy mountain,
so all the nations will drink without stopping.
Yes, they will drink and swallow it down.
And they will be as though they had never been.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: A basic rule of God’s justice: What you do to others comes back to you. Jesus says something similar: “With the measure you use to measure, it will be measured to you” (Matthew 7:2). And said in a positive way: “So whatever you want people to do to you, you shall also do to them” (Matthew 7:12).
> Verse 16: A picture from the prophets: the “cup” of judgment. Edom celebrated and drank on Mount Zion when Jerusalem fell. Now all the nations must drink the cup of judgment (compare Jeremiah 25:15–29).

---

## Rescue on Mount Zion (Verses 17–21)

[17] “But on Mount Zion there will be people who escape.
And it will be holy.
The house of Jacob will take back what belongs to them.
[18] The house of Jacob will be a fire,
the house of Joseph a flame,
and the house of Esau dry straw.
They will burn among them and burn them up.
No one will be left of the house of Esau.”
Yes, the LORD has spoken.
[19] The people of the South will take the mountain of Esau.
The people of the lowland will take the land of the Philistines.
They will take the land of Ephraim
and the land of Samaria.
Benjamin will take Gilead.
[20] The prisoners of this army of the people of Israel,
who are among the Canaanites,
will take the land as far as Zarephath.
The prisoners from Jerusalem, who are in Sepharad,
will take the cities of the Negev.
The Negev is the dry land in the south.
[21] Saviors will go up on Mount Zion
to judge the mountains of Esau.
And the kingdom will belong to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: After the disaster there is hope: There will be people who are saved on Zion. Those who were taken away will come back and get their land again.
> Verse 18: A hard picture: Jacob becomes a fire, Esau becomes dry straw. This language came out of times of war, after great injustice had been suffered. It announces God’s judgment. It is not a command to people to destroy a nation. No people today may use such verses to justify hate or violence against another people.
> Verses 19–20: The land will be taken back, in all directions. Zarephath lies on the coast in the north (near Sidon). It is not certain where “Sepharad” was. People often think of Sardis in Asia Minor. Later the Jews called Spain “Sepharad.” That is where the name “Sephardic Jews” comes from.
> Verse 21: The last word: “And the kingdom will belong to the LORD.” Not Edom, not Israel, not any power of the world has the last word. God has it. The “saviors” are people whom God calls to bring justice.

---

## Looking back: What did we read in the book of Obadiah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The shortest book:** Obadiah has only 21 verses. It is a word against Edom, the brother people of Israel.
> **Pride comes before a fall:** Edom felt safe in its rocks. “Who will bring me down to the ground?” God says: “I will bring you down from there” (verses 3–4).
> **Looking away makes you share the guilt:** “On the day you stood by and watched … you were like one of them” (verse 11).
> **Do not be glad about others’ trouble:** Do not be glad when things go badly for your brother. Do not take advantage of his need. Do not hand over refugees (verses 12–14).
> **What you do comes back:** “As you have done, it will be done to you” (verse 15).
> **God has the last word:** “And the kingdom will belong to the LORD” (verse 21).
> **For today:** How do I react when things go badly for others, even for people I am in a quarrel with? Do I look away? Am I secretly glad? Or do I help?
> **What comes next?** Next comes Jonah, the prophet who ran away from God. It is a story about God’s mercy, even for the enemies of Israel.
