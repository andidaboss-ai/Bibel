# 2 Kings – Chapter 15
### Kings in quick succession

---

## Azariah (Uzziah), king of Judah (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel,
Azariah the son of Amaziah, king of Judah, began to rule.
[2] He was 16 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 52 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jecoliah, from Jerusalem.
[3] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his father Amaziah had done.
[4] But the high places were not taken away.
The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places.
[5] The LORD struck the king,
so that he was a leper until the day of his death.
He lived in a separate house.
Jotham, the king’s son, was in charge of the palace
and judged the people of the land.
[6] The rest of the things Azariah did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[7] Azariah died and joined his ancestors,
and they buried him with his ancestors in David’s city.
And his son Jotham became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Azariah is also called Uzziah. He ruled for a very long time and with success. 2 Chronicles 26 tells more about him: he became powerful, but then proud. He wanted to burn incense in the temple himself, which only priests were allowed to do. After that he became a leper.
> Because he was a leper, he had to live apart. His son Jotham ruled for him.
> In the year that King Uzziah died, the prophet Isaiah had his great vision in the temple, in which God called him (Isaiah 6:1).
> In Jerusalem a stone tablet was found that was made much later. It says in Aramaic that the bones of Uzziah, king of Judah, were brought here. Today it is in the Israel Museum.
> Azariah (Uzziah) ruled from about 792 to 740 BC, partly together with his father and his son.

## Zechariah and Shallum, kings of Israel (Verses 8–16)

[8] In the thirty-eighth year of Azariah king of Judah,
Zechariah the son of Jeroboam ruled over Israel in Samaria for six months.
[9] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, as his ancestors had done.
He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which that Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[10] Shallum the son of Jabesh plotted against him.
He struck him in front of the people and killed him,
and he became king in his place.
[11] The rest of the things Zechariah did,
they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
[12] This was the LORD’s word that he spoke to Jehu:
“Your sons down to the fourth generation will sit on the throne of Israel.”
And so it happened.
[13] Shallum the son of Jabesh became king in the thirty-ninth year of Uzziah king of Judah.
He ruled for one month in Samaria.
[14] Then Menahem the son of Gadi went up from Tirzah.
He came to Samaria,
struck Shallum the son of Jabesh in Samaria,
killed him, and became king in his place.
[15] The rest of the things Shallum did, and the plot that he made,
they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
[16] Then Menahem attacked Tiphsah
and all who were in it and the land around it, starting from Tirzah.
He attacked it because they did not open their gates to him.
And he ripped open all their women who were pregnant.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Zechariah, Jehu’s family comes to an end after four generations, just as God had said.
> After that, chaos begins in the northern kingdom: Zechariah rules for six months, Shallum for only one month. Each one is murdered by the one who comes after him. In this time the prophet Hosea laments: “They have set up kings, but not by my choice” (Hosea 8:4).
> Menahem commits a horrible war crime against a city that did not want to surrender to him. The Bible names it without making it sound better. Such cruelty against defenseless people, especially against women and unborn children, can never be justified.
> It is not certain where Tiphsah lay. Some old translations have “Tappuah” here, a city in Israel.

## Menahem and the Assyrians (Verses 17–22)

[17] In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah king of Judah,
Menahem the son of Gadi became king over Israel, for ten years in Samaria.
[18] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
All his life he did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which that Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[19] Pul the king of Assyria came against the land.
Menahem gave Pul 1,000 talents of silver (about 75,000 pounds),
so that Pul would help him make the kingdom firm in his hand.
[20] Menahem forced Israel to pay the money,
all the wealthy and powerful men,
50 shekels of silver (about 20 ounces) from each man,
to give it to the king of Assyria.
So the king of Assyria turned back
and did not stay there in the land.
[21] The rest of the things Menahem did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[22] Menahem died and joined his ancestors,
and his son Pekahiah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a new great power appears: Assyria, in the area of today’s Iraq. The Assyrians were feared for their cruelty.
> “Pul” is another name for Tiglath Pileser III, who ruled from 745 to 727 BC. He is known from many Assyrian inscriptions. They also mention that “Menahem of Samaria” paid him tribute.
> The Bible says “1,000 talents of silver” (a talent is about 75 pounds) and “50 shekels” for each man (a shekel is about 0.4 ounces). If you do the math, about 60,000 wealthy men had to pay.
> Menahem buys himself protection. With this, Israel becomes dependent on Assyria.

## Pekahiah and Pekah, kings of Israel (Verses 23–31)

[23] In the fiftieth year of Azariah king of Judah,
Pekahiah the son of Menahem became king over Israel in Samaria, for two years.
[24] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which that Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[25] Pekah the son of Remaliah, his officer, plotted against him.
He attacked him in Samaria, in the fortress of the king’s house,
together with Argob and Arieh.
With him were 50 men of the Gileadites.
He killed him and became king in his place.
[26] The rest of the things Pekahiah did, and all that he did,
they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
[27] In the fifty-second year of Azariah king of Judah,
Pekah the son of Remaliah became king over Israel in Samaria, for 20 years.
[28] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which that Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[29] In the days of Pekah king of Israel,
Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria came.
He captured Ijon, Abel Beth Maacah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, and Galilee,
all the land of Naphtali,
and he carried the people away captive to Assyria.
[30] Hoshea the son of Elah made a plot against Pekah the son of Remaliah.
He attacked him, killed him, and became king in his place,
in the twentieth year of Jotham the son of Uzziah.
[31] The rest of the things Pekah did, and all that he did,
they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> One murder of a king follows the next.
> Under Pekah the Assyrians conquer the whole north of Israel, Galilee, and the land east of the Jordan. For the first time many Israelites are carried away to Assyria. That was about 733/732 BC.
> The northern kingdom gets smaller and smaller. Almost only the area around the capital city Samaria is left.
> In this time the prophet Isaiah speaks of “the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali”: “The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light” (Isaiah 9:1–2). Matthew applies this word to Jesus, who began his work in Galilee (Matthew 4:13–16).
> Pekah’s 20 years are hard to fit in. Perhaps he had already ruled earlier in the east of the land, alongside other kings.
> Hoshea is also mentioned in Assyrian inscriptions. There Tiglath Pileser claims that he made Hoshea king.

## Jotham, king of Judah (Verses 32–38)

[32] In the second year of Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel,
Jotham the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, began to rule.
[33] He was 25 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 16 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jerusha, the daughter of Zadok.
[34] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
He did just as his father Uzziah had done.
[35] But the high places were not taken away.
The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places.
He built the upper gate of the LORD’s house.
[36] The rest of the things Jotham did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[37] In those days the LORD began
to send Rezin the king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah against Judah.
[38] Jotham died and joined his ancestors.
He was buried with his ancestors in the city of his ancestor David.
And his son Ahaz became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jotham was a good king, like his father Uzziah.
> Syria and Israel join together against Judah. They want to force Judah to fight with them against Assyria. That is told in the next chapter.
> Compared with the northern kingdom, Judah is more stable: there the sons follow their fathers, from David’s family. In the north, on the other hand, there were six kings in only about 20 years, and four of them were murdered.
> Jotham ruled from about 750 to 735 BC, at first for his sick father.
