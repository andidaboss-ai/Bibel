# 2 Kings – Chapter 10
### Jehu’s bloody rule

---

## The 70 sons of Ahab (Verses 1–11)

[1] Ahab had 70 sons in Samaria.
Jehu wrote letters and sent them to Samaria,
to the rulers of Jezreel, the elders,
and to those who were bringing up Ahab’s sons.
He wrote:
[2] “Your master’s sons are with you.
You have chariots and horses, a walled city, and weapons.
As soon as this letter comes to you,
[3] choose the best and most able of your master’s sons.
Put him on his father’s throne,
and fight for your master’s house.”
[4] But they were terribly afraid and said,
“Look, the two kings could not stand against him!
How then can we stand?”
[5] Then a message came to Jehu.
It was from the man in charge of the palace, the man in charge of the city,
the elders, and those who were bringing up the children:
“We are your servants.
We will do everything you tell us.
We will not make anyone king.
Do what is good in your eyes.”
[6] Then he wrote them a second letter:
“If you are on my side, and if you will listen to my voice,
take the heads of the men, your master’s sons,
and come to me at Jezreel by this time tomorrow.”
The king’s sons, 70 persons, were with the great men of the city.
These men were bringing them up.
[7] When the letter came to them,
they took the king’s sons and killed them, 70 persons.
They put their heads in baskets
and sent them to him at Jezreel.
[8] A messenger came and told him,
“They have brought the heads of the king’s sons.”
He said,
“Lay them in two heaps at the entrance of the gate until morning.”
[9] In the morning he went out, stood there, and said to all the people,
“You are righteous.
Look, I plotted against my master and killed him.
But who killed all these?
[10] Know now that nothing of the LORD’s word will fall to the ground,
the word that the LORD spoke against Ahab’s house.
For the LORD has done what he said through his servant Elijah.”
[11] So Jehu struck down all who were left of Ahab’s house in Jezreel,
all his great men, his close friends, and his priests,
until he left him no one.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is very bloody and hard to read.
> “70 sons” probably means all of Ahab’s male descendants, including grandsons.
> Verse 9: “Righteous” means: living right before God. Here Jehu means: you are not guilty.
> Jehu is clever and ruthless. He has the leading men of Samaria kill the princes. Then he says to the people: I killed only one, but who killed all these? In this way he makes everyone share the guilt.
> Jehu points to God’s word. But he goes far beyond it. He also kills friends, officials, and priests. About 100 years later the prophet Hosea condemns the blood shed at Jezreel (Hosea 1:4). God had announced a judgment on Ahab’s house. But he had not given Jehu permission for violence without limits.
> The Bible reports these deeds honestly. They are not an example to follow. No one may point to God to justify killing people.

## Ahaziah’s brothers (Verses 12–14)

[12] He got up and set out and went to Samaria.
On the way he was at the shearing house of the shepherds.
A shearing house is a place where sheep are sheared, that is, where their wool is cut off.
[13] There Jehu met the brothers of Ahaziah king of Judah.
He asked,
“Who are you?”
They answered,
“We are the brothers of Ahaziah.
We are going down to greet the sons of the king and the sons of the queen.”
[14] He said,
“Take them alive!”
They took them alive
and killed them at the pit of the shearing house, 42 men.
He did not leave any of them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The relatives of the king of Judah do not yet know anything about the overthrow. They want to visit Ahab’s family. Jehu has them all killed too.
> “Brothers” here probably means relatives in general. Ahaziah’s real brothers had already died earlier (2 Chronicles 21:17).
> “The queen” is Jezebel, the queen mother.

## Jehonadab the son of Rechab (Verses 15–17)

[15] When he had left there,
he met Jehonadab the son of Rechab coming to meet him.
He greeted him and said to him,
“Is your heart true, as my heart is with your heart?”
Jehonadab answered,
“It is.”
Jehu said,
“If it is, give me your hand.”
He gave him his hand,
and Jehu took him up into the chariot with him.
[16] He said,
“Come with me, and see how zealous I am for the LORD.”
Being zealous means: being full of eager, burning devotion.
So they had him ride in his chariot.
[17] When he came to Samaria,
he struck down all who were left of Ahab’s family in Samaria,
until he had destroyed them,
just as the LORD had said to Elijah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehonadab was the leader of an especially strict group, the Rechabites. They drank no wine, built no houses, and lived in tents, as a sign of their faithfulness to God and against the comfortable life in the cities. Later the prophet Jeremiah praises them for their faithfulness (Jeremiah 35).
> With Jehonadab, Jehu wants to show that he is on the side of the devout people. “See how zealous I am for the LORD” sounds proud. True zeal for God does not need an audience.

## The worshipers of Baal are killed (Verses 18–28)

[18] Jehu gathered all the people together and said to them,
“Ahab served Baal a little.
Jehu will serve him much.
[19] So now call all the prophets of Baal to me,
all his worshipers and all his priests.
Let no one be missing,
because I have a great sacrifice for Baal.
Whoever is missing will not live.”
But Jehu was acting with a trick,
so that he could destroy the worshipers of Baal.
[20] Jehu said,
“Set apart a holy gathering for Baal!”
So they announced it.
[21] Jehu sent messengers through all Israel.
And all the worshipers of Baal came.
Not one man was left who did not come.
They came into the house of Baal,
and the house of Baal was filled from one end to the other.
[22] He said to the man in charge of the wardrobe,
“Bring out robes for all the worshipers of Baal!”
So he brought robes out for them.
[23] Jehu went into the house of Baal with Jehonadab the son of Rechab.
Then he said to the worshipers of Baal,
“Search and make sure that none of the LORD’s servants are here with you,
but only the worshipers of Baal.”
[24] So they went in to offer sacrifices and burnt offerings.
Jehu had placed 80 men outside and had said,
“If any of the men I bring into your hands escapes,
the man who lets him go will pay for it with his own life.”
[25] As soon as he had finished offering the burnt offering,
Jehu said to the guards and to the officers,
“Go in and kill them! Let no one escape!”
So they struck them down with the sword.
The guards and the officers threw the bodies out
and went into the inner room of the house of Baal.
[26] They brought out the stone pillars that were in the house of Baal
and burned them.
[27] They broke down the pillar of Baal
and broke down the house of Baal
and made it into a toilet, and it is that to this day.
[28] So Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> With a lie, Jehu lures all the worshipers of Baal into the temple and has them killed there.
> With this, the worship of Baal in the northern kingdom comes to an end. But the means are lies and mass murder. That is deeply shocking.
> We have to see this story in its own time: in those days a struggle for power between royal families and religions was often carried out with the greatest cruelty. The Bible reports it, but in other places it makes clear that God takes no pleasure in death (Ezekiel 18:32). Religious violence is not God’s will. Jesus clearly rejected violence in God’s name (Matthew 26:52).
> The temple of Baal is turned into a latrine, that is, a toilet. That was the greatest contempt that could be shown.

## Jehu stays with the golden calves (Verses 29–36)

[29] But Jehu did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which he had made Israel sin:
the golden calves that were in Bethel and in Dan.
[30] The LORD said to Jehu,
“You have done well in doing what is right in my eyes,
and you have done to Ahab’s house all that was in my heart.
So your descendants will sit on the throne of Israel down to the fourth generation.”
[31] But Jehu was not careful to live by the law of the LORD, the God of Israel,
with all his heart.
He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam,
the sins through which he had made Israel sin.
[32] In those days the LORD began to cut away parts of Israel.
Hazael struck them in all the territory of Israel,
[33] from the Jordan eastward:
all the land of Gilead, the Gadites, the Reubenites, and the Manassites,
from Aroer, which is by the valley of the Arnon,
both Gilead and Bashan.
[34] The rest of Jehu’s story is written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
It tells everything he did and how strong he was.
[35] Jehu died and joined his ancestors.
They buried him in Samaria.
His son Jehoahaz became king in his place.
[36] The time that Jehu ruled over Israel in Samaria was 28 years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehu has gotten rid of Baal. For this he receives a promise: his family is to rule for four generations. And that is what happens: Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam II, and Zechariah.
> But Jehu goes only halfway. He leaves Jeroboam’s golden calves standing. He probably cared more about power than about God. He was zealous against others, but not with his whole heart for God.
> At the same time, under Jehu, Israel loses all the land east of the Jordan to Hazael of Syria.
> On the “Black Obelisk” of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III (today in the British Museum in London) there is a picture of a man bowing down to the ground before the Assyrian king. The inscription calls him “Jehu, son of Omri” (or a messenger of Jehu). It is the oldest known picture of an Israelite king. Jehu had to pay tribute to the Assyrians.
> Jehu ruled from about 841 to 814 BC.
