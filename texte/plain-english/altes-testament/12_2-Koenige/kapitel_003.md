# 2 Kings – Chapter 3
### The war against Moab

---

## Jehoram, king of Israel (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jehoram the son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria
in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.
He ruled for twelve years.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
but not like his father and his mother.
For he took away the pillar of Baal that his father had made.
[3] But he held on to the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
He did not turn away from them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoram is another son of Ahab and Jezebel. He is not quite as bad as his parents. He takes away a sign of the worship of Baal. But he stays with Jeroboam’s golden calves.
> Jehoram ruled from about 852 to 841 BC.

---

## Three kings march against Moab (Verses 4–12)

[4] Mesha king of Moab was a sheep breeder.
He had to supply the king of Israel with 100,000 lambs
and the wool of 100,000 rams.
[5] But when Ahab was dead,
the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel.
[6] King Jehoram went out of Samaria at that time
and gathered all Israel for war.
[7] He went and sent a message to Jehoshaphat the king of Judah:
“The king of Moab has rebelled against me.
Will you go with me to fight against Moab?”
He said,
“I will go up.
I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses.”
[8] Then he asked,
“Which way shall we go up?”
Jehoram answered,
“The way through the wilderness of Edom.”
[9] So the king of Israel went with the king of Judah and the king of Edom.
They marched for seven days on a roundabout way.
There was no water for the army or for the animals that followed them.
[10] The king of Israel said,
“Oh no!
The LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab.”
[11] But Jehoshaphat said,
“Isn’t there a prophet of the LORD here, so that we can ask the LORD through him?”
One of the king of Israel’s servants answered,
“Elisha the son of Shaphat is here,
who poured water on the hands of Elijah.”
[12] Jehoshaphat said,
“The LORD’s word is with him.”
So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat and the king of Edom went down to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moab lies east of the Dead Sea. Every year it had to pay Israel a high tax in the form of sheep and wool.
> Mesha is also known outside the Bible: in 1868 the “Mesha Stone” was found in Jordan. It is a large inscription. In it Mesha himself tells how Moab freed itself from Israel. There he also names Omri, the king of Israel, and the God of Israel by his name. Today the stone is in the Louvre in Paris.
> The three kings want to attack Moab from the south, through Edom. But in the wilderness they run out of water.
> Again it is Jehoshaphat who asks: “Isn’t there a prophet of the LORD here” (as in 1 Kings 22:7).
> “Who poured water on the hands of Elijah” means: who was Elijah’s servant and student.

---

## Elisha promises water (Verses 13–20)

[13] Elisha said to the king of Israel,
“What do I have to do with you?
Go to the prophets of your father and to the prophets of your mother.”
The king of Israel said to him,
“No, for the LORD has called these three kings together
to hand them over to Moab.”
[14] Elisha said,
“As surely as the LORD of Armies lives, before whom I stand:
if I did not respect Jehoshaphat the king of Judah,
I would not look at you or even see you.
[15] But now bring me a musician.”
When the musician played, the LORD’s hand came on Elisha.
[16] He said,
“The LORD says,
‘Make this valley full of ditches.’
[17] For the LORD says,
‘You will not see wind, and you will not see rain,
yet that valley will be filled with water.
And you will drink, you and your livestock and your other animals.
[18] This is an easy thing in the LORD’s eyes.
He will also hand the Moabites over to you.
[19] You will strike every walled city and every fine city.
You will cut down every good tree.
You will block all springs of water.
And you will ruin every good field with stones.’”
[20] In the morning, about the time when the sacrifice is offered,
look, water came from the direction of Edom,
and the land was filled with water.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elisha is very direct with Jehoram: go to your prophets of Baal! He helps only because of the faithful King Jehoshaphat.
> Elisha has music played before he prophesies. Music can help us become quiet and listen to God. David also played the harp for Saul (1 Samuel 16:23).
> The water comes without rain. Probably it rained far away in the mountains of Edom, and the water flowed down through the dry riverbed. For the people there it was still a miracle at the right time.
> Verse 19 is hard: cutting down trees and ruining fields was a common way of making war in those days. In Deuteronomy 20:19 God had even forbidden cutting down fruit trees during a siege. Here it is announced, not necessarily approved. Today international law clearly forbids destroying what ordinary people need to live.

---

## The victory over Moab and a terrible sacrifice (Verses 21–27)

[21] All the Moabites heard that the kings had come up to fight against them.
So everyone who could put on armor gathered together, young and old,
and they stood at the border.
[22] They got up early in the morning.
The sun shone on the water,
and the Moabites saw the water across from them, as red as blood.
[23] They said,
“This is blood!
The kings have surely been destroyed, and they have struck each other.
So now, Moab, go and take the plunder!”
[24] When they came to the camp of Israel,
the Israelites got up and struck the Moabites,
so that they fled in front of them.
And they went forward into the land, attacking the Moabites.
[25] They tore down the cities.
On every good field each man threw his stone, until it was filled.
They blocked all the springs of water and cut down all the good trees.
In the end, in Kir Hareseth, only its stones were left.
But the men with slings surrounded it and attacked it.
[26] The king of Moab saw that the battle was too hard for him.
He took with him 700 men who drew the sword,
to break through to the king of Edom.
But they could not.
[27] Then he took his oldest son, who would have become king in his place,
and offered him as a burnt offering on the wall.
There was great anger against Israel.
And they went away from him and went back to their own land.

> **What does this mean?**
> The water shines red in the morning sun, perhaps because of the red earth in Edom. The Moabites think it is blood, and they run into a trap.
> In the end Mesha is shut in his fortress Kir Hareseth. In his despair he sacrifices his own son on the city wall, probably to his god Chemosh. This is cruel and horrible. In many places the Bible strongly condemns sacrificing children (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 12:31).
> “Great anger against Israel” is hard to understand. Perhaps it was horror among the Israelites themselves. Perhaps the Moabites’ fighting spirit grew so much that Israel had to withdraw. The text does not say exactly. It does not say that Chemosh really had power.
> Elisha’s announcement had come true: Israel won. But they did not get the final victory. On the Mesha Stone, Mesha describes the end of the war as Moab being set free.
> Kir Hareseth (also called Kir Heres) was the capital city of Moab. People think it lay near today’s Kerak in Jordan.
