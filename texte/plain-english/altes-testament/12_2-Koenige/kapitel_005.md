# 2 Kings – Chapter 5
### Naaman is healed

---

## A girl gives advice (Verses 1–7)

[1] Naaman was the commander of the army of the king of Syria.
He was a great man in his master’s eyes and highly respected,
because through him the LORD had given victory to Syria.
He was also a mighty and brave warrior.
But he was a leper.
A leper is a person with leprosy, a serious skin disease.
[2] The Syrians had gone out in raiding groups.
They had taken a little girl captive from the land of Israel.
She served Naaman’s wife.
[3] She said to her mistress,
“I wish that my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria!
Then he would heal him of his leprosy.”
[4] Someone went in and told his lord,
“The girl from the land of Israel said this.”
[5] The king of Syria said,
“Go now,
and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.”
Naaman set out.
He took with him 10 talents of silver (about 750 pounds),
6,000 pieces of gold, and 10 changes of clothes.
[6] He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It said,
“When this letter comes to you, look:
I have sent my servant Naaman to you,
so that you may heal him of his leprosy.”
[7] When the king of Israel had read the letter,
he tore his clothes and said,
“Am I God?
Can I kill and make alive?
This man sends someone to me,
so that I will heal him of his leprosy!
Please think about it.
See how he is looking for a quarrel with me!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Naaman is a powerful man, a successful general from Syria (Aram), Israel’s enemy. But he has a disease against which all his power is no help.
> Remarkable: the Bible says that the LORD gave victory to Syria through Naaman. God is not only the God of Israel. He guides the history of all peoples.
> In those days “leprosy” was a name for many different skin diseases, not only for what is called leprosy today. Sick people were often pushed out of the community.
> The real hero at the beginning is a little girl without a name. She was carried away from her home and has to serve as a slave. Even so, she wishes healing for her master. She has no bitterness, but compassion, and she trusts in God.
> The king of Israel (probably Jehoram) becomes afraid. He thinks the letter is a trap. He does not think of the prophet Elisha.
> The Bible says “10 talents of silver.” A talent is about 75 pounds. The “6,000 pieces of gold” probably means shekels, together about 150 pounds of gold. That was a huge fortune.

## Wash in the Jordan (Verses 8–14)

[8] Elisha the man of God heard
that the king of Israel had torn his clothes.
So he sent a message to the king:
“Why have you torn your clothes?
Let him come to me now.
Then he will know that there is a prophet in Israel.”
[9] So Naaman came with his horses and his chariots
and stopped at the door of Elisha’s house.
[10] Elisha sent a messenger out to him, who said,
“Go and wash in the Jordan seven times.
Then your body will be healthy again,
and you will be clean.”
[11] But Naaman became angry, went away, and said,
“Look, I thought,
‘He will surely come out to me and stand there
and call on the name of the LORD his God,
and wave his hand over the place
and heal the leprosy.’
[12] Aren’t Abanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus,
better than all the waters of Israel?
Couldn’t I wash in them and be clean?”
So he turned and went away in a rage.
[13] His servants came near, spoke to him, and said,
“My father, if the prophet had told you to do some great thing,
wouldn’t you have done it?
How much more, then, when he only says to you,
‘Wash, and be clean’?”
[14] Then he went down and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan,
just as the man of God had said.
And his body became like the body of a little child again,
and he was clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> Naaman arrives in grand style, with horses and chariots. But Elisha does not even come out. He only sends a messenger.
> Naaman is offended. He had imagined an impressive healing. Instead he is to wash in a small, muddy river. His pride stands in his way.
> Again it is servants, ordinary people, who bring the powerful man to his senses: “If the prophet had told you to do some great thing, wouldn’t you have done it?”
> Naaman has to become humble. He goes down, quite literally, into the Jordan. And he is healed. His skin becomes like that of a little child.
> In Nazareth Jesus reminded people of Naaman: there were many lepers in Israel, but the one who was healed was a foreigner (Luke 4:27).
> Abanah and Pharpar are rivers near Damascus.

## Naaman turns to the LORD (Verses 15–19)

[15] He went back to the man of God, he and all the people with him.
He came, stood in front of him, and said,
“Look, now I know
that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel.
So now, please accept a gift from your servant.”
[16] But Elisha said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, before whom I stand,
I will accept nothing.”
Naaman urged him to take it,
but he refused.
[17] Naaman said,
“If not, then please let your servant be given as much earth as two mules can carry.
For from now on your servant will not offer burnt offerings or sacrifices to other gods anymore,
but only to the LORD.
[18] In one thing may the LORD forgive your servant:
when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to worship there,
and he leans on my hand,
and I bow down in the house of Rimmon.
When I bow down in the house of Rimmon,
may the LORD forgive your servant in this thing.”
[19] He said to him,
“Go in peace.”
So Naaman went a short distance away from him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Naaman is not only healed in his body. He has come to know the living God. An enemy of Israel becomes a worshiper of the LORD.
> Elisha accepts no gift. God’s help cannot be bought. It is a gift.
> Naaman wants to take earth from Israel with him. In those days people thought that a god was tied to his land. On this earth Naaman wants to offer sacrifices to the LORD in his home country.
> Naaman has an honest problem: as an official, he has to go with his king into the temple of the god Rimmon (Rimmon is a name of the storm god Hadad). He asks for forgiveness ahead of time. Elisha answers wisely and kindly: “Go in peace.” He does not put a burden on him that he cannot carry.

## Gehazi’s greed (Verses 20–27)

[20] But Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said,
“Look, my master has let this Naaman the Syrian off easily.
He did not accept from his hand what he brought.
As surely as the LORD lives,
I will run after him and take something from him.”
[21] So Gehazi ran after Naaman.
When Naaman saw someone running after him,
he got down from the chariot to meet him and said,
“Is everything all right?”
[22] He said,
“Everything is all right.
My master has sent me to say,
‘Look, just now two young men of the sons of the prophets
have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim.
Please give them a talent of silver (about 75 pounds) and two changes of clothes.’”
[23] Naaman said,
“Please take two talents (about 150 pounds).”
He urged him.
He tied up two talents of silver in two bags,
with two changes of clothes,
and gave them to two of his servants.
They carried them in front of Gehazi.
[24] When he came to the hill, he took them from their hands
and stored them in the house.
Then he let the men go, and they went away.
[25] But he went in and stood in front of his master.
Elisha said to him,
“Where have you come from, Gehazi?”
He said,
“Your servant went nowhere.”
[26] He said to him,
“Didn’t my heart go with you
when the man turned from his chariot to meet you?
Is this a time to take money and to take clothes,
olive groves and vineyards,
sheep and cattle,
and male and female servants?
[27] So the leprosy of Naaman will cling to you and to your descendants forever.”
And Gehazi went out from his presence a leper, as white as snow.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gehazi is greedy. He lies to Naaman and then also to his master Elisha. He turns God’s gift into a business deal.
> In this way he damages the message: Naaman was meant to learn that God’s help is free. Gehazi destroys this witness.
> Elisha knows what happened: “Didn’t my heart go with you?” He saw it in his spirit.
> Gehazi probably wanted to buy land, animals, and servants with the money. Elisha lists all of these.
> In the end the foreigner is healed and the Israelite is a leper. What matters is not where someone comes from, but the heart.
> The Bible says “a talent of silver” and “two talents.” A talent is about 75 pounds.
> “Forever” is a hard punishment. But later Gehazi is mentioned again, telling the king about Elisha’s deeds (Chapter 8:4–5).
