# 2 Kings – Chapter 24
### Babylon comes

---

## Jehoiakim and Nebuchadnezzar (Verses 1–7)

[1] In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up.
Jehoiakim became his servant for three years.
Then he turned and rebelled against him.
[2] The LORD sent bands of raiders of the Chaldeans,
bands of the Syrians,
bands of the Moabites,
and bands of the people of Ammon against him.
He sent them against Judah to destroy it,
according to the LORD’s word that he had spoken through his servants the prophets.
[3] Yes, at the LORD’s command this came on Judah,
to remove them from his sight,
because of the sins of Manasseh, because of all that he had done,
[4] and also because of the innocent blood that he had shed.
For he had filled Jerusalem with innocent blood,
and the LORD would not forgive.
[5] The rest of the things Jehoiakim did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[6] Jehoiakim died and joined his ancestors,
and his son Jehoiachin became king in his place.
[7] The king of Egypt did not come out of his land anymore.
For the king of Babylon had taken everything that belonged to the king of Egypt,
from the Brook of Egypt to the Euphrates River.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the new world power arrives: Babylon, in the south of today’s Iraq. The Babylonians are also called “Chaldeans”. Their king Nebuchadnezzar II ruled from 605 to 562 BC. In 605 BC he defeated the Egyptians at Carchemish.
> Jehoiakim becomes a servant of Babylon, but then he rebels. That was a big mistake. The prophet Jeremiah had warned against rebelling against Babylon.
> Again the text says: the disaster is the result of guilt, especially the guilt of Manasseh. “Innocent blood” is a heavy guilt. God does not take it lightly when innocent people are killed.
> “Syria” is called “Aram” in Hebrew.

## Jehoiachin and the first exile (Verses 8–17)

[8] Jehoiachin was 18 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Nehushta the daughter of Elnathan, from Jerusalem.
[9] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his father had done.
[10] At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem,
and the city was besieged.
[11] Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to the city himself
while his servants were besieging it.
[12] Then Jehoiachin the king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon,
he, his mother, his servants, his princes, and his officers.
And the king of Babylon took him prisoner in the eighth year of his reign.
[13] He carried away from there all the treasures of the LORD’s house
and the treasures of the king’s house.
And he cut in pieces all the gold objects
that Solomon king of Israel had made in the LORD’s temple,
as the LORD had said.
[14] He carried away all Jerusalem:
all the princes and all the brave warriors, 10,000 captives,
and also all the craftsmen and the smiths.
No one was left except the poorest people of the land.
[15] He carried Jehoiachin away to Babylon,
and also the king’s mother, the king’s wives, his officers,
and the powerful men of the land.
He took them as captives from Jerusalem to Babylon.
[16] All the warriors, 7,000,
and the craftsmen and smiths, 1,000,
all strong and fit for war,
the king of Babylon brought as captives to Babylon.
[17] The king of Babylon made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin’s uncle, king in his place
and changed his name to Zedekiah.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the year 597 BC Jerusalem is taken by the Babylonians for the first time. A Babylonian chronicle reports this too and even gives the day: in March 597 BC.
> Jehoiachin surrenders. So at first the city is not destroyed. But the upper class, the soldiers, and the craftsmen are taken away to Babylon. Among them was the prophet Ezekiel (Ezekiel 1:1–3).
> With this the word comes true that Isaiah had spoken to King Hezekiah (chapter 20:17).
> The numbers in verses 14 and 16 are not quite the same. Perhaps 10,000 is the total number. Jeremiah 52:28 gives a smaller number (3,023).
> Only the poorest stay in the land. The Babylonians took the educated and skilled people with them, so that Judah could not rise up again.
> Zedekiah too receives a new name from the foreign king.

## Zedekiah, the last king of Judah (Verses 18–20)

[18] Zedekiah was 21 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Hamutal the daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah.
[19] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
just as Jehoiakim had done.
[20] For because of the LORD’s anger this happened to Jerusalem and Judah,
until he had thrown them out from his presence.
And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zedekiah is a son of Josiah, a brother of Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim.
> He was a weak king, torn back and forth. He often secretly asked the prophet Jeremiah for advice, but he did not have the courage to follow it (Jeremiah 37–38). Jeremiah advised him to submit to Babylon to save the city. But Zedekiah rebelled.
> Zedekiah ruled from about 597 to 587 BC.
