# 2 Kings – Chapter 16
### King Ahaz and the foreign altar

---

## Ahaz, an evil king in Judah (Verses 1–4)

[1] In the seventeenth year of Pekah the son of Remaliah,
Ahaz the son of Jotham, king of Judah, began to rule.
[2] Ahaz was 20 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 16 years in Jerusalem.
He did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God,
as his ancestor David had done.
[3] But he walked in the way of the kings of Israel.
He even made his son pass through the fire,
following the disgusting practices of the nations
whom the LORD had driven out from before the people of Israel.
[4] He offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places,
on the hills, and under every green tree.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahaz is one of the worst kings of Judah.
> “Made his son pass through the fire” very probably means: he burned his child as a sacrifice to a foreign god. That is a horrible crime. God’s law clearly forbids it (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 18:10). God does not want human sacrifices. Already with Abraham and Isaac he showed: no child is to be sacrificed (Genesis 22).
> Ahaz ruled from about 735 to 715 BC.

---

## Ahaz calls the Assyrians for help (Verses 5–9)

[5] Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel,
came up to Jerusalem to make war.
They laid siege to Ahaz, but they could not defeat him.
[6] At that time Rezin king of Syria won Elath back for Syria
and drove the Jews out of Elath.
And the Syrians came to Elath, and they live there to this day.
[7] So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria with this message:
“I am your servant and your son.
Come up and save me from the power of the king of Syria
and from the power of the king of Israel,
who are rising up against me.”
[8] Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the LORD’s house
and in the treasuries of the king’s house,
and he sent it as a gift to the king of Assyria.
[9] The king of Assyria listened to him.
The king of Assyria went up against Damascus and captured it.
He carried its people away captive to Kir,
and he killed Rezin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Syria and Israel want to force Judah to fight with them against Assyria. They lay siege to Jerusalem.
> In this time of need the prophet Isaiah said to Ahaz: “Be careful, and stay calm! Don’t be afraid” (Isaiah 7:4). And he gave him the famous sign: “Look, the virgin will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and she will call him Immanuel” (Isaiah 7:14). Immanuel means: God with us. Later, Christians apply this word to Jesus (Matthew 1:23).
> But Ahaz does not trust in God. Instead he buys the help of the Assyrians. He calls himself their “servant and son”. With this he makes Judah dependent on cruel Assyria.
> In 732 BC the Assyrians conquer Damascus. The kingdom of the Arameans comes to an end.
> In verse 6 some Hebrew manuscripts have “Edom” instead of “Aram” (Syria). In Hebrew the two words look almost the same. Many scholars think that the Edomites were meant originally.

## The new altar (Verses 10–18)

[10] King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria.
There he saw the altar that was in Damascus.
And King Ahaz sent Urijah the priest a drawing of the altar
and plans for building it.
[11] Urijah the priest built an altar.
He made it exactly according to everything King Ahaz had sent from Damascus.
Urijah the priest finished it before King Ahaz came back from Damascus.
[12] When the king came back from Damascus, the king saw the altar.
The king went up to the altar and offered sacrifices on it.
[13] He burned his burnt offering and his grain offering,
poured out his drink offering,
and sprinkled the blood of his peace offerings on the altar.
[14] He moved the bronze altar, which stood before the LORD,
away from the front of the house,
from between his altar and the LORD’s house,
and put it on the north side of his own altar.
[15] King Ahaz commanded Urijah the priest,
“On the great altar burn
the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering,
the king’s burnt offering and his grain offering,
with the burnt offering of all the people of the land,
their grain offering, and their drink offerings.
And sprinkle on it all the blood of the burnt offering and all the blood of the sacrifice.
But I will use the bronze altar to ask God for guidance.”
[16] Urijah the priest did everything just as King Ahaz commanded.
[17] King Ahaz cut off the side panels of the stands
and took the basins off them.
He took down the Sea from the bronze oxen that were under it
and put it on a stone pavement.
The Sea was a very large bronze basin for water.
[18] He removed from the LORD’s house the covered walkway for the Sabbath
that they had built in the house,
and the king’s outer entrance,
because of the king of Assyria.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Damascus Ahaz has to bow down to the Assyrian king. There he sees an altar and wants to have a copy of it built in Jerusalem.
> Solomon’s altar is pushed aside. The new, foreign altar becomes the main altar. That shows: Ahaz shapes the worship of God to please the powerful. Instead of putting God in the center, he puts the great power in the center.
> Urijah the priest goes along with everything without saying a word against it.
> Ahaz also takes bronze off the temple objects, probably to pay the tribute to Assyria.
> No one knows for sure what the “covered walkway for the Sabbath” was. Ahaz probably removed royal signs at the temple so as not to anger the Assyrian king.

## Ahaz dies (Verses 19–20)

[19] The rest of the things Ahaz did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[20] Ahaz died and joined his ancestors.
He was buried with his ancestors in David’s city.
And his son Hezekiah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> One of the worst kings is followed by one of the best: Hezekiah. A son does not have to go the way of his father.
> A seal impression with the name “Ahaz, son of Jotham, king of Judah” is known. It comes from the antiquities trade, so where it really comes from is not certain.
