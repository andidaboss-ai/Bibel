# 2 Kings – Chapter 14
### Amaziah of Judah and Jeroboam II of Israel

---

## Amaziah, king of Judah (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the second year of Joash the son of Joahaz, king of Israel,
Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah began to rule.
[2] He was 25 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 29 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jehoaddin, from Jerusalem.
[3] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes,
but not like his ancestor David.
He did everything just as his father Joash had done.
[4] But the high places were not taken away.
The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places.
[5] As soon as the kingdom was firmly in his hand,
he killed his servants who had killed his father the king.
[6] But he did not put the children of the murderers to death,
as it is written in the book of the law of Moses,
where the LORD commanded,
“The fathers shall not be put to death for the children,
and the children shall not be put to death for the fathers.
But every man shall die for his own sin.”
[7] He killed 10,000 Edomites in the Valley of Salt.
He captured Sela in war
and called it Joktheel, and that is its name to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amaziah is a fairly good king, but not as faithful as David.
> Verse 6 is remarkable: in those days it was common to kill the family of a criminal too, so that no one would take revenge. Amaziah keeps God’s law (Deuteronomy 24:16): each person is responsible only for his own guilt. That was a big step forward for justice. Today too this is a basic rule of our law: a family is not punished for the crime of one of its members.
> The Valley of Salt probably lies south of the Dead Sea. Sela means “rock”. It is not certain exactly where it was.

## The thistle and the cedar (Verses 8–14)

[8] Then Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz, the son of Jehu, king of Israel.
He said,
“Come, let’s look each other in the face.”
[9] Jehoash the king of Israel sent a message to Amaziah king of Judah:
“The thistle in Lebanon sent a message to the cedar in Lebanon:
‘Give your daughter to my son as his wife.’
Then a wild animal in Lebanon passed by
and trampled the thistle.
[10] You have indeed struck Edom,
and your heart has become proud.
Enjoy your glory and stay at home.
Why should you go looking for trouble,
so that you fall, you and Judah with you?”
[11] But Amaziah would not listen.
So Jehoash king of Israel went up.
He and Amaziah king of Judah looked each other in the face
at Beth Shemesh, which belongs to Judah.
[12] Judah was defeated by Israel,
and every man fled to his tent.
[13] Jehoash king of Israel captured Amaziah king of Judah,
the son of Jehoash, the son of Ahaziah, at Beth Shemesh.
Then he came to Jerusalem
and broke down the wall of Jerusalem
from the Gate of Ephraim to the Corner Gate, about 600 feet.
[14] He took all the gold and silver
and all the objects that were found in the LORD’s house
and in the treasuries of the king’s house,
and hostages too,
and went back to Samaria.

> **What does this mean?**
> After his victory over Edom, Amaziah has become overconfident. He challenges the king of Israel to battle. “Let’s look each other in the face” means: let’s fight.
> Jehoash answers with a fable: a small thistle wants to be equal with the great cedar, and it gets trampled. He means: you think too highly of yourself.
> “Pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). Amaziah does not listen and loses everything.
> For the first time Jerusalem is conquered by a king from Israel. A war between brothers.
> The Bible says “400 cubits.” A cubit is about 18 inches.

## The death of Jehoash and the end of Amaziah (Verses 15–22)

[15] The rest of the things Jehoash did, and his power,
and how he fought against Amaziah king of Judah,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[16] Jehoash died and joined his ancestors.
He was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel.
And his son Jeroboam became king in his place.
[17] Amaziah the son of Joash, king of Judah,
lived for 15 years after the death of Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.
[18] The rest of the things Amaziah did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[19] People made a plot against him in Jerusalem.
He fled to Lachish.
But they sent men after him to Lachish and killed him there.
[20] They brought him back on horses,
and he was buried in Jerusalem with his ancestors in David’s city.
[21] All the people of Judah took Azariah, who was 16 years old,
and made him king in the place of his father Amaziah.
[22] He built up Elath and gave it back to Judah.
After that the king died and joined his ancestors.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amaziah is murdered too, just like his father Joash. Lachish was a large fortress city in the southwest of Judah.
> Azariah becomes king at the age of 16. He is also called Uzziah (2 Kings 15:13). Elath lies on the Red Sea, on the Gulf of Aqaba. With this, Judah has a harbor again.
> “The king died and joined his ancestors”: this probably means Amaziah. Azariah built up Elath after his father’s death.
> Amaziah ruled from about 796 to 767 BC.

## Jeroboam II, king of Israel (Verses 23–29)

[23] In the fifteenth year of Amaziah the son of Joash, king of Judah,
Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel became king in Samaria, for 41 years.
[24] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He did not turn away from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which that Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
[25] He won back the border of Israel,
from the entrance of Hamath to the sea of the Arabah,
just as the LORD, the God of Israel, had said
through his servant Jonah the son of Amittai, the prophet, who was from Gath Hepher.
[26] For the LORD saw the suffering of Israel,
that it was very bitter for everyone, slave and free,
and that there was no one to help Israel.
[27] The LORD had not said that he would wipe out the name of Israel from under the sky.
So he saved them through Jeroboam the son of Joash.
[28] The rest of the things Jeroboam did,
all that he did, and his power, how he fought,
and how he won back for Israel Damascus and Hamath, which had belonged to Judah,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[29] Jeroboam died and joined his ancestors, the kings of Israel.
And his son Zechariah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeroboam II is one of the most successful kings of the northern kingdom. He rules for a very long time and wins back a lot of land. Israel is great again, almost as in the time of Solomon.
> Even so, the Bible’s judgment is short: “He did what was evil.” Success and wealth are no sign that someone is faithful to God.
> The prophet Jonah is mentioned here. He is the same Jonah that the book of Jonah tells about. Gath Hepher lay in Galilee, near the later town of Nazareth.
> The prophets Amos and Hosea also worked in this time. They sharply criticized that the rich lived in luxury while the poor were oppressed (Amos 6:4–6).
> Verses 26–27 show God’s mercy: he saw the need and helped, even though the king was not faithful to him.
> “The sea of the Arabah” is the Dead Sea. “The entrance of Hamath” is also called Lebo Hamath. It means the far north.
> Verse 28 is hard to understand: Damascus and Hamath had once belonged to the kingdom of David and Solomon. That is probably why it says “which had belonged to Judah” here.
> Jeroboam II ruled from about 793 to 753 BC. The years overlap with those of his father, because he probably ruled together with him for some years.
