# 2 Kings – Chapter 9
### Jehu becomes king

---

## Jehu is anointed (Verses 1–13)

[1] Elisha the prophet called one of the sons of the prophets and said to him,
“Put your belt around your waist,
take this little bottle of oil in your hand,
and go to Ramoth Gilead.
[2] When you get there,
find Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat, the son of Nimshi.
Go in, have him get up from among his brothers,
and take him into an inner room.
[3] Then take the bottle of oil,
pour it on his head,
and say,
‘The LORD says,
“I have anointed you king over Israel.”’
Then open the door, run away, and don’t wait.”
[4] So the young man, the young prophet, went to Ramoth Gilead.
[5] When he came, look, the commanders of the army were sitting together.
He said,
“I have a message for you, commander.”
Jehu asked,
“For which one of us?”
He said,
“For you, commander.”
[6] So Jehu got up and went into the house.
The young man poured the oil on his head and said to him,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘I have anointed you king over the LORD’s people, over Israel.
[7] You must strike the family of your master Ahab.
Then I will take revenge on Jezebel for the blood of my servants the prophets
and for the blood of all the LORD’s servants.
[8] The whole family of Ahab will die.
I will cut off from Ahab everyone who urinates against a wall in Israel,
the one who is shut up and the one who is free.
[9] I will make Ahab’s family like the family of Jeroboam, the son of Nebat,
and like the family of Baasha, the son of Ahijah.
[10] The dogs will eat Jezebel on the piece of ground at Jezreel,
and there will be no one to bury her.’”
Then he opened the door and ran away.
[11] Jehu came out to the servants of his lord.
One of them said to him,
“Is everything all right? Why did this madman come to you?”
He said to them,
“You know the man and how he talks.”
[12] They said,
“That’s a lie. Tell us now.”
He said,
“He said to me,
‘The LORD says, I have anointed you king over Israel.’”
[13] Then they hurried.
Each man took his cloak and put it under him on the top of the stairs.
They blew the trumpet and said,
“Jehu is king!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now what God had told Elijah at Horeb comes true: Jehu is to become king (1 Kings 19:16).
> Jehu is an army commander. A young prophet anoints him in secret and then runs away at once, because this is life-threatening: it is a revolt against the king.
> The soldiers call the prophet a “madman.” Prophets often seemed strange to other people.
> The officers lay their cloaks under Jehu’s feet. That is a way of honoring a king. In a similar way, people spread their clothes on the road when Jesus rode into Jerusalem (Matthew 21:8).
> God’s command to Jehu is very harsh. The Bible tells it because Ahab and Jezebel had killed many innocent people, among them prophets and Naboth. But you need to know: later Jehu goes far beyond the command. A later prophet, Hosea, condemns the blood that Jehu shed at Jezreel (Hosea 1:4). God’s judgment is no permission for human cruelty.

## Jehu kills Joram (Verses 14–26)

[14] So Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat, the son of Nimshi, plotted against Joram.
(Joram, he and all Israel, had been defending Ramoth Gilead against Hazael king of Syria.
[15] But King Joram had gone back to Jezreel to be healed
from the wounds that the Syrians had given him when he fought against Hazael king of Syria.)
Jehu said,
“If this is what you think,
then let no one escape from the city
to go and tell it in Jezreel.”
[16] So Jehu got into a chariot and went to Jezreel,
because Joram was lying there.
Ahaziah king of Judah had also come down to see Joram.
[17] The watchman was standing on the tower in Jezreel.
He saw Jehu’s troop coming and said,
“I see a troop.”
Joram said,
“Take a horseman and send him to meet them.
Let him ask, ‘Is it peace?’”
[18] So a man rode on horseback to meet him and said,
“The king says, ‘Is it peace?’”
Jehu said,
“What do you have to do with peace? Fall in behind me!”
The watchman said,
“The messenger came to them, but he isn’t coming back.”
[19] Then he sent out a second horseman.
He came to them and said,
“The king says, ‘Is it peace?’”
Jehu answered,
“What do you have to do with peace? Fall in behind me!”
[20] The watchman said,
“He came to them and isn’t coming back.
And the driving is like the driving of Jehu the son of Nimshi,
because he drives like a madman.”
[21] Joram said,
“Get ready!”
And they got his chariot ready.
Then Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah went out,
each in his chariot.
They went out to meet Jehu
and found him on the land of Naboth the Jezreelite.
[22] When Joram saw Jehu, he said,
“Is it peace, Jehu?”
He answered,
“What peace can there be,
as long as the prostitution of your mother Jezebel and her witchcraft are everywhere?”
[23] Then Joram turned his chariot around and fled.
He said to Ahaziah,
“This is treason, Ahaziah!”
[24] Jehu drew his bow with all his strength
and struck Joram between his arms.
The arrow went out through his heart,
and he sank down in his chariot.
[25] Then Jehu said to Bidkar his officer,
“Pick him up and throw him onto the field of Naboth the Jezreelite.
Remember how you and I were riding together behind his father Ahab
when the LORD spoke this heavy word against him:
[26] ‘Surely I saw the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons yesterday,’
says the LORD,
‘and I will pay you back on this piece of ground,’
says the LORD.
So now pick him up and throw him onto the piece of ground, just as the LORD said.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Between his arms” probably means: in the back, between the shoulder blades, when Joram turned around to flee.
> Jehu is known for his wild driving. The watchman recognizes him by it from far away.
> Three times the question is asked: “Is it peace?” But there is no peace.
> In the Bible, “prostitution” is often a picture for worshiping idols: being unfaithful to God, like being unfaithful in marriage. Jezebel had brought the worship of Baal to Israel.
> Joram dies on Naboth’s field, of all places. Here the circle closes: Ahab and Jezebel had had Naboth murdered there (1 Kings 21). Now the payback comes at this place.
> Here we also learn that Naboth’s sons were murdered with him (verse 26).

## Ahaziah of Judah dies (Verses 27–29)

[27] When Ahaziah the king of Judah saw this,
he fled along the road to the garden house.
Jehu chased him and said,
“Strike him too, in the chariot!”
They struck him at the slope going up to Gur, which is near Ibleam.
He fled to Megiddo and died there.
[28] His servants carried him in a chariot to Jerusalem.
They buried him in his tomb with his ancestors in David’s city.
[29] Ahaziah had become king over Judah in the eleventh year of Joram the son of Ahab.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king of Judah also loses his life, because he was connected with Ahab’s house.
> Interesting: in 1993 and 1994 an inscription was found at Tel Dan in the north of Israel. In it a king of Damascus, probably Hazael, boasts that he killed Joram the king of Israel and Ahaziah of the “house of David.” The Bible says it was Jehu. Perhaps Hazael saw himself as the winner because Jehu worked together with him, or he was simply boasting. This inscription is famous because it mentions the “house of David”. It is one of the oldest pieces of evidence outside the Bible for David’s royal family.
> Verse 29 gives a different year than 2 Kings 8:25 (the eleventh instead of the twelfth year). The years were probably counted in different ways.

## The end of Jezebel (Verses 30–37)

[30] When Jehu came to Jezreel, Jezebel heard about it.
She painted her eyes with makeup, made her hair beautiful,
and looked out of the window.
[31] As Jehu came in at the gate, she said,
“Do you come in peace, Zimri, you murderer of your master?”
[32] He lifted up his face to the window and said,
“Who is on my side? Who?”
Two or three eunuchs looked down at him.
Eunuchs were servants at the royal court who had been made unable to father children.
[33] He said,
“Throw her down!”
So they threw her down.
Some of her blood splashed on the wall and on the horses.
Then he trampled her under his horses’ feet.
[34] When he had gone in, he ate and drank.
Then he said,
“Now take care of this cursed woman and bury her,
because she is a king’s daughter.”
[35] They went to bury her,
but they found nothing more of her than the skull, the feet, and the palms of her hands.
[36] So they came back and told him.
He said,
“This is the LORD’s word,
which he spoke through his servant Elijah the Tishbite:
‘The dogs will eat the flesh of Jezebel on the piece of ground at Jezreel.
[37] And the body of Jezebel will be like dung lying on the field in the land of Jezreel,
so that no one will be able to say, “This is Jezebel.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jezebel knows that her end is coming. But she dies proud. She puts on makeup and faces Jehu as a queen. She calls him “Zimri”: about 45 years earlier, Zimri had murdered his king and then ruled for only seven days (1 Kings 16:9–18). Jezebel wants to say: the same will happen to you.
> The eunuchs, officials of the royal court, quickly change sides and throw their mistress out of the window.
> Jehu eats and drinks as if nothing had happened. That shows how hard he is.
> So Elijah’s word comes true (1 Kings 21:23). Jezebel had had many of God’s prophets and Naboth killed. The Bible shows: even the powerful do not escape God’s judgment.
> Even so, this scene is cruel and not an example to follow. The Bible tells it to show how violence and the worship of idols bring their own punishment, not to approve of them.
> The name “Jezebel” later became a symbol for leading people to worship idols (Revelation 2:20). Sadly, it has also been used to put women down. That is wrong: Jezebel is not condemned because she was a woman, but because of what she did.
