# 2 Kings – Chapter 17
### The end of the northern kingdom of Israel

---

## Hoshea, the last king of Israel (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah,
Hoshea the son of Elah became king in Samaria over Israel, for nine years.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
but not like the kings of Israel who were before him.
[3] Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against him.
Hoshea became his servant and paid him tribute.
Tribute is money or goods that a weaker king must pay to a stronger king.
[4] But the king of Assyria discovered a plot by Hoshea.
For Hoshea had sent messengers to So king of Egypt,
and he no longer paid tribute to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year.
So the king of Assyria seized him
and put him in chains in prison.
[5] Then the king of Assyria came up through the whole land.
He went up to Samaria
and laid siege to it for three years.
[6] In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria captured Samaria
and carried Israel away to Assyria.
He settled them in Halah, on the Habor, the river of Gozan,
and in the cities of the Medes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hoshea tries to free himself from Assyria with the help of Egypt. But Egypt does not help. Assyria attacks.
> After a siege of three years Samaria falls, about 722 before Christ. The northern kingdom of Israel comes to an end. It had lasted about 200 years.
> The Assyrian king Sargon II, who came after Shalmaneser, boasts in his inscriptions that he carried away more than 27,000 people from Samaria.
> Halah, Habor, and Gozan lay in the north of Mesopotamia, in the area of today’s border between Syria and Turkey. The Medes lived in today’s Iran.
> It is not certain who “So king of Egypt” was. At that time Egypt had broken apart into several small kingdoms.
> Hoshea ruled from about 732 to 722 before Christ.

## Why did this happen? (Verses 7–23)

[7] This happened because the people of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God,
who had brought them up out of the land of Egypt,
out from under the power of Pharaoh king of Egypt.
They had worshiped other gods.
[8] They had lived by the customs of the nations
whom the LORD had driven out from before the people of Israel,
and by the customs that the kings of Israel had made.
[9] The people of Israel secretly did things against the LORD their God that were not right.
They built high places for themselves in all their cities,
from the watchtower to the walled city.
[10] They set up stone pillars and Asherah poles for themselves
on every high hill and under every green tree.
[11] There they burned incense on all the high places,
as the nations had done whom the LORD carried away before them.
And they did wicked things to make the LORD angry.
[12] They served idols,
about which the LORD had said to them,
“You shall not do this.”
[13] Yet the LORD had warned Israel and Judah
through every prophet and every seer:
“Turn from your evil ways,
and keep my commandments and my laws,
following the whole law that I commanded your fathers
and that I sent to you through my servants the prophets.”
[14] But they would not listen.
They made their necks stiff, like the necks of their fathers,
who did not believe in the LORD their God.
[15] They rejected his laws and his covenant that he had made with their fathers,
and his warnings that he had given them.
They followed worthless things and became worthless themselves.
They followed the nations around them,
although the LORD had commanded them not to do as they did.
[16] They left all the commandments of the LORD their God.
They made metal images for themselves, two calves,
and they made an Asherah pole.
They worshiped all the stars of the sky
and served Baal.
[17] They made their sons and their daughters pass through the fire.
They practiced fortune-telling and magic.
And they sold themselves to do what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
to make him angry.
[18] So the LORD was very angry with Israel
and removed them from his sight.
No one was left except the tribe of Judah alone.
[19] Judah also did not keep the commandments of the LORD their God,
but lived by the customs that Israel had made.
[20] The LORD rejected all the descendants of Israel.
He made them suffer and handed them over to raiders,
until he had thrown them out of his sight.
[21] For he had torn Israel away from David’s house.
And they made Jeroboam the son of Nebat king.
And Jeroboam drove Israel away from following the LORD
and made them commit a great sin.
[22] The people of Israel lived in all the sins that Jeroboam had committed.
They did not turn away from them,
[23] until the LORD removed Israel from his sight,
as he had said through all his servants the prophets.
So Israel was carried away from its own land to Assyria, and it is still there to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the books of Kings. The text stops and asks: why did the northern kingdom come to an end?
> The answer is: not because God was too weak. Not because Assyria was so strong. But because the people left God again and again, even though God warned them through many prophets: through Elijah, Elisha, Amos, Hosea, and many others.
> God was patient for a long time, about 200 years. Again and again he called: “Turn from your evil ways!” But they would not listen.
> “They followed worthless things and became worthless themselves”: whoever runs after empty things becomes empty himself. You become like what you worship.
> Verse 19 is already a warning to Judah: you too are in danger.
> This explanation is a way of understanding history through faith. It wants to call the people of that time to turn back to God. It does not say that every misfortune in a person’s life is a punishment from God. Jesus clearly rejected that idea (John 9:2–3; Luke 13:1–5).
> What became of the ten tribes? Many were scattered and became part of other peoples. Some fled to Judah and lived on there. The question of the “lost ten tribes” has kept people busy until today.

## Foreign peoples in Samaria (Verses 24–33)

[24] The king of Assyria brought people from Babylon, from Cuthah, from Avva, from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim.
He settled them in the cities of Samaria in place of the people of Israel.
They took Samaria for their own and lived in its cities.
[25] When they first began to live there, they did not fear the LORD.
So the LORD sent lions among them,
which killed some of them.
[26] So people said to the king of Assyria,
“The nations that you carried away and settled in the cities of Samaria
do not know the law of the god of the land.
That is why he has sent lions among them.
And look, the lions are killing them,
because they do not know the law of the god of the land.”
[27] Then the king of Assyria commanded,
“Take there one of the priests whom you brought from there.
Let him go and live there
and teach them the law of the god of the land.”
[28] So one of the priests whom they had carried away from Samaria came
and lived in Bethel.
And he taught them how they should fear the LORD.
[29] But every nation made gods of its own.
They put them in the houses of the high places that the Samaritans had made,
every nation in the cities where it lived.
[30] The men of Babylon made Succoth Benoth.
The men of Cuth made Nergal.
The men of Hamath made Ashima.
[31] And the Avvites made Nibhaz and Tartak.
And the Sepharvites burned their children in the fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech,
the gods of Sepharvaim.
[32] So they feared the LORD.
But they also made priests of the high places from among their own people,
who offered sacrifices for them in the houses of the high places.
[33] They feared the LORD,
and they also served their own gods,
following the customs of the nations from which they had been carried away.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Assyrians had a cruel method: they carried away conquered peoples and settled foreign peoples in their land. In this way no revolts were supposed to start.
> The new settlers bring their own gods with them. They worship the LORD in addition, as one god among many. A mixed religion comes about.
> The lions became more numerous because the land was ruined and empty after the war. The people understood this as a sign from the god of the land.
> In the Jewish view, the Samaritans later came from this population. But the Samaritans see themselves as descendants of the Israelites who stayed in the land, especially of the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh. They still exist today, a small community near Nablus and in Holon in Israel.
> Jews and Samaritans were enemies for a long time. Jesus broke through this hostility: he told the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37) and spoke with the Samaritan woman at the well (John 4).
> The names of the foreign gods are hardly known otherwise. Nergal was a god of the underworld from Mesopotamia.
> The Sepharvites also sacrificed children. The Bible names this as the worst example of worshiping idols.

## To this day (Verses 34–41)

[34] To this day they do what they did before.
They do not fear the LORD,
and they do not follow the laws or the rules,
the law or the commandment
that the LORD commanded the children of Jacob,
whom he named Israel.
[35] With them the LORD had made a covenant and commanded them,
“You shall not fear other gods.
You shall not bow down to them,
serve them, or sacrifice to them.
[36] But the LORD, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt with great power and with an outstretched arm,
him you shall fear,
to him you shall bow down,
and to him you shall sacrifice.
[37] The laws and the rules,
the law and the commandment that he wrote for you,
you shall carefully keep forever.
You shall not fear other gods.
[38] You shall not forget the covenant that I have made with you.
You shall not fear other gods.
[39] But you shall fear the LORD your God.
Then he will save you from the power of all your enemies.”
[40] But they did not listen.
They did what they had done before.
[41] So these nations feared the LORD,
and they also served their carved images.
Their children did the same, and so did their children’s children.
They do as their fathers did, to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> The text repeats the most important commandment: “You shall not fear other gods.” It appears three times here. It is the first of the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:3).
> Verse 33 says: “They feared the LORD.” Verse 34 says: “They do not fear the LORD.” This is not a contradiction. It means: they honored him a little, but not him alone and not from the heart. For the Bible, half-hearted worship of God is not true fear of God.
> In the Bible, “fearing God” does not mean being afraid of God. It means: taking God seriously, honoring him, and trusting him.
