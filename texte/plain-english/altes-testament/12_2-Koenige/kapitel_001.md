# 2 Kings – Chapter 1
### King Ahaziah and the prophet Elijah

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of 2 Kings?

The book of 2 Kings continues the book of 1 Kings. In the beginning, both were one single book. It tells about 300 years of history, from about 850 to 560 BC.
At the beginning are the prophets Elijah and Elisha. Elijah is taken up into heaven. Elisha becomes the one who follows him, and he does many miracles. He helps poor people, sick people, and even enemies.
Then the book goes on telling about the two kingdoms: Israel in the north and Judah in the south. In the north many kings follow one after another who are not faithful to God. In the year 722 BC the Assyrians conquer the northern kingdom. Many people are carried away. The northern kingdom comes to an end.
Judah in the south lasts about 135 years longer. There are good kings there, like Hezekiah and Josiah, who want to lead the people back to God. But in the end the Babylonians conquer Jerusalem. In the year 587 BC the temple is destroyed, and many people are carried away to Babylon.
The book asks: why did this happen? The answer is: because the people and their kings left God again and again, even though the prophets warned them.
But the book does not end without hope: in the last section King Jehoiachin is set free from prison in Babylon. That is a small sign: God has not forgotten David’s family.

## Ahaziah asks a foreign god (Verses 1–8)

[1] After the death of Ahab, Moab rebelled against Israel.
[2] Ahaziah fell down through the lattice in his upper room in Samaria,
and he was sick.
A lattice is a screen made of crossed strips of wood.
So he sent messengers and said to them,
“Go and ask Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron,
whether I will get well from this sickness.”
[3] But the LORD’s angel said to Elijah the Tishbite,
“Get up, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria,
and tell them,
‘Is there no God in Israel?
Is that why you go to ask Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron?
[4] So now the LORD says,
“You will not come down from the bed you have gone up on.
You will surely die.”’”
Then Elijah went away.
[5] The messengers came back to Ahaziah.
He said to them,
“Why have you come back?”
[6] They said to him,
“A man came up to meet us and said to us,
‘Go, go back to the king who sent you, and tell him,
“The LORD says,
‘Is there no God in Israel?
Is that why you send people to ask Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron?
So you will not come down from the bed you have gone up on.
You will surely die.’”’”
[7] He said to them,
“What kind of man was he who came up to meet you and told you these words?”
[8] They answered him,
“He was a hairy man,
and he wore a leather belt around his waist.”
He said,
“It is Elijah the Tishbite.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahaziah is the son of Ahab and Jezebel. In his house he falls through a wooden lattice, probably at a window or on the roof, and he is badly hurt.
> Instead of asking the God of Israel, he sends messengers to a god of the Philistines in Ekron. “Baal Zebub” means “lord of the flies.” This is probably a mocking name. The real name was perhaps “Baal Zebul,” “Baal, the prince.” In the New Testament, “Beelzebul” is a name for the chief of the demons (Mark 3:22).
> Elijah’s question is the heart of the story: “Is there no God in Israel?” Ahaziah looks for help everywhere, only not from God.
> “A hairy man”: this can also mean that he wore a coat made of animal hair or skin. Later John the Baptist is described in a similar way, with camel’s hair and a leather belt (Mark 1:6). Many people saw John as a new Elijah (Malachi 4:5; Matthew 11:14).
> Moab’s rebellion is told further in Chapter 3.
> In some Bibles the name of this king is spelled a little differently.

## Fire from the sky (Verses 9–16)

[9] Then the king sent a captain with his 50 men to Elijah.
The captain went up to him.
Elijah was sitting on the top of the hill.
The captain said to him,
“Man of God, the king has said, ‘Come down!’”
[10] Elijah answered the captain,
“If I am a man of God,
then let fire come down from the sky
and burn up you and your 50 men!”
Then fire came down from the sky
and burned up the captain and his 50 men.
[11] Again the king sent another captain with his 50 men to Elijah.
The captain said to him,
“Man of God, the king has said, ‘Come down quickly!’”
[12] Elijah answered them,
“If I am a man of God,
then let fire come down from the sky
and burn up you and your 50 men!”
Then God’s fire came down from the sky
and burned up the captain and his 50 men.
[13] Again the king sent a third captain with his 50 men.
The third captain went up.
He came and fell on his knees in front of Elijah.
He begged him and said to him,
“Man of God, please let my life and the life of these 50 men, your servants,
be precious in your eyes.
[14] Look, fire came down from the sky
and burned up the first two captains with their 50 men each.
But now let my life be precious in your eyes.”
[15] The LORD’s angel said to Elijah,
“Go down with him. Don’t be afraid of him.”
So Elijah got up and went down with him to the king.
[16] Elijah said to the king,
“The LORD says,
‘You have sent messengers to ask Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron.
Is there no God in Israel whose word you can ask?
So you will not come down from the bed you have gone up on.
You will surely die.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The king wants to have Elijah brought to him by force. The first two captains act very proudly: they give orders to the man of God in the name of the king. The question is: who has more power, the king or God?
> The third captain is different. He kneels down and humbly begs for his life. He is spared. Humility is protected.
> This story is hard. More than 100 soldiers die who were only following orders. The Bible wants to show how serious it is to stand against God. But it is not an example for us to follow: later, when the disciples asked Jesus whether they should call down fire from the sky like Elijah on a village that had not welcomed them, Jesus told them they were wrong (Luke 9:54–55).
> In the end Elijah goes to the king without fear and tells him God’s word to his face.

## Ahaziah’s death (Verses 17–18)

[17] So Ahaziah died, just as the LORD had said through Elijah.
Ahaziah had no son.
So Jehoram became king in his place.
This was in the second year of Jehoram, the son of Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah.
[18] The rest of Ahaziah’s story and everything he did
are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ahaziah had no son. That is why his brother Jehoram became king.
> This can be confusing: at the same time there were two kings named Jehoram (also called Joram): one in Israel (Ahab’s son) and one in Judah (Jehoshaphat’s son).
> The dates in the books of Kings are sometimes hard to fit together. 2 Kings 3:1 gives a different date. Probably some kings ruled together with their father for a while, and the years were counted in different ways.
