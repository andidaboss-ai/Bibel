# 2 Kings – Chapter 4
### Elisha helps in times of need

---

## The widow’s oil (Verses 1–7)

[1] A woman, one of the wives of the sons of the prophets, cried out to Elisha,
“Your servant, my husband, is dead.
You know that your servant feared the LORD.
Now the man he owed money to has come
to take my two children as his slaves.”
[2] Elisha said to her,
“What should I do for you?
Tell me, what do you have in the house?”
She said,
“Your servant has nothing in the house except a pot of oil.”
[3] Then he said,
“Go, borrow empty containers from all your neighbors.
Don’t borrow just a few containers.
[4] Then go in
and shut the door behind you and your sons.
Pour oil into all those containers,
and set aside the ones that are full.”
[5] So she went away from him
and shut the door behind herself and her sons.
They brought the containers to her, and she poured oil.
[6] When the containers were full, she said to her son,
“Bring me another container.”
He said to her,
“There isn’t another container.”
Then the oil stopped flowing.
[7] She came and told the man of God.
He said,
“Go, sell the oil and pay your debt.
And you and your sons can live on the rest.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A widow is in great need. Her husband is dead, and she has debts. In those days a person who was owed money could take the children as slaves for the debt, until the debt was worked off.
> Elisha asks: “What do you have in the house?” God begins with the little that is already there.
> The oil flows as long as there are containers. The woman could have received even more if she had had more containers. God’s gifts are greater than we often expect.
> God sees the poor and the weak. In the Bible, widows and orphans are under God’s special protection (Exodus 22:22–24).
> Anyone today whose debts have grown too big for them can turn to a free debt counseling service. In many countries, nonprofit debt counseling services help for free.

## The woman from Shunem (Verses 8–17)

[8] One day Elisha went to Shunem.
An important woman lived there.
She urged him to eat a meal.
So whenever he passed by, he stopped there to eat a meal.
[9] She said to her husband,
“Look, I can see that this man who keeps passing by us
is a holy man of God.
[10] Please, let’s make a little room on the roof.
Let’s put a bed, a table, a chair, and a lampstand there for him.
When he comes to us, he can stay there.”
[11] One day he came there.
He went into the room and lay down there.
[12] He said to Gehazi his servant,
“Call this Shunammite.”
He called her, and she stood in front of him.
[13] Elisha said to Gehazi,
“Say to her now,
‘Look, you have taken all this trouble to care for us.
What can be done for you?
Would you like someone to speak for you to the king or to the commander of the army?’”
She answered,
“I live among my own people.”
[14] He said,
“Then what can be done for her?”
Gehazi answered,
“Well, she has no son, and her husband is old.”
[15] He said,
“Call her.”
He called her, and she stood in the doorway.
[16] He said,
“At this time next year you will hold a son in your arms.”
She said,
“No, my lord, you man of God, don’t lie to your servant.”
[17] The woman became pregnant.
She gave birth to a son at that same time the next year,
as Elisha had told her.

> **What does this mean?**
> The woman from Shunem is rich and generous. She builds Elisha his own guest room, without expecting anything for it.
> When Elisha wants to do something good for her, she says: “I live among my own people.” That means: I don’t need anything, I am content.
> But she carries a deep pain that she does not talk about: she has no son. She hardly dares to hope: “Don’t lie to your servant.” She does not want to be disappointed.
> The story reminds us of Sarah, who still had a son in her old age (Genesis 18:10–14).
> Shunem lay in the plain of Jezreel, in the north of Israel.

## The son dies (Verses 18–28)

[18] When the child had grown,
one day he went out to his father, to the men who were cutting the grain.
[19] He said to his father,
“My head! My head!”
The father said to his servant,
“Carry him to his mother.”
[20] The servant took him and brought him to his mother.
He sat on her knees until noon.
Then he died.
[21] She went up and laid him on the bed of the man of God.
She shut the door behind him and went out.
[22] She called to her husband and said,
“Please send me one of the servants and one of the donkeys.
I want to hurry to the man of God and come back again.”
[23] He said,
“Why do you want to go to him today?
It is not a new moon or a Sabbath.”
She said,
“It is all right.”
[24] Then she saddled a donkey and said to her servant,
“Drive on and go forward!
Don’t slow down for me unless I tell you to.”
[25] So she went and came to the man of God at Mount Carmel.
When the man of God saw her far away, he said to Gehazi his servant,
“Look, there is the Shunammite.
[26] Please run now to meet her and ask her,
‘Is it well with you? Is it well with your husband? Is it well with your child?’”
She answered,
“It is well.”
[27] When she came to the man of God on the hill,
she took hold of his feet.
Gehazi came near to push her away.
But the man of God said,
“Leave her alone, for her soul is deeply troubled.
And the LORD has hidden it from me and has not told me.”
[28] Then she said,
“Did I ask you for a son, my lord?
Didn’t I say, ‘Don’t deceive me’?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The child suddenly gets a strong headache, perhaps from too much sun during the harvest, and dies on his mother’s lap. That is the worst thing parents can go through.
> The mother tells no one anything. She only says: “It is well.” With all her strength she holds back her pain until she is with the man of God. Only there does it break out of her.
> Elisha does not know what is wrong. Even a prophet does not know everything. But he lets the desperate woman come close to him.
> Her accusation is honest: “Did I ask you for a son, my lord?” Whoever loses something that was a gift suffers twice. The Bible allows such laments.
> “New moon and Sabbath” were holy days on which people went to a prophet.
> In Hebrew, verses 22 and 24 probably say “female donkey”. In those days people liked to ride female donkeys.
> Anyone who has lost a child can find help, for example, in support groups for bereaved parents, or here: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## The boy comes back to life (Verses 29–37)

[29] Then Elisha said to Gehazi,
“Tuck your cloak into your belt,
take my staff in your hand, and go.
If you meet anyone, don’t greet him.
And if anyone greets you, don’t answer him.
Lay my staff on the child’s face.”
[30] The child’s mother said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, and as surely as you live,
I will not leave you.”
So he got up and followed her.
[31] Gehazi went ahead of them
and laid the staff on the child’s face.
But there was no sound and no sign that he heard.
So he went back to meet Elisha and told him,
“The child has not woken up.”
[32] When Elisha came into the house,
look, the child was dead, lying on his bed.
[33] He went in,
shut the door behind the two of them,
and prayed to the LORD.
[34] He went up and lay on the child.
He put his mouth on his mouth,
his eyes on his eyes,
and his hands on his hands.
He stretched himself out on him,
and the child’s body grew warm.
[35] Then he got up again
and walked back and forth in the house once.
Then he went up and stretched himself out on him again.
Then the child sneezed seven times,
and the child opened his eyes.
[36] He called Gehazi and said,
“Call this Shunammite!”
So he called her.
When she came in to him, he said,
“Pick up your son.”
[37] Then she went in, fell at his feet,
and bowed down to the ground.
Then she picked up her son and went out.

> **What does this mean?**
> The mother says the same thing that Elisha once said to Elijah: “I will not leave you” (Chapter 2). She does not give up.
> The staff alone does not help. No magic is needed, but prayer and Elisha’s whole effort.
> Elisha prays and lies on the child, very much like Elijah with the son of the widow in Zarephath (1 Kings 17:21). God gives the child his life back.
> Sneezing seven times is a lively detail: the breath comes back.
> The mother does not say a word. She falls down, full of thanks, and takes her son in her arms.

## Death in the pot (Verses 38–41)

[38] Elisha came back to Gilgal.
There was a famine in the land.
A famine is a time when there is not enough food.
The sons of the prophets were sitting in front of him.
He said to his servant,
“Put on the large pot and cook stew for the sons of the prophets.”
[39] One of them went out into the field to gather herbs.
He found a wild vine
and gathered wild gourds from it, as many as his clothes could hold.
A gourd is a fruit like a small, hard pumpkin.
He came and cut them up into the pot of stew,
because they did not know what they were.
[40] Then they poured it out for the men to eat.
As they were eating the stew, they cried out,
“Man of God, there is death in the pot!”
And they could not eat it.
[41] But he said,
“Then bring flour.”
He threw it into the pot and said,
“Serve it to the people, so that they can eat.”
And there was nothing harmful in the pot anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> During the famine the men gather whatever they can find. One of them picks poisonous fruit. They were probably colocynths, a kind of wild gourd that is very bitter and poisonous and causes strong diarrhea.
> Elisha throws flour into the pot. As with the salt in Chapter 2, it is not the flour itself that works. God makes the food safe to eat.

## Bread for 100 men (Verses 42–44)

[42] A man came from Baal Shalishah.
He brought the man of God bread from the first part of the harvest:
20 loaves of barley bread and fresh heads of grain in his sack.
Elisha said,
“Give it to the people, so that they can eat.”
[43] His servant said,
“What? How can I set this in front of 100 men?”
But he said,
“Give it to the people, so that they can eat.
For the LORD says,
‘They will eat and have some left over.’”
[44] So he set it in front of them.
They ate and had some left over,
just as the LORD had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first part of the harvest really belonged to God and to the priests. Because there were no rightful priests in the northern kingdom, the man brings it to the prophet.
> 20 small loaves of barley bread for 100 men is far too little. But God makes sure that everyone has enough to eat and that some is even left over.
> This story is very much like Jesus feeding the 5,000. There too there were barley loaves, the disciples doubted, everyone had enough to eat, and some was left over (John 6:9–13).
