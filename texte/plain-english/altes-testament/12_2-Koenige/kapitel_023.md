# 2 Kings – Chapter 23
### Josiah’s great reform

---

## The covenant is renewed (Verses 1–3)

[1] The king sent messengers,
and they gathered to him all the elders of Judah and of Jerusalem.
[2] The king went up to the LORD’s house,
and with him all the men of Judah and all the people living in Jerusalem,
the priests, the prophets, and all the people, both small and great.
And he read aloud to them all the words of the book of the covenant
that had been found in the LORD’s house.
[3] The king stood by the pillar
and made a covenant before the LORD:
to follow the LORD
and to keep his commandments, his testimonies, and his laws
with all his heart and all his soul,
and to carry out the words of this covenant that were written in this book.
And all the people agreed to the covenant.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah does not keep the book to himself. He reads it aloud to the whole people, to everyone, small and great. God’s word is there for everyone.
> Then king and people together renew the covenant with God. “With all his heart and all his soul” reminds us of the most important commandment: “You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength” (Deuteronomy 6:5). Jesus called it the greatest commandment (Mark 12:29–30).

---

## The temple is cleansed (Verses 4–14)

[4] The king commanded Hilkiah the high priest,
the priests of the second rank, and the keepers of the doorway
to bring out of the LORD’s temple all the objects
that had been made for Baal, for the Asherah, and for all the stars of the sky.
He burned them outside Jerusalem in the fields of the Kidron
and carried their ashes to Bethel.
[5] He got rid of the idol priests
whom the kings of Judah had appointed
to burn incense on the high places in the cities of Judah and in the places around Jerusalem,
and also those who burned incense to Baal, to the sun, to the moon, to the planets, and to all the stars of the sky.
[6] He brought the Asherah out of the LORD’s house, outside Jerusalem, to the Kidron Valley.
He burned it at the Kidron Valley,
ground it to dust,
and threw its dust on the graves of the common people.
[7] He broke down the houses of the male shrine prostitutes that were in the LORD’s house,
where the women wove hangings for the Asherah.
[8] He brought all the priests out of the cities of Judah.
He made the high places unclean where the priests had burned incense,
from Geba to Beersheba.
He broke down the high places of the gates
that were at the entrance of the gate of Joshua the governor of the city,
on the left as you go in at the city gate.
[9] But the priests of the high places did not come up to the LORD’s altar in Jerusalem.
Yet they ate bread made without yeast among their brothers.
[10] He made Topheth unclean, which is in the Valley of the Sons of Hinnom,
so that no one could make his son or his daughter pass through the fire to Molech anymore.
[11] He took away the horses that the kings of Judah had dedicated to the sun,
at the entrance of the LORD’s house,
by the room of Nathan Melech the officer, which was in the court.
And he burned the chariots of the sun with fire.
[12] The king broke down the altars on the roof of the upper room of Ahaz, which the kings of Judah had made,
and the altars that Manasseh had made in the two courtyards of the LORD’s house.
He smashed them there
and threw their dust into the Kidron Valley.
[13] The king made unclean the high places that were east of Jerusalem,
on the right side of the Mount of Corruption,
which Solomon the king of Israel had built for Ashtoreth, the disgusting idol of the Sidonians,
for Chemosh, the disgusting idol of Moab,
and for Milcom, the disgusting idol of the people of Ammon.
[14] He broke the stone pillars to pieces,
cut down the Asherah poles,
and filled their places with human bones.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah cleans up thoroughly. The list is long and shows how much worship of idols had piled up, even in God’s own temple.
> “The stars of the sky” (the WEB says “the army of the sky”): sun, moon, and stars were worshiped as gods. There were even horses and chariots for the sun god.
> “Topheth” in the Valley of Hinnom was the place where children were sacrificed. Josiah puts an end to this. The Valley of Hinnom (in Hebrew “Ge-Hinnom”) later became a picture for the place of punishment. From this came the word “Gehenna”, which is used for hell in the New Testament.
> Even Solomon’s altars for foreign gods were still standing after about 300 years (1 Kings 11:7). The “Mount of Corruption” is a mocking name for a part of the Mount of Olives.
> Human bones made a place unclean. With this, these places could never be used for worship again.
> The priests of the high places in the countryside were not allowed to serve at the altar in Jerusalem. But they received a share of their brothers’ gifts. They were not simply dropped.
> In verse 7 our English source text speaks of male shrine prostitutes (see the explanation on 1 Kings 14:24).

## Bethel and Samaria (Verses 15–20)

[15] He also broke down the altar at Bethel
and the high place that Jeroboam the son of Nebat had made,
who had made Israel sin.
He broke down that altar and the high place too.
He burned the high place, ground it to dust,
and burned the Asherah.
[16] As Josiah turned around,
he saw the tombs that were there on the hill.
He sent men and took the bones out of the tombs.
He burned them on the altar and made it unclean,
just as the LORD’s word had said,
which the man of God had announced, who had announced these things.
[17] Then he asked,
“What is that monument that I see?”
The men of the city told him,
“It is the tomb of the man of God who came from Judah
and announced these things that you have done against the altar of Bethel.”
[18] He said,
“Leave him alone!
Let no one move his bones.”
So they left his bones alone,
together with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.
[19] Josiah also took away all the houses of the high places that were in the cities of Samaria,
which the kings of Israel had made to make the LORD angry.
He did to them everything that he had done in Bethel.
[20] He killed all the priests of the high places who were there, on the altars,
and burned human bones on them.
Then he went back to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a word comes true that is about 300 years old: the unnamed man of God from Judah had announced exactly this, even with the name Josiah (1 Kings 13:2). Now it happens.
> Josiah leaves the prophet’s tomb untouched, and with it the tomb of the old prophet from Bethel, who had wanted to be buried next to him (1 Kings 13:31).
> The northern kingdom had been destroyed for 100 years. The Assyrians had become weak. So Josiah could carry out his reform in the area of the old northern kingdom too.
> Verse 20 is very hard: Josiah has the priests of the high places in the north killed. In Judah he had only removed them from their office. The Bible reports this, but it is not an example for today. Questions of faith must never be decided by violence.

## A Passover like never before (Verses 21–23)

[21] The king commanded all the people,
“Keep the Passover to the LORD your God,
as it is written in this book of the covenant.”
[22] Surely no such Passover had been kept
since the days of the judges who judged Israel,
nor in all the days of the kings of Israel or of the kings of Judah.
[23] But in the eighteenth year of King Josiah,
this Passover was kept to the LORD in Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover feast remembers the rescue from Egypt (Exodus 12). Now it is celebrated again, and together in Jerusalem, as Deuteronomy 16:1–8 says.
> To this day Jews celebrate the Passover feast (Pesach) every year. Jesus too celebrated the Passover with his disciples on the evening before his death. From this came the Lord’s Supper of the Christians.

## No one was like Josiah (Verses 24–27)

[24] Josiah also got rid of the people who called up the spirits of the dead, the sorcerers,
the teraphim, the idols, and all the disgusting things
that were seen in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem,
so that he might carry out the words of the law
that were written in the book
that Hilkiah the priest had found in the LORD’s house.
[25] Before him there was no king like him,
who turned to the LORD with all his heart, with all his soul, and with all his strength,
following all the law of Moses.
And after him no one like him arose.
[26] But the LORD did not turn away from the heat of his great anger,
with which his anger burned against Judah,
because of all the things by which Manasseh had made him angry.
[27] The LORD said,
“I will also remove Judah from my sight,
as I have removed Israel.
And I will throw away this city that I have chosen, Jerusalem,
and the house about which I said, ‘My name will be there.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah receives the highest praise in all the books of Kings. He loves God exactly as the most important commandment asks: with all his heart, with all his soul, and with all his strength.
> “Teraphim” were small figures of household gods.
> But then comes the sad sentence: even so, Jerusalem will fall. The guilt that had piled up over generations, especially under Manasseh, was too great. Josiah’s reform came late. And after his death the people quickly went back to the old ways.
> This is hard to understand. It shows: a single good person can do a lot, but he cannot undo everything that a whole people has done over a long time. The prophet Jeremiah, who lived in this time, lamented that the people had not truly turned back with their whole heart (Jeremiah 3:10).

## Josiah’s death (Verses 28–30)

[28] The rest of the things Josiah did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[29] In his days Pharaoh Necoh king of Egypt went up
to the king of Assyria at the Euphrates River.
King Josiah went out against him.
But Pharaoh Necoh killed him at Megiddo when he saw him.
[30] His servants carried him dead in a chariot from Megiddo.
They brought him to Jerusalem and buried him in his own tomb.
And the people of the land took Jehoahaz the son of Josiah,
anointed him, and made him king in his father’s place.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the year 609 before Christ the Egyptian Pharaoh Necoh II marches north. He wants to help the Assyrians, who are fighting against the Babylonians. Josiah stands in his way and is killed at Megiddo. He is only 39 years old.
> The Bible does not say exactly why Josiah went out against Necoh. Perhaps he wanted to keep Assyria, the old enemy, from being rescued.
> His early death was a great shock. Jeremiah wrote a lament about him (2 Chronicles 35:25).
> Megiddo was an important place for battles. From it comes the name “Armageddon” (mountain of Megiddo) in Revelation 16:16.

## Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim (Verses 31–37)

[31] Jehoahaz was 23 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Hamutal the daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah.
[32] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his fathers had done.
[33] Pharaoh Necoh put him in chains at Riblah in the land of Hamath,
so that he could not rule in Jerusalem.
And he made the land pay a tribute of 100 talents of silver and a talent of gold.
[34] Pharaoh Necoh made Eliakim the son of Josiah king in the place of his father Josiah
and changed his name to Jehoiakim.
But he took Jehoahaz away,
and Jehoahaz came to Egypt and died there.
[35] Jehoiakim gave the silver and the gold to Pharaoh.
But he had to tax the land to give the money as Pharaoh commanded.
He forced the people of the land to pay the silver and the gold,
from everyone according to what was set for him,
to give it to Pharaoh Necoh.
[36] Jehoiakim was 25 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Zebidah the daughter of Pedaiah, from Rumah.
[37] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
just as his fathers had done.

> **What does this mean?**
> After Josiah’s death things go downhill quickly. Judah is now dependent on Egypt. Pharaoh removes kings and puts kings in place as he likes.
> He even gives the new king a new name. That shows: Judah is no longer free.
> The Jeremiah in verse 31 is not the prophet Jeremiah, but another man from Libnah.
> The Bible says “100 talents of silver and a talent of gold.” A talent is about 75 pounds.
> Jehoiakim was a harsh king. The prophet Jeremiah accused him because he had a splendid palace built for himself without paying the workers their wages (Jeremiah 22:13–19). Jehoiakim even burned the scroll with Jeremiah’s words (Jeremiah 36).
> Jehoiakim ruled from about 609 to 598 before Christ.
