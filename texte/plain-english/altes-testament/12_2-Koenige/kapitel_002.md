# 2 Kings – Chapter 2
### Elijah goes up into heaven

---

## Elisha stays with Elijah (Verses 1–8)

[1] When the LORD was about to take Elijah up into heaven in a whirlwind,
Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal.
A whirlwind is a strong storm wind that turns around and around.
[2] Elijah said to Elisha,
“Please wait here, because the LORD has sent me as far as Bethel.”
Elisha said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, and as surely as you live,
I will not leave you.”
So they went down to Bethel.
[3] The sons of the prophets who were at Bethel came out to Elisha and said to him,
“Do you know that the LORD will take your master away from over you today?”
He said,
“Yes, I know it. Be quiet.”
[4] Elijah said to him,
“Elisha, please wait here, because the LORD has sent me to Jericho.”
He said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, and as surely as you live,
I will not leave you.”
So they came to Jericho.
[5] The sons of the prophets who were at Jericho came near to Elisha and said to him,
“Do you know that the LORD will take your master away from over you today?”
He answered,
“Yes, I know it. Be quiet.”
[6] Elijah said to him,
“Please wait here, because the LORD has sent me to the Jordan.”
He said,
“As surely as the LORD lives, and as surely as you live,
I will not leave you.”
Then the two of them went on.
[7] 50 men of the sons of the prophets went too.
They stood facing them, some distance away.
And the two of them stood by the Jordan.
[8] Elijah took his cloak, rolled it up, and struck the water.
Then the water divided to this side and to that side,
so that the two of them went across on dry ground.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elijah knows that his end on earth is near. Three times he says to Elisha: “Wait here.” Perhaps he wants to test him. But Elisha stays faithful: “I will not leave you.” This reminds us of Ruth, who said to Naomi: “For where you go, I will go” (Ruth 1:16).
> The road leads from Gilgal through Bethel and Jericho to the Jordan. It is like a journey backward through Israel’s history: under Joshua, Israel came across the Jordan to Jericho and Gilgal.
> Elijah divides the Jordan with his cloak, just as Moses divided the Red Sea (Exodus 14) and Joshua the Jordan (Joshua 3).
> “Sons of the prophets” were groups of students who lived and learned with a prophet.

## The chariot of fire (Verses 9–14)

[9] When they had gone across, Elijah said to Elisha,
“Ask what I should do for you, before I am taken away from you.”
Elisha said,
“Please let a double share of your spirit be on me.”
[10] He said,
“You have asked a hard thing.
If you see me when I am taken from you,
it will be so for you.
But if not, it will not be so.”
[11] As they went on and talked,
look, a chariot of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them.
And Elijah went up into heaven in a whirlwind.
[12] Elisha saw it, and he cried out,
“My father, my father,
the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!”
And he did not see him anymore.
Then he took hold of his own clothes and tore them in two pieces.
[13] He also picked up Elijah’s cloak that had fallen from him.
He went back and stood on the bank of the Jordan.
[14] He took Elijah’s cloak that had fallen from him,
struck the water, and said,
“Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?”
When he too had struck the water,
it divided to this side and to that side,
and Elisha went across.

> **What does this mean?**
> “A double share”: by the law, the oldest son received a double share of the inheritance (Deuteronomy 21:17). So Elisha asks to be Elijah’s true heir and the one who follows him.
> Elijah does not die. He is taken up into heaven alive. Elsewhere in the Bible this is only told about Enoch (Genesis 5:24).
> That is why the Jews expected that Elijah would come back one day, before the great day of the LORD (Malachi 4:5). To this day, at the Jewish Passover feast, a cup is set out for Elijah and the door is opened for him. In the New Testament, Elijah appears when Jesus is transfigured on the mountain (Mark 9:4).
> “The chariots of Israel and its horsemen!”: Elisha means: Elijah was Israel’s true strength, more than all the chariots and soldiers.
> Elisha tears his clothes in grief. Then he picks up Elijah’s cloak. The cloak is the sign: now you are the prophet. And God confirms it: the Jordan divides for Elisha too.
> “Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?” That is the question of every new generation: is God still with us when the great examples are no longer there? The answer is: yes.

## The sons of the prophets look for Elijah (Verses 15–18)

[15] The sons of the prophets who were at Jericho, facing him, saw him.
They said,
“The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha.”
They came to meet him and bowed down in front of him to the ground.
[16] They said to him,
“See now, with your servants there are 50 strong men.
Please let them go and look for your master.
Perhaps the LORD’s Spirit has taken him up
and put him on some mountain or in some valley.”
He said,
“Don’t send them.”
[17] But they kept urging him until he was ashamed, and he said,
“Send them.”
So they sent 50 men.
They searched for three days, but they did not find him.
[18] They came back to him while he was staying in Jericho.
He said to them,
“Didn’t I tell you, ‘Don’t go’?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The sons of the prophets accept Elisha as their new leader.
> They cannot believe that Elijah is really gone. The useless search shows everyone: Elijah is not somewhere on earth. God has really taken him to himself.

## Elisha heals the water (Verses 19–22)

[19] The men of the city said to Elisha,
“Look, please, this city is in a pleasant place, as my lord sees.
But the water is bad, and the land is barren.”
Barren means: nothing grows there.
[20] He said,
“Bring me a new jar, and put salt in it.”
They brought it to him.
[21] He went out to the spring of water,
threw salt into it, and said,
“The LORD says,
‘I have healed this water.
From there, no more death or barren wasteland will come.’”
[22] So the water was healed, and it stays healed to this day,
just as Elisha had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elisha’s first miracle is a miracle of healing and of life. That will be typical for him: he helps people in their need.
> The salt does not work by itself. Elisha says clearly: the LORD has healed the water.
> The Hebrew word for “barren” can also mean “causing miscarriages”. The water made the land barren and perhaps also harmed people and animals.
> Near Jericho there is a large spring to this day. It is called “Elisha’s Spring”.

## The youths of Bethel (Verses 23–25)

[23] From there he went up to Bethel.
As he was going up on the road,
some youths came out of the city and mocked him.
They called to him,
“Go up, you baldy! Go up, you baldy!”
[24] He turned around, looked at them,
and cursed them in the LORD’s name.
Then two female bears came out of the woods
and mauled 42 of those youths.
[25] From there he went to Mount Carmel,
and from there he went back to Samaria.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is one of the hardest in the Bible. Many people are troubled by it, and that is understandable.
> What you should know: the Hebrew word for “youths” can also mean young men, not only small children. It was a large group, at least 42. They came from Bethel, the place of Jeroboam’s golden calf. Their mocking is aimed at God’s prophet. “Go up” is perhaps a hint at Elijah going up into heaven, as if to say: “Why don’t you disappear too!”
> “Mauled” means badly hurt. The text does not say clearly that they all died.
> The text wants to show: whoever despises God’s messengers despises God himself. Even so, this story is not an example for how we should act. Jesus taught: “Bless those who curse you!” (Luke 6:28).
> Jews and Christians have thought a lot about this passage. The Talmud (Sotah 47a) even says that Elisha later became sick partly because of it.
