# 2 Kings – Chapter 22
### The book that was found again

---

## Josiah, a good king (Verses 1–2)

[1] Josiah was eight years old when he became king.
He ruled for 31 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jedidah.
She was the daughter of Adaiah, from Bozkath.
[2] Josiah did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
He lived in all the ways of his father David.
He did not turn away from them, to the right or to the left.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah becomes king while still a child, after his father Amon is murdered. In spite of his evil father and grandfather, he becomes one of the most faithful kings.
> “To the right or to the left”: he stays on God’s straight path, without turning aside (compare Deuteronomy 5:32).
> Josiah ruled from about 640 to 609 BC. In his time the prophet Jeremiah also began his work (Jeremiah 1:2).

---

## The repair of the temple (Verses 3–7)

[3] In the eighteenth year of King Josiah,
the king sent Shaphan the scribe to the LORD’s house.
Shaphan was the son of Azaliah, the son of Meshullam.
The king said,
[4] “Go up to Hilkiah, the high priest.
Let him count the money that people have brought into the LORD’s house.
The guards at the door have collected it from the people.
[5] They should give it to the men who are in charge of the work on the LORD’s house.
These men should pay the workers in the LORD’s house
to repair the damage to the house.
[6] They should pay the carpenters, the builders, and the stonemasons.
They should also buy wood and cut stone to repair the house.
[7] But do not ask them to report what they did with the money they get.
They are honest.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah is now 26 years old. He has the temple repaired, which had been neglected under Manasseh and Amon. The method is the same as under King Joash (Chapter 12).
> The eighteenth year of Josiah was about 622 BC.
> Shaphan and his family also play an important part later: his son Ahikam protected the prophet Jeremiah (Jeremiah 26:24). In Jerusalem a seal impression with the name of Gemariah son of Shaphan was found. A Gemariah son of Shaphan is mentioned in Jeremiah 36:10.

---

## The book of the law is found (Verses 8–13)

[8] Hilkiah, the high priest, said to Shaphan the scribe,
“I have found the book of the law in the LORD’s house.”
Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and Shaphan read it.
[9] Shaphan the scribe went to the king.
He reported to the king,
“Your servants have emptied out the money that was in the house.
They have given it to the men who are in charge of the work on the LORD’s house.”
[10] Then Shaphan the scribe told the king,
“Hilkiah the priest has given me a book.”
And Shaphan read it to the king.
[11] The king heard the words of the book of the law.
Then he tore his clothes.
[12] The king gave an order to Hilkiah the priest,
Ahikam the son of Shaphan,
Achbor the son of Micaiah,
Shaphan the scribe,
and Asaiah, the king’s servant.
He said,
[13] “Go and ask the LORD about the words of this book that has been found.
Ask for me, for the people, and for all Judah.
The LORD is very angry with us.
His anger burns against us,
because our fathers did not listen to the words of this book.
They did not do what is written in it for us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> During the building work a forgotten book is found: the book of the law. Under the evil kings God’s word had been so neglected that people no longer knew it at all.
> Which book was it? Most scholars think that it was the book of Deuteronomy, all of it or a part of it. For Josiah’s reforms fit exactly with what it says there: only one place for worship, no foreign gods, the Passover feast in Jerusalem.
> Josiah hears God’s word and is shocked. He tears his clothes as a sign of grief and repentance. He realizes: we have lived completely wrongly.
> This shows how powerfully God’s word can work when people really listen to it.

## The prophetess Huldah (Verses 14–20)

[14] So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asaiah went
to Huldah the prophetess.
She was the wife of Shallum, the son of Tikvah, the son of Harhas, the keeper of the wardrobe.
Huldah lived in Jerusalem, in the Second Quarter.
They talked with her.
[15] She said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Tell the man who sent you to me:
[16] “The LORD says,
‘I will bring disaster on this place and on the people who live here.
Everything the king of Judah has read in the book will happen.
[17] They have left me.
They have burned incense to other gods.
With everything they have made, they have made me angry.
So my anger will burn against this place,
and it will not go out.’”
[18] But the king of Judah sent you to ask the LORD.
Tell him,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says this
about the words you have heard:
[19] ‘You heard what I said against this place and against the people who live here.
I said that they would become a ruin and a curse.
Your heart was tender.
You humbled yourself before the LORD.
You tore your clothes and cried before me.
So I have heard you,’
says the LORD.
[20] ‘That is why I will let you die and join your fathers.
You will go to your grave in peace.
Your eyes will not see all the disaster
that I will bring on this place.’”’”
So the men brought this message back to the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king’s men go to a woman: to the prophetess Huldah. Even though Jeremiah and Zephaniah were also working as prophets at that time, they ask Huldah. Her word is taken seriously and is accepted as God’s word. The Bible has several prophetesses, for example Miriam (Exodus 15:20) and Deborah (Judges 4:4).
> In Jerusalem, the “Huldah Gates” on the south side of the Temple Mount still remind people of her today.
> Huldah’s message has two parts: the disaster on Jerusalem can no longer be stopped. But Josiah himself will not live to see it, because he humbled himself.
> “Your heart was tender”: God pays attention to a heart that lets itself be moved.
> “You will go to your grave in peace”: it is true that Josiah later dies in war (Chapter 23:29). But he dies before Jerusalem is destroyed. He does not have to see its fall.
> The “Second Quarter” was probably a newer part of Jerusalem that had been added under Hezekiah.
