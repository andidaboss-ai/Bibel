# 2 Kings – Chapter 22
### The book that was found again

---

## Josiah, a good king (Verses 1–2)

[1] Josiah was eight years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 31 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Jedidah the daughter of Adaiah, from Bozkath.
[2] He did what was right in the LORD’s eyes.
He walked in all the ways of his father David
and did not turn away to the right or to the left.

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah becomes king while still a child, after his father Amon is murdered. In spite of his evil father and grandfather, he becomes one of the most faithful kings.
> “To the right or to the left”: he stays on God’s straight path, without turning aside (compare Deuteronomy 5:32).
> Josiah ruled from about 640 to 609 before Christ. In his time the prophet Jeremiah also began his work (Jeremiah 1:2).

---

## The repair of the temple (Verses 3–7)

[3] In the eighteenth year of King Josiah,
the king sent Shaphan the scribe, the son of Azaliah, the son of Meshullam,
to the LORD’s house and said,
[4] “Go up to Hilkiah the high priest.
Let him count the money that has been brought into the LORD’s house,
which the keepers of the doorway have collected from the people.
[5] Let them hand it over to the work supervisors
who are in charge of the LORD’s house.
And let them give it to the workers in the LORD’s house
to repair the damage to the house:
[6] to the carpenters, the builders, and the masons,
and for buying wood and cut stone to repair the house.
[7] But they shall not be asked for an accounting of the money
that is handed to them,
because they are honest.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Josiah is now 26 years old. He has the temple repaired, which had been neglected under Manasseh and Amon. The method is the same as under King Joash (Chapter 12).
> The eighteenth year of Josiah was about 622 before Christ.
> Shaphan and his family also play an important part later: his son Ahikam protected the prophet Jeremiah (Jeremiah 26:24). In Jerusalem a seal impression with the name of Gemariah son of Shaphan was found. A Gemariah son of Shaphan is mentioned in Jeremiah 36:10.

---

## The book of the law is found (Verses 8–13)

[8] Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the scribe,
“I have found the book of the law in the LORD’s house.”
Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it.
[9] Shaphan the scribe came to the king
and reported to the king:
“Your servants have emptied out the money that was found in the house
and have handed it over to the work supervisors who are in charge of the LORD’s house.”
[10] And Shaphan the scribe told the king,
“Hilkiah the priest has given me a book.”
Then Shaphan read it to the king.
[11] When the king had heard the words of the book of the law,
he tore his clothes.
[12] The king commanded Hilkiah the priest,
Ahikam the son of Shaphan,
Achbor the son of Micaiah,
Shaphan the scribe,
and Asaiah the king’s servant:
[13] “Go and ask the LORD for me, for the people, and for all Judah
about the words of this book that has been found.
For great is the LORD’s anger that burns against us,
because our fathers have not listened to the words of this book,
to do everything that is written in it about us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> During the building work a forgotten book is found: the book of the law. Under the evil kings God’s word had been so neglected that people no longer knew it at all.
> Which book was it? Most scholars think that it was the book of Deuteronomy, all of it or a part of it. For Josiah’s reforms fit exactly with what it says there: only one place for worship, no foreign gods, the Passover feast in Jerusalem.
> Josiah hears God’s word and is shocked. He tears his clothes as a sign of grief and repentance. He realizes: we have lived completely wrongly.
> This shows how powerfully God’s word can work when people really listen to it.

## The prophetess Huldah (Verses 14–20)

[14] So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asaiah went
to Huldah the prophetess,
the wife of Shallum the son of Tikvah, the son of Harhas, the keeper of the wardrobe.
(She lived in Jerusalem in the Second Quarter.)
And they talked with her.
[15] She said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Tell the man who sent you to me,
[16] “The LORD says,
‘Look, I will bring disaster on this place and on its people,
all the words of the book that the king of Judah has read.
[17] Because they have left me
and have burned incense to other gods,
to make me angry with all the work of their hands,
my anger will burn against this place,
and it will not be put out.’”
[18] But tell the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the LORD,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says
about the words that you have heard:
[19] ‘Because your heart was tender,
and you humbled yourself before the LORD
when you heard what I said against this place and against its people,
that they would become a ruin and a curse,
and because you tore your clothes and wept before me,
I also have heard you,’
says the LORD.
[20] ‘So look, I will gather you to your fathers,
and you will be gathered to your grave in peace.
Your eyes will not see all the disaster
that I will bring on this place.’”’”
So they brought this message back to the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king’s men go to a woman: to the prophetess Huldah. Even though Jeremiah and Zephaniah were also working as prophets at that time, they ask Huldah. Her word is taken seriously and is accepted as God’s word. The Bible has several prophetesses, for example Miriam (Exodus 15:20) and Deborah (Judges 4:4).
> In Jerusalem, the “Huldah Gates” on the south side of the Temple Mount still remind people of her today.
> Huldah’s message has two parts: the disaster on Jerusalem can no longer be stopped. But Josiah himself will not live to see it, because he humbled himself.
> “Because your heart was tender”: God pays attention to a heart that lets itself be moved.
> “To your grave in peace”: it is true that Josiah later dies in war (Chapter 23:29). But he dies before Jerusalem is destroyed. He does not have to see its fall.
> The “Second Quarter” was probably a newer part of Jerusalem that had been added under Hezekiah.
