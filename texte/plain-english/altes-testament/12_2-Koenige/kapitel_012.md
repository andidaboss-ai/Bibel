# 2 Kings – Chapter 12
### King Jehoash repairs the temple

---

## A good beginning (Verses 1–3)

[1] Jehoash became king in the seventh year of Jehu,
and he ruled for 40 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Zibiah, from Beersheba.
[2] Jehoash did what was right in the LORD’s eyes
all his days in which Jehoiada the priest taught him.
[3] But the high places were not taken away.
The people still offered sacrifices and burned incense on the high places.
High places were places of worship on hills.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoash (also called Joash) has a good teacher: Jehoiada the priest. As long as Jehoiada lives, Jehoash acts well.
> “All his days in which Jehoiada the priest taught him” is a quiet hint: after Jehoiada’s death things changed. 2 Chronicles 24:17–22 tells that Joash later even had Jehoiada’s son killed.
> In some Bibles the verse numbering in this chapter is shifted by one: verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 21 here, which is verse 22 there.
> Jehoash ruled from about 835 to 796 before Christ.

---

## Money for repairing the temple (Verses 4–8)

[4] Jehoash said to the priests,
“All the money from the holy gifts that is brought into the LORD’s house,
in the money that is in use:
the money that each person pays according to the value set for him,
and all the money that anyone freely decides in his heart to bring into the LORD’s house,
[5] the priests shall take for themselves, each one from his donor.
And they shall repair the damage to the house,
wherever any damage is found.”
[6] But in the twenty-third year of King Jehoash,
the priests still had not repaired the damage to the house.
[7] Then King Jehoash called Jehoiada the priest and the other priests
and said to them,
“Why aren’t you repairing the damage to the house?
So now take no more money from your treasurers,
but hand it over for repairing the damage to the house.”
[8] The priests agreed
that they would take no more money from the people,
and that they would not repair the damage to the house themselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple was more than 100 years old and had been neglected under Athaliah. There was a lot of damage.
> Jehoash orders: the gifts are to be used for the repairs. But for more than 20 years nothing happens. The text does not say directly where the money went.
> “The value set for him”: whoever had made a vow could buy himself free with a fixed amount of money (Leviticus 27:1–8).
> The Hebrew word for “donor” and “treasurer” is unclear. It probably means “someone you know”. Perhaps it means an official who received the money from the people.

## The collection chest (Verses 9–16)

[9] Then Jehoiada the priest took a chest,
bored a hole in its lid,
and set it beside the altar,
on the right side as you come into the LORD’s house.
The priests who kept watch at the doorway
put into it all the money that was brought into the LORD’s house.
[10] When they saw that there was a lot of money in the chest,
the king’s scribe and the high priest came up.
They put it in bags
and counted the money that was found in the LORD’s house.
[11] They gave the money that had been weighed out into the hands of the work supervisors,
who were in charge of the LORD’s house.
They paid it out to the carpenters and the builders
who worked on the LORD’s house,
[12] and to the masons and the stonecutters.
They also bought wood and cut stone
to repair the damage to the LORD’s house,
and paid for everything that was spent on repairing the house.
[13] But from the money that was brought into the LORD’s house,
no silver cups, wick trimmers, basins, or trumpets were made,
no objects of gold or silver for the LORD’s house.
[14] For they gave it to the workers,
and with it they repaired the LORD’s house.
[15] And they did not ask for an accounting from the men to whom they gave the money to pay the workers,
because they were honest.
[16] The money for the guilt offerings and the money for the sin offerings
was not brought into the LORD’s house.
It belonged to the priests.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jehoiada finds a simple solution: a collection chest with a hole in its lid. The money is counted together by an official of the king and by the high priest. So no one can take anything for himself alone. This is an early example of good, well-controlled handling of money.
> To this day many churches and synagogues have such collection boxes.
> At first all the money is used for the repairs, not for beautiful new objects. The important things first.
> The work supervisors were so honest that no one had to ask them for an accounting.
> Even so, the priests receive what is due to them: the money from certain offerings. They too have to live.
> Wick trimmers were needed to trim the wicks of the lamps.

---

## Hazael threatens Jerusalem (Verses 17–18)

[17] Then Hazael king of Syria went up,
fought against Gath, and captured it.
Then Hazael made up his mind to go up to Jerusalem.
[18] Jehoash king of Judah took all the holy gifts
that his fathers Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, kings of Judah, had dedicated,
and his own holy gifts,
and all the gold that was found in the treasuries of the LORD’s house
and of the king’s house,
and he sent it to Hazael king of Syria.
Then Hazael went away from Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hazael, the cruel king of Syria, advances right up to Jerusalem. Jehoash buys his freedom with the treasures of the temple.
> That is bitter: the king who has just repaired the temple must now strip it, in order to save Jerusalem.
> Gath was a city of the Philistines.

---

## The end of Joash (Verses 19–21)

[19] The rest of the things Joash did, and all that he did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[20] His servants rose up and made a plot.
They struck down Joash at the house of Millo, on the road that goes down to Silla.
[21] For Jozacar the son of Shimeath and Jehozabad the son of Shomer, his servants,
struck him, and he died.
And they buried him with his fathers in David’s city.
And his son Amaziah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joash, who was saved so wonderfully as a child, is murdered in the end by his own servants. According to 2 Chronicles 24:25 this happened as revenge for the son of Jehoiada the priest, whom Joash had had killed.
> Joash’s life shows: a good beginning with a good teacher is a great gift. But each person must stay faithful to God himself, even when the teacher is no longer there.
> No one knows exactly where “Millo” and “Silla” were. Millo was probably a fortification in Jerusalem.
