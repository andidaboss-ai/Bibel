# 2 Kings – Chapter 13
### Elisha’s last prophecy

---

## Jehoahaz, king of Israel (Verses 1–9)

[1] In the twenty-third year of Joash the son of Ahaziah, king of Judah,
Jehoahaz the son of Jehu became king over Israel in Samaria, for 17 years.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He followed the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which Jeroboam had made Israel sin.
He did not turn away from them.
[3] The LORD’s anger burned against Israel.
Again and again he handed them over to Hazael king of Syria
and to Ben Hadad the son of Hazael.
[4] Jehoahaz begged the LORD,
and the LORD listened to him,
because he saw how Israel was oppressed,
how the king of Syria oppressed them.
To oppress means: to treat people harshly and keep them down.
[5] (The LORD gave Israel a savior,
so that they got out from under the power of the Syrians.
And the people of Israel lived in their tents as before.
[6] But they did not turn away from the sins of the house of Jeroboam,
the sins through which he had made Israel sin,
but they kept living in them.
And the Asherah also remained in Samaria.)
An Asherah was a wooden pole or image of a goddess.
[7] For the king of Syria had not left Jehoahaz any more of the army
than 50 horsemen, ten chariots, and 10,000 foot soldiers.
For the king of Syria had destroyed them
and made them like the dust at threshing time.
[8] The rest of the things Jehoahaz did,
all that he did, and his power,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[9] Jehoahaz died and joined his fathers,
and they buried him in Samaria.
And his son Joash became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Under Jehu’s son Jehoahaz, Israel is almost destroyed by Syria. Only ten chariots are left of the army. For comparison: Ahab still had 2,000 chariots.
> Jehoahaz prays in his need, and God listens to him, even though he is not a good king. God’s mercy is greater than what we deserve.
> The text does not say who the “savior” was. Perhaps it means the later King Jeroboam II. Or the Assyrian king, who attacked Syria and so gave Israel room to breathe.
> Verses 5–6 are in parentheses: they are an insertion in the middle of the report.
> Jehoahaz ruled from about 814 to 798 before Christ.

## Jehoash, king of Israel (Verses 10–13)

[10] In the thirty-seventh year of Joash king of Judah,
Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz became king over Israel in Samaria, for 16 years.
[11] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
He did not turn away from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat,
the sins through which Jeroboam had made Israel sin,
but he lived in them.
[12] The rest of the things Joash did,
all that he did, and his power with which he fought against Amaziah king of Judah,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
[13] Joash died and joined his fathers,
and Jeroboam sat on his throne.
Joash was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the names are confusing: at the same time, the king of Judah was called Joash, and so was the king of Israel. Both are sometimes also called “Jehoash”.
> Here the end of Joash is already told. After that the text goes back once more and tells a story from his time.
> Joash of Israel ruled from about 798 to 782 before Christ.

---

## The arrows of victory (Verses 14–19)

[14] Elisha became sick with the sickness from which he was going to die.
Joash the king of Israel came down to him.
He wept over him and said,
“My father, my father,
the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!”
[15] Elisha said to him,
“Take a bow and arrows.”
And he took a bow and arrows for himself.
[16] He said to the king of Israel,
“Put your hand on the bow.”
And he put his hand on it.
Elisha laid his hands on the king’s hands.
[17] He said,
“Open the window toward the east.”
And he opened it.
Then Elisha said,
“Shoot!”
And he shot.
He said,
“The LORD’s arrow of victory,
the arrow of victory over Syria!
For you will strike the Syrians at Aphek until you have destroyed them.”
[18] He said,
“Take the arrows.”
And he took them.
He said to the king of Israel,
“Strike the ground.”
He struck three times and stopped.
[19] The man of God was angry with him and said,
“You should have struck five or six times.
Then you would have struck Syria until you had destroyed it.
But now you will strike Syria only three times.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Elisha is old and dying. The king weeps and says the same words that Elisha once cried out when Elijah was taken from him (Chapter 2:12). Elisha was Israel’s true strength.
> Elisha’s last prophecy is an action that is a sign. He lays his hands on the king’s hands: victory comes from God, not from one’s own strength.
> The arrow flies toward the east, in the direction of Syria.
> Then the king is to strike the ground with the arrows. But he strikes only three times, half-heartedly. Elisha is disappointed. The lesson: whoever trusts only timidly also receives only a part. God invites us to believe and to act with our whole heart.

## Elisha’s death and his bones (Verses 20–21)

[20] Elisha died, and they buried him.
Raiding groups of Moabites came into the land every year when the year began.
[21] Once, as people were burying a man,
look, they saw a raiding group.
So they threw the man into Elisha’s tomb.
As soon as the man touched Elisha’s bones,
he came back to life and stood up on his feet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elisha dies. Unlike Elijah, he is not taken up into heaven. He dies like all people.
> But even after his death another miracle happens: a dead man who is thrown into his tomb comes back to life. God’s power works beyond death.
> The miracle does not come from the bones themselves. It is a sign that God is the Lord over life and death.
> “When the year began” means: in spring, when military campaigns and raids began.
> Later this passage was often used to defend honoring relics, that is, the remains of holy people. Christians of different churches see this differently.

## God has mercy on Israel (Verses 22–25)

[22] Hazael king of Syria oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz.
[23] But the LORD was gracious to them.
He had compassion on them and turned toward them,
because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
He would not destroy them,
and up to that time he did not throw them away from his presence.
[24] Hazael king of Syria died,
and his son Ben Hadad became king in his place.
[25] Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz took back from Ben Hadad the son of Hazael
the cities that Ben Hadad had taken from his father Jehoahaz in war.
Joash struck him three times
and won back the cities of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23 is a great word of hope: God remembers his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Even when the people are unfaithful, God stays faithful.
> “Up to that time” is a quiet, sad hint: about 60 years later the northern kingdom will come to an end after all.
> Elisha’s word comes true exactly: Joash wins against Syria three times, not more.
