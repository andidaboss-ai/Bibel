# 2 Kings – Chapter 20
### Hezekiah’s sickness and the messengers from Babylon

---

## Hezekiah is deathly sick and prays (Verses 1–7)

[1] In those days Hezekiah was sick and close to death.
The prophet Isaiah the son of Amoz came to him and said to him,
“The LORD says,
‘Put your house in order, because you will die and not live.’”
[2] Then he turned his face to the wall
and prayed to the LORD:
[3] “Please, LORD, remember
how I have lived before you faithfully and with my whole heart,
and have done what is good in your eyes.”
And Hezekiah wept bitterly.
[4] Isaiah had not yet gone out into the middle part of the city
when the LORD’s word came to him:
[5] “Turn back and tell Hezekiah, the leader of my people,
‘The LORD, the God of your father David, says:
“I have heard your prayer.
I have seen your tears.
Look, I will heal you.
On the third day you will go up to the LORD’s house.
[6] I will add 15 years to your life.
I will save you and this city from the power of the king of Assyria.
I will defend this city for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David.”’”
[7] Isaiah said,
“Get a cake of figs.”
They got it and laid it on the boil,
and he got well.
A boil is a painful, swollen sore on the skin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah receives terrible news: you will die. He turns to the wall to be alone with God, and he weeps.
> God answers at once, even before Isaiah has gone far: “I have heard your prayer. I have seen your tears.” God sees our tears. Prayer can change things.
> In those days a cake of figs was a well-known remedy for boils. Here God heals through a miracle and at the same time through an ordinary remedy. Faith and medicine are not opposites.
> Instead of “the middle part of the city”, many Hebrew manuscripts have “the middle courtyard”. Then Isaiah was still in the palace.
> A prayer of Hezekiah after his healing is found in Isaiah 38:9–20.

## The shadow goes back (Verses 8–11)

[8] Hezekiah said to Isaiah,
“What will be the sign that the LORD will heal me,
and that I will go up to the LORD’s house on the third day?”
[9] Isaiah said,
“This will be the sign for you from the LORD
that the LORD will do what he has said:
Should the shadow go forward ten steps,
or go back ten steps?”
[10] Hezekiah answered,
“It is easy for the shadow to go forward ten steps.
No, let the shadow go back ten steps.”
[11] Isaiah the prophet cried out to the LORD.
And the LORD brought the shadow back ten steps,
on the steps it had already gone down on the sundial of Ahaz.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “sundial of Ahaz” was probably a staircase on which the shadow moved on, step by step, during the day. In this way people could tell the time.
> The shadow going back is a sign: God can even turn back time. He gives Hezekiah more time to live.
> The Bible does not say exactly how this happened.

## The messengers from Babylon (Verses 12–19)

[12] At that time Berodach Baladan the son of Baladan, king of Babylon,
sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah,
because he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.
[13] Hezekiah listened to them
and showed them his whole storehouse of precious things:
the silver, the gold, the spices, and the precious oil,
his armory,
and everything that was found in his treasuries.
There was nothing in his house or in all his kingdom
that Hezekiah did not show them.
[14] Then Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him,
“What did these men say?
Where did they come to you from?”
Hezekiah said,
“They have come from a faraway country, from Babylon.”
[15] He asked,
“What have they seen in your house?”
Hezekiah answered,
“They have seen everything that is in my house.
There is nothing among my treasures that I have not shown them.”
[16] Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah,
“Hear the LORD’s word:
[17] ‘Look, the days are coming
when everything that is in your house,
and what your fathers have stored up until this day,
will be carried away to Babylon.
Nothing will be left,’
says the LORD.
[18] ‘And some of your sons who will come from you, whom you will father,
will be taken away,
and they will be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.’”
[19] Then Hezekiah said to Isaiah,
“The LORD’s word that you have spoken is good.”
And he said,
“Isn’t it so, if there will be peace and truth in my days?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Berodach Baladan (also called Merodach Baladan) was a king of Babylon who fought against the Assyrians. He was looking for allies. He is also known from Assyrian and Babylonian sources.
> Hezekiah feels flattered and proudly shows the messengers all his treasures and weapons. He looks for help from people and wants to impress them.
> Isaiah announces: one day Babylon will take everything away. That happened more than 100 years later (Chapters 24–25). The prophet Daniel and his friends were also brought to the royal court in Babylon as young men (Daniel 1:3–6).
> “Eunuchs” here means servants at the royal court.
> Hezekiah’s answer is hard to understand. Perhaps he humbly accepts the judgment. Perhaps he is also thinking a little selfishly: the main thing is that there is peace while I live. The Bible leaves this open.

## The end of Hezekiah (Verses 20–21)

[20] The rest of the things Hezekiah did,
all his power,
and how he made the pool and the water channel
and brought water into the city,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[21] Hezekiah died and joined his fathers,
and his son Manasseh became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hezekiah had a tunnel dug through the rock to bring the water of the Gihon spring into the city. In this way Jerusalem had water even during a siege.
> This tunnel still exists today. It is about 1,750 feet long, and you can still walk through it. In 1880 an inscription was found in it that tells how two groups of workers dug from both sides and met in the middle. Today it is in a museum in Istanbul.
> In 2015, during excavations in Jerusalem, a seal impression was also found with the words: “Hezekiah, son of Ahaz, king of Judah”.
