# 2 Kings – Chapter 21
### Manasseh and Amon

---

## Manasseh, the worst king of Judah (Verses 1–9)

[1] Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king,
and he ruled for 55 years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Hephzibah.
[2] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
following the disgusting practices of the nations
whom the LORD had driven out before the people of Israel.
[3] For he built again the high places that his father Hezekiah had destroyed.
He set up altars for Baal
and made an Asherah pole, as Ahab king of Israel had done.
He worshiped all the stars of the sky and served them.
[4] He built altars in the LORD’s house,
about which the LORD had said,
“I will put my name in Jerusalem.”
[5] He built altars for all the stars of the sky
in the two courtyards of the LORD’s house.
[6] He made his son pass through the fire.
He practiced magic and fortune-telling,
and he dealt with people who called up the spirits of the dead and with sorcerers.
He did much evil in the LORD’s eyes, to make him angry.
[7] He put the carved image of Asherah that he had made
in the house about which the LORD had said to David and to his son Solomon,
“In this house, and in Jerusalem,
which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel,
I will put my name forever.
[8] And I will not make the feet of Israel wander away anymore from the land
that I gave their fathers,
if only they will be careful to do everything I have commanded them,
and to follow the whole law that my servant Moses commanded them.”
[9] But they did not listen.
And Manasseh led them astray to do evil,
more than the nations had done whom the LORD destroyed before the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Manasseh is the son of the good King Hezekiah. But he undoes everything his father had built up. He rules for a very long time, 55 years, longer than any other king of Judah.
> “The stars of the sky” (the WEB says “the army of the sky”) are the stars and planets. The Assyrians worshiped them as gods. Manasseh takes over this worship, probably also to please the Assyrians. Assyrian inscriptions name “Manasseh, king of Judah” as one of the kings who had to pay tribute.
> He even puts an idol in God’s temple. That is the worst breaking of the covenant.
> He also sacrifices his son in the fire, like his grandfather Ahaz.
> Manasseh ruled from about 697 to 642 before Christ, at first together with his father.

---

## God’s judgment on Jerusalem (Verses 10–16)

[10] The LORD spoke through his servants the prophets:
[11] “Manasseh king of Judah has done these disgusting things.
He has done more evil than everything the Amorites did, who were before him.
And with his idols he has also made Judah sin.
[12] So the LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Look, I will bring such disaster on Jerusalem and Judah
that both ears of everyone who hears about it will ring.
[13] I will stretch over Jerusalem the measuring line of Samaria
and the plumb line of Ahab’s house.
A plumb line is a string with a weight on it, used to check whether a wall is straight.
I will wipe Jerusalem clean as a man wipes a dish:
he wipes it and turns it upside down.
[14] I will throw away the remnant of my inheritance
and hand them over to their enemies.
They will become prey and plunder for all their enemies,
[15] because they have done what is evil in my eyes
and have made me angry,
from the day their fathers came out of Egypt until this day.’”
[16] Besides this, Manasseh shed very much innocent blood,
until he had filled Jerusalem with it from one end to the other.
This was in addition to his sin through which he made Judah sin,
so that they did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God announces that Jerusalem too will fall, like Samaria.
> The pictures are strong: with a measuring line and a plumb line a builder checks whether a wall is straight. Jerusalem will be measured with the same standard as Samaria and Ahab’s house. And as you wipe out a dirty dish and turn it upside down, so Jerusalem will be emptied.
> Manasseh also had many innocent people killed. According to an old Jewish tradition, even the prophet Isaiah is said to have been killed under Manasseh. This is not in the Bible.
> 2 Chronicles 33:11–17 also tells that Manasseh was later taken captive to Babylon, turned back to God there, prayed to God, and was heard. Even for such an evil person there was still a way back. The “Prayer of Manasseh” is an old prayer of repentance that is printed in an appendix in some Bibles.

## Manasseh’s death (Verses 17–18)

[17] The rest of the things Manasseh did,
all that he did, and the sin that he committed,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[18] Manasseh died and joined his fathers.
He was buried in the garden of his own house, in the garden of Uzza.
And his son Amon became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Manasseh is not buried in the royal tombs in David’s city, but in the garden of his palace. No one knows who Uzza was.

---

## Amon, king of Judah (Verses 19–26)

[19] Amon was 22 years old when he became king,
and he ruled for two years in Jerusalem.
His mother’s name was Meshullemeth the daughter of Haruz, from Jotbah.
[20] He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes,
as his father Manasseh had done.
[21] He walked in all the ways that his father had walked in.
He served the idols that his father had served,
and he worshiped them.
[22] He left the LORD, the God of his fathers,
and did not walk in the way of the LORD.
[23] The servants of Amon plotted against him
and killed the king in his own house.
[24] But the people of the land killed all those who had plotted against King Amon.
And the people of the land made his son Josiah king in his place.
[25] The rest of the things Amon did,
aren’t they written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
[26] He was buried in his tomb in the garden of Uzza.
And his son Josiah became king in his place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Amon continues on his father’s way. After only two years he is murdered.
> “The people of the land” were probably the free men of Judah who owned land. They punish the murderers and make sure that David’s family stays on the throne.
> The new king is a child of eight years: Josiah. He will become one of the best kings of Judah. 300 years before, a prophet had already named him (1 Kings 13:2).
> Amon ruled from about 642 to 640 before Christ.
