# Joshua – Chapter 19
### The territories of the other tribes

---

## Simeon (Verses 1–9)

[1] The second lot came out for Simeon, for the tribe of the children of Simeon by their clans.
Their inheritance was in the middle of the inheritance of the children of Judah.
[2] For their inheritance they had: Beersheba (or Sheba), Moladah,
[3] Hazar Shual, Balah, Ezem,
[4] Eltolad, Bethul, Hormah,
[5] Ziklag, Beth Marcaboth, Hazar Susah,
[6] Beth Lebaoth, and Sharuhen.
Thirteen cities with their villages.
[7] Ain, Rimmon, Ether, and Ashan.
Four cities with their villages.
[8] And all the villages around these cities as far as Baalath Beer, Ramah of the South.
This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Simeon by their clans.
[9] The inheritance of the children of Simeon came out of the share of the children of Judah.
For the share of the children of Judah was too large for them.
So the children of Simeon received their inheritance in the middle of Judah’s inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon gets no area of its own, but cities in the middle of Judah. This fulfills what Jacob said before he died: Simeon will be scattered in Israel (Genesis 49:7).
> Later, the tribe of Simeon became completely part of the tribe of Judah. That is why it is no longer mentioned in the blessing of Moses in Deuteronomy 33.
> Judah had too much land. It shares with its smaller brother tribe.
> If you count “Sheba” as its own place, there are fourteen names in verses 2–6. But “Beersheba (or Sheba)” probably means the same place. Then the number 13 is right.

## Zebulun (Verses 10–16)

[10] The third lot came up for the children of Zebulun by their clans.
The border of their inheritance went as far as Sarid.
[11] Their border went up to the west as far as Maralah and reached Dabbesheth.
It reached the brook that is opposite Jokneam.
[12] From Sarid it turned east, toward the sunrise, to the border of Chisloth Tabor.
It went out to Daberath and went up to Japhia.
[13] From there it went along east to Gath Hepher and to Ethkazin.
It went out at Rimmon, which stretches to Neah.
[14] The border turned around it on the north to Hannathon
and ended at the valley of Iphtah El.
[15] Also Kattath, Nahalal, Shimron, Idalah, and Bethlehem.
Twelve cities with their villages.
[16] This is the inheritance of the children of Zebulun by their clans, these cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zebulun received an area in Galilee in the north. Later, the town of Nazareth, where Jesus grew up, lay in this area.
> Gath Hepher was the home of the prophet Jonah (2 Kings 14:25).
> The Bethlehem here is a different town in Galilee, not the Bethlehem in Judah where David and Jesus were born.

---

## Issachar (Verses 17–23)

[17] The fourth lot came out for Issachar, for the children of Issachar by their clans.
[18] Their territory included Jezreel, Chesulloth, Shunem,
[19] Hapharaim, Shion, Anaharath,
[20] Rabbith, Kishion, Ebez,
[21] Remeth, Engannim, En Haddah, and Beth Pazzez.
[22] The border reached Tabor, Shahazumah, and Beth Shemesh.
And their border ended at the Jordan.
Sixteen cities with their villages.
[23] This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Issachar by their clans, the cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Issachar received the fertile plain of Jezreel. Tabor is a striking, round mountain. Many Christians believe that the transfiguration of Jesus took place there (Mark 9:2).
> In Shunem lived the woman whose son the prophet Elisha raised from the dead (2 Kings 4).

---

## Asher (Verses 24–31)

[24] The fifth lot came out for the tribe of the children of Asher by their clans.
[25] Their territory included Helkath, Hali, Beten, Achshaph,
[26] Allammelech, Amad, and Mishal.
It reached Carmel in the west and Shihorlibnath.
[27] It turned east, toward the sunrise, to Beth Dagon,
and reached Zebulun and the valley of Iphtah El in the north, Beth Emek, and Neiel.
It went out to Cabul on the left side,
[28] and Ebron, Rehob, Hammon, and Kanah, as far as great Sidon.
[29] The border turned to Ramah, to the walled city of Tyre.
And the border turned to Hosah and ended at the sea, by the region of Achzib,
[30] also Ummah, Aphek, and Rehob.
Twenty-two cities with their villages.
[31] This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Asher by their clans, these cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Asher received the coast in the north, as far as the great trading cities of Sidon and Tyre in what is now Lebanon. Israel never really ruled these cities.
> Carmel is a mountain range by the sea, near the modern city of Haifa. Later, the prophet Elijah had his great contest with the prophets of Baal there (1 Kings 18).

## Naphtali (Verses 32–39)

[32] The sixth lot came out for the children of Naphtali, for the children of Naphtali by their clans.
[33] Their border went from Heleph, from the oak in Zaanannim,
Adami-nekeb, and Jabneel, to Lakkum.
And it ended at the Jordan.
[34] The border turned west to Aznoth Tabor
and went out from there to Hukkok.
It reached Zebulun on the south, Asher on the west,
and Judah at the Jordan toward the sunrise.
[35] The walled cities were Ziddim, Zer, Hammath, Rakkath, Chinnereth,
[36] Adamah, Ramah, Hazor,
[37] Kedesh, Edrei, En Hazor,
[38] Iron, Migdal El, Horem, Beth Anath, and Beth Shemesh.
Nineteen cities with their villages.
[39] This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Naphtali by their clans, the cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Naphtali received the land by the Sea of Galilee (Chinnereth) and north of it. Later, Jesus preached and did miracles here a lot. Matthew quotes the prophet Isaiah about it: The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, the people who sat in darkness, have seen a great light (Matthew 4:13–16).
> “Judah at the Jordan” in the east is a puzzle, because Judah lay far to the south. Maybe a place with a similar name is meant. The Greek translation does not have these words.

## Dan (Verses 40–48)

[40] The seventh lot came out for the tribe of the children of Dan by their clans.
[41] The territory of their inheritance included Zorah, Eshtaol, Irshemesh,
[42] Shaalabbin, Aijalon, Ithlah,
[43] Elon, Timnah, Ekron,
[44] Eltekeh, Gibbethon, Baalath,
[45] Jehud, Bene Berak, Gath Rimmon,
[46] Me Jarkon, and Rakkon, with the territory opposite Joppa.
[47] The territory of the children of Dan went out beyond them.
For the children of Dan went up and fought against Leshem.
They took it and struck it with the edge of the sword.
They took it as their own and lived in it.
And they called Leshem “Dan,” after the name of their forefather Dan.
[48] This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Dan by their clans, these cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The territory went out beyond them”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew sentence is hard to understand. Many translate: “The territory was lost to them,” because the Amorites pressed them hard (Judges 1:34).
> Dan first received an area on the coast, near the modern city of Tel Aviv. “Joppa” is today’s Jaffa. “Bene Berak” still exists as a city today.
> Because they could not hold on there, they moved far to the north and took the city of Leshem (also called Laish). This story is told in detail in Judges 18. After that, people said for all of Israel: from Dan to Beersheba (Judges 20:1), that is, from the north to the south.
> Zorah belonged to Dan. Samson came from there (Judges 13:2).

## Joshua’s inheritance (Verses 49–51)

[49] So they finished dividing the land as an inheritance by its borders.
And the children of Israel gave Joshua, the son of Nun, an inheritance among them.
[50] As the LORD had commanded, they gave him the city he asked for:
Timnathserah in the hill country of Ephraim.
He built the city and lived there.
[51] These are the inheritances that Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun,
and the heads of the families of the tribes of the children of Israel gave out by lot,
in Shiloh, in front of the LORD, at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
So they finished dividing the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua takes his inheritance last. Only when everyone else has been provided for does he receive his piece of land. And he does not take a large, splendid city, but a place that he must first build himself. He is a leader who thinks of others first.
> With this chapter, the dividing of the land is complete. The promise to Abraham is fulfilled: His descendants have a land where they can live.
