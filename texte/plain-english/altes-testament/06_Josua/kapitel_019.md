# Joshua – Chapter 19
### The territories of the other tribes

---

## Simeon (Verses 1–9)

[1] The second lot came out for Simeon.
It was for the tribe of Simeon and its clans.
Their land lay in the middle of the land of the people of Judah.
[2] These cities became their land: Beersheba (or Sheba), Moladah,
[3] Hazar Shual, Balah, Ezem,
[4] Eltolad, Bethul, Hormah,
[5] Ziklag, Beth Marcaboth, Hazar Susah,
[6] Beth Lebaoth, and Sharuhen.
That makes 13 cities with their villages.
[7] Ain, Rimmon, Ether, and Ashan.
That makes 4 cities with their villages.
[8] They also got all the villages around these cities, as far as Baalath Beer.
Baalath Beer is Ramah of the South.
This is the land of the tribe of Simeon and its clans.
[9] The land of the people of Simeon was taken from the share of the people of Judah.
The share of the people of Judah was too large for them.
So the people of Simeon received their land in the middle of Judah’s land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon gets no area of its own, but cities in the middle of Judah. This fulfills what Jacob said before he died: Simeon will be scattered in Israel (Genesis 49:7).
> Later, the tribe of Simeon became completely part of the tribe of Judah. That is why it is no longer mentioned in the blessing of Moses in Deuteronomy 33.
> Judah had too much land. It shares with its smaller brother tribe.
> If you count “Sheba” as its own place, there are fourteen names in verses 2–6. But “Beersheba (or Sheba)” probably means the same place. Then the number 13 is right.

---

## Zebulun (Verses 10–16)

[10] The third lot came up for the people of Zebulun and their clans.
The border of their land went as far as Sarid.
[11] From there their border went up west to Maralah and reached Dabbesheth.
It reached the brook across from Jokneam.
[12] From Sarid the border turned east, toward the sunrise,
to the border of Chisloth Tabor.
It came out at Daberath and went up to Japhia.
[13] From there it went on east to Gath Hepher and to Ethkazin.
It came out at Rimmon, which reaches to Neah.
[14] On the north, the border turned around to Hannathon.
It ended at the valley of Iphtah El.
[15] Kattath, Nahalal, Shimron, Idalah, and Bethlehem also belonged to them.
That makes 12 cities with their villages.
[16] This is the land of the people of Zebulun and their clans.
These cities and their villages were theirs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zebulun received an area in Galilee in the north. Later, the town of Nazareth, where Jesus grew up, lay in this area.
> Gath Hepher was the home of the prophet Jonah (2 Kings 14:25).
> The Bethlehem here is a different town in Galilee, not the Bethlehem in Judah where David and Jesus were born.

---

## Issachar (Verses 17–23)

[17] The fourth lot came out for Issachar.
It was for the people of Issachar and their clans.
[18] Their land reached to Jezreel, Chesulloth, Shunem,
[19] Hapharaim, Shion, Anaharath,
[20] Rabbith, Kishion, Ebez,
[21] Remeth, Engannim, En Haddah, and Beth Pazzez.
[22] The border reached Tabor, Shahazumah, and Beth Shemesh.
Their border ended at the Jordan.
That makes 16 cities with their villages.
[23] This is the land of the tribe of Issachar and its clans.
These cities and their villages were theirs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Issachar received the fertile plain of Jezreel. Tabor is a striking, round mountain. Many Christians believe that the transfiguration of Jesus took place there (Mark 9:2).
> In Shunem lived the woman whose son the prophet Elisha raised from the dead (2 Kings 4).

---

## Asher (Verses 24–31)

[24] The fifth lot came out for the tribe of Asher and its clans.
[25] Their border took in Helkath, Hali, Beten, Achshaph,
[26] Allammelech, Amad, and Mishal.
In the west it reached Carmel and Shihorlibnath.
[27] Then it turned east, toward the sunrise, to Beth Dagon.
It reached Zebulun and the valley of Iphtah El.
In the north it went to Beth Emek and Neiel.
It came out at Cabul on the left side,
[28] and at Ebron, Rehob, Hammon, and Kanah, as far as the great city of Sidon.
[29] The border turned to Ramah and to the strong, walled city of Tyre.
Then it turned to Hosah.
It ended at the sea, in the area of Achzib.
[30] Ummah, Aphek, and Rehob also belonged to them.
That makes 22 cities with their villages.
[31] This is the land of the tribe of Asher and its clans.
These cities and their villages were theirs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Asher received the coast in the north, as far as the great trading cities of Sidon and Tyre in what is now Lebanon. Israel never really ruled these cities.
> Carmel is a mountain range by the sea, near the modern city of Haifa. Later, the prophet Elijah had his great contest with the prophets of Baal there (1 Kings 18).

## Naphtali (Verses 32–39)

[32] The sixth lot came out for the people of Naphtali.
It was for the people of Naphtali and their clans.
[33] Their border went from Heleph, from the oak tree in Zaanannim,
past Adami-nekeb and Jabneel, to Lakkum.
It ended at the Jordan.
[34] Then the border turned west to Aznoth Tabor.
From there it came out at Hukkok.
In the south it reached Zebulun.
In the west it reached Asher.
And in the east, toward the sunrise, it reached Judah at the Jordan.
[35] The strong, walled cities were Ziddim, Zer, Hammath, Rakkath, Chinnereth,
[36] Adamah, Ramah, Hazor,
[37] Kedesh, Edrei, En Hazor,
[38] Iron, Migdal El, Horem, Beth Anath, and Beth Shemesh.
That makes 19 cities with their villages.
[39] This is the land of the tribe of Naphtali and its clans.
These cities and their villages were theirs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Naphtali received the land by the Sea of Galilee (Chinnereth) and north of it. Later, Jesus preached and did miracles here a lot. Matthew quotes the prophet Isaiah about it: The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, the people who sat in darkness, have seen a great light (Matthew 4:13–16).
> “Judah at the Jordan” in the east is a puzzle, because Judah lay far to the south. Maybe a place with a similar name is meant. The Greek translation does not have these words.

## Dan (Verses 40–48)

[40] The seventh lot came out for the tribe of Dan and its clans.
[41] The border of their land took in Zorah, Eshtaol, Irshemesh,
[42] Shaalabbin, Aijalon, Ithlah,
[43] Elon, Timnah, Ekron,
[44] Eltekeh, Gibbethon, Baalath,
[45] Jehud, Bene Berak, Gath Rimmon,
[46] Me Jarkon, and Rakkon, with the land across from Joppa.
[47] The territory of the people of Dan went out beyond them.
The people of Dan went up and fought against Leshem.
They took the city and killed its people with the sword.
They took it as their own and lived there.
They called Leshem “Dan.”
They named it after their ancestor Dan.
[48] This is the land of the tribe of Dan and its clans.
These cities and their villages were theirs.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The territory of the people of Dan went out beyond them”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew sentence is hard to understand. Many translate: “The territory was lost to them,” because the Amorites pressed them hard (Judges 1:34).
> Dan first received an area on the coast, near the modern city of Tel Aviv. “Joppa” is today’s Jaffa. “Bene Berak” still exists as a city today.
> Because they could not hold on there, they moved far to the north and took the city of Leshem (also called Laish). This story is told in detail in Judges 18. After that, people said for all of Israel: from Dan to Beersheba (Judges 20:1), that is, from the north to the south.
> Zorah belonged to Dan. Samson came from there (Judges 13:2).

## Joshua’s inheritance (Verses 49–51)

[49] So they finished dividing up the land.
Every tribe got its land with its borders.
Then the people of Israel gave Joshua, the son of Nun, land of his own among them.
[50] The LORD had commanded it.
They gave Joshua the city he asked for:
Timnathserah in the hill country of Ephraim.
Joshua built up the city and lived there.
[51] Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun,
and the heads of the families of the tribes of Israel divided up the land.
They did it by lot, in Shiloh, in front of the LORD,
at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
These are the shares they gave out.
So they finished dividing the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua takes his inheritance last. Only when everyone else has been provided for does he receive his piece of land. And he does not take a large, splendid city, but a place that he must first build himself. He is a leader who thinks of others first.
> With this chapter, the dividing of the land is complete. The promise to Abraham is fulfilled: His descendants have a land where they can live.
