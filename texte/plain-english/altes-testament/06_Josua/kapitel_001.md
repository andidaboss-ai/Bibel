# Joshua – Chapter 1
### Be strong and courageous!

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Joshua?

The book of Joshua is named after its main person: Joshua, who came after Moses as leader. The name “Joshua” means “The LORD saves.” It is the same name as “Jesus” in its Hebrew form.
The book tells how Israel finally comes into the promised land. The people cross the Jordan, the walls of Jericho fall, and the land is divided among the twelve tribes. What God promised to Abraham now comes true.
The book also has many reports of war. Some of them are very hard to bear, because women and children are killed too. We do not leave these passages out. We explain them honestly. Many scholars also think that the conquest really happened more slowly and with less violence than the book describes in its language. One thing is clear: No one may use these reports to justify violence or war today.
At the end of the book, Joshua lets the people choose whom they want to serve. And he says the famous sentence: “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD” (Joshua 24:15).
In the Jewish Bible, the book of Joshua belongs to the “Former Prophets.” In Christian Bibles, it stands at the beginning of the “History Books.”

## God speaks to Joshua (Verses 1–9)

[1] After the death of Moses, the servant of the LORD,
the LORD spoke to Joshua, the son of Nun, who had served Moses. He said,
[2] “Moses my servant is dead.
So now get up and go across this Jordan, you and all these people,
into the land that I am giving to them, to the people of Israel.
[3] I have given you every place that the sole of your foot will step on,
as I told Moses.
[4] From the wilderness and this Lebanon to the great river, the river Euphrates,
all the land of the Hittites,
and to the great sea in the west, where the sun goes down, will be your border.
[5] No one will be able to stand against you all the days of your life.
As I was with Moses, so I will be with you.
I will not fail you, and I will not leave you.
[6] Be strong and courageous!
For you will lead this people to receive the land as their inheritance,
the land that I swore to their fathers to give them.
[7] Only be strong and very courageous!
Be careful to do everything the law says that Moses my servant commanded you.
Do not turn from it to the right or to the left,
so that you may have success wherever you go.
[8] This book of the law must not leave your mouth.
You must think about it day and night,
so that you are careful to do everything that is written in it.
For then you will make your way go well,
and then you will have success.
[9] Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous!
Don’t be afraid. Don’t lose heart,
for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses, the great leader, is dead. For the people, this is a great loss. Now Joshua has to fill very big shoes.
> God tells him three times: “Be strong and courageous!” Joshua is probably afraid. But God promises: “As I was with Moses, so I will be with you.” Joshua does not have to be like Moses. He only has to trust God.
> The strength does not come from Joshua himself. It comes from two sources: from God being with him (“the LORD your God is with you”) and from God’s word (“think about it day and night”).
> “Think about it” here means: think about God’s word again and again, say it quietly to yourself, and keep it in your heart. Psalm 1 says the same about a happy person.
> The large area up to the Euphrates is a promise that Israel reached only for a short time, under King Solomon. It is not a map for politics today.
> To this day, verse 9 comforts many people, for example before a hard task, a move, or an operation.

## Get ready! (Verses 10–11)

[10] Then Joshua commanded the officers of the people:
[11] “Go through the middle of the camp and command the people:
‘Get food ready for the journey.
For in three days you will cross this Jordan,
to go in and take the land
that the LORD your God is giving you to own.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua does not wait. He gives the first commands right away. God’s word has given him courage.

---

## The tribes east of the Jordan keep their word (Verses 12–18)

[12] Joshua spoke to the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh. He said,
[13] “Remember the word that Moses, the servant of the LORD, commanded you, when he said,
‘The LORD your God gives you rest and will give you this land.
[14] Your wives, your little ones, and your livestock will stay in the land
that Moses gave you beyond the Jordan.
But all of you brave warriors must cross over armed in front of your brothers and help them,
[15] until the LORD has given your brothers rest, as he has given you,
and until they also have taken the land that the LORD your God is giving them.
Then you may return to the land that belongs to you and own it,
the land that Moses, the servant of the LORD, gave you beyond the Jordan, toward the sunrise.’”
[16] They answered Joshua,
“We will do everything you have commanded us,
and we will go wherever you send us.
[17] Just as we listened to Moses in everything, so we will listen to you.
Only may the LORD your God be with you, as he was with Moses.
[18] Whoever rebels against your command
and does not listen to your words in everything you command him must be put to death.
Only be strong and courageous!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua reminds the two and a half tribes of their promise from Numbers 32: They already have their land, but they still fight together with their brothers.
> And they keep their word. They stand behind the new leader. At the end, they even tell him what God told him: “Only be strong and courageous!” So Joshua is encouraged not only by God but also by his people.
> The harsh threat in verse 18 shows how serious the situation was before a war. Unity was a matter of life and death.
