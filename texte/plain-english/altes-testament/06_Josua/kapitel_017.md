# Joshua – Chapter 17
### The territory of Manasseh

---

## The clans of Manasseh (Verses 1–2)

[1] This was the lot for the tribe of Manasseh, for he was the firstborn of Joseph.
Machir, the firstborn of Manasseh, the father of Gilead, was a man of war.
That is why he received Gilead and Bashan.
[2] And this lot was for the rest of the people of Manasseh by their clans:
for the children of Abiezer, for the children of Helek, for the children of Asriel,
for the children of Shechem, for the children of Hepher, and for the children of Shemida.
These were the male descendants of Manasseh the son of Joseph, by their clans.

> **What does this mean?**
> Machir had already received land east of the Jordan (Chapter 13). Now the other clans of Manasseh receive land west of the Jordan.
> Later, Gideon, one of the best-known judges, came from the clan of Abiezer (Judges 6:11).

---

## The daughters of Zelophehad receive their inheritance (Verses 3–6)

[3] But Zelophehad, the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh,
had no sons, only daughters.
These are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah.
[4] They came to Eleazar the priest, to Joshua the son of Nun, and to the leaders and said,
“The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brothers.”
So, as the LORD had commanded, he gave them an inheritance among their father’s brothers.
[5] So ten shares fell to Manasseh,
in addition to the land of Gilead and Bashan, which is beyond the Jordan.
[6] For the daughters of Manasseh received an inheritance among his sons.
And the land of Gilead belonged to the rest of the sons of Manasseh.

> **What does this mean?**
> We know the five sisters from Numbers 27 and 36. Moses had promised them an inheritance. Now, years later, they confidently remind the leaders of it. And the promise is kept.
> The ten shares: five for the clans of the sons (without Hepher) and five for the daughters of Zelophehad. The women receive land just like the men.

---

## The borders of Manasseh (Verses 7–13)

[7] The border of Manasseh went from Asher to Michmethath, which is opposite Shechem.
And the border went along to the right, to the people of En Tappuah.
[8] The land of Tappuah belonged to Manasseh,
but the town of Tappuah on the border of Manasseh belonged to the people of Ephraim.
[9] The border went down to the brook of Kanah, south of the brook.
These cities belonged to Ephraim, among the cities of Manasseh.
The border of Manasseh was on the north side of the brook and ended at the sea.
[10] What lay to the south belonged to Ephraim, and what lay to the north belonged to Manasseh.
The sea was its border.
They reached Asher on the north and Issachar on the east.
[11] In Issachar and in Asher, Manasseh had three heights:
Beth Shean and its towns, Ibleam and its towns,
the people of Dor and its towns,
the people of Endor and its towns,
the people of Taanach and its towns,
and the people of Megiddo and its towns.
[12] But the people of Manasseh could not drive out the people of those cities.
The Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.
[13] When the people of Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor.
But they did not drive them out completely.

> **What does this mean?**
> “To the right” often means “to the south” in the Bible, because people faced east, toward the sunrise, when they named directions.
> “The three heights”: It is not clear what exactly this means. Maybe it means three cities on hills from this list.
> Beth Shean, Megiddo, and Taanach were strong, walled cities in the fertile plain. Manasseh could not take them. Again the Bible honestly says: The conquest was not complete.
> Later, the woman who called up the dead, whom King Saul visited, lived in Endor (1 Samuel 28).
> Forced labor for people of another nation does not fit our understanding of human dignity today.

## The children of Joseph want more land (Verses 14–18)

[14] The children of Joseph spoke to Joshua. They said,
“Why have you given me only one lot and one share as an inheritance?
We are a large people, because the LORD has blessed us so far.”
[15] Joshua said to them,
“If you are a large people,
go up to the forest and clear land for yourselves there,
in the land of the Perizzites and of the Rephaim,
since the hill country of Ephraim is too small for you.”
[16] The children of Joseph said,
“The hill country is not enough for us.
And all the Canaanites who live in the land of the valley have chariots of iron,
both those in Beth Shean and its towns and those in the valley of Jezreel.”
[17] Joshua spoke to the house of Joseph, to Ephraim and to Manasseh. He said,
“You are a large people and have great power.
You will not have just one lot.
[18] The hill country will be yours too.
Even though it is a forest, you will cut it down, and it will be yours to its farthest edges.
For you will drive out the Canaanites, even though they have chariots of iron and are strong.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The children of Joseph complain: We are so many, we need more land! Joshua answers wisely: If you are so many and so strong, then work! Clear the forest.
> They are afraid of the iron chariots in the plain. But Joshua gives them courage: You are strong enough.
> Joshua himself came from the tribe of Ephraim. Still, he does not favor his own tribe. He does not give it land as a gift. He challenges it.
> The hill country was still thickly wooded at that time. Archaeologists have found that around 1200 before Christ many new small villages came into being in the hill country. Many scholars see this as the beginnings of Israel.
