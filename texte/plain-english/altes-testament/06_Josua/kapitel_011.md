# Joshua – Chapter 11
### The victory in the north

---

## The kings of the north join together (Verses 1–5)

[1] When Jabin, king of Hazor, heard about it,
he sent messengers to Jobab king of Madon, to the king of Shimron, to the king of Achshaph,
[2] and to the kings who lived in the north, in the hill country, in the Arabah south of Chinneroth,
in the lowland, and on the heights of Dor in the west,
[3] to the Canaanite in the east and in the west, to the Amorite, the Hittite, the Perizzite,
the Jebusite in the hill country,
and the Hivite below Mount Hermon in the land of Mizpah.
[4] They went out, they and all their armies with them,
many people, as many as the sand on the seashore,
with very many horses and chariots.
[5] All these kings met together.
They came and camped together at the waters of Merom, to fight against Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the south comes the north. Hazor was the largest city in the land of Canaan. Archaeologists have found huge ruins there.
> The enemies have horses and chariots, the most modern weapons of their time. Israel had nothing like that.
> “Chinneroth” is the Sea of Galilee.

---

## The victory at the waters of Merom (Verses 6–9)

[6] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Don’t be afraid of them.
For tomorrow at this time I will give them all over to Israel, killed.
You must hamstring their horses and burn their chariots with fire.”
To hamstring means: to cut a tendon in the leg, so that the horse can no longer run.
[7] So Joshua came on them suddenly with all the warriors, at the waters of Merom, and attacked them.
[8] The LORD gave them into the hand of Israel.
They struck them and chased them as far as great Sidon and Misrephoth Maim,
and as far as the valley of Mizpah in the east.
They struck them until no one of them was left.
[9] Joshua did to them as the LORD had told him.
He hamstrung their horses and burned their chariots with fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hamstringing the horses meant they could no longer be used for war. This is cruel to the animals.
> Why should Israel not keep the horses and chariots? Israel was meant to trust in God, not in weapons. The law for the king says this too (Deuteronomy 17:16). And Psalm 20:7 (in some Bibles 20:8) says that some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the LORD our God.

---

## Hazor is destroyed (Verses 10–15)

[10] At that time Joshua turned back and took Hazor.
He struck its king with the sword.
For Hazor used to be the head of all those kingdoms.
[11] They struck every person in it with the edge of the sword
and completely destroyed them.
No one was left who breathed.
And he burned Hazor with fire.
[12] Joshua took all the cities of those kings, and all their kings.
He struck them with the edge of the sword and completely destroyed them,
as Moses, the servant of the LORD, had commanded.
[13] But Israel did not burn any of the cities that stood on their hills.
Joshua burned only Hazor.
[14] The children of Israel took all the goods of these cities and the livestock for themselves.
But they struck every person with the edge of the sword until they had destroyed them.
They left no one who breathed.
[15] As the LORD had commanded Moses his servant, so Moses commanded Joshua.
And Joshua did so.
He left nothing undone of all that the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Cities on their hills”: Old cities were built again and again on the ruins of the earlier cities. This made hills that today are called a “tell.”
> In Hazor, archaeologists have found a thick layer of ash from the time around 1200 before Christ. But who destroyed the city at that time is disputed.
> Here too: The reports of the destruction of whole cities are hard to bear. They belong to their time and must never justify violence.

---

## The whole land is taken (Verses 16–20)

[16] So Joshua took this whole land:
the hill country, all the South, all the land of Goshen, the lowland, the Arabah,
the hill country of Israel and its lowland,
[17] from Mount Halak, which goes up toward Seir, to Baal Gad in the valley of Lebanon below Mount Hermon.
He captured all their kings, struck them, and put them to death.
[18] Joshua made war with all those kings for a long time.
[19] There was not one city that made peace with the children of Israel,
except the Hivites who lived in Gibeon.
They took all the others in battle.
[20] For it came from the LORD that their hearts became hard,
so that they came against Israel in battle,
so that he might completely destroy them,
so that they would find no mercy, but that he might destroy them,
as the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Halak” means “bare.” It is a bare mountain in the south of the land.
> “For a long time”: The conquest did not take a few days but many years. Jewish interpreters counted about seven years.
> Verse 19 is remarkable: Peace would have been possible. The Gibeonites showed it. But no other city chose this way.
> “Their hearts became hard” because of God: This is hard to understand. The same was said about Pharaoh. The Bible wants to say: Even the resistance of the peoples was not outside God’s plan. This does not mean that the people had no choice. Rahab and the Gibeonites chose differently.

---

## The Anakim (Verses 21–23)

[21] At that time Joshua came and wiped out the Anakim from the hill country,
from Hebron, from Debir, from Anab, from all the hill country of Judah, and from all the hill country of Israel.
Joshua completely destroyed them and their cities.
[22] No Anakim were left in the land of the children of Israel.
Only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod did some remain.
[23] So Joshua took the whole land,
just as the LORD had said to Moses.
And Joshua gave it to Israel as their inheritance, by their divisions, by their tribes.
Then the land had rest from war.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Anakim were the “giants” that the spies were so afraid of 40 years earlier (Numbers 13:33). Now they are defeated.
> Some remained in Gath. Later, the giant Goliath, whom David defeated, came from Gath (1 Samuel 17:4).
> “The whole land”: This is a summary. Right in Chapter 13 it says that very much land is still left. The narrator means: The great battles are over, the most important areas belong to Israel.
> “The land had rest from war”: Peace at last. Now the land can be divided.
