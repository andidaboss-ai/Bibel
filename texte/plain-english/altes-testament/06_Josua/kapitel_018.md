# Joshua – Chapter 18
### The tent in Shiloh – the territory of Benjamin

---

## The Tent of Meeting comes to Shiloh (Verse 1)

[1] The whole community of the people of Israel gathered at Shiloh,
and they set up the Tent of Meeting there.
The land was under their control.

> **What does this mean?**
> Shiloh lies in the middle of the land, in the territory of Ephraim. The holy tent is set up here. For more than 200 years, Shiloh is the center of worship in Israel. Later, Hannah, the mother of the prophet Samuel, prayed here (1 Samuel 1).
> It is an important moment: God now lives in the middle of his land.

---

## Seven tribes still have no land (Verses 2–10)

[2] Among the people of Israel, there were still seven tribes that had not yet received their inheritance.
[3] Joshua said to the people of Israel,
“How long will you wait to go in and take the land
that the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has given you?
[4] Choose three men from each tribe.
I will send them out.
They will get up, walk through the land,
and describe it by the inheritance each tribe will receive.
Then they will come back to me.
[5] They will divide it into seven parts.
Judah will stay in its territory in the south,
and the house of Joseph will stay in its territory in the north.
[6] Describe the land in seven parts,
and bring the description here to me.
Then I will cast lots for you here in front of the LORD our God.
[7] But the Levites have no share among you,
for the priesthood of the LORD is their inheritance.
And Gad, Reuben, and the half-tribe of Manasseh have received their inheritance east of the Jordan,
which Moses, the servant of the LORD, gave them.”
[8] The men got up and went.
Joshua commanded those who went to describe the land:
“Go, walk through the land, describe it, and come back to me.
Then I will cast lots for you here in front of the LORD in Shiloh.”
[9] The men went and passed through the land.
They described it city by city, in seven parts, in a book.
And they came back to Joshua at the camp at Shiloh.
[10] Joshua cast lots for them in Shiloh in front of the LORD.
There Joshua divided the land among the people of Israel by their divisions.

> **What does this mean?**
> Seven tribes are still waiting. Joshua asks them: “How long will you wait to go in and take the land?” God has given them the land as a gift, but they also have to take it.
> This is a lesson for today too: God’s gifts want to be received. We must not just sit and wait.
> The 21 men are like land surveyors. They write everything down in a book. This is one of the first descriptions of a land survey in history.

## The borders of Benjamin (Verses 11–20)

[11] The lot of the tribe of the people of Benjamin came out, by their clans.
The territory of their lot lay between the people of Judah and the people of Joseph.
[12] Their border on the north side began at the Jordan.
The border went up along the side of Jericho on the north
and went up through the hill country to the west.
And it ended at the wilderness of Beth Aven.
[13] From there the border went along to Luz, to the side of Luz (that is, Bethel) on the south.
The border went down to Ataroth Addar, by the mountain south of Lower Beth Horon.
[14] The border went on and turned south on the west side,
from the mountain that lies south, in front of Beth Horon.
And it ended at Kiriath Baal (that is, Kiriath Jearim), a city of the people of Judah.
This was the west side.
[15] The south side began at the far edge of Kiriath Jearim.
The border went out to the west and went out to the spring of the waters of Nephtoah.
[16] The border went down to the far edge of the mountain that lies in front of the valley of the son of Hinnom,
which is at the north end of the valley of Rephaim.
It went down to the valley of Hinnom, along the south side of the Jebusite city,
and went down to En Rogel.
[17] It stretched north, went out at En Shemesh,
and went out to Geliloth, which is opposite the ascent of Adummim.
And it went down to the stone of Bohan, the son of Reuben.
[18] It went along to the side opposite the Arabah, on the north, and went down into the Arabah.
[19] The border went along to the side of Beth Hoglah, on the north.
And the border ended at the north bay of the Salt Sea, at the south end of the Jordan.
This was the southern border.
[20] And the Jordan was its border on the east side.
This was the inheritance of the people of Benjamin, by its borders all around, by their clans.

> **What does this mean?**
> Benjamin was a small tribe. It received a small but important area between the two large tribes of Judah and Ephraim.
> The southern border of Benjamin is exactly the northern border of Judah (Chapter 15). It runs along the valley of Hinnom, right past Jerusalem.
> Later, the first king of Israel, Saul, came from Benjamin (1 Samuel 9:1–2). And in the New Testament, the apostle Paul says of himself that he comes from the tribe of Benjamin (Philippians 3:5).

## The cities of Benjamin (Verses 21–28)

[21] The cities of the tribe of the people of Benjamin by their clans were:
Jericho, Beth Hoglah, Emek Keziz,
[22] Beth Arabah, Zemaraim, Bethel,
[23] Avvim, Parah, Ophrah,
[24] Chephar Ammoni, Ophni, and Geba.
That makes 12 cities with their villages.
[25] Gibeon, Ramah, Beeroth,
[26] Mizpeh, Chephirah, Mozah,
[27] Rekem, Irpeel, Taralah,
[28] Zelah, Eleph, the Jebusite city (that is, Jerusalem), Gibeath, and Kiriath.
That makes 14 cities with their villages.
This is the inheritance of the people of Benjamin by their clans.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Jerusalem is given to Benjamin. In Joshua 15:63 it was named with Judah. It lay exactly on the border between the two tribes.
> “Gibeath” is probably Gibeah, the hometown of King Saul.
> Gibeon also belongs to Benjamin. It is the city of the Gibeonites, with whom Israel had made a covenant (Chapter 9).
