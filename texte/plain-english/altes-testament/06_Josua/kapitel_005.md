# Joshua – Chapter 5
### A new beginning in Gilgal

---

## The kings lose courage (Verse 1)

[1] All the kings of the Amorites who lived beyond the Jordan to the west,
and all the kings of the Canaanites who lived by the sea,
heard how the LORD had dried up the water of the Jordan in front of the people of Israel until we had crossed over.
Then their hearts melted, and they had no more courage because of the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Until we had crossed over”: The narrator says “we.” He counts himself among the people who were there at that time.

---

## The circumcision (Verses 2–9)

[2] At that time, the LORD said to Joshua,
“Make knives out of flint
and circumcise the sons of Israel again, a second time.”
[3] So Joshua made himself flint knives
and circumcised the sons of Israel at the “hill of the foreskins.”
[4] This is the reason Joshua circumcised them:
All the people who came out of Egypt, all the males, all the men of war,
had died in the wilderness along the way, after they came out of Egypt.
[5] For all the people who came out had been circumcised.
But all the people who were born in the wilderness along the way, after they came out of Egypt,
had not been circumcised.
[6] For the people of Israel walked forty years in the wilderness,
until the whole nation, the men of war who came out of Egypt, had died,
because they did not listen to the voice of the LORD.
The LORD swore to them that he would not let them see the land
that the LORD swore to their fathers to give us,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
[7] Joshua circumcised their sons, whom the LORD raised up in their place.
For they were not circumcised, because no one had circumcised them on the way.
[8] When the whole nation was circumcised, they stayed in their places in the camp until they were healed.
[9] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Today I have rolled away the shame of Egypt from you.”
That is why the name of that place is Gilgal to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Circumcision is the sign of the covenant that God gave to Abraham (Genesis 17). In the wilderness, it was no longer done. Now, at the beginning of life in the land, the covenant is renewed.
> Flint knives were common for holy acts at that time, even when people already had metal.
> It is amazing: Right in enemy land, shortly before battle, all the men make themselves defenseless for a few days. They trust that God will protect them.
> “Gilgal” sounds like the Hebrew word “galal,” which means “to roll.” God has “rolled away” the shame. The “shame of Egypt” may mean the time of slavery, or the mocking claim that God led his people into the wilderness only to let them die there.

---

## The first Passover in the land (Verses 10–12)

[10] The people of Israel camped in Gilgal.
They kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month, in the evening, in the plains of Jericho.
[11] On the day after the Passover, they ate food that grew in the land:
bread without yeast and roasted grain, on that very day.
[12] The manna stopped on the next day, after they had eaten food from the land.
The people of Israel had no more manna.
That year they ate the fruit of the land of Canaan.

> **What does this mean?**
> For 40 years, God fed his people with manna every day. Now it stops. Not because God stops caring, but because the people are now in the land, and the land gives enough.
> Sometimes God provides through miracles, and sometimes through very normal things: through the harvest. Both are his gift.
> The first Passover in the land reminds them of the beginning: of the night of freedom in Egypt. Now the journey is over.

---

## The commander of the LORD’s army (Verses 13–15)

[13] When Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up.
Look! A man stood in front of him with his sword drawn in his hand.
Joshua went to him and asked him,
“Are you for us, or for our enemies?”
[14] He said, “No.
But I have come now as commander of the LORD’s army.”
Joshua fell on his face to the ground, worshiped, and asked him,
“What does my lord say to his servant?”
[15] The commander of the LORD’s army said to Joshua,
“Take off your sandals,
for the place where you are standing is holy.”
And Joshua did so.

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua asks: Are you for us or against us? And the answer is: “No.” The stranger belongs to neither side. He belongs to God. God is not on Joshua’s side; Joshua must be on God’s side.
> This is an important lesson for all times: No one can use God for their own wars and goals. The question is not: “Is God on our side?” but: “Are we on God’s side?”
> “Take off your sandals”: Moses heard almost the same words at the burning bush (Exodus 3:5). Joshua has a meeting with God much like Moses did.
> The Bible does not say who this commander was. Many think of an angel. Some Christian interpreters see Christ himself in him.
