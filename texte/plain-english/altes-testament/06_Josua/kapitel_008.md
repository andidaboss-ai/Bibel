# Joshua – Chapter 8
### Ai is taken – the altar on Mount Ebal

---

## God encourages Joshua (Verses 1–2)

[1] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Don’t be afraid, and don’t lose heart.
Take all the warriors with you. Get up and go up to Ai.
Look, I have given the king of Ai, his people, his city, and his land into your hand.
[2] Do to Ai and its king what you did to Jericho and its king.
Only this time, you may take its goods and its livestock for yourselves.
Set an ambush against the city, behind it.”
An ambush means: soldiers hide and attack by surprise.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the guilt comes a new beginning. God says again: “Don’t be afraid.” The matter with Achan is settled. God is with his people again.
> This time the Israelites may keep the goods. If Achan had waited, he would have received what he wanted.

---

## The plan with the ambush (Verses 3–13)

[3] So Joshua got up with all the warriors to go up to Ai.
Joshua chose 30,000 men, brave warriors, and sent them out at night.
[4] He commanded them,
“Look, you must lie in ambush against the city, behind the city.
Don’t go very far from the city, but all of you be ready.
[5] I and all the people with me will come near the city.
When they come out against us, as the first time, we will run away from them.
[6] They will come out after us until we have drawn them away from the city.
For they will say, ‘They are running away from us, like the first time.’
So we will run away from them,
[7] and you must rise up from the ambush and take the city.
For the LORD your God will give it into your hand.
[8] When you have taken the city, set the city on fire.
Do this as the LORD has said.
Look, I have commanded you.”
[9] Joshua sent them out, and they went to the place of the ambush.
They stayed between Bethel and Ai, west of Ai.
But Joshua stayed with the people that night.
[10] Joshua got up early in the morning and gathered the people.
He went up to Ai in front of the people, he and the elders of Israel.
[11] All the men of war who were with him went up and came near.
They came in front of the city and camped north of Ai.
There was a valley between him and Ai.
[12] He took about 5,000 men and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, west of the city.
[13] So they set up the people: the whole army north of the city,
and the ambush west of the city.
That night Joshua went into the middle of the valley.

> **What does this mean?**
> The numbers are confusing: In verse 3 there are 30,000 men, in verse 12 only 5,000 in the ambush. Interpreters explain this in different ways. Maybe there were two groups. Maybe the text was changed when it was copied over the centuries. The Hebrew word for “thousand” can also mean a military unit that was much smaller. Then the numbers would have been much smaller.
> Joshua uses the defeat from Chapter 7 as a trick: The men of Ai think Israel is running away again.

---

## The victory over Ai (Verses 14–23)

[14] When the king of Ai saw it,
the men of the city hurried, got up early, and went out to fight against Israel,
he and all his people, at the agreed time, facing the Arabah.
But he did not know that there was an ambush against him behind the city.
[15] Joshua and all Israel pretended to be beaten by them
and ran away on the road to the wilderness.
[16] All the people in the city were called together to chase them.
They chased Joshua and were drawn away from the city.
[17] Not one man was left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel.
They left the city open and chased Israel.
[18] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Stretch out the javelin in your hand toward Ai.
A javelin is a short spear.
For I will give it into your hand.”
So Joshua stretched out the javelin in his hand toward the city.
[19] As soon as he stretched out his hand, the men in the ambush quickly rose up from their place.
They ran, entered the city, and took it.
They quickly set the city on fire.
[20] When the men of Ai looked behind them, they saw it.
Look! The smoke of the city was rising up to the sky.
They had no way to run this way or that way.
And the people who had run toward the wilderness turned back against those chasing them.
[21] When Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had taken the city
and that the smoke of the city was rising,
they turned back and killed the men of Ai.
[22] The others came out of the city against them,
so the men of Ai were caught in the middle of Israel, some on this side and some on that side.
They struck them until not one of them was left or escaped.
[23] But they captured the king of Ai alive and brought him to Joshua.

> **What does this mean?**
> The stretched-out javelin reminds us of Moses, who held up his hands during the battle against the Amalekites (Exodus 17:11). It is a sign: God gives the victory.
> Bethel was only a few miles away. Its men also came to help Ai.

---

## Ai is destroyed (Verses 24–29)

[24] Israel finished killing all the people of Ai in the field, in the wilderness where they had chased them.
They had all fallen by the edge of the sword, until they were all dead.
Then all Israel went back to Ai and struck it with the edge of the sword.
[25] All who fell that day, men and women, were 12,000,
all the people of Ai.
[26] For Joshua did not pull back his hand, with which he stretched out the javelin,
until he had completely destroyed all the people of Ai.
[27] Israel took for themselves only the livestock and the goods of that city,
as the LORD had commanded Joshua.
[28] So Joshua burned Ai and made it a heap of ruins forever, an empty place, to this day.
[29] He hanged the king of Ai on a tree until evening.
At sundown, Joshua gave a command, and they took his body down from the tree
and threw it at the entrance of the city gate.
They piled a great heap of stones on it, which is still there to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again a whole city is destroyed, including the women. For us, this is terrible. What was said about Jericho is true here too: The Bible describes the war of its time. These reports must never be used to justify violence.
> For the time of Joshua, archaeologists have found no lived-in city at Ai (today “et-Tell”). There is much discussion about this. Some think the real Ai has not been found yet.
> The king’s body is taken down that same evening, as the law requires (Deuteronomy 21:22–23).

## The altar on Mount Ebal (Verses 30–35)

[30] Then Joshua built an altar to the LORD, the God of Israel, on Mount Ebal,
[31] as Moses, the servant of the LORD, had commanded the children of Israel,
as it is written in the book of the law of Moses:
an altar of uncut stones, on which no one had used an iron tool.
On it they offered burnt offerings to the LORD and sacrificed peace offerings.
[32] There he wrote on the stones a copy of the law of Moses,
which he wrote while the children of Israel watched.
[33] All Israel, with their elders, officers, and judges,
stood on both sides of the ark, facing the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD,
the foreigner as well as the one born in Israel.
Half of them stood in front of Mount Gerizim, and half of them in front of Mount Ebal,
as Moses, the servant of the LORD, had commanded at the beginning, so that they would bless the people of Israel.
[34] After that, he read all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse,
exactly as it is written in the book of the law.
[35] There was not one word of all that Moses had commanded
that Joshua did not read in front of the whole assembly of Israel,
including the women, the little ones, and the foreigners who lived among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the time of battles, Joshua stops. He does what Moses had commanded (Deuteronomy 27). The law is written on stones and read aloud to all the people.
> “There was not one word … that Joshua did not read”: Joshua reads everything without leaving anything out. That is also the goal of this Bible: to leave out no word.
> Again everyone is there: women, children, and foreigners. God’s word is for everyone.
> On Mount Ebal, archaeologists have found an old stone structure that some think is this altar. Others doubt it.
