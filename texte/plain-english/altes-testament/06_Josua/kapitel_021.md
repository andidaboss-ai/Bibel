# Joshua – Chapter 21
### The cities of the Levites – God keeps his word

---

## The Levites ask for their cities (Verses 1–8)

[1] Then the heads of the families of the Levites came to Eleazar the priest,
to Joshua the son of Nun, and to the heads of the families of the tribes of the children of Israel.
[2] They spoke to them at Shiloh in the land of Canaan. They said,
“The LORD commanded through Moses that we should be given cities to live in,
with their pasture lands for our livestock.”
[3] So the children of Israel gave the Levites these cities with their pasture lands
from their own inheritance, as the LORD had commanded.
[4] The lot came out for the clans of the Kohathites.
The children of Aaron the priest, who belonged to the Levites,
received thirteen cities by lot from the tribe of Judah, from the tribe of the Simeonites, and from the tribe of Benjamin.
[5] The rest of the children of Kohath received ten cities by lot
from the clans of the tribe of Ephraim, from the tribe of Dan, and from the half-tribe of Manasseh.
[6] The children of Gershon received thirteen cities by lot
from the clans of the tribe of Issachar, from the tribe of Asher, from the tribe of Naphtali,
and from the half-tribe of Manasseh in Bashan.
[7] The children of Merari, by their clans, received twelve cities
from the tribe of Reuben, from the tribe of Gad, and from the tribe of Zebulun.
[8] So the children of Israel gave these cities with their pasture lands to the Levites by lot,
as the LORD had commanded through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites have no territory of their own. But they need a place to live. So all the other tribes give them cities, as Moses had ordered (Numbers 35:1–8).
> The Levites were made up of three large families, named after the sons of Levi: Kohath, Gershon, and Merari. The priests, the descendants of Aaron, belonged to the family of Kohath.
> The priests received cities near Jerusalem. That fits well, because later the temple stood there.

---

## The cities of the priests (Verses 9–19)

[9] From the tribe of the children of Judah and from the tribe of the children of Simeon
they gave these cities, which are named here.
[10] They were for the children of Aaron, from the clans of the Kohathites, from the children of Levi.
For the first lot had fallen to them.
[11] They gave them Kiriath Arba, named after the father of Anak – that is Hebron –,
in the hill country of Judah, with its pasture lands around it.
[12] But they gave the fields of the city and its villages to Caleb the son of Jephunneh as his property.
[13] To the children of Aaron the priest they gave
Hebron with its pasture lands, the city of refuge for anyone who had killed a person,
Libnah with its pasture lands,
[14] Jattir with its pasture lands, Eshtemoa with its pasture lands,
[15] Holon with its pasture lands, Debir with its pasture lands,
[16] Ain with its pasture lands, Juttah with its pasture lands, and Beth Shemesh with its pasture lands.
Nine cities from those two tribes.
[17] From the tribe of Benjamin: Gibeon with its pasture lands, Geba with its pasture lands,
[18] Anathoth with its pasture lands, and Almon with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[19] All the cities of the children of Aaron, the priests, were thirteen cities with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hebron now belongs to both the priests and Caleb. The priests get the city to live in, and Caleb gets the fields and villages around it. That is how it is shared.
> Anathoth later was the hometown of the prophet Jeremiah. He came from a family of priests (Jeremiah 1:1).
> Nine and four make thirteen. The count is right.

---

## The cities of the other Kohathites (Verses 20–26)

[20] The clans of the children of Kohath, the Levites, the rest of the children of Kohath,
received the cities of their lot from the tribe of Ephraim.
[21] They gave them Shechem with its pasture lands in the hill country of Ephraim,
the city of refuge for anyone who had killed a person,
and Gezer with its pasture lands,
[22] Kibzaim with its pasture lands, and Beth Horon with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[23] From the tribe of Dan: Elteke with its pasture lands, Gibbethon with its pasture lands,
[24] Aijalon with its pasture lands, and Gath Rimmon with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[25] From the half-tribe of Manasseh: Taanach with its pasture lands and Gath Rimmon with its pasture lands.
Two cities.
[26] All the cities of the clans of the rest of the children of Kohath were ten, with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gath Rimmon is named twice, with Dan and with Manasseh. The second time, maybe a different place is meant. In 1 Chronicles 6:70 there is a different name at this place.
> Four, four, and two make ten.

---

## The cities of the Gershonites (Verses 27–33)

[27] To the children of Gershon, from the clans of the Levites, they gave
from the half-tribe of Manasseh: Golan in Bashan with its pasture lands, the city of refuge for anyone who had killed a person,
and Be Eshterah with its pasture lands.
Two cities.
[28] From the tribe of Issachar: Kishion with its pasture lands, Daberath with its pasture lands,
[29] Jarmuth with its pasture lands, and En Gannim with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[30] From the tribe of Asher: Mishal with its pasture lands, Abdon with its pasture lands,
[31] Helkath with its pasture lands, and Rehob with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[32] From the tribe of Naphtali: Kedesh in Galilee with its pasture lands, the city of refuge for anyone who had killed a person,
Hammothdor with its pasture lands, and Kartan with its pasture lands.
Three cities.
[33] All the cities of the Gershonites by their clans were thirteen cities with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two, four, four, and three make thirteen.
> The Gershonites lived in the north, in Galilee and in Bashan.

---

## The cities of the Merarites (Verses 34–40)

[34] To the clans of the children of Merari, the rest of the Levites, they gave
from the tribe of Zebulun: Jokneam with its pasture lands, Kartah with its pasture lands,
[35] Dimnah with its pasture lands, and Nahalal with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[36] From the tribe of Reuben: Bezer with its pasture lands, Jahaz with its pasture lands,
[37] Kedemoth with its pasture lands, and Mephaath with its pasture lands.
Four cities.
[38] From the tribe of Gad: Ramoth in Gilead with its pasture lands, the city of refuge for anyone who had killed a person,
and Mahanaim with its pasture lands,
[39] Heshbon with its pasture lands, and Jazer with its pasture lands.
Four cities in all.
[40] All these were the cities of the children of Merari by their clans, the rest of the clans of the Levites.
Their lot was twelve cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> Four, four, and four make twelve.
> Many Hebrew manuscripts do not have verses 36 and 37 about the cities of Reuben. Other manuscripts and the WEB do have them. Without them, the number twelve would not be right.

---

## 48 cities in all (Verses 41–42)

[41] All the cities of the Levites among the property of the children of Israel were 48 cities with their pasture lands.
[42] Each of these cities had its pasture lands around it.
It was this way with all these cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> 13 + 10 + 13 + 12 = 48. That is exactly as many as Moses had ordered (Numbers 35:7).
> Now the Levites lived spread out over the whole land. So they were close to the people everywhere and could teach them God’s law.

---

## God has fulfilled everything (Verses 43–45)

[43] So the LORD gave Israel the whole land that he had sworn to give to their fathers.
They took it and lived in it.
[44] The LORD gave them rest all around, just as he had sworn to their fathers.
Not one of all their enemies could stand against them.
The LORD gave all their enemies into their hand.
[45] Not one of all the good things that the LORD had promised to the house of Israel failed.
All of it came true.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important sentences in the whole book of Joshua: “Not one of all the good things that the LORD had promised to the house of Israel failed. All of it came true.”
> Hundreds of years earlier, God had promised Abraham a land. Now it is fulfilled. God keeps his word.
> Of course, we know from the chapters before that not all the peoples had been driven out yet. But the narrator wants to say: On God’s side, nothing is missing. If something is still open, it is not because of God.
> In hard times, many people have read this verse as a comfort: Even if I cannot see it yet, God keeps his promises.
