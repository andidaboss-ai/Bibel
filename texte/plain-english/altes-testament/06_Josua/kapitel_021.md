# Joshua – Chapter 21
### The cities of the Levites – God keeps his word

---

## The Levites ask for their cities (Verses 1–8)

[1] The heads of the families of the Levites came to Eleazar the priest
and to Joshua, the son of Nun.
They also came to the heads of the families of the tribes of Israel.
[2] They spoke to them at Shiloh, in the land of Canaan.
They said,
“The LORD gave a command through Moses.
He said that we should get cities to live in.
And we should get the pasture lands around them for our animals.”
[3] So the people of Israel gave the Levites these cities and their pasture lands.
They gave them from their own land, as the LORD had commanded.
[4] The first lot came out for the clans of the Kohathites.
The descendants of Aaron the priest, who were Levites,
received 13 cities by lot.
The cities came from the tribe of Judah, the tribe of Simeon, and the tribe of Benjamin.
[5] The other descendants of Kohath received 10 cities by lot.
The cities came from the clans of the tribe of Ephraim, from the tribe of Dan, and from the half-tribe of Manasseh.
[6] The descendants of Gershon received 13 cities by lot.
The cities came from the clans of the tribe of Issachar, from the tribe of Asher, from the tribe of Naphtali,
and from the half-tribe of Manasseh in Bashan.
[7] The descendants of Merari and their clans received 12 cities.
The cities came from the tribe of Reuben, the tribe of Gad, and the tribe of Zebulun.
[8] So the people of Israel gave these cities and their pasture lands to the Levites by lot.
The LORD had commanded this through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites have no territory of their own. But they need a place to live. So all the other tribes give them cities, as Moses had ordered (Numbers 35:1–8).
> The Levites were made up of three large families, named after the sons of Levi: Kohath, Gershon, and Merari. The priests, the descendants of Aaron, belonged to the family of Kohath.
> The priests received cities near Jerusalem. That fits well, because later the temple stood there.

---

## The cities of the priests (Verses 9–19)

[9] The tribe of Judah and the tribe of Simeon gave these cities.
Here are their names.
[10] The cities were for the descendants of Aaron.
They belonged to the clans of the Kohathites, who were descendants of Levi.
The first lot had come out for them.
[11] The tribes gave them Kiriath Arba, with the pasture lands around it.
It was named after the father of Anak.
Kiriath Arba is Hebron, in the hill country of Judah.
[12] But the fields of the city and its villages went to Caleb, the son of Jephunneh.
They became his own land.
[13] The descendants of Aaron the priest received these cities:
Hebron with its pasture lands.
Hebron was a city of refuge for a person who had killed someone.
They also received Libnah with its pasture lands,
[14] Jattir with its pasture lands, Eshtemoa with its pasture lands,
[15] Holon with its pasture lands, Debir with its pasture lands,
[16] Ain with its pasture lands, Juttah with its pasture lands, and Beth Shemesh with its pasture lands.
That makes 9 cities from those two tribes.
[17] From the tribe of Benjamin they received Gibeon with its pasture lands, Geba with its pasture lands,
[18] Anathoth with its pasture lands, and Almon with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[19] So the priests, the descendants of Aaron, received 13 cities in all, with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hebron now belongs to both the priests and Caleb. The priests get the city to live in, and Caleb gets the fields and villages around it. That is how it is shared.
> Anathoth later was the hometown of the prophet Jeremiah. He came from a family of priests (Jeremiah 1:1).
> Nine and four make thirteen. The count is right.

---

## The cities of the other Kohathites (Verses 20–26)

[20] Then there were the other clans of the descendants of Kohath.
They were Levites too.
By lot, they received cities from the tribe of Ephraim.
[21] The tribes gave them Shechem with its pasture lands, in the hill country of Ephraim.
Shechem was a city of refuge for a person who had killed someone.
They also gave them Gezer with its pasture lands,
[22] Kibzaim with its pasture lands, and Beth Horon with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[23] From the tribe of Dan they received Elteke with its pasture lands, Gibbethon with its pasture lands,
[24] Aijalon with its pasture lands, and Gath Rimmon with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[25] From the half-tribe of Manasseh they received Taanach with its pasture lands and Gath Rimmon with its pasture lands.
That makes 2 cities.
[26] So the clans of the other descendants of Kohath received 10 cities in all, with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gath Rimmon is named twice, with Dan and with Manasseh. The second time, maybe a different place is meant. In 1 Chronicles 6:70 there is a different name at this place.
> Four, four, and two make ten.

---

## The cities of the Gershonites (Verses 27–33)

[27] The descendants of Gershon belonged to the clans of the Levites.
From the half-tribe of Manasseh they received Golan in Bashan with its pasture lands.
Golan was a city of refuge for a person who had killed someone.
They also received Be Eshterah with its pasture lands.
That makes 2 cities.
[28] From the tribe of Issachar they received Kishion with its pasture lands, Daberath with its pasture lands,
[29] Jarmuth with its pasture lands, and En Gannim with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[30] From the tribe of Asher they received Mishal with its pasture lands, Abdon with its pasture lands,
[31] Helkath with its pasture lands, and Rehob with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[32] From the tribe of Naphtali they received Kedesh in Galilee with its pasture lands.
Kedesh was a city of refuge for a person who had killed someone.
They also received Hammothdor with its pasture lands, and Kartan with its pasture lands.
That makes 3 cities.
[33] So the Gershonites and their clans received 13 cities in all, with their pasture lands.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two, four, four, and three make thirteen.
> The Gershonites lived in the north, in Galilee and in Bashan.

---

## The cities of the Merarites (Verses 34–40)

[34] The clans of the descendants of Merari were the rest of the Levites.
From the tribe of Zebulun they received Jokneam with its pasture lands, Kartah with its pasture lands,
[35] Dimnah with its pasture lands, and Nahalal with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[36] From the tribe of Reuben they received Bezer with its pasture lands, Jahaz with its pasture lands,
[37] Kedemoth with its pasture lands, and Mephaath with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities.
[38] From the tribe of Gad they received Ramoth in Gilead with its pasture lands.
Ramoth was a city of refuge for a person who had killed someone.
They also received Mahanaim with its pasture lands,
[39] Heshbon with its pasture lands, and Jazer with its pasture lands.
That makes 4 cities in all.
[40] These were all the cities of the descendants of Merari and their clans.
They were the rest of the clans of the Levites.
By lot they received 12 cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> Four, four, and four make twelve.
> Many Hebrew manuscripts do not have verses 36 and 37 about the cities of Reuben. Other manuscripts and the WEB do have them. Without them, the number twelve would not be right.

---

## 48 cities in all (Verses 41–42)

[41] So the Levites received 48 cities in all, with their pasture lands.
These cities lay in the land that belonged to the people of Israel.
[42] Each of these cities had its pasture lands around it.
It was the same with all these cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> 13 + 10 + 13 + 12 = 48. That is exactly as many as Moses had ordered (Numbers 35:7).
> Now the Levites lived spread out over the whole land. So they were close to the people everywhere and could teach them God’s law.

---

## God has fulfilled everything (Verses 43–45)

[43] So the LORD gave Israel all the land.
He had promised with an oath to give it to their ancestors.
The people took the land and lived in it.
[44] The LORD gave them peace on every side.
He had promised this to their ancestors with an oath.
None of their enemies could stand against them.
The LORD gave all their enemies into their power.
[45] The LORD had promised good things to the people of Israel.
Not one of these good things failed.
Everything came true.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important sentences in the whole book of Joshua: “The LORD had promised good things to the people of Israel. Not one of these good things failed. Everything came true.”
> Hundreds of years earlier, God had promised Abraham a land. Now it is fulfilled. God keeps his word.
> Of course, we know from the chapters before that not all the peoples had been driven out yet. But the narrator wants to say: On God’s side, nothing is missing. If something is still open, it is not because of God.
> In hard times, many people have read this verse as a comfort: Even if I cannot see it yet, God keeps his promises.
