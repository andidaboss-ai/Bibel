# Joshua – Chapter 22
### The altar by the Jordan

---

## The tribes east of the Jordan may go home (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then Joshua called the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh.
[2] He said to them,
“You have kept everything that Moses, the servant of the LORD, commanded you.
And you have listened to my voice in everything I commanded you.
[3] You have not left your brothers alone all this long time, to this day.
You have faithfully done what the LORD your God commanded.
[4] And now the LORD your God has given rest to your brothers, as he told them.
So now turn back and go to your tents,
to the land that belongs to you,
which Moses, the servant of the LORD, gave you beyond the Jordan.
[5] Only be very careful to do the commandment and the law
that Moses, the servant of the LORD, commanded you:
to love the LORD your God, to walk in all his ways,
to keep his commandments, to hold on to him,
and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul.”
[6] Then Joshua blessed them and sent them away.
And they went to their tents.
[7] To one half-tribe of Manasseh, Moses had given an inheritance in Bashan.
But to the other half, Joshua had given an inheritance among their brothers west of the Jordan.
When Joshua sent them away to their tents, he blessed them
[8] and said to them,
“Go back to your tents with great wealth,
with very much livestock, with silver, gold, bronze, and iron, and with very much clothing.
Share the goods you took from your enemies with your brothers.”
[9] So the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh turned back.
They left the people of Israel at Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan,
to go to the land of Gilead, to the land that belonged to them,
which they had taken as the LORD had commanded through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The two and a half tribes have kept their promise (Numbers 32). For many years they fought together with their brothers, even though they already had their own land. Joshua praises them for it.
> And he gives them the most important thing to take with them: Love God. Hold on to him. With all your heart and all your soul. This is the same message as in the “Shema Israel” (Deuteronomy 6:5).
> “Share the goods … with your brothers”: Those who stayed at home should also receive something.

## A large altar (Verses 10–12)

[10] When they came to the region by the Jordan that is in the land of Canaan,
the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh built an altar there by the Jordan,
a large altar that could be seen from far away.
[11] The people of Israel heard about it:
“Look, the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh
have built an altar on the border of the land of Canaan, in the region by the Jordan,
on the side that belongs to the people of Israel.”
[12] When the people of Israel heard it,
the whole community of the people of Israel gathered at Shiloh
to go to war against them.

> **What does this mean?**
> According to the law, there could only be one altar for sacrifices, at the place God chooses (Deuteronomy 12). A second altar looked like turning away from God.
> The other tribes are ready right away to go to war against their own brothers. The situation is very dangerous.

---

## Messengers are sent (Verses 13–20)

[13] The people of Israel sent Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest,
to the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, in the land of Gilead.
[14] With him were 10 leaders, one leader of a family for each tribe of Israel.
Each of them was the head of his family among the thousands of Israel.
[15] They came to the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, in the land of Gilead,
and spoke with them. They said,
[16] “The whole community of the LORD says:
‘What is this unfaithfulness that you have done against the God of Israel?
Today you are turning away from following the LORD
by building yourselves an altar, to rebel against the LORD today!
[17] Is the sin of Peor not enough for us?
We have not cleansed ourselves from it to this day,
even though a plague came on the community of the LORD.
[18] And today you are turning away from following the LORD?
If you rebel against the LORD today,
then tomorrow he will be angry with the whole community of Israel.
[19] But if the land that belongs to you is unclean,
then come over to the land that belongs to the LORD, where the LORD’s tabernacle stands,
and take land among us.
But do not rebel against the LORD, and do not rebel against us,
by building yourselves an altar other than the altar of the LORD our God.
[20] Didn’t Achan the son of Zerah act unfaithfully with the devoted things,
and anger fell on the whole community of Israel?
And that man was not the only one who died because of his sin.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before they fight, the tribes send messengers. They want to talk first. That is wise. Many wars could have been avoided if people had talked with each other first.
> Phinehas was the priest who had acted so firmly against the worship of idols at Baal Peor back then (Numbers 25).
> The messengers remind them of Peor and of Achan: When some are unfaithful, the whole people suffers.
> Verse 19 is remarkable: They even offer to share their own land if the eastern tribes do not feel at home over there. That is a generous offer.

## The answer: an altar as a witness (Verses 21–29)

[21] Then the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh answered.
They said to the heads of the thousands of Israel,
[22] “The Mighty One, God, the LORD! The Mighty One, God, the LORD, he knows!
And Israel shall know:
If it was in rebellion or in unfaithfulness against the LORD,
then do not save us today!
[23] If we have built ourselves an altar to turn away from following the LORD,
or to offer burnt offerings or grain offerings on it,
or to sacrifice peace offerings on it,
then let the LORD himself punish us for it.
[24] No, we did this out of worry, for a reason.
We thought, ‘In the future your children might say to our children,
“What do you have to do with the LORD, the God of Israel?
[25] For the LORD has made the Jordan a border between us and you,
you people of Reuben and people of Gad.
You have no share in the LORD.”’
So your children might make our children stop fearing the LORD.
[26] That is why we said,
‘Let’s get ready and build ourselves an altar,
not for burnt offerings and not for sacrifices,
[27] but to be a witness between us and you,
and between our generations after us,
that we serve the LORD in front of him
with our burnt offerings, our sacrifices, and our peace offerings.’
Then your children cannot say to our children in the future,
‘You have no share in the LORD.’
[28] That is why we said,
‘If they say this to us or to our descendants in the future,
we will answer,
“Look at the copy of the LORD’s altar, which our ancestors made,
not for burnt offerings and not for sacrifices,
but as a witness between us and you.”’
[29] Far be it from us to rebel against the LORD
and to turn away from following the LORD today
by building an altar for burnt offerings, grain offerings, or sacrifices,
besides the altar of the LORD our God that stands in front of his tabernacle!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The Mighty One, God, the LORD”: In Hebrew, three names of God stand here one after the other: “El, Elohim, YHWH.” They say it twice. It is a solemn oath.
> It was a misunderstanding! The eastern tribes did not want to turn away from God. Just the opposite: They were afraid that their children would later be shut out because the Jordan lies between them. The altar was not meant to be an altar for sacrifices but a memorial. A reminder: We belong to God’s people too.
> This is an important lesson: Before you judge someone, ask and listen. What looks like turning away from the outside can really be a sign of faithfulness.

## Peace instead of war (Verses 30–34)

[30] Phinehas the priest and the leaders of the community,
the heads of the thousands of Israel who were with him,
heard the words that the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the people of Manasseh spoke.
They were very pleased.
[31] Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest, said to the people of Reuben, the people of Gad, and the people of Manasseh,
“Today we know that the LORD is among us,
because you have not done this unfaithful thing against the LORD.
Now you have saved the people of Israel from the hand of the LORD.”
[32] Then Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest, and the leaders
went back from the people of Reuben and the people of Gad, from the land of Gilead, to the land of Canaan, to the people of Israel,
and brought them the report.
[33] The people of Israel were pleased with it.
The people of Israel praised God
and spoke no more of going to war against them
to destroy the land where the people of Reuben and the people of Gad lived.
[34] The people of Reuben and the people of Gad named the altar
“A Witness Between Us that the LORD is God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A war between brothers is avoided because people talked with each other. Phinehas, who used to be so strict, now listens and is glad.
> At the end, everyone praises God together. A quarrel becomes a shared confession: “The LORD is God.”
> This story is an example for dealing with conflicts, in families, in congregations, and between nations: Do not attack right away, but ask, listen, and understand.
