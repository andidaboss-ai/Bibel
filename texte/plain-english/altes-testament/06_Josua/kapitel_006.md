# Joshua – Chapter 6
### The walls of Jericho

---

## God’s plan (Verses 1–5)

[1] Jericho was tightly shut because of the people of Israel.
No one went out, and no one came in.
[2] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Look, I have given Jericho into your hand, with its king and its brave warriors.
[3] All your men of war must march around the city, going around the city once.
Do this for six days.
[4] Seven priests must carry seven trumpets made of rams’ horns in front of the ark.
On the seventh day, you must march around the city seven times,
and the priests must blow the trumpets.
[5] When they make a long blast with the ram’s horn, and you hear the sound of the trumpet,
all the people must shout with a great shout.
Then the city wall will fall down flat.
And the people must go up, every man straight ahead of him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an unusual battle plan. No siege, no weapons, but a worship service: priests, the ark, and horns. It should be very clear: Israel does not conquer Jericho. God gives the city.
> The ram’s horn is called “shofar” in Hebrew. It is still blown in synagogues today, for example at the Jewish New Year.
> The number seven comes up again and again: seven priests, seven horns, seven days, seven times around. Seven is the number of completeness, as in the seven days of creation.

---

## Six days around the city (Verses 6–14)

[6] Joshua, the son of Nun, called the priests and said to them,
“Pick up the ark of the covenant.
Seven priests must carry seven trumpets of rams’ horns in front of the ark of the LORD.”
[7] They said to the people,
“Go forward! March around the city.
The armed men must go ahead of the ark of the LORD.”
[8] So it happened: When Joshua had spoken to the people,
the seven priests carrying the seven trumpets of rams’ horns in front of the LORD went forward and blew the trumpets.
And the ark of the covenant of the LORD followed them.
[9] The armed men went ahead of the priests who blew the trumpets,
and the rear guard went after the ark.
The trumpets sounded as they went.
[10] Joshua had commanded the people,
“Do not shout, and do not let your voice be heard.
Not a word may come out of your mouth until the day I tell you to shout.
Then you must shout.”
[11] So he had the ark of the LORD go around the city, once around.
Then they came into the camp and stayed the night in the camp.
[12] Joshua got up early in the morning, and the priests picked up the ark of the LORD.
[13] The seven priests carrying the seven trumpets of rams’ horns in front of the ark of the LORD
kept walking and blew the trumpets.
The armed men went ahead of them,
and the rear guard came after the ark of the LORD.
The trumpets sounded as they went.
[14] On the second day, they marched around the city once and went back to the camp.
They did this for six days.

> **What does this mean?**
> For six days, nothing seems to happen. The people march silently around the city. Only the horns sound. The people on the wall watch and maybe wonder. This takes a lot of patience and trust.
> The silence is important: no boasting, no grumbling. Only waiting for God’s time.

---

## On the seventh day the walls fall (Verses 15–21)

[15] On the seventh day, they got up early, at dawn,
and marched around the city in the same way seven times.
Only on this day did they march around the city seven times.
[16] The seventh time, when the priests blew the trumpets, Joshua said to the people,
“Shout, for the LORD has given you the city!
[17] The city and everything in it must be devoted to the LORD.
That means: It belongs completely to God and must be destroyed.
Only Rahab the prostitute will live,
she and everyone who is with her in the house,
because she hid the messengers that we sent.
[18] But as for you, keep away from what is devoted to destruction.
Otherwise, after you have devoted it, you may take some of the devoted things.
Then you would make the camp of Israel cursed and bring trouble on it.
[19] But all the silver and gold, and all the things made of bronze and iron, are holy to the LORD.
They must go into the LORD’s treasury.”
[20] So the people shouted, and the priests blew the trumpets.
When the people heard the sound of the trumpet, the people shouted with a great shout.
Then the wall fell down flat.
The people went up into the city, every man straight ahead of him, and they took the city.
[21] They completely destroyed everything in the city,
both man and woman, both young and old,
and ox, sheep, and donkey, with the edge of the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> The walls do not fall by weapons but by God’s power. This is the best-known story in the book of Joshua. There is even a famous song about it (“Joshua fit the battle of Jericho”), which enslaved people in America sang. For them it was a song of hope: Even the strongest walls of oppression can fall.
> But verse 21 is terrible: All the people in the city are killed, men, women, children, and old people. This is the “devoting to destruction” that we read about in Deuteronomy (Deuteronomy 7 and 20).
> We must not make this sound nice. By our understanding today, this is a grave wrong. Jews and Christians have thought about it for a long time. Many scholars think: The language of such reports is typical of war reports of that time and greatly exaggerates. Other texts of the Bible show that the Canaanites continued to live in the land.
> Archaeologists argue about whether Jericho was even a lived-in, walled city at the time of Joshua. Some say no, others say yes. This has still not been settled for sure.
> One thing is clear: This story must never be used to justify violence against a people or a city.

## Rahab is saved (Verses 22–25)

[22] Joshua said to the two men who had spied out the land,
“Go into the prostitute’s house,
and bring the woman and everything she has out of there, as you swore to her.”
[23] So the young men who had been spies went in
and brought out Rahab, her father, her mother, her brothers, and everything she had.
They also brought out all her relatives.
They put them in a place outside the camp of Israel.
[24] They burned the city and everything in it with fire.
Only the silver, the gold, and the things made of bronze and iron
they put into the treasury of the house of the LORD.
[25] But Joshua let Rahab the prostitute live,
together with her father’s family and everything she had.
She lives in the middle of Israel to this day,
because she hid the messengers whom Joshua sent to spy out Jericho.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the destruction, there is rescue. The promise to Rahab is kept.
> At first, Rahab and her family live “outside the camp of Israel.” But then it says: “She lives in the middle of Israel to this day.” The foreign woman becomes part of God’s people.
> “House of the LORD”: This means the holy tent, which was later replaced by the temple.

---

## A curse on Jericho (Verses 26–27)

[26] At that time Joshua made the people take an oath. He said,
“Cursed before the LORD is the man who rises up and builds this city Jericho again.
With the loss of his firstborn son he will lay its foundation,
and with the loss of his youngest son he will set up its gates.”
[27] So the LORD was with Joshua,
and he became famous in all the land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jericho is to stay a ruin, as a reminder of God’s deed.
> Many centuries later, at the time of King Ahab, a man named Hiel built Jericho again. The Bible reports that two of his sons died as a result, just as Joshua had said (1 Kings 16:34).
