# Joshua – Chapter 16
### The territory of Ephraim

---

## The lot for the children of Joseph (Verses 1–4)

[1] The lot for the children of Joseph came out like this:
from the Jordan at Jericho, at the waters of Jericho on the east,
through the wilderness that goes up from Jericho through the hill country to Bethel.
[2] From Bethel it went out to Luz
and went along to the territory of the Archites, to Ataroth.
[3] It went down west to the territory of the Japhletites,
as far as the territory of Lower Beth Horon and on to Gezer.
And it ended at the sea.
[4] So the children of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, received their inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> After Judah in the south, the children of Joseph now receive their territory in the middle of the land. Joseph had two sons: Manasseh and Ephraim. Jacob had blessed both and put the younger one, Ephraim, ahead of the older one (Genesis 48).
> “Bethel” and “Luz”: Luz was the old name of Bethel (Genesis 28:19). Here they are named like two places close to each other.
> “The Archites”: Later, Hushai came from this family. He was the faithful friend and adviser of King David (2 Samuel 15:32).

---

## The borders of Ephraim (Verses 5–9)

[5] This was the territory of the children of Ephraim by their clans:
The border of their inheritance on the east was Ataroth Addar, as far as Upper Beth Horon.
[6] The border went out to the west, at Michmethath on the north.
And the border turned east to Taanath Shiloh
and went along past it, east of Janoah.
[7] It went down from Janoah to Ataroth and to Naarah,
reached Jericho, and went out at the Jordan.
[8] From Tappuah the border went west to the brook of Kanah and ended at the sea.
This is the inheritance of the tribe of the children of Ephraim by their clans,
[9] together with the cities that were set apart for the children of Ephraim in the middle of the inheritance of the children of Manasseh,
all the cities with their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ephraim received fertile land in the hill country in the middle of the land. Later, Ephraim became one of the most powerful tribes. After the kingdom split, people sometimes called the whole northern kingdom simply “Ephraim” (for example Hosea 11:8).
> Some cities of Ephraim lay in the territory of Manasseh. The brother tribes lived closely together.

---

## The Canaanites in Gezer (Verse 10)

[10] But they did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer.
So the Canaanites live in the middle of Ephraim to this day.
But they became servants who had to do forced labor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Another honest sentence: Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites in Gezer. They stayed, but they had to do forced labor.
> Much later, the Pharaoh of Egypt conquered Gezer and gave the city to his daughter as a wedding gift when she married King Solomon (1 Kings 9:16).
> The book of Judges looks critically at this incomplete conquest: The peoples who stayed in the land later led Israel into worshiping idols (Judges 1:29; 2:1–3). Forced labor for people of another nation does not fit our understanding of human dignity today.
