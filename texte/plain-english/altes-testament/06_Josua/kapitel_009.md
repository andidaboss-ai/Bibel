# Joshua – Chapter 9
### The trick of the Gibeonites

---

## The kings join together (Verses 1–2)

[1] All the kings beyond the Jordan heard about it:
the kings in the hill country, in the lowland, and on the whole coast of the great sea opposite Lebanon,
the Hittite, the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.
[2] They gathered together to fight with one mind against Joshua and against Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Beyond the Jordan” here means: west of the Jordan, seen from Joshua’s camp. The kings of the land join together against Israel.

---

## The Gibeonites dress up (Verses 3–15)

[3] But the people of Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and to Ai.
[4] So they also used a trick.
They went and pretended to be messengers from a far country.
They put old sacks on their donkeys,
and old wineskins that were torn and tied up,
[5] and old, patched sandals on their feet,
and they wore old clothes.
All the bread they took with them was dry and moldy.
[6] They went to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal and said to him and to the men of Israel,
“We have come from a far country.
So now make a covenant with us.”
[7] The men of Israel said to the Hivites,
“What if you live among us? How could we make a covenant with you?”
[8] They said to Joshua, “We are your servants.”
Joshua said to them, “Who are you? Where do you come from?”
[9] They said to him,
“Your servants have come from a very far country because of the name of the LORD your God.
For we have heard about his fame and all that he did in Egypt,
[10] and all that he did to the two kings of the Amorites beyond the Jordan,
to Sihon, king of Heshbon, and to Og, king of Bashan, who lived at Ashtaroth.
[11] Our elders and all the people of our country said to us,
‘Take food for the journey and go to meet them.
Tell them, “We are your servants. So now make a covenant with us.”’
[12] This bread of ours was still hot when we took it from our houses as food for the journey,
on the day we left to come to you.
But now, look, it is dry and has become moldy.
[13] These wineskins that we filled were new.
And look, they are torn.
And our clothes and our sandals have worn out because of the very long journey.”
[14] The men tasted some of their food.
But they did not ask for advice from the mouth of the LORD.
[15] Joshua made peace with them and made a covenant with them to let them live.
The leaders of the community swore an oath to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gibeon was a city only about 6 miles northwest of Jerusalem, very close to Ai. The Gibeonites knew: Israel must not make a covenant with the peoples in the land (Deuteronomy 7:2). But Israel may make peace with peoples far away (Deuteronomy 20:10–15). That is why they pretend to come from far away.
> They thought up a clever disguise: old bread, torn wineskins, worn-out sandals.
> The key sentence is verse 14: “But they did not ask for advice from the mouth of the LORD.” They trusted their eyes and not God. That was the mistake.

---

## The trick is discovered (Verses 16–21)

[16] Three days after they had made a covenant with them,
they heard that they were their neighbors and lived among them.
[17] The people of Israel set out and came to their cities on the third day.
Their cities were Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath Jearim.
[18] The people of Israel did not kill them,
because the leaders of the community had sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel.
And the whole community grumbled against the leaders.
[19] But all the leaders said to the whole community,
“We have sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel.
So now we may not touch them.
[20] This is what we will do with them: We will let them live,
so that no anger comes on us because of the oath we swore to them.”
[21] The leaders said to them, “Let them live.”
So they became woodcutters and water carriers for the whole community,
as the leaders had told them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Even though Israel was tricked, it keeps its oath. A promise in God’s name is holy, even if it was given under false conditions.
> Many centuries later, King Saul broke this oath and killed Gibeonites. This was punished severely (2 Samuel 21:1–9). God takes promises seriously.
> The result is something good: The Gibeonites stay alive. An oath saved human lives.

---

## Joshua confronts the Gibeonites (Verses 22–27)

[22] Joshua called for them and said to them,
“Why did you trick us and say, ‘We live very far from you,’
when you live among us?
[23] So now you are cursed.
Some of you will always be slaves,
woodcutters and water carriers for the house of my God.”
[24] They answered Joshua,
“Your servants were clearly told what the LORD your God commanded his servant Moses:
to give you the whole land and to destroy all the people of the land in front of you.
So we were very afraid for our lives because of you,
and that is why we did this.
[25] Now, look, we are in your hand.
Do with us what seems good and right to you.”
[26] So he did that with them.
He saved them from the hand of the people of Israel, so that they did not kill them.
[27] That day Joshua made them woodcutters and water carriers
for the community and for the altar of the LORD, to this day,
in the place that the LORD would choose.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Gibeonites honestly say why they lied: out of fear of death. They recognized God’s power, much like Rahab.
> Now they must always do simple work: chopping wood and carrying water. That was a low position. But they serve at God’s altar. So they become part of Israel’s worship.
> Later, Gibeonites continued to live in Israel. After the exile in Babylon, descendants of Gibeon returned to Jerusalem and helped rebuild the city wall (Nehemiah 3:7; 7:25).
> Being slaves like this does not fit our understanding of human dignity today. But compared to death, it was rescue.
