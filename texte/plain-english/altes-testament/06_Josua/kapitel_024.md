# Joshua – Chapter 24
### The covenant at Shechem – Joshua’s death

---

## God tells the story of his people (Verses 1–13)

[1] Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel at Shechem.
He called for the elders of Israel, their heads, their judges, and their officers.
And they stood in front of God.
[2] Joshua said to all the people,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says:
‘Long ago your ancestors lived beyond the River:
Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor.
They served other gods.
[3] I took your father Abraham from beyond the River
and led him through the whole land of Canaan.
I gave him many descendants, and I gave him Isaac.
[4] To Isaac I gave Jacob and Esau.
To Esau I gave Mount Seir to own.
But Jacob and his children went down into Egypt.
[5] Then I sent Moses and Aaron.
I struck Egypt with plagues by what I did among them.
And after that I brought you out.
[6] I brought your ancestors out of Egypt, and you came to the sea.
The Egyptians chased your ancestors with chariots and horsemen to the Red Sea.
[7] When they cried out to the LORD,
he put darkness between you and the Egyptians
and brought the sea over them, and it covered them.
Your eyes saw what I did in Egypt.
And you lived in the wilderness for a long time.
[8] Then I brought you into the land of the Amorites, who lived beyond the Jordan.
They fought against you, and I gave them into your hand.
You took their land, and I destroyed them in front of you.
[9] Then Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab, rose up and fought against Israel.
He sent for Balaam the son of Beor to curse you.
[10] But I would not listen to Balaam.
So he had to bless you instead.
That is how I saved you from his hand.
[11] Then you crossed the Jordan and came to Jericho.
The men of Jericho fought against you,
and also the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Girgashite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.
And I gave them into your hand.
[12] I sent the hornet ahead of you.
It drove them out in front of you, even the two kings of the Amorites.
It was not by your sword and not by your bow.
[13] I gave you a land you had not worked for,
and cities you did not build, and you live in them.
You eat from vineyards and olive groves that you did not plant.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Shechem is a special place. Here God first promised Abraham that he would give him the land (Genesis 12:6–7). Here Jacob told his family to throw away the foreign gods (Genesis 35:2–4). Now the whole people comes back here.
> “Beyond the River” means the Euphrates River, in what is now Iraq. Abraham’s family came from there.
> An honest sentence in verse 2: Abraham’s family also served other gods in earlier times. Israel was not better than others from the beginning. God called it.
> In this speech, God himself speaks and tells the whole story from Abraham to today. The goal: Everything Israel has is a gift. “It was not by your sword and not by your bow.”
> “Red Sea”: This is what the WEB says. The Hebrew says “Sea of Reeds.”

## Choose today whom you will serve (Verses 14–15)

[14] So now fear the LORD and serve him honestly and faithfully.
Put away the gods that your ancestors served beyond the River and in Egypt,
and serve the LORD.
[15] But if you do not like to serve the LORD,
then choose today whom you will serve:
the gods that your ancestors served beyond the River,
or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you live.
As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the most famous sentence in the book of Joshua: “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.” Many families have hung this sentence over their front door or in their home.
> Joshua forces no one. He lets the people make a free choice: Decide! But he also says clearly how he himself has decided.
> Faith is a decision. And every generation has to make this decision again, every person for himself or herself.
> “Put away the gods”: Some of the people obviously still had images of gods from Egypt or from their old homeland. Joshua calls for a clear break.

---

## The people decide (Verses 16–24)

[16] The people answered,
“Far be it from us to leave the LORD to serve other gods!
[17] For it is the LORD our God
who brought us and our ancestors up out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery,
who did those great signs in front of our eyes,
and who protected us on the whole way we went,
and among all the peoples we passed through.
[18] The LORD drove out all the peoples in front of us, even the Amorites who lived in the land.
So we too will serve the LORD, for he is our God.”
[19] Joshua said to the people,
“You cannot serve the LORD.
For he is a holy God.
He is a jealous God.
He will not forgive your disobedience and your sins.
[20] If you leave the LORD and serve foreign gods,
then he will turn and do you harm and destroy you,
after he has done you good.”
[21] The people said to Joshua,
“No! We will serve the LORD!”
[22] Joshua said to the people,
“You are witnesses against yourselves
that you yourselves have chosen the LORD, to serve him.”
They said, “We are witnesses.”
[23] “So now put away the foreign gods that are among you,
and turn your heart to the LORD, the God of Israel.”
[24] The people said to Joshua,
“We will serve the LORD our God, and we will listen to his voice.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people answer with excitement: Yes, we will serve the LORD!
> But Joshua says something surprising: “You cannot serve the LORD.” Why? He wants them to mean it seriously. A quick “yes” is not enough. God is holy. You cannot believe in him a little on the side and have other gods at the same time.
> “He will not forgive your disobedience and your sins”: This sounds harsh. It probably means: Whoever knowingly leaves God and serves other gods cannot simply count on forgiveness as if nothing had happened. But the rest of the Bible also shows: Whoever turns back finds mercy with God (Deuteronomy 30).
> Three times the people say yes. And they become witnesses against themselves. That means: You said it freely. Later you cannot say you did not know.
> “Turn your heart to the LORD”: It is not only about outward things but about the heart.

## The covenant is made (Verses 25–28)

[25] So that day Joshua made a covenant with the people,
and in Shechem he gave them rules and laws.
[26] Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God.
And he took a large stone and set it up there under the oak that was by the holy place of the LORD.
[27] Joshua said to all the people,
“Look, this stone will be a witness against us,
for it has heard all the words of the LORD that he spoke to us.
It will be a witness against you, so that you do not deny your God.”
[28] Then Joshua sent the people away, each to his own inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> The covenant from Mount Sinai is renewed here. Every generation has to enter into it for itself.
> The large stone is like a silent witness. It reminds them again and again of their promise. Archaeologists have found a large standing stone at Shechem. Some connect it with this story. That is not certain.

## Joshua’s death (Verses 29–31)

[29] After these things, Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died.
He was 110 years old.
[30] They buried him in the area of his inheritance, in Timnathserah,
which is in the hill country of Ephraim, north of the mountain of Gaash.
[31] Israel served the LORD as long as Joshua lived,
and as long as the elders lived who outlived Joshua
and who had known all the work that the LORD had done for Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, Joshua receives the same title of honor as Moses: “the servant of the LORD.” He served faithfully.
> 110 years: Joseph also lived that long (Genesis 50:26). That was seen as a perfect, blessed age.
> Verse 31 is beautiful and sad at the same time: As long as the people lived who had seen God’s deeds themselves, Israel stayed faithful. What happens when this generation dies? The book of Judges tells about that (Judges 2:10).

## Joseph’s bones and Eleazar’s death (Verses 32–33)

[32] They buried the bones of Joseph, which the people of Israel had brought up from Egypt,
in Shechem, on the piece of land
that Jacob had bought from the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred pieces of silver.
It became the inheritance of the people of Joseph.
[33] Eleazar the son of Aaron also died.
They buried him on the hill of his son Phinehas,
which had been given to him in the hill country of Ephraim.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before he died, Joseph had asked: Take my bones with you when God leads you out of Egypt (Genesis 50:25). Moses had taken them along (Exodus 13:19). Now, hundreds of years later, they are buried in the promised land. One last promise is fulfilled.
> They are buried at Shechem, on the very field that Jacob had bought (Genesis 33:19). Where everything began.
> The book ends with three graves: Joshua, Joseph, and Eleazar. A generation comes to an end. But God’s story with his people goes on.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Joshua?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of Joshua tells how God’s promise to Abraham is fulfilled: Israel receives the land.
> 1. Entering the land (Chapters 1–5): God tells Joshua: “Be strong and courageous!” Rahab saves the spies. The people cross the Jordan.
> 2. The conquest (Chapters 6–12): The walls of Jericho fall. Achan’s guilt brings trouble. The Gibeonites make peace. The sun stands still.
> 3. The dividing of the land (Chapters 13–21): Every tribe receives its land. Caleb, the daughters of Zelophehad, and the Levites receive their inheritance. “Not one of all the good things that the LORD had promised to the house of Israel failed.”
> 4. The farewell (Chapters 22–24): A war between brothers is avoided. Joshua calls for a decision: “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”
> The book has many harsh war reports. We have not left them out, but we have said honestly: They belong to a distant time, they often exaggerate in the language of their time, and they must never be used to justify violence or war.
> And the book also shows: People from foreign nations like Rahab and the Gibeonites find a place in God’s people. Caleb, whose family came from outside, becomes an example of faithfulness.
> The most important message: God keeps his word. And every person has to decide whom they will serve.
