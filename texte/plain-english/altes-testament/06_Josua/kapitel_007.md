# Joshua – Chapter 7
### Achan’s theft

---

## The defeat at Ai (Verses 1–5)

[1] But the children of Israel were unfaithful with the devoted things.
For Achan, the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, from the tribe of Judah,
took some of the devoted things.
So the anger of the LORD burned against the children of Israel.
[2] Joshua sent men from Jericho to Ai, which is near Beth Aven, east of Bethel.
He said to them, “Go up and spy out the land.”
The men went up and spied out Ai.
[3] They came back to Joshua and said to him,
“Don’t let all the people go up.
Let about two or three thousand men go up and strike Ai.
Don’t make all the people work hard there, for there are only a few of them.”
[4] So about three thousand men from the people went up there.
But they ran away from the men of Ai.
[5] The men of Ai killed about 36 of their men.
They chased them from the gate to Shebarim and struck them on the way down the hill.
Then the hearts of the people melted and became like water.

> **What does this mean?**
> The reader already knows what Joshua does not know yet: Someone has secretly taken something that belonged to God.
> After the great victory at Jericho, Israel is overconfident. The spies say: “Ai is small. We can do it easily.” No one asks God.
> “Ai” means “heap of ruins” in Hebrew.
> 36 men die. For the people, this is a shock. Suddenly their courage is gone.

---

## Joshua cries out to God (Verses 6–9)

[6] Joshua tore his clothes and fell with his face to the ground in front of the ark of the LORD until evening,
he and the elders of Israel.
They put dust on their heads.
[7] Joshua said,
“Oh, Lord GOD, why did you bring this people across the Jordan at all?
To give us into the hand of the Amorites, so that they destroy us?
If only we had been content and stayed beyond the Jordan!
[8] Oh, Lord, what can I say, now that Israel has turned and run from its enemies?
[9] The Canaanites and all the people who live in the land will hear about it.
They will surround us and wipe our name off the earth.
And what will you do for your great name?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Tearing your clothes and putting dust on your head were signs of deep grief.
> Joshua honestly pours out his heart to God. He sounds almost like the people in the wilderness: “If only we had stayed over there!” Even great believers have moments of despair. The Bible does not hide that.

---

## God names the reason (Verses 10–15)

[10] The LORD said to Joshua,
“Get up! Why are you lying on your face like that?
[11] Israel has sinned.
Yes, they have broken my covenant, which I commanded them.
Yes, they have even taken some of the devoted things,
and they have also stolen and also lied.
They have even put it among their own things.
[12] That is why the children of Israel cannot stand against their enemies.
They turn and run from their enemies, because they themselves have become devoted to destruction.
I will not be with you anymore
unless you destroy the devoted things from among you.
[13] Get up! Make the people holy and say,
‘Make yourselves holy for tomorrow.
For the LORD, the God of Israel, says,
“There is a devoted thing among you, Israel.
You cannot stand against your enemies
until you take away the devoted thing from among you.”
[14] So in the morning you must come forward by your tribes.
The tribe that the LORD chooses must come forward by clans.
The clan that the LORD chooses must come forward by families.
And the family that the LORD chooses must come forward man by man.
[15] Whoever is caught with the devoted thing
must be burned with fire, he and everything he has,
because he has broken the covenant of the LORD
and because he has done a shameful thing in Israel.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> God says: Stop complaining, get up and act! There is a reason for the defeat.
> It is surprising that God says “Israel has sinned,” even though only one person took something. The people are a community. What one person does affects everyone. A hidden wrong harms the whole community.
> The text does not say exactly how God “chooses” the guilty person. Probably by casting lots, with the Urim and Thummim of the priest.

---

## Achan is found (Verses 16–21)

[16] Joshua got up early in the morning and had Israel come forward by tribes.
The tribe of Judah was chosen.
[17] He had the clans of Judah come forward, and the clan of the Zerahites was chosen.
He had the clan of the Zerahites come forward man by man, and Zabdi was chosen.
[18] He had his family come forward man by man,
and Achan was chosen, the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, from the tribe of Judah.
[19] Joshua said to Achan,
“My son, please give glory to the LORD, the God of Israel, and confess to him.
Tell me now what you have done. Don’t hide it from me!”
[20] Achan answered Joshua,
“It is true. I have sinned against the LORD, the God of Israel.
This is what I did:
[21] Among the things we took, I saw a beautiful robe from Babylon,
200 shekels of silver, about 5 pounds, and a bar of gold that weighed 50 shekels, about 1¼ pounds.
I wanted them so much that I took them.
Look, they are hidden in the ground in the middle of my tent, with the silver underneath.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible says 200 shekels of silver and 50 shekels of gold. A shekel weighed a little less than half an ounce.
> Step by step the truth comes to light: from the tribe to the clan to the single man. Achan could have stepped forward the whole time. He did not.
> Joshua speaks to him kindly: “My son.” He asks him to tell the truth.
> Achan’s confession shows how sin often happens: He saw something. He wanted it. He took it. He hid it. It was the same with Eve in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:6). The tenth commandment forbids wanting to have something that belongs to someone else (Exodus 20:17). Such greed stands at the beginning of many sins.

---

## Achan’s punishment (Verses 22–26)

[22] So Joshua sent messengers, and they ran to the tent.
Look! It was hidden in his tent, with the silver underneath.
[23] They took the things out of the middle of the tent,
brought them to Joshua and to all the children of Israel,
and laid them down in front of the LORD.
[24] Then Joshua, and all Israel with him, took Achan the son of Zerah,
the silver, the robe, the bar of gold,
his sons and his daughters, his cattle, his donkeys, and his sheep,
his tent, and everything he had,
and they brought them up to the valley of Achor.
[25] Joshua said,
“Why have you brought trouble on us?
The LORD will bring trouble on you today.”
All Israel stoned him with stones.
They burned them with fire and stoned them with stones.
[26] They piled a great heap of stones over him, which is still there to this day.
And the LORD turned away from his burning anger.
That is why the name of that place is “The valley of Achor” to this day.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Achor” means “trouble.” The name sounds almost like “Achan.” He brought trouble on Israel, and so his life ends in the “valley of trouble.”
> This punishment is shockingly harsh. And it is especially hard that, according to verse 24, his sons and daughters are taken too. The text does not say completely clearly whether they were punished as well. If they were, that goes against the commandment in Deuteronomy 24:16: Children must not be punished for the guilt of their parents. Some interpreters think the family knew about the theft and helped hide it, because the hiding place was in the middle of the shared tent.
> We have to say it honestly: This story is hard to bear. It shows how seriously the devoting to destruction was taken at that time. But it is not an example of how to deal with guilt. No one may use it to punish people harshly or to make their families suffer.
> Later, the prophet Hosea promises something wonderful: God wants to make the valley of Achor, the valley of trouble, into a door of hope (Hosea 2:15; in some Bibles 2:17). Even out of a place of shame, God can make a new beginning.
