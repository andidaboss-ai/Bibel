# Joshua – Chapter 2
### Rahab and the spies

---

## Two spies in Jericho (Verses 1–7)

[1] Joshua, the son of Nun, secretly sent two men out of Shittim as spies. He said,
“Go and look at the land, and Jericho too.”
They went and came into the house of a prostitute whose name was Rahab.
They spent the night there.
[2] Someone told the king of Jericho,
“Look, men from the children of Israel came here tonight to spy out the land.”
[3] So the king of Jericho sent a message to Rahab:
“Bring out the men who came to you, who went into your house.
For they have come to spy out the whole land.”
[4] But the woman took the two men and hid them.
Then she said, “Yes, the men came to me,
but I didn’t know where they came from.
[5] When it was dark, at the time to shut the city gate, the men went out.
I don’t know where the men went.
Chase them quickly. You may still catch them.”
[6] But she had brought them up to the roof
and hidden them under the stalks of flax that she had laid out on the roof.
[7] The men chased them on the road to the fords of the Jordan River.
Fords are shallow places where people can walk through a river.
As soon as the chasers had gone out, the gate was shut.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jericho is one of the oldest cities in the world. It lies in an oasis by the Jordan, right across from Israel’s camp.
> Rahab was a prostitute. The spies may have gone to her house because strange men often came and went there, so they were not noticed.
> Rahab lies to protect the men. Jews and Christians have thought a lot about whether this lie was right. Many say: She risked her life to save people. Later, people did something similar in the time of the Nazis, when they hid Jews and lied to those hunting them.
> “Flax” is a plant used to make linen cloth. People laid the stalks on the flat roofs to dry.

---

## Rahab’s confession of faith (Verses 8–14)

[8] Before the men lay down to sleep, she came up to them on the roof.
[9] She said to the men,
“I know that the LORD has given you the land.
Fear of you has fallen on us,
and all the people who live in the land are melting away with fear because of you.
[10] For we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red Sea in front of you
when you came out of Egypt.
And we heard what you did to the two kings of the Amorites beyond the Jordan,
to Sihon and to Og, whom you completely destroyed.
[11] As soon as we heard it, our hearts melted,
and no one had any courage left because of you.
For the LORD your God is God in heaven above and on earth below.
[12] So now, please swear to me by the LORD:
Because I have been kind to you, you will also be kind to my father’s family.
Give me a sure sign
[13] that you will let my father, my mother, my brothers, and my sisters live,
and everyone who belongs to them,
and that you will save us from death.”
[14] The men said to her,
“Our lives for yours, if you don’t tell anyone about our business.
And when the LORD gives us the land,
we will be kind and faithful to you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Rahab is a woman from a foreign people, and a prostitute. Yet she speaks one of the most beautiful confessions of faith in the Bible: “The LORD your God is God in heaven above and on earth below.” These are almost the same words Moses used (Deuteronomy 4:39).
> She heard about God’s great deeds and drew the right conclusion. All the people of Jericho heard the same thing, but only Rahab trusts it.
> She does not think only of herself but of her whole family.
> “Kindness” in Hebrew is “chesed.” It means faithfulness, love, and kindness that people owe each other. It is a word that is often used for God’s faithfulness.

## The scarlet cord (Verses 15–21)

[15] Then she let the men down through the window with a rope.
For her house was built into the city wall, and she lived in the wall.
[16] She said to them,
“Go to the hills, so that the chasers don’t find you.
Hide there for three days, until the chasers have come back.
After that, you can go on your way.”
[17] The men said to her,
“We will be free from this oath that you made us swear, unless you do this:
[18] Look, when we come into the land,
tie this cord of scarlet thread in the window that you let us down through.
Scarlet means bright red.
And gather your father, your mother, your brothers, and your father’s whole family into your house.
[19] If anyone goes out of the door of your house into the street,
his blood will be on his own head, and we will not be guilty.
But if anyone who is with you in the house is harmed,
his blood will be on our head.
[20] But if you tell anyone about our business,
then we will be free from the oath that you made us swear.”
[21] She said, “Let it be as you have said.”
She sent them away, and they left.
Then she tied the scarlet cord in the window.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rahab’s house was part of the city wall. That is why she could let the men out directly to the outside.
> The scarlet cord in the window is a sign of rescue. Many interpreters think of the Passover in Egypt: There, the red blood on the doorposts was the sign that death would pass over that house (Exodus 12). Whoever stays in the house is safe.
> Rahab ties the cord right away, not later. She trusts the promise.

## The spies return (Verses 22–24)

[22] They went into the hills and stayed there three days, until the chasers had gone back.
The chasers searched for them all along the road, but they did not find them.
[23] Then the two men turned back, came down from the hills,
crossed the river, and came to Joshua, the son of Nun.
They told him everything that had happened to them.
[24] They said to Joshua,
“The LORD has truly given the whole land into our hands.
And all the people who live in the land are melting away with fear because of us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> What a difference from the spies 40 years earlier! Back then, ten of the twelve came back full of fear (Numbers 13). Now the spies bring a message full of trust.
> Rahab is saved later (Chapter 6). She marries an Israelite. In the New Testament, she appears in the family tree of Jesus: She was the mother of Boaz, and so an ancestor of King David (Matthew 1:5). The letter to the Hebrews and the letter of James praise her for her faith (Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25).
> God writes history with people whom others look down on.
