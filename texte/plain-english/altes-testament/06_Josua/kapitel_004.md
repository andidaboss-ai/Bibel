# Joshua – Chapter 4
### Twelve stones to remember

---

## Twelve stones from the Jordan (Verses 1–8)

[1] When the whole nation had completely crossed the Jordan, the LORD spoke to Joshua. He said,
[2] “Take twelve men from the people, one man from each tribe,
[3] and command them:
‘Take twelve stones from the middle of the Jordan, from the place where the priests’ feet stood firm.
Carry them over with you
and put them down in the place where you will camp tonight.’”
[4] Then Joshua called the twelve men whom he had chosen from the children of Israel, one man from each tribe.
[5] Joshua said to them,
“Go ahead of the ark of the LORD your God into the middle of the Jordan.
Each of you, pick up a stone and put it on your shoulder,
one for each tribe of the children of Israel,
[6] so that this may be a sign among you.
In the future, when your children ask you,
‘What do these stones mean to you?’
[7] then tell them,
‘The water of the Jordan was cut off in front of the ark of the covenant of the LORD.
When it crossed the Jordan, the water of the Jordan was cut off.
These stones will be a reminder for the children of Israel forever.’”
[8] The children of Israel did as Joshua commanded.
They took twelve stones from the middle of the Jordan, as the LORD had told Joshua,
one for each tribe of the children of Israel.
They carried them over with them to the place where they camped, and they put them down there.

> **What does this mean?**
> People quickly forget what God has done. That is why stones are set up as a memorial. When children ask, “What do these stones mean to you?” the parents should tell the story.
> Each tribe carries one stone. All twelve tribes belong, including those whose land lies east of the Jordan.
> This is often how faith grew: Children ask, and parents tell. We know this from the Passover feast too (Exodus 12:26; Deuteronomy 6:20).

## Twelve stones in the Jordan too (Verses 9–14)

[9] Joshua also set up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan,
in the place where the feet of the priests who carried the ark of the covenant had stood.
They are there to this day.
[10] For the priests who carried the ark stood in the middle of the Jordan
until everything was finished that the LORD had commanded Joshua to tell the people,
just as Moses had commanded Joshua.
And the people hurried and crossed over.
[11] When all the people had completely crossed over,
the ark of the LORD crossed over with the priests, while the people watched.
[12] The children of Reuben, the children of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh crossed over armed in front of the children of Israel,
as Moses had told them.
[13] About 40,000 men, ready and armed for war,
crossed over in front of the LORD to battle, to the plains of Jericho.
[14] On that day, the LORD made Joshua great in the eyes of all Israel.
They respected him with awe, as they had respected Moses, all the days of his life.

> **What does this mean?**
> So there are two memorials: one in the river, where the priests stood, and one on the bank, in the camp.
> The priests stayed in the middle of the riverbed until the last person was across. Those who carried the ark went in first and came out last. Leaders should be like this: first into danger, last into safety.
> The warriors of Reuben, Gad, and Manasseh keep their promise (Numbers 32).

## The water comes back (Verses 15–18)

[15] The LORD spoke to Joshua. He said,
[16] “Command the priests who carry the ark of the covenant to come up out of the Jordan.”
[17] So Joshua commanded the priests, “Come up out of the Jordan!”
[18] The priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD came up out of the middle of the Jordan.
As soon as the soles of the priests’ feet touched the dry ground,
the water of the Jordan went back to its place
and flowed over all its banks again, as before.

> **What does this mean?**
> In verse 16, the Hebrew text calls it the ark of the testimony. The WEB calls it the ark of the covenant. It is another name for the same ark. The word testimony means the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments that lay inside the ark.
> As soon as the ark is out, the river flows again. This shows: It was not chance. The water was gone only as long as God wanted.

## The stones of Gilgal (Verses 19–24)

[19] The people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month.
They camped in Gilgal, on the east border of Jericho.
[20] Joshua set up the twelve stones that they had taken out of the Jordan in Gilgal.
[21] He said to the children of Israel,
“In the future, when your children ask their fathers,
‘What do these stones mean?’
[22] then explain it to your children and say,
‘Israel crossed this Jordan on dry land.
[23] For the LORD your God dried up the water of the Jordan in front of you until you had crossed over,
just as the LORD your God did to the Red Sea,
which he dried up in front of us until we had crossed over,
[24] so that all the peoples of the earth may know that the hand of the LORD is mighty,
and so that you may fear the LORD your God forever.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The tenth day of the first month is exactly the day when the Passover lambs were chosen in Egypt (Exodus 12:3). Four days later, Israel celebrates the first Passover in the land (Chapter 5:10). 40 years after leaving Egypt, the circle closes.
> “Gilgal” became Israel’s first permanent camp in the land. The name sounds like the Hebrew word for “circle” or “circle of stones.”
> In verse 23, the WEB says “Red Sea.” The Hebrew says “Sea of Reeds.”
> The goal comes at the end: “so that all the peoples of the earth may know.” God’s deeds for Israel are meant to show the whole world who God is.
