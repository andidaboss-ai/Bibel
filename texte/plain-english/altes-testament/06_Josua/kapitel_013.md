# Joshua – Chapter 13
### What is still left – the land east of the Jordan

---

## Much land is still left (Verses 1–7)

[1] Joshua was old and well along in years.
The LORD said to him,
“You are old and well along in years,
and very much land is still left to be taken.
[2] This is the land that is still left:
all the regions of the Philistines and all the land of the Geshurites,
[3] from the Shihor, which is east of Egypt, to the border of Ekron in the north,
which is counted as Canaanite land:
the five rulers of the Philistines, those of Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron,
and also the Avvim
[4] in the south.
Also all the land of the Canaanites, and Mearah, which belongs to the Sidonians,
as far as Aphek, to the border of the Amorites,
[5] and the land of the Gebalites, and all Lebanon to the east,
from Baal Gad below Mount Hermon to the entrance of Hamath,
[6] all the people of the hill country from Lebanon to Misrephoth Maim, all the Sidonians.
I myself will drive them out in front of the people of Israel.
Just give the land to Israel by lot as an inheritance, as I have commanded you.
[7] So now divide this land as an inheritance to the nine tribes and the half-tribe of Manasseh.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Joshua has grown old. He has done a lot, but not everything. Much land is left, especially on the coast (where the Philistines live) and in the north (in Lebanon).
> God says: Divide the land anyway, already now. I myself will take care of the rest. Joshua does not have to finish everything. God takes care of what comes after him.
> The Philistines lived in five large cities on the coast. Later, for a long time, they were Israel’s most dangerous enemies, at the time of Samson, Saul, and David.
> “The entrance of Hamath”: Many Bibles translate this as the place name “Lebo Hamath.” Hamath was a city in what is now Syria.
> The “Gebalites” are the people from Gebal, the city of Byblos in what is now Lebanon.

## The inheritance east of the Jordan (Verses 8–14)

[8] With the other half of Manasseh, the Reubenites and the Gadites had received their inheritance,
which Moses gave them beyond the Jordan to the east,
just as Moses, the servant of the LORD, gave it to them:
[9] from Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon,
and the city in the middle of the valley,
and the whole plain of Medeba to Dibon,
[10] and all the cities of Sihon, king of the Amorites, who ruled in Heshbon,
as far as the border of the people of Ammon,
[11] and Gilead, and the territory of the Geshurites and Maacathites,
all Mount Hermon, and all Bashan as far as Salecah,
[12] the whole kingdom of Og in Bashan, who ruled in Ashtaroth and in Edrei.
He was one of the last of the Rephaim who were left.
Moses had attacked them and driven them out.
[13] But the people of Israel did not drive out the Geshurites or the Maacathites.
So Geshur and Maacath live in the middle of Israel to this day.
[14] Only to the tribe of Levi he gave no inheritance.
The offerings made by fire to the LORD, the God of Israel, are his inheritance, as the LORD told him.

> **What does this mean?**
> “With the other half of Manasseh”: The Hebrew says only “with him.” It means the other half-tribe of Manasseh, which lived east of the Jordan.
> Verse 13 is honest: Not all peoples were driven out. They continued to live in the middle of Israel. Later, King David married a princess from Geshur. Her son was Absalom (2 Samuel 3:3).
> The tribe of Levi gets no land. It has God himself as its inheritance. This is said twice in this chapter.

## The territory of Reuben (Verses 15–23)

[15] Moses gave land to the tribe of the people of Reuben by their clans.
[16] Their territory went from Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon,
and the city in the middle of the valley, and the whole plain by Medeba,
[17] Heshbon and all its cities on the plain:
Dibon, Bamoth Baal, Beth Baal Meon,
[18] Jahaz, Kedemoth, Mephaath,
[19] Kiriathaim, Sibmah, Zereth Shahar on the mountain in the valley,
[20] Beth Peor, the slopes of Pisgah, Beth Jeshimoth,
[21] all the cities of the plain, and the whole kingdom of Sihon, king of the Amorites, who ruled in Heshbon.
Moses had struck him, together with the chiefs of Midian,
Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur, and Reba, the princes of Sihon, who lived in the land.
[22] The people of Israel also killed Balaam the son of Beor, the fortune-teller, with the sword,
together with the others they killed.
[23] The border of the people of Reuben was the bank of the Jordan.
This was the inheritance of the people of Reuben by their clans, the cities and their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Balaam is called a “fortune-teller” here. He had blessed Israel when he was supposed to curse it (Numbers 22–24). But later he had advised the Midianites to lead Israel into worshiping idols. That is why he died in the war against Midian (Numbers 31:8, 16).

---

## The territory of Gad (Verses 24–28)

[24] Moses gave land to the tribe of Gad, to the people of Gad, by their clans.
[25] Their territory was Jazer and all the cities of Gilead,
and half the land of the people of Ammon, as far as Aroer, which is near Rabbah,
[26] and from Heshbon to Ramath Mizpeh and Betonim,
and from Mahanaim to the border of Debir,
[27] and in the valley: Beth Haram, Beth Nimrah, Succoth, and Zaphon,
the rest of the kingdom of Sihon, king of Heshbon,
with the bank of the Jordan to the far end of the sea of Chinnereth, beyond the Jordan to the east.
[28] This is the inheritance of the people of Gad by their clans, the cities and their villages.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mahanaim is the place where Jacob met angels (Genesis 32:2). Succoth is the place where Jacob built shelters for his livestock (Genesis 33:17). The old stories of the ancestors are connected with this land.

---

## The territory of the half-tribe of Manasseh (Verses 29–33)

[29] Moses gave an inheritance to the half-tribe of Manasseh.
It was for the half-tribe of the people of Manasseh by their clans.
[30] Their territory went from Mahanaim: all Bashan, the whole kingdom of Og, king of Bashan,
and all the villages of Jair, which are in Bashan, 60 cities.
[31] Half of Gilead, Ashtaroth, and Edrei, the cities of the kingdom of Og in Bashan,
were for the people of Machir, the son of Manasseh,
for half of the people of Machir by their clans.
[32] These are the inheritances that Moses gave out in the plains of Moab,
beyond the Jordan at Jericho, to the east.
[33] But Moses gave no inheritance to the tribe of Levi.
The LORD, the God of Israel, is their inheritance, as he told them.

> **What does this mean?**
> With this chapter, the second large part of the book of Joshua begins: the dividing of the land (Chapters 13–21). There are many names of places and borders. For us today, this is sometimes hard work to read. But for Israel it was very important: Every tribe and every family knew where their place in the land was. The land was a gift from God, and everyone received their share.
> At the end comes the sentence about Levi once more: “The LORD, the God of Israel, is their inheritance.” That is the most beautiful inheritance of all.
