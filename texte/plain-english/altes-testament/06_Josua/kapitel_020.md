# Joshua – Chapter 20
### The cities of refuge

---

## God’s command (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD spoke to Joshua. He said,
[2] “Speak to the people of Israel and say,
‘Choose the cities of refuge that I spoke to you about through Moses.
A refuge is a safe place.
[3] Anyone who kills a person by accident or without meaning to may run there.
These cities will be a refuge for you from the avenger of blood.
The avenger of blood is the relative who wants to take revenge for the dead person.
[4] He must run to one of these cities.
He must stand at the entrance of the city gate
and explain his case to the elders of that city.
Then they must take him into the city with them
and give him a place, so that he can live among them.
[5] If the avenger of blood chases him,
they must not hand over the one who killed into his hand.
For he struck his neighbor without meaning to and did not hate him before.
[6] He must live in that city until he has stood trial in front of the community,
until the death of the high priest who is serving in those days.
Then the one who killed may return
and come to his own city and his own house, to the city he ran away from.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The cities of refuge were announced in Numbers 35 and Deuteronomy 19. Now, in the land, they are really set up. God’s promises are carried out step by step.
> What is new here: The person running away must explain his case at the city gate. The elders listen to him and take him in. At that time, the gate was the place where court was held.
> This arrangement protects against people taking the law into their own hands. Revenge should not decide, but a fair trial.

---

## Six cities (Verses 7–9)

[7] So they set apart Kedesh in Galilee in the hill country of Naphtali,
Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim,
and Kiriath Arba – that is Hebron – in the hill country of Judah.
[8] And beyond the Jordan at Jericho, to the east, they chose:
Bezer in the wilderness, on the plain, from the tribe of Reuben,
Ramoth in Gilead from the tribe of Gad,
and Golan in Bashan from the tribe of Manasseh.
[9] These were the chosen cities for all the people of Israel and for the foreigner who lives among them,
so that anyone who killed a person without meaning to could run there
and not die by the hand of the avenger of blood
before he had stood trial in front of the community.

> **What does this mean?**
> The six cities are well spread out over the land: three west and three east of the Jordan, each in the north, in the middle, and in the south. That way everyone could find a city of refuge within reach.
> “They set apart” means: They chose these cities for a special task and made them holy.
> Again it is said clearly: The right to refuge is for foreigners too. Before God’s law, everyone is equal.
> All six cities of refuge were also cities of the Levites (Chapter 21). So the servants of God were at the same time the protectors of those who were being hunted.
