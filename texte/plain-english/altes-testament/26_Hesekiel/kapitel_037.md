# Ezekiel – Chapter 37
### The dry bones come to life

---

## The valley full of bones (Verses 1–10)

[1] The hand of the LORD was on me.
He brought me out by the Spirit of the LORD
and set me down in the middle of the valley.
It was full of bones.
[2] He led me all around among them.
Look, there were very many of them on the floor of the valley,
and look, they were very dry.
[3] He said to me,
“Son of man, can these bones live?”
I answered,
“Lord GOD, you know.”
[4] Again he said to me,
“Prophesy over these bones and tell them,
‘You dry bones, hear the word of the LORD!
[5] This is what the Lord GOD says to these bones:
“Look, I will make breath come into you,
and you will live.
[6] I will put tendons on you.
I will make flesh grow on you.
I will cover you with skin.
I will put breath in you,
and you will live.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.”’”
[7] So I prophesied as I was commanded.
While I was prophesying, there was a noise,
and look, there was an earthquake.
Then the bones came together,
each bone to its own bone.
[8] I looked, and there were tendons on them.
Flesh grew on them,
and skin covered them.
But there was no breath in them.
[9] Then he said to me,
“Prophesy to the wind.
Prophesy, son of man, and tell the wind,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Come from the four winds, breath,
and breathe on these people who were killed,
so that they may live.”’”
[10] So I prophesied as he commanded me.
And the breath came into them,
and they came to life.
They stood up on their feet,
a very, very large army.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most famous visions in the Bible. Ezekiel stands in a valley full of bones, completely dry, so they have been dead for a long time. A picture of complete hopelessness.
> Verse 3: God asks: “Can these bones live?” From a human point of view: impossible. Ezekiel answers wisely and humbly: “You know.”
> Verses 7–10: It happens in two steps. First the bones come together, and tendons, flesh and skin grow. But they are still dead. Then the breath comes, and they live.
> An important play on words: The Hebrew word “ruach” means “breath,” “wind,” and “spirit” at the same time. It appears again and again in this chapter. The breath that makes the dead come alive is God’s own Spirit. This is like the creation: God breathed the breath of life into the first human being (Genesis 2:7).

## I will open your graves (Verses 11–14)

[11] Then he said to me,
“Son of man, these bones are all the people of Israel.
Look, they say,
‘Our bones are dried up,
and our hope is lost.
We are completely cut off.’
[12] So prophesy and tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I will open your graves.
I will make you come up out of your graves, my people.
And I will bring you into the land of Israel.
[13] You will know that I am the LORD
when I have opened your graves
and made you come up out of your graves, my people.
[14] I will put my Spirit in you,
and you will live.
Then I will settle you in your own land.
And you will know that I, the LORD, have spoken and have done it,”
says the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here God explains the vision: The bones are the people of Israel in exile. The people say: “Our hope is lost.” They feel as if they were dead. After the destruction of Jerusalem they felt as if they were in a mass grave.
> God answers: “Look, I will open your graves.” God can make a dead people come alive again. He brings them back into their land. Nothing is hopeless for God.
> In Judaism, this text is read on the Sabbath during the Passover festival. Jews read it as a promise that Israel will be restored, and also as a sign pointing to the resurrection of the dead. After the Holocaust, many people found comfort in this text.
> Christians also read it as a picture for the resurrection and for the work of the Holy Spirit, who gives life.
> If you yourself feel “dried up” inside and without hope: This text says that God can give new life even there. And it is good to get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Two sticks become one (Verses 15–28)

[15] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[16] “You, son of man, take a stick
and write on it,
‘For Judah, and for the people of Israel who belong with him.’
Then take another stick
and write on it,
‘For Joseph, the stick of Ephraim,
and for all the people of Israel who belong with him.’
[17] Then join them together into one stick,
so that they become one in your hand.
[18] “The people of your nation will say to you,
‘Won’t you show us what you mean by this?’
[19] Then tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I will take the stick of Joseph,
which is in the hand of Ephraim,
and the tribes of Israel who belong with him.
I will put them together with the stick of Judah.
I will make them one stick,
and they will be one in my hand.”’
[20] The sticks you write on
must be in your hand where they can see them.
[21] Say to them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I will take the people of Israel from among the nations
where they have gone.
I will gather them from every side
and bring them into their own land.
[22] I will make them one nation in the land,
on the mountains of Israel.
One king will be king over all of them.
They will no longer be two nations.
They will never again be divided into two kingdoms.
[23] They will not make themselves unclean anymore
with their idols, with their disgusting images,
or with any of their sins.
I will save them from all the places where they lived
and where they sinned.
I will make them clean.
So they will be my people,
and I will be their God.
[24] “‘“My servant David will be king over them.
All of them will have one shepherd.
They will follow my laws
and keep my rules and do them.
[25] They will live in the land
that I gave to my servant Jacob,
where your ancestors lived.
They will live there,
they and their children and their grandchildren, forever.
And David my servant will be their prince forever.
[26] I will make a covenant of peace with them.
It will be a covenant that lasts forever.
I will settle them and make them many.
I will put my sanctuary among them forever.
[27] My tent will also be with them.
I will be their God,
and they will be my people.
[28] Then the nations will know that I am the LORD,
who makes Israel holy,
when my sanctuary is among them forever.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> After the death of King Solomon, the people were divided into two kingdoms: the northern kingdom of Israel (often called “Ephraim” or “Joseph”) and the southern kingdom of Judah (1 Kings 12). This split lasted for hundreds of years, with much fighting and even wars.
> Ezekiel takes two sticks and holds them in his hand so that they look like one. God promises: I will make the separated parts into one people again.
> Verses 24–25: Again it speaks of “my servant David,” the coming king from the family of David (see 34:23–24).
> Verses 26–27: A covenant of peace that lasts forever, and God lives in the middle of his people. “My tent will also be with them” means: God will live among them. The Hebrew word here means a “tent,” like God’s tent in the wilderness. In the New Testament it says: “The Word became flesh and lived among us” (John 1:14). In Greek it says there, word for word: He “pitched his tent” among us. And at the end of the Bible: “Look! God’s home is with people. He will live with them. They will be his people” (Revelation 21:3).
> We know the longing for unity today too: between nations, churches, families. God can join together what is broken.
