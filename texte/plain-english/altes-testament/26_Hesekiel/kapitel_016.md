# Ezekiel – Chapter 16
### Jerusalem, the abandoned baby

---

## First: How should we read this chapter?

Ezekiel 16 is one of the longest and harshest chapters in the Bible. In it, Jerusalem is pictured as a woman: first as an abandoned baby whom God rescues, then as a wife who is unfaithful to him.
This picture is a metaphor for the relationship between God and his people. “Unfaithfulness” and “prostitution” mean worshiping idols and trusting in foreign great powers instead of God. The message is spoken to all the people of Jerusalem, men and women alike, and above all to the powerful.
Some pictures in this chapter are violent and shaming. They come from a time and culture in which women had few rights. Many readers today find them hurtful, and that is understandable.
Very important: This chapter never justifies violence against women or against any person. Anyone who experiences violence deserves protection and help, not blame.
The chapter does not end with punishment but with God’s faithfulness: an everlasting covenant and forgiveness (verses 60–63).

## The abandoned child (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, make Jerusalem see her disgusting deeds.
[3] Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says to Jerusalem:
“You come from the land of the Canaanites.
You were born there.
Your father was an Amorite,
and your mother was a Hittite.
[4] On the day you were born,
no one cut your cord.
No one washed you with water to make you clean.
No one rubbed you with salt
or wrapped you in cloths.
[5] No one looked at you with pity.
No one did any of these things for you
or showed you kindness.
Instead, you were thrown out into the open field,
because people hated you on the day you were born.
[6] “‘“Then I passed by you
and saw you kicking about in your blood.
I said to you,
‘Even though you are lying in your blood, live!’
Yes, I said to you,
‘Even though you are lying in your blood, live!’
[7] I made you grow like the plants in the field.
You grew and became tall
and became very beautiful.
Your breasts were formed,
and your hair grew.
But you were still naked and bare.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Jerusalem was first a Canaanite city, before David captured it (2 Samuel 5:6–9). The message is: Jerusalem has no reason to be proud of where it came from. Everything it is, it owes to God.
> Verses 4–5: In those days, a newborn baby was washed, rubbed with salt (people believed this was good for the skin), and wrapped in cloths. This baby got none of that. It was abandoned, unwanted and hated. Sadly, abandoning babies, often girls, did happen in the ancient world.
> Verse 6: One of the most touching verses in the Bible: God passes by, sees the helpless child, and says: “Live!” Twice! God rescues where no one else helps. In Judaism, this verse is spoken at the circumcision of a baby boy, and it is connected with the Passover festival.

## God makes a covenant (Verses 8–14)

[8] “‘“Later I passed by you again and looked at you.
You were old enough for love.
So I spread my cloak over you
and covered your nakedness.
Yes, I promised myself to you
and made a covenant with you,”
says the Lord GOD,
“and you became mine.
[9] “‘“Then I washed you with water.
Yes, I washed all your blood off you,
and I rubbed you with oil.
[10] I also dressed you in clothes with colorful embroidery
and put leather sandals on your feet.
I wrapped you in fine linen
and covered you with silk.
[11] I decorated you with jewelry.
I put bracelets on your arms
and a necklace around your neck.
[12] I put a ring in your nose,
earrings in your ears,
and a beautiful crown on your head.
[13] So you were decorated with gold and silver.
Your clothes were made of fine linen, silk, and embroidered cloth.
You ate fine flour, honey, and oil.
You became very, very beautiful,
and you rose to be a queen.
[14] You became famous among the nations for your beauty.
It was perfect
because of the glory I had put on you,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: God says: “I spread my cloak over you.” This was a sign that a man wanted to marry and protect someone (compare Ruth 3:9). God enters into a covenant, a firm bond full of faithfulness. This means the covenant at Mount Sinai.
> Verses 9–13: God does everything the parents failed to do: washing, rubbing with oil, dressing, decorating, feeding. The despised abandoned baby becomes a queen. This reminds us of the great time of Jerusalem under David and Solomon.
> Verse 14: The beauty did not come from Jerusalem itself, but “because of the glory I had put on you.” Everything was a gift.

## The unfaithfulness (Verses 15–22)

[15] “‘“But you trusted in your beauty.
You used your fame to become a prostitute.
You poured out your prostitution on everyone who passed by.
You gave yourself to anyone.
[16] You took some of your clothes
and made colorful high places for yourself.
You acted like a prostitute on them.
Such things should not happen, and they must never be.
[17] You also took your beautiful jewelry
made of my gold and my silver, which I had given you.
You made images of men for yourself
and acted like a prostitute with them.
[18] You took your embroidered clothes and covered the images with them.
You set my oil and my incense in front of them.
[19] You also took my bread that I had given you,
the fine flour, oil, and honey I fed you with,
and you set it in front of them as a sweet-smelling offering.
That is what happened,” says the Lord GOD.
[20] “‘“You also took your sons and your daughters,
whom you had given birth to for me,
and you sacrificed them to the idols as food.
Was your prostitution not bad enough?
[21] You killed my children
and gave them up
by making them pass through the fire for the idols.
[22] In all your disgusting deeds and your prostitution,
you did not remember the days when you were young,
when you were naked and bare
and were kicking about in your blood.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jerusalem takes God’s gifts, clothes, gold, silver, flour, oil, and honey, and gives them to the idols. That is what “prostitution” means here: being unfaithful to God, who has given everything. The picture describes the relationship between God and his people. It is not a statement about women, and it does not look down on people in prostitution.
> Verses 20–21: The worst thing: child sacrifice. At times, children really were burned for foreign gods in Judah (compare 2 Kings 16:3; 21:6; Jeremiah 7:31). God calls them “my children.” He is on the side of the children.
> Verse 22: Jerusalem has forgotten where it came from. Ungratefulness and forgetting are often the beginning of moving away from God.

## Alliances with foreign powers (Verses 23–34)

[23] “‘“After all your wickedness
– how terrible, how terrible for you! says the Lord GOD –
[24] you built yourself a shrine with a vaulted roof,
and you made yourself a raised place in every square.
[25] You built your raised place at the start of every road.
You turned your beauty into something disgusting.
You offered yourself to everyone who passed by,
and you did more and more prostitution.
[26] You also slept around with the Egyptians,
your neighbors with the big bodies.
You did more and more prostitution to make me angry.
[27] So I have reached out my hand against you.
I have made your share smaller.
I have handed you over to the will of those who hate you,
the daughters of the Philistines.
Even they are ashamed of your shameless ways.
[28] You also acted like a prostitute with the Assyrians,
because you could never get enough.
Yes, you acted like a prostitute with them,
and still you were not satisfied.
[29] You did even more prostitution
with the land of merchants, with Chaldea.
And still this did not satisfy you.
[30] “‘“How weak your heart is,” says the Lord GOD,
“since you do all these things,
the things a shameless prostitute does!
[31] You build your vaulted shrine at the start of every road,
and you make your raised place in every square.
But you are not like a prostitute,
because you refuse to be paid.
[32] “‘“You are an unfaithful wife
who takes strangers instead of her husband!
[33] People give gifts to all prostitutes.
But you give your gifts to all your lovers.
You bribe them
to come to you from every side for your prostitution.
[34] So in your prostitution you are different from other women.
No one runs after you to sleep with you.
You pay, and no one pays you.
That is why you are different.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it is about politics: Jerusalem made alliances with Egypt, Assyria, and Babylon (Chaldea). Instead of trusting God, it looked for protection from the great powers, and in doing so it also took over their gods.
> Verse 26: “With the big bodies”: The WEB says literally “great of flesh.” It is a crude picture for how attractive the great power Egypt was.
> Verse 27: Even the Philistines, Israel’s old enemies, are ashamed of how Jerusalem behaves.
> Verses 31–34: A bitter paradox: Jerusalem does not even get anything for its unfaithfulness. Instead it pays for it itself. This means the heavy tribute payments to foreign kings (compare 2 Kings 16:8). Jerusalem gives away its riches to those who will exploit it in the end.

## The judgment (Verses 35–43)

[35] “So, you prostitute, listen to the LORD’s word!
[36] ‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Your filth was poured out,
and your nakedness was uncovered
through your prostitution with your lovers.
You worshiped all your disgusting idols,
and you gave them the blood of your children.
[37] That is why I will gather all your lovers,
the ones you enjoyed,
all the ones you loved
and all the ones you hated.
I will gather them against you from every side.
I will uncover your nakedness in front of them,
so that they see all your nakedness.
[38] I will judge you
the way women who break their marriage and spill blood are judged.
I will bring on you the bloodshed of anger and jealousy.
[39] I will also hand you over to them.
They will tear down your vaulted shrine
and break down your raised places.
They will take off your clothes
and take your beautiful jewelry.
They will leave you naked and bare.
[40] They will also bring a crowd against you.
They will throw stones at you
and stab you with their swords.
[41] They will burn your houses with fire.
They will carry out judgments on you while many women watch.
I will put an end to your prostitution,
and you will no longer pay anyone.
[42] Then my anger against you will come to rest,
and my jealousy will leave you.
I will be calm
and will not be angry anymore.
[43] “‘“You did not remember the days when you were young.
Instead, you raged against me with all these things.
So I will bring on your own head what you have done,”
says the Lord GOD.
“Then you will no longer do this shameful thing
on top of all your other disgusting deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are especially hard. In pictures, they describe what really happened to Jerusalem in 586 BC: The former “lovers,” that is, the great powers, turn against the city. The Babylonians loot it, tear down the walls, burn the houses, and kill many people (2 Kings 25:8–10).
> For this, Ezekiel uses the language of the punishments that were given for adultery and murder in those days. It is a picture from a world ruled by men, and the violence in it is shocking.
> Very important: This text is not an instruction and not a permission. It never justifies one person hitting, exposing, humiliating, or killing another person, not in marriage, and not because of unfaithfulness. Violence is wrong. Jesus clearly stood up for a woman who was about to be stoned (John 8:1–11).
> If you are experiencing violence in your relationship or family, it is not your fault, and you are not alone. Help is available for free, without giving your name, day and night: Domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the US: RAINN 1-800-656-4673. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Like mother, like daughter (Verses 44–52)

[44] “‘“Everyone who uses sayings
will use this saying about you:
‘Like mother, like daughter.’
[45] You are your mother’s daughter.
She hated her husband and her children.
And you are the sister of your sisters,
who hated their husbands and their children.
Your mother was a Hittite,
and your father was an Amorite.
[46] Your older sister is Samaria,
who lives on your left with her daughters.
And your younger sister, who lives on your right,
is Sodom with her daughters.
[47] You did not only follow their ways
and do the disgusting things they did.
Very soon you became even more corrupt than they were
in all your ways.
[48] As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“your sister Sodom and her daughters never did
what you and your daughters have done.
[49] “‘“This was the sin of your sister Sodom:
She and her daughters were proud.
They had more than enough food,
and they lived in comfort without a care.
But she did not help the poor and needy.
[50] They were arrogant
and did disgusting things in front of me.
So I removed them when I saw it.
[51] Samaria did not commit even half of your sins.
You have done many more disgusting things than they did.
All the disgusting things you have done
make your sisters look righteous.
[52] You must also carry your own shame,
because you have judged your sisters.
Your sins are more disgusting than theirs,
so they are more righteous than you.
Yes, be ashamed too, and carry your shame,
because you have made your sisters look righteous.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jerusalem thought it was better than Samaria (the northern kingdom that had been destroyed) and Sodom (the city God had destroyed, Genesis 19). But God says: You are worse than both of them!
> Verse 49: An important verse! Here the Bible itself says what the sin of Sodom was: pride, too much of everything, a life without a care, and not helping the poor and needy. Whoever is rich and full and forgets the poor is in danger of becoming like Sodom.
> Verse 52: God says to Jerusalem: “You must also carry your own shame, because you have judged your sisters.” Whoever is quick to judge others should first look at himself (compare Matthew 7:3–5).

## All will be restored (Verses 53–58)

[53] “‘“I will turn their fate around,
the fate of Sodom and her daughters,
and the fate of Samaria and her daughters.
And I will turn around the fate of your own captives among them.
[54] Then you will carry your own shame
and be ashamed of everything you have done,
because you are a comfort to them.
[55] Your sisters, Sodom and her daughters,
will go back to what they were before.
Samaria and her daughters
will go back to what they were before.
And you and your daughters
will go back to what you were before.
[56] In the days of your pride,
you would not even speak the name of your sister Sodom.
[57] That was before your wickedness was uncovered.
Now you are mocked like the daughters of Syria
and all who are around her,
and like the daughters of the Philistines.
All around you, people look down on you.
[58] You have carried the punishment for your shameful acts and your disgusting deeds,”
says the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> An amazing promise: God will restore not only Jerusalem, but also Samaria and even Sodom! God’s mercy is greater than Jerusalem could imagine.
> Verse 54: Jerusalem will be “a comfort” to the others. That means: If even Jerusalem finds grace after all this, then there is hope for the others too.
> Verse 56: In its pride, Jerusalem did not even want to speak the name Sodom. It looked down on the others.
> Verse 57: Here the WEB has “Syria” (Syria, which was called Aram in those days). Some Hebrew manuscripts have “Edom.” The two words look very much alike in Hebrew.

## An everlasting covenant (Verses 59–63)

[59] “‘For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will deal with you as you have deserved.
You have despised your promise
by breaking the covenant.
[60] But I will still remember my covenant
that I made with you in the days when you were young.
And I will set up an everlasting covenant with you.
[61] Then you will remember your ways and be ashamed
when you welcome your sisters,
both your older and your younger sisters.
I will give them to you as daughters,
but not because of your covenant.
[62] I will set up my covenant with you.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[63] Then you will remember and be ashamed.
You will never open your mouth again because of your shame,
when I have forgiven you for all that you have done,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is God’s great “And yet”! Jerusalem has broken the covenant. But God does not forget his covenant. He even makes an “everlasting covenant” that will never break.
> Verse 60: Jerusalem forgot “the days when you were young” (verses 22 and 43). But God remembers them. God’s faithfulness is stronger than people’s unfaithfulness (compare 2 Timothy 2:13).
> Verse 63: In the end there is forgiveness. The shame spoken of here is not a destructive shame. It is amazement at receiving so much grace, even though it is not deserved. You become silent out of thankfulness.
> Christians see a line from here to the “new covenant” (Jeremiah 31:31–34; Luke 22:20). For Jews, it is God’s lasting faithfulness to Israel.
