# Ezekiel – Chapter 28
### The king of Tyre

---

## “I am a god” (Verses 1–10)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[2] “Son of man, tell the prince of Tyre,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Your heart has become proud.
You have said,
‘I am a god.
I sit on the throne of God, in the middle of the seas.’
But you are a man and not a god,
even though you think in your heart that you are like a god.
[3] Look, you are wiser than Daniel.
No secret is hidden from you.
[4] With your wisdom and your understanding
you have gotten riches for yourself.
You have filled your treasure rooms with gold and silver.
[5] With your great wisdom and your trading
you have made your riches grow.
And your heart has become proud because of your riches.”
[6] “‘So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“You think in your heart that you are like God.
[7] So look, I will bring foreigners against you,
the most terrifying of the nations.
They will pull out their swords against your beautiful wisdom.
They will make your splendor unholy.
[8] They will throw you down into the pit.
You will die a violent death in the heart of the seas.
[9] Will you still say, ‘I am God,’
in front of the one who kills you?
You are a man and not God
in the hands of the one who wounds you.
[10] You will die the death of the uncircumcised
at the hands of foreigners.
For I have spoken,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The prince of Tyre is so rich and powerful that he thinks he is a god. That is the heart of all pride: A human being puts himself in God’s place.
> Verse 3: “Wiser than Daniel” is meant with irony. About “Daniel,” see Ezekiel 14:14.
> Verse 9: A sharp question: Will you still say “I am God” when someone kills you? At the latest in death it becomes clear: Every human being must die.
> Verse 10: To die “the death of the uncircumcised” means to die a shameful death, without honor.
> This warning is still true today: Whoever thinks that wealth, success, or power puts him above everything will one day notice that he is only a human being.

## Song of mourning: In the garden of Eden (Verses 11–19)

[11] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[12] “Son of man, sing a song of mourning about the king of Tyre.
Tell him,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“You were the seal of perfection,
full of wisdom
and perfect in beauty.
[13] You were in Eden, the garden of God.
Every precious stone decorated you:
ruby, topaz, emerald, chrysolite,
onyx, jasper, sapphire, turquoise, and beryl.
Gold work of tambourines and of flutes was in you.
They were prepared on the day you were created.
[14] You were the anointed cherub who covers and protects.
I set you on the holy mountain of God.
You walked back and forth among the stones of fire.
[15] You were perfect in your ways
from the day you were created,
until wrongdoing was found in you.
[16] Because you traded so much,
you were filled with violence inside,
and you sinned.
So I threw you out of God’s mountain as something unholy.
I destroyed you, you cherub who covers,
and drove you away from the stones of fire.
[17] Your heart became proud because of your beauty.
You ruined your wisdom because of your splendor.
I threw you to the ground.
I laid you down in front of kings,
so that they could look at you.
[18] You sinned so much,
and you were so dishonest in your trading,
that you made your holy places unholy.
So I made a fire come out from inside you.
It burned you up.
I turned you to ashes on the ground
in front of everyone who sees you.
[19] All who know you among the peoples
are shocked at you.
You have become a horror,
and you will be no more.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This song of mourning is one of the most mysterious passages in the Bible. The king of Tyre is described like a beautiful being in the garden of Eden, decorated with precious stones, a “cherub” on God’s holy mountain. Then he becomes proud, wrongdoing is found in him, and he is thrown out.
> This is like the story of Adam in paradise (Genesis 2–3): created perfect, then fallen, then thrown out.
> Verse 14: A cherub is a powerful heavenly being.
> Verse 13: The precious stones are like the stones on the breastpiece of the high priest (Exodus 28:17–20).
> How do we read this text?
> – Many Jewish and Christian Bible teachers say: Ezekiel uses pictures from old stories about paradise to describe the deep fall of the proud king. What is meant is the human king of Tyre.
> – Later, many Christians also saw in this text (together with Isaiah 14:12–15) a picture of the fall of Satan, an angel who turned away from God out of pride. The text itself does not say this directly, but this understanding is widespread in Christian tradition.
> Verse 16: Again trade and violence are named together. Greed often leads to violence and injustice.

## Against Sidon (Verses 20–23)

[20] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[21] “Son of man, turn your face toward Sidon,
and prophesy against it.
[22] Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I am against you, Sidon.
I will show my glory in you.
Then they will know that I am the LORD,
when I carry out judgment on her
and show that I am holy in her.
[23] For I will send a deadly disease into her
and blood into her streets.
The wounded will fall inside her,
with the sword against her on every side.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sidon was the neighbor and sister city of Tyre. It was also a rich Phoenician port city (today Saida in Lebanon). Queen Jezebel came from Sidon. She had spread the worship of Baal in Israel (1 Kings 16:31).

## Israel will live in safety (Verses 24–26)

[24] “‘“The people of Israel will no longer have a briar that pricks them
or a thorn that hurts them
from any of the nations around them that treated them with scorn.
Then they will know that I am the Lord GOD.”
[25] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will gather the people of Israel from the peoples
among whom they are scattered.
I will show that I am holy among them in front of the nations.
Then they will live in their own land,
which I gave to my servant Jacob.
[26] They will live there in safety.
Yes, they will build houses and plant vineyards.
They will live in safety
when I have carried out judgment on all those around them
who have treated them with contempt.
Then they will know that I am the LORD their God.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the words of judgment about the nations comes a ray of hope for Israel: God will gather his people, and they will live in the land in safety. Building houses and planting vineyards are signs of peace and of a normal, good life (see Jeremiah 29:5; Amos 9:14).
