# Ezekiel – Chapter 43
### The glory comes back

---

## God’s glory fills the temple (Verses 1–5)

[1] After that he brought me to the gate,
the gate that faces east.
[2] Look, the glory of the God of Israel was coming from the east.
His voice was like the roar of many waters,
and the earth shone with his glory.
[3] What I saw looked like the vision I had seen before,
like the vision I saw when I came to destroy the city.
And the visions were like the vision
I had seen by the river Chebar.
I fell on my face.
[4] The glory of the LORD came into the temple
through the gate that faces east.
[5] The Spirit lifted me up
and brought me into the inner courtyard.
Look, the glory of the LORD filled the temple.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point of the whole book!
> In Chapters 10–11, Ezekiel had seen God’s glory leave the temple through the east gate and go to the Mount of Olives in the east. Now it comes back the same way: from the east, through the east gate, into the temple.
> Verse 2: “Like the roar of many waters” – the same picture as in Ezekiel 1:24. Revelation describes the risen Christ with these words (Revelation 1:15).
> Verse 3: “When I came to destroy the city” means: when I announced that the city would be destroyed (Chapters 8–11).
> Verse 5: God’s glory fills the temple, as it did long ago when the tent of meeting was dedicated (Exodus 40:34) and when Solomon’s temple was dedicated (1 Kings 8:10–11). God is back. He lives with his people again.

---

## The place of my throne (Verses 6–12)

[6] I heard someone speaking to me from inside the temple,
while a man stood beside me.
[7] He said to me,
“Son of man, this is the place of my throne
and the place for the soles of my feet.
Here I will live forever among the people of Israel.
The people of Israel will never again make my holy name unclean,
neither they nor their kings,
by their unfaithfulness
and by the dead bodies of their kings at their shrines.
[8] They put their threshold next to my threshold
and their doorpost next to my doorpost.
Only a wall was between me and them.
They made my holy name unclean
by the disgusting things they did.
So I destroyed them in my anger.
[9] Now let them put away their unfaithfulness
and the dead bodies of their kings far from me.
Then I will live among them forever.
[10] “You, son of man, show the temple to the people of Israel,
so that they will be ashamed of their sins.
Let them measure the plan.
[11] If they are ashamed of everything they have done,
then show them the design of the temple,
its layout, its exits, its entrances,
its whole structure, all its rules,
all its designs, and all its laws.
Write it down where they can see it,
so that they keep its whole design
and all its rules and do them.
[12] “This is the law of the temple:
All the area around it
on the top of the mountain will be most holy.
Look, this is the law of the temple.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: God wants to live here, “forever among the people of Israel.” The temple is God’s throne and the footstool for his feet on earth.
> Verses 7–8: In old Jerusalem, the king’s palace was right next to the temple. Only a wall separated them. The kings made the temple unholy with their idols, and maybe also with graves or monuments for dead kings. In the new temple this will not happen anymore.
> Verses 10–11: When the people see the beautiful plan of the new temple, they are to realize how far they had moved away from God. God’s kindness leads people to turn back (see Romans 2:4).
> Verse 12: The “law of the temple” is very simple: Everything is holy.

## The altar (Verses 13–17)

[13] “These are the measurements of the altar in long cubits.
Each of these cubits is a normal cubit plus a hand width.
The gutter at the bottom is to be about 21 inches deep
and about 21 inches wide.
The rim around its edge is to be about 10 inches.
This is to be the base of the altar.
[14] From the gutter on the ground up to the lower ledge, it is to be about 3½ feet high,
and the ledge about 21 inches wide.
From the smaller ledge up to the larger ledge, it is to be about 7 feet high,
and the ledge about 21 inches wide.
[15] The top part of the altar is to be about 7 feet high.
From the hearth of the altar four horns are to stick up.
[16] The hearth of the altar is to be about 21 feet long and about 21 feet wide.
It is to be a square with four equal sides.
[17] The ledge is to be about 24½ feet long and about 24½ feet wide,
square on its four sides.
The rim around it is to be about 10½ inches,
and the gutter around it about 21 inches.
Its steps are to face east.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The altar is built like a tower with steps, with several ledges one above the other. At the very top is the hearth, the place where the fire burns, 12 by 12 cubits (about 21 by 21 feet), with four “horns” at the corners. A long cubit is about 21 inches.
> The horns of the altar were especially holy. Whoever needed protection could hold on to them (1 Kings 1:50).
> All together the altar is about 11 cubits high, more than 19 feet.

## The dedication of the altar (Verses 18–27)

[18] He said to me,
“Son of man, this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘These are the rules for the altar
on the day it is built,
so that burnt offerings can be offered on it
and blood can be sprinkled on it.
[19] You must give a young bull as a sin offering
to the priests from the tribe of Levi
who are descendants of Zadok
and who come near to me to serve me,’
says the Lord GOD.
[20] ‘You must take some of its blood
and put it on the four horns of the altar,
on the four corners of the ledge,
and on the rim all around.
That is how you will make it clean and make atonement for it.
[21] You must also take the bull for the sin offering.
It must be burned at the place chosen for it in the temple area,
outside the sanctuary.
[22] “‘On the second day you must offer a male goat without any defect
as a sin offering.
The priests must make the altar clean,
as they made it clean with the bull.
[23] When you have finished making it clean,
you must offer a young bull without any defect
and a ram from the flock without any defect.
[24] You must bring them before the LORD.
The priests must throw salt on them
and offer them up to the LORD as a burnt offering.
[25] “‘For seven days you must prepare a goat every day as a sin offering.
They must also prepare a young bull and a ram from the flock,
without any defect.
[26] For seven days they must make atonement for the altar
and make it clean.
That is how they will set it apart for God.
[27] When these days are over,
from the eighth day on,
the priests will offer your burnt offerings
and your peace offerings on the altar.
Then I will accept you,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before the altar may be used, it is made clean and set apart for God for seven days. This is like the dedication of the first altar by Moses (Exodus 29:36–37).
> Verse 20: To make atonement means to take away guilt so that things are right with God again.
> Verse 24: The salt was a sign of the lasting covenant between God and his people (see Leviticus 2:13: “the salt of the covenant”).
> Verse 27: At the end comes the goal: “Then I will accept you.” It is not about the ritual for its own sake, but about the restored relationship with God.
> Christians believe that the sacrifices of the temple found their fulfillment in Jesus Christ (Hebrews 10:10–14). In Judaism today, without a temple, prayer, the study of the Torah, and good deeds take the place of the sacrifices.
