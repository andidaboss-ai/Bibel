# Ezekiel – Chapter 3
### The watchman

---

## Ezekiel eats the scroll (Verses 1–11)

[1] He said to me,
“Son of man, eat what you find.
Eat this scroll,
and go and speak to the people of Israel.”
[2] So I opened my mouth,
and he gave me the scroll to eat.
[3] He said to me,
“Son of man, eat this scroll that I am giving you.
Fill your stomach and your insides with it.”
Then I ate it.
It was as sweet as honey in my mouth.
[4] He said to me,
“Son of man, go to the people of Israel,
and speak my words to them.
[5] For I am not sending you to a people
whose language is strange and hard to understand.
I am sending you to the people of Israel.
[6] I am not sending you to many peoples
whose language is strange and hard to understand,
whose words you cannot understand.
If I sent you to them,
they would certainly listen to you.
[7] But the people of Israel will not listen to you,
because they will not listen to me.
For all the people of Israel are stubborn and hard-hearted.
[8] Look, I have made your face as hard as their faces,
and your forehead as hard as their foreheads.
[9] I have made your forehead like a diamond,
harder than flint.
Do not be afraid of them.
Do not be frightened by the way they look at you,
even though they are a rebellious people.”
[10] He also said to me,
“Son of man, take into your heart all my words that I speak to you,
and listen to them with your ears.
[11] Go to the captives,
to the people of your own nation.
Speak to them and tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says,’
whether they listen or refuse to listen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Ezekiel eats the scroll. This is a picture: God’s word should go deep into him and become a part of him. And even though the message is bitter, it tastes “as sweet as honey.” Receiving God’s word is a gift, even when the message is hard (compare Jeremiah 15:16; Psalm 119:103; Revelation 10:9–10).
> Verses 5–7: A sad irony: Foreign peoples with a foreign language would listen to Ezekiel. But his own people, who understand his language, do not want to listen.
> Verses 8–9: Because the people have hard foreheads, God makes Ezekiel’s forehead even harder, “like a diamond.” This is a play on his name: Ezekiel means “God makes strong.”

## Seven days without words (Verses 12–15)

[12] Then the Spirit lifted me up.
Behind me I heard the sound of a great rushing noise:
“Blessed be the LORD’s glory from his place!”
[13] I heard the noise of the wings of the living creatures
as they touched one another,
and the noise of the wheels beside them,
the sound of a great rushing noise.
[14] So the Spirit lifted me up and took me away.
I went full of bitterness, with my spirit burning inside me.
And the LORD’s hand was strong on me.
[15] Then I came to the captives at Tel Aviv,
who lived by the river Chebar.
I came to the place where they lived.
I sat there among them for seven days,
completely shaken.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Blessed be the LORD’s glory from his place!” This sentence is part of an important Jewish prayer, the “Kedushah,” together with “Holy, holy, holy” from Isaiah 6:3.
> Verse 14: Ezekiel is shaken and bitter. Meeting God has upset him deeply.
> Verse 15: Tel Aviv (many Bibles write “Tel Abib”) means “hill of ears of grain.” The modern city of Tel Aviv in Israel (founded in 1909) got its name from here in a roundabout way: The Hebrew translation of Theodor Herzl’s book “Old New Land” was called “Tel Aviv,” and that title was taken from this verse. Ezekiel sits among the exiles for seven days without speaking. Seven days was the usual time of mourning.

## I am making you a watchman (Verses 16–21)

[16] At the end of the seven days, the LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[17] “Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the people of Israel.
So listen to the word from my mouth,
and warn them for me.
[18] I may say to a wicked man, ‘You will certainly die.’
If you do not warn him,
and you do not speak to warn him to leave his wicked way
so that he can save his life,
then that wicked man will die because of his sin.
But I will hold you responsible for his death.
[19] But if you warn the wicked man,
and he does not turn away from his wickedness and his wicked way,
he will die because of his sin.
But you have saved yourself.”
[20] “And when a righteous man turns away from doing what is right
and does wrong,
and I put a stumbling block in front of him,
he will die.
Because you did not warn him,
he will die in his sin.
The good things he did will not be remembered.
But I will hold you responsible for his death.
[21] But if you warn the righteous man not to sin,
and he does not sin,
he will certainly live, because he listened to the warning.
And you have saved yourself.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the past, a watchman stood on the city wall. If he saw danger, he had to sound the alarm. If he stayed silent, he was to blame for the disaster.
> In the same way, Ezekiel is to be a “watchman” for his people: He is to warn them. Whether the people listen is their responsibility. But if he does not warn them, he shares the responsibility.
> Verses 20–21: Righteous means: living right before God.
> This is also a message for today: Whoever sees wrong and stays silent shares the responsibility. Sometimes it is our task to warn people honestly, even when it is uncomfortable.

## Ezekiel cannot speak (Verses 22–27)

[22] The LORD’s hand was on me there.
He said to me,
“Get up, go out into the plain,
and I will talk with you there.”
[23] So I got up and went out into the plain.
And the LORD’s glory was standing there,
like the glory I had seen by the river Chebar.
Then I fell on my face.
[24] Then the Spirit came into me and set me on my feet.
He spoke with me and said to me,
“Go, shut yourself inside your house.
[25] And you, son of man,
they will put ropes on you and tie you up with them.
You will not be able to go out among the people.
[26] I will make your tongue stick to the roof of your mouth,
so that you cannot speak
and cannot correct them,
for they are a rebellious people.
[27] But when I speak with you,
I will open your mouth.
Then you must tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says.’
Whoever wants to listen, let him listen.
Whoever refuses, let him refuse.
For they are a rebellious people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel must shut himself inside his house and be silent. He may only speak when God gives him something to say. This makes it clear: Ezekiel does not speak his own thoughts, but only God’s word.
> This silence lasts until the destruction of Jerusalem (compare 24:27; 33:22).
> Verse 27: “Whoever wants to listen, let him listen.” Jesus says something similar: “Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear!” (Matthew 13:9).
