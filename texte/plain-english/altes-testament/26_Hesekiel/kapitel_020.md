# Ezekiel – Chapter 20
### God acts for the sake of his name

---

## The elders come to ask God (Verses 1–4)

[1] It was in the seventh year, in the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month.
Some of the elders of Israel came to ask the LORD for advice.
They sat down in front of me.
[2] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[3] “Son of man, speak to the elders of Israel and tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Have you come to ask me for advice?
As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“I will not let you ask me anything.”’
[4] “Will you judge them, son of man?
Will you judge them?
Make them see the disgusting things their fathers did.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is in the year 591 before Christ, about five years before the destruction of Jerusalem.
> The elders want an answer from God. But God says: No. He will not let himself be asked by people who only want to use him but do not want to turn back.
> Instead, they get a long look back at their history. It shows: From the beginning, the people turned away from God again and again, and yet God stayed faithful again and again.

---

## In Egypt (Verses 5–10)

[5] “Tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“On the day I chose Israel,
I made a promise to the descendants of the family of Jacob.
I made myself known to them in the land of Egypt.
I promised them and said,
‘I am the LORD your God.’
[6] On that day I promised them
that I would bring them out of the land of Egypt
into a land I had chosen for them.
It is a land flowing with milk and honey,
the most beautiful of all lands.
[7] I said to them,
‘Each of you, throw away the disgusting idols you look at.
Do not make yourselves unclean with the idols of Egypt.
I am the LORD your God.’
[8] “‘“But they rebelled against me
and would not listen to me.
Not all of them threw away the disgusting idols they looked at.
They did not leave the idols of Egypt.
Then I said I would pour out my anger on them
and finish my anger against them
right there in the land of Egypt.
[9] But I acted for the sake of my name,
so that it would not be dishonored
in front of the nations they were living among.
Those nations had seen me make myself known to my people
by bringing them out of the land of Egypt.
[10] So I led them out of the land of Egypt
and brought them into the wilderness.

> **What does this mean?**
> God tells the history of Israel again, and he tells it very honestly. Already in Egypt, many clung to the Egyptian idols.
> Verse 9: The key sentence of the chapter: “But I acted for the sake of my name.” God did not save Israel because it was so good, but because God is faithful to what he has promised. His “name” stands for his reputation and who he is. The nations were to see who God really is.

---

## In the wilderness: the first generation (Verses 11–17)

[11] I gave them my rules
and taught them my laws.
Whoever obeys them will live by them.
[12] I also gave them my Sabbaths
as a sign between me and them,
so that they would know that I am the LORD,
who makes them holy.
[13] “‘“But the people of Israel rebelled against me in the wilderness.
They did not live by my rules,
and they rejected my laws.
Whoever keeps them will live by them.
They completely dishonored my Sabbaths.
Then I said I would pour out my anger on them in the wilderness
and destroy them.
[14] But I acted for the sake of my name,
so that it would not be dishonored in front of the nations
who had seen me bring my people out.
[15] I also promised them in the wilderness
that I would not bring them into the land I had given them,
the land flowing with milk and honey,
the most beautiful of all lands.
[16] I did this because they rejected my laws,
did not live by my rules,
and dishonored my Sabbaths.
For their hearts followed their idols.
[17] But my eye had pity on them,
and I did not destroy them.
I did not put an end to them in the wilderness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: God’s commandments are good. They are there so that people can “live” (compare Leviticus 18:5). They are not a burden but a gift for a good life.
> Verse 12: The Sabbath is a sign of the special relationship between God and his people (Exodus 31:13). Especially in exile, without a temple, the Sabbath became very important for the Jews.
> Verses 15–17: The first generation was not allowed to enter the promised land (Numbers 14). But God did not destroy them. He spared them.

---

## In the wilderness: the second generation (Verses 18–26)

[18] “‘“I said to their children in the wilderness,
‘Do not live by the rules of your fathers.
Do not follow their laws,
and do not make yourselves unclean with their idols.
[19] I am the LORD your God.
Live by my rules.
Keep my laws and obey them.
[20] Keep my Sabbaths holy.
They will be a sign between me and you,
so that you will know that I am the LORD your God.’
[21] “‘“But the children rebelled against me too.
They did not live by my rules.
They did not keep my laws and obey them.
Whoever obeys them will live by them.
They dishonored my Sabbaths.
Then I said I would pour out my anger on them
and finish my anger against them in the wilderness.
[22] But I held back my hand
and acted for the sake of my name,
so that it would not be dishonored in front of the nations
who had seen me bring my people out.
[23] I also promised them in the wilderness
that I would scatter them among the nations
and spread them through the countries.
[24] I did this because they had not obeyed my laws.
They had rejected my rules
and dishonored my Sabbaths,
and their eyes longed for their fathers’ idols.
[25] I also gave them rules that were not good,
and laws by which they could not live.
[26] I let them make themselves unclean through their own gifts.
They made every firstborn child pass through the fire.
I did this to fill them with horror,
so that they would know that I am the LORD.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> The second generation makes the same mistakes. And again God acts “for the sake of his name” and spares them.
> Verses 25–26: These are very difficult verses. “Rules that were not good” cannot mean that God gave bad commandments. In other places, God clearly forbids child sacrifice (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 18:10; Jeremiah 7:31, where God says that he never commanded it and that it never even came into his mind).
> A common explanation: God let them go their own wrong way. Because they did not want his good commandments, he left them to the bad customs they chose for themselves, even to the cruel child sacrifices of the foreign religions. (Compare Romans 1:24: “So God gave them up to the lusts of their hearts …”)
> Another explanation: The commandment to “give” the firstborn to God (Exodus 13:2, 12) was completely misunderstood by some people and misused as child sacrifice. But God always wanted the firstborn to be bought back (Exodus 13:13).

## In the promised land (Verses 27–29)

[27] “So, son of man, speak to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Your fathers insulted me in this way too:
they were unfaithful to me.
[28] I brought them into the land
that I had promised to give them.
But whenever they saw a high hill or a thick tree,
they offered their sacrifices there.
There they brought the offerings that made me angry.
There they also burned their sweet-smelling incense,
and there they poured out their drink offerings.
[29] Then I said to them,
‘What is this high place you go to?’
That is why it is called Bamah to this day.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> In the promised land it went on: On every hill and under every tree, they made sacrifices to foreign gods.
> Verse 29: “Bamah” is the Hebrew word for “high place” or “hilltop shrine.” There is probably a play on words here: The word sounds a bit like “ba” (to come, to go there) and “mah” (what). So it means something like: “What is this place you go to?” God is mocking these places of worship.

---

## You want to be like the other nations (Verses 30–32)

[30] “So tell the people of Israel,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Will you make yourselves unclean the way your fathers did?
Will you be unfaithful to me and chase after their disgusting idols?
[31] When you bring your gifts,
when you make your sons pass through the fire,
you make yourselves unclean with all your idols to this very day.
And should I let you ask me for advice, people of Israel?
As surely as I live, says the Lord GOD,
I will not let you ask me anything!
[32] “‘“What you have in mind will never happen.
You say,
‘We want to be like the nations,
like the peoples of other countries,
and serve wood and stone.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God comes to the present: The elders are doing the same as their fathers.
> Verse 32: Here it comes out what they really think: “We want to be like the nations, like the peoples of other countries, and serve wood and stone.” In exile, the temptation was great simply to fit in, to worship the gods of the Babylonians, and to give up their own identity.
> But God says: That will not happen. God does not let go of his people.

---

## A new exodus (Verses 33–38)

[33] As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“with a mighty hand,
with an outstretched arm,
and with anger poured out,
I will be king over you.
[34] I will bring you out from the peoples.
I will gather you out of the countries where you are scattered,
with a mighty hand, with an outstretched arm,
and with anger poured out.
[35] I will bring you into the wilderness of the peoples.
There I will judge you
face to face.
[36] I judged your fathers in the wilderness of the land of Egypt.
In the same way I will judge you,”
says the Lord GOD.
[37] “I will make you pass under the shepherd’s staff,
and I will bring you into the bond of the covenant.
[38] I will remove from among you the rebels
and those who disobey me.
I will bring them out of the land where they are living as foreigners,
but they will not come into the land of Israel.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> God announces a new exodus, like the one from Egypt long ago, “by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm” (compare Deuteronomy 5:15). This time it is out of Babylon and out of all the countries.
> Verses 35–36: As in the wilderness long ago, there will be a time of testing.
> Verse 37: God says: “I will make you pass under the shepherd’s staff.” That is how a shepherd counted his sheep. Each sheep walked under his staff, and he looked at it closely. God knows each person.

## On my holy mountain (Verses 39–44)

[39] “‘As for you, people of Israel, this is what the Lord GOD says:
“Go ahead, each of you, serve your idols,
now and later too, if you will not listen to me.
But you will no longer dishonor my holy name
with your gifts and your idols.
[40] For on my holy mountain,
on the high mountain of Israel,”
says the Lord GOD,
“there all the people of Israel, all of them,
will serve me in the land.
There I will accept them.
There I will ask for your offerings
and the best of your gifts,
with all your holy things.
[41] I will accept you like a sweet-smelling offering
when I bring you out from the peoples
and gather you out of the countries where you have been scattered.
I will show through you that I am holy
while the nations watch.
[42] You will know that I am the LORD
when I bring you into the land of Israel,
into the country I promised to give to your fathers.
[43] There you will remember your ways
and all the things you did to make yourselves unclean.
Then you will be disgusted with yourselves
because of all the evil things you have done.
[44] You will know that I am the LORD
when I deal with you for the sake of my name,
not as your evil ways deserve
and not as your corrupt deeds deserve,
people of Israel,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 39: God speaks with bitter irony: “Go ahead, each of you, serve your idols, now and later too, if you will not listen to me. But you will no longer dishonor my holy name …” That means: Do not pretend that you are serving me. God does not want divided loyalty.
> Verses 40–42: Then comes the promise: All Israel will come together again on God’s holy mountain (Zion in Jerusalem) and serve God. God will accept them “like a sweet-smelling offering.”
> Verse 43: When they experience God’s goodness, only then will they really see how wrongly they have acted. True sorrow over sin often does not come through punishment but through experiencing love.
> Verse 44: At the end, the key sentence again: God deals with them “for the sake of my name” and “not as your evil ways deserve.” That is grace: God does not treat us as we deserve (compare Psalm 103:10).

## Fire in the forest of the south (Verses 45–49)

[45] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[46] “Son of man, turn your face toward the south.
Preach against the south,
and prophesy against the forest of the field in the south.
[47] Say to the forest of the south,
‘Listen to the LORD’s word!
This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will light a fire in you.
It will burn up every green tree in you
and every dry tree.
The blazing flame will not be put out.
Every face from the south to the north will be burned by it.
[48] All people will see that I, the LORD, have lit it.
It will not be put out.”’”
[49] Then I said,
“Oh, Lord GOD!
They are saying about me,
‘Isn’t he just telling stories in pictures?’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Note: In some Bibles these verses are Chapter 21:1–5. Here we follow the numbering of the WEB.
> Seen from Babylon, the “south” is Judah and Jerusalem (people came into the land from the north). A forest fire will burn up everything, green and dry trees, that is, righteous and unrighteous people. In Chapter 21 the picture is explained.
> Verse 49: Ezekiel complains: The people do not take him seriously. They say: “Isn’t he just telling stories in pictures?” You can brush off God’s word as mere entertainment, but then you miss its serious message.
