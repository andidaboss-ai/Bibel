# Ezekiel – Chapter 47
### The river from the temple

---

## The water gets deeper and deeper (Verses 1–6)

[1] He brought me back to the entrance of the temple.
Look, water was flowing out from under the threshold of the temple toward the east,
for the front of the temple faced east.
The water was flowing down from under
the right side of the temple,
south of the altar.
[2] Then he brought me out through the north gate.
He led me around on the outside to the outer gate,
the gate that faces east.
Look, water was trickling out on the right side.
[3] The man went out toward the east with a measuring cord in his hand.
He measured about 1,750 feet
and led me through the water.
The water came up to my ankles.
[4] Again he measured about 1,750 feet
and led me through the water.
The water came up to my knees.
Again he measured about 1,750 feet
and led me through water that came up to my waist.
[5] Then he measured about 1,750 feet more.
Now it was a river that I could not walk through.
The water had risen.
It was deep enough to swim in,
a river that no one could walk through.
[6] He said to me,
“Son of man, have you seen this?”
Then he led me back to the bank of the river.

> **What does this mean?**
> A beautiful vision: Water comes out from under the threshold of the temple. At first it is only a little stream. But it gets deeper and deeper: up to the ankles, up to the knees, up to the waist, and finally it is a river you have to swim in.
> The measurement is 1,000 cubits each time, about 1,750 feet.
> The amazing thing: Normally a river only gets bigger when other streams flow into it. This river grows all by itself. It comes directly from God.
> The picture shows: Where God lives, life flows. And this life grows and becomes more and more.
> Jesus says: “The Scriptures say this about whoever believes in me: Rivers of living water will flow from inside him” (John 7:38). See also Psalm 46:4 (in some Bibles 46:5): “There is a river. Its streams make the city of God glad.”

## The water makes everything healthy (Verses 7–12)

[7] When I came back,
look, there were very many trees on the bank of the river,
on both sides.
[8] Then he said to me,
“This water flows out toward the eastern region.
It will go down into the Arabah.
Then it will flow toward the sea
and into the sea, which will be made to flow out.
And the water there will become healthy and fresh.
[9] Every living creature that swarms
will live wherever the river goes.
There will be very many fish,
because this water goes there.
The water of the sea will become healthy,
and everything will live wherever the river goes.
[10] Fishermen will stand along its shore.
From En Gedi all the way to En Eglaim there will be places to spread out nets.
There will be many kinds of fish,
like the fish of the great sea,
very many.
[11] But its swamps and marshes will not become fresh.
They will be left for salt.
[12] On both banks of the river
all kinds of fruit trees will grow.
Their leaves will not dry up,
and their fruit will not fail.
They will bear new fruit every month,
because their water flows out from the sanctuary.
Their fruit will be for food,
and their leaves for healing.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The river flows through the desert (the Arabah is the valley of the Jordan down to the Dead Sea) and then into the Dead Sea. The Dead Sea is so salty that no fish can live in it. But this water makes even the Dead Sea come alive! Fishermen will throw out their nets there.
> En Gedi is an oasis on the west shore of the Dead Sea that still exists today.
> Verse 11: The swamps stay salty. Salt was important for life back then (for seasoning food and for keeping it from spoiling). God provides for everything.
> Verse 12: Trees that bear fruit every month and whose leaves heal. That is a picture of paradise. At the end of the Bible this picture is taken up: “The angel showed me a river of the water of life … On both sides of the river was the tree of life. It bears twelve kinds of fruit. It gives its fruit every month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations” (Revelation 22:1–2).
> The message: God’s presence changes even what is dead into what is alive, the desert into a garden.

## The borders of the land (Verses 13–21)

[13] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“These are the borders
by which you must divide the land to own
among the twelve tribes of Israel.
Joseph will have two shares.
[14] You must divide it equally, each tribe as much as another.
For I swore with raised hand to give it to your ancestors.
This land will be yours to own.
[15] “This will be the border of the land:
On the north side it goes from the great sea,
by the road to Hethlon,
to the entrance of Zedad,
[16] to Hamath, Berothah, and Sibraim
(which is between the border of Damascus and the border of Hamath),
as far as Hazer Hatticon, which is on the border of Hauran.
[17] So the border will run from the sea to Hazar Enon,
on the border of Damascus.
To the north is the border of Hamath.
This is the north side.
[18] “On the east side
the border runs between Hauran and Damascus,
and between Gilead and the land of Israel.
It is the Jordan.
You must measure from the north border to the eastern sea.
This is the east side.
[19] “On the south side
the border goes from Tamar
to the waters of Meriboth Kadesh,
along the brook
to the great sea.
This is the south side.
[20] “On the west side the border is the great sea,
from the south border to a point across from the entrance of Hamath.
This is the west side.
[21] “So you must divide this land among yourselves
by the tribes of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the borders of the new land are described. They are like the borders in Numbers 34:1–12.
> The “great sea” is the Mediterranean Sea. The “eastern sea” is the Dead Sea. The “brook” is the Brook of Egypt (Wadi el-Arish).
> Verse 13: Joseph gets two shares, for his sons Ephraim and Manasseh (see Genesis 48:5). So there are still twelve shares, even though the tribe of Levi does not get any land of its own.
> Verse 14: “Each tribe as much as another” – all the tribes get shares of the same size. Justice for everyone.
> These borders describe a vision and a promise. They are not a plan for political borders today.

## The foreigners get a share too (Verses 22–23)

[22] You must divide it by lot
as land to own for yourselves
and for the foreigners who live among you
and who have children among you.
They will be to you like those born among the people of Israel.
They will get land to own with you
among the tribes of Israel.
[23] In whatever tribe the foreigner lives,
there you must give him his share of land,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A revolutionary sentence for that time! The foreigners who live among Israel for good and have their families there are to get land just like those born in the land. They are to be “like those born among the people of Israel.”
> In the old law, the land to inherit was tied to the tribes of Israel. Foreigners usually did not have their own land to inherit. Here Ezekiel takes a big step further: In God’s new land, the foreigners fully belong.
> This is a strong message for today: People who have come to us from other countries and live here are to receive equal rights and a real home. God shuts no one out (see Leviticus 19:34: “The foreigner who lives with you shall be to you like someone born among you. And you shall love him as yourself”).
