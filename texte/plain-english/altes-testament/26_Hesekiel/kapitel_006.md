# Ezekiel – Chapter 6
### Against the mountains of Israel

---

## The high places will be destroyed (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, turn your face toward the mountains of Israel,
and prophesy to them.
[3] Say,
‘You mountains of Israel, listen to the word of the Lord GOD!
This is what the Lord GOD says
to the mountains and to the hills,
to the streams and to the valleys:
“I, yes, I myself, will bring a sword against you,
and I will destroy your high places.
[4] Your altars will be ruined,
and your incense altars will be broken.
I will throw down your dead in front of your idols.
[5] I will lay the dead bodies of the people of Israel
in front of their idols.
I will scatter your bones all around your altars.
[6] Wherever you live,
the cities will be ruined,
and the high places will be empty and destroyed.
Then your altars will be ruined and destroyed.
Your idols will be broken and will be gone.
Your incense altars will be cut down.
And the things you have made will be wiped out.
[7] People will be killed and will fall among you.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> On the mountains and hills of Israel there were many “high places.” Foreign gods were worshiped there. Often the LORD was also worshiped there, mixed with foreign customs.
> God announces: These places will be destroyed. The idols the people trusted cannot save them. The dead bodies lie in front of the idols, and the idols can do nothing.
> Verse 7: “Then you will know that I am the LORD.” This sentence comes more than 60 times in the book of Ezekiel. It is the goal of everything: People should come to know who God really is.

---

## A remnant will remember (Verses 8–10)

[8] “‘“But I will leave a remnant.
Some of you will escape the sword among the nations,
when you are scattered through the countries.
[9] Those of you who escape
will remember me among the nations
where they have been taken as captives.
They will remember how I was broken
by their unfaithful hearts, which turned away from me,
and by their eyes, which were unfaithful to me and chased after their idols.
Then they will be disgusted with themselves
because of the evil things they did
with all their disgusting deeds.
[10] They will know that I am the LORD.
I did not say for nothing
that I would bring this disaster on them.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the judgment, hope comes: A remnant will survive. A remnant is a small group of people who are left. In exile they will remember God.
> Verse 9: A touching sentence: God says that he “was broken” by their unfaithful hearts. God is not cold and without feeling. The unfaithfulness of his people hurts him, like a person whose love has been betrayed.
> The Hebrew text uses a picture of prostitution here. It describes being unfaithful to God, like adultery. It is a picture of the relationship between God and his people. It says nothing bad about women or about people in prostitution.
> “Disgusted with themselves” means: seeing honestly what they have done wrong. That is the first step toward turning back. But it is not about hating yourself forever. God wants people to turn back and live.

---

## A lament over Israel (Verses 11–14)

[11] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Clap your hands and stamp your foot,
and say, “How terrible!”
because of all the evil and disgusting deeds of the people of Israel.
For they will die by the sword, by hunger, and by disease.
[12] Whoever is far away will die of disease.
Whoever is near will be killed by the sword.
Whoever is left and is shut in by the siege will die of hunger.
That is how I will finish pouring out my anger on them.
[13] You will know that I am the LORD
when their dead lie among their idols,
all around their altars,
on every high hill, on all the mountaintops,
under every green tree and under every thick oak.
Those are the places where they offered sweet-smelling sacrifices
to all their idols.
[14] I will reach out my hand against them.
I will make the land empty and ruined,
from the wilderness to Diblah,
in all the places where they live.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Ezekiel is to clap his hands and stamp his foot. These are gestures of lament and horror.
> Verse 12: No one can escape, not far away, not nearby, and not in the city under siege.
> Verse 13: “Under every green tree”: Foreign gods were often worshiped under shady trees on hills (compare Deuteronomy 12:2; Jeremiah 2:20).
> Verse 14: “Diblah” is a place no one knows. Many scholars think that “Riblah” is meant, a city far in the north. The Hebrew letters for d and r look very much alike. Then it would mean: from the wilderness in the south to Riblah in the north, so the whole land.
