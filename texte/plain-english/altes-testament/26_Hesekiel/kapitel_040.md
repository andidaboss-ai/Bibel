# Ezekiel – Chapter 40
### The vision of the new temple

---

## The man with the measuring rod (Verses 1–4)

[1] It was in the twenty-fifth year of our captivity,
at the beginning of the year, on the tenth day of the month,
in the fourteenth year after the city was conquered.
On that very day
the hand of the LORD was on me,
and he brought me there.
[2] In visions from God he brought me into the land of Israel.
He set me down on a very high mountain.
On it, to the south, was something like the buildings of a city.
[3] He brought me there.
Look, there was a man
who looked like bronze.
He had a cord made of flax in his hand
and a measuring rod.
He was standing in the gateway.
[4] The man said to me,
“Son of man, look with your eyes,
listen with your ears,
and pay close attention to everything I will show you.
For you have been brought here
so that I can show it to you.
Tell the people of Israel everything you see.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the last great part of the book begins (Chapters 40–48): the vision of a new temple and a new land.
> Verse 1: This is in the year 573 BC, 25 years after Ezekiel was taken away, and 14 years after Jerusalem was destroyed. The old temple lies in ruins.
> Verses 2–3: Again in a vision, Ezekiel is brought to Israel. A heavenly man “who looked like bronze” has a measuring cord and a measuring rod. He will measure the whole new temple exactly.
> Why so many measurements? For the people in exile this was a message: The temple will not only be a dream. God has an exact plan. There is a future.
> A note: The following chapters contain very many measurements and descriptions of buildings. They are not easy to read, and some details are hard to understand even for experts. You do not have to understand every detail to grasp the message: God wants to live among his people again.

---

## The east gate (Verses 5–16)

[5] Look, there was a wall all around the outside of the temple area.
In the man’s hand was a measuring rod six cubits long.
Each of these cubits was a normal cubit plus a hand width.
So the rod was about 10½ feet long.
He measured the wall.
It was one rod thick and one rod high.
[6] Then he came to the gate that faces east
and went up its steps.
He measured the threshold of the gate. It was one rod deep.
The other threshold was one rod deep too.
[7] Each guard room was one rod long and one rod wide.
Between the guard rooms the wall was about 9 feet thick.
The threshold of the gate next to the porch of the gate,
on the side toward the temple, was one rod.
[8] He also measured the porch of the gate toward the temple. It was one rod.
[9] Then he measured the porch of the gate. It was about 14 feet.
Its posts were about 3½ feet thick.
The porch of the gate was on the side toward the temple.
[10] There were three side rooms on each side of the east gate.
All three had the same size.
The posts on each side also had the same size.
[11] He measured the width of the opening of the gate. It was about 17½ feet.
The length of the gateway was about 23 feet.
[12] In front of the guard rooms was a low wall
about 21 inches high on each side.
The side rooms were about 10½ feet on each side.
[13] He measured the gateway from the roof of one side room
to the roof of the other.
It was about 44 feet wide, from one door to the door across from it.
[14] He also measured the posts. They were about 105 feet.
The courtyard reached to the posts all around the gate.
[15] From the front of the gate at the entrance
to the front of the inner porch of the gate
was about 88 feet.
[16] There were narrow windows with frames that could not be opened
in the side rooms and in their posts inside the gateway, all around,
and also in the arches.
There were windows all around facing inward.
Palm trees were carved on each post.

> **What does this mean?**
> A cubit is the length from the elbow to the tip of the finger, about 18 inches. The “long cubit” used here (a cubit and a hand width) is about 21 inches long. The measuring rod is six of these cubits long, so about 10½ feet. In the text we have changed the measurements into feet.
> The east gate is a large gate building with three guard rooms on each side, like old city gates in Israel (for example at Megiddo, Hazor and Gezer). It is about 88 feet long and 44 feet wide.
> The palm trees (verse 16) were a sign of life, fruitfulness and victory. Solomon’s temple already had decorations of palm trees (1 Kings 6:29).
> The east gate is especially important: God’s glory had left the temple through this gate (Chapters 10–11). And it will come back through this gate (Chapter 43).

## The outer courtyard and the other gates (Verses 17–27)

[17] Then he brought me into the outer courtyard.
Look, there were rooms there,
and a stone pavement had been made all around the courtyard.
Thirty rooms faced the pavement.
[18] The pavement ran along the side of the gates.
It was as long as the gates were deep.
This was the lower pavement.
[19] Then he measured the distance
from the front of the lower gate
to the front of the inner courtyard on the outside.
It was about 175 feet, both on the east side and on the north side.
[20] He measured the length and the width of the gate of the outer courtyard
that faces north.
[21] It had three guard rooms on each side.
Its posts and its arches had the same measurements as the first gate.
It was about 88 feet long
and about 44 feet wide.
[22] Its windows, its arches, and its palm trees
had the same measurements as the gate that faces east.
People went up to it by seven steps.
Its arches were in front of them.
[23] There was a gate to the inner courtyard
across from the north gate, just as on the east side.
He measured from gate to gate. It was about 175 feet.
[24] He led me toward the south.
Look, there was a gate facing south.
He measured its posts and its arches.
They had the same measurements as the others.
[25] There were windows all around in it and in its arches,
like the other windows.
It was about 88 feet long
and about 44 feet wide.
[26] Seven steps led up to it,
and its arches were in front of them.
It had palm trees on its posts,
one on each side.
[27] There was a gate to the inner courtyard facing south.
He measured from gate to gate toward the south. It was about 175 feet.

> **What does this mean?**
> The outer courtyard has three gates that are the same: in the east, in the north, and in the south. There is no gate in the west. That is where the temple building itself stands.
> Seven steps lead up to the outer gates. So the temple is on higher ground. Whoever comes to God goes up.
> The thirty rooms along the edge were probably for the people’s sacrifice meals.
> The many measurements that are the same show order, harmony and perfection.

## The gates of the inner courtyard (Verses 28–37)

[28] Then he brought me into the inner courtyard through the south gate.
He measured the south gate.
It had the same measurements as the others,
[29] with its guard rooms, its posts, and its arches.
There were windows all around in it and in its arches.
It was about 88 feet long and about 44 feet wide.
[30] There were arches all around,
about 44 feet long and about 9 feet wide.
[31] Its arches faced the outer courtyard.
Palm trees were carved on its posts.
The stairway up to it had eight steps.
[32] He brought me into the inner courtyard on the east side.
He measured the gate.
It had the same measurements as the others,
[33] with its guard rooms, its posts, and its arches.
There were windows all around in it and in its arches.
It was about 88 feet long and about 44 feet wide.
[34] Its arches faced the outer courtyard.
Palm trees were carved on its posts on each side.
The stairway up to it had eight steps.
[35] He brought me to the north gate.
He measured it, and it had the same measurements:
[36] its guard rooms, its posts, and its arches.
There were windows all around in it.
It was about 88 feet long and about 44 feet wide.
[37] Its posts faced the outer courtyard.
Palm trees were carved on its posts on each side.
The stairway up to it had eight steps.

> **What does this mean?**
> The inner courtyard also has three gates, exactly across from the outer gates and just as big.
> Eight steps lead up to the inner gates, so they are even higher than the outer gates (seven steps). The closer you come to the holy place, the higher you go. Up to the temple building there are even more steps (40:49).

## The tables for the sacrifices and the rooms for the priests (Verses 38–47)

[38] There was a room with a door by the posts of the gates.
There they washed the burnt offering.
A burnt offering is an animal that was completely burned on the altar for God.
[39] In the porch of the gate there were two tables on each side.
On them they killed the animals for the burnt offering,
the sin offering, and the guilt offering.
[40] Outside, on one side,
as you go up to the entrance of the north gate,
there were two tables.
On the other side, by the porch of the gate,
there were two tables.
[41] So there were four tables on one side
and four tables on the other side
by the side of the gate:
eight tables on which they killed the animals for the sacrifices.
[42] For the burnt offering there were four tables of cut stone.
They were about 2½ feet long,
about 2½ feet wide,
and about 21 inches high.
On them they laid the tools
they used to kill the animals for the burnt offering and the other sacrifices.
[43] Hooks about 3 inches long were fastened all around on the inside.
The meat of the offerings was put on the tables.
[44] Outside the inner gate, in the inner courtyard, there were rooms for the singers.
One was at the side of the north gate.
It faced south.
Another was at the side of the east gate and faced north.
[45] He said to me,
“This room that faces south
is for the priests who take care of the temple.
[46] The room that faces north
is for the priests who take care of the altar.
They are the sons of Zadok.
Of all the descendants of Levi, only they may come near to the LORD
to serve him.”
[47] He measured the courtyard.
It was about 175 feet long and about 175 feet wide. It was a square.
The altar stood in front of the temple.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here it describes where the sacrifices were prepared: rooms for washing, tables for killing the animals, hooks for hanging them up.
> Verse 44: There are also rooms for the singers. Music and singing were part of the worship in the temple (see 1 Chronicles 25).
> Verse 46: Zadok was the high priest at the time of David and Solomon (1 Kings 1:32–39). Only his descendants are to serve at the altar. Ezekiel explains this in more detail in Chapter 44.
> Verse 47: The inner courtyard is a perfect square. In its middle stands the altar. The altar is the center of the whole area.

## The porch of the temple building (Verses 48–49)

[48] Then he brought me to the porch of the temple.
He measured each post of the porch.
They were about 9 feet thick on each side.
The width of the gateway was about 5 feet on each side.
[49] The porch was about 35 feet long
and about 19 feet wide.
People went up to it by steps.
There were pillars by the posts,
one on each side.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now Ezekiel comes to the temple building itself. First the porch with two pillars. This is like the two famous pillars “Jachin” and “Boaz” in front of Solomon’s temple (1 Kings 7:15–22).
