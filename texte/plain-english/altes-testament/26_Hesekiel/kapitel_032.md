# Ezekiel – Chapter 32
### Lament over Pharaoh

---

## The monster in the net (Verses 1–10)

[1] In the twelfth year, in the twelfth month, on the first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, sing a song of mourning about Pharaoh, king of Egypt.
Tell him,
‘You were compared to a young lion among the nations.
But you are like a monster in the seas.
You burst out in your rivers.
You stirred up the waters with your feet
and made their rivers dirty.’”
[3] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will spread out my net over you
with a crowd of many peoples.
They will pull you up in my net.
[4] I will leave you lying on the land.
I will throw you out onto the open field.
I will make all the birds of the sky settle on you.
I will feed the animals of the whole earth with you until they are full.
[5] I will lay your flesh on the mountains
and fill the valleys with your huge body.
[6] I will also soak the land where you swim
with your blood, all the way to the mountains.
The streams will be full of you.
[7] When I put out your light,
I will cover the sky
and make its stars dark.
I will cover the sun with a cloud,
and the moon will not give its light.
[8] I will make all the bright lights in the sky dark over you.
I will bring darkness over your land,”
says the Lord GOD.
[9] “I will also trouble the hearts of many peoples
when I spread the news of your destruction among the nations,
into countries you have not known.
[10] Yes, I will make many peoples shocked at you.
Their kings will shake with terror because of you
when I swing my sword in front of them.
They will tremble every moment,
every man for his own life,
on the day of your fall.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Pharaoh thinks he is a lion, a king of the nations. But God says: You are more like a crocodile that churns around in the water and makes everything dirty.
> Verses 3–6: As in Chapter 29, the monster is caught with a net and thrown onto the land. The pictures are harsh: They show the complete end of Pharaoh’s power.
> Verses 7–8: The sun, the moon and the stars become dark. In Egypt the sun was worshiped as a god (the sun god Ra). When God makes the sun dark, he shows: He is more powerful than the gods of Egypt. Jesus uses similar pictures (Mark 13:24).

---

## The sword of the king of Babylon (Verses 11–16)

[11] For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“The sword of the king of Babylon will come against you.
[12] I will make your crowds of people fall
by the swords of mighty warriors.
All of them are the most ruthless of the nations.
They will bring the pride of Egypt to nothing,
and all its crowds will be destroyed.
[13] I will also destroy all its animals
from beside the many waters.
No human foot will stir up the waters anymore,
and no animal’s hoofs will stir them up.
[14] Then I will make their waters clear
and make their rivers flow smoothly like oil,”
says the Lord GOD.
[15] “I will make the land of Egypt a ruin and a wasteland.
It will be a land empty of everything that once filled it.
I will strike down all who live there.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.
[16] “‘“This is the song of mourning that people will sing.
The daughters of the nations will sing it.
They will sing it over Egypt
and over all her crowds of people,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: A strangely quiet picture: When there are no more people and animals, the water becomes very clear and calm, “like oil.” It is the silence after the disaster.
> Verse 16: Here “the daughters of the nations” are to mourn over Egypt. Songs of mourning were often sung by women whose job was to mourn (see Jeremiah 9:17–20; in some Bibles 9:16–19).

---

## Down into the world of the dead (Verses 17–32)

[17] In the twelfth year, on the fifteenth day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[18] “Son of man, wail for the crowds of Egypt.
Send them down,
her and the daughters of the famous nations,
to the lowest parts of the earth,
with those who go down into the pit.
[19] Who are you more beautiful than?
Go down,
and lie down with the uncircumcised.
[20] They will fall among those
who are killed by the sword.
She is handed over to the sword.
Drag her away with all her crowds.
[21] The strongest of the mighty warriors will speak to him
from the middle of Sheol (the world of the dead),
together with those who helped him.
They will say,
‘They have gone down.
The uncircumcised lie still,
killed by the sword.’
[22] “Asshur is there with all her army.
Her graves are all around her.
All of them were killed. They fell by the sword.
[23] Their graves are in the deepest corners of the pit.
Her army lies around her grave.
All of them were killed. They fell by the sword.
They were the ones who spread terror in the land of the living.
[24] “Elam is there with all her crowds around her grave.
All of them were killed. They fell by the sword.
They went down uncircumcised into the lowest parts of the earth.
They were the ones who spread terror in the land of the living.
Now they bear their shame with those who go down to the pit.
[25] They have made a bed for Elam among the dead
with all her crowds.
Her graves are around her.
All of them are uncircumcised, killed by the sword.
For they spread terror in the land of the living.
Now they bear their shame with those who go down to the pit.
He is laid among the dead.
[26] “Meshech, Tubal, and all their crowds are there.
Their graves are around them.
All of them are uncircumcised, killed by the sword.
For they spread terror in the land of the living.
[27] They will not lie with the fallen mighty warriors of the uncircumcised,
who went down to Sheol with their weapons of war.
Those warriors had their swords laid under their heads.
But the guilt of these is on their bones,
for they spread terror among the mighty in the land of the living.
[28] “But you will be broken among the uncircumcised.
You will lie with those who are killed by the sword.
[29] “Edom is there, her kings and all her princes.
In spite of their power, they are laid with those who are killed by the sword.
They will lie with the uncircumcised
and with those who go down to the pit.
[30] “All the princes of the north are there,
and all the Sidonians.
They have gone down with the dead.
They are put to shame,
in spite of the terror that they spread with their power.
They lie uncircumcised with those who are killed by the sword.
They bear their shame with those who go down to the pit.
[31] “Pharaoh will see them.
He will be comforted about all his crowds,
Pharaoh and all his army, killed by the sword,”
says the Lord GOD.
[32] “For I let him spread terror in the land of the living.
He will be laid among the uncircumcised,
with those who are killed by the sword,
Pharaoh and all his crowds,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an eerie picture: Ezekiel describes a kind of tour through the world of the dead. There lie the great empires that once “spread terror in the land of the living”: Asshur (Assyria), Elam (in today’s Iran), Meshech and Tubal (in Asia Minor), Edom, the princes of the north, and Sidon. Now they all lie dead and still, each one in his grave.
> And now Pharaoh joins them. He will be “comforted” (verse 31), because he sees: The other great ones are here too.
> The repeated sentence “they spread terror in the land of the living” shows the reason: These empires ruled with violence and terror. In the end it did them no good.
> Verse 27: Fallen heroes were sometimes buried with their weapons, as an honor. These here are refused that honor.
> The message: All tyrants and world empires end the same way, in the grave. Only God remains (see Psalm 146:3–4: Don’t put your trust in princes …).
