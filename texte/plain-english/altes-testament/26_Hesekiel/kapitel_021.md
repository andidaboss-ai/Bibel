# Ezekiel – Chapter 21
### The sword of the LORD

---

## The sword is drawn (Verses 1–7)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, turn your face toward Jerusalem.
Preach against the holy places,
and prophesy against the land of Israel.
[3] Tell the land of Israel,
‘This is what the LORD says:
“I am against you.
I will pull my sword out of its sheath.
I will cut off from you both the righteous and the wicked.
[4] Because I will cut off from you the righteous and the wicked,
my sword will go out of its sheath
against all people, from the south to the north.
[5] All people will know
that I, the LORD, have pulled my sword out of its sheath.
It will not go back again.”’
[6] “So sigh, son of man!
Sigh in front of them with a broken heart and with bitterness.
[7] When they ask you,
‘Why are you sighing?’
then you must say,
‘Because of the news, for it is coming!
Every heart will melt with fear.
All hands will be weak.
Every spirit will grow faint.
All knees will be as weak as water.
Look, it is coming, and it will happen,
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> A note on the numbering: In some Bibles, Chapter 21 begins five verses earlier (with 20:45–49 of the WEB). So the verse numbers here are always 5 lower than in many other Bibles. We follow the numbering of the WEB.
> Now the picture of the forest fire (20:45–49) is explained: The “forest of the south” is Jerusalem. And the fire is a sword, that is, the army of the Babylonians.
> Verses 3–4: “The righteous and the wicked” – in war everyone suffers, even innocent people. Ezekiel does not make this sound nicer than it is. This is an honest, painful description of what war is like.
> Verses 6–7: Ezekiel is to sigh in public, with a broken heart. He does not announce the judgment coldly. He suffers with his people.

## The song of the sword (Verses 8–17)

[8] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[9] “Son of man, prophesy and say,
‘This is what the LORD says:
“A sword! A sword!
It is sharpened, and it is polished too.
[10] It is sharpened so that it can kill many people.
It is polished so that it flashes like lightning.
Should we be happy then?
The rod of my son looks down on every tree.
[11] It is given to be polished,
so that someone can hold it in his hand.
The sword is sharpened.
Yes, it is polished
to be given into the hand of the killer.”’
[12] Cry and wail, son of man,
for it is coming against my people.
It is coming against all the princes of Israel.
They are handed over to the sword together with my people.
So beat your thigh.
[13] “For there is a test.
And what if even the rod that looks down on others is no more?”
says the Lord GOD.
[14] “So you, son of man, prophesy,
and clap your hands together.
Let the sword strike twice, even three times,
the sword for those who are wounded to death.
It is the sword of the great one who is wounded to death.
It goes into their rooms.
[15] I have set the threatening sword against all their gates,
so that their hearts melt with fear,
and so that many of them stumble and fall.
Oh! It is made like lightning.
It is sharpened to kill.
[16] Pull yourself together. Go to the right.
Get ready. Go to the left,
wherever your edge is turned.
[17] I will also clap my hands together,
and I will let my anger come to rest.
I, the LORD, have spoken.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a wild, terrifying song. The sword is sharpened and polished until it flashes. You can feel the fear and the horror of the war that is coming.
> Verses 10 and 13 are among the hardest verses in the whole book. The Hebrew text is hard to understand here, and the translations are very different from each other. Maybe it means: The scepter (the rod) of the king of Judah, “my son,” can do nothing against this sword.
> Verse 12: “So beat your thigh.” This was a sign of great sorrow and despair.
> War songs like this one do not praise violence. They show how terrible war is. If you have lived through war, or if you cannot get pictures of war out of your head, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## The king of Babylon at the fork in the road (Verses 18–24)

[18] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[19] “And you, son of man,
mark out two roads
for the sword of the king of Babylon to come by.
Both of them will start from the same land.
And make a signpost.
Put it where the road to the city begins.
[20] Mark out one road
for the sword to come to Rabbah of the Ammonites,
and one to Judah, to Jerusalem, the city with strong walls.
[21] For the king of Babylon stood at the fork in the road,
where the two roads begin,
to ask for a sign about the future.
He shook the arrows back and forth.
He asked the teraphim.
Teraphim are small idols of the household.
He looked at the liver.
[22] In his right hand was the lot for Jerusalem.
It told him to set up battering rams,
to give the order to kill,
to raise the battle cry,
to set up battering rams against the gates,
to pile up ramps of earth,
and to build siege towers.
[23] To the people of Jerusalem, it will look like a false sign.
They have sworn oaths of loyalty.
But he will remind them of their guilt,
so that they will be captured.
[24] “So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘You have made people remember your guilt.
Your wrongdoing has come out into the open,
so that your sins can be seen in everything you do.
Because you have been remembered,
you will be captured by force.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel is to draw a map: One road leads to Rabbah (the capital city of the Ammonites, today Amman in Jordan). The other road leads to Jerusalem. Both had rebelled against Babylon.
> Verse 21: Nebuchadnezzar stands at the crossroads and asks his gods. He uses three methods: He draws arrows like lots, he asks household gods (teraphim), and he looks at the liver of an animal that was sacrificed (a well-known way of telling the future in Babylon).
> Verse 22: The lot falls on Jerusalem. The Bible does not say that telling the future works. But God is Lord even over the decisions of foreign kings.
> Verse 23: The people in Jerusalem think this is wrong, because they had made an agreement with Babylon. But it was exactly this agreement that Zedekiah had broken (see Chapter 17).

## Take off the crown! (Verses 25–27)

[25] “‘And you, wicked man, wounded to death,
prince of Israel,
your day has come,
the time when guilt comes to its end.
[26] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Take off the turban,
and take off the crown.
Things will not stay the way they were.
Lift up what is low,
and bring down what is high.
[27] I will turn it upside down, upside down, upside down.
This too will be no more,
until the one comes who has the right to it.
And I will give it to him.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> The “prince of Israel” is King Zedekiah. He will lose his crown and his turban. The kingdom of Judah comes to an end.
> Verse 26: “Lift up what is low, and bring down what is high.” This is a basic theme of the Bible (see 17:24 and Luke 1:52).
> Verse 27: An important verse: The throne stays empty “until the one comes who has the right to it.” This is like Genesis 49:10. Jews and Christians have seen in this a promise of the Messiah, the rightful king from the family of David. Christians apply this to Jesus.

## The sword against the Ammonites (Verses 28–32)

[28] “You, son of man, prophesy and say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says
about the Ammonites and about their insults:
“A sword! A sword is drawn!
It is polished to kill,
to devour,
so that it flashes like lightning.
[29] People see false visions for you.
They tell you lies about the future.
This happens to lay you on the necks of the wicked who are wounded to death.
Their day has come,
the time when guilt comes to its end.
[30] Put it back into its sheath!
In the place where you were created,
in the land where you were born,
I will judge you.
[31] I will pour out my anger on you.
I will blow on you with the fire of my anger.
I will hand you over to cruel men,
who are skilled at destroying.
[32] You will be wood for the fire.
Your blood will be spilled in the middle of the land.
No one will remember you anymore,
for I, the LORD, have spoken.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ammonites were maybe happy that the king of Babylon went to Jerusalem and not to them. And they mocked Jerusalem. But they will be judged too.
> Verse 30: “Put it back into its sheath!” This is probably spoken to the sword of Babylon: When Babylon has finished its task, it will be judged itself, in its own land.
> These words of judgment about nations do not give permission to hate other nations. They show that God is the Lord of all history, and that injustice and pride have consequences everywhere.
