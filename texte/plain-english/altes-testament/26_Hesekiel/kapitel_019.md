# Ezekiel – Chapter 19
### A lament over the princes

---

## The lioness and her cubs (Verses 1–9)

[1] “Now sing a song of mourning for the princes of Israel.
[2] Say,
‘What was your mother?
A lioness.
She lay down among lions.
Among the young lions she raised her cubs.
[3] She raised one of her cubs.
He became a young lion.
He learned to catch his prey.
He ate people.
[4] The nations heard about him.
He was caught in their pit.
They brought him with hooks to the land of Egypt.
[5] “‘When she saw that she had waited in vain
and her hope was lost,
she took another of her cubs
and made him a young lion.
[6] He walked around among the lions.
He became a young lion.
He learned to catch his prey.
He ate people.
[7] He knew their palaces
and destroyed their cities.
The land and everything in it became empty
because of the sound of his roaring.
[8] Then the nations from the provinces
attacked him from every side.
They spread their net over him.
He was caught in their pit.
[9] They put him in a cage with hooks
and brought him to the king of Babylon.
They put him in prison,
so that his voice would never again be heard
on the mountains of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> A song of mourning is really a song for people who have died. Ezekiel sings it about the last kings of Judah, even though some of them are still alive. In this way he says: The kingdom is as good as dead.
> The lioness stands for Judah or for the king’s mother. The lion was the symbol of the tribe of Judah (Genesis 49:9).
> The first cub (verses 3–4) is King Jehoahaz. He ruled for only three months, and Pharaoh took him to Egypt (2 Kings 23:31–34).
> The second cub (verses 5–9) is probably King Jehoiachin, who was taken away to Babylon (2 Kings 24:8–15). Some people also think of Zedekiah.
> “He ate people” describes how these kings oppressed and exploited their own people. Instead of being shepherds, they were beasts of prey.

---

## The dried-up vine (Verses 10–14)

[10] “‘Your mother was like a vine in your blood,
planted by the water.
It was full of fruit and branches
because there was plenty of water.
[11] It had strong branches,
good for the scepters of rulers.
It grew tall among the thick branches.
People could see how tall it was
and how many branches it had.
[12] But it was pulled up in anger.
It was thrown down to the ground.
The east wind dried up its fruit.
Its strong branches were broken off and dried up.
The fire burned them up.
[13] Now it is planted in the wilderness,
in a dry and thirsty land.
[14] Fire has come out of its branches.
It has burned up its fruit.
No strong branch is left on it
that could be a scepter for a ruler.’
This is a song of mourning,
and it will be used as a song of mourning.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the picture changes: The mother is a splendid vine. It had strong branches, that is, kings with scepters. A scepter is a staff that a king holds.
> Verse 10: “In your blood” is a difficult phrase. Some translate it “in your vineyard” or “like you.”
> Verses 12–13: The vine is pulled up and planted again in the wilderness. That means: The royal family is taken into exile in Babylon.
> Verse 14: “Fire has come out of its branches.” The disaster came from the royal family itself. The last king (Zedekiah) caused the destruction by his rebellion.
> “No strong branch is left”: The kingdom of David seems to be over. But Ezekiel 17:22–24 and 34:23–24 promise that God will give a new shoot.
