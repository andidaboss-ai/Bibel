# Ezekiel – Chapter 2
### Ezekiel is sent

---

## Son of man, stand on your feet (Verses 1–7)

[1] He said to me,
“Son of man, stand up on your feet,
and I will speak with you.”
[2] When he spoke to me, the Spirit came into me
and set me on my feet.
And I heard the one who was speaking to me.
[3] He said to me,
“Son of man, I am sending you to the people of Israel,
to a nation of rebels who have rebelled against me.
They and their ancestors have sinned against me
until this very day.
[4] The children are rude and stubborn.
I am sending you to them.
You must say to them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says.’
[5] They may listen, or they may refuse to listen,
for they are a rebellious people.
But either way, they will know
that there has been a prophet among them.
[6] And you, son of man, do not be afraid of them.
Do not be afraid of their words,
even if thorn bushes and thorns are all around you,
and you live among scorpions.
Do not be afraid of their words.
Do not be frightened by the way they look at you,
even though they are a rebellious people.
[7] You must speak my words to them,
whether they listen or refuse to listen,
for they are very rebellious.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: God calls Ezekiel “son of man” (in Hebrew: “ben adam”). That simply means “human being.” In the book of Ezekiel, God speaks to him this way more than 90 times. It shows how small a human being is before God. At the same time, it shows that God wants to speak with this human being. (Later Jesus often calls himself “Son of Man,” taking up Daniel 7:13.)
> Verse 2: Ezekiel is lying on his face. God’s Spirit sets him back on his feet. God himself gives him the strength to stand.
> Verse 5: Whether the people listen or not, they will know “that there has been a prophet among them.” Ezekiel’s task is not to be successful. His task is to pass on God’s word faithfully.
> Verse 6: For him, the people will be like thorns and scorpions, painful and dangerous. But God says three times: “Do not be afraid!”

---

## The scroll (Verses 8–10)

[8] But you, son of man, listen to what I tell you.
Do not be rebellious like that rebellious people.
Open your mouth, and eat what I give you.”
[9] When I looked, I saw a hand reaching out to me.
In it was a scroll.
A scroll is a long roll of writing material.
[10] He spread it out in front of me.
There was writing on the front and on the back.
Words of lament, mourning, and sorrow were written on it.

> **What does this mean?**
> God gives Ezekiel a scroll. Usually people wrote on only one side. This scroll has writing on both sides, so much is written on it. And what it says is hard: laments, mourning, and words of sorrow. That is the message Ezekiel must pass on: judgment on Jerusalem.
