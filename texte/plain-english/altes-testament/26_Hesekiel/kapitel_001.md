# Ezekiel – Chapter 1
### The glory of the LORD

---

## Before we begin: Who was Ezekiel?

Ezekiel was a priest from Jerusalem. In the year 597 BC, he was taken away to Babylon together with King Jehoiachin and many others. There he lived with other exiles by the river Chebar.
Five years later, around 593 BC, God called him to be a prophet. So Ezekiel worked at the same time as Jeremiah, but not in Jerusalem. He worked in Babylon.
His name means “God makes strong.”
Ezekiel had powerful visions: He saw God’s glory on a throne chariot. He saw God’s glory leave the temple. And he saw a field full of dry bones that come to life again. He did many unusual sign actions to make his message visible.
The book has 48 chapters and three parts:
– Chapters 1–24: Judgment on Jerusalem, before the city is destroyed.
– Chapters 25–32: Words about the neighboring peoples.
– Chapters 33–48: Hope after the destruction: a new heart, a new spirit, the good shepherd, the bones that come to life, and a new temple.
Again and again God calls Ezekiel “son of man.” That simply means “human being.” It shows how small a human being is before the great God.
Jews and Christians read Ezekiel as a book of hope: God is with his people even in exile, and he can make dead things live.

## The vision by the river Chebar (Verses 1–14)

[1] It was in the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, on the fifth day of the month.
I was among the captives by the river Chebar.
Then the heavens opened, and I saw visions of God.
[2] It was on the fifth day of the month.
That was the fifth year since King Jehoiachin was taken captive.
[3] The LORD’s word came to Ezekiel the priest, the son of Buzi,
in the land of the Chaldeans by the river Chebar.
The Chaldeans are the Babylonians.
And the LORD’s hand was on him there.
[4] I looked, and I saw a storm wind coming out of the north.
There was a great cloud with flashing lightning,
and a bright light all around it.
In the middle of it, in the middle of the fire,
there was something like glowing metal.
[5] Out of the middle of it came something like four living creatures.
This is what they looked like:
They had the shape of a man.
[6] Each one had four faces,
and each one of them had four wings.
[7] Their legs were straight.
The soles of their feet were like the sole of a calf’s foot.
They sparkled like polished bronze.
[8] Under their wings, on their four sides,
they had the hands of a man.
All four of them had faces and wings like this:
[9] Their wings touched one another.
They did not turn when they moved.
Each one went straight forward.
[10] This is what their faces looked like:
They had the face of a man.
All four had the face of a lion on the right side.
All four had the face of an ox on the left side.
All four also had the face of an eagle.
[11] Those were their faces.
Their wings were spread out above.
Two wings of each one touched the wings of another,
and two wings covered their bodies.
[12] Each one went straight forward.
Wherever the spirit wanted to go, they went.
They did not turn when they moved.
[13] The living creatures looked like burning coals of fire,
like torches.
The fire moved back and forth among the living creatures.
The fire was bright, and lightning came out of the fire.
[14] The living creatures ran back and forth
like flashes of lightning.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Ezekiel is 30 years old (the “thirtieth year” probably means his age). At that age, priests usually began their service in the temple (Numbers 4:3). But Ezekiel is far away from the temple, in exile. And right there the heavens open! God is not tied to Jerusalem. He also comes to the exiles.
> Verse 4: A mighty storm, a cloud, fire, and lightning: These are signs that God is coming (compare Exodus 19:16).
> Verses 5–14: Ezekiel tries to describe what cannot be described. He often uses the word “like.” For example, the creatures “looked like burning coals of fire.” The four creatures have four faces: a man, a lion, an ox, and an eagle. These are the wisest, the strongest, the most powerful, and the fastest of the living things. Later Ezekiel calls them “cherubim” (Chapter 10).
> In Christian tradition, the four faces became symbols of the four Gospel writers: Matthew (man), Mark (lion), Luke (ox), and John (eagle). The book of Revelation describes similar creatures (Revelation 4:6–8).

## Wheels full of eyes (Verses 15–21)

[15] As I looked at the living creatures,
I saw one wheel on the ground beside the living creatures,
one for each of their four faces.
[16] The wheels and the way they were made looked like beryl.
All four of them looked the same.
They looked as if one wheel was inside another wheel.
[17] When they moved, they went in any of their four directions.
They did not turn when they moved.
[18] Their rims were high and frightening.
The rims of all four were full of eyes all around.
[19] When the living creatures moved, the wheels moved beside them.
When the living creatures rose up from the ground,
the wheels rose up too.
[20] Wherever the spirit wanted to go, they went.
The spirit wanted to go there.
The wheels rose up beside them,
because the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.
[21] When the creatures moved, the wheels moved.
When the creatures stood still, the wheels stood still.
When the creatures rose up from the ground,
the wheels rose up beside them,
because the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.

> **What does this mean?**
> Beside each creature there is a wheel, as if one wheel was inside another wheel. That way they can move in all directions without turning. The wheels are full of eyes: God sees everything.
> The whole thing is like a moving throne chariot. The message is: God’s throne is not fixed in one place. It is not only in the temple in Jerusalem. God can go anywhere, even to his people in exile in Babylon.
> In Judaism this vision is called “Merkavah” (throne chariot). Later it became the basis for a whole branch of Jewish mysticism.
> Verse 16: The WEB has “beryl.” This means a precious stone that shines green or blue.

## The throne and the glory (Verses 22–28)

[22] Over the heads of the living creatures
there was something like an expanse.
An expanse is a wide, open space.
It looked like amazing crystal,
stretched out high above their heads.
[23] Under the expanse their wings were stretched out straight,
one toward the other.
Each one had two wings that covered it on this side,
and two wings that covered its body on that side.
[24] When they moved, I heard the noise of their wings.
It was like the noise of great waters,
like the voice of the Almighty,
a loud roar like the noise of an army.
When they stood still, they let their wings down.
[25] There was a voice from above the expanse over their heads.
When they stood still, they let their wings down.
[26] Above the expanse over their heads
there was something like a throne.
It looked like a sapphire stone.
High up on the throne
there was a shape that looked like a man.
[27] From what looked like his waist upward,
I saw something like glowing metal,
with something like fire all around it inside.
From what looked like his waist downward,
I saw something that looked like fire.
There was a bright light all around him.
[28] The bright light all around him looked like the rainbow
that is in the clouds on a rainy day.
This is what the likeness of the LORD’s glory looked like.
When I saw it, I fell on my face.
And I heard the voice of someone speaking.

> **What does this mean?**
> Above the creatures there is a kind of crystal ceiling. Above that there is a throne like sapphire. And on the throne there is a shape “that looked like a man,” surrounded by fire and light.
> Verse 28: Ezekiel is very careful with his words. He does not say, I saw God. He says, “This is what the likeness of the LORD’s glory looked like.” God is too great to be described directly.
> The rainbow reminds us of God’s covenant with Noah (Genesis 9:13). In the middle of fire and storm, a sign of faithfulness shines.
> Ezekiel falls on his face. Meeting God’s holiness overwhelms him.
