# Ezekiel – Chapter 4
### Signs of the siege

---

## Jerusalem on a brick (Verses 1–8)

[1] “And you, son of man, take a brick.
Put it in front of you,
and draw a city on it: Jerusalem.
[2] Put the city under siege.
Build towers against it,
and pile up a ramp of earth against it.
Also set up army camps against it,
and put battering rams all around it.
[3] Take an iron pan,
and set it up as a wall of iron between you and the city.
Then turn your face toward the city.
It will be under siege, and you will attack it.
This will be a sign for the people of Israel.
[4] “Then lie down on your left side,
and put the guilt of the people of Israel on it.
For as many days as you lie on that side,
you will carry their guilt.
[5] For I have turned the years of their guilt
into a number of days for you:
three hundred ninety days.
That is how long you will carry the guilt of the people of Israel.
[6] “When you have finished these days,
lie down on your right side,
and carry the guilt of the people of Judah.
I have given you forty days,
one day for each year.
[7] Turn your face toward Jerusalem under siege,
with your arm bare,
and prophesy against it.
[8] Look, I will tie you up with ropes,
so that you cannot turn from one side to the other
until you have finished the days of your siege.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel cannot speak (Ezekiel 3:26). That is why he “preaches” with sign actions, like street theater. He draws Jerusalem on a clay brick and acts out the siege, with ramps, camps, and battering rams. The exiles in Babylon hoped that Jerusalem would be spared. Ezekiel shows them: The city will be put under siege.
> Verse 2: A siege is when an army surrounds a city and attacks it. Battering rams are heavy beams for breaking down walls and gates.
> Verse 3: The iron pan shows a separation: God has turned away from the city, as if behind a wall of iron.
> Verses 4–6: 390 days on the left side for Israel (the northern kingdom), 40 days on the right side for Judah. Each day stands for one year of guilt. What exactly the numbers mean is still uncertain today. Bible scholars have suggested different explanations.
> Ezekiel probably did not lie there day and night without a break, but for a certain time each day. After all, he also had to bake bread (verses 9–12).
> Verse 7: The “bare arm” is a sign of being ready to fight, like rolling up your sleeves.

---

## Bread by weight (Verses 9–17)

[9] “Also take wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and spelt,
and put them into one pot.
Make bread out of it.
You will eat it for as many days as you lie on your side:
three hundred ninety days.
[10] The food you eat must be weighed:
twenty shekels (about 8 ounces) a day.
Eat it at set times.
[11] You must also measure the water you drink:
one sixth of a hin (about 1 to 2 pints).
Drink it at set times.
[12] Eat it as you would eat barley cakes.
Bake it where the people can see you,
using human dung as fuel.”
[13] The LORD said,
“In the same way, the people of Israel will eat their bread unclean
among the nations where I will drive them.”
[14] Then I said,
“Oh, Lord GOD!
I have never made myself unclean.
From the time I was young until now,
I have never eaten an animal that died by itself
or was torn apart by wild animals.
No disgusting meat has ever come into my mouth!”
[15] Then he said to me,
“All right, I will let you use cow dung instead of human dung.
You may bake your bread on it.”
[16] He also said to me,
“Son of man, I will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem.
They will weigh their bread and eat it with worry.
They will measure their water and drink it in fear.
[17] They will run out of bread and water.
They will look at one another in horror.
They will waste away because of their guilt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Ezekiel must bake an emergency bread out of all kinds of grains, beans, and lentils. That is how people eat when food runs short and they scrape together whatever is left.
> Verses 10–11: A shekel is a weight of about 0.4 ounces. Twenty shekels are about 8 ounces of bread a day. A hin is a measure for liquids. One sixth of a hin is about 1 to 2 pints of water. Those are starvation rations, as in a city under siege.
> Verses 12–15: In the ancient Near East, dried dung was a common fuel. But human waste was thought to be unclean. Ezekiel is a priest and protests: All his life he has kept the food laws. And God listens to him! He allows cow dung instead of human dung. This shows: God is willing to talk with us. Honest objections in prayer are allowed.
> Verse 13: The message is: In exile, among foreign peoples, it will be hard to live by God’s commandments.
> Verse 16: The “staff of bread” is a picture: Bread supports life like a walking stick. When this staff breaks, hunger comes.
