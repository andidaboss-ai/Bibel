# Ezekiel – Chapter 36
### A new heart and a new spirit

---

## You mountains of Israel, listen! (Verses 1–7)

[1] “You, son of man, prophesy to the mountains of Israel and say,
‘You mountains of Israel, hear the word of the LORD.
[2] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“The enemy has said about you, ‘Aha!’
and, ‘The ancient heights are ours now!’”’
[3] So prophesy and say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Yes, they have ruined you
and swallowed you up from every side,
so that you became the property of the rest of the nations.
People gossip about you,
and they say bad things about you.”
[4] So, you mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord GOD!
This is what the Lord GOD says
to the mountains and to the hills,
to the streams and to the valleys,
to the ruins that lie empty and to the cities that are abandoned.
They have been robbed and mocked
by the rest of the nations all around.
[5] So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“In the fire of my jealous love I have spoken
against the rest of the nations and against all of Edom.
They took my land for themselves as their property,
with joy in all their hearts
and with contempt,
so that they could take it as plunder.”’
[6] So prophesy about the land of Israel.
Say to the mountains, the hills, the streams, and the valleys,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I have spoken in my jealous love and in my fury,
because you have had to bear the scorn of the nations.”
[7] So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“I have sworn an oath:
The nations around you will surely
bear their own shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 6, Ezekiel had prophesied against the mountains of Israel, because idols were worshiped there. Now he speaks to the mountains again, but this time with a message of hope!
> The enemies have mocked and grabbed the abandoned land. God says: I am on the side of my land. The mockers will be put to shame themselves.

---

## The mountains will become green again (Verses 8–15)

[8] “‘“But you, mountains of Israel,
you will grow your branches
and give your fruit to my people Israel,
for they will soon come home.
[9] For look, I am for you.
I will turn to you,
and you will be plowed and planted.
[10] I will make many people live on you,
all the people of Israel, all of them.
People will live in the cities,
and the ruins will be rebuilt.
[11] I will make many people and animals live on you.
They will grow in number and have many young.
I will let people live on you as they did before.
I will do more good to you than at the beginning.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[12] Yes, I will let people walk on you,
my people Israel.
They will own you,
and you will be their land.
You will never again take their children away from them.”
[13] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“People say to you,
‘You are a land that eats people.
You have taken away the children of your nation.’
[14] So you will not eat people anymore,
and you will not take away the children of your nation anymore,”
says the Lord GOD.
[15] “I will not let you hear the scorn of the nations anymore.
You will not have to bear the insults of the peoples anymore.
You will not make your nation stumble anymore,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “Look, I am for you.” What a sentence after all the judgment! (See Romans 8:31: “If God is for us, who can be against us?”)
> The ruined land will become fruitful again. Fields will be plowed, cities will be rebuilt, people and animals will become many. God even promises: “I will do more good to you than at the beginning.”
> Verses 13–14: People said to the land: “You are a land that eats people.” There had been so much war, hunger, and death there. God promises: This will stop.

---

## For the sake of my holy name (Verses 16–23)

[16] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[17] “Son of man, when the people of Israel lived in their own land,
they made it unclean by the way they lived and by what they did.
To me, their way of life was like the uncleanness of a woman
during her monthly period.
[18] So I poured out my fury on them
because of the blood they had poured out on the land,
and because they had made it unclean with their idols.
[19] I scattered them among the nations,
and they were spread out through the countries.
I judged them by the way they lived and by what they did.
[20] When they came to the nations where they went,
they caused my holy name to be dishonored.
For people said about them,
‘These are the LORD’s people,
and yet they had to leave his land.’
[21] But I cared about my holy name,
which the people of Israel had caused to be dishonored
among the nations where they went.
[22] “So say to the people of Israel,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I am not doing this for your sake, people of Israel.
I am doing it for the sake of my holy name,
which you have caused to be dishonored
among the nations where you went.
[23] I will show that my great name is holy,
the name that has been dishonored among the nations,
the name that you dishonored among them.
Then the nations will know that I am the LORD,”
says the Lord GOD,
“when I show through you that I am holy, in front of their eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Here the guilt of Israel is compared to the “uncleanness of a woman” during her period. This refers to the old rules about being clean (Leviticus 15:19–24). Important: A period is something completely natural. It is not a sin and nothing dirty. The comparison only means being unclean for worship by the rules of that time. Today people would not use a picture like this anymore.
> Verse 20: The other nations saw the people in exile and mocked: “These are the LORD’s people, and yet they had to leave his land.” That means: Their God could not even keep them in their land. So God’s name was made to look ridiculous.
> Verse 22: “I am not doing this for your sake.” God does not save Israel because Israel deserved it, but because God is faithful and wants to show his name, that is, who he is and his reputation, to the world. This is pure grace.
> Verse 23: “I will show that my great name is holy.” Jesus teaches us to pray: “May your name be kept holy” (Matthew 6:9).

## A new heart (Verses 24–32)

[24] “‘“For I will take you out from among the nations.
I will gather you from all the countries
and bring you into your own land.
[25] I will sprinkle clean water on you,
and you will be clean.
I will make you clean from all your filth
and from all your idols.
[26] I will also give you a new heart,
and I will put a new spirit inside you.
I will take the heart of stone out of your body
and give you a heart of flesh.
[27] I will put my Spirit inside you.
I will make you follow my rules,
and you will keep my laws and do them.
[28] You will live in the land that I gave to your ancestors.
You will be my people,
and I will be your God.
[29] I will save you from all your uncleanness.
I will call for the grain and make it grow in plenty.
I will not bring famine on you.
[30] I will make the fruit of the trees and the harvest of the fields grow in plenty,
so that you will never again have to bear the shame of famine among the nations.
[31] “‘“Then you will remember your evil ways
and your deeds that were not good.
You will be disgusted with yourselves
because of your sins and because of the disgusting things you did.
[32] I am not doing this for your sake,”
says the Lord GOD.
“You should know that.
Be ashamed and embarrassed about your ways, people of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the whole Old Testament!
> Verse 25: God makes his people clean with clean water. He washes away everything old. (Christians think of baptism here.)
> Verse 26: God gives a new heart. A heart of stone is hard, cold, and does not move. A heart of flesh is alive, warm, and can feel. God changes people from the inside. What people cannot do themselves, God gives as a gift.
> Verse 27: God puts his own Spirit into people. Then they keep God’s commandments not because they are forced to, but because they want to from the inside.
> This promise is like the “new covenant” in Jeremiah 31:31–34. Jesus says: “Unless one is born of water and Spirit, he can’t enter into God’s Kingdom” (John 3:5). Christians see the fulfillment in the gift of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2). In Judaism this text is read as a promise for the renewal of Israel.
> Verse 31: When people experience God’s goodness, they see their own past more honestly. This means being sorry, not hating yourself.

## Like the garden of Eden (Verses 33–38)

[33] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“On the day I make you clean from all your sins,
I will let people live in the cities again,
and the ruins will be rebuilt.
[34] The land that lay in ruins will be plowed again.
It will no longer be a wasteland in the eyes of everyone who passes by.
[35] They will say,
‘This land that lay in ruins
has become like the garden of Eden.
The cities that were empty, ruined, and torn down
now have strong walls, and people live in them.’
[36] Then the nations that are left around you will know
that I, the LORD, have rebuilt what was torn down
and planted what was ruined.
I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will do it.”
[37] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Once more I will let the people of Israel ask me
to do this for them:
I will make their people as many as a flock of sheep.
[38] They will be like the flocks for sacrifice,
like the flocks in Jerusalem at her festivals.
In the same way, the ruined cities will be filled with flocks of people.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 35: The ruined land becomes “like the garden of Eden.” After the judgment comes a new beginning, more beautiful than before. It is as if God creates paradise again.
> Verse 37: Before, God had said that he would not let them ask him for anything (20:3). Now he says: “Once more I will let the people of Israel ask me.” The relationship has been restored. Prayer is possible again.
> Verse 38: At the great festivals, Jerusalem was full of people and animals for sacrifice. The empty cities will be that full again, full of life.
