# Ezekiel – Chapter 14
### Noah, Daniel, and Job

---

## Idols in the heart (Verses 1–11)

[1] Then some of the elders of Israel came to me
and sat down in front of me.
[2] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[3] “Son of man, these men have taken their idols into their hearts.
They have put in front of their faces the things that make them stumble into sin.
Should I let them ask me anything at all?
[4] So speak to them and tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Suppose someone from the people of Israel
takes his idols into his heart
and puts in front of his face the things that make him stumble into sin,
and then he comes to the prophet.
I, the LORD, will answer him there myself,
in a way that fits his many idols.
[5] I will do this to take hold of the hearts of the people of Israel,
because they have all turned away from me through their idols.”’
[6] “So tell the people of Israel,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Turn back! Turn away from your idols!
Turn your faces away from all your disgusting things.
[7] “‘“Suppose someone from the people of Israel,
or one of the foreigners who live in Israel,
separates himself from me.
He takes his idols into his heart.
He puts in front of his face the things that make him stumble into sin.
Then he comes to the prophet to ask me something for himself.
I, the LORD, will answer him myself.
[8] I will turn my face against that man.
I will make him a horror,
a warning sign and a saying people repeat.
I will cut him off from my people.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[9] “‘“If the prophet is deceived and speaks a message,
then I, the LORD, have deceived that prophet.
I will reach out my hand against him
and destroy him from among my people Israel.
[10] They will carry the punishment for their guilt.
The prophet’s guilt will be the same as the guilt of the one who asks him.
[11] Then the people of Israel will no longer wander away from me.
They will no longer make themselves unclean with all their sins.
Instead, they will be my people,
and I will be their God,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The elders come to Ezekiel to ask God for advice. On the outside they seem to be devout. But in their hearts they cling to idols.
> “Idols in the heart”: This does not have to be a figure made of wood or stone. An idol is anything that is more important to us than God: money, power, success, reputation. You can have an idol in your heart even if you look devout on the outside.
> Verse 6: In the middle of the hard words is the call: “Turn back!” God does not want to punish. He wants people to come back.
> Verse 9: A difficult sentence: “Then I, the LORD, have deceived that prophet.” In the Bible, everything is often traced back to God, even hard things (compare 1 Kings 22:19–23). It probably means: Whoever does not want the truth gets, in the end, the deception he is looking for. God lets the lie run its course.
> Verse 11: The goal is not destruction but relationship. God does all this so that “they will be my people, and I will be their God.”

## Only their own lives (Verses 12–20)

[12] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[13] “Son of man, suppose a land sins against me by being unfaithful.
And suppose I reach out my hand against it.
I break its staff of bread and send hunger on it.
I wipe out both people and animals from it.
[14] Even if these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were in it,
their righteousness would save only their own lives,”
says the Lord GOD.
[15] “Suppose I send wild animals through the land,
and they leave it without people,
and it becomes an empty ruin,
so that no one can travel through it because of the animals.
[16] Even if these three men were in it,
as surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“they could not save their sons or their daughters.
Only they themselves would be saved,
but the land would be an empty ruin.
[17] “Or suppose I bring a sword against that land and say,
‘Sword, go through the land,’
and I wipe out both people and animals from it.
[18] Even if these three men were in it,
as surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“they could not save their sons or their daughters.
Only they themselves would be saved.
[19] “Or suppose I send a disease into that land
and pour out my anger on it with bloodshed,
to wipe out both people and animals from it.
[20] Even if Noah, Daniel, and Job were in it,
as surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“they could not save a son or a daughter.
Their righteousness would save only their own lives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Noah, Daniel, and Job were famous for being righteous people. Noah was saved from the flood. Job stayed faithful in his suffering.
> About “Daniel” there are two opinions: Many people think of the prophet Daniel, who lived in Babylon at the same time as Ezekiel. Other scholars think of a very old, famous righteous man named “Danel,” who appears in texts from the neighbors of Israel. The Hebrew spelling here fits that. Both are possible.
> The message is: The people in Jerusalem may have thought: There are a few good people among us. Because of them, God will spare the city (compare Abraham and Sodom, Genesis 18:23–32). But God says: Not this time. Each person is responsible for himself. You cannot hide behind the faith of other people.
> The theme of personal responsibility is explained in detail in Chapter 18.

## A remnant will come (Verses 21–23)

[21] For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“How much worse it will be
when I send my four terrible judgments on Jerusalem,
the sword, hunger, wild animals, and disease,
to wipe out both people and animals from it!
[22] But a remnant will be left in it.
Some sons and daughters will be brought out.
They will come out to you,
and you will see how they live and what they do.
Then you will be comforted about the disaster
that I have brought on Jerusalem,
about everything I have brought on it.
[23] They will comfort you
when you see how they live and what they do.
Then you will know
that I did not do without a reason
all that I have done in it,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Some people from Jerusalem will survive and come to the exiles in Babylon. When the exiles see how these people have lived, they will understand why the disaster came.
> “You will be comforted” means here: You will understand and be able to accept it. It does not mean that the suffering was good. But it was not meaningless, and it was not random.
> Important: This text explains one particular disaster in history. It must not be used today to claim that people who suffer are to blame for it themselves (compare Jesus in John 9:2–3 and Luke 13:1–5).
