# Ezekiel – Chapter 45
### The holy area and honest measures

---

## The holy share of the land (Verses 1–8)

[1] “‘“When you divide the land by lot among the tribes,
you must give a gift to the LORD,
a holy part of the land.
It is to be 25,000 rods (about 50 miles) long
and 10,000 rods (about 20 miles) wide.
The whole area within its borders will be holy.
[2] Out of this, a square of 500 by 500
will be for the sanctuary.
Around it there will be an open space about 88 feet wide.
[3] In this holy area you must measure off a part
25,000 long and 10,000 wide.
In it will be the sanctuary, the most holy place.
[4] It is a holy part of the land.
It will belong to the priests,
who serve in the sanctuary
and who come near to serve the LORD.
It will be a place for their houses
and a holy place for the sanctuary.
[5] An area 25,000 cubits (about 8 miles) long
and 10,000 cubits (about 3⅓ miles) wide
will belong to the Levites, who serve in the temple.
It will be their own property, for twenty rooms.
[6] “‘“As the property of the city you must set aside an area
5,000 cubits (about 1⅔ miles) wide and 25,000 cubits long,
next to the holy area.
It will belong to all the people of Israel.
[7] “‘“The land for the prince
will be on both sides of the holy area
and of the property of the city.
It will lie next to the holy area and next to the property of the city,
on the west side toward the west
and on the east side toward the east.
Its length will be the same as one of the tribes’ shares,
from the western border to the eastern border.
[8] This land will be his property in Israel.
My princes will no longer oppress my people.
Instead they will give the rest of the land to the people of Israel
by their tribes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the land a holy area is measured off. In it are the temple, the land of the priests, the land of the Levites, and the city. To the left and right of it is the land of the prince.
> Verse 1: The WEB says “reeds” (rods) here, but in verses 5 and 6 “cubits.” Here too many Bible teachers think that cubits are meant everywhere (see 42:16–20). With cubits the area would be about 8 by 3 miles.
> Verse 8: An important sentence: “My princes will no longer oppress my people.” The prince gets enough land of his own, so that he is not tempted to take land away from the people. Kings had done this in the past (see Naboth’s vineyard, 1 Kings 21).

## Justice and what is right (Verses 9–12)

[9] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Enough, you princes of Israel!
Put away violence and robbery,
and do what is just and right!
Stop driving my people off their land!”
says the Lord GOD.
[10] “You must use honest scales,
an honest ephah and an honest bath.
[11] The ephah and the bath must be the same size.
The bath must hold one tenth of a homer,
and the ephah one tenth of a homer.
The homer is the standard for both.
[12] The shekel must be twenty gerahs.
Twenty shekels plus twenty-five shekels plus fifteen shekels
make one mina.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “Enough!” God calls on the powerful to stop violence and oppression. Justice and doing what is right are the foundation of a good life together.
> Verses 10–12: Honest measures and weights. In trade people often cheated, for example with false weights (see Amos 8:5; Micah 6:11). God wants people to be honest in business too.
> The measures: An ephah (for grain) and a bath (for liquids) are the same size, about 6 gallons. A homer is ten times as much, about 60 gallons. A shekel is a weight of about 0.4 ounces. A gerah is one twentieth of a shekel. 20 + 25 + 15 = 60 shekels make one mina.
> For today: Being honest at work, fair prices, fair wages – all this is part of living by God’s will.

## Gifts for the prince (Verses 13–17)

[13] “‘“This is the gift you must give:
one sixth of an ephah from every homer of wheat,
and one sixth of an ephah from every homer of barley.
[14] The set share of oil, measured by the bath, is this:
one tenth of a bath from every cor.
A cor is ten baths, the same as a homer.
(For ten baths are a homer.)
[15] And give one lamb out of every two hundred in the flock
from the well-watered pastures of Israel.
These are for grain offerings, burnt offerings, and peace offerings,
to make atonement for the people,”
says the Lord GOD.
To make atonement means to take away guilt so that things are right with God again.
[16] “All the people of the land must give this gift
to the prince in Israel.
[17] It will be the prince’s duty to provide the burnt offerings,
the grain offerings, and the drink offerings
at the festivals, at the new moons, and on the Sabbaths,
at all the appointed festivals of the people of Israel.
He must provide the sin offering, the grain offering, the burnt offering,
and the peace offerings,
to make atonement for the people of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people give a gift: about 1/60 of their grain, 1/100 of their oil, 1/200 of their sheep. That is modest.
> These gifts go to the prince. He is responsible for providing the sacrifices for the whole people at the festivals.
> Here the prince is above all a servant in worship, not a powerful ruler who collects things for himself.

## Festivals and making clean (Verses 18–25)

[18] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“In the first month, on the first day of the month,
you must take a young bull without any defect
and make the sanctuary clean.
[19] The priest must take some of the blood of the sin offering
and put it on the doorposts of the temple,
on the four corners of the ledge of the altar,
and on the posts of the gate of the inner courtyard.
[20] You must do the same on the seventh day of the month
for anyone who sins by mistake
or because he does not know better.
That is how you will make atonement for the temple.
[21] “‘“In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month,
you must celebrate the Passover,
a festival of seven days.
You must eat bread made without yeast.
[22] On that day the prince must provide a bull as a sin offering
for himself and for all the people of the land.
[23] On each of the seven days of the festival
he must provide a burnt offering for the LORD:
seven bulls and seven rams without any defect,
every day for the seven days,
and a male goat every day as a sin offering.
[24] He must provide a grain offering:
an ephah for each bull,
an ephah for each ram,
and a hin of oil (about 1 gallon) for each ephah.
[25] “‘“In the seventh month, on the fifteenth day of the month,
during the festival, he must do the same for seven days.
He must provide the same things
for the sin offering, the burnt offering, the grain offering, and the oil.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 18–20: Twice a year the sanctuary is made clean, especially for the people who sinned “by mistake” or “because they did not know better.” God understands human weakness.
> Verse 21: The Passover (Pesach) reminds people of the freedom from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 12). It is still celebrated in Judaism today. Before his death, Jesus celebrated the Passover meal with his disciples (Luke 22:15).
> Verse 25: The festival in the seventh month on the 15th day is the Feast of Booths (Sukkot), a joyful harvest festival (Leviticus 23:33–43).
> Interesting: Some festivals from the book of Leviticus, like the Feast of Weeks (Shavuot) or the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), are not mentioned here. Ezekiel’s order is different from the laws of Moses in some places. Jewish scholars discussed this very early.
