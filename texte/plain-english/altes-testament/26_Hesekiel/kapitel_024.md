# Ezekiel – Chapter 24
### The cooking pot and the death of Ezekiel’s wife

---

## The day the siege begins (Verses 1–2)

[1] In the ninth year, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[2] “Son of man, write down the date of this day,
of this very day.
On this very day the king of Babylon
has come close to Jerusalem to attack it.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a historical date: In the winter of 588/587 BC (scholars disagree about the exact year), Nebuchadnezzar began the siege of Jerusalem (see 2 Kings 25:1; Jeremiah 52:4). Ezekiel is about 560 miles away in Babylon, and through God he knows it on the same day.
> In Judaism, the 10th of Tevet is still a day of fasting today. It reminds people of the beginning of this siege.

---

## The parable of the cooking pot (Verses 3–14)

[3] Tell a parable to the rebellious people of Israel.
Tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Put the cooking pot on the fire.
Put it on, and pour water into it too.
[4] Put the pieces of meat into it,
all the good pieces, the thigh and the shoulder.
Fill it with the best bones.
[5] Take the best animals of the flock.
Pile up wood under the pot for the bones.
Make it boil well.
Yes, let its bones cook inside it.”
[6] “‘So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“How terrible it will be for the city of blood,
for the pot that is full of rust,
whose rust has not come off!
Take the pieces out of it one after another,
without throwing lots for them.
[7] “‘“For the blood she shed is still inside her.
She poured it on the bare rock.
She did not pour it on the ground,
where dust could cover it.
[8] I have put her blood on the bare rock,
so that it is not covered,
so that my anger rises up to pay her back.”
[9] “‘So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“How terrible it will be for the city of blood!
I too will make the pile of wood big.
[10] Pile on the wood.
Make the fire hot.
Cook the meat well.
Make the broth thick,
and let the bones burn.
[11] Then set the pot empty on its coals,
so that it gets hot
and its bronze glows,
so that its filth melts inside it
and its rust is burned away.
[12] She has worn herself out with hard work.
But her thick rust does not come off her,
not even in the fire.
[13] “‘“Your filth is your shameless sin.
I tried to make you clean, but you did not become clean.
So you will not become clean from your filth again
until my anger against you has come to rest.
[14] “‘“I, the LORD, have spoken.
It will happen, and I will do it.
I will not hold back.
I will not spare you.
I will not change my mind.
They will judge you
by your ways and by what you have done,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: A parable is a story with a picture that teaches something.
> Do you remember Ezekiel 11:3? There the powerful people in Jerusalem said proudly that the city is the pot and they are the meat, so they are well protected. Ezekiel takes up this picture and turns it around: Yes, Jerusalem is a pot. But it stands on the fire, and the meat is cooked to nothing.
> Verse 6: The pot is rusty. The rust is the guilt of the city, which cannot be washed off.
> Verses 7–8: By the law, blood had to be covered with earth (Leviticus 17:13). But Jerusalem has poured out the blood of innocent people openly on the rock. It cries out to heaven like the blood of Abel (Genesis 4:10).
> Verse 13: God tried again and again to make his people clean, through prophets and warnings. But they did not want it.

## Ezekiel’s wife dies (Verses 15–18)

[15] The word of the LORD also came to me. He said,
[16] “Son of man, look, with one blow I will take away from you
the delight of your eyes.
But you must not mourn or cry,
and you must not let your tears flow.
[17] Sigh, but not out loud.
Do not hold any mourning for the dead.
Tie on your turban,
and put your sandals on your feet.
Do not cover your lips,
and do not eat the bread that people bring to mourners.”
[18] So I spoke to the people in the morning,
and in the evening my wife died.
The next morning I did as I was commanded.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the saddest passages in the whole book. Ezekiel’s wife, “the delight of your eyes,” dies suddenly. Here we hear something about her for the first and only time, and you can feel how much he loved her.
> God asks Ezekiel to do something very hard: He must not mourn in public, not wear mourning clothes, and not keep the usual customs of mourning. These customs included, for example, taking off the turban, going barefoot, covering the lower part of the face (“the lips”), and eating mourners’ bread that neighbors brought. He may only sigh quietly. This is a sign-act for the people (see verses 19–24).
> We must not understand this text to mean that God lets people die in order to give a sign. And it is not a rule that people who believe are not allowed to grieve. Quite the opposite: The Bible is full of laments and tears, and Jesus himself cried at the grave of his friend (John 11:35). Grief is allowed and important.
> If you are grieving someone right now and need someone to talk to: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## A sign for the people (Verses 19–24)

[19] The people asked me,
“Won’t you tell us what these things mean for us?
Why are you acting like this?”
[20] Then I said to them,
“The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[21] ‘Speak to the people of Israel.
“This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will make my sanctuary unholy.
It is the pride of your power,
the delight of your eyes,
and what your heart longs for.
And your sons and your daughters whom you left behind
will die by the sword.
[22] You will do what I have done.
You will not cover your lips
or eat the bread that people bring to mourners.
[23] Your turbans will stay on your heads
and your sandals on your feet.
You will not mourn or cry.
Instead you will waste away because of your sins,
and you will groan to one another.
[24] So Ezekiel will be a sign for you.
You will do everything that he has done.
When this happens,
then you will know that I am the Lord GOD.’”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people ask: Why aren’t you mourning? Ezekiel explains: This is what will happen to you. You will lose something that is just as dear to you: the temple, “the delight of your eyes,” and your children in Jerusalem.
> The pain will be so great that you will not even be able to mourn properly. You will be frozen with shock.
> Here Ezekiel’s personal suffering and the suffering of the people become one. The prophet carries the pain of his people in his own body.

## The messenger will come (Verses 25–27)

[25] “And you, son of man,
on the day when I take from them their strength,
the glory that gave them joy,
the delight of their eyes,
and what their hearts long for,
their sons and their daughters,
[26] won’t it be on that day that someone who escaped will come to you
to tell you the news, so that you hear it with your own ears?
[27] On that day your mouth will be opened
to the one who escaped.
You will speak, and you will not be silent anymore.
So you will be a sign for them.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Since his call, Ezekiel had been mostly silent (3:26). He was only allowed to speak when God gave him something to say. Now God announces: When someone who fled brings the news that Jerusalem has been destroyed, Ezekiel’s mouth will be opened. This happens in Ezekiel 33:21–22.
> Here the first great part of the book ends (Chapters 1–24), the words of judgment about Jerusalem. Now come words about the neighboring nations (Chapters 25–32). And after that, starting with Chapter 33, the message of hope begins.
