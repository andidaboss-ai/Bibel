# Ezekiel – Chapter 12
### Bags packed for exile

---

## Ezekiel packs his bags (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, you live among a rebellious people.
They have eyes to see, but they do not see.
They have ears to hear, but they do not hear,
for they are a rebellious people.
[3] “So, son of man,
pack your bags as if you were moving away,
and move out during the day while they watch.
While they watch, move from your place to another place.
Maybe they will understand,
even though they are a rebellious people.
[4] Bring out your bags during the day while they watch,
like bags for moving away.
Then in the evening, go out yourself while they watch,
like people who are going into exile.
[5] While they watch, dig through the wall,
and carry your bags out through the hole.
[6] While they watch, carry the bags on your shoulder,
and take them out in the dark.
Cover your face, so that you cannot see the land.
For I have made you a sign to the people of Israel.”
[7] I did what I was commanded.
During the day I brought out my bags, like bags for moving away.
In the evening I dug through the wall with my hand.
I took my bags out in the dark
and carried them on my shoulder while they watched.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “They have eyes to see, but they do not see.” Jesus takes up these words later (Mark 8:18).
> A new sign action: Ezekiel packs a bag for running away, like someone who has to leave his home. In the evening he digs a hole through the wall of the house and slips away with his face covered. In those days, houses were often made of clay bricks, so it was possible to dig a hole in them.
> Verse 3: “Maybe they will understand.” God does not give up hope.
> Today many millions of people around the world have to flee from their homes, often with only one bag. This text can help us imagine what it is like to be a refugee.

## The prince will run away (Verses 8–16)

[8] In the morning the LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[9] “Son of man, the people of Israel, that rebellious people,
have asked you, ‘What are you doing?’
[10] “Tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“This burden is about the prince in Jerusalem
and all the people of Israel who live there.”’
[11] “Say,
‘I am a sign for you.
What I have done will be done to them.
They will go into exile, into captivity.
[12] “‘The prince who is among them
will carry his bags on his shoulder in the dark
and go out.
They will dig through the wall to carry things out through it.
He will cover his face,
because he will not see the land with his eyes.
[13] I will also spread my net over him,
and he will be caught in my trap.
I will bring him to Babylon, to the land of the Chaldeans.
But he will not see it,
even though he will die there.
[14] All who are around him to help him,
and all his troops,
I will scatter to every wind.
I will pull out a sword and go after them.
[15] “‘They will know that I am the LORD
when I scatter them among the nations
and spread them through the countries.
[16] But I will let a few of them escape
from the sword, from hunger, and from disease.
Then they can tell about all their disgusting deeds
among the nations where they go.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “Burden”: The Hebrew word “massa” means both “burden” and “message” (of a prophecy). Ezekiel carries a real burden on his shoulder, and at the same time it is a message. (Compare Jeremiah 23:33–40.)
> The “prince” is King Zedekiah, the last king of Judah. That is exactly what happened: When Jerusalem fell in 586 before Christ, Zedekiah ran away at night through an opening in the wall. He was caught, and his eyes were put out. Then he was taken to Babylon (2 Kings 25:4–7; Jeremiah 39:4–7).
> Verse 13: “I will bring him to Babylon, to the land of the Chaldeans. But he will not see it, even though he will die there.” That sounds like a riddle, but it came true word for word: Zedekiah was blind when he came to Babylon.
> Verse 16: A few survivors will tell the other nations what happened and why.

## Eating with trembling (Verses 17–20)

[17] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[18] “Son of man, shake while you eat your bread.
Tremble and be afraid while you drink your water.
[19] Tell the people of the land,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says
about the people who live in Jerusalem and in the land of Israel:
“They will eat their bread with fear
and drink their water in horror.
Their land will be stripped empty of everything in it,
because of the violence of all who live there.
[20] The cities where people live will be destroyed,
and the land will be an empty ruin.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Another sign: Ezekiel eats and drinks while trembling with fear. That is how it will be for the people in Jerusalem when the siege comes.
> Verse 19: Again the reason is named: “because of the violence.” Violence destroys not only the victims. In the end it also destroys the community where it happens.

## God’s word will not be delayed (Verses 21–28)

[21] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[22] “Son of man, what is this saying that you have in the land of Israel:
‘The days go on and on,
and every vision comes to nothing’?
[23] So tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will put an end to this saying.
People will not use it as a saying in Israel anymore.”’
Instead, tell them,
‘“The days are near
when every vision will come true.
[24] For there will be no more false visions
and no more flattering fortune-telling among the people of Israel.
[25] For I am the LORD.
I will speak,
and the word I speak will be done.
It will not be put off any longer.
For in your days, you rebellious people,
I will speak the word, and I will do it,”
says the Lord GOD.’”
[26] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[27] “Son of man, the people of Israel are saying,
‘The vision he sees is for many days from now.
He is prophesying about times far in the future.’
[28] “So tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“None of my words will be put off any longer.
The word I speak will be done,”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The people have two excuses:
> Verse 22: The people have a saying: “The days go on and on, and every vision comes to nothing.” That means: The prophets always talk about disaster, but it never happens.
> Verse 27: The people say: “The vision he sees is for many days from now.” That means: Maybe it will happen someday, but not in our time.
> God answers: My word will come true, and soon, “in your days.” And in fact, Jerusalem was destroyed a few years later.
> Today, too, people like to push warnings away: “It won’t be that bad,” or, “It doesn’t affect me.” The text reminds us: It is wise to take warnings seriously and to turn back in time (compare 2 Peter 3:9).
