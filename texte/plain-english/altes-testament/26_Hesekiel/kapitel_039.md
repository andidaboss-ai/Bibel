# Ezekiel – Chapter 39
### Gog’s end and God’s Spirit

---

## Gog falls on the mountains of Israel (Verses 1–8)

[1] “You, son of man, prophesy against Gog and say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I am against you, Gog,
prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal.
[2] I will turn you around.
I will lead you on.
I will make you come up from the far north.
And I will bring you onto the mountains of Israel.
[3] I will knock your bow out of your left hand.
I will make your arrows fall out of your right hand.
[4] You will fall on the mountains of Israel,
you and all your troops and the peoples who are with you.
I will give you as food to all kinds of birds of prey
and to the wild animals.
[5] You will fall on the open field,
for I have spoken,”
says the Lord GOD.
[6] “I will send fire on Magog
and on those who live in safety on the islands.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.
[7] “‘“I will make my holy name known
among my people Israel.
I will not let my holy name be dishonored anymore.
Then the nations will know that I am the LORD,
the Holy One in Israel.
[8] Look, it is coming, and it will happen,”
says the Lord GOD.
“This is the day I have spoken about.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gog is defeated without Israel having to fight. God himself knocks the weapons out of his hands.
> Verse 7: The goal of everything: God’s holy name is to be made known. All nations are to know who God is.

---

## The weapons burn for seven years (Verses 9–10)

[9] “‘“The people who live in the cities of Israel will go out.
They will make fires with the weapons and burn them,
the small and large shields,
the bows and the arrows,
the war clubs and the spears.
They will make fires with them for seven years.
[10] They will not need to take any wood from the field
or cut down any in the forests,
because they will make fires with the weapons.
They will rob those who robbed them,
and take plunder from those who plundered them,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A powerful picture: There are so many weapons that people can use them to heat their homes for seven years. The weapons are no longer needed. They are burned.
> This is like Isaiah 2:4: “They will beat their swords into plowshares.” And like Psalm 46:9 (in some Bibles 46:10): “He breaks the bow and smashes the spear. He burns the chariots in the fire.”
> The hope of the Bible is a world without weapons and without war.

## The burial in the valley of Hamon Gog (Verses 11–16)

[11] “On that day I will give Gog a burial place in Israel,
the valley of the travelers, east of the sea.
It will block the way of the travelers.
There they will bury Gog and all his crowd of people.
They will call it ‘The valley of Hamon Gog.’
[12] “‘“The people of Israel will spend seven months burying them,
to make the land clean.
[13] Yes, all the people of the land will bury them.
And they will become famous for it
on the day I show my glory,”
says the Lord GOD.
[14] “They will choose men whose full-time job
is to go through the land.
These men will go with those who bury the bodies
that are still lying on the ground,
to make the land clean.
After the seven months are over, they will search.
[15] The searchers will go through the land.
When one of them sees a human bone,
he will put up a sign next to it,
until the gravediggers have buried it in the valley of Hamon Gog.
[16] A city will also be called Hamonah.
This is how they will make the land clean.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hamon Gog” means “crowd of Gog.” There are so many dead that it takes seven months to bury them all.
> By the rules about being clean, a dead body made the land unclean (Numbers 19:11–16). So all the bones must be found and buried.
> Remarkable: The enemies are buried too. They get a grave. The dignity of the dead is respected, even that of enemies.
> In the Bible, the number seven (seven years, seven months) often stands for completeness.

## The great sacrifice meal (Verses 17–20)

[17] “You, son of man,
this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Speak to all kinds of birds
and to all the wild animals.
Say, “Gather together and come!
Gather from every side
to my sacrifice that I am killing for you,
a great sacrifice on the mountains of Israel.
There you may eat meat and drink blood.
[18] You will eat the flesh of mighty warriors
and drink the blood of the princes of the earth,
as if they were rams, lambs, and goats,
and bulls, all of them fattened animals from Bashan.
[19] You will eat fat until you are full
and drink blood until you are drunk,
at my sacrifice that I have killed for you.
[20] At my table you will be filled
with horses and chariot drivers,
with mighty warriors and with all soldiers,”
says the Lord GOD.’

> **What does this mean?**
> A horrifying picture: The birds and wild animals are invited to a “sacrifice meal,” and the “sacrifice” is the fallen warriors. The rams, lambs, goats, and bulls in verse 18 are a picture for the leaders and the soldiers.
> The picture is cruel on purpose. It shows the complete end of the enemy’s power. Revelation takes up this picture (Revelation 19:17–18).
> Pictures like this describe God’s judgment on tyrants. They are not joy over people’s death, and they do not give permission for violence.

## God will not hide his face anymore (Verses 21–29)

[21] “I will show my glory among the nations.
Then all the nations will see the judgment I have carried out,
and my hand that I have laid on them.
[22] So the people of Israel will know that I am the LORD their God,
from that day on.
[23] The nations will know
that the people of Israel went into captivity because of their guilt.
They were unfaithful to me,
so I hid my face from them.
I handed them over to their enemies,
and all of them died by the sword.
[24] I treated them as their uncleanness and their sins deserved.
I hid my face from them.
[25] “So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Now I will bring Jacob’s people back from captivity
and have mercy on all the people of Israel.
I will be jealous for my holy name.
[26] They will forget their shame
and all the ways they were unfaithful to me,
when they live in safety in their land,
and no one makes them afraid.
[27] That will be when I have brought them back from the peoples,
gathered them out of the lands of their enemies,
and shown through them that I am holy
in the eyes of many nations.
[28] They will know that I am the LORD their God,
because I sent them into captivity among the nations,
and I have gathered them back into their own land.
I will not leave any of them behind as captives anymore.
[29] I will not hide my face from them anymore,
for I have poured out my Spirit on the people of Israel,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of this dark section there is a wonderful promise.
> Verses 23–24: The nations are to understand: Israel did not go into exile because its God was weak, but because God had hidden his face, because the people were unfaithful.
> Verse 25: “Now I will bring Jacob’s people back from captivity and have mercy on all the people of Israel.” God turns their fate around.
> Verse 29: “I will not hide my face from them anymore, for I have poured out my Spirit on the people of Israel.” God will stay close. This is like Joel, where God promises to pour out his Spirit on all people (Joel 2:28; in some Bibles 3:1). Christians see this fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2:17).
> “God’s face” is a picture for his attention and his closeness. The blessing of Aaron says: “The LORD make his face shine on you” (Numbers 6:25).
