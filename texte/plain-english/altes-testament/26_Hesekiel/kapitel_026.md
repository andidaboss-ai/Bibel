# Ezekiel – Chapter 26
### Against Tyre

---

## Tyre is glad that Jerusalem has fallen (Verses 1–6)

[1] In the eleventh year, on the first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, Tyre has said about Jerusalem,
‘Aha! She is broken!
She was the gateway of the peoples,
but now she has been handed over to me.
I will grow rich, now that she lies in ruins.’
[3] So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I am against you, Tyre.
I will make many nations come up against you,
as the sea makes its waves come up.
[4] They will destroy the walls of Tyre
and tear down her towers.
I will even scrape her dust off her
and make her a bare rock.
[5] She will be a place where fishermen spread out their nets
in the middle of the sea.
For I have spoken,’
says the Lord GOD.
‘She will become plunder for the nations.
[6] Her daughters on the mainland
will be killed with the sword.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Tyre (today Sur in Lebanon) was a rich trading city of the Phoenicians. The most important part of the city was on an island off the coast, so it was very hard to conquer. The Phoenicians were the great sailors and traders of their time.
> Verse 2: Tyre is glad that Jerusalem has fallen, but for business reasons: Jerusalem was a competitor in trade, the “gateway of the peoples.” Now Tyre hopes for more profit. This is about greed: The misfortune of others is my advantage.
> Verse 6: The “daughters on the mainland” are the smaller towns that belonged to Tyre.

---

## Nebuchadnezzar is coming (Verses 7–14)

[7] “For this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will bring Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
the king of kings, against Tyre from the north.
He will come with horses, with chariots, with horsemen,
and with an army of many people.
[8] He will kill your daughters on the mainland with the sword.
He will build siege towers against you.
He will pile up a ramp of earth against you
and raise up shields against you.
[9] He will set his battering rams against your walls.
With his axes he will tear down your towers.
[10] He has so many horses
that their dust will cover you.
Your walls will shake at the noise of the horsemen,
of the wagons, and of the chariots,
when he comes in through your gates,
as men come into a city whose walls have been broken open.
[11] The hoofs of his horses will trample all your streets.
He will kill your people with the sword.
Your strong pillars will fall to the ground.
[12] They will rob you of your riches
and steal your goods.
They will tear down your walls
and destroy your beautiful houses.
They will throw your stones, your wood, and your rubble
into the middle of the water.
[13] I will put an end to the noise of your songs.
The sound of your harps will not be heard anymore.
[14] I will make you a bare rock.
You will be a place where fishermen spread out their nets.
You will never be built again.
For I, the LORD, have spoken,’
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nebuchadnezzar really did lay siege to Tyre, for about 13 years (around 586–573 BC). The towns on the mainland were conquered. But he could not completely destroy the island city. In the end, Tyre surrendered to him. Ezekiel himself later says openly that Nebuchadnezzar got no reward for this siege (Ezekiel 29:17–20).
> About 250 years later, in 332 BC, Alexander the Great conquered Tyre. To do this, he built a causeway of rubble and stones from the mainland into the sea to the island. Many Bible teachers see in this a further fulfillment of verse 12: “They will throw your stones, your wood, and your rubble into the middle of the water.”
> Verse 3: “Many nations … as the sea makes its waves come up” – the judgment comes in several waves, not only through one single king.
> Verse 13: The happy, rich city with its songs and harps will become silent.

---

## The princes of the sea mourn (Verses 15–21)

[15] “This is what the Lord GOD says to Tyre:
‘The islands will shake at the sound of your fall,
when the wounded groan,
when people are killed inside you.
Won’t they?
[16] Then all the princes of the sea will come down from their thrones.
They will take off their robes
and their embroidered clothes.
They will wrap themselves in trembling as if it were clothing.
They will sit on the ground.
They will tremble every moment,
and they will be shocked at what happened to you.
[17] They will sing a song of mourning about you and say to you,
“How you are destroyed,
you city where seafaring men lived,
you famous city!
You were strong on the sea,
you and the people who lived in you.
You made everyone who lived there afraid of you!”
[18] Now the islands will tremble on the day of your fall.
Yes, the islands in the sea will be terrified that you are gone.’
[19] “For this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘I will make you a ruined city,
like the cities where no one lives.
I will bring up the deep ocean over you,
and the great waters will cover you.
[20] Then I will bring you down to those who go down into the pit,
to the people of long ago.
I will make you live in the lowest parts of the earth,
in places that have been in ruins since ancient times,
with those who go down to the pit,
so that no one lives in you anymore.
And I will give glory to the land of the living.
[21] I will make you a horror,
and you will be no more.
People will look for you,
but you will never be found again,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–18: Tyre traded with many coasts and islands in the Mediterranean Sea. When Tyre falls, all its trading partners are terrified. Their wealth depended on Tyre.
> Verse 20: The “pit” is a picture for the world of the dead. Tyre will fall as deep as a dead person into the grave.
> “Glory to the land of the living” – while Tyre goes down, God will give honor and life to his people again.
> The message for today: Wealth and power in trade do not give real security. Whoever thinks only of profit and is glad about the misfortune of others is building on sand.
