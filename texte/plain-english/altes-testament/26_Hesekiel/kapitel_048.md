# Ezekiel – Chapter 48
### The LORD is there

---

## The tribes in the north (Verses 1–7)

[1] “These are the names of the tribes and their shares:
At the northern end,
along the road to Hethlon, to the entrance of Hamath,
as far as Hazar Enan on the border of Damascus,
north next to Hamath
(each share will run from the east side to the west side):
Dan will have one share.
[2] “Next to the border of Dan, from the east side to the west side,
Asher will have one share.
[3] “Next to the border of Asher, from the east side to the west side,
Naphtali will have one share.
[4] “Next to the border of Naphtali, from the east side to the west side,
Manasseh will have one share.
[5] “Next to the border of Manasseh, from the east side to the west side,
Ephraim will have one share.
[6] “Next to the border of Ephraim, from the east side to the west side,
Reuben will have one share.
[7] “Next to the border of Reuben, from the east side to the west side,
Judah will have one share.

> **What does this mean?**
> The land is divided up in a completely new way, into strips of the same width from east to west, like ribbons. Seven tribes live north of the holy area.
> This division is different from the one in Joshua (Joshua 13–19). It is very regular and fair: Every tribe gets a strip of the same width with access to the Jordan and to the sea.
> The tribes that came from the servant women of Jacob (Dan, Asher, Naphtali, Gad) are more toward the edges. The tribes of Leah and Rachel are closer to the sanctuary.

---

## The holy area (Verses 8–22)

[8] “Next to the border of Judah, from the east side to the west side,
will be the special gift of land that you must set apart.
It will be 25,000 rods (about 50 miles) wide,
and as long as one of the tribes’ shares,
from the east side to the west side.
The sanctuary will be in the middle of it.
[9] “The gift of land that you must set apart for the LORD
will be 25,000 rods long
and 10,000 rods (about 20 miles) wide.
[10] This holy gift of land will be for the priests.
On the north side it will be 25,000 long,
on the west side 10,000 wide,
on the east side 10,000 wide,
and on the south side 25,000 long.
The sanctuary of the LORD will be in the middle of it.
[11] It will be for the priests who have been set apart as holy,
the sons of Zadok.
They kept my instructions.
They did not go astray when the people of Israel went astray,
as the Levites went astray.
[12] It will be their special share out of the gift of the land,
a most holy area,
next to the border of the Levites.
[13] “Next to the border of the priests, the Levites will have an area
25,000 cubits (about 8 miles) long
and 10,000 cubits (about 3⅓ miles) wide.
The whole length will be 25,000,
and the width 10,000.
[14] They must not sell any of it
or trade any of it.
This best part of the land must not pass into other hands,
for it is holy to the LORD.
[15] “The remaining area, 5,000 cubits (about 1⅔ miles) wide
and 25,000 long,
will be for ordinary use:
for the city,
for houses, and for pastures.
The city will be in the middle of it.
[16] These will be the city’s measurements:
the north side 4,500,
the south side 4,500,
the east side 4,500,
and the west side 4,500.
[17] The city will have pastures:
250 to the north,
250 to the south,
250 to the east,
and 250 to the west.
[18] What is left of the length, next to the holy gift of land,
will be 10,000 to the east
and 10,000 to the west.
It will lie next to the holy gift of land.
Its harvest will be food for the people who work in the city.
[19] The people who work in the city,
from all the tribes of Israel,
will farm it.
[20] The whole gift of land will be a square,
25,000 by 25,000.
You must set it apart as a holy gift,
together with the property of the city.
[21] “What is left will belong to the prince.
It will lie on both sides of the holy gift of land
and of the property of the city.
It will reach from the 25,000 of the holy area east to the eastern border,
and west from the 25,000 to the western border,
alongside the tribes’ shares.
It will belong to the prince.
The holy gift of land and the sanctuary of the temple
will be in the middle of it.
[22] The property of the Levites
and the property of the city
will lie in the middle of the prince’s land.
The prince’s land will lie between the border of Judah and the border of Benjamin.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the land, between Judah and Benjamin, lies the holy area, a large square. In it are:
> – the land of the priests with the temple in the middle,
> – the land of the Levites,
> – the city with the land around it.
> To the left and right of it is the land of the prince.
> Again the WEB says “reeds” (rods) in verses 8–9, but “cubits” in other places (see 42:16–20 and 45:1). Probably cubits are meant everywhere. Then the holy area would be about 8 miles on each side.
> Verse 14: The holy land must not be sold. It belongs to God.
> Verse 19: The city does not belong to one tribe, but to all. People from all the tribes work there together. The city is a place of unity.
> Remarkable: The temple does not stand in the city, but next to it, in the land of the priests.

## The tribes in the south (Verses 23–29)

[23] “As for the rest of the tribes:
From the east side to the west side,
Benjamin will have one share.
[24] “Next to the border of Benjamin, from the east side to the west side,
Simeon will have one share.
[25] “Next to the border of Simeon, from the east side to the west side,
Issachar will have one share.
[26] “Next to the border of Issachar, from the east side to the west side,
Zebulun will have one share.
[27] “Next to the border of Zebulun, from the east side to the west side,
Gad will have one share.
[28] “Next to the border of Gad, on the south side,
the border will run from Tamar
to the waters of Meribath Kadesh,
along the brook,
to the great sea.
[29] “This is the land that you must divide by lot
among the tribes of Israel to own,
and these are their shares,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Five tribes live south of the holy area. Together with the seven in the north, there are 12 tribes. The whole people of Israel is united again. No tribe is forgotten, not even the tribes of the northern kingdom, which had been taken away more than 100 years before.

## The city with 12 gates (Verses 30–35)

[30] “These are the exits of the city:
The north side is 4,500 rods (about 9 miles) long.
[31] The gates of the city will be named after the tribes of Israel.
There will be three gates on the north side:
one gate for Reuben,
one gate for Judah,
and one gate for Levi.
[32] “The east side is 4,500 rods long.
It has three gates:
one gate for Joseph,
one gate for Benjamin,
and one gate for Dan.
[33] “The south side is 4,500 rods long.
It has three gates:
one gate for Simeon,
one gate for Issachar,
and one gate for Zebulun.
[34] “The west side is 4,500 rods long.
It has three gates:
one gate for Gad,
one gate for Asher,
and one gate for Naphtali.
[35] “The distance all around the city will be 18,000 rods (about 36 miles).
And from that day on, the name of the city will be,
‘The LORD is there.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The city is a square with three gates on each side, 12 gates in all, each named after a tribe of Israel. Here Levi also has a gate, and Joseph stands for Ephraim and Manasseh together. All the tribes have access to the city.
> Revelation takes up this picture: The new Jerusalem has 12 gates with the names of the 12 tribes of Israel (Revelation 21:12–13).
> Verse 35: This is the last sentence of the book, and it is beautiful. The city is no longer called Jerusalem, but “The LORD is there” (in Hebrew “Yahweh Shammah”).
> The book began with a man in exile, far from home, who saw God’s glory in a foreign land. It ends with the certainty: God lives among his people. He is there. Forever.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Ezekiel?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A prophet in exile:** Ezekiel was a priest who was taken away to Babylon in 597 BC. There, far from the temple, heaven opened, and he saw God’s glory (Chapter 1). God is not tied to one place.
> **Judgment on Jerusalem (Chapters 1–24):** With words and many sign-acts, Ezekiel announced that Jerusalem would be destroyed. In a vision he saw God’s glory leave the temple that had been made unholy (Chapters 8–11).
> **The watchman:** Ezekiel was to warn the people like a watchman on the wall (Chapters 3 and 33). He suffered with his people, even through the death of his own wife (Chapter 24).
> **Everyone is responsible:** “The fathers have eaten sour grapes …” is no longer true. Every person can turn back. “I take no pleasure in the death … So turn back, and live!” (18:32; 33:11).
> **The nations (Chapters 25–32):** God is Lord over all nations. Pride, being glad when others suffer, and violence have consequences everywhere: for Tyre, for Egypt, and for all the others.
> **The good shepherd:** “Look, I myself, yes I, will search for my sheep” (Chapter 34). God looks for the lost and bandages the injured.
> **A new heart:** “I will also give you a new heart, and I will put a new spirit inside you” (36:26). God changes people from the inside.
> **Dry bones:** God can make what is dead come alive. “Look, I will open your graves” (Chapter 37). No situation is hopeless for God.
> **The new temple (Chapters 40–48):** God’s glory comes back (Chapter 43). A river flows out of the temple that makes even the Dead Sea come alive (Chapter 47). The foreigners also get a share of the land.
> **Hard texts:** Some pictures in Ezekiel are harsh and violent, especially the pictures of women in Chapters 16 and 23. They describe the disaster of their time. They never justify violence against women or other people.
> **The last sentence:** “The LORD is there” (48:35). The whole book leads up to this.
> **For today:** If you feel as if you are in exile, far from home, without hope, “dried up”: Ezekiel says that God can be found exactly there and can give new life. If you need help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the book of Daniel. Daniel lived at the same time as Ezekiel, at the court of the king of Babylon. The book tells about Daniel and his friends, who stay faithful to God even in a foreign land, about the fiery furnace and the lions’ den. And it contains great visions about the kingdoms of the world and about the Kingdom of God.
