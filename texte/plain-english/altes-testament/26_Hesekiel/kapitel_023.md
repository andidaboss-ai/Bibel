# Ezekiel – Chapter 23
### Oholah and Oholibah

---

## First: How do we read this chapter?

Ezekiel 23, like Chapter 16, is a very harsh chapter full of pictures. Two sisters stand for two cities: Oholah for Samaria (the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel) and Oholibah for Jerusalem (the capital city of the southern kingdom of Judah).
Their “unfaithfulness” is a picture for the politics and the faith of these cities: Instead of trusting God, they held on to foreign world powers, Egypt, Assyria and Babylon, and took over their gods.
In some places the language is very direct about sex, and it is also violent. It was meant to shock even back then. Today many people feel it is hurtful and hostile toward women, and it is all right to say that.
Very important: This chapter is a picture for the guilt of two cities and their leaders, and those were mostly men. It says nothing about what women are like. And it never justifies violence against women or any other person, or shaming them or punishing them.

## Two sisters (Verses 1–4)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[2] “Son of man, there were two women.
They were daughters of the same mother.
[3] They acted like prostitutes in Egypt.
They acted like prostitutes when they were young.
There men touched their breasts.
There men stroked their young nipples.
[4] Their names were Oholah, the older one,
and Oholibah, her sister.
They became mine,
and they had sons and daughters.
As for their names:
Samaria is Oholah,
and Jerusalem is Oholibah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The names are plays on words: “Oholah” means something like “her tent.” “Oholibah” means “my tent is in her.” The “tent” makes us think of the holy place of worship. Maybe it means: Samaria had its own sanctuary that it made for itself, but in Jerusalem stood God’s own temple.
> Verse 3: Already in Egypt, at the very beginning of their history, the people of Israel held on to foreign gods (see 20:7–8). The picture describes young girls who were used for sex in Egypt. From today’s point of view, we must say clearly: When girls are treated like this, it is abuse, and the guilt is never with the girls.

## Oholah: Samaria and Assyria (Verses 5–10)

[5] “Oholah acted like a prostitute while she was mine.
She was madly in love with her lovers,
with the Assyrians, her neighbors.
[6] They were dressed in blue.
They were governors and rulers,
all of them attractive young men,
horsemen riding on horses.
[7] She gave herself to them like a prostitute,
to all of them, the finest men of Assyria.
She made herself unclean
with the idols of every man she desired.
[8] She has not stopped acting like a prostitute since she left Egypt.
For when she was young, men slept with her.
They stroked her young nipples,
and they poured out their lust on her.
[9] “So I handed her over to her lovers,
to the Assyrians she was madly in love with.
[10] They stripped her naked.
They took her sons and her daughters,
and they killed her with the sword.
Women everywhere talked about her as a warning,
because judgment was carried out on her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Samaria, the northern kingdom, was impressed by the shining world power Assyria: the splendid uniforms, the horsemen, the power. It tried to make alliances with Assyria and took over its gods (see 2 Kings 17).
> Verses 9–10: In the end, exactly these “lovers” became the destroyers. In the year 722 BC, Assyria conquered Samaria and took the people away (2 Kings 17:5–6). The sons and daughters, that is, the people who lived there, were killed or taken away.
> The message: Whoever trusts in power and glamour instead of in God will in the end be destroyed by exactly this power.

## Oholibah: Jerusalem is even worse (Verses 11–21)

[11] “Her sister Oholibah saw this.
And yet she was even more corrupt than her sister in her lust.
Her prostitution was even worse
than the prostitution of her sister.
[12] She lusted after the Assyrians,
governors and rulers, her neighbors.
They were dressed in splendid clothes.
They were horsemen riding on horses,
all of them attractive young men.
[13] I saw that she had made herself unclean.
Both sisters went the same way.
[14] “She did even more as a prostitute.
For she saw men drawn on the wall,
pictures of Chaldeans painted in red.
[15] They wore belts around their waists
and flowing turbans on their heads.
All of them looked like princes.
They looked like the Babylonians in Chaldea,
the land where they were born.
[16] As soon as she saw them, she lusted after them.
She sent messengers to them in Chaldea.
[17] The Babylonians came to her, into her bed of love.
They made her unclean with their lust.
She became unclean through them,
and then she turned away from them in disgust.
[18] So she showed her prostitution openly
and showed her naked body.
Then I turned away from her in disgust,
just as I had turned away from her sister.
[19] But she did even more as a prostitute.
She remembered the days when she was young,
when she had acted like a prostitute in the land of Egypt.
[20] She lusted after her lovers there.
Their bodies were like the bodies of donkeys,
and their semen was like the semen of horses.
[21] So you longed again for the shameless acts of your youth,
when the Egyptians stroked your nipples
because your breasts were young.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jerusalem has learned nothing from what happened to Samaria. It makes the same mistakes, only worse:
> – First Judah looked for protection from Assyria (see 2 Kings 16:7–8).
> – Then it was fascinated by Babylon. Even pictures of Babylonian officers were enough (verses 14–16). Judah sent messengers to Babylon.
> – Then it turned away from Babylon again and looked for help from Egypt (verses 19–21).
> Verse 20: This language is crude and shocking on purpose. It is meant to show how undignified and disgusting this back and forth between the world powers is. Egypt was famous for its horses (see 1 Kings 10:28).
> Verses 17–18: An important detail: Jerusalem turns away from Babylon, and God turns away from Jerusalem. Unfaithfulness destroys relationships.

## The judgment on Oholibah (Verses 22–35)

[22] “So, Oholibah, this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will stir up your lovers against you,
the ones you turned away from in disgust.
I will bring them against you from every side:
[23] the Babylonians and all the Chaldeans,
Pekod, Shoa, and Koa,
and all the Assyrians with them.
All of them are attractive young men,
governors and rulers,
princes and famous men.
All of them ride on horses.
[24] They will come against you with weapons, chariots, and wagons,
and with a crowd of peoples.
They will take their places against you all around,
with large shields, small shields, and helmets.
I will give them the right to judge you,
and they will judge you by their own laws.
[25] I will turn my jealous anger against you,
and they will treat you with fury.
They will cut off your nose and your ears.
Those of you who are left will die by the sword.
They will take your sons and your daughters.
And the rest of you will be burned up by fire.
[26] They will also strip off your clothes
and take away your beautiful jewelry.
[27] So I will put an end to your shameless acts
and to the prostitution you began in the land of Egypt.
You will not look at them with longing anymore,
and you will not think of Egypt anymore.’
[28] “For this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will hand you over to the people you hate,
to the people you turned away from in disgust.
[29] They will treat you with hatred.
They will take away everything you worked for.
They will leave you naked and bare.
Your naked prostitution will be shown openly,
your shameless acts and your prostitution.
[30] These things will be done to you
because you acted like a prostitute with the nations,
and because you made yourself unclean with their idols.
[31] You have walked in the way of your sister.
So I will put her cup into your hand.’
[32] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘You will drink from your sister’s cup.
It is deep and wide.
People will laugh at you and mock you.
The cup holds a lot.
[33] You will be filled with drunkenness and sorrow.
It is the cup of horror and destruction,
the cup of your sister Samaria.
[34] You will drink it and empty it.
You will chew on its broken pieces,
and you will tear at your breasts.
For I have spoken,’
says the Lord GOD.
[35] “So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘You have forgotten me
and thrown me behind your back.
So you too must bear the results of your shameless acts and your prostitution.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–24: The world powers that Jerusalem “loved” now come as enemies. Pekod, Shoa, and Koa were peoples in the area of Babylon.
> Verse 25: Cutting off the nose and ears was a cruel punishment in war used by the great powers back then. Ezekiel describes what the conquerors will do. This is a description of the horrors of war, not approval of them.
> Verses 31–34: The “cup” is a well-known picture in the Bible for the fate that a person must “drink” (see Jeremiah 25:15–29). Jerusalem must drink the same bitter cup as Samaria.
> Verse 35: God names the real reason: “You have forgotten me and thrown me behind your back.” Being unfaithful to God is the heart of the problem.

## Both sisters on trial (Verses 36–49)

[36] The LORD also said to me,
“Son of man, will you judge Oholah and Oholibah?
Then tell them the disgusting things they have done.
[37] For they have broken their marriage vows,
and there is blood on their hands.
They have been unfaithful with their idols.
They have even made their sons, whom they had borne to me,
pass through the fire to the idols, to be burned up as food for them.
[38] They have also done this to me:
On that same day they made my sanctuary unclean,
and they treated my Sabbaths as if they were not holy.
[39] For when they had killed their children for their idols,
they came into my sanctuary that same day and made it unholy.
Look, they did this inside my house.
[40] “And you sisters even sent for men
who came from far away.
A messenger was sent to them,
and look, they came.
For them you washed yourself,
put on eye makeup,
and decorated yourself with jewelry.
[41] You sat on a splendid couch,
with a table set in front of it.
On it you put my incense and my oil.
[42] “The noise of a carefree crowd was around her.
Along with the ordinary men,
drunkards were brought in from the wilderness.
They put bracelets on the women’s arms
and beautiful crowns on their heads.
[43] Then I said about the woman who had grown old in adultery,
‘Now they will use her as a prostitute, and she will act like one with them.’
[44] They went in to her,
as men go in to a prostitute.
That is how they went in to Oholah and to Oholibah,
the shameless women.
[45] Righteous men will judge them
with the punishment for women who break their marriage vows
and with the punishment for women who shed blood.
For they have broken their marriage vows,
and there is blood on their hands.
[46] “For this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘I will bring up a mob against them.
I will hand them over to be pushed around and robbed.
[47] The crowd will throw stones at them
and cut them down with their swords.
They will kill their sons and their daughters
and burn down their houses.
[48] “‘So I will put an end to shameless acts in the land,
so that all women may be warned
and not act shamelessly as you did.
[49] People will pay you back for your shameless acts.
You will bear the results of your sins with your idols.
Then you will know that I am the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 37–39: Here it becomes clear what this is really about: the worship of idols and the sacrifice of children. On the same day that they had killed children for idols, they went into God’s temple as if nothing had happened. God cannot bear this hypocrisy.
> Verses 40–42: Jerusalem makes itself beautiful for foreign messengers. This probably means official receptions and talks about alliances, where God’s gifts (incense and oil) were wasted on foreigners.
> Verses 45–47: Again the language of the punishments of that time is used. The “mob” is the army that will destroy the cities.
> Verse 48: “So that all women may be warned” – in the picture, the “women” are probably the other cities and nations. This verse must never be understood to mean that real women should be frightened or controlled with violence. Violence against women is wrong, always.
> In another place, the Bible itself shows a completely different way: Jesus does not condemn the woman who was going to be stoned. Instead he says to the men: “He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her” (John 8:7).
