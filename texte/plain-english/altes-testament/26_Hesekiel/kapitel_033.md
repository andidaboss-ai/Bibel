# Ezekiel – Chapter 33
### The city has fallen

---

## The watchman (Verses 1–9)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, speak to the people of your nation and tell them,
‘Suppose I bring the sword against a land.
The people of the land choose a man from among them
and make him their watchman.
[3] He sees the sword coming against the land.
He blows the trumpet
and warns the people.
[4] Then whoever hears the sound of the trumpet
and does not pay attention to the warning,
if the sword comes and takes him away,
his blood will be on his own head.
[5] He heard the sound of the trumpet,
but he did not let himself be warned.
His blood will be on him.
If he had paid attention to the warning,
he would have saved his life.
[6] But suppose the watchman sees the sword coming
and does not blow the trumpet.
The people are not warned.
The sword comes and takes someone from among them.
That person is taken away because of his own guilt.
But I will hold the watchman responsible for his blood.’
[7] “So you, son of man,
I have made you a watchman for the people of Israel.
So hear the word that I speak,
and warn them for me.
[8] Suppose I say to a wicked person,
‘You wicked man, you will surely die,’
and you do not speak up to warn the wicked person to leave his way.
Then that wicked person will die because of his guilt,
but I will hold you responsible for his blood.
[9] But suppose you warn the wicked person to turn away from his way,
and he does not turn away from his way.
Then he will die because of his guilt,
but you have saved your life.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the third part of the book begins. God calls Ezekiel once more to be a “watchman,” as he already did in Ezekiel 3:16–21.
> The picture is simple: A watchman on the city wall is to sound the alarm when enemies come. If he warns and the people do not listen, it is their own fault. If he stays silent, he shares the guilt.
> The repetition shows: Even after the disaster, Ezekiel’s task stays the same. He is to warn the people and call them to turn back. But now the goal is that they live.

## Why do you want to die? (Verses 10–20)

[10] “You, son of man, say to the people of Israel,
‘This is what you are saying:
“Our wrongdoings and our sins weigh on us,
and we are wasting away because of them.
How can we live?”’
[11] Tell them,
‘“As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked.
I want the wicked to turn from his way and live.
Turn back, turn back from your evil ways!
Why should you die, people of Israel?”’
[12] “You, son of man, say to the people of your nation,
‘The righteousness of a righteous person will not save him
on the day he disobeys.
And as for the wickedness of a wicked person,
it will not make him fall
on the day he turns away from his wickedness.
And a righteous person will not be able to live because of his righteousness
on the day he sins.
[13] Suppose I tell a righteous person that he will surely live.
But then he trusts in his righteousness and does wrong.
Then none of his righteous deeds will be remembered.
He will die because of the wrong he has done.
[14] And suppose I say to a wicked person,
“You will surely die.”
But then he turns away from his sin
and does what is just and right.
[15] The wicked person gives back what he took as a pledge for a loan.
He pays back what he stole.
He follows the rules that lead to life
and does nothing wrong.
Then he will surely live.
He will not die.
[16] None of the sins he has done will be held against him.
He has done what is just and right.
He will surely live.
[17] “‘But the people of your nation say,
“The way of the Lord is not fair.”
But it is their own way that is not fair.
[18] When a righteous person turns away from his righteousness
and does wrong,
he will die because of it.
[19] When a wicked person turns away from his wickedness
and does what is just and right,
he will live because of it.
[20] But you say,
“The way of the Lord is not fair.”
People of Israel, I will judge each one of you by his ways.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: After the disaster, the people are in despair: “Our wrongdoings and our sins weigh on us, and we are wasting away because of them. How can we live?” They have lost all hope.
> Verse 11: God’s answer is one of the most beautiful sentences in the Bible: “I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked. I want the wicked to turn from his way and live. Turn back, turn back from your evil ways! Why should you die” (verse 11). The same thought is in Ezekiel 18:23 and 18:32. God wants life, not death.
> Verses 12–20: This repeats the message from Chapter 18: Every person can turn back. The past does not have to decide the future.
> Verse 15: Turning back is very practical: giving back what was stolen, making up for wrong (see Zacchaeus in Luke 19:8).
> If you feel that your guilt or your situation is crushing you, and you see no way out: Talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## The news arrives (Verses 21–22)

[21] In the twelfth year of our captivity,
in the tenth month, on the fifth day of the month,
a man who had escaped from Jerusalem came to me.
He said,
“The city has been conquered!”
[22] The hand of the LORD had been on me in the evening,
before the man who escaped arrived.
The LORD had opened my mouth
by the time the man came to me in the morning.
So my mouth was opened,
and I was no longer silent.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the turning point in the whole book! A refugee brings the terrible news: Jerusalem has fallen. (The city had been destroyed in the summer of 586 BC. The messenger needed several months for the long journey.)
> Just as God had announced in 24:25–27, Ezekiel’s mouth is now opened. His silence is over. From now on he speaks mostly words of hope.

## The survivors in the land (Verses 23–29)

[23] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[24] “Son of man, the people who live in the ruins in the land of Israel are saying,
‘Abraham was only one man,
and he received the land as his own.
But we are many.
The land has been given to us as our own.’
[25] So tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“You eat meat with the blood still in it.
You look to your idols for help.
You shed blood.
And should you own the land?
[26] You rely on your sword.
You do disgusting things.
Each of you sleeps with his neighbor’s wife.
And should you own the land?”’
[27] “Tell them,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“As surely as I live,
those who are in the ruins will die by the sword.
Whoever is in the open field I will give to the wild animals to be eaten.
And those who are in the fortresses and in the caves
will die of a deadly disease.
[28] I will make the land a ruin and a horror.
The pride of her power will come to an end.
The mountains of Israel will be ruined,
so that no one will travel through them.
[29] Then they will know that I am the LORD,
when I have made the land a ruin and a horror
because of all the disgusting things they have done.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> Some people have stayed behind in the ruins of Jerusalem. They say: Abraham was alone and got the whole land. We are many, so the land belongs to us all the more.
> God answers: You appeal to Abraham, but you do not live like Abraham. You do not keep God’s commandments. You trust in violence (“you rely on your sword”). You cannot appeal to God’s promises and do wrong at the same time.
> Verse 25: God says: “You eat meat with the blood still in it.” That was forbidden (Genesis 9:4; Leviticus 17:10–14).

## A beautiful song, but no one acts (Verses 30–33)

[30] “As for you, son of man,
the people of your nation are talking about you
by the walls and in the doorways of the houses.
They say to one another, each to his brother,
‘Come and hear
what the word is that comes from the LORD.’
[31] They come to you the way people come,
and they sit in front of you as my people.
They hear your words,
but they do not do them.
With their mouths they show a lot of love,
but their hearts chase after their own profit.
[32] Look, to them you are like a very lovely song
sung by someone with a pleasant voice
who can play an instrument well.
For they hear your words,
but they do not do them.
[33] “But when this happens
– and look, it is coming –
then they will know
that a prophet has been among them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now that Ezekiel’s predictions have come true, he is suddenly popular. People talk about him and come to hear him.
> But God says: They listen to you like a good singer with a beautiful voice. They find it entertaining. But they change nothing in their lives.
> This is an important warning, for today too: You can find a sermon beautiful, enjoy a worship service, or find a Bible chapter interesting, and still not put any of it into practice in your life. Jesus says: “So everyone who hears these words of mine and does them is like a wise man who built his house on the rock” (Matthew 7:24). See also James 1:22: “But be doers of the word, not only hearers.”
