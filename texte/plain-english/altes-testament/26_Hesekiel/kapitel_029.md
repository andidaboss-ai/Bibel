# Ezekiel – Chapter 29
### Against Pharaoh of Egypt

---

## The great monster in the Nile (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the tenth year, in the tenth month, on the twelfth day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, turn your face against Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.
Prophesy against him and against all of Egypt.
[3] Speak and say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I am against you, Pharaoh, king of Egypt,
you great monster lying in the middle of your rivers.
You have said,
‘My river is my own.
I made it for myself.’
[4] I will put hooks in your jaws.
I will make the fish of your rivers stick to your scales.
I will pull you up out of the middle of your rivers,
with all the fish of your rivers
that stick to your scales.
[5] I will throw you out into the wilderness,
you and all the fish of your rivers.
You will fall on the open field.
No one will pick you up or gather you.
I have given you as food to the wild animals
and to the birds of the sky.
[6] “‘“Then all the people who live in Egypt will know that I am the LORD.
For they have been a walking stick made of reed for the people of Israel.
[7] When they took hold of you with their hand,
you broke and tore open their shoulders.
When they leaned on you,
you broke and made their legs give way.”

> **What does this mean?**
> From here on come four chapters against Egypt (29–32). The date is about January 587 BC, during the siege of Jerusalem. Jerusalem was hoping for help from Egypt.
> Verse 3: Pharaoh is described as a “great monster” in the Nile, probably a crocodile. He says proudly: The Nile belongs to me, I made it. Again the sin of pride: A human being thinks he is the creator.
> Verses 4–5: God will pull the crocodile out of the water with hooks and throw it into the wilderness, where it dies.
> Verses 6–7: For Israel, Egypt was like a walking stick made of reed: If you lean on it, it breaks and even hurts you (see Isaiah 36:6). The message: Whoever trusts in people and world powers instead of in God will be disappointed.

---

## Egypt will be ruined (Verses 8–12)

[8] “‘So this is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I will bring a sword against you.
I will cut off people and animals from you.
[9] The land of Egypt will become a ruin and a wasteland.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.
“‘“He has said,
‘The river is mine. I made it.’
[10] So look, I am against you and against your rivers.
I will make the land of Egypt a complete wasteland and ruin,
from the tower of Seveneh all the way to the border of Ethiopia.
[11] No human foot will walk through it,
and no animal’s foot will walk through it.
No one will live in it for 40 years.
[12] I will make the land of Egypt a ruin
among the countries that are ruined.
Her cities will lie in ruins for 40 years
among the cities that are destroyed.
I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations
and spread them out through the countries.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Egypt will be ruined “from the tower of Seveneh all the way to the border of Ethiopia.” Seveneh is today’s Aswan in the south of Egypt. Ethiopia here means the land south of Egypt, in today’s Sudan (in Hebrew: Cush).
> Verses 11–12: In the Bible, “40 years” is often a round number for a long time, about one generation (see the 40 years in the wilderness). We do not know from other sources that Egypt was completely ruined for exactly 40 years. But Nebuchadnezzar really did attack Egypt later (around 568 BC), and Egypt lost its earlier position as a world power.

## A lowly kingdom (Verses 13–16)

[13] “‘For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“At the end of 40 years
I will gather the Egyptians from the peoples
where they were scattered.
[14] I will bring Egypt back from captivity.
I will bring them back to the land of Pathros,
the land where they came from.
There they will be a lowly kingdom.
[15] It will be the lowest of the kingdoms.
It will never again raise itself above the nations.
I will make them small,
so that they will not rule over the nations anymore.
[16] The people of Israel will never again trust in Egypt.
That trust would only remind them of their guilt,
when they turned to Egypt for help.
Then they will know that I am the Lord GOD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Interesting: There is a restoration after the judgment for Egypt too. God gathers the Egyptians again. God cares about other nations too (see Isaiah 19:19–25, where Egypt is even called “my people”).
> Pathros is Upper Egypt, the south of the country, where the Egyptian kingdom first came from.
> Verse 16: Egypt will no longer be a powerful kingdom. So Israel can no longer be tempted to trust in Egypt instead of in God.

---

## Egypt as payment for Nebuchadnezzar (Verses 17–21)

[17] In the twenty-seventh year, in the first month, on the first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[18] “Son of man, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon,
made his army work very hard against Tyre.
Every head was rubbed bald,
and every shoulder was rubbed raw.
But he and his army got no pay from Tyre
for the hard work he did against it.
[19] So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will give the land of Egypt to Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.
He will carry off its people,
take its riches as plunder,
and rob it.
That will be the pay for his army.
[20] I have given him the land of Egypt as his payment
for the work he did,
because they worked for me,’
says the Lord GOD.
[21] “On that day I will make a horn grow for the people of Israel.
And I will open your mouth among them.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: This is the latest date in the whole book of Ezekiel: about 571 BC, 16 years after Jerusalem was destroyed. So this word was added here later, because it fits the theme of Egypt.
> Verse 18: A remarkably honest text! In Chapter 26, Ezekiel had announced that Nebuchadnezzar would completely destroy Tyre. Here he says openly: Nebuchadnezzar besieged Tyre for 13 years. His soldiers carried so much that their heads were bald and their shoulders were raw. But he got no plunder. The island city held out.
> The Bible does not hide this. Prophecy is not simply telling the future in every detail. God remains free in the way he fulfills his words.
> Verse 21: To “make a horn grow” is a picture for new power and strength (see Psalm 132:17).
