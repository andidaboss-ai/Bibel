# Ezekiel – Chapter 42
### The holy rooms and the wall

---

## The rooms in the north (Verses 1–9)

[1] Then he led me out into the outer courtyard,
on the way toward the north.
He brought me to the building with rooms
that was across from the separate yard
and across from the building on the north side.
[2] The building was about 175 feet long,
with its door facing north.
It was about 88 feet wide.
[3] It stood across from the open space of about 35 feet
that belonged to the inner courtyard,
and across from the pavement of the outer courtyard.
Gallery faced gallery on all three floors.
[4] In front of the rooms was a walkway on the inside.
It was about 17½ feet wide
and about 175 feet long.
Their doors faced north.
[5] The upper rooms were narrower,
because the galleries took more space away from them
than from the lower and middle rooms of the building.
[6] For the rooms were on three floors,
and they did not have pillars like the pillars of the courtyards.
So the top floor was set back more
than the lowest and the middle floor.
[7] There was an outer wall alongside the rooms,
toward the outer courtyard, in front of the rooms.
It was about 88 feet long.
[8] For the rooms on the side of the outer courtyard
were about 88 feet long.
Look, the rooms facing the temple
were about 175 feet long.
[9] Below these rooms was the entrance on the east side,
where people go into them from the outer courtyard.

> **What does this mean?**
> Next to the temple there are large buildings with rooms on three floors. The floors are set back step by step toward the top (like terraces).
> The exact measurements and the layout are hard to follow even for experts. What is important is what the rooms are used for. That is explained in verses 13–14.
> A note about verse 4: In the WEB it says that the walkway was “ten cubits’ width inward, a way of one cubit.” The Greek translation reads “one hundred cubits” as its length here, and we have followed that in the text.

---

## The rooms in the south (Verses 10–12)

[10] In the thickness of the wall of the courtyard toward the east,
in front of the separate yard and in front of the building,
there were rooms.
[11] The walkway in front of them looked like the one
in front of the rooms on the north side.
They had the same length and the same width.
All their exits had the same layout and the same doors.
[12] Like the doors of the rooms on the south side,
there was a door at the beginning of the walkway,
the walkway right in front of the wall toward the east,
where people go into them.

> **What does this mean?**
> On the south side there are the same rooms as on the north side. Again it is clear: The whole temple is built in a balanced and even way.

---

## What the holy rooms are for (Verses 13–14)

[13] Then he said to me,
“The north rooms and the south rooms,
which are across from the separate yard,
are the holy rooms.
There the priests who come near to the LORD
are to eat the most holy offerings.
There they are to put the most holy offerings:
the grain offering, the sin offering, and the guilt offering.
For the place is holy.
[14] When the priests go in,
they must not go out of the holy place into the outer courtyard
until they have taken off the clothes
they wear when they serve there.
For these clothes are holy.
They must put on other clothes
before they go near the places that are for the people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In these rooms the priests eat their share of the sacrifices (see Leviticus 6:16, 26; in some Bibles 6:9, 19). They also keep the holy gifts there.
> Verse 14: The priests wear special clothes in the sanctuary. Before they go out to the people, they must change their clothes. This makes a clear difference between what is holy and what is ordinary.

## The wall around the temple (Verses 15–20)

[15] When he had finished measuring the inner temple area,
he led me out through the gate that faces east.
He measured the area all around.
[16] He measured the east side with the measuring rod.
It was 500 rods (about 5,250 feet) by the measuring rod.
[17] He measured the north side.
It was 500 rods by the measuring rod.
[18] He measured the south side.
It was 500 rods by the measuring rod.
[19] He turned to the west side
and measured 500 rods by the measuring rod.
[20] So he measured the area on all four sides.
It had a wall all around it.
It was 500 cubits (about 875 feet) long
and 500 cubits wide.
The wall separated what is holy from what is ordinary.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, the whole temple area is measured: a large square with a wall all around.
> Verses 16–20: In verses 16–19 the WEB says “five hundred reeds,” but in verse 20 “five hundred cubits.” That is a big difference (500 rods would be about a mile, 500 cubits about 875 feet). The Greek translation (the Septuagint) has “cubits” everywhere, and that also fits the other measurements of the temple. So many Bible teachers think that 500 cubits is right.
> Verse 20: The purpose of the wall: to separate what is holy from what is ordinary. God’s holiness is to be protected and respected. This was very important to Ezekiel, because the old temple had been made unholy by the worship of idols (Chapter 8).
> Jews and Christians read this vision of the temple in different ways: Some Jews expect that such a temple will be built one day. Many Christians understand it more as a picture: God lives among people in Christ and in his church (see John 2:19–21; 1 Corinthians 3:16; Revelation 21–22).
