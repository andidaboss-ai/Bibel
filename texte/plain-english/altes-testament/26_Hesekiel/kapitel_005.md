# Ezekiel – Chapter 5
### The shaved hair

---

## Hair in the fire, by the sword, and in the wind (Verses 1–4)

[1] “And you, son of man, take a sharp sword.
Use it like a barber’s razor.
Shave your head and your beard with it.
Then take scales for weighing, and divide the hair into parts.
[2] When the days of the siege are over,
burn one third of the hair in the fire in the middle of the city.
Take another third,
and strike it with the sword all around the city.
Scatter the last third into the wind.
And I will pull out a sword and go after them.
[3] Take a few of the hairs,
and tie them up in the folds of your robe.
[4] Take some of these again,
and throw them into the middle of the fire, and burn them up.
From there a fire will spread to all the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The next sign: Ezekiel shaves his head and beard with a sword. Really this was forbidden for a priest (Leviticus 21:5). A shaved head was a sign of mourning and shame.
> The hair is divided into three parts. They show what will happen to the people in Jerusalem: One part dies in the city, one part by the sword, and one part is scattered to all the winds.
> Verse 3: A few hairs are kept in the robe. That is a small ray of hope: A remnant is kept safe. But even some of this remnant is burned (verse 4). Even those who are rescued are not simply safe.

## This is Jerusalem (Verses 5–17)

[5] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘This is Jerusalem.
I have put her in the middle of the nations,
with other countries all around her.
[6] But she has rebelled against my laws.
She has done more evil than the nations.
She has rebelled against my rules
more than the countries around her.
For her people have rejected my laws,
and they have not lived by my rules.’
[7] “That is why the Lord GOD says:
‘You have been more unruly than the nations around you.
You have not lived by my rules.
You have not kept my laws.
You have not even followed the laws of the nations around you.
[8] That is why the Lord GOD says:
I, yes, I myself, am against you.
I will carry out judgments among you
while the nations watch.
[9] Because of all your disgusting deeds,
I will do something to you that I have never done before,
and I will never do anything like it again.
[10] That is why among you fathers will eat their sons,
and sons will eat their fathers.
I will carry out judgments on you.
I will scatter all of you who are left
to all the winds.
[11] That is why, as surely as I live,’ says the Lord GOD,
‘because you have made my holy place unclean
with all your disgusting idols and all your disgusting deeds,
I will also cut you down.
My eye will not spare you,
and I will have no pity.
[12] One third of you will die of disease
or will be destroyed by hunger inside the city.
One third will be killed by the sword around you.
And one third I will scatter to all the winds,
and I will pull out a sword and go after them.
[13] “‘That is how my anger will come to an end.
I will let my anger against them come to rest,
and I will be comforted.
When I have finished pouring out my anger on them,
they will know that I, the LORD, have spoken in my burning love.
[14] “‘I will also make you a ruin
and a shame among the nations around you,
in front of everyone who passes by.
[15] You will be a shame and a mockery,
a warning and a horror
to the nations around you,
when I carry out judgments on you
in anger, in fury, and with angry punishments.
I, the LORD, have spoken.
[16] I will shoot the deadly arrows of hunger at them,
arrows that bring destruction.
I will send them to destroy you.
I will make the hunger worse and worse for you,
and I will break your staff of bread.
[17] I will send hunger and wild animals against you,
and they will take your children from you.
Disease and bloodshed will sweep through you.
I will bring the sword against you.
I, the LORD, have spoken.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: God says about Jerusalem: “I have put her in the middle of the nations.” She was meant to be an example, a light for the other peoples. But Jerusalem behaved even worse than her neighbors.
> Verses 9–10: These words are terrible. During long sieges, there really was such terrible hunger that people became cannibals (compare Lamentations 2:20; 4:10). Ezekiel describes the disaster that is coming without making anything sound nicer than it is.
> Verses 11–13: God speaks very harshly here: “I will have no pity.” Words like these show how seriously God takes wrongdoing. But they do not stand alone: Later in the book, God says that he takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked. He wants the wicked to turn from his way and live (Ezekiel 33:11; compare 18:23).
> Important: These texts describe God’s judgment in a certain situation. They give no one the right to do violence to other people or to explain suffering as a “punishment from God.”
> Verse 13: “I will be comforted” is what the WEB says. It means: God’s anger comes to rest.
