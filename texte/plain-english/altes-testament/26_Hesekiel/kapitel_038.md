# Ezekiel – Chapter 38
### Gog of the land of Magog

---

## Gog and his armies (Verses 1–9)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, turn your face toward Gog of the land of Magog,
the prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal.
Prophesy against him.
[3] Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I am against you, Gog,
prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal.
[4] I will turn you around
and put hooks into your jaws.
I will lead you out with your whole army,
horses and horsemen,
all of them in full armor,
a great crowd with large and small shields,
all of them holding swords.
[5] Persia, Cush, and Put are with them,
all of them with shields and helmets.
[6] Gomer and all his troops are there,
the house of Togarmah from the far north
and all his troops,
many peoples with you.
[7] “‘“Be ready, yes, get yourself ready,
you and all the troops gathered around you,
and be their commander.
[8] After many days you will be called up.
In the years to come you will come into a land
that has recovered from the sword.
Its people were gathered from many peoples
to the mountains of Israel,
which had lain in ruins for a long time.
They were brought out from the peoples,
and all of them live in safety.
[9] You will go up.
You will come like a storm.
You will be like a cloud that covers the land,
you and all your troops,
and many peoples with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Chapters 38–39 are very different from the rest of the book. In huge, almost unreal pictures they describe one last great attack on Israel, after the people have come back and are living in peace.
> Who is “Gog of the land of Magog”? We do not know for sure. Maybe the name is like that of a king Gyges of Lydia (in today’s Turkey). It is more likely that Gog is a symbolic figure: the essence of all enemies of God and his people, who come from the far-off, frightening north.
> “Rosh” (verse 2): The WEB says “prince of Rosh.” The Hebrew word “rosh” simply means “head” or “chief.” So many translations write “chief prince of Meshech and Tubal.” Some people have said that “Rosh” means “Russia.” But there is no basis in language or history for this.
> The peoples who are named come from all directions: Meshech, Tubal, Gomer and Togarmah from the north (Asia Minor), Persia from the east, Cush (the area south of Egypt) and Put (Libya) from the south. This shows: The whole world seems to stand against God’s people.
> Verse 4: “Hooks into your jaws” – Gog thinks he is acting by his own will. But really God leads him like an animal on a hook.

## Gog’s evil plan (Verses 10–16)

[10] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“On that day thoughts will come into your mind,
and you will make an evil plan.
[11] You will say,
‘I will go up against the land of villages without walls.
I will attack the people who are at peace,
who live in safety,
all of them living without walls,
without bars or gates.
[12] I will take plunder and rob them.
I will turn my hand against the ruins where people live again,
and against the people who were gathered from the nations.
They have gotten livestock and goods,
and they live in the center of the earth.’
[13] Sheba, Dedan, and the traders of Tarshish
with all its young lions will ask you,
‘Have you come to take plunder?
Have you gathered your troops to rob,
to carry away silver and gold,
to take away livestock and goods,
to take a great amount of plunder?’”’
[14] “So, son of man, prophesy and tell Gog,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“On that day, when my people Israel live in safety,
won’t you notice it?
[15] You will come from your place
in the far north,
you and many peoples with you,
all of them riding on horses,
a great crowd and a mighty army.
[16] You will come up against my people Israel
like a cloud that covers the land.
In the last days
I will bring you against my land,
so that the nations may know me,
when I show through you, Gog, that I am holy, in front of their eyes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gog plans to attack a peaceful people who live without walls, only to rob them. This is pure greed and violence against people who cannot defend themselves.
> Verse 12: The people live “in the center of the earth” (word for word: at the “navel of the earth”). Jerusalem was seen as the center of the world.
> Verse 13: The trading nations ask, mocking or curious: “Have you come to take plunder?” Maybe they want to make money from it themselves.
> Verse 16: “In the last days” – that is why many people have applied these chapters to the end of time.

## God steps in (Verses 17–23)

[17] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Are you the one I spoke about long ago
through my servants, the prophets of Israel?
In those days they prophesied for years
that I would bring you against them.
[18] On that day, when Gog comes against the land of Israel,”
says the Lord GOD,
“my anger will rise up in my nostrils.
[19] For I have spoken in my jealous love and in the fire of my anger.
On that day there will surely be a great earthquake in the land of Israel.
[20] The fish of the sea, the birds of the sky,
the animals of the field,
every creature that crawls on the ground,
and all the people on the face of the earth
will shake because I am there.
The mountains will be thrown down.
The steep cliffs will fall.
Every wall will fall to the ground.
[21] I will call for a sword against him on all my mountains,”
says the Lord GOD.
“Every man’s sword will be against his brother.
[22] I will judge him
with deadly disease and with bloodshed.
I will rain down on him,
on his troops, and on the many peoples who are with him,
pouring rain and huge hailstones,
fire and sulfur.
[23] I will show how great and how holy I am.
I will make myself known in the eyes of many nations.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> When Gog attacks, it is not Israel that fights, but God himself. He sends an earthquake, rain, hail, fire and sulfur. And the enemies kill one another (verse 21).
> The pictures are like the great flood, like Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:24), and like the plagues in Egypt. God is more powerful than all the armies of the world.
> How do we read these chapters?
> – In Judaism, the “war of Gog and Magog” is often connected with the time before the coming of the Messiah.
> – In Revelation, “Gog and Magog” appear as a picture for the last enemies of God (Revelation 20:8).
> – Again and again, people have tried to say that Gog is a certain country or ruler of their own time. These guesses have always turned out to be wrong, and sometimes they have stirred up fear and hatred. The Bible itself gives no date and names no country of today.
> – The real message is a comfort: No matter how powerful the enemies are, God protects his people, and in the end God wins.
