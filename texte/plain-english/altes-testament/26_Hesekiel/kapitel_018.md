# Ezekiel – Chapter 18
### Turn back, and you will live!

---

## Sour grapes (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[2] “What do you mean by using this saying about the land of Israel:
‘The fathers have eaten sour grapes,
and the children’s teeth are set on edge’?
[3] As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“you will not use this saying in Israel anymore.
[4] Every soul belongs to me.
The soul of the father belongs to me,
and the soul of the son belongs to me too.
The soul who sins is the one who will die.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people in exile had a bitter saying: “The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children’s teeth are set on edge.” That means: Our parents sinned, and we have to pay for it. We are innocent victims. We cannot change anything.
> God disagrees: Every person belongs to him directly. Each person is responsible before God himself. No one is simply a victim of his family’s history.
> Jeremiah quotes the same saying (Jeremiah 31:29–30).
> This is also a message that sets people free: You are not stuck because of what your parents did. You can go your own way.

---

## The righteous man (Verses 5–9)

[5] “Suppose a man is righteous
and does what is lawful and right.
[6] He does not eat at the shrines on the mountains.
He does not look to the idols of the people of Israel.
He does not dishonor his neighbor’s wife.
He does not come near a woman during her monthly period.
[7] He does not wrong anyone.
He gives back to the one who owes him what he took as a pledge.
He does not rob anyone.
He gives his bread to the hungry
and gives clothes to those who have none.
[8] He does not lend money to them for interest
or take profit from them.
He has held back his hand from wrongdoing.
He judges fairly between one person and another.
[9] He lives by my rules
and keeps my laws, and he is faithful.
He is righteous. He will certainly live,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is a kind of checklist of what a righteous person does. Interesting: Most of the points are not about worship, but about how we treat other people:
> – no idols,
> – faithfulness in marriage, respect for women,
> – not oppressing or robbing anyone,
> – giving back to debtors what they gave as a pledge (for example the coat they need at night, Exodus 22:26–27, in some Bibles 22:25–26),
> – giving bread to the hungry, clothing the naked,
> – not taking high interest from the poor,
> – judging fairly and justly.
> “Eating at the shrines on the mountains” means sacrificial meals at places where idols were worshiped.
> Jesus names similar deeds in Matthew 25:35–36: “For I was hungry, and you gave me food to eat … I was naked, and you gave me clothes.”

## The violent son (Verses 10–13)

[10] “But suppose he has a son who is a robber,
who spills blood,
and who does any of these things.
[11] He does none of the good things his father did.
Instead, he eats at the shrines on the mountains
and dishonors his neighbor’s wife.
[12] He wrongs the poor and needy.
He robs people.
He does not give back what he took as a pledge.
He looks to the idols.
He does disgusting things.
[13] He lends money for interest
and takes profit from the poor.
Will he live?
No, he will not live.
He has done all these disgusting things.
He will certainly die.
His blood will be on his own head.

> **What does this mean?**
> The son of a righteous father can still become evil. His father’s righteousness does not save him. You cannot hide behind your family’s good name.
> “His blood will be on his own head” means: He himself is responsible for what happens to him.

---

## The grandson chooses a different way (Verses 14–20)

[14] “Now suppose this man has a son
who sees all the sins his father has done.
He is afraid, and he does not do the same.
[15] He does not eat at the shrines on the mountains.
He does not look to the idols of the people of Israel.
He does not dishonor his neighbor’s wife.
[16] He does not wrong anyone.
He does not take anything as a pledge.
He does not rob anyone.
Instead, he gives his bread to the hungry
and gives clothes to those who have none.
[17] He has held back his hand from the poor.
He does not take interest or profit.
He obeys my laws
and lives by my rules.
He will not die because of his father’s sin.
He will certainly live.
[18] But his father was cruel and oppressed people.
He robbed his brother
and did what was not good among his people.
So he will die because of his own sin.
[19] “Yet you ask,
‘Why doesn’t the son carry the punishment for his father’s sin?’
If the son has done what is lawful and right,
and has kept all my rules and obeyed them,
he will certainly live.
[20] The soul who sins is the one who will die.
The son will not carry the punishment for his father’s sin,
and the father will not carry the punishment for his son’s sin.
A righteous person will receive the reward of his own righteousness,
and a wicked person will receive the punishment of his own wickedness.

> **What does this mean?**
> The grandson sees the evil his father does, and he does things differently. He breaks the cycle! God says: He will live. He does not have to pay for his father’s guilt.
> Verse 17: This is a person who “has held back his hand from the poor.” Here that means: He does not harm the poor or oppress them (compare verse 8: “He has held back his hand from wrongdoing”).
> This is a great hope for everyone who grew up in a difficult family: You do not have to repeat what was done to you or what you saw. With God’s help you can live differently.
> If you have experienced violence or addiction in your family and want to talk about it: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453. For addiction, US: SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357.

## God does not want death (Verses 21–24)

[21] “But suppose a wicked person turns away from all the sins he has done.
He keeps all my rules
and does what is lawful and right.
Then he will certainly live.
He will not die.
[22] None of the sins he has done
will be held against him anymore.
He will live because of the right things he has done.
[23] Do I take any pleasure in the death of the wicked?”
says the Lord GOD.
“Don’t I want him to turn from his way and live?
[24] “But suppose a righteous person turns away from his righteousness
and does wrong.
He does all the disgusting things that the wicked person does.
Will he live?
None of the right things he has done will be remembered.
He will die because of his unfaithfulness
and because of the sins he has committed.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is not only about families, but also about our own lives: No one is fixed forever, not by his past and not by good deeds done earlier.
> Verses 21–22: Whoever turns back is forgiven. God no longer holds the old sins against him. A real new beginning is possible!
> Verse 23: This is one of the most important sentences in the Bible: God takes no pleasure when anyone dies, not even an evil person. He wants people to turn back and live. (Compare 1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9.)
> Verse 24: On the other hand, you cannot rest on good deeds you did earlier. What counts is how you live now.

## Is God’s way unfair? (Verses 25–29)

[25] “Yet you say,
‘The way of the Lord is not fair.’
Listen now, people of Israel:
Is my way not fair?
Isn’t it your ways that are unfair?
[26] When a righteous person turns away from his righteousness
and does wrong and dies because of it,
he dies because of the wrong he has done.
[27] And when a wicked person turns away from the wickedness
he has done,
and does what is lawful and right,
he will save his life.
[28] Because he thinks it over
and turns away from all the sins he has committed,
he will certainly live.
He will not die.
[29] Yet the people of Israel say,
‘The way of the Lord is not fair.’
People of Israel, are my ways not fair?
Isn’t it your ways that are unfair?

> **What does this mean?**
> The people complain: “That’s not fair! If someone was evil all his life and then turns back, he gets to live?”
> God answers: It is not my way that is unfair, but your thinking. God gives everyone a chance. That is grace.
> Jesus tells a similar parable: The workers who come only late get the same pay as the others (Matthew 20:1–16). And the lost son is welcomed with a party, while the older brother complains (Luke 15:11–32).

---

## Make yourselves a new heart! (Verses 30–32)

[30] “That is why I will judge you, people of Israel,
each one by his own ways,”
says the Lord GOD.
“Turn back! Turn away from all your sins,
so that your guilt will not destroy you.
[31] Throw away all the sins
you have committed.
Make yourselves a new heart and a new spirit.
Why should you die, people of Israel?
[32] For I take no pleasure in the death of anyone who dies,”
says the Lord GOD.
“So turn back, and live!”

> **What does this mean?**
> The chapter ends with a passionate invitation. God asks: “Why should you die?” You can hear how much God wants his people to live.
> Verse 31: “Make yourselves a new heart.” In Chapter 11:19 and 36:26, God promises that he himself will give them a new heart. Both belong together: God gives the new beginning, and people should accept it and open themselves to it.
> Verse 32: “So turn back, and live!” That is the main message of this chapter and maybe of the whole book of Ezekiel. It is never too late to turn back.
