# Ezekiel – Chapter 41
### The temple building

---

## The holy place and the most holy place (Verses 1–4)

[1] He brought me into the main hall
and measured the posts.
They were about 10½ feet wide on one side
and about 10½ feet wide on the other side.
That was the width of the tent.
[2] The entrance was about 17½ feet wide.
The walls on the sides of the entrance were about 9 feet on one side
and about 9 feet on the other side.
He measured the length of the hall. It was about 70 feet.
It was about 35 feet wide.
[3] Then he went further in.
He measured each post of the entrance. It was about 3½ feet.
The entrance was about 10½ feet,
and the width of the entrance was about 12 feet.
[4] He measured the room beyond the main hall.
It was about 35 feet long
and about 35 feet wide.
He said to me,
“This is the most holy place.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple building has three parts, like Solomon’s temple (1 Kings 6): the porch, the main hall (“the holy place”), and at the very back the most holy place.
> The main hall is 40 cubits long and 20 cubits wide (about 70 by 35 feet).
> Verses 3–4: Only the heavenly man goes into the most holy place. Ezekiel stays outside, even though he is a priest. The most holy place is a perfect square of 20 by 20 cubits. It is the holiest place, where God himself is present.
> Verse 1: “The width of the tent” is a reminder of the holy tent in the wilderness, the model for all the later temples.

---

## The side rooms and the building in the west (Verses 5–15)

[5] Then he measured the wall of the temple. It was about 10½ feet thick.
Each side room was about 7 feet wide.
The side rooms went all around the temple on every side.
[6] The side rooms were on three floors, one above the other,
30 on each floor.
They rested on ledges in the wall that belonged to the temple
for the side rooms all around.
So they were supported
and did not have to go into the wall of the temple.
[7] The side rooms were wider on the higher floors,
because the walls were thinner on the higher floors.
So the building became wider toward the top.
People went up from the lowest floor
through the middle floor to the highest floor.
[8] I also saw that the temple had a raised platform all around.
The foundations of the side rooms were one full rod high,
six long cubits, about 10½ feet.
[9] The outer wall of the side rooms was about 9 feet thick.
The space that was left was the place of the side rooms
that belonged to the temple.
[10] Between the rooms there was an open space
about 35 feet wide all around the temple on every side.
[11] The doors of the side rooms opened toward an open area that was left free.
One door faced north,
and another door faced south.
The open area was about 9 feet wide all around.
[12] On the west side, facing the separate yard,
there was a building.
It was about 123 feet wide.
Its wall was about 9 feet thick all around,
and it was about 158 feet long.
[13] Then he measured the temple. It was about 175 feet long.
The separate yard and the building with its walls
were also about 175 feet long.
[14] The width of the front of the temple
and of the separate yard on the east side
was also about 175 feet.
[15] He measured the length of the building facing the separate yard
at the back of the temple,
with its galleries on each side.
It was about 175 feet.
He also measured the inner temple
and the porches of the courtyard,

> **What does this mean?**
> Around the temple building, on three floors, there are 30 side rooms on each floor, so 90 rooms. They were probably storerooms for temple objects and gifts. Solomon’s temple also had buildings like this along its sides (1 Kings 6:5–10).
> Verse 6: The beams of the rooms were not to go into the holy temple wall. The wall became thinner step by step toward the top, and the beams rested on the steps. So the temple wall stayed undamaged.
> Verse 12: Behind the temple, in the west, stands a large building. It does not say what it was used for.
> Verses 13–15: Everything is exactly 100 cubits long and wide (about 175 feet). The numbers show: The temple is perfectly balanced and harmonious.

## The decoration of the temple (Verses 16–26)

[16] the thresholds, the narrow windows,
and the galleries around them on their three floors.
Across from the threshold
the walls were covered with wood all around,
from the floor up to the windows.
The windows were covered.
[17] This went up to the space above the door,
to the inner room and outside,
and on the whole wall all around, inside and outside.
Everything was measured.
[18] The walls were decorated with cherubim and palm trees.
Cherubim are powerful heavenly beings.
A palm tree was between each cherub and the next.
Each cherub had two faces.
[19] The face of a man looked toward the palm tree on one side,
and the face of a young lion looked toward the palm tree on the other side.
It was made like this all around the whole temple.
[20] From the floor to above the door
there were cherubim and palm trees.
That is how the wall of the main hall looked.
[21] The doorposts of the main hall were square.
The front of the holy place
looked the same as the main hall.
[22] There was an altar made of wood.
It was about 5 feet high and about 3½ feet long.
Its corners, its base, and its sides were made of wood.
He said to me,
“This is the table that stands before the LORD.”
[23] The main hall and the holy place each had a double door.
[24] Each door had two parts,
two parts that could swing open:
two for one door
and two parts for the other.
[25] On the doors of the main hall
there were cherubim and palm trees,
like the ones on the walls.
There was a wooden threshold in front of the porch outside.
[26] There were narrow windows and palm trees
on each side,
on the side walls of the porch.
This is how the side rooms of the temple and the thresholds were arranged.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: These verses are very hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: The walls inside were covered with wood all around, from the floor to above the windows.
> Verses 18–20: The walls are decorated with cherubim and palm trees. Here the cherubim have two faces (a man and a lion). Palm trees and cherubim are a reminder of the garden of Eden, where cherubim guarded the way to the tree of life (Genesis 3:24). The temple is like a new paradise, a place where God and people meet.
> Verse 22: In the main hall stands a table made of wood, “the table that stands before the LORD.” This is probably the table for the bread of the presence (see Exodus 25:23–30). This was bread that lay before God as a sign of fellowship with him.
> Interesting: Many objects from the old temple, like the great lampstand or the ark of the covenant, are not mentioned here.
