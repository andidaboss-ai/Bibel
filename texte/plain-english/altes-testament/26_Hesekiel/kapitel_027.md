# Ezekiel – Chapter 27
### The song of mourning for the ship Tyre

---

## Tyre, the splendid ship (Verses 1–11)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[2] “You, son of man, sing a song of mourning about Tyre.
[3] Say to Tyre,
‘You live where the sea begins.
You trade with the peoples of many islands.
This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Tyre, you have said,
‘I am perfect in beauty.’
[4] Your borders are in the heart of the seas.
Your builders have made your beauty perfect.
[5] They made all your boards
from cypress trees from Senir.
They took a cedar from Lebanon
to make a mast for you.
[6] They made your oars
from the oaks of Bashan.
They made your benches from ivory
set into cypress wood from the islands of Kittim.
[7] Your sail was made of fine linen with embroidery from Egypt,
so that it would be your flag.
Your sunshade was made of blue and purple cloth
from the islands of Elishah.
[8] The people of Sidon and Arvad were your rowers.
Your wise men, Tyre, were on board.
They were your pilots who steered you.
[9] The old men of Gebal and its wise men were on board.
They fixed the leaks in your seams.
All the ships of the sea and their sailors were with you
to trade your goods.
[10] “‘“Persia, Lud, and Put were in your army.
They were your soldiers.
They hung up their shields and helmets in you.
They made you look splendid.
[11] The men of Arvad and your army
were on your walls all around.
Brave men were in your towers.
They hung their shields on your walls all around.
They made your beauty perfect.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel describes Tyre like a beautiful, proud trading ship. Everything about it is of the finest quality, brought together from all over the world:
> – wood from Senir (the Hermon mountains) and from Lebanon,
> – oaks from Bashan (east of the Sea of Galilee),
> – ivory and cypress wood from Kittim (Cyprus),
> – linen from Egypt,
> – expensive blue and purple cloth from Elishah (maybe Cyprus or Greece).
> By the way, purple was the most famous product of the Phoenicians. It was made from sea snails, and it was very expensive.
> Verses 8–11: The crew also comes from many cities: Sidon, Arvad, Gebal (Byblos). The soldiers even come from Persia, Lud (in Asia Minor or Africa) and Put (Libya).
> Verse 3: The problem is pride: “I am perfect in beauty.”

---

## The trading partners of Tyre (Verses 12–25)

[12] “‘“Tarshish traded with you
because you had so many kinds of riches.
They paid for your goods with silver, iron, tin, and lead.
[13] “‘“Javan, Tubal, and Meshech traded with you.
They traded people
and bronze pots for your goods.
[14] “‘“The people of the house of Togarmah traded for your goods
with horses, war horses, and mules.
[15] “‘“The men of Dedan traded with you.
Many islands were your markets.
They brought you ivory tusks and ebony wood in exchange.
[16] “‘“Syria traded with you
because you made so many things.
They paid for your goods with emeralds, purple cloth, embroidery, fine linen,
coral, and rubies.
[17] “‘“Judah and the land of Israel traded with you.
They paid for your goods with wheat from Minnith, sweets,
honey, oil, and balm.
Balm is a healing oil from plants.
[18] “‘“Damascus traded with you
because you made so many things
and had so many kinds of riches.
They brought wine from Helbon and white wool.
[19] “‘“Vedan and Javan traded yarn for your goods.
Worked iron, cassia, and calamus
were among your goods.
Cassia and calamus are spices with a sweet smell.
[20] “‘“Dedan traded with you
in expensive saddle blankets for riding.
[21] “‘“Arabia and all the princes of Kedar
were your favorite traders.
They traded lambs, rams, and goats.
With these they did business with you.
[22] “‘“The traders of Sheba and Raamah traded with you.
They paid for your goods with the best of all kinds of spices,
with all kinds of precious stones, and with gold.
[23] “‘“Haran, Canneh, and Eden,
the traders of Sheba, Asshur, and Chilmad
traded with you.
[24] They traded with you in fine goods,
in cloaks of blue cloth and embroidery,
and in chests of cedar wood full of rich clothing,
tied with cords.
These were among your goods.
[25] “‘“The ships of Tarshish carried your goods for you.
You were filled with riches
and became very glorious in the heart of the seas.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here there is a long list of the trading partners of Tyre, almost like a map of the world at that time. It reaches from Tarshish (probably in Spain) in the west to Arabia and Mesopotamia in the east.
> Javan is Greece (Ionia). Tubal and Meshech were in Asia Minor (today Turkey). Togarmah was probably in Armenia. Sheba was in the south of Arabia (today Yemen).
> Verse 13: “They traded people and bronze pots” – the slave trade was also part of the wealth of Tyre (see Joel 3:6; Amos 1:9). Here the Bible states it plainly. In other places it clearly condemns kidnapping and human trafficking (Exodus 21:16; 1 Timothy 1:10). Human trafficking is a serious wrong, then as now.
> Verse 17: Judah and Israel also sold goods to Tyre, mostly farm products: wheat, honey, oil, and balm.

## The ship sinks (Verses 26–36)

[26] “‘“Your rowers have taken you out into the great waters.
The east wind has broken you to pieces in the heart of the seas.
[27] Your riches, your goods, your merchandise,
your sailors, your pilots,
the men who fixed the leaks in your seams,
the traders of your goods,
and all your soldiers on board,
with all the people on board,
will sink into the heart of the seas
on the day of your ruin.
[28] At the sound of your pilots crying out,
the land along the coast will shake.
[29] All who row with oars,
the sailors and all the pilots of the sea,
will come down from their ships.
They will stand on the land.
[30] They will raise their voices over you
and cry bitterly.
They will throw dust on their heads.
They will roll in the ashes.
[31] They will shave their heads bald because of you
and put on sackcloth.
Sackcloth is rough cloth that people wore when they mourned.
They will weep for you with bitter hearts,
with bitter mourning.
[32] As they wail, they will sing a song of mourning for you.
They will mourn over you and say,
‘Who was ever like Tyre,
like her who has been made silent in the middle of the sea?’
[33] When your goods came over the seas,
you met the needs of many peoples.
With your great riches and your goods
you made the kings of the earth rich.
[34] Now you are broken by the seas,
in the depths of the waters.
Your goods and all the people on board
have sunk with you.
[35] All the people who live on the islands are shocked at you.
Their kings shake with terror.
Their faces are full of fear.
[36] The traders among the peoples hiss at you in scorn.
You have come to a terrible end,
and you will be no more.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The splendid ship sails out onto the open sea, and then a storm comes from the east and breaks it to pieces. Everything sinks: riches, goods, crew. The east wind stands for Babylon.
> Verses 29–32: All the sailors stand on the shore and mourn. They throw dust on their heads and put on sackcloth, as at a funeral.
> The message: Even the most beautiful, richest, and most powerful “ship” can sink. Wealth and trade are not bad. But whoever puts all their trust in them and becomes proud is building on a ship that can sink.
> In Revelation 18 there is a very similar song of mourning about the fall of “Babylon”: Merchants and sailors stand far away and weep over the rich city (Revelation 18:9–19). That text clearly takes up Ezekiel 27.
