# Ezekiel – Chapter 31
### The cedar that was cut down

---

## The splendid cedar (Verses 1–9)

[1] In the eleventh year, in the third month, on the first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, say to Pharaoh, king of Egypt,
and to all his people,
‘Who are you like in your greatness?
[3] Look, the Assyrian was a cedar in Lebanon.
It had beautiful branches
and gave shade like a forest.
It was very tall,
and its top reached up among the thick branches.
[4] The waters made it grow.
The deep waters under the earth made it grow tall.
Its rivers flowed all around the place where it was planted.
It sent out its streams to all the trees of the field.
[5] So it grew taller than all the trees of the field.
It had very many branches.
Its branches grew long because of all the water,
when it spread them out.
[6] All the birds of the sky built their nests in its branches.
Under its branches all the animals of the field had their young.
All the great nations lived in its shade.
[7] So it was beautiful in its greatness
and in the length of its branches,
for its roots reached down to plenty of water.
[8] The cedars in the garden of God could not hide it.
The cypress trees did not have branches like it.
The pine trees did not have branches like it.
No tree in the garden of God was as beautiful as it was.
[9] I made it beautiful with its many branches,
so that all the trees of Eden in the garden of God
were jealous of it.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel asks Pharaoh a question: “Who are you like?” Then he tells about a huge tree, a cedar that is taller than all the others. All the animals and nations find protection in its shade.
> Verse 3: The WEB says: “The Assyrian was a cedar.” This means the Assyrian Empire, which was once the greatest power in the world and then fell (its capital city Nineveh fell in 612 BC). Some Bible teachers read the Hebrew word differently, as the name of a tree (“a cypress”). Then the text is directly about Egypt as a tree.
> Verses 8–9: The tree is even more beautiful than the trees in the garden of Eden. A picture for the splendor of a world empire.
> The picture of the great tree where birds build their nests also appears in Daniel 4 and in Jesus’ parable of the mustard seed (Mark 4:32).

---

## The fall of the cedar (Verses 10–14)

[10] “So this is what the Lord GOD said:
‘He grew very tall.
He stretched his top up among the thick branches,
and his heart became proud because he was so tall.
[11] So I will hand him over to the mightiest ruler of the nations.
He will surely deal with him.
I have driven him out because of his wickedness.
[12] Foreigners, the cruelest of the nations,
have cut him down and left him lying there.
His branches have fallen on the mountains and into all the valleys.
His boughs lie broken by all the streams of the land.
All the peoples of the earth have come out from his shade
and left him.
[13] All the birds of the sky will live on his fallen trunk.
All the animals of the field will be on his branches.
[14] This happened so that none of the trees by the waters
will become proud because they are tall.
None of them will stretch its top up among the thick branches.
None of their mighty ones will stand proudly in their height,
none of all the trees that drink water.
For all of them are handed over to death,
to the lowest parts of the earth,
together with human beings,
with those who go down to the pit.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The mistake of the tree is that “his heart became proud because he was so tall.” That means: He became proud. Again pride is the problem.
> Verse 11: The “mightiest ruler of the nations” means Babylon.
> Verse 14: A lesson for all powerful people: No one should lift himself up too high. All human beings must die, even the greatest kings and empires.

## Down into the world of the dead (Verses 15–18)

[15] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘On the day he went down to Sheol,
I made everyone mourn.
Sheol is the world of the dead.
I covered the deep waters because of him.
I held back their rivers.
The great waters were stopped.
I made Lebanon mourn for him,
and all the trees of the field became faint because of him.
[16] I made the nations shake at the sound of his fall,
when I threw him down to Sheol
with those who go down into the pit.
All the trees of Eden,
the finest and best trees of Lebanon,
all the trees that drink water,
were comforted in the lowest parts of the earth.
[17] They also went down into Sheol with him,
to those who were killed by the sword.
These were the ones who had been his strong arm,
who lived in his shade among the nations.
[18] “‘Which of the trees of Eden
is like you in glory and greatness?
Yet you will be brought down with the trees of Eden
to the lowest parts of the earth.
You will lie among the uncircumcised,
with those who were killed by the sword.
“‘This is Pharaoh and all his people,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Sheol” in the Old Testament is the place of the dead, a dark world under the earth.
> Verse 16: The other “trees,” that is, kings and empires that have already died, are “comforted” when the great tree comes down to them. They see: Even the greatest one ends like us.
> Verse 18: At the end comes the point: “This is Pharaoh!” It is the answer to the question from verse 2. As Assyria fell, so Egypt will fall too.
