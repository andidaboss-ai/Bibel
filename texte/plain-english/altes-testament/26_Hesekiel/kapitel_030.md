# Ezekiel – Chapter 30
### The day of the LORD over Egypt

---

## How terrible that day will be! (Verses 1–9)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me again. He said,
[2] “Son of man, prophesy and say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Wail and cry, ‘How terrible that day will be!’
[3] For the day is near.
Yes, the day of the LORD is near.
It will be a day of clouds,
a time of judgment for the nations.
[4] A sword will come against Egypt.
There will be great fear in Ethiopia
when the dead fall in Egypt.
Her people will be taken away,
and her foundations will be torn down.
[5] “‘“Ethiopia, Put, Lud, all the mixed peoples, Cub,
and the people of the land that is allied with them
will die by the sword together with Egypt.”
[6] “‘This is what the LORD says:
“Those who support Egypt will also fall.
The pride of her power will come down.
From the tower of Seveneh on,
they will die there by the sword,”
says the Lord GOD.
[7] “They will be ruined among the countries that are ruined.
Her cities will be among the cities that are destroyed.
[8] They will know that I am the LORD
when I set fire to Egypt,
and all her helpers are crushed.
[9] “‘“On that day messengers will go out from me in ships
to frighten the Ethiopians, who feel so safe.
Great fear will come over them, as on the day of Egypt.
For look, it is coming.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The day of the LORD” is a big theme in the prophets: a day when God steps in and judges (see Joel 2:1–2; Zephaniah 1:14–16). This time it strikes Egypt.
> Verses 4–5: “Ethiopia” here is the land south of Egypt (today Sudan; in Hebrew: Cush). Put is probably Libya. Lud is a people in Africa or in Asia Minor. These peoples were allies of Egypt or soldiers who fought for pay. We do not know for sure what “Cub” was. Some old manuscripts read “Libya” here.
> Important: These texts speak about the states and armies of that time, not about people with a certain skin color or background. They must not be used for prejudice against people from Africa.

---

## Through Nebuchadnezzar (Verses 10–12)

[10] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will also put an end to the crowds of Egypt
through Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon.
[11] He and his people with him,
the most terrifying of the nations,
will be brought in to destroy the land.
They will pull out their swords against Egypt
and fill the land with the dead.
[12] I will make the rivers dry.
I will hand the land over to evil men.
Through foreigners I will ruin the land
and everything in it.
I, the LORD, have spoken.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Egypt lived from the Nile. If the Nile dries up, the land is lost. The picture shows: God is also Lord over the Nile, which the Egyptians worshiped.

---

## The cities of Egypt (Verses 13–19)

[13] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will also destroy the idols.
I will put an end to the images in Memphis.
There will no longer be a prince from the land of Egypt.
I will fill the land of Egypt with fear.
[14] I will ruin Pathros.
I will set fire to Zoan.
I will carry out judgment on No.
[15] I will pour out my anger on Sin,
the fortress of Egypt.
I will cut off the crowds of No.
[16] I will set fire to Egypt.
Sin will twist in great pain.
No will be broken open.
Memphis will have enemies in broad daylight.
[17] The young men of Aven and of Pibeseth
will die by the sword.
The people will be taken away as prisoners.
[18] At Tehaphnehes the day will turn dark
when I break the yokes of Egypt there.
The pride of her power will come to an end in her.
A cloud will cover her,
and her daughters will be taken away as prisoners.
[19] This is how I will carry out judgment on Egypt.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the most important cities of Egypt are listed:
> – Memphis: the old capital, near today’s Cairo.
> – Pathros: Upper Egypt (the south).
> – Zoan: Tanis, in the Nile Delta.
> – No: Thebes, the great temple city in the south (today Luxor).
> – Sin: Pelusium, a border fortress in the northeast.
> – Aven: On, also called Heliopolis, the city of the sun god.
> – Pibeseth: Bubastis, in the Delta.
> – Tehaphnehes (also called Tahpanhes): a border town in the east. Jeremiah fled there with other people from Judah (Jeremiah 43:7–9).
> Verse 13: The idols of Egypt will be destroyed. The many gods of Egypt cannot save the land.

## Pharaoh’s broken arm (Verses 20–26)

[20] In the eleventh year, in the first month, on the seventh day of the month,
the word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[21] “Son of man, I have broken the arm of Pharaoh,
king of Egypt.
Look, no one has tied it up.
No one has put medicine on it
or wrapped a bandage around it,
so that it could become strong enough to hold a sword.
[22] So this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I am against Pharaoh, king of Egypt.
I will break his arms,
both the strong arm and the one that is already broken.
I will make the sword fall out of his hand.
[23] I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations
and spread them out through the countries.
[24] I will make the arms of the king of Babylon strong
and put my sword in his hand.
But I will break the arms of Pharaoh.
He will groan in front of the king of Babylon,
like a man who is wounded to death.
[25] I will hold up the arms of the king of Babylon,
but the arms of Pharaoh will drop down.
Then they will know that I am the LORD,
when I put my sword into the hand of the king of Babylon,
and he stretches it out against the land of Egypt.
[26] I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations
and spread them out through the countries.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: This is about April 587 BC. A short time before this, an Egyptian army had tried to help Jerusalem, but the Babylonians drove it back (see Jeremiah 37:5–11). That is Pharaoh’s “broken arm.”
> In the Bible, the “arm” stands for strength and power. Egypt is made weak, Babylon is made strong.
> Here it says that God puts his sword into the hand of the king of Babylon. That does not mean that Babylon was good or just. Later Babylon itself is judged (Isaiah 47; Jeremiah 50–51). God can even use foreign rulers in his history, but they are still responsible for the wrong they do.
