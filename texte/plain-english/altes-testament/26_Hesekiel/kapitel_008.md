# Ezekiel – Chapter 8
### Disgusting things in the temple

---

## Ezekiel is brought to Jerusalem (Verses 1–6)

[1] It was in the sixth year, in the sixth month, on the fifth day of the month.
I was sitting in my house,
and the elders of Judah were sitting in front of me.
Then the hand of the Lord GOD came on me there.
[2] I looked, and I saw a shape that looked like fire.
From what looked like his waist downward, there was fire.
From his waist upward, there was something like a bright light,
like glowing metal.
[3] He reached out something that looked like a hand,
and he took hold of me by a lock of my hair.
The Spirit lifted me up between the earth and the sky.
In visions from God, he brought me to Jerusalem,
to the entrance of the inner court gate that faces north.
That is where the idol of jealousy stood,
the idol that makes God jealous.
[4] And there was the glory of the God of Israel,
just as I had seen it in the plain.
[5] Then he said to me,
“Son of man, now look toward the north.”
So I looked toward the north.
North of the altar gate, in the entrance,
I saw this idol of jealousy.
[6] He said to me,
“Son of man, do you see what they are doing?
Do you see the terrible, disgusting things that the people of Israel are doing here?
These things are driving me far away from my holy place.
But you will see even more terrible, disgusting things.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This is in the year 592 BC, a little more than a year after Ezekiel was called. The elders of the exiles are sitting with him, maybe to ask for advice.
> Verse 3: In a vision, Ezekiel is brought to Jerusalem. His body stays in Babylon, but in the Spirit he sees what is happening in the temple.
> The “idol of jealousy” is an idol right at the temple. It makes God jealous. God does not want his people to serve other gods, just as a loving partner does not want unfaithfulness (compare Exodus 20:5).
> Verse 6: Because of these disgusting things, God has to go far away from his holy place. That is the great message of Chapters 8–11: God’s glory leaves the temple.

## Secret idols in the dark (Verses 7–13)

[7] He brought me to the entrance of the court.
When I looked, I saw a hole in the wall.
[8] Then he said to me,
“Son of man, now dig into the wall.”
When I had dug into the wall,
I saw a door.
[9] He said to me,
“Go in, and see the evil, disgusting things they are doing here.”
[10] So I went in and looked.
All around on the wall
there were pictures of every kind of crawling thing
and disgusting animal,
and of all the idols of the people of Israel.
[11] 70 of the elders of the people of Israel
were standing in front of them.
Jaazaniah the son of Shaphan was standing in the middle of them.
Each man had his incense burner in his hand,
and a sweet-smelling cloud of incense was rising up.
[12] Then he said to me,
“Son of man, have you seen what the elders of the people of Israel are doing in the dark,
each man in his own room full of idol pictures?
For they say,
‘The LORD does not see us.
The LORD has left the land.’”
[13] He also said to me,
“You will see even more terrible, disgusting things that they are doing.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Behind a wall, in secret, 70 elders worship pictures of animals and idols. These men were supposed to lead the people.
> Verse 11: Jaazaniah is a son of Shaphan. It is not certain whether this Shaphan is the same as the scribe under King Josiah, whose family helped Jeremiah. If so, that would be especially sad: a son from a faithful family among the idol worshipers.
> Verse 12: “The LORD does not see us.” That is the excuse of many people who do wrong in secret. But the vision shows: God sees everything, even what happens in the dark (compare Psalm 139:11–12).

## Women weep for Tammuz (Verses 14–15)

[14] Then he brought me to the entrance of the gate of the LORD’s house
that faces north.
I saw women sitting there,
weeping for Tammuz.
[15] Then he said to me,
“Have you seen this, son of man?
You will see even more terrible, disgusting things than these.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Tammuz was a god from Mesopotamia. According to the myth, he died and went down into the world of the dead. Every year there were rituals in which people wept for him. He was connected with the growing and dying of plants.
> Right at the temple of the LORD, people are weeping for a foreign god. What is criticized here is the foreign worship, not the women as women. In Chapter 8, men are criticized in the same way (the 70 elders and the 25 men).
> By the way: One month in the Jewish calendar is still called “Tammuz” today.

## Worshiping the sun (Verses 16–18)

[16] He brought me into the inner court of the LORD’s house.
At the entrance of the LORD’s temple,
between the porch and the altar,
there were about 25 men.
Their backs were toward the LORD’s temple,
and their faces were toward the east.
They were bowing down to the sun in the east.
[17] Then he said to me,
“Have you seen this, son of man?
Is it a small thing for the people of Judah
to do the disgusting things they are doing here?
They have also filled the land with violence,
and they make me angry again and again.
Look, they are holding the branch to their nose.
[18] That is why I will act in anger too.
My eye will not spare them,
and I will have no pity.
Even if they shout into my ears with a loud voice,
I will not listen to them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: The worst disgusting thing comes at the end: In the innermost court, right in front of the temple, 25 men turn their backs to God and worship the sun. That is a strong picture: They literally turn their backs on God.
> Verse 17: Idol worship and violence go together: “They have also filled the land with violence.” People who leave God often also lose respect for other people.
> “Holding the branch to their nose” was probably a ritual of the foreign religion. What exactly it means is no longer known for certain today.
> Verse 18: God will no longer listen. That is frightening. It shows: There is a point where it is too late. But the book of Ezekiel does not end with judgment. It ends with a new beginning (Chapters 36–48).
