# Ezekiel – Chapter 7
### The end is coming

---

## An end! The end has come! (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[2] “You, son of man, this is what the Lord GOD says to the land of Israel:
‘An end!
The end has come on the four corners of the land.
[3] Now the end has come on you.
I will send my anger against you,
and I will judge you for the way you have lived.
I will make you pay for all your disgusting deeds.
[4] My eye will not spare you,
and I will have no pity.
I will make you pay for the way you have lived,
and your disgusting deeds will be right among you.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.’
[5] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘A disaster! A disaster like no other!
Look, it is coming.
[6] An end has come.
The end has come!
It has woken up against you.
Look, it is coming.
[7] Your doom has come on you,
you who live in the land!
The time has come!
The day is near.
It is a day of panic on the mountains,
not a day of happy shouting.
[8] Soon I will pour out my anger on you.
I will finish my anger against you,
and I will judge you for the way you have lived.
I will make you pay for all your disgusting deeds.
[9] My eye will not spare,
and I will have no pity.
I will punish you for the way you have lived.
Your disgusting deeds will be right among you.
Then you will know that I, the LORD, am the one who strikes you.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter sounds like alarm bells: “An end! The end has come!” The words are repeated again and again, short and hard. The people are meant to wake up.
> The “four corners of the land” means: the whole land, from one end to the other.
> Verses 3–4: “I will send my anger against you, and I will judge you for the way you have lived.” God brings the evil that the people have done back on themselves. Wrongdoing has consequences.
> Compare also Amos 8:2: “The end has come for my people Israel.”

---

## The day is here (Verses 10–13)

[10] “‘Look, the day!
Look, it is coming!
Your doom has gone out.
The rod has blossomed.
Pride has budded.
[11] Violence has grown into a rod of wickedness.
None of them will be left,
none of their crowds,
none of their riches.
Nothing of value will be left among them.
[12] The time has come!
The day is coming near.
Do not let the buyer be glad,
and do not let the seller be sad,
for my anger is on all the crowds of the land.
[13] For the seller will not get back what he sold,
even if they are both still alive.
For the vision is about all the crowds of the land.
None of them will come back.
No one will make himself strong
while living in his sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: The “rod” has blossomed. The picture means: Evil has grown like a plant and has put out blossoms: pride and violence. Now it becomes a stick that strikes.
> Verses 12–13: Usually a buyer is glad about a good purchase, and a seller is sad when he has to sell his land. According to the law, land that had been sold could come back to its owner in the Year of Jubilee (Leviticus 25). But now that does not matter: Everyone will lose the land. Business and property lose their value when the disaster comes.

---

## No one goes to battle (Verses 14–22)

[14] “‘They have blown the trumpet
and made everything ready.
But no one goes to battle,
for my anger is on all the crowds of the land.
[15] “‘The sword is outside,
and disease and hunger are inside.
Whoever is in the field will die by the sword.
Whoever is in the city will be eaten up by hunger and disease.
[16] Those who escape will get away.
They will be on the mountains like doves of the valleys.
All of them will moan,
each one because of his sin.
[17] All hands will be weak,
and all knees will be as weak as water.
[18] They will put on sackcloth,
and horror will cover them.
Shame will be on every face,
and every head will be shaved bald.
[19] They will throw their silver into the streets,
and their gold will be like something unclean.
Their silver and their gold will not be able to save them
on the day of the LORD’s anger.
These things will not satisfy their hunger
or fill their stomachs.
For their silver and gold made them stumble into sin.
[20] He had made his beautiful jewel glorious.
But they used it to make the images of their disgusting
and hateful idols.
That is why I have made it unclean for them.
[21] I will hand it over to strangers as loot,
and to the wicked people of the earth as plunder.
They will make it unholy.
[22] I will also turn my face away from them,
and they will make my hidden place unholy.
Robbers will go into it and make it unholy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: The trumpet calls people to battle, but no one goes. The people have no courage left.
> Verse 16: The refugees moan on the mountains like doves. To people in those days, the cooing of doves sounded like lamenting.
> Verse 19: The people throw their money away. Silver and gold cannot save them, and they cannot be eaten. Riches were more important to them than God, and now they are worth nothing. (Compare Zephaniah 1:18.)
> Verses 20–22: The “beautiful jewel” is probably the temple, or the gold and silver from which idols were made. God’s “hidden place” is probably the Most Holy Place in the temple. Foreigners will go into it and make it unholy. That happened in 586 before Christ, when the Babylonians destroyed the temple.

## They will look for peace (Verses 23–27)

[23] “‘Make chains,
for the land is full of crimes of bloodshed,
and the city is full of violence.
[24] That is why I will bring the worst of the nations,
and they will take over their houses.
I will also put an end to the pride of the strong.
Their holy places will be made unholy.
[25] Destruction is coming!
They will look for peace,
but there will be none.
[26] Disaster will come on disaster,
and one rumor will follow another.
They will look for a vision from the prophet.
But the priest will have no more teaching from the law,
and the elders will have no more advice.
[27] The king will mourn,
and the prince will be clothed in despair.
The hands of the people of the land will shake.
I will deal with them the way they have lived.
I will judge them by their own standards.
Then they will know that I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: “Make chains”: The people will be taken away into captivity in chains. The reason is clearly named: violence and bloodshed in the land.
> Verse 26: In the crisis, people look for advice from prophets, priests, and elders. But no one knows what to do anymore. All the leaders are at a loss.
> Verse 27: “I will judge them by their own standards.” God measures them with the measure they used for others. Jesus says something similar: “With the measure you use to measure, it will be measured to you” (Matthew 7:2).
