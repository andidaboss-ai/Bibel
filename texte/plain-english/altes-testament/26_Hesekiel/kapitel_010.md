# Ezekiel – Chapter 10
### The glory leaves the temple

---

## Fire between the cherubim (Verses 1–8)

[1] Then I looked.
In the expanse over the heads of the cherubim,
I saw something above them like a sapphire stone.
It looked like the shape of a throne.
[2] He spoke to the man dressed in linen and said,
“Go in between the whirling wheels, under the cherub.
Fill both your hands with burning coals
from between the cherubim,
and scatter them over the city.”
And he went in while I watched.
[3] The cherubim were standing on the right side of the temple
when the man went in.
And the cloud filled the inner court.
[4] The LORD’s glory rose up from the cherub
and stood over the doorway of the temple.
The temple was filled with the cloud,
and the court was full of the bright light of the LORD’s glory.
[5] The sound of the cherubim’s wings could be heard
as far as the outer court.
It sounded like the voice of God Almighty when he speaks.
[6] He gave the man dressed in linen this command:
“Take fire from between the whirling wheels,
from between the cherubim.”
Then the man went in and stood beside a wheel.
[7] One cherub reached out his hand from between the cherubim
to the fire that was between the cherubim.
He took some of it
and put it into the hands of the man dressed in linen.
The man took it and went out.
[8] Under the wings of the cherubim
I could see something that looked like a man’s hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel sees the throne chariot from Chapter 1 again. Now he calls the living creatures “cherubim” (one of them is called a cherub). Cherubim are heavenly beings who surround God’s throne. In the temple there were figures of cherubim above the ark of the covenant (Exodus 25:18–22).
> Verse 2: Before this, the man in linen had marked the righteous (Chapter 9). Now he is to scatter burning coals over the city. This is a picture of the fire that will destroy Jerusalem.
> In Isaiah 6:6–7, a burning coal from the altar touches Isaiah’s lips and makes him clean. Here the fire is a fire of judgment.

---

## The wheels and the cherubim (Verses 9–17)

[9] I looked, and I saw four wheels beside the cherubim,
one wheel beside each cherub.
The wheels looked like beryl stone.
[10] As for the way they looked,
all four of them looked the same,
like a wheel inside a wheel.
[11] When they moved, they went in any of their four directions.
They did not turn as they moved.
Wherever the head faced, they followed it.
They did not turn as they moved.
[12] Their whole bodies, their backs, their hands, their wings,
and the wheels were full of eyes all around.
That was true of the wheels of all four of them.
[13] I heard the wheels being called
“the whirling wheels.”
[14] Each one had four faces.
The first face was the face of the cherub.
The second face was the face of a man.
The third face was the face of a lion.
The fourth was the face of an eagle.
[15] The cherubim rose up.
These were the living creatures I had seen by the river Chebar.
[16] When the cherubim moved, the wheels moved beside them.
When the cherubim lifted their wings to rise up from the ground,
the wheels did not leave their side.
[17] When the cherubim stood still, the wheels stood still.
When the cherubim rose up, the wheels rose up with them,
for the spirit of the living creature was in them.

> **What does this mean?**
> This description repeats much of Chapter 1. Ezekiel wants to make it clear: It is the same God, the same glory, that he saw by the river Chebar.
> Verse 14: Here there is a difference from Ezekiel 1:10: Instead of the face of an ox, verse 14 says: the face of the cherub. Why is not completely clear. Maybe the face of an ox was the typical face of a cherub.
> Verse 13: “Whirling wheels”: The Hebrew word “galgal” means something that turns, like a whirlwind.

---

## The glory goes to the east gate (Verses 18–22)

[18] The LORD’s glory left the doorway of the temple
and stood over the cherubim.
[19] As I watched, the cherubim lifted their wings
and rose up from the ground as they went out.
The wheels were beside them.
They stopped at the entrance of the east gate of the LORD’s house,
and the glory of the God of Israel was high above them.
[20] These were the living creatures
that I had seen under the God of Israel by the river Chebar.
And I knew that they were cherubim.
[21] Each one had four faces,
and each one had four wings.
Under their wings was something that looked like a man’s hands.
[22] Their faces looked
just like the faces I had seen by the river Chebar.
They looked the same, and they were the same.
Each one went straight forward.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now something unthinkable happens: God’s glory leaves the temple step by step. First it went to the doorway (9:3; 10:4). Now it gets onto the throne chariot and goes to the east gate (verse 19).
> For the people in Jerusalem, it was clear: God lives in the temple. So nothing can happen to them (compare Jeremiah 7:4). Ezekiel shows: God is not tied to the building. If the people leave him, he can also leave the temple.
> But God goes slowly, step by step. It is as if he is hesitating, as if he is waiting to see whether his people will turn back after all.
> In Chapter 43, the glory will come back through the east gate!
