# Ezekiel – Chapter 13
### False prophets

---

## How terrible for the foolish prophets (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, prophesy against the prophets of Israel who are prophesying.
Say to those who prophesy out of their own hearts,
‘Listen to the LORD’s word!
[3] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“How terrible it will be for the foolish prophets
who follow their own spirit
and have seen nothing!
[4] Israel, your prophets have been like foxes among the ruins.
[5] You have not gone up into the gaps in the wall.
You have not built up the wall for the people of Israel,
so that they could stand firm in the battle on the day of the LORD.
[6] They have seen false things and told lying fortunes.
They say, ‘The LORD says,’
but the LORD has not sent them.
And they have made people hope
that their words would come true.
[7] Have you not seen a false vision?
Have you not told a lying fortune
when you say, ‘The LORD says,’
even though I have not spoken?”
[8] “‘That is why the Lord GOD says:
“You have spoken false things and seen lies.
That is why I am against you,”
says the Lord GOD.
[9] “My hand will be against the prophets
who see false visions and tell lying fortunes.
They will not be in the council of my people.
They will not be written in the list of the people of Israel.
They will not come into the land of Israel.
Then you will know that I am the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time there were many prophets. Some said only what people wanted to hear. They spoke “out of their own hearts,” that is, their own wishes and ideas. And then they claimed: “The LORD says.”
> Verse 4: “Foxes among the ruins”: Foxes look for a comfortable spot in ruins, but they do not build anything up. That is what the false prophets are like: They use the crisis for themselves.
> Verse 5: A true prophet would have gone “into the gaps,” that is, into the holes in a broken wall, to protect the people. He would have called the people to turn back.
> Verse 9: The false prophets will not be in the “list” of the people (like a list of citizens). They no longer belong.
> Today, too: Not everyone who speaks “in God’s name” tells the truth. It is good to test things carefully (compare 1 John 4:1).

## The whitewashed wall (Verses 10–16)

[10] “‘They have led my people astray.
They say, “Peace,”
when there is no peace.
When someone builds a wall,
they cover it with whitewash.
[11] Tell those who cover it with whitewash
that it will fall.
A pouring rain will come.
You, great hailstones, will fall.
A storm wind will tear it apart.
[12] When the wall has fallen,
people will ask you,
“Where is the whitewash you put on it?”
[13] “‘That is why the Lord GOD says:
“In my anger I will tear it apart with a storm wind.
In my anger a pouring rain will come,
and great hailstones in my fury will destroy it.
[14] I will break down the wall you covered with whitewash.
I will knock it down to the ground,
so that its foundation is laid bare.
It will fall,
and you will be destroyed under it.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[15] That is how I will pour out all my anger on the wall
and on those who covered it with whitewash.
And I will tell you,
‘The wall is gone,
and so are those who covered it with whitewash.
[16] They are the prophets of Israel who prophesy about Jerusalem
and see visions of peace for her,
when there is no peace,’”
says the Lord GOD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “Peace,” the false prophets say, “everything will be fine.” But there is no peace. (Jeremiah says the same thing: Jeremiah 6:14; 8:11.)
> Whitewash is a thin white paint. The picture: The people build a shaky wall, that is, an uncertain hope. The false prophets paint it nicely white, so that it looks strong. But when the storm comes, it falls down anyway. Nice paint does not make a bad wall stronger.
> Today we would say: “Whitewashing” problems or talking them up does not solve them. Honesty is better than false comfort.
> Compare also Jesus: the house on the sand and the house on the rock (Matthew 7:24–27).

## Against the false prophetesses (Verses 17–23)

[17] “And you, son of man, turn your face against the women of your people
who prophesy out of their own hearts.
Prophesy against them.
[18] Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“How terrible it will be for the women who sew magic bands on everyone’s elbows
and make veils for the heads of people of every size,
to hunt people’s souls!
Will you hunt the souls of my people
and keep souls alive for your own gain?
[19] You have dishonored me among my people
for a few handfuls of barley and a few pieces of bread.
You kill people who should not die,
and you keep alive people who should not live,
by lying to my people, who listen to lies.”
[20] “‘That is why the Lord GOD says:
“I am against your magic bands
that you use to hunt souls as if they were birds.
I will tear them off your arms.
I will set free the souls
that you trap like birds.
[21] I will also tear off your veils
and save my people from your hands.
They will no longer be in your power to be trapped.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[22] With your lies, you have made the hearts of righteous people sad,
when I have not made them sad.
You have encouraged wicked people,
so that they do not turn from their wicked ways
and save their lives.
[23] That is why you will no longer see false visions
or tell fortunes.
I will save my people from your hands.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Besides false prophets, there were also women who worked with magic and fortune-telling. With magic bands and veils, they made people afraid or gave them false hope, and they let people pay them for it. What exactly the bands and veils were is no longer known for certain today.
> Important: They are criticized not because they are women, but because they lie and manipulate people. The Bible knows many true prophetesses, for example Miriam (Exodus 15:20), Deborah (Judges 4:4), and Huldah (2 Kings 22:14). The male false prophets are criticized just as harshly (verses 1–16).
> Verse 19: “For a few handfuls of barley”: They sell God’s name for a small profit.
> Verse 22: The worst thing is: They make good people sad and encourage evil people to keep going. That is the exact opposite of what God wants.
> Verses 20–21: God himself frees people from these traps. Today, too, there are people who manipulate others with fear, “curses,” or false promises and make money from it. God wants people to be free.
