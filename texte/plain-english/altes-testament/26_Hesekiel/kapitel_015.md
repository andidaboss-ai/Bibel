# Ezekiel – Chapter 15
### The wood of the vine

---

## What is the wood of the vine good for? (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, how is the wood of the vine better than any other tree,
the branch of the vine among the trees of the forest?
[3] Do people take its wood to make anything?
Do they make a peg from it to hang a pot on?
[4] No, it is thrown into the fire as fuel.
The fire has burned up both of its ends,
and the middle is burned black.
Is it good for anything now?
[5] When it was whole,
it was not good for anything.
How much less is it good for anything
after the fire has burned it
and it is burned black!”
[6] That is why the Lord GOD says:
“The wood of the vine among the trees of the forest
is something I have given to the fire as fuel.
In the same way I will give up the people who live in Jerusalem.
[7] I will turn my face against them.
They will come out of one fire,
but the fire will still burn them up.
Then you will know that I am the LORD,
when I turn my face against them.
[8] I will make the land an empty ruin,
because they have been unfaithful,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, Israel was often compared to a vine (Psalm 80:8–16, in some Bibles 80:9–17; Isaiah 5:1–7). A vine is valuable, but only because of its fruit. Its wood is crooked and soft. You cannot even make a hook out of it. When a vine does not bring grapes, it is only good as firewood.
> Verses 4–5: Jerusalem is like a piece of vine wood that has already been burned at both ends. The ends probably stand for the disasters the people have already lived through (the destruction of the northern kingdom and the first exile in 597 before Christ). What is left will also be burned.
> Jesus takes up this picture in the New Testament: “I am the vine. You are the branches. If you remain in me and I remain in you, you will bear much fruit” (John 15:1–8). For Jesus, it is above all an invitation: Stay connected with me, then you will grow and bring fruit.
