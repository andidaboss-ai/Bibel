# Ezekiel – Chapter 11
### A new heart

---

## The pot and the meat (Verses 1–13)

[1] Then the Spirit lifted me up
and brought me to the east gate of the LORD’s house, which faces east.
At the entrance of the gate there were twenty-five men.
Among them I saw Jaazaniah the son of Azzur
and Pelatiah the son of Benaiah, leaders of the people.
[2] He said to me,
“Son of man, these are the men who plan evil
and give wicked advice in this city.
[3] They say,
‘The time to build houses is not near.
This city is the cooking pot, and we are the meat.’
[4] So prophesy against them.
Prophesy, son of man.”
[5] Then the LORD’s Spirit came on me,
and he said to me,
“Say, ‘This is what the LORD says:
“That is what you have said, people of Israel.
I know the thoughts that come into your minds.
[6] You have killed many people in this city.
You have filled its streets with the dead.”
[7] “‘That is why the Lord GOD says:
“The dead you have left lying in the middle of the city
are the meat, and this city is the pot.
But you will be brought out of it.
[8] You have been afraid of the sword,
and I will bring the sword against you,”
says the Lord GOD.
[9] “I will bring you out of the city
and hand you over to strangers.
I will carry out judgments on you.
[10] You will be killed by the sword.
I will judge you at the border of Israel.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.
[11] This city will not be your cooking pot,
and you will not be the meat in it.
I will judge you at the border of Israel.
[12] You will know that I am the LORD.
For you have not lived by my rules.
You have not kept my laws.
Instead, you have followed the laws of the nations around you.”’”
[13] While I was prophesying, Pelatiah the son of Benaiah died.
Then I fell down on my face.
I cried out with a loud voice,
“Oh, Lord GOD!
Will you completely destroy all who are left in Israel?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: The powerful men in Jerusalem are proud. They say: “This city is the cooking pot, and we are the meat.” That means: We are the best part, well protected in the city like meat in a pot. The others, who were taken away, are the garbage. There is no need to “build houses” somewhere else, because we are staying here.
> Verses 6–7: God turns the picture around: The real “meat” in the pot is the innocent people these men have killed. The powerful men themselves will be taken out of the city and judged.
> Verse 10: “At the border of Israel”: This came true in Riblah, far in the north. There the king of Babylon had the leaders of Judah executed (2 Kings 25:18–21).
> Verse 13: While Ezekiel is prophesying, Pelatiah dies. Ezekiel is not glad about it. He is shocked and cries out to God. Again he speaks up for his people. By the way, the name Pelatiah means “The LORD rescues.” That makes Ezekiel’s question even more urgent.

## God will be their holy place (Verses 14–21)

[14] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[15] “Son of man, the people of Jerusalem have spoken to your brothers,
yes, your brothers, the men of your own family,
and to all the people of Israel, all of them.
They have said to them,
‘Go far away from the LORD.
This land has been given to us to own.’
[16] “So say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I have sent them far away among the nations.
I have scattered them among the countries.
But for a little while I will be a holy place for them
in the countries where they have gone.”’
[17] “So say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“I will gather you from the peoples.
I will bring you together out of the countries
where you have been scattered.
And I will give you the land of Israel.”
[18] “‘They will come there,
and they will remove from it all its hateful idols
and all its disgusting things.
[19] I will give them one heart,
and I will put a new spirit inside them.
I will take the heart of stone out of their bodies,
and I will give them a heart of flesh.
[20] Then they will live by my rules,
and they will keep my laws and obey them.
They will be my people,
and I will be their God.
[21] But some people’s hearts will keep following
their hateful idols and their disgusting things.
I will bring on their own heads what they have done,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The people in Jerusalem say to the exiles: “Go far away from the LORD. This land has been given to us to own.” They think they are the winners.
> Verse 16: But God says the opposite: He wants to be with the exiles! He himself will be their “holy place,” even without a temple. That is a great comfort: God is there even in a foreign land. You can find him anywhere. (Later, in Judaism, this verse was also connected with the synagogues, as a “little holy place.”)
> Verse 19: One of the most beautiful promises in the Bible: God takes away the “heart of stone,” which is hard and cold. He gives a “heart of flesh,” which is alive and can feel. God himself changes people from the inside. This promise comes again in Ezekiel 36:26.
> Compare Jeremiah 31:33. There God promises to put his law inside his people and to write it on their hearts.

## The glory goes to the Mount of Olives (Verses 22–25)

[22] Then the cherubim lifted their wings,
and the wheels were beside them.
The glory of the God of Israel was high above them.
[23] The LORD’s glory rose up from the middle of the city
and stopped on the mountain east of the city.
[24] The Spirit lifted me up.
In the vision given by the Spirit of God,
he brought me to Chaldea, to the captives.
Then the vision I had seen left me.
[25] And I told the captives everything
that the LORD had shown me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Now God’s glory leaves the city completely and stops on the mountain east of Jerusalem. That is the Mount of Olives. From there it can still look at the city.
> Interesting: In the New Testament, the Mount of Olives plays a big part. Jesus weeps over Jerusalem there (Luke 19:37–41), and from there he goes up into heaven (Acts 1:9–12).
> Verses 24–25: The vision is over. Ezekiel is back with the exiles in Babylon and tells them everything. The message to the exiles is: The temple in Jerusalem is no longer safe. But God is with you.
