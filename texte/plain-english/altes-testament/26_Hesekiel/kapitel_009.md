# Ezekiel – Chapter 9
### The mark on the forehead

---

## A mark for those who sigh (Verses 1–4)

[1] Then he shouted into my ears with a loud voice,
“Bring near the men who are in charge of the city,
each man with his weapon for destroying in his hand!”
[2] Then six men came from the direction of the upper gate,
which faces north.
Each man had his weapon for killing in his hand.
One man among them was dressed in linen.
He had a writer’s ink case at his side.
They went in and stood beside the bronze altar.
[3] The glory of the God of Israel rose up
from the cherub it had been on,
and moved to the doorway of the temple.
He called to the man dressed in linen,
who had the writer’s ink case at his side.
[4] The LORD said to him,
“Go through the middle of the city, through the middle of Jerusalem.
Put a mark on the foreheads of the men
who sigh and cry
over all the disgusting things that are being done in it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a vision, a picture in Ezekiel’s mind. It is not a description of something people are supposed to do. The six men are heavenly messengers of judgment. The seventh man, dressed in linen, is like God’s scribe. Linen was the clothing of the priests and of the angels (compare Daniel 10:5).
> Verse 3: God’s glory leaves its place above the cherubim in the Most Holy Place and moves to the doorway of the temple. That is the first step: God begins to leave the temple.
> Verse 4: Before the judgment comes, God takes care of the righteous. Whoever is sad about the wrongdoing, whoever “sighs and cries,” gets a mark on the forehead and is spared. God sees the people who do not take part.
> The Hebrew word for “mark” is “tav.” That is also the name of the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet. In the old writing it looked like a cross or an X. Later, many Christians saw in it a hint of the cross. Compare also Exodus 12:13 (the blood on the doors) and Revelation 7:3 (the seal on the forehead).

## The judgment begins at the holy place (Verses 5–11)

[5] I heard him say to the others,
“Follow him through the city, and strike.
Do not let your eye spare anyone.
Do not have pity.
[6] Kill them all: old men, young men, young women,
little children, and women.
But do not come near anyone who has the mark.
Begin at my holy place.”
So they began with the elders who were in front of the temple.
[7] He said to them,
“Make the temple unclean,
and fill the courts with the dead.
Go out!”
So they went out and struck people down in the city.
[8] While they were killing,
I was left alone.
I fell on my face
and cried out,
“Oh, Lord GOD!
Will you destroy everyone who is left in Israel
when you pour out your anger on Jerusalem?”
[9] Then he said to me,
“The sin of the people of Israel and Judah is very, very great.
The land is full of blood,
and the city is full of injustice.
For they say,
‘The LORD has left the land,
and the LORD does not see.’
[10] So my eye will not spare them either,
and I will have no pity.
I will bring on their own heads what they have done.”
[11] Then the man dressed in linen,
who had the ink case at his side,
came back with a report.
He said,
“I have done what you commanded me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This text is very hard to read. In a vision, it describes the destruction of Jerusalem that really happened in the year 586 BC through the Babylonians. Children, women, and old people also died then. The Bible does not keep quiet about this horror.
> Very important: This text is not an order for people to follow. No one may use it as an excuse to do violence to others. Judgment belongs to God alone (compare Romans 12:19). Violence against children and defenseless people is always wrong.
> Verse 6: “Begin at my holy place.” The judgment begins with those who should have known best: with the elders at the temple. Compare 1 Peter 4:17: “For the time has come for judgment to begin with the household of God.”
> Verse 8: Ezekiel throws himself down and cries out: “Will you destroy everyone who is left in Israel?” He speaks up for his people, as Abraham did for Sodom (Genesis 18) and Moses did for Israel (Exodus 32). A prophet is not cold. He suffers along with the people.
> Verse 11: At the end the scribe reports: “I have done what you commanded me.” That means: The people with the mark were protected. God did not forget those who lamented over the wrongdoing.
