# Ezekiel – Chapter 44
### The closed gate and the priests

---

## The east gate stays closed (Verses 1–3)

[1] Then he brought me back to the outer gate of the sanctuary,
the one that faces east.
It was closed.
[2] The LORD said to me,
“This gate must stay closed.
It must not be opened.
No one may go in through it,
because the LORD, the God of Israel, has gone in through it.
So it must stay closed.
[3] As for the prince, he may sit in it as prince
to eat bread before the LORD.
He must come in through the porch of the gate
and go out the same way.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Because God himself has come back through the east gate, it is closed forever. No one else may use this way. This also shows: God will never leave the temple again.
> Today in Jerusalem there is a gate in the eastern city wall that has been bricked up, the “Golden Gate” (in Hebrew “Shaar haRachamim,” the Gate of Mercy). It was bricked up hundreds of years ago. Many Jews, Christians, and Muslims connect hopes for the end of time with it.
> In Christian tradition, the closed gate was later also understood as a picture for Mary, through whom God came into the world.

## Foreigners in the sanctuary (Verses 4–9)

[4] Then he brought me through the north gate to the front of the temple.
I looked, and the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.
I fell on my face.
[5] The LORD said to me,
“Son of man, pay close attention.
Look with your eyes,
and listen with your ears to everything I tell you
about all the rules of the house of the LORD and all its laws.
Pay close attention to the entrance of the temple
and to all the exits of the sanctuary.
[6] Say to the rebels, to the people of Israel,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“People of Israel, enough of all the disgusting things you have done!
[7] You brought in foreigners,
uncircumcised in heart and uncircumcised in body,
to be in my sanctuary and make it unholy, my house,
while you offered me my food, the fat and the blood.
They broke my covenant,
in addition to all the disgusting things you did.
[8] You did not take care of my holy things yourselves.
Instead you put others in my sanctuary
to do the work for you.”
[9] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“No foreigner who is uncircumcised in heart and uncircumcised in body
may enter my sanctuary,
none of the foreigners who live among the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the old temple they had apparently used foreign workers or guards who did not know or worship God (“uncircumcised in heart”). They did holy work that the priests and Levites should have done. The people of Israel had simply handed over their responsibility.
> Important: This is about the service in the innermost sanctuary, not about foreigners in general. In many places the Bible commands people to love and protect foreigners (Leviticus 19:33–34). Isaiah 56:6–7 even says that foreigners who join the LORD are welcome in his house: “For my house will be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” And Ezekiel himself says in 47:22–23 that foreigners are to receive land as their share, just like those born in the land.
> So this text must not be used to exclude foreigners or people of a different background.

## The Levites (Verses 10–14)

[10] “‘“But the Levites
who went far away from me when Israel went astray,
who wandered away from me to follow their idols,
must bear the results of their guilt.
[11] Still, they are to serve in my sanctuary.
They are to watch over the gates of the temple
and serve in the temple.
They are to kill the animals for the burnt offering and the sacrifices for the people.
They are to stand before the people to serve them.
[12] They served the people in front of their idols
and made the people of Israel fall into sin.
So I have raised my hand and sworn an oath against them,”
says the Lord GOD,
“that they will bear the results of their guilt.
[13] They must not come near me
to serve me as priests.
They must not come near any of my holy things,
the things that are most holy.
They must bear their shame
and the results of the disgusting things they have done.
[14] Still, I will put them in charge of the work of the temple,
of all its work
and of everything that is done in it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Levites were the tribe that was responsible for worship. Many of them had served at shrines for idols. So they may no longer be priests, but they keep a task: They guard the gates, kill the animals for the sacrifices, and help the people.
> This shows: Guilt has consequences. But God does not reject the Levites completely. They still get a place in the service.

## The priests, the sons of Zadok (Verses 15–16)

[15] “‘“But the priests from the tribe of Levi, the sons of Zadok,
took care of my sanctuary
when the people of Israel went astray from me.
They will come near me to serve me.
They will stand before me
to offer me the fat and the blood,”
says the Lord GOD.
[16] “They will enter my sanctuary.
They will come near my table to serve me,
and they will keep my instructions.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests from the family of Zadok had stayed faithful when others turned away. So they may do the work of priests. Ezekiel himself probably came from a priestly family in Jerusalem, maybe from this line.

## Rules for the priests (Verses 17–27)

[17] “‘“When they go in through the gates of the inner courtyard,
they must wear linen clothes.
They must not wear any wool
while they serve at the gates of the inner courtyard and inside it.
[18] They must wear linen turbans on their heads
and linen pants around their waists.
They must not wear anything that makes them sweat.
[19] When they go out into the outer courtyard,
into the outer courtyard where the people are,
they must take off the clothes they serve in
and leave them in the holy rooms.
They must put on other clothes,
so that they do not make the people holy with their clothes.
[20] “‘“They must not shave their heads,
and they must not let their hair grow long.
They must only trim the hair on their heads.
[21] No priest may drink wine
when he goes into the inner courtyard.
[22] They must not marry a widow or a divorced woman.
They must marry young women who have never been with a man,
from the descendants of the people of Israel,
or a widow whose husband was a priest.
[23] They must teach my people the difference
between what is holy and what is ordinary.
They must help them tell the difference between what is unclean and what is clean.
[24] “‘“When there is a dispute, they must stand up and judge.
They must judge by my rulings.
They must keep my laws and my rules
at all my festivals.
They must keep my Sabbaths holy.
[25] “‘“A priest must not make himself unclean
by going near a dead person.
But he may make himself unclean
for his father or mother,
for his son or daughter,
for his brother, or for his sister who has never had a husband.
[26] After he has been made clean,
he must wait seven more days.
[27] On the day he goes into the sanctuary,
into the inner courtyard, to serve in the sanctuary,
he must offer his sin offering,”
says the Lord GOD.

> **What does this mean?**
> These rules are like the laws for the priests in the book of Leviticus (Chapters 10 and 21).
> Verses 17–18: Linen is cool and light. The priests are not to sweat in the sanctuary. Sweat was considered unclean.
> Verse 21: No alcohol while serving (see Leviticus 10:9). Whoever serves before God is to have a clear head.
> Verse 22: These marriage rules for priests match the old rules about being clean (Leviticus 21:7, 14). They do not look down on widows or divorced women. In many places the Bible gives special protection to widows (for example Deuteronomy 24:17; James 1:27).
> Verses 23–24: The priests are responsible not only for sacrifices. They are also teachers and judges. They are to help the people understand God’s will.
> Verse 25: An exception when a close family member dies: Priests may mourn too.

## God is their share (Verses 28–31)

[28] “‘They will have something to inherit:
I am what they inherit.
You must not give them any land of their own in Israel.
I am their property.
[29] They will eat the grain offering, the sin offering, and the guilt offering.
Everything in Israel that is given completely to God will belong to them.
[30] The best of all the first fruits of every kind
and every gift of every kind,
of all your gifts,
will belong to the priests.
You must also give the priests the first part of your dough,
so that a blessing may rest on your house.
[31] The priests must not eat anything that died by itself
or was torn apart by wild animals,
whether bird or animal.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 28: “I am what they inherit.” The priests get no land. Their wealth is God himself (see Numbers 18:20: “I am your share and your inheritance”; Psalm 16:5).
> They live from the gifts of the people. The people take care of the priests, and the priests serve the people.
> Verse 30: Whoever gives will be blessed: “so that a blessing may rest on your house.”
