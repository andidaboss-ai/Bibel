# Ezekiel – Chapter 17
### Two eagles and a vine

---

## The riddle (Verses 1–10)

[1] The LORD’s word came to me.
He said,
[2] “Son of man, give the people of Israel a riddle.
Tell them a parable.
[3] Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“A great eagle with great wings and long feathers,
full of feathers of many colors,
came to Lebanon
and took the top of the cedar tree.
[4] He broke off the highest of its young twigs
and carried it to a land of trade.
He planted it in a city of merchants.
[5] “‘“He also took some of the seed of the land
and planted it in rich soil.
He put it beside plenty of water.
He planted it like a willow tree.
[6] It grew and became a vine that spread out
but stayed low.
Its branches turned toward the eagle,
and its roots stayed under it.
So it became a vine.
It grew branches and sent out shoots.
[7] “‘“There was also another great eagle
with great wings and many feathers.
And this vine bent its roots toward him.
It sent out its branches toward him,
away from the bed where it was planted,
so that he would water it.
[8] But it had been planted in good soil beside plenty of water,
so that it could grow branches and bear fruit
and be a fine vine.”’
[9] “Say,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Will it do well?
Will the first eagle not pull up its roots
and cut off its fruit, so that it dries up,
so that all its fresh new leaves dry up?
No strong arm and no crowd of people
will be able to lift it up again by its roots.
[10] Yes, it is planted, but will it do well?
Will it not dry up completely
when the east wind touches it?
It will dry up in the bed where it grew.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ezekiel tells a riddle in pictures. The answer comes from verse 11 on. In short:
> The first great eagle is Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon.
> The top of the cedar is King Jehoiachin. In 597 before Christ, Nebuchadnezzar took him to Babylon (the “land of trade”).
> The seed he plants is Zedekiah, whom Nebuchadnezzar made king in Jerusalem. He is a “low” vine, that is, a king who depends on someone else. But he could have lived well.
> The second eagle is the Pharaoh of Egypt. Zedekiah stretches out his “roots” toward Egypt and hopes for help against Babylon.
> Verse 10: The “east wind” is a hot desert wind that makes plants dry up. It stands for Babylon, which comes from the east.

---

## The meaning: Zedekiah breaks his oath (Verses 11–21)

[11] The LORD’s word came to me again.
He said,
[12] “Say to these rebellious people,
‘Don’t you know what these things mean?’
Tell them,
‘The king of Babylon came to Jerusalem.
He took its king and its leaders
and brought them to himself in Babylon.
[13] He took a man from the royal family
and made a covenant with him.
He made him swear an oath.
And he took away the powerful men of the land.
[14] He did this so that the kingdom would stay weak
and would not rise up again,
but would last by keeping his covenant.
[15] But the king rebelled against him.
He sent his messengers to Egypt
to get horses and many soldiers.
Will he succeed?
Will someone who does such things escape?
Can he break the covenant and still escape?
[16] “‘As surely as I live,’ says the Lord GOD,
‘he will die in Babylon,
in the land of the king who made him king,
the king whose oath he despised and whose covenant he broke.
[17] Pharaoh with his mighty army and his great crowd of soldiers
will not help him in the war,
when ramps of earth are built and towers are put up
to kill many people.
[18] For he despised the oath by breaking the covenant.
He had given his hand on it,
and yet he did all these things.
He will not escape.’
[19] “That is why the Lord GOD says:
‘As surely as I live,
I will bring on his own head
my oath that he despised
and my covenant that he broke.
[20] I will spread my net over him,
and he will be caught in my trap.
I will bring him to Babylon.
There I will judge him
for being unfaithful to me.
[21] All the soldiers in his army who run away will be killed by the sword.
Those who are left will be scattered to every wind.
Then you will know that I, the LORD, have spoken.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Zedekiah had sworn an oath of loyalty to Nebuchadnezzar, and he had sworn it in God’s name (compare 2 Chronicles 36:13). Then he broke this oath and made an alliance with Egypt.
> Verse 19: God calls the oath “my oath” and the covenant “my covenant.” Whoever promises something in God’s name and breaks it insults God himself. A promise matters even when you have made it to an enemy.
> Everything happened this way: Egypt did not really help (compare Jeremiah 37:5–8), Jerusalem was captured, and Zedekiah died as a prisoner in Babylon (compare Ezekiel 12:13).
> For today: Honesty and being reliable are important. Jesus says: “Let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No’” (Matthew 5:37).

## God plants a new shoot (Verses 22–24)

[22] “This is what the Lord GOD says:
‘I myself will take a shoot from the high top of the cedar
and plant it.
From the highest of its young twigs
I will break off a tender one.
I will plant it on a high and lofty mountain.
[23] I will plant it on the high mountain of Israel.
It will grow branches and bear fruit
and become a fine cedar.
Birds of every kind will live in the shade of its branches.
[24] All the trees of the field will know
that I, the LORD, have brought down the high tree
and made the low tree high.
I have made the green tree dry up
and made the dry tree bloom.
I, the LORD, have spoken, and I have done it.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> After the judgment comes hope. Now it is no longer an eagle that acts, but God himself. He takes a tender shoot from the royal family of David and plants it again on Mount Zion. It becomes a great cedar in which all the birds find shelter.
> Jews and Christians read this as a promise of a coming king from the family of David, the Messiah (compare Isaiah 11:1; Jeremiah 23:5).
> Jesus used a similar picture: The Kingdom of God is like a tiny mustard seed that becomes a great tree, so that the birds live in its branches (Mark 4:30–32).
> Verse 24: God brings down the high and lifts up the low. That is a basic theme of the Bible (compare 1 Samuel 2:7–8; Luke 1:52).
