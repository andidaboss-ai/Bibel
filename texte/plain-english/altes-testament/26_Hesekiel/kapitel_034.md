# Ezekiel – Chapter 34
### The good shepherd

---

## How terrible for the shepherds who feed only themselves (Verses 1–10)

[1] The word of the LORD came to me. He said,
[2] “Son of man, prophesy against the shepherds of Israel.
Prophesy and say to them, to the shepherds,
‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“How terrible it will be for the shepherds of Israel who feed only themselves!
Shouldn’t the shepherds feed the sheep?
[3] You eat the fat.
You dress yourselves with the wool.
You kill the fattened animals,
but you do not feed the sheep.
[4] You have not made the weak ones strong.
You have not healed the sick ones.
You have not bandaged the injured ones.
You have not brought back the ones that were driven away.
You have not searched for the lost ones.
Instead you have ruled over them with force and harshness.
[5] They were scattered because there was no shepherd.
They became food for all the wild animals,
and they were scattered.
[6] My sheep wandered over all the mountains
and on every high hill.
Yes, my sheep were scattered over the whole earth.
No one looked for them or searched for them.”
[7] “‘So, you shepherds, hear the word of the LORD:
[8] “As surely as I live,” says the Lord GOD,
“my sheep have become prey.
My sheep have become food for all the wild animals,
because there was no shepherd.
My shepherds did not look for my sheep.
The shepherds fed themselves
and did not feed my sheep.
[9] So, you shepherds, hear the word of the LORD!”
[10] This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I am against the shepherds.
I will hold them responsible for my sheep.
I will stop them from taking care of the sheep.
The shepherds will not feed themselves anymore.
I will rescue my sheep from their mouths,
so that the sheep will not be food for them anymore.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The “shepherds” are the kings and leaders of Israel. In the ancient Near East, kings were often called “shepherds of their people.”
> The charge: These shepherds thought only of themselves. They ate the fat and dressed themselves with the wool, that is, they used the people for their own gain. But they did nothing for the weak, the sick and the lost. Instead they ruled with harshness and force.
> Verse 4 is a kind of job description for good leadership: making the weak strong, healing the sick, bandaging the injured, bringing back those who have wandered off, searching for the lost.
> This is still true today for everyone who has responsibility: in politics, church, school, business or family. Good leadership serves people, especially the weakest. Whoever uses power only for himself fails before God.

---

## I myself will search for my sheep (Verses 11–16)

[11] “‘For this is what the Lord GOD says:
“Look, I myself, yes I,
will search for my sheep
and look after them.
[12] A shepherd looks for his flock
on the day he is among his scattered sheep.
In the same way I will look for my sheep.
I will rescue them from all the places
where they were scattered
on the day of clouds and darkness.
[13] I will bring them out from the peoples.
I will gather them from the countries.
I will bring them into their own land.
I will feed them on the mountains of Israel,
by the streams,
and in all the places in the land where people live.
[14] I will feed them on good pasture.
Their resting place will be on the high mountains of Israel.
There they will lie down in a good resting place.
They will eat rich grass on the mountains of Israel.
[15] I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep,
and I will let them lie down and rest,”
says the Lord GOD.
[16] “I will search for the lost ones.
I will bring back the ones that were driven away.
I will bandage the injured ones.
I will make the sick ones strong.
But I will destroy the fat and the strong ones.
I will take care of them with justice.”’

> **What does this mean?**
> Because the human shepherds have failed, God says: “I myself will be the shepherd!” And he does exactly what the bad shepherds did not do (verse 16 is the answer to verse 4).
> This is like Psalm 23: “The LORD is my shepherd. I will not lack anything.”
> Jesus takes up this chapter: “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives his life for the sheep” (John 10:11). And he tells the parable of the lost sheep, which the shepherd searches for until he finds it (Luke 15:4–7). “For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost” (Luke 19:10).
> Verse 16: “I will destroy the fat and the strong ones.” This means those who became fat and strong at the cost of others (see verses 17–21).
> For you this means: If you feel lost, hurt or weak, you are exactly the one God is looking for.

## God judges between sheep and sheep (Verses 17–22)

[17] “As for you, my flock,
this is what the Lord GOD says:
‘Look, I will judge between one sheep and another,
between the rams and the male goats.
[18] Is it not enough for you to eat the good grass?
Must you also trample the rest of your pasture with your feet?
Is it not enough for you to drink the clear water?
Must you also make the rest muddy with your feet?
[19] And my sheep must eat what you have trampled with your feet.
They must drink what you have made muddy with your feet.’
[20] “So this is what the Lord GOD says to them:
‘Look, I myself, yes I, will judge
between the fat sheep and the thin sheep.
[21] You push with your side and your shoulder.
You butt all the weak ones with your horns,
until you have driven them away.
[22] So I will save my flock,
and they will not be prey anymore.
I will judge between one sheep and another.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God speaks not only to the leaders, but to the whole people. Among the “sheep” there is injustice too: The strong ones eat the best grass and drink the clear water. Then they trample the rest, so that the weak ones only get what is dirty. And they push the weak away with their horns.
> This is a picture for social injustice: The rich and powerful take the best for themselves and leave only the leftovers for the poor, often leftovers that are ruined.
> God takes the side of the “thin sheep,” the weak and the disadvantaged.
> Jesus also speaks about this in Matthew 25:31–46: The Son of Man will separate people from one another “as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats.” What counts there is how people treated the weakest.

## My servant David (Verses 23–24)

[23] I will put one shepherd over them,
and he will take care of them.
He is my servant David.
He will feed them,
and he will be their shepherd.
[24] I, the LORD, will be their God,
and my servant David will be prince among them.
I, the LORD, have spoken.

> **What does this mean?**
> At this time King David had been dead for more than 400 years. What is meant is a new king from the family of David, a king who is like David, only better: a shepherd after God’s own heart.
> Jews are waiting for this Messiah from the house of David. Christians see the promise fulfilled in Jesus, who came from the family of David (Matthew 1:1) and calls himself the good shepherd.
> Remarkable: God is the shepherd (verse 15), and David is the shepherd (verse 23). Both belong together: Through the new David, God himself takes care of his people.

## A covenant of peace (Verses 25–31)

[25] “‘I will make a covenant of peace with them.
I will make the dangerous wild animals disappear from the land.
They will live safely in the wilderness
and sleep in the woods.
[26] I will make them and the places around my hill a blessing.
I will send down the rain at the right time.
There will be showers of blessing.
[27] The trees of the field will give their fruit.
The earth will give its harvest.
They will be safe in their land.
Then they will know that I am the LORD,
when I have broken the bars of their yoke
and rescued them from the hands of those who made them slaves.
[28] They will not be prey for the nations anymore.
The wild animals will not eat them anymore.
They will live in safety,
and no one will make them afraid.
[29] I will give them a land where plants grow well, and it will be famous.
They will not be destroyed by famine in the land anymore.
They will not have to bear the scorn of the nations anymore.
[30] They will know that I, the LORD their God, am with them,
and that they, the people of Israel, are my people,
says the Lord GOD.
[31] You are my sheep, the sheep of my pasture.
You are human beings,
and I am your God,’
says the Lord GOD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A wonderful picture of peace: no more wild animals, you can even sleep safely in the woods. Rain at the right time, trees full of fruit, no fear, no hunger, no more slavery.
> Verse 27: God breaks the yoke of oppression. He is a God who sets people free (see Exodus 6:6).
> Verse 26: “Showers of blessing” has become a well-known phrase, also in songs.
> Verse 31: A touching ending: “You are my sheep, the sheep of my pasture. You are human beings, and I am your God.” The picture of the sheep is explained: God really means people, and he wants to be their God.
