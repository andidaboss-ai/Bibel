# Ezekiel – Chapter 46
### Rules for the prince and the people

---

## Sabbath and new moon (Verses 1–8)

[1] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“The gate of the inner courtyard that faces east
must stay closed on the six working days.
But on the Sabbath day it must be opened,
and on the day of the new moon it must be opened.
[2] The prince must come in from outside through the porch of the gate
and stand by the post of the gate.
The priests must prepare his burnt offering and his peace offerings.
He must worship at the threshold of the gate.
Then he must go out.
But the gate must not be closed until evening.
[3] The people of the land must worship before the LORD
at the entrance of that gate
on the Sabbaths and on the new moons.
[4] The burnt offering that the prince offers to the LORD
on the Sabbath day must be
six lambs without any defect and one ram without any defect.
[5] The grain offering must be an ephah for the ram.
The grain offering for the lambs is as much as he is able to give.
He must also give a hin of oil for each ephah.
[6] On the day of the new moon he must offer a young bull without any defect,
six lambs, and a ram.
They must be without any defect.
[7] He must provide a grain offering:
an ephah for the bull,
an ephah for the ram,
and for the lambs as much as he is able to give,
and a hin of oil for each ephah.
[8] When the prince comes in,
he must come in through the porch of the gate.
And he must go out the same way.

> **What does this mean?**
> On normal days the inner east gate is closed. It is opened only on the Sabbath and at the new moon (the beginning of the month). Then the prince prays at the threshold, and the people pray outside at the entrance. Through the open gate everyone can see the altar and the temple.
> The prince may not go into the inner courtyard. He is the leader of the people, but he is not a priest. God is above all rulers.
> Verses 5 and 7: “As much as he is able to give” – the gift depends on what a person can do. God does not ask for anything impossible.
> An ephah is about 6 gallons. A hin is about 1 gallon.

---

## How people walk through the temple (Verses 9–15)

[9] “‘“But when the people of the land come before the LORD at the appointed festivals,
whoever comes in through the north gate to worship
must go out through the south gate.
And whoever comes in through the south gate
must go out through the north gate.
No one may go back through the gate he came in by.
Everyone must go straight ahead and out.
[10] The prince must go in with them when they go in.
When they go out, he must go out.
[11] “‘“At the festivals and on the appointed holy days,
the grain offering must be an ephah for a bull
and an ephah for a ram,
and for the lambs as much as he is able to give,
and a hin of oil for each ephah.
[12] When the prince provides a freewill offering,
a burnt offering or peace offerings as a freewill gift to the LORD,
the gate that faces east must be opened for him.
He must provide his burnt offering and his peace offerings,
as he does on the Sabbath day.
Then he must go out,
and after he has gone out, the gate must be closed.
[13] “‘“Every day you must provide a lamb one year old without any defect
as a burnt offering to the LORD.
You must provide it every morning.
[14] Every morning you must also provide a grain offering with it:
one sixth of an ephah of flour,
and one third of a hin of oil to moisten the fine flour.
This is a grain offering to the LORD.
It must be given always, as a rule that lasts forever.
[15] So they must provide the lamb, the grain offering, and the oil
every morning,
as a burnt offering that is given always.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: At the great festivals the temple is full of people. So that there is no crowding, there is a one-way rule: Whoever comes in from the north goes out to the south, and the other way around. A practical rule! Some Bible teachers also see a picture in it: Whoever has met God does not go back the same way, but goes on changed.
> Verse 10: The prince walks in the middle of the people, not ahead of them and not apart from them. He is one of them.
> Verses 13–15: Every morning a lamb is sacrificed, the “continual offering.” It shows: Every day belongs to God.

## The prince’s property (Verses 16–18)

[16] “‘This is what the Lord GOD says:
“Suppose the prince gives a gift of land to one of his sons.
Then it is his son’s inheritance.
It will belong to his sons.
It is their property to inherit.
[17] But suppose he gives a gift from his land to one of his servants.
Then it will belong to the servant only until the year of freedom.
After that it will go back to the prince.
His land will belong only to his sons.
[18] The prince must not take any of the people’s land
and drive them off their property.
He must give his sons land from his own property,
so that none of my people are driven away from their property.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: The “year of freedom” is the year of release (the Year of Jubilee), every 50 years. Then land went back to its original owners (Leviticus 25:10–13).
> Verse 18: Again the ordinary people are protected: The prince may not take land away from the people. He may provide for his sons only from his own property. The powerful must not grow rich at the cost of the weak.

## The kitchens of the temple (Verses 19–24)

[19] Then he brought me through the entrance
at the side of the gate
into the holy rooms for the priests,
which faced north.
Look, there was a place at the back, on the west side.
[20] He said to me,
“This is the place where the priests must boil the guilt offering and the sin offering,
and where they must bake the grain offering.
Then they do not have to carry them out into the outer courtyard
and make the people holy.”
[21] Then he brought me out into the outer courtyard
and led me past the four corners of the courtyard.
Look, in each corner of the courtyard there was a smaller courtyard.
[22] In the four corners of the courtyard there were enclosed courtyards,
about 70 feet long and about 52½ feet wide.
All four in the corners were the same size.
[23] Around each of the four there was a wall,
and fireplaces for cooking were built along the bottom of the walls all around.
[24] Then he said to me,
“These are the kitchens
where those who serve in the temple must boil the sacrifices of the people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The WEB says 40 cubits long and 30 cubits wide. In Ezekiel’s temple a cubit is about 21 inches.
> The temple even has kitchens! There are two kinds: the kitchens of the priests for the holy shares of the sacrifices, and four large kitchens in the corners of the outer courtyard, where the Levites cook the sacrifice meals for the people.
> Many sacrifices ended with a shared festive meal. Worship and eating together belonged together. This is like the Lord’s Supper (Communion) for Christians and the festive meals in Judaism, for example on the Sabbath and at Passover.
