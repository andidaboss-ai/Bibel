# Micah – Chapter 4
### Swords into plows

---

## Peace for all nations (Verses 1–5)

[1] But in the last days this will happen:
The mountain of the LORD’s temple
will stand firm as the highest of the mountains.
It will be raised high above the hills.
And peoples will stream to it.
[2] Many nations will go and say,
“Come! Let’s go up to the mountain of the LORD,
to the house of the God of Jacob!
He will teach us his ways,
and we will walk on his paths.”
For the law will go out from Zion,
and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.
[3] He will judge between many peoples.
He will settle the quarrels of strong nations far away.
They will hammer their swords into plows
and their spears into pruning knives.
A pruning knife is a knife for cutting back grapevines.
Nation will not lift up the sword against nation.
They will not learn war any more.
[4] Instead everyone will sit under his own vine
and under his own fig tree.
No one will make them afraid,
for the mouth of the LORD of Armies has said it.
[5] All the nations may walk
in the name of their own gods.
But we will walk in the name of the LORD our God
forever and ever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Right after the announcement “That is why, because of you, Zion will be plowed like a field” (3:12) comes one of the most beautiful visions of peace in the Bible.
> Verses 1–2: At the end of time, the temple mountain will become the center of the world. Not because it is the highest mountain, but because all nations come there to learn from God. They come freely. No one forces them.
> Verse 3: God will settle quarrels between the nations. Then no one needs weapons any more. Swords become plows, spears become pruning knives. Weapons become tools for life.
> “They will not learn war any more”: There is no more training for war.
> Almost the same words are in Isaiah 2:2–4. The two prophets lived at the same time.
> “Swords into plowshares” (a plowshare is the part of the plow that cuts open the ground) became the motto of the peace movement, especially in East Germany in the 1980s. A sculpture with this picture stands in front of the United Nations building in New York.
> Verse 4: Everyone sits in peace under his own vine and fig tree. That is a picture of safety, wealth, and peace for everyone, also for the ordinary people whose land was taken away in Chapter 2.
> Verse 5: Until then the nations go their own ways. But Israel stays faithful to its God.

## God gathers the weak (Verses 6–8)

[6] “On that day,” says the LORD,
“I will gather those who limp.
I will bring together those who were driven away,
and those whom I have made to suffer.
[7] I will make those who limp into a remnant,
and those who were driven far away into a strong nation.
And the LORD will rule as king over them on Mount Zion
from then on and forever.”
[8] And you, tower of the flock,
hill of the daughter of Zion,
it will come to you.
Yes, the rule of former times will come back,
the kingdom of the daughter of Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: God gathers especially the weak: those who limp, those who were driven out, those who were hurt. From them he makes a strong nation. God begins something new with those who stand at the edge.
> Verse 8: The “tower of the flock” was a watchtower from which shepherds protected their flocks. Here it is a picture for Jerusalem. The old rule of David will come back.

---

## The birth pains of the daughter of Zion (Verses 9–13)

[9] Now why do you cry out so loudly?
Is there no king in you?
Has your adviser died,
so that pains have taken hold of you like a woman giving birth?
[10] Twist and groan, daughter of Zion,
like a woman giving birth.
For now you will go out of the city
and live in the open field.
You will go all the way to Babylon.
There you will be rescued.
There the LORD will set you free
from the hand of your enemies.
[11] Now many nations have gathered against you.
They say, “Let her be made unclean!
Let our eyes look with joy at the shame of Zion!”
[12] But they do not know the thoughts of the LORD.
They do not understand his plan.
For he has gathered them like bundles of grain on the threshing floor.
[13] Get up and thresh, daughter of Zion!
For I will make your horn like iron
and your hoofs like bronze.
You will crush many peoples.
I will give what they gained to the LORD,
and their riches to the Lord of the whole earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: Jerusalem is compared to a woman giving birth. The pains are great: The people will be taken away all the way to Babylon. But birth pains lead to new life. And in Babylon of all places, God will save his people. This happened when the people who had been taken away were allowed to return after about 50 years.
> Verse 11: The enemies want to enjoy seeing Zion “made unclean,” that is, abused and humiliated. This language reminds us of the violence done to women in war. God stands on the side of those who are humiliated. If you have experienced violence, you can find help. US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 12–13: The nations think they are the winners. But God has a different plan. The picture of threshing: An ox tramples the grain on the threshing floor. In this way Zion will defeat the enemies. This is picture language for God’s judgment on those who oppress others. In the end, everything belongs “to the Lord of the whole earth,” not to the winners. These verses do not call people to violence. The goal remains the peace of verse 3.
