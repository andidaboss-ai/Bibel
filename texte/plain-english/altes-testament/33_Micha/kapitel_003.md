# Micah – Chapter 3
### Against the leaders, priests, and prophets

---

## The leaders eat up the people (Verses 1–4)

[1] I said,
“Please listen, you heads of Jacob
and rulers of the house of Israel!
Shouldn’t you be the ones who know what justice is?
[2] You hate what is good and love what is evil.
You tear the skin off my people
and the flesh off their bones.
[3] You eat the flesh of my people.
You strip their skin off them.
You break their bones
and chop them up like meat for the pot,
like meat in the cooking pot.
[4] Then they will cry out to the LORD,
but he will not answer them.
Yes, at that time he will hide his face from them,
because they have done evil things.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Micah speaks to the powerful: judges, officials, and leaders. Their task would be to make sure there is justice.
> Verses 2–3: A shocking picture: They treat the people like animals for slaughter. They strip off their skin and cook them in the pot. Of course this is meant as a picture. It means: They squeeze people dry, down to the last bit. Nothing is left for them.
> Verse 4: Whoever has no ears for the cries of the poor will not get an answer from God either when he himself is in need (compare Proverbs 21:13).

---

## Against the false prophets (Verses 5–8)

[5] This is what the LORD says about the prophets
who lead my people the wrong way:
When they have something to chew on, they shout, “Peace!”
But if someone does not put food in their mouths,
they get ready to make war against him.
[6] “That is why night will come over you, with no vision.
It will be dark for you, so that you cannot tell the future.
The sun will go down on the prophets,
and the day will become black over them.
[7] The seers will be put to shame,
and the fortune-tellers will be confused.
Yes, they will all cover their lips,
for there is no answer from God.”
[8] But as for me, I am full of power by the Spirit of the LORD,
full of justice and strength,
to tell Jacob that he has not obeyed,
and to tell Israel his sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: The false prophets can be bought: To whoever pays them, they promise “peace,” that is, good times. Whoever does not pay, they threaten. Their message depends on money, not on God.
> Verses 6–7: The punishment: God will be silent. They will have no more visions. Covering your lips was a sign of shame and sorrow.
> Verse 8: Micah takes a clear stand against this: He cannot be bought. He is filled with God’s Spirit, and he has the courage to tell the truth, even when it is uncomfortable.

---

## Zion will be plowed like a field (Verses 9–12)

[9] Please listen to this, you heads of the house of Jacob
and rulers of the house of Israel.
You hate justice.
You twist everything that is straight.
[10] You build Zion with blood
and Jerusalem with wrongdoing!
[11] Her leaders judge for bribes.
Her priests teach for pay.
Her prophets tell the future for money.
And yet they lean on the LORD and say,
“Isn’t the LORD among us?
No disaster will come on us.”
[12] That is why, because of you, Zion will be plowed like a field.
Jerusalem will become heaps of rubble,
and the mountain of the temple will become like hills covered with forest.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Jerusalem was built up in a splendid way, but with “blood” and “wrongdoing”: at the cost of the poor, through exploitation and maybe forced labor.
> Verse 11: Everyone can be bought: judges, priests, prophets. And yet they feel safe: “Isn’t the LORD among us? No disaster will come on us.” They use God as insurance, without living the way he wants.
> Verse 12: The answer: Zion will be plowed up like a field. The temple mountain will become an overgrown hill.
> These words were so famous that people still quoted them 100 years later. When the prophet Jeremiah was to be killed because of similar words, some elders reminded the people: Micah said this too, and King Hezekiah did not kill him, but turned back to God (Jeremiah 26:17–19).
