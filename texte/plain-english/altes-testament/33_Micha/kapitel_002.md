# Micah – Chapter 2
### How terrible for those who steal fields

---

## The land robbers (Verses 1–5)

[1] How terrible it will be for those who think up wrongs
and plan evil on their beds!
When morning comes, they do it,
because they have the power to do it.
[2] They want fields, and they grab them.
They want houses, and they take them away.
They treat a man and his family badly.
They take away a man and the land he inherited.
[3] That is why the LORD says:
“Look, I am planning a disaster against this family.
You will not be able to pull your necks out of it.
And you will no longer walk around proudly,
for it is an evil time.
[4] On that day people will make up a mocking song about you.
They will sing a bitter song of mourning and say,
‘We are completely ruined!
The land of my people is being divided up.
He takes it away from me.
He gives our fields to traitors!’”
[5] That is why you will have no one in the assembly of the LORD
who measures the land with a line and divides it by lot.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: These people lie awake at night and think about how they can take advantage of others. And in the morning they do it, because they have the power to.
> Verse 2: Rich landowners take the fields and houses away from ordinary farmers, for example through debts, high interest, or judges who took bribes. In Israel, the land was an inheritance from God. Every family was to keep its own piece of land (Leviticus 25:23). Whoever took a family’s land took away what they needed to live.
> This reminds us of the story of Naboth’s vineyard (1 Kings 21) and of the commandment that you must not want your neighbor’s house for yourself (Exodus 20:17).
> Verses 3–5: The punishment fits the guilt: Whoever takes land from others will lose his own land. When the land is divided again by lot, they will get no share.

## “Don’t prophesy like that!” (Verses 6–11)

[6] “Don’t prophesy!” That is what they prophesy.
“Don’t prophesy about these things.
Disgrace won’t catch up with us.”
[7] Should this be said, house of Jacob:
“Is the Spirit of the LORD angry?
Are these the things he does?”
Don’t my words do good
to the one who lives an honest life?
[8] But lately my people have risen up like an enemy.
You tear the robe and the clothes
from people who pass by without worry,
who are coming home from battle.
[9] You drive the women of my people
out of their lovely houses.
You take my blessing away from their little children forever.
[10] Get up and go away!
For this is not your place of rest.
It is unclean, and that destroys,
with a terrible destruction.
[11] Suppose a man came who lives by lies.
Suppose he lied and said,
“I will prophesy to you about wine and strong drink.”
He would be the right prophet for this people!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: The powerful do not want to hear Micah’s message: “Don’t prophesy! … Disgrace won’t catch up with us.” That means: Nothing will happen to us. We already know this from Amos (Amos 7:12–13).
> Verse 7: God answers: My words are good for everyone who lives an honest life. Only those who do wrong feel threatened by them.
> Verses 8–9: What the rich do: They rob people who suspect nothing, even soldiers coming home. They drive widows and women out of their houses. They take the future away from the children.
> Verse 10: They are told: You yourselves will be driven out of the land.
> Verse 11: With sharp mockery: The people want a preacher who promises them wine and beer, that is, pleasant things. They do not want to hear the truth.
> For today: Whoever drives people out of their homes, takes advantage of the weak, and only wants to hear pleasant messages should take Micah’s words seriously.

## The shepherd gathers his flock (Verses 12–13)

[12] I will surely gather all of you, Jacob.
I will surely bring together the rest of Israel.
I will put them together like the sheep of Bozrah,
like a flock in the middle of its pasture.
The place will be full of people.
[13] The one who breaks open the way goes up ahead of them.
They break through the gate and go out.
Their king goes on ahead of them,
with the LORD leading them.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the words of judgment there is a promise: God will gather the rest of his people, as a shepherd gathers his sheep. Bozrah was a city in Edom, known for its flocks of sheep.
> Verse 13: God himself is the “one who breaks open the way.” Like a leader, he breaks open the gate and leads his people into freedom. As at the time of leaving Egypt, God goes at the front.
> Some scholars also read these verses as words of the false prophets, who promise cheap comfort. Most understand them as a real promise from God.
