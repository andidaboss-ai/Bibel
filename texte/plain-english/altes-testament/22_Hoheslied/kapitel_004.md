# Song of Songs – Chapter 4
### You are all beautiful, my love

---

## A song about her beauty (Verses 1–7)

[1] Look, you are beautiful, my love.
Look, you are beautiful.
Your eyes are like doves behind your veil.
Your hair is like a flock of goats
coming down from Mount Gilead.
[2] Your teeth are like a flock of sheep just shorn,
coming up from the washing.
Each of them has twins,
and none of them has lost its young.
[3] Your lips are like a scarlet thread.
Your mouth is lovely.
Your temples are like a piece of a pomegranate
behind your veil.
[4] Your neck is like the tower of David,
built as an armory,
on which a thousand shields hang,
all the shields of the mighty men.
[5] Your two breasts are like two fawns,
twins of a gazelle,
that graze among the lilies.
[6] Until the day is cool and the shadows flee away,
I will go to the mountain of myrrh,
to the hill of frankincense.
[7] You are all beautiful, my love.
There is no flaw in you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The man describes the beauty of the woman he loves from head to toe. In Arabic, such songs are called “wasf,” a song of description. The pictures sound strange to us, but back then they were very beautiful:
> Her black hair flows like a flock of black goats moving down a mountain. Her teeth are white like freshly washed sheep, and complete, none is missing. Her neck is slim and proud like a tower, decorated with necklaces as if with shields.
> Verse 7: “You are all beautiful, my love. There is no flaw in you.” That is how love sees the other person. Christians have also applied this verse to the church, which Christ loves (Ephesians 5:27).

---

## My sister, my bride (Verses 8–11)

[8] Come with me from Lebanon, my bride,
with me from Lebanon!
Look down from the top of Amana,
from the top of Senir and Hermon,
from the dens of the lions,
from the mountains of the leopards.
[9] You have stolen my heart, my sister, my bride.
You have stolen my heart with a single glance of your eyes,
with a single chain of your necklace.
[10] How beautiful is your love, my sister, my bride!
How much better is your love than wine,
and the scent of your perfumes than all spices!
[11] Your lips, my bride, drip like the honeycomb.
Honey and milk are under your tongue.
The scent of your clothes is like the scent of Lebanon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: The high mountains in the north with wild animals are a picture that she seems far away and out of reach to him. He calls her to come to him.
> Verse 9: “My sister, my bride”: In the ancient Middle East, “sister” was a tender pet name for the woman one loved. It does not mean that they are related. It expresses great closeness and trust.
> A single glance from her has stolen his heart.
> Verse 11: Honey and milk remind us of the promised land, the land flowing with milk and honey. For him, she is like a paradise.

---

## A locked garden (Verses 12–16)

[12] My sister, my bride, is a locked garden,
a locked spring, a sealed fountain.
[13] Your shoots are a garden full of pomegranates
with precious fruits,
henna with nard,
[14] nard and saffron,
calamus and cinnamon,
with every kind of incense tree,
myrrh and aloes,
with all the finest spices,
[15] a spring in the gardens,
a well of living water,
flowing streams from Lebanon.
[16] Wake up, north wind, and come, south wind!
Blow on my garden,
so that its scents may flow out.
Let my beloved come into his garden
and eat its precious fruits.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The woman he loves is like a garden with a wall around it. Not everyone may go in. This is a tender picture that she is keeping herself for the one she loves.
> Verses 13–15: The garden is full of precious plants and spices from all over the world. It even has its own spring. A paradise.
> Verse 16: Now the woman answers: She invites the one she loves to come into “his” garden. She opens herself to him of her own free will. Love goes both ways and is freely given.
> The Song of Songs speaks tenderly and openly about bodily love as something beautiful that God has given.
