# Song of Songs – Chapter 2
### The winter is past

---

## A lily among thorns (Verses 1–7)

[1] I am a rose of Sharon,
a lily of the valleys.
[2] As a lily among thorns,
so is my love among the young women.
[3] As an apple tree among the trees of the forest,
so is my beloved among the young men.
I sat in his shade with great delight,
and his fruit was sweet to my taste.
[4] He brought me to the banquet hall,
and his banner over me is love.
[5] Strengthen me with raisins,
refresh me with apples,
for I am faint with love.
[6] His left hand is under my head,
and his right hand embraces me.
[7] I make you promise, daughters of Jerusalem,
by the gazelles or by the female deer of the field,
that you do not stir up or awaken love
until it is ready.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The woman humbly calls herself a simple flower, one of many on the plain of Sharon.
> Verse 2: He answers: To me you are like a lily among nothing but thorns. No one is like you.
> Verse 3: She answers: And you are like an apple tree in the middle of the forest, giving shade and sweet fruit.
> Verse 4: “His banner over me is love”: A banner shows whom you belong to. His love is like a flag over her.
> Verse 5: “Faint with love”: Anyone who has been in love knows the feeling.
> Verse 7: A sentence that appears three times in the book: Don’t awaken love too early! Love needs its time. You should not force it.

---

## Get up, my love (Verses 8–17)

[8] The voice of my beloved!
Look, he is coming,
leaping over the mountains, skipping over the hills.
[9] My beloved is like a gazelle or a young deer.
Look, he is standing behind our wall!
He looks in through the windows.
He peeks through the lattice.
[10] My beloved spoke and said to me,
“Get up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away!
[11] For look, the winter is past.
The rain is over and gone.
[12] The flowers appear on the earth.
The time of singing has come,
and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.
[13] The fig tree ripens its green figs.
The vines are in blossom.
They give off their scent.
Get up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away!”
[14] My dove in the clefts of the rock,
in the hiding places on the mountainside,
let me see your face,
let me hear your voice,
for your voice is sweet, and your face is lovely.
[15] Catch the foxes for us,
the little foxes that ruin the vineyards,
for our vineyards are in blossom.
[16] My beloved is mine, and I am his.
He grazes among the lilies.
[17] Until the day is cool and the shadows flee away,
turn, my beloved, and be like a gazelle
or a young deer on the mountains of Bether.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–9: The man she loves comes leaping with joy like a young gazelle. Impatiently, he looks through the window.
> Verses 10–13: One of the most beautiful spring poems in the world: The winter is past, the flowers are blooming, the birds are singing. Come with me! Love is like a new spring.
> Verse 15: Little foxes eat the young grapes. Perhaps this means: Small troubles can ruin a young love. Watch out for them!
> Verse 16: “My beloved is mine, and I am his”: This sentence is often engraved in wedding rings. It expresses that they belong to each other.
> Verse 17: “On the mountains of Bether”: The name is uncertain, perhaps “rugged mountains.”
