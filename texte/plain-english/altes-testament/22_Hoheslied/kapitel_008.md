# Song of Songs – Chapter 8
### Love is as strong as death

---

## If only you were my brother (Verses 1–4)

[1] Oh, if only you were like my brother,
who nursed at my mother’s breasts!
If I found you outside, I would kiss you,
yes, and no one would look down on me.
[2] I would lead you and bring you into my mother’s house,
who would teach me.
I would give you spiced wine to drink,
the juice of my pomegranate.
[3] His left hand would be under my head,
and his right hand would embrace me.
[4] I make you promise, daughters of Jerusalem,
that you do not stir up or awaken love
until it is ready.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Back then, a couple was not allowed to show their love in public. But brothers and sisters were allowed to kiss in the street. So she wishes: If only you were my brother, then I could kiss you anywhere!
> Verse 4: For the third and last time, the warning: Don’t awaken love before it is ready by itself.

---

## Love is as strong as death (Verses 5–7)

[5] Who is this coming up from the wilderness,
leaning on her beloved?
Beloved: Under the apple tree I awakened you.
There your mother conceived you.
There she was in labor and gave birth to you.
[6] Put me like a seal on your heart,
like a seal on your arm,
for love is as strong as death.
Its jealous passion is as cruel as Sheol, the world of the dead.
Its flames are flames of fire,
a flame of Yah.
[7] Many waters cannot put out love,
and rivers cannot drown it.
If someone gave all the wealth of his house for love,
he would only be scorned.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: The couple comes out of the wilderness together. She leans on him. Under the apple tree, where life begins, she awakened his love.
> Verses 6–7 are the high point of the whole book and one of the most beautiful texts about love there is. It is often read at weddings.
> “Put me like a seal on your heart”: A seal was worn on a cord over the heart or as a ring. It was very personal and precious. She wants to belong to him forever.
> “Love is as strong as death”: Death defeats everyone. But love is just as strong.
> “A flame of Yah”: Here we find the short form of God’s name, the only time in the whole book. Love is a fire that comes from God.
> Verse 7: No water can put out this fire. And love cannot be bought. Whoever tries makes a fool of himself.
> Christians also think here of God’s love, which is stronger than death (Romans 8:38–39; 1 Corinthians 13).

## Our little sister (Verses 8–10)

[8] We have a little sister.
She has no breasts yet.
What shall we do for our sister
on the day when someone asks to marry her?
[9] If she is a wall,
we will build a tower of silver on her.
If she is a door,
we will close her in with boards of cedar.
[10] I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers.
Then I was in his eyes like one who finds peace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–9: The brothers talk about their little sister. They want to protect her until she is old enough to marry. “Wall” and “door” are pictures of whether she closes herself off or opens up.
> Verse 10: The woman answers with confidence: I am grown up now. I decide for myself. And I have found peace with the one I love. In Hebrew, this is a play on words: “shalom” (peace), “Shulammite” and “Solomon.”
> Young people need protection. But adults may decide for themselves about their love.

---

## My own vineyard (Verses 11–14)

[11] Solomon had a vineyard at Baal Hamon.
He rented out the vineyard to keepers.
Each was to bring a thousand pieces of silver for its fruit.
[12] My own vineyard is before me.
The thousand are for you, Solomon,
and two hundred for those who tend its fruit.
[13] You who live in the gardens,
the friends are listening.
Let me hear your voice!
[14] Hurry, my beloved,
and be like a gazelle or a young deer
on the mountains of spices!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: Solomon has a huge vineyard that brings in a lot of money. But the woman says: My vineyard, that is, I myself, belongs to me. I give myself to whom I want. He can keep his wealth. Love cannot be bought (compare verse 7).
> Verses 13–14: The book ends open: He asks to hear her voice. She calls him to come to her. The longing goes on. Love is never completely “finished.”

## Looking back: What have we read in the Song of Songs?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The most beautiful song:** The Song of Songs is a collection of love poems. A woman and a man praise each other, look for each other and find each other.
> **The woman speaks the most:** Amazing for such an old book: The woman has the most words. She is confident, takes the first step and decides for herself about her love.
> **Love that goes both ways:** “My beloved is mine, and I am his” (2:16; 6:3; 7:10). Neither rules over the other. Their love is equal.
> **The body is good:** The book speaks openly and tenderly about beauty and bodily love. The Bible sees love between a man and a woman as God’s good gift.
> **Don’t awaken love too early:** This warning comes three times (2:7; 3:5; 8:4). Love needs time and freedom. It cannot be forced and cannot be bought.
> **Strong as death:** The high point: “Love is as strong as death. … Many waters cannot put out love” (8:6–7).
> **A picture of God’s love:** Jews read the Song of Songs at Passover as a song of love between God and Israel. Christians have seen in it the love between Christ and his church. Both ways of understanding add to the literal reading.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the book of Isaiah, the first of the great books of the prophets. It speaks of judgment and hope, of the Prince of Peace and of the suffering Servant of God.
