# Song of Songs – Chapter 6
### I belong to my beloved

---

## Where is your beloved? (Verses 1–3)

[1] Where has your beloved gone,
most beautiful among women?
Which way has your beloved turned,
so that we may look for him with you?
[2] My beloved has gone down to his garden,
to the beds of spices,
to graze his flock in the gardens
and to gather lilies.
[3] I belong to my beloved, and my beloved belongs to me.
He grazes among the lilies.

> **What does this mean?**
> The women offer to help her search. But the woman now knows where he is: in “his garden,” that is, with her.
> Verse 3: As in chapter 2:16, but the other way around: “I belong to my beloved, and my beloved belongs to me.” This sentence is often engraved in wedding rings, in Hebrew “Ani le-dodi ve-dodi li.”

---

## One of a kind (Verses 4–10)

[4] You are beautiful, my love, like Tirzah,
lovely like Jerusalem,
awe-inspiring like an army with banners.
[5] Turn your eyes away from me,
for they have overwhelmed me.
Your hair is like a flock of goats
lying along the slopes of Gilead.
[6] Your teeth are like a flock of ewes
coming up from the washing.
Each of them has twins,
and none of them has lost its young.
[7] Your temples are like a piece of a pomegranate
behind your veil.
[8] There are 60 queens, 80 concubines,
and young women without number.
[9] My dove, my perfect one, is one of a kind.
She is her mother’s only daughter.
She is the favorite of the woman who gave birth to her.
The daughters saw her and called her blessed.
The queens and the concubines saw her and praised her.
[10] Who is she who looks down like the morning,
beautiful as the moon, clear as the sun,
awe-inspiring like an army with banners?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Tirzah and Jerusalem were two splendid capital cities. That is how beautiful she is. And as impressive as an army with flags: Her beauty has power.
> Verse 5: One glance from her overwhelms him.
> Verses 5–7 repeat chapter 4:1–3 almost word for word. His love does not change.
> Verses 8–9: A king may have many wives. But for him there is only one: her. True love sees something one of a kind in the other person.
> Verse 10: She shines like the dawn, the moon and the sun.

---

## In the nut grove (Verses 11–13)

[11] I went down to the nut grove
to see the green plants of the valley,
to see whether the vine had budded
and whether the pomegranates were in flower.
[12] Before I knew it,
my desire set me among the chariots of my noble people.
[13] Come back, come back, Shulammite!
Come back, come back, so that we may look at you!
Lover: Why do you want to look at the Shulammite
as at the dance of Mahanaim?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: In spring, she goes into the garden to see whether everything is in bloom. A picture of love that is waking up.
> Verse 12 is one of the hardest verses in Hebrew. No one knows exactly what it means. Perhaps: Her longing carries her away like a fast chariot.
> Verse 13: Here the woman is named for the first time: “Shulammite.” Perhaps this means “the woman from Shunem” or “the peaceful one,” similar to the name Solomon (both are connected with “shalom,” peace).
> The people call out: Turn around, we want to see you! The man she loves asks: Why are you staring at her as at a dance? “Mahanaim” is a place name and means “two camps.” What exactly this dance was is unknown.
> In some Bibles, and in the Hebrew text, verse 13 is the first verse of chapter 7 (7:1).
