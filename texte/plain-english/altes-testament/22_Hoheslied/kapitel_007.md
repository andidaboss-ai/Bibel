# Song of Songs – Chapter 7
### Come, my beloved

---

## How beautiful you are (Verses 1–9)

[1] How beautiful are your feet in sandals, prince’s daughter!
The curves of your thighs are like jewels,
the work of the hands of an artist.
[2] Your body is like a round bowl
that never lacks mixed wine.
Your waist is like a heap of wheat,
surrounded by lilies.
[3] Your two breasts are like two fawns,
twins of a gazelle.
[4] Your neck is like a tower of ivory.
Your eyes are like the pools in Heshbon
by the gate of Bath Rabbim.
Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon
that looks toward Damascus.
[5] Your head on you is like Carmel.
The hair of your head is like purple.
The king is held captive in its locks.
[6] How beautiful and how pleasant you are,
love, full of delights!
[7] Your figure is like a palm tree,
and your breasts are like its fruit.
[8] I said, “I will climb the palm tree.
I will take hold of its fruit.”
Let your breasts be like clusters of grapes on the vine,
and the scent of your breath like apples.
[9] Your mouth is like the best wine,
that goes down smoothly for my beloved,
gliding over the lips of those who are asleep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again a song of description, this time from the feet to the head. Perhaps the woman is dancing, and the man admires her.
> The pictures come from nature and from the landscape: pools, towers, Mount Carmel, a palm tree. To us, “your nose is like a tower” sounds strange. Back then it meant: proud, noble, upright.
> Verse 5: “The king is held captive in its locks”: This means the locks of her hair. A loving sentence. He is completely enchanted by her.
> The Song of Songs speaks openly and tenderly about the beauty of the body and about bodily love. The Bible does not see the body as dirty, but as God’s good creation.

---

## Let’s go out into the field (Verses 10–13)

[10] I belong to my beloved.
His desire is for me.
[11] Come, my beloved!
Let’s go out into the field.
Let’s spend the night in the villages.
[12] Let’s go up early to the vineyards.
Let’s see whether the vine has budded,
whether its blossom has opened,
and whether the pomegranates are in flower.
There I will give you my love.
[13] The mandrakes give off their scent.
At our doors are all kinds of precious fruits,
new and old,
which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “His desire is for me.” A remarkable sentence: In Genesis 3:16, after the fall into sin, it says that the woman’s desire will be for the man, and he will rule over her. Here it is the other way around, and there is no talk of ruling. In the Song of Songs, love is equal and goes both ways, as in paradise.
> Verses 11–12: The woman takes the first step. She invites him to go out into nature with her.
> Verse 13: Mandrakes were thought to be plants that encourage love (compare Genesis 30:14–16).
> In many Bibles, for example in Hebrew and German, the verses of this chapter are shifted by one, because 6:13 is 7:1 there.
