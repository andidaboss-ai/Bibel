# Deuteronomy – Chapter 9
### Not because you are righteous – the golden calf

---

## God goes ahead of you (Verses 1–3)

[1] Hear, Israel!
Today you are crossing over the Jordan
to drive out nations that are greater and mightier than you,
great cities with walls up to the sky,
[2] a great and tall people, the sons of the Anakim.
You know them, and you have heard people say,
“Who can stand against the sons of Anak?”
[3] So know this today:
The LORD your God is the one who crosses over ahead of you like a consuming fire.
He will destroy them, and he will bring them down before you.
So you will drive them out and make them die out quickly, as the LORD has told you.

> **What does this mean?**
> 38 years ago the people were afraid of the Anakim, the “giants.” That is why they did not want to go into the land (Chapter 1). Now Moses says: Do not be afraid. God himself goes ahead of you.

---

## Not because of your righteousness (Verses 4–6)

[4] When the LORD your God has driven them out before you,
do not say in your heart,
“The LORD has brought me in to take this land as my own because I am righteous.”
For it is because of the wickedness of these nations that the LORD is driving them out before you.
[5] It is not because you are righteous, and not because your heart is honest,
that you are going in to take their land as your own.
It is because of the wickedness of these nations that the LORD your God is driving them out before you,
and so that he keeps the word that the LORD swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.
[6] So know this:
It is not because you are righteous that the LORD your God is giving you this good land to own.
For you are a stiff-necked people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses warns against pride. Israel should not think: We are getting the land because we are so good. That is not true.
> Moses gives two reasons: First, the wickedness of the peoples in the land. God had already told Abraham that he was waiting until their guilt was full (Genesis 15:16). Second, God’s faithfulness to his promise.
> “A stiff-necked people” means: a stubborn people. The picture comes from cattle that make their necks stiff and do not want to be led.
> It is honest that a holy book speaks this way about its own people. The Bible does not celebrate Israel as heroes. Everything is grace.

---

## Remember: At Horeb (Verses 7–11)

[7] Remember, and do not forget,
how you made the LORD your God angry in the wilderness.
From the day you left the land of Egypt
until you came to this place,
you have rebelled against the LORD.
[8] At Horeb, too, you made the LORD angry.
And the LORD was so angry with you that he wanted to destroy you.
[9] I had gone up onto the mountain to receive the stone tablets,
the tablets of the covenant that the LORD had made with you.
Then I stayed on the mountain for forty days and forty nights.
I did not eat bread, and I did not drink water.
[10] The LORD gave me the two stone tablets, written with God’s finger.
On them were all the words that the LORD had spoken with you on the mountain out of the middle of the fire,
on the day of the assembly.
[11] At the end of the forty days and forty nights,
the LORD gave me the two stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses reminds them of the worst hour at Mount Sinai. The story is in Exodus 32–34.
> “Written with God’s finger”: God himself wrote the Ten Commandments.

---

## The golden calf (Verses 12–17)

[12] The LORD said to me,
“Get up, and go down from here quickly.
For your people, whom you brought out of Egypt, have ruined themselves.
They have quickly turned away from the way that I commanded them.
They have made themselves an idol out of melted metal!”
[13] And the LORD said to me,
“I have seen this people.
Look! They are a stiff-necked people.
[14] Leave me alone, so that I can destroy them and wipe out their name from under the sky.
And I will make you into a nation that is mightier and greater than they are.”
[15] So I turned and came down from the mountain.
The mountain was burning with fire.
The two tablets of the covenant were in my two hands.
[16] I looked, and I saw: You had sinned against the LORD your God.
You had made yourselves a calf out of melted metal.
You had quickly turned away from the way that the LORD had commanded you.
[17] Then I took hold of the two tablets, threw them out of my two hands,
and broke them before your eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only 40 days after the covenant, the people are already worshiping a golden calf. Moses breaks the tablets. That is a sign: The covenant is broken.
> God offers to make a new people out of Moses. But Moses does not accept the offer. He stands up for his people.

---

## Moses prays for the people (Verses 18–21)

[18] I fell down before the LORD, as the first time, for forty days and forty nights.
I did not eat bread, and I did not drink water,
because of all the sin that you had committed
by doing what is evil in the LORD’s eyes, so that you made him angry.
[19] For I was afraid of the anger and the burning rage
with which the LORD was angry with you, to destroy you.
But the LORD listened to me that time too.
[20] The LORD was also so angry with Aaron that he wanted to destroy him.
And at that time I prayed for Aaron too.
[21] Then I took your sin, the calf that you had made, and burned it with fire.
I crushed it and ground it very small, until it was as fine as dust.
And I threw its dust into the brook that flows down from the mountain.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses fasts again for 40 days for his people. He prays, and God listens to him.
> Here we learn something that is not in Exodus 32: Aaron was in great danger too. Moses also prayed for his brother.
> Moses is an example: He stands up for others, even when they are guilty. Praying for others like this is called “intercession.”

---

## Rebellion again and again (Verses 22–24)

[22] At Taberah, at Massah, and at Kibroth Hattaavah you also made the LORD angry.
[23] And when the LORD sent you out from Kadesh Barnea and said,
“Go up and take as your own the land that I have given you,”
you rebelled against the command of the LORD your God.
You did not believe him, and you did not listen to his voice.
[24] You have rebelled against the LORD from the day that I have known you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses lists more places where the people grumbled against God: Taberah, where fire burned (Numbers 11:1–3); Massah, where it was about water (Exodus 17); Kibroth Hattaavah, where it was about meat (Numbers 11).
> That is a hard record. But Moses does not want to shame the people. He wants them to understand: We owe everything only to God’s grace.

---

## Moses’ prayer (Verses 25–29)

[25] So I fell down before the LORD for the forty days and forty nights that I lay there,
because the LORD had said that he would destroy you.
[26] I prayed to the LORD and said,
“Lord GOD, do not destroy your people and your inheritance,
which you set free through your greatness,
which you brought out of Egypt with a mighty hand.
[27] Remember your servants, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
Do not look at the stubbornness of this people, or at their wickedness, or at their sin.
[28] Otherwise the land you brought us out of will say,
‘The LORD was not able to bring them into the land that he had promised them.
And he hated them. That is why he brought them out, to kill them in the wilderness.’
[29] But they are your people and your inheritance,
which you brought out by your great power and by your outstretched arm.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses prays very wisely. He does not say: The people are not really that bad. He admits the guilt. But he reminds God of three things:
> 1. It is your people. You set it free.
> 2. Remember your promises to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
> 3. What will the other peoples think? Your name would be mocked.
> Moses does not point to the righteousness of the people, but only to God’s faithfulness and grace. People can still pray like this today when they have become guilty.
