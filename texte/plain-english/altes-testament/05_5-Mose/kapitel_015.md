# Deuteronomy – Chapter 15
### The year of release – help for the poor

---

## Every seven years debts are canceled (Verses 1–6)

[1] At the end of every seven years, you shall cancel debts.
[2] This is how it shall be done:
Everyone who has lent something shall cancel what he has lent to his neighbor.
He shall not demand payment from his neighbor and his brother,
because a release for the LORD has been announced.
[3] From a foreigner you may demand payment.
But whatever your brother owes you, your hand shall let go of it.
[4] But there will be no poor among you –
for the LORD will surely bless you in the land
that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to own –
[5] if only you really listen to the voice of the LORD your God
and are careful to do this whole commandment that I command you today.
[6] For the LORD your God will bless you, as he promised you.
You will lend to many nations, but you will not need to borrow.
You will rule over many nations, but they will not rule over you.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is an amazing law: Every seven years, debts among Israelites are canceled. No one should stay trapped in debt forever. Everyone gets a new chance.
> Back then, people borrowed money mostly when they were in need, for example after a bad harvest. Whoever was poor should not keep getting poorer.
> Foreigners were mostly traders who did business. The law did not apply to them. It was about helping the poor in their own people.
> Verse 4 is God’s great goal: “There will be no poor among you.” If everyone lives by God’s commandments, there is enough for everyone. The first Christians in Jerusalem took up this verse (Acts 4:34).

---

## Open your hand (Verses 7–11)

[7] There may be a poor man among you, one of your brothers,
in one of your towns in your land that the LORD your God is giving you.
Then you shall not make your heart hard,
and you shall not close your hand to your poor brother.
[8] But you shall open your hand wide to him
and gladly lend him as much as he needs for what he lacks.
[9] Be careful that this evil thought does not come up in your heart:
“The seventh year, the year of release, is near.”
And be careful that you do not look at your poor brother with an evil eye and give him nothing.
Then he will cry out to the LORD against you, and it will be counted as sin for you.
[10] You shall gladly give to him,
and your heart shall not be sorry when you give to him.
For because of this, the LORD your God will bless you in all your work
and in everything you do.
[11] For there will always be poor people in the land.
That is why I command you:
You shall open your hand wide to your brother, to the needy, and to the poor in your land.

> **What does this mean?**
> God knows the human heart. Someone could think: Soon it is the year of release. If I lend something now, I will never get it back. Moses says: Do not think like that! Give anyway, and give gladly.
> It is not only about giving, but about your attitude: You should not give grudgingly or stingily, but with an open heart. Paul later says: God loves someone who gives with joy (2 Corinthians 9:7).
> Verse 4 and verse 11 seem to contradict each other: “There will be no poor among you” and “there will always be poor people in the land.” But they belong together: God’s goal is that there is no poverty. But because people do not always live by God’s will, there will be poor people. That is why we should help. Jesus quotes verse 11 (Mark 14:7).

## Hebrew servants (Verses 12–18)

[12] Your brother, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, may be sold to you
and serve you for six years.
Then in the seventh year you shall let him go free.
[13] And when you let him go free,
you shall not let him go away with empty hands.
[14] You shall give him plenty from your flock, from your threshing floor, and from your winepress.
As the LORD your God has blessed you, so you shall give to him.
[15] Remember that you yourself were a slave in the land of Egypt,
and that the LORD your God set you free.
That is why I am commanding you this today.
[16] But he may say to you, “I do not want to leave you,”
because he loves you and your household, because things go well for him with you.
[17] Then you shall take an awl and push it through his ear into the door.
Then he will be your servant forever.
You shall do the same with your female servant.
[18] It should not seem hard to you to let him go free.
For he has served you for six years and was worth twice as much as a hired worker.
And the LORD your God will bless you in everything you do.

> **What does this mean?**
> Someone who had become completely poor could “sell” himself as a servant to work off his debts. That was a kind of slavery for debt. But it was limited: After six years, you were free again.
> New compared with Exodus 21: Here the rule clearly applies to women too. And the master should not send the servant away with empty hands. He should give him plenty to take along, so that he can start a new life.
> The reason is again remembering: You yourselves were slaves. God set you free. So treat others the way God has treated you.
> An “awl” is a pointed tool for making holes. The hole in the ear was a sign: This person has chosen freely to stay.
> Even limited slavery does not match what we understand today by human dignity. But compared with other peoples back then, this law was a great protection for the poor.

## The firstborn animals (Verses 19–23)

[19] You shall set apart for the LORD your God all the firstborn males
that are born in your herd and in your flock.
You shall not work with the firstborn of your herd,
and you shall not shear the firstborn of your flock.
[20] You shall eat it before the LORD your God every year,
at the place that the LORD will choose, you and your household.
[21] But if it has a defect, if it is lame or blind,
or has any other serious defect,
you shall not sacrifice it to the LORD your God.
[22] You shall eat it in your towns.
The unclean and the clean may eat it alike,
as they eat the gazelle and the deer.
[23] Only you shall not eat its blood.
You shall pour it out on the ground like water.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first young animal belongs to God. That is a sign of thanks: All life comes from him.
> God gets only the best. You should not sacrifice an animal with a defect. But you may eat it at home in the ordinary way.
