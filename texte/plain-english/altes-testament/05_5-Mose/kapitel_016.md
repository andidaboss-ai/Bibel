# Deuteronomy – Chapter 16
### The three great feasts – fair judges

---

## The Passover (Verses 1–8)

[1] Pay attention to the month of Abib.
Keep the Passover for the LORD your God.
For in the month of Abib the LORD your God brought you out of Egypt by night.
[2] You shall kill the Passover sacrifice for the LORD your God.
It shall come from the flock and from the herd.
You shall do it at the place that the LORD will choose,
to let his name live there.
[3] You shall not eat bread made with yeast with it.
For seven days you shall eat bread without yeast with it.
It is the bread of hardship,
because you left the land of Egypt in a great hurry.
Then you will remember all the days of your life
the day when you came out of the land of Egypt.
[4] For seven days no yeast shall be seen with you anywhere in your land.
And none of the meat that you sacrifice on the evening of the first day
shall be left over through the night until the morning.
[5] You may not kill the Passover sacrifice in just any of your towns
that the LORD your God gives you.
[6] You shall do it only at the place that the LORD your God will choose,
to let his name live there.
There you shall kill the Passover sacrifice in the evening, when the sun goes down,
at the same time of day that you came out of Egypt.
[7] You shall roast it and eat it at the place that the LORD your God chooses.
In the morning you shall go back to your tents.
[8] For six days you shall eat bread without yeast.
On the seventh day there shall be a special gathering for the LORD your God.
You shall do no work.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Abib” means “young ears of grain.” It is the spring month, March or April. Later it was called “Nisan.”
> The three great feasts of Israel are named here once more: Passover, the Feast of Weeks, and the Feast of Booths (see also Leviticus 23).
> What is new: Passover should no longer be celebrated at home as in Egypt, but at the one place that God chooses. Later this was Jerusalem. That is why Jesus also went to Jerusalem for Passover.
> “The bread of hardship”: The bread without yeast reminds people of the suffering in Egypt and of the hurry when they left. There was no time to let the dough rise.
> “Roast”: The Hebrew has a word that usually means “cook.” According to Exodus 12:9, the Passover lamb was to be roasted over the fire. The WEB, our English source, translates “roast” here.

## The Feast of Weeks (Verses 9–12)

[9] You shall count seven weeks for yourselves.
Begin to count the seven weeks
from the day you first put the sickle to the grain in the field.
A sickle is a curved knife for cutting grain.
[10] Then you shall keep the Feast of Weeks for the LORD your God.
Bring a gift from your hand that you give freely.
Give as much as the LORD your God blesses you.
[11] And you shall be glad before the LORD your God:
you, your son and your daughter,
your male servant and your female servant,
the Levite who lives in your town,
and the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow who live among you.
Do this at the place that the LORD your God will choose,
to let his name live there.
[12] Remember that you were a slave in Egypt.
Be careful to keep these rules and do them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Feast of Weeks is the harvest feast for the wheat, seven weeks after the barley harvest begins. In Hebrew it is called “Shavuot.” In Christianity it became Pentecost.
> The joy should be shared with everyone. Again and again the laws about the feasts name the same groups: children, male and female servants, Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows. At God’s feast no one should be left alone.
> “Remember that you were a slave in Egypt”: Whoever knows what it is like to be poor and oppressed should be generous.

## The Feast of Booths (Verses 13–17)

[13] You shall keep the Feast of Booths for seven days,
after you have brought in the harvest from your threshing floor and from your winepress.
[14] And you shall be glad at your feast:
you, your son and your daughter,
your male servant and your female servant,
the Levite, the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow who live in your towns.
[15] For seven days you shall keep a feast for the LORD your God
at the place that the LORD chooses.
For the LORD your God will bless you in all your harvest
and in all the work of your hands.
And you shall be completely joyful.
[16] Three times a year all your men shall come before the LORD your God
at the place that he chooses:
at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Booths.
They shall not come before the LORD with empty hands.
[17] Every man shall give as much as he can,
according to the blessing that the LORD your God has given you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Feast of Booths is in the fall, after the harvest of grapes and olives. People live for seven days in huts made of branches. This reminds them of the time in the wilderness.
> “You shall be completely joyful”: That is a commandment! God orders his people to be glad.
> Everyone gives what he can. No one has to give more than he has. Whoever has received much gives much. Whoever has little gives little.

## Fair judges (Verses 18–20)

[18] You shall appoint judges and officials for each of your tribes
in all your towns that the LORD your God gives you.
They shall judge the people fairly.
[19] You shall not twist justice.
You shall not favor anyone.
You shall not take a bribe.
For a bribe makes the eyes of wise people blind,
and it twists the words of people who do right.
[20] You shall follow what is completely fair, only what is fair.
Then you will live and take over the land that the LORD your God gives you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “What is completely fair, only what is fair”: The Hebrew has the word “justice” twice in a row: “Tsedek, tsedek.” This shows how important it is. The WEB, our English source, says: “that which is altogether just.”
> A bribe makes people blind. Even wise and good people are influenced by gifts, often without noticing it.
> These verses are part of the foundation for independent courts.

---

## No sacred poles and stones (Verses 21–22)

[21] You shall not plant any kind of tree as an Asherah pole
beside the altar of the LORD your God that you will make for yourself.
[22] And you shall not set up a sacred stone pillar for yourself.
The LORD your God hates such things.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Canaanites put wooden poles for the goddess Asherah and stone pillars for their gods next to their altars. Israel should not mix the worship of the LORD with these signs.
