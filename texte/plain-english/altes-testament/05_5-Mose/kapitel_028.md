# Deuteronomy – Chapter 28
### Blessing and curse

---

## The blessing (Verses 1–14)

[1] If you really listen to the voice of the LORD your God,
and are careful to do all his commandments that I command you today,
then the LORD your God will set you high above all the nations of the earth.
[2] All these blessings will come to you and reach you,
if you listen to the voice of the LORD your God:
[3] You will be blessed in the city,
and you will be blessed in the field.
[4] Your children will be blessed,
and the crops of your ground, and the young of your animals,
the calves of your cattle and the lambs of your flock.
[5] Your basket will be blessed,
and the bowl in which you knead your dough.
[6] You will be blessed when you come in,
and you will be blessed when you go out.
[7] The LORD will make your enemies who rise up against you
be defeated in front of you.
They will come out against you on one road,
but they will run away from you on seven roads.
[8] The LORD will command his blessing to be with you in your barns
and in everything you do.
He will bless you in the land that the LORD your God gives you.
[9] The LORD will make you a holy people for himself,
as he has sworn to you,
if you keep the commandments of the LORD your God
and walk in his ways.
[10] All the peoples of the earth will see
that you are called by the name of the LORD,
and they will be afraid of you.
[11] The LORD will give you great riches:
many children, many young animals, and good crops from your ground,
in the land that the LORD swore to your fathers to give you.
[12] The LORD will open his good storehouse in the sky for you,
to give rain to your land at the right time
and to bless all the work of your hands.
You will lend to many nations,
but you will not need to borrow.
[13] The LORD will make you the head and not the tail.
You will always be on top,
and you will not be at the bottom.
This will happen if you listen to the commandments of the LORD your God
that I command you today,
and if you are careful to do them.
[14] Do not turn away from any of the words that I command you today,
not to the right and not to the left,
to follow other gods and serve them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, a covenant often ended with blessing and curse. It was like that in treaties between kings too. Moses sets it before the people’s eyes: What happens if you stay faithful to God? And what happens if you leave him?
> The blessing covers all of life: city and countryside, children and animals, food and work, coming and going.
> “You will be blessed when you come in, and you will be blessed when you go out”: These words are also found in Psalm 121:8, and they are still spoken as a blessing today.
> The basket is for the harvest, the bowl for the bread dough. God also blesses everyday life in the kitchen.

---

## The curse: Everything becomes hard (Verses 15–26)

[15] But if you do not listen to the voice of the LORD your God,
and are not careful to do all his commandments and his rules
that I command you today,
then all these curses will come on you and reach you:
[16] You will be cursed in the city,
and you will be cursed in the field.
[17] Your basket will be cursed,
and the bowl in which you knead your dough.
[18] Your children will be cursed,
and the crops of your ground,
the calves of your cattle and the lambs of your flock.
[19] You will be cursed when you come in,
and you will be cursed when you go out.
[20] The LORD will send on you curse, confusion, and punishment
in everything you try to do,
until you are destroyed and quickly die,
because of the evil things you have done
when you turned away from me.
[21] The LORD will make plague cling to you,
until he has wiped you out from the land
that you are going into to take over.
[22] The LORD will strike you with wasting disease, with fever, with swelling,
with burning heat, with the sword,
with blight and with mold in your grain.
They will chase you until you die.
[23] The sky over your head will be like bronze,
and the ground under you will be like iron.
[24] The LORD will turn the rain of your land into powder and dust.
It will come down on you from the sky
until you are destroyed.
[25] The LORD will let you be defeated in front of your enemies.
You will go out against them on one road,
but you will run away from them on seven roads.
You will be tossed back and forth among all the kingdoms of the earth.
[26] Your dead bodies will be food for all the birds of the sky
and for the wild animals of the earth.
And there will be no one to scare them away.

> **What does this mean?**
> The curse is the exact opposite of the blessing. The sentences are repeated almost word for word, only with “cursed” instead of “blessed.”
> “The sky over your head will be like bronze, and the ground under you will be like iron”: A strong picture for drought. No rain falls, and the ground becomes hard and produces nothing.
> The part with the curse is much longer than the part with the blessing. That is a serious warning. Moses does not want to torment the people. He wants with all his strength to keep them from leaving God.

---

## Sickness, blindness, oppression (Verses 27–37)

[27] The LORD will strike you with the boils of Egypt,
with tumors, with sores, and with itching,
and you will not be able to be healed from them.
[28] The LORD will strike you with madness, with blindness,
and with confusion of heart.
[29] At noon you will feel your way around,
like a blind man feels his way in the dark.
Nothing you do will succeed.
You will always be oppressed and robbed,
and there will be no one to save you.
[30] You will be promised a wife in marriage,
but another man will sleep with her.
You will build a house,
but you will not live in it.
You will plant a vineyard,
but you will not enjoy its fruit.
[31] Your ox will be killed in front of your eyes,
but you will not eat any of it.
Your donkey will be taken from you by force right in front of you,
and it will not be given back to you.
Your sheep will be given to your enemies,
and there will be no one to save you.
[32] Your sons and your daughters will be given to another people.
Your eyes will watch for them all day long
and grow tired with longing,
but you will be powerless to do anything.
[33] A nation that you do not know will eat the crops of your ground
and everything you worked for.
You will always be oppressed and crushed.
[34] The things that you see with your eyes
will drive you mad.
[35] The LORD will strike you on the knees and on the legs
with painful boils that cannot be healed,
from the soles of your feet to the top of your head.
[36] The LORD will bring you and your king, whom you will set over you,
to a nation that neither you nor your fathers have known.
There you will serve other gods made of wood and stone.
[37] You will become a horror, a saying, and a joke
among all the peoples where the LORD will lead you away.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 30 is the opposite of 20:5–7: There, men with a new house, a new vineyard, or a bride-to-be were allowed to go home to enjoy their happiness. Here everything is taken from them.
> Verse 36 says ahead of time that the people will be led away with their king into a foreign land. That really happened later: In the year 586 BC, King Zedekiah was taken to Babylon with many people (2 Kings 25).

---

## Work without fruit (Verses 38–48)

[38] You will carry much seed out into the field,
but you will gather in little,
because the locusts will eat it up.
Locusts are large insects that come in huge swarms.
[39] You will plant vineyards and take care of them,
but you will not drink the wine or gather the grapes,
because worms will eat them.
[40] You will have olive trees everywhere in your land,
but you will not rub yourself with the oil,
because your olives will drop off.
[41] You will have sons and daughters,
but they will not stay yours,
because they will be taken away as prisoners.
[42] Locusts will eat up all your trees
and the crops of your ground.
[43] The foreigner who lives among you will rise higher and higher above you,
and you will sink lower and lower.
[44] He will lend to you,
but you will not lend to him.
He will be the head,
and you will be the tail.
[45] All these curses will come on you.
They will chase you and catch up with you until you are destroyed,
because you did not listen to the voice of the LORD your God,
to keep his commandments and his rules that he commanded you.
[46] They will be a sign and a warning
on you and on your descendants forever.
[47] You did not serve the LORD your God with joy and with a glad heart,
even though you had plenty of everything.
[48] That is why you will serve your enemies,
whom the LORD will send against you.
You will serve them in hunger, in thirst, in nakedness,
and in need of everything.
He will put an iron yoke on your neck
until he has destroyed you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 47 is especially remarkable: The reason for the disaster is not only disobedience, but that the people did not serve God “with joy,” even though they had plenty of everything. God does not only want obedience, but a thankful, joyful heart.
> A “yoke” is a wooden bar that is put on the necks of cattle so that they can pull. An “iron yoke” is a picture for very harsh oppression.

## A foreign nation and the siege (Verses 49–57)

[49] The LORD will bring a nation against you from far away,
from the end of the earth,
as fast as an eagle flies.
It will be a nation whose language you will not understand,
[50] a nation with a fierce face,
that has no respect for the old
and no mercy for the young.
[51] They will eat the young of your animals and the crops of your ground,
until you are destroyed.
They will not leave you any grain, new wine, or oil,
no calves of your cattle and no lambs of your flock,
until they have wiped you out.
[52] They will lay siege to you in all your towns,
until your high, strong walls that you trusted in
fall down everywhere in your land.
They will lay siege to you in all your towns
everywhere in the land that the LORD your God has given you.
[53] During the siege and the great distress
that your enemies will bring on you,
you will eat your own children,
the flesh of your sons and of your daughters,
whom the LORD your God has given you.
[54] The man among you who is most gentle and very sensitive
will turn mean and stingy toward his brother,
toward the wife he loves,
and toward the rest of his children who are still left.
[55] He will not give any of them any of the flesh of his children that he is eating,
because he has nothing else left.
That is how bad the siege and the distress will be
that your enemy will bring on you in all your towns.
[56] The woman among you who is most gentle and sensitive
would never dare to set the sole of her foot on the ground,
because she is so delicate and sensitive.
But she will turn mean and stingy toward the husband she loves,
toward her son and toward her daughter,
[57] toward her newborn baby
that comes out from between her legs,
and toward the children she gives birth to.
For she will eat them in secret,
because she has nothing at all.
That is how bad the siege and the distress will be
that your enemy will bring on you in your towns.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are almost unbearable. They describe what can happen in a long siege: The hunger becomes so terrible that people lose all humanity and even eat their own children.
> This is not a command and not something God wants. It is a warning against the worst misery that can come upon people. Sadly, such things have really happened in sieges in history. The Bible tells of it at the siege of Samaria (2 Kings 6:28–29) and of Jerusalem (Lamentations 4:10).
> A nation “from the end of the earth,” “as fast as an eagle flies,” with a foreign language: Later people thought of the Assyrians, the Babylonians, and the Romans.

## Scattered among all nations (Verses 58–68)

[58] You may not be careful to do all the words of this law
that are written in this book.
You may not fear this glorious and awesome name,
the LORD your God.
[59] Then the LORD will send terrible plagues on you and on your descendants,
great plagues that last a long time,
and severe sicknesses that last a long time.
[60] He will bring back on you all the diseases of Egypt
that you were afraid of,
and they will cling to you.
[61] The LORD will also bring on you every sickness and every plague
that is not written in the book of this law,
until you are destroyed.
[62] Only a few of you will be left,
even though you were once as many as the stars in the sky,
because you did not listen to the voice of the LORD your God.
[63] Once the LORD was glad to do you good and to make you many.
In the same way the LORD will be glad to wipe you out and destroy you.
You will be pulled out of the land that you are going into to take over.
[64] The LORD will scatter you among all peoples,
from one end of the earth to the other end of the earth.
There you will serve other gods
that neither you nor your fathers have known,
gods of wood and stone.
[65] Among these nations you will find no peace,
and there will be no place for the sole of your foot to rest.
There the LORD will give you a trembling heart,
eyes that grow weak, and a soul that wastes away.
[66] Your life will hang in doubt before you.
You will be afraid night and day,
and you will never be sure that you will stay alive.
[67] In the morning you will say, “If only it were evening!”
And in the evening you will say, “If only it were morning!”
That is because of the fear that will fill your heart,
and because of the things that your eyes will see.
[68] The LORD will bring you back to Egypt in ships,
by the way that I told you you would never see again.
There you will try to sell yourselves to your enemies
as male and female slaves,
but no one will buy you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “This glorious and awesome name, the LORD your God”: In the WEB, our English source, the name here is written all in capital letters: “YAHWEH.” That is the holy name of God, which we give as “the LORD.”
> The end is the opposite of the beginning: God had set Israel free from Egypt. Now a return to slavery threatens, and no one even wants them as slaves. The whole rescue would be undone.
> Very important: This chapter was terribly misused in history. For centuries Christians claimed that the suffering of the Jews was a just punishment from God. With this they justified persecution, expulsion, and murder, all the way to the Holocaust. That was a terrible sin. No human being has the right to make himself the one who carries out a curse, or to declare the suffering of others “deserved.”
> These words were a warning to Moses’ own people, spoken out of love, to protect them. And the Bible does not end here. Right in Chapter 30 God promises: If you turn back, I will gather you from wherever you have been scattered. God’s last word is not the curse, but mercy.
