# Deuteronomy – Chapter 34
### The death of Moses

---

## Moses sees the land (Verses 1–4)

[1] Moses went up from the plains of Moab to Mount Nebo,
to the top of Pisgah, which is across from Jericho.
And the LORD showed him the whole land:
Gilead as far as Dan,
[2] all of Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh,
and the whole land of Judah as far as the Western Sea,
[3] the south, and the plain, the valley of Jericho,
the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar.
[4] The LORD said to him,
“This is the land that I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob,
when I said,
‘I will give it to your descendants.’
I have let you see it with your own eyes.
But you shall not go over there.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God himself shows Moses the whole land, from north to south. It is as if God took him by the hand and said: Look, I promised all this, and now I am keeping my promise.
> Moses may not go in. But he sees the goal he has been walking toward for 40 years. The promise to Abraham is almost fulfilled.

---

## Moses dies (Verses 5–8)

[5] So Moses, the servant of the LORD, died there in the land of Moab,
as the LORD had said.
[6] And he buried him in the valley in the land of Moab,
across from Beth Peor.
But to this day no one knows where his grave is.
[7] Moses was 120 years old when he died.
His eyes had not grown weak,
and his strength was not gone.
[8] The people of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab for thirty days.
Then the days of weeping and mourning for Moses came to an end.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The servant of the LORD”: That is the highest title of honor for Moses. He served God his whole life.
> “As the LORD had said”: The Hebrew literally says “according to the mouth of the LORD.” Jewish teachers made a beautiful picture from this: Moses died by a kiss from God.
> “He buried him”: God himself buried Moses. No one knows his grave. Maybe this was so that no one would make a cult of Moses and worship at his grave. Moses always wanted to point to God, not to himself.
> At 120 years old, Moses was still healthy and strong. He did not die because he was too weak, but because his time had come according to God’s will.
> The people mourned for 30 days. To this day, Judaism has a time of mourning of 30 days after the death of someone close.

---

## Joshua takes over (Verse 9)

[9] Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom,
because Moses had laid his hands on him.
And the people of Israel listened to him
and did what the LORD had commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Life goes on. God’s work does not depend on one single person. Moses appointed a successor in good time, and the people follow him.

---

## No prophet was like Moses (Verses 10–12)

[10] Since then, no prophet like Moses has risen in Israel.
The LORD knew him face to face.
[11] No one else did all the signs and wonders
that the LORD sent him to do in the land of Egypt,
to Pharaoh, to all his servants, and to his whole land.
[12] No one else showed such great power
or did such great and awesome deeds
as Moses did in front of all Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> That is the last word about Moses: There was no prophet like him. No one else knew God as closely, “face to face.”
> Who wrote these last verses? Moses can hardly have described his own death. Jewish scholars already said in the Talmud: Joshua wrote the last eight verses. Others think that Moses wrote them in tears, because God dictated them to him. Many scholars today think that the five books of Moses grew over a longer period of time.
> In Chapter 18 Moses had promised: God will send a prophet like me. At the end of the book it says: So far no one has come. That is why Israel waited for this prophet. Christians believe that Jesus is this prophet (Acts 3:22). The Gospel of John says: “For the law was given through Moses. Grace and truth became real through Jesus Christ” (John 1:17).

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Deuteronomy?

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of Deuteronomy is the farewell book of Moses. At the Jordan, shortly before his death, he speaks once more to all the people:
> 1. He reminds them of the journey through the wilderness: of God’s faithfulness and of the people’s unfaithfulness (Chapters 1–4).
> 2. He repeats the Ten Commandments and the most important commandment: “Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength” (Chapters 5–11).
> 3. He explains the commandments for life in the land: for worship and feasts, for courts and kings, for the family, and above all for protecting the weak: the poor, the foreigners, the orphans, and the widows (Chapters 12–26).
> 4. He sets before the people the choice between blessing and curse, and he calls out: “Choose life!” (Chapters 27–30).
> 5. He appoints Joshua, sings his song, blesses the tribes, and dies (Chapters 31–34).
> The book also contains hard and dark texts, about war, about punishments, and about how to treat other nations. We have not left them out, but we have explained them honestly. They belong to a distant time, and no one may use them to justify violence or hatred.
> The main message of the book is love: God loves his people, not because they are great or good, but simply because he does. And he wishes that his people love him too, and that all people live together with justice and mercy, especially with the weakest. “For you yourselves were foreigners in the land of Egypt.”

## Looking back: The five books of Moses, the Torah

> **What does this mean?**
> With this chapter the five books of Moses come to an end. Jewish people call them together the “Torah,” which means “instruction.” They are the most important part of the Hebrew Bible. In every synagogue the Torah is kept as a handwritten scroll in the holy ark.
> Genesis: God creates the world and human beings. He calls Abraham and promises him a land and many descendants.
> Exodus: God sets his people free from slavery in Egypt, makes a covenant with them at Sinai, and gives them the Ten Commandments.
> Leviticus: God shows how a holy people can live with a holy God. Right in the middle of it: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
> Numbers: The people wander through the wilderness for 40 years. They doubt and complain, but God stays faithful.
> Deuteronomy: Moses says goodbye and calls out: “Choose life!”
> The Torah does not end in the promised land, but just before it. The people are standing at the Jordan. The goal is in sight, but not reached yet. It is often like that with faith too: You are on the way, with God’s promise in your hand.
> And when Jewish people finish the last verse at the feast of “Simchat Torah,” they start again from the beginning right away: “In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.” God’s word is never finished being read.
