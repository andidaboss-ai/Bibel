# Deuteronomy – Chapter 29
### The covenant in the land of Moab

---

## A second covenant (Verse 1)

[1] These are the words of the covenant that the LORD commanded Moses
to make with the people of Israel in the land of Moab.
It was in addition to the covenant that he had made with them at Horeb.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles this verse is the last verse of Chapter 28 (28:69). Then the whole of Chapter 29 is shifted by one verse: verse 2 here is verse 1 there, and so on up to verse 28. We follow the numbering of the WEB, our English source text.
> At Horeb (Sinai) the covenant was made with the generation that came out of Egypt. Now, 40 years later, it is renewed with the new generation, just before they go into the land.

---

## You have seen everything (Verses 2–9)

[2] Moses called all Israel together and said to them:
Your eyes have seen everything that the LORD did in the land of Egypt
to Pharaoh, to all his servants, and to his whole land.
[3] Your eyes saw the great trials, the signs, and those great wonders.
[4] But to this day the LORD has not given you a heart to understand,
eyes to see, and ears to hear.
[5] I have led you for forty years in the wilderness.
Your clothes have not worn out on you,
and your sandals have not worn out on your feet.
[6] You have not eaten bread,
and you have not drunk wine or beer.
This was so that you would know that I am the LORD your God.
[7] When you came to this place,
Sihon the king of Heshbon and Og the king of Bashan
came out to fight against us,
and we defeated them.
[8] We took their land
and gave it as an inheritance to the Reubenites, to the Gadites,
and to the half-tribe of the Manassites.
[9] So keep the words of this covenant and do them.
Then you will succeed in everything you do.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4 is an honest and painful sentence: The people have seen so many wonders, and still they have not really understood. Deep understanding is a gift from God. You can see everything and still not grasp it. Jesus says something similar about such people: “seeing, they don’t see” (Matthew 13:13–15).
> In verses 5–6 Moses suddenly speaks as if God himself were speaking: “so that you would know that I am the LORD your God.” Moses is God’s mouthpiece.
> “You have not eaten bread”: This means no normal bread made from grain. God fed the people with manna.

## Everyone stands before God (Verses 10–15)

[10] Today all of you are standing before the LORD your God:
your leaders, your tribes, your elders, and your officials,
all the men of Israel,
[11] your little children, your wives,
and the foreigners who live in the middle of your camp,
from the one who chops your wood to the one who carries your water.
[12] You are standing here to enter into the covenant of the LORD your God
and into his oath,
which the LORD your God is making with you today.
An oath is a solemn promise.
[13] So today he will make you his people,
and he will be your God,
as he told you,
and as he swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.
[14] I am not making this covenant and this oath only with you.
[15] I am making it with those who are standing here with us today
before the LORD our God,
and also with those who are not here with us today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everyone belongs: leaders and ordinary people, men, women, and children, even the foreigners who do the simplest work, chopping wood and carrying water. Before God everyone is equal.
> Verse 15 is especially beautiful: The covenant also applies to “those who are not here with us today.” This means the future generations. Jewish teachers say: Every Jewish person who will ever live was there on that day. The covenant still applies today.

## Warning against a poisonous root (Verses 16–21)

[16] (For you know how we lived in the land of Egypt,
and how we passed through the middle of the nations
that you went through.
[17] And you have seen their terrible things,
and their idols of wood and stone, of silver and gold,
that were among them.)
[18] There must not be any man or woman, any family or tribe among you
whose heart turns away today from the LORD our God,
to go and serve the gods of those nations.
There must not be a root among you that produces bitter poison.
[19] Such a person may hear the words of this curse.
But in his heart he blesses himself and says,
“Things will go well for me,
even though I live by my own stubborn heart.”
This would destroy the wet together with the dry.
[20] The LORD will not be willing to forgive him.
Instead, the anger of the LORD and his jealousy will smoke against that man.
Every curse that is written in this book will fall on him,
and the LORD will wipe out his name from under the sky.
[21] The LORD will single him out from all the tribes of Israel for disaster,
according to all the curses of the covenant
that are written in this book of the law.

> **What does this mean?**
> “A root … that produces bitter poison”: One single person who falls away from God can pull others along, like a poisonous plant that spreads. The letter to the Hebrews takes up this picture (Hebrews 12:15).
> The greatest danger is fooling yourself: “Nothing will happen to me.” Whoever thinks like that does not take God seriously.
> “Destroy the wet together with the dry” is an old saying. It probably means: destroy everything, with no exception. Or: One guilty person drags the innocent down to ruin too.

## What the nations will say (Verses 22–28)

[22] The next generation, your children who grow up after you,
and the foreigner who comes from a faraway land,
will see the plagues of that land
and the sicknesses with which the LORD has made it sick.
[23] They will say that its whole land is sulfur, salt, and burning.
Nothing is sown in it, nothing grows, and no grass comes up in it.
It is like the destruction of Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim,
which the LORD destroyed in his anger and his fury.
[24] All the nations will ask,
“Why has the LORD done this to this land?
Why is his anger so great and so hot?”
[25] Then people will say,
“Because they abandoned the covenant of the LORD, the God of their ancestors,
which he made with them when he brought them out of the land of Egypt.
[26] They went and served other gods and worshiped them,
gods that they did not know and that he had not given them.
[27] That is why the anger of the LORD burned against this land,
and he brought on it all the curses that are written in this book.
[28] In anger, in fury, and in great rage
the LORD pulled them out of their land by the roots
and threw them into another land, as it is today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Sodom and Gomorrah were cities that God destroyed (Genesis 19). Admah and Zeboiim lay in the same region by the Dead Sea. To this day the landscape there is salty and bare.
> Moses sees ahead that Israel will one day lose its land. Later the prophets and the people in exile in Babylon read these words and understood: It has happened just as Moses warned.
> Here too, what was said about Chapter 28 applies: These words are a warning to Moses’ own people. No one may use them to explain the suffering of the Jewish people as a “deserved punishment.”

---

## What is hidden and what is shown (Verse 29)

[29] The hidden things belong to the LORD our God.
But the things that he has shown us belong to us and to our children forever,
so that we may do all the words of this law.

> **What does this mean?**
> A wise sentence at the end: We humans do not understand everything. Much remains God’s secret, for example why something happens or what the future will bring. We may leave that to God.
> But what God has shown us, we should do. We do not have to know everything in order to do what is right.
> In the Hebrew Bible there are special dots written above some words of this verse. They show how important and mysterious this sentence was to the scribes of old.
