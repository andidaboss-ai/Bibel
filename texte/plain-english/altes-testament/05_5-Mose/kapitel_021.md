# Deuteronomy – Chapter 21
### An unsolved murder – rules for the family

---

## When a dead body is found (Verses 1–9)

[1] In the land that the LORD your God gives you to own,
someone may be found killed, lying in the field,
and no one knows who struck him down.
[2] Then your elders and your judges shall go out.
They shall measure the distance to the towns around the dead man.
[3] The elders of the town that is nearest to the dead man
shall take a young cow from the herd.
It must be a cow that has never been worked
and has never pulled with a yoke.
A yoke is a wooden bar that is put on animals so that they can pull a load.
[4] The elders of that town shall lead the young cow down to a valley with flowing water,
a valley that is not plowed and not sown.
There in the valley they shall break the young cow’s neck.
[5] Then the priests, the sons of Levi, shall come forward.
For the LORD your God has chosen them
to serve him and to bless in the name of the LORD.
Every quarrel and every case of violence shall be decided by what they say.
[6] All the elders of the town that is nearest to the dead man
shall wash their hands over the young cow
whose neck was broken in the valley.
[7] They shall say,
“Our hands have not shed this blood,
and our eyes have not seen it.
[8] LORD, forgive your people Israel, whom you have set free.
Do not let the guilt of innocent blood stay among your people Israel.”
Then the guilt for the blood will be forgiven them.
[9] So you shall remove the guilt of innocent blood from among you,
when you do what is right in the eyes of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A person was murdered, but no one knows who did it. What now? Such a death must not simply be forgotten. Every human life counts.
> The nearest town takes responsibility. Its elders solemnly declare: We did not do it, and we did not see anything. And they ask God to forgive the whole people.
> Washing the hands is a sign of innocence. From this comes the saying that someone “washes their hands” of something. Pilate washed his hands when Jesus was sentenced (Matthew 27:24).
> Jewish teachers also asked: Did the elders perhaps fail to take care of a stranger, who then traveled without protection? A community shares responsibility for the safety of everyone.

## A captive woman (Verses 10–14)

[10] You may go out to battle against your enemies,
and the LORD your God hands them over to you,
and you take prisoners.
[11] Among the prisoners you may see a beautiful woman.
You are attracted to her, and you want to take her as your wife.
[12] Then you shall bring her home to your house.
She shall shave her head and cut her nails.
[13] She shall take off the clothes that she wore as a prisoner.
She shall stay in your house
and weep for her father and her mother for a whole month.
After that you may go in to her and be her husband,
and she shall be your wife.
[14] If you are no longer pleased with her,
then you shall let her go wherever she wants.
But you shall never sell her for money.
You shall not treat her as a slave,
because you have humiliated her.

> **What does this mean?**
> This section describes a situation that is terrible for us: A woman is taken prisoner in war and has to marry a foreign man. She has no choice in this. That clearly goes against what we understand today as human rights.
> Back then, among almost all nations, women taken prisoner were abused right away and sold as slaves. This law sets limits: The man may not take her right away. She gets a month to mourn for her parents. She becomes his wife with the rights of a wife, not his slave. And if he no longer wants her, he must set her free. He may not sell her.
> “Because you have humiliated her”: So the Bible itself says that the man should be aware that he has done her wrong.
> This rule was a step toward more protection. But it is not a model. Violence against women in war is a serious crime, and no one may use the Bible to defend it.

## The right of the firstborn (Verses 15–17)

[15] A man may have two wives.
He loves one, and he hates the other.
Both the loved wife and the hated wife have borne him children.
And the firstborn son belongs to the hated wife.
[16] On the day when he divides what he owns among his sons,
he may not give the son of the loved wife the rights of the firstborn
ahead of the son of the hated wife, who is the firstborn.
[17] He shall recognize the firstborn, the son of the hated wife.
He shall give him a double share of everything he has.
For he is the first sign of his father’s strength.
The right of the firstborn belongs to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The WEB, our English source, says “beloved” and “hated.” It means: One wife is loved more than the other.
> Back then, some men had more than one wife. That often led to jealousy and quarreling, as with Jacob, Leah, and Rachel (Genesis 29–30).
> The law protects the wife who is loved less, and her child. The father may not divide the inheritance according to his feelings. What is right stands above what he prefers.
> The firstborn got the double share because he had to take care of the family after his father’s death.

## A rebellious son (Verses 18–21)

[18] A man may have a stubborn and rebellious son,
who does not listen to the voice of his father or the voice of his mother.
They correct him, but he does not listen to them.
[19] Then his father and his mother shall take hold of him
and bring him out to the elders of his town,
to the gate of his town.
[20] They shall say to the elders of his town,
“This son of ours is stubborn and rebellious.
He does not listen to us.
He eats too much, and he is a drunkard.”
[21] Then all the men of his town shall stone him with stones until he dies.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.
And all Israel will hear about it and be afraid.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a very hard text. It does not mean a small child, but probably a grown son who has gone completely wild. He drinks, wastes the family’s wealth, and may even become violent.
> One thing stands out: The parents may not punish their son themselves. They have to go to the court of the elders. That protected the son from the whims of an angry father. And both parents, father and mother, must agree.
> The Jewish scholars added so many conditions to this law that they said: It has never been carried out and never will be (Talmud, Sanhedrin 71a). It only shows how seriously respect for parents was taken.
> Jesus tells a very different story about a son who wastes his inheritance: When he comes back, the father runs to meet him and hugs him (Luke 15:11–32).
> No one may use this passage to justify violence against children or young people.

## A man hanged on a tree (Verses 22–23)

[22] A man may have done a sin that deserves death.
He is put to death, and you hang him on a tree.
[23] Then his body shall not stay on the tree through the night.
You must bury him on the same day.
For whoever is hanged is cursed by God.
You shall not make your land unclean,
the land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sometimes the body of an executed person was hung on a tree or a pole after his death, as a warning to others. But that must not last long. He must be buried on the same day. Even a criminal has a right to a burial and to dignity.
> That is why Jesus too was taken down from the cross on the day he was crucified and buried (John 19:31).
> Paul quotes this verse: “Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree.” He says: On the cross Jesus took the curse on himself, to set people free from it (Galatians 3:13).
