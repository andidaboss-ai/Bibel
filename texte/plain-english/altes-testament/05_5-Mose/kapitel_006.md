# Deuteronomy – Chapter 6
### Hear, Israel!

---

## So that it may go well with you (Verses 1–3)

[1] These are the commandments, the rules, and the laws
that the LORD your God commanded me to teach you,
so that you do them in the land that you are crossing over to take as your own.
[2] So you shall fear the LORD your God
and keep all his rules and his commandments, which I command you –
you, your son, and your grandson, all the days of your life –
so that you may live long.
[3] So hear, Israel, and be careful to do it,
so that it may go well with you and so that you become very many,
as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has promised you,
in a land flowing with milk and honey.

> **What does this mean?**
> “A land flowing with milk and honey”: That is a picture of a rich, fertile land. There is enough pasture for goats and cows, and many flowers for the bees.

---

## The most important commandment (Verses 4–5)

[4] Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one.
[5] You shall love the LORD your God
with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength.

> **What does this mean?**
> These may be the most important sentences in the whole Old Testament. In Hebrew they begin with the word “Shema.” That means “Hear.” That is why this prayer is called the “Shema Israel.”
> To this day, Jews pray it every morning and every evening. It is the first prayer that Jewish children learn. And it is often the last thing a person says before death.
> “The LORD is one” means: There is only one God. And he is whole and undivided. The words can also be translated like this: “The LORD is our God, the LORD alone.”
> And then comes the commandment: Love God! Not only obey, but love, with everything you are: with the heart (thinking and feeling), with the soul (your whole life), and with all your strength.
> When Jesus was asked which commandment is the most important, he quoted exactly these words. And he added the commandment from Leviticus 19:18: Love your neighbor as yourself (Mark 12:29–31).

---

## The words in everyday life (Verses 6–9)

[6] These words, which I command you today, shall be on your heart.
[7] You shall teach them to your children again and again.
You shall talk about them when you sit in your house and when you are on the road,
when you lie down and when you get up.
[8] You shall tie them as a sign on your hand.
And they shall be as a reminder between your eyes.
[9] You shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s word should not only be found in church or in the synagogue, but everywhere in life: at home, on the road, in the evening and in the morning.
> To this day, Jews also take these verses literally:
> At morning prayer, Jewish men (and in some communities women too) tie small leather boxes to the arm and to the forehead. They are called “tefillin.” Inside are small pieces of paper with these Bible verses.
> On the doorposts of Jewish homes hangs a small case, the “mezuzah.” It also contains the “Shema Israel.” Many people touch it when they go through the door.
> The deeper meaning: We act with our hands, we think with our heads, and we go in and out through the door. Everything should be shaped by God’s word.

---

## Do not forget God when things go well for you (Verses 10–15)

[10] The LORD your God will bring you into the land
that he swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give to you.
There are great and beautiful cities that you did not build,
[11] houses full of good things that you did not fill,
cisterns cut into the rock that you did not dig,
vineyards and olive trees that you did not plant.
And you will eat and be full.
[12] Then be careful that you do not forget the LORD,
who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
[13] You shall fear the LORD your God.
You shall serve him, and you shall swear by his name.
[14] You shall not follow other gods,
the gods of the peoples who live around you.
[15] For the LORD your God, who is among you, is a jealous God.
Otherwise the anger of the LORD your God will burn against you,
and he will destroy you from the face of the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> A great danger is not need, but wealth. When things go well for us, we easily forget whom we owe everything to.
> Israel will receive many things it did not work for. Everything is a gift. Whoever knows that stays thankful.
> A “cistern” is a basin in the rock where people collect rainwater.
> Jesus quotes verse 13 when the devil tempts him: You shall worship the Lord your God, and serve only him (Matthew 4:10).

---

## Do not put God to the test (Verses 16–19)

[16] You shall not test the LORD your God,
as you tested him at Massah.
[17] You shall carefully keep the commandments of the LORD your God,
his testimonies and his rules, which he has commanded you.
[18] You shall do what is right and good in the LORD’s eyes,
so that it may go well with you, and so that you may go in and take as your own the good land
that the LORD swore to your ancestors,
[19] and so that he drives out all your enemies before you, as the LORD has said.

> **What does this mean?**
> At Massah the people had no water. They asked: Is the LORD among us or not? (Exodus 17:7). They wanted to force God to prove himself.
> “To test God” means: not to trust him, but to demand proof. Jesus also quotes this verse when he is tempted, when the devil tells him to jump down from the temple (Matthew 4:7).

---

## When your child asks you (Verses 20–25)

[20] Later your son will ask you,
“What do the testimonies, the rules, and the laws mean
that the LORD our God has commanded you?”
[21] Then you shall tell your son,
“We were Pharaoh’s slaves in Egypt.
And the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand.
[22] Before our eyes the LORD did great and terrible signs and wonders
against Egypt, against Pharaoh, and against all his household.
[23] And he brought us out from there, so that he could bring us in here
and give us the land that he swore to our ancestors.
[24] The LORD commanded us to do all these rules
and to fear the LORD our God,
for our good, for all time,
so that he would keep us alive, as we are today.
[25] And it will be righteousness for us
if we are careful to do all these commandments before the LORD our God,
as he has commanded us.”
Righteousness means: living in the right way before God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Children ask questions. That is good! The answer should not be a list of rules, but a story: “We were Pharaoh’s slaves in Egypt,” and God set us free.
> Whoever wants to understand God’s commandments must first know what God has done. The commandments are the answer to God’s love.
> This passage has become part of the Jewish Passover celebration. At the Passover meal, the children ask, and the parents tell the story of how they were set free.
> “For our good”: God’s commandments serve life.
