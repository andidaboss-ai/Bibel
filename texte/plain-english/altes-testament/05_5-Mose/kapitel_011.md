# Deuteronomy – Chapter 11
### Blessing and curse

---

## You have seen it yourselves (Verses 1–7)

[1] So you shall love the LORD your God
and keep his instructions, his rules, his laws, and his commandments, always.
[2] Know it today –
for I am not speaking with your children, who have not known it and have not seen it –
know the discipline of the LORD your God:
his greatness, his mighty hand, his outstretched arm,
[3] his signs and his works, which he did in the middle of Egypt
to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, and to all his land,
[4] and what he did to the army of Egypt, to their horses and to their chariots:
how he made the water of the Red Sea flow over them as they were chasing you,
and how the LORD has destroyed them to this day,
[5] and what he did for you in the wilderness until you came to this place,
[6] and what he did to Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab, the son of Reuben:
how the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up,
with their households, their tents, and every living thing that followed them,
in the middle of all Israel.
[7] For your eyes have seen all the great work of the LORD that he did.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is speaking to people who have experienced many things themselves. They were there in Egypt and in the wilderness as children and young people. They can tell their children about it as witnesses.
> Dathan and Abiram were the leaders of the rebellion with Korah (Numbers 16).
> “Red Sea”: That is what the WEB, our English source text, says. The Hebrew says “Sea of Reeds.”

---

## A land that lives from the rain (Verses 8–12)

[8] So you shall keep the whole commandment that I command you today,
so that you are strong, and go in,
and take as your own the land that you are crossing over to take,
[9] and so that you live long in the land
that the LORD swore to your ancestors to give to them and to their descendants,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
[10] For the land that you are going into to take as your own
is not like the land of Egypt, which you came out of.
There you sowed your seed and watered it with your foot, like a vegetable garden.
[11] But the land that you are crossing over to take as your own
is a land of hills and valleys that drinks water from the rain of the sky,
[12] a land that the LORD your God cares for.
The eyes of the LORD your God are always on it,
from the beginning of the year to the end of the year.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Egypt there is the great river Nile. There people could lead the water onto the fields with canals. “Watering with your foot” probably means: People opened small earth walls with their foot, or they turned a water wheel with their feet.
> In the land of Canaan there is no river like that. The land depends completely on the rain. That is why people there must trust God every day.
> But the land is not forgotten: God’s eyes are on it all year long.

## Rain as a blessing (Verses 13–17)

[13] If you really listen to my commandments, which I command you today,
to love the LORD your God and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul,
[14] then I will give rain to your land at the right time,
the early rain and the late rain,
so that you can gather in your grain, your new wine, and your oil.
[15] I will make grass grow in your fields for your livestock.
And you will eat and be full.
[16] Be careful that your heart is not fooled,
and that you do not turn away and serve other gods and worship them.
[17] Otherwise the LORD’s anger will burn against you.
He will shut up the sky, so that no rain falls,
and the land does not give its crops.
And you will quickly disappear from the good land that the LORD is giving you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “early rain” comes in the fall. It makes the hard ground soft, so that people can sow. The “late rain” comes in the spring. It makes the grain ripen.
> The Canaanites believed that the god Baal brings the rain. Moses says: No, the LORD gives the rain. Whoever runs to Baal loses exactly what he hopes for.
> Jews pray these verses 13–21 as the second part of the “Shema Israel,” every morning and every evening.

## Put the words into your heart (Verses 18–21)

[18] So you shall put these words of mine into your heart and into your soul.
You shall tie them as a sign on your hand.
And they shall be as a reminder between your eyes.
[19] You shall teach them to your children.
You shall talk about them when you sit in your house and when you are on the road,
when you lie down and when you get up.
[20] You shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates,
[21] so that you and your children may live long in the land
that the LORD swore to your ancestors to give them,
as long as the sky is above the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> We already know these words from Chapter 6:6–9. The repetition shows how important they are. They are also written in the tefillin and in the mezuzah (see Chapter 6).

## Every place your foot steps on (Verses 22–25)

[22] For if you really keep all these commandments that I command you, and do them,
if you love the LORD your God, walk in all his ways, and hold on to him,
[23] then the LORD will drive out all these nations before you.
And you will drive out nations that are greater and mightier than you.
[24] Every place that the sole of your foot steps on shall be yours.
Your border shall reach from the wilderness and from Lebanon, from the river, the river Euphrates, to the western sea.
[25] No one will be able to stand against you.
The LORD your God will put fear and terror of you
on all the land that you step on, as he has told you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “western sea” is the Mediterranean Sea.
> Israel ruled this large area as far as the Euphrates only for a short time, under King Solomon (1 Kings 4:21). The Bible describes a promise here, not a map for politics today.

## The choice between blessing and curse (Verses 26–32)

[26] Look! Today I am putting before you a blessing and a curse:
[27] the blessing, if you listen to the commandments of the LORD your God, which I command you today,
[28] and the curse, if you do not listen to the commandments of the LORD your God,
but turn away from the way that I command you today,
to follow other gods that you have not known.
[29] When the LORD your God brings you into the land that you are going into to take as your own,
then you shall put the blessing on Mount Gerizim and the curse on Mount Ebal.
[30] Are they not beyond the Jordan, behind the road to the west, where the sun goes down,
in the land of the Canaanites who live in the Arabah, near Gilgal, beside the oaks of Moreh?
[31] For you are crossing over the Jordan to go in
and take as your own the land that the LORD your God is giving you.
You will own it and live in it.
[32] So be careful to do all the rules and the laws that I am putting before you today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses puts a decision before the people. There are two ways: the way of blessing and the way of the curse. Everyone has to choose.
> Gerizim and Ebal are two mountains in the middle of the land, near today’s city of Nablus. Between them lies the old city of Shechem. Later a great ceremony is to take place there (Chapter 27; Joshua 8:30–35).
> Abraham had already camped by the “oaks of Moreh” when he came into the land (Genesis 12:6). Israel returns to the place where everything began.
