# Deuteronomy – Chapter 8
### Man does not live by bread alone

---

## Remember the wilderness (Verses 1–6)

[1] Be careful to do all the commandments that I command you today,
so that you may live and become many,
and go in and take as your own the land that the LORD swore to your ancestors.
[2] Remember the whole way that the LORD your God has led you these forty years in the wilderness.
He wanted to make you humble and to test you.
He wanted to know what was in your heart:
whether you would keep his commandments or not.
[3] He made you humble. He let you be hungry.
And he fed you with manna, which you did not know, and which your ancestors did not know either.
With this he wanted to teach you:
Man does not live by bread alone.
Man lives by every word that comes out of the LORD’s mouth.
[4] Your clothes did not wear out on you,
and your feet did not swell, these forty years.
[5] So understand in your heart:
As a man trains and corrects his son, so the LORD your God trains and corrects you.
[6] Keep the commandments of the LORD your God.
Walk in his ways, and fear him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The time in the wilderness was hard. But it had a purpose. Israel was to learn to trust God.
> “Man does not live by bread alone” is one of the best-known sentences in the Bible. It means: People need more than food. They need God and his word. Jesus quotes this sentence when the devil tempts him in the wilderness to make bread out of stones (Matthew 4:4).
> The manna came new every day. People could not gather it for later. So the people learned: We depend on God every day. That is why Christians pray in the Lord’s Prayer: Give us today our daily bread.
> God trains his people like a good father. Sometimes it is hard, but it is done out of love.

---

## A good land (Verses 7–10)

[7] For the LORD your God is bringing you into a good land,
a land with brooks of water, with springs, and with water under the ground
that flows out in the valleys and on the hills,
[8] a land of wheat and barley, of grapevines, fig trees, and pomegranates,
a land of olive trees and honey,
[9] a land where you will eat bread and never run short,
where you will not lack anything,
a land whose stones are iron,
and out of whose hills you can dig copper.
[10] You will eat and be full.
Then you shall praise the LORD your God for the good land that he has given you.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the bare wilderness comes a land full of good things. The seven foods in verse 8 (wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives, honey from dates or bees) are still especially valued in Israel today.
> Verse 10 is the basis for the Jewish prayer at the table after the meal. Jews thank God not only before eating, but above all after eating. Whoever is full should not forget to say thank you.

## Do not forget God when you are rich (Verses 11–20)

[11] Be careful that you do not forget the LORD your God
by not keeping his commandments, his laws, and his rules,
which I command you today.
[12] Otherwise this could happen: You have eaten and are full.
You have built fine houses and live in them.
[13] Your herds and your flocks grow.
Your silver and your gold grow,
and everything you have grows.
[14] Then your heart becomes proud,
and you forget the LORD your God,
who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery,
[15] who led you through the great and terrible wilderness,
with poisonous snakes and scorpions,
through thirsty ground where there was no water,
who made water pour out for you from the hard rock,
[16] who fed you in the wilderness with manna, which your ancestors did not know,
to make you humble and to test you,
so that he could do good to you in the end.
[17] And then you might say in your heart,
“My power and the strength of my hand have gotten me this wealth.”
[18] But you shall remember the LORD your God.
For he is the one who gives you the power to gain wealth.
In this way he wants to confirm his covenant, which he swore to your ancestors, as it is today.
[19] But if you forget the LORD your God,
and follow other gods, and serve them, and worship them,
then I warn you today: You will surely die out.
[20] Like the nations that the LORD is destroying before you,
so you too will be destroyed,
because you would not listen to the voice of the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is a warning that is true for all times: Whoever is rich and full easily becomes proud. He thinks: I did all this myself.
> Moses says: No! Even the strength to work is a gift from God. Health, talents, good circumstances – all of it comes from him.
> This does not mean that work is unimportant. But whoever knows that everything is a gift stays thankful and humble. And he finds it easier to share with others.
> The last verses are serious: Israel is not better than the other peoples. If it leaves God, the same thing will happen to it as to them.
