# Deuteronomy – Chapter 17
### Court cases – the law for the king

---

## Only sacrifices without defects (Verse 1)

[1] You shall not sacrifice an ox or a sheep to the LORD your God
if it has a defect or anything bad about it.
For the LORD your God hates that.

> **What does this mean?**
> You should not give God the worst, the things you no longer want yourself. He deserves the best.

---

## Two or three witnesses (Verses 2–7)

[2] A man or a woman may be found among you,
in one of your towns that the LORD your God gives you,
who does what is evil in the eyes of the LORD your God
and breaks his covenant.
[3] This person has gone and served other gods and worshiped them,
or the sun, or the moon, or any of the stars of the sky.
I have not commanded that.
[4] If you are told about it and you hear of it,
then you shall look into it carefully.
If it is true and the matter is certain
that such a terrible thing has been done in Israel,
[5] then you shall bring out that man or that woman who has done this evil thing
to the gates of your town, that very man or woman.
And you shall stone them with stones until they die.
[6] A person who is to die shall be put to death
only on the word of two witnesses or three witnesses.
He shall not be put to death on the word of only one witness.
[7] The hands of the witnesses shall be the first against him to put him to death.
After that, the hands of all the people shall be against him.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: A covenant is a binding agreement.
> Again it is about turning away from God, and again the death penalty applied back then (see Chapter 13). That is very hard for us. Here too: No one may use this passage to persecute people because of their faith.
> But the rules of protection are important: The matter must be looked into carefully. One witness alone is not enough. There must be at least two. And the witnesses themselves must act first. So whoever gives false testimony carries a heavy responsibility. This was meant to prevent false accusations.
> Jesus took up the rule about two or three witnesses (Matthew 18:16). It is still an important principle of law today.

## Difficult court cases (Verses 8–13)

[8] A case may come up in your towns that is too hard for you to judge.
It may be about blood and blood,
about one claim against another claim,
or about one injury against another injury.
These are matters of quarrel in your towns.
Then you shall get up and go up to the place that the LORD your God chooses.
[9] You shall go to the priests, who are Levites,
and to the judge who is there in those days.
You shall ask them, and they will tell you the verdict.
[10] You shall act according to the verdict that they tell you
from that place that the LORD chooses.
Be careful to do everything they teach you.
[11] You shall act according to the law that they teach you
and according to the decision that they tell you.
You shall not turn away from the verdict that they announce to you,
not to the right and not to the left.
[12] But a man may act proudly and not listen to the priest
who stands there to serve the LORD your God,
or to the judge.
That man shall die.
So you shall remove the evil from Israel.
[13] Then all the people will hear it and be afraid,
and they will no longer act proudly.

> **What does this mean?**
> “About blood and blood” means: Was it murder, or a killing by accident? “About one injury against another injury” means: Who hurt whom, and how badly?
> For difficult cases there is a highest court at the central holy place. That is something like an early court of appeals.
> Whoever deliberately resists the verdict of this highest court is punished harshly. Without respect for the court, a community cannot live in peace.

## The law for the king (Verses 14–20)

[14] You will come into the land that the LORD your God gives you.
You will take it over and live in it.
Then you may say,
“I want to set a king over me, like all the nations around me.”
[15] Then you shall set over you as king only the one whom the LORD your God chooses.
You shall set one of your brothers as king over you.
You may not put a foreigner over you who is not your brother.
[16] But he shall not get many horses for himself.
And he shall not make the people go back to Egypt to get many horses.
For the LORD has said to you,
“You shall never go back that way again.”
[17] He shall not take many wives for himself,
so that his heart does not turn away.
And he shall not gather very much silver and gold for himself.
[18] When he sits on the throne of his kingdom,
he shall write for himself a copy of this law in a book.
He shall copy it from the book that the priests, the Levites, keep.
[19] The copy shall be with him,
and he shall read from it all the days of his life.
Then he will learn to fear the LORD his God,
and to keep all the words of this law and these rules and do them.
[20] Then his heart will not be lifted up above his brothers,
and he will not turn away from the commandment,
not to the right and not to the left.
Then he will rule for a long time in his kingdom, he and his children,
in the middle of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel does not have a king yet. But Moses sees ahead that the people will one day want a king. That happened later, at the time of the prophet Samuel (1 Samuel 8).
> The king in Israel should be completely different from the kings of other nations:
> No great number of horses: Horses were for chariots, so for war and power.
> No great number of wives: Back then, kings often had many wives from foreign countries in order to make alliances. These wives brought their gods along.
> No great wealth: A king should not make himself rich.
> Instead he should read in God’s law every day. He does not stand above the law, but under it. And he should not lift himself up above his “brothers.” He stays a brother among brothers.
> That is a very modern idea: Even the most powerful person must obey the law.
> Later, King Solomon did exactly these three things: many horses from Egypt, 700 wives, and countless amounts of gold (1 Kings 10–11). And his heart really did turn away from God.
