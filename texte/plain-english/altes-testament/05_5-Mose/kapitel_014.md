# Deuteronomy – Chapter 14
### Children of God – clean food – the tithe

---

## You are children of God (Verses 1–2)

[1] You are the children of the LORD your God.
You shall not cut yourselves for someone who has died,
and you shall not shave your head bald above your forehead.
[2] For you are a holy people to the LORD your God.
And the LORD has chosen you to be his very own people,
out of all the peoples who live on the face of the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> “You are the children of the LORD”: What dignity! God is like a father to his people.
> Among other peoples, people cut their skin or shaved off their hair when they were grieving. Israel should not do that. Even in grief you remain God’s child. You may grieve, but not in a way that hurts your own body.

---

## Which land animals you may eat (Verses 3–8)

[3] You shall not eat anything disgusting.
[4] These are the animals that you may eat:
the ox, the sheep, the goat,
[5] the deer, the gazelle, the roebuck, the wild goat,
the ibex, the antelope, and the chamois.
[6] Among the animals, you may eat every animal that has a split hoof,
split completely in two, and that chews the cud.
To chew the cud means: to bring food back up from the stomach and chew it again, like a cow.
[7] But among those that chew the cud or have a split hoof, you shall not eat these:
the camel, the hare, and the rabbit.
For they chew the cud, but they do not have a split hoof.
They are unclean for you.
[8] And the pig, because it has a split hoof, but it does not chew the cud.
It is unclean for you.
You shall not eat their meat, and you shall not touch their dead bodies.

> **What does this mean?**
> We already know these food laws from Leviticus 11. Here they are repeated for the new generation.
> “Rabbit”: That is what the WEB says. The Hebrew says “shaphan.” That is probably the rock hyrax, a small animal that lives among rocks. The hare and the rock hyrax chew all the time, and when they do, it looks as if they are chewing the cud.
> Why exactly these rules? The Bible does not say. What is important: Israel should show even in eating that it belongs to God. To this day, many Jews keep these rules. This is called “kosher.”
> According to the New Testament, these food laws no longer apply to Christians (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

---

## Animals in the water (Verses 9–10)

[9] Of everything that lives in the water, you may eat these:
You may eat everything that has fins and scales.
[10] But you shall not eat anything that does not have fins and scales.
It is unclean for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Fish with fins and scales are allowed. Mussels, crabs, squid and, for example, eels are forbidden.

---

## Birds (Verses 11–20)

[11] You may eat all clean birds.
[12] But these are the ones that you shall not eat:
the eagle, the vulture, the osprey,
[13] the red kite, the falcon, every kind of kite,
[14] every kind of raven,
[15] the ostrich, the owl, the seagull, every kind of hawk,
[16] the little owl, the great owl, the horned owl,
[17] the pelican, the vulture, the cormorant,
[18] the stork, every kind of heron, the hoopoe, and the bat.
[19] All flying creatures that creep are unclean for you.
They shall not be eaten.
[20] You may eat all clean birds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Most of the forbidden birds are birds of prey or birds that eat dead animals. They eat meat with blood in it or animals that have died.
> For many Hebrew bird names, we no longer know exactly which bird is meant. That is why Bibles often translate them differently here. We follow the WEB.
> The bat is not a bird. But back then, people sorted animals by what they saw: It flies, so it belongs with the flying animals.
> “Flying creatures that creep” are insects. According to Leviticus 11:21–22, people were allowed to eat some kinds of locusts.

---

## No animal that died by itself (Verse 21)

[21] You shall not eat anything that has died by itself.
You may give it to the foreigner who lives within your gates, so that he can eat it,
or you may sell it to a foreigner.
For you are a holy people to the LORD your God.
You shall not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.

> **What does this mean?**
> When an animal has died by itself, the blood has not run out of it properly. That is why Israelites may not eat it. This rule does not apply to foreigners. They may have it, and so nothing is wasted.
> “You shall not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk”: This commandment is in the Bible three times (also Exodus 23:19 and 34:26). It shows respect for life: The milk that was meant to feed the young animal should not be used for its death. From this, Jews made the rule not to eat meat and milk products together.

## The tithe: a feast before God (Verses 22–27)

[22] You shall surely give a tithe of everything your seed brings in,
of what grows in your field year by year.
A tithe is a tenth.
[23] And you shall eat before the LORD your God,
at the place that he chooses to let his name live there:
the tithe of your grain, of your new wine, and of your oil,
and the firstborn of your herd and of your flock,
so that you learn to fear the LORD your God always.
[24] But the LORD your God will bless you.
And maybe the way is too long for you, so that you cannot carry it,
because the place that the LORD your God will choose to put his name there is too far from you.
[25] Then you shall exchange it for money.
You shall tie up the money in your hand
and go to the place that the LORD your God will choose.
[26] There you shall spend the money on whatever you want:
on cattle or on sheep, on wine or on strong drink,
or on anything you wish for.
And you shall eat there before the LORD your God and be glad, you and your household.
[27] And you shall not abandon the Levite who lives within your gates,
for he has no share and no inheritance with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “tithe” is a tenth of the harvest. Amazing: Here you should not simply hand it over. You should celebrate a big feast with it yourself, before God, with your whole family!
> Whoever lives far away may sell the harvest. Then, at the holy place, he may buy with the money anything he wishes for, even wine and strong drink. God wants his people to be glad.
> But you should not forget the Levites. They should celebrate too.

---

## The tithe for the poor (Verses 28–29)

[28] At the end of every three years
you shall bring out the whole tithe of your harvest of that year
and store it within your gates.
[29] Then they shall come: the Levite, because he has no share and no inheritance with you,
and the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow who live within your gates.
They shall eat and be full,
so that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands that you do.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every third year, the tithe is meant for the poor. It is stored in your own town, and all the people in need may come and eat until they are full.
> This is an early form of social help: a fixed supply for those who have no property of their own. The Levites, foreigners, orphans and widows should not have to beg. They have a right to this help.
> And God promises: Whoever shares will be blessed.
