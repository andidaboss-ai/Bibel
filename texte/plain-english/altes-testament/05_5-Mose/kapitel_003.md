# Deuteronomy – Chapter 3
### The victory over Og – Moses may not enter the land

---

## The victory over Og, the king of Bashan (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then we turned and went up on the way to Bashan.
Og, the king of Bashan, came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Edrei.
[2] The LORD said to me,
“Do not be afraid of him.
For I have given him into your hand, with all his people and his land.
You shall do to him what you did to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon.”
[3] So the LORD our God also gave Og, the king of Bashan, and all his people into our hand.
We struck him down until no one was left to him.
[4] At that time we took all his cities.
There was not one city that we did not take from them:
60 cities, the whole region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.
[5] All these were cities made strong with high walls, gates, and bars.
And there were also very many villages without walls.
[6] We completely destroyed them, as we had done to Sihon, the king of Heshbon.
We completely destroyed every city where people lived, with the women and the little children.
[7] But all the livestock and the plunder from the cities we took as plunder for ourselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bashan is a fertile high plain in the north, east of the Sea of Galilee. Today the area is called the Golan.
> Here too, the cities are “completely destroyed,” as with Sihon in Chapter 2. That means: The people who lived there were killed, even women and children. That is terrible, and we must not make it sound nicer than it is. What the explanation in Chapter 2 says is true here too: This passage must never be used to justify violence against people.

## The whole land east of the Jordan (Verses 8–11)

[8] So at that time we took the land out of the hand of the two kings of the Amorites who lived beyond the Jordan,
from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon.
[9] (The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir.)
[10] We took all the cities of the plain, all Gilead, and all Bashan,
as far as Salecah and Edrei, the cities of the kingdom of Og in Bashan.
[11] (For only Og, the king of Bashan, was left of the rest of the Rephaim.
Look! His bed was a bed of iron.
Is it not in Rabbah of the people of Ammon?
It was about 13 and a half feet long and about 6 feet wide, measured by the ordinary cubit.)

> **What does this mean?**
> Hermon is the highest mountain in the area, more than 9,200 feet high. There is often snow on its top.
> The Bible says: nine cubits long and four cubits wide. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> Og was the last of the “Rephaim,” an ancient people of very tall people. His huge bed could still be seen in Rabbah long afterward, like a famous sight. Rabbah is today’s city of Amman, the capital of Jordan.
> Some interpreters think that “bed” means a coffin made of dark stone that looked like iron. That is not certain.

## The land is shared out (Verses 12–17)

[12] At that time we took this land as our own.
From Aroer, which is by the valley of the Arnon,
and half of the hill country of Gilead with its cities,
I gave to the Reubenites and to the Gadites.
[13] And the rest of Gilead and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og,
I gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh:
the whole region of Argob, all Bashan.
(This is called the land of Rephaim.
[14] Jair, the son of Manasseh, took the whole region of Argob,
as far as the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites.
And he named them, all Bashan, after his own name: Havvoth Jair, as it is called to this day.)
[15] I gave Gilead to Machir.
[16] To the Reubenites and to the Gadites I gave the land from Gilead to the valley of the Arnon,
with the middle of the valley as the border,
and as far as the river Jabbok, which is the border of the people of Ammon.
[17] I also gave them the Arabah, with the Jordan as the border,
from Chinnereth to the sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea,
below the slopes of Pisgah to the east.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here we hear again what was told in Numbers 32: Reuben, Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh receive the land east of the Jordan.
> The “sea of the Arabah” and the “Salt Sea” are names for the Dead Sea.
> “Chinnereth” is an old name for the Sea of Galilee.
> “Pisgah” is a mountain peak east of the Dead Sea. From there Moses will later see the land.

## The men of Reuben and Gad are to fight too (Verses 18–22)

[18] At that time I commanded you:
“The LORD your God has given you this land to own it.
All your brave men shall go across armed, ahead of your brothers, the people of Israel.
[19] But your wives, your little children, and your livestock
(I know that you have a lot of livestock)
shall stay in your cities that I have given you,
[20] until the LORD gives rest to your brothers, as he has to you,
and until they also own the land that the LORD your God is giving them beyond the Jordan.
Then each of you shall return to his own land, which I have given you.”
[21] At that time I commanded Joshua:
“Your eyes have seen everything that the LORD your God has done to these two kings.
The LORD will do the same to all the kingdoms that you cross over to.
[22] You shall not be afraid of them.
For the LORD your God himself fights for you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses reminds them of the agreement with Reuben and Gad from Numbers 32.
> And he gives Joshua courage: You have seen with your own eyes what God has done. That is why you do not need to be afraid of the future.

## Moses’ request is not granted (Verses 23–29)

[23] At that time I begged the LORD:
[24] “Lord GOD,
you have begun to show your servant your greatness and your strong hand.
For what god in heaven or on earth can do works like yours
and mighty deeds like yours?
[25] Please let me go across.
Let me see the good land that is beyond the Jordan,
that beautiful hill country, and Lebanon.”
[26] But the LORD was angry with me because of you, and he did not listen to me.
The LORD said to me,
“That is enough! Do not speak to me about this matter anymore.
[27] Go up to the top of Pisgah.
Lift up your eyes to the west, to the north, to the south, and to the east.
Look at it with your eyes.
For you will not cross this Jordan.
[28] But appoint Joshua. Give him courage and make him strong.
For he will go across ahead of this people.
And he will lead them to receive as their inheritance the land that you will see.”
[29] So we stayed in the valley near Beth Peor.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Lord GOD”: Moses calls God by two names. He uses “Lord” as a title, and God’s holy name, which we write as “GOD” or “the LORD.”
> This is one of the most personal passages in the book. Moses led the people for 40 years. His greatest wish is to enter the land. He asks God for it. But God says no.
> Even great people of faith do not get everything they ask for. But God also tells Moses something comforting: You may see the land. And your work goes on, through Joshua.
> Christians remember a story in the New Testament: Much later, Moses does stand in the land, on a mountain, together with Jesus (Matthew 17:1–3).
