# Deuteronomy – Chapter 1
### Moses remembers the way from Horeb

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Deuteronomy?

The book of Deuteronomy is also called “the Fifth Book of Moses.” The name “Deuteronomy” is Greek. It means “second law” or “repeating the law.” That is because Moses repeats many commandments here that God gave at Mount Sinai.
In Hebrew, the book is called “Devarim.” That means “words.” That is because the whole book is made of the speeches of Moses.
The people are standing at the Jordan, shortly before the promised land. Moses knows: I will die soon. I may not go across with them. That is why he gives long farewell speeches. He reminds them of everything God has done. He explains the commandments once more for the new generation. And he begs the people with all his heart: Stay faithful to God!
This book has the most important prayer of Israel: “Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength” (Deuteronomy 6:4–5). Jesus called it the most important commandment.
At the end of the book, Moses dies.
In this book, Moses usually calls Mount Sinai “Horeb.” It is the same mountain.

---

## Where and when Moses speaks (Verses 1–5)

[1] These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel
beyond the Jordan, in the wilderness, in the Arabah, opposite Suf,
between Paran, Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth, and Dizahab.
[2] From Horeb by the way of Mount Seir to Kadesh Barnea is a journey of eleven days.
[3] In the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first day of the month,
Moses spoke to the people of Israel.
He told them everything that the LORD had commanded him to tell them.
[4] That was after he had struck down Sihon, the king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon,
and Og, the king of Bashan, who lived in Ashtaroth, at Edrei.
[5] Beyond the Jordan, in the land of Moab, Moses began to explain this law. He said:

> **What does this mean?**
> “Beyond the Jordan” means here: east of the Jordan, where the people are camping right now.
> The “Arabah” is the deep valley that reaches from the Sea of Galilee past the Dead Sea down to the Red Sea.
> Verse 2 is a quiet complaint: The way from Horeb to Kadesh takes only eleven days. But the people needed 40 years, because they did not trust God.
> Moses begins “to explain this law.” He does not give a completely new law. He explains it, so that the new generation understands it.

---

## God says: Set out! (Verses 6–8)

[6] “The LORD our God spoke to us at Horeb. He said,
‘You have lived long enough at this mountain.
[7] Turn around, set out, and go to the hill country of the Amorites
and to all the places near there:
to the Arabah, to the hill country, to the lowland, to the South, to the seashore,
to the land of the Canaanites, and to Lebanon,
as far as the great river, the river Euphrates.
[8] Look! I have put the land in front of you.
Go in and take the land as your own.
The LORD swore to your ancestors – to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob –
that he would give it to them and to their descendants after them.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel camped at Mount Horeb (Sinai) for almost a year. There they received the commandments. But then God says: It is enough. You should not stand still. Go!
> Faith is a journey. You cannot always stay at the holy mountain. You have to go with God into everyday life.

---

## Moses appoints judges (Verses 9–18)

[9] At that time I said to you,
“I cannot carry you all by myself.
[10] The LORD your God has made you many.
Look! Today you are as many as the stars in the sky.
[11] May the LORD, the God of your ancestors, make you a thousand times as many as you are now.
And may he bless you, as he has promised you!
[12] How can I alone carry your problems, your burdens, and your quarrels?
[13] Choose wise men who have understanding and who are respected in your tribes.
I will make them leaders over you.”
[14] You answered me,
“What you have said is good. We should do it.”
[15] So I took the leaders of your tribes, wise and respected men,
and I made them leaders over you:
captains of thousands, captains of hundreds, captains of fifties, captains of tens,
and officers, for your tribes.

[16] At that time I commanded your judges,
“Listen to the cases between your brothers.
Judge fairly between a man and his brother,
and between a man and the foreigner who lives with him.
[17] When you judge, you must not favor anyone.
You must listen to the small person just like the great person.
You must not be afraid of any person,
because the judgment belongs to God.
If a case is too hard for you, bring it to me. I will listen to it.”
[18] At that time I commanded you everything that you should do.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses reminds them how he shared out the work (Exodus 18). A leader cannot do everything alone.
> The rules for the judges are still the basis of a good court today:
> All people are equal before the law: poor and rich, people born in the land and foreigners.
> A judge must not let anyone frighten him, not even powerful people.
> “The judgment belongs to God”: A judge does not judge in his own name. He must answer to God.

## The spies (Verses 19–25)

[19] We set out from Horeb and went through all that great and terrible wilderness, which you saw.
We went on the way to the hill country of the Amorites, as the LORD our God had commanded us.
And we came to Kadesh Barnea.
[20] I said to you,
“You have come to the hill country of the Amorites, which the LORD our God is giving us.
[21] Look! The LORD your God has put the land in front of you.
Go up and take it as your own,
as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has told you.
Do not be afraid, and do not lose courage.”
[22] Then all of you came to me and said,
“Let’s send men ahead of us.
They should explore the land for us.
They should tell us which way we should go up,
and which cities we will come to.”
[23] I liked the idea very much.
I took twelve of your men, one man from each tribe.
[24] They turned and went up into the hill country.
They came to the valley of Eshcol and spied it out.
[25] They took some of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought it down to us.
They told us,
“It is a good land that the LORD our God is giving us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story was told in detail in Numbers 13. Here Moses tells it again from his point of view.
> Here we learn something new: The idea of the spies came from the people. Numbers 13 tells that God then commanded it. Both fit together: The people asked, and God allowed it.

---

## The people do not want to go (Verses 26–33)

[26] But you did not want to go up.
You rebelled against the command of the LORD your God.
[27] You grumbled in your tents and said,
“Because the LORD hated us, he brought us out of the land of Egypt.
He wants to hand us over to the Amorites, so that they destroy us.
[28] Where are we going up to?
Our brothers have made our hearts melt with fear.
They said, ‘The people are greater and taller than we are.
The cities are great, with walls up to the sky.
And we even saw the sons of the Anakim there!’”
[29] Then I said to you,
“Do not be terrified. Do not be afraid of them.
[30] The LORD your God goes ahead of you. He will fight for you,
just as he did everything for you in Egypt before your eyes,
[31] and in the wilderness. There you saw
how the LORD your God carried you, as a man carries his son,
on the whole way that you went, until you came to this place.”
[32] But even so, you did not trust the LORD your God.
[33] He went ahead of you on the way, to look for a place for you to set up your tents.
At night he went ahead in fire, to show you the way you should go,
and by day in the cloud.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Because the LORD hated us”: That is how far fear can take a person. God freed his people from slavery. But the people think: He only wants to destroy us.
> The “Anakim” were thought to be a people of very tall people, like giants.
> Verse 31 is one of the most beautiful pictures in the Bible: God carried his people like a father carries his child. When the child is tired, the father picks it up in his arms. That is how God carried Israel through the wilderness.

---

## God’s judgment (Verses 34–40)

[34] The LORD heard what you said.
He became angry and swore:
[35] “Surely not one of these men of this evil generation
will see the good land that I swore to give to your ancestors,
[36] except Caleb, the son of Jephunneh. He will see it.
I will give him and his children the land that he has walked on,
because he has followed the LORD completely.”
[37] The LORD was also angry with me because of you. He said,
“You also will not go in there.
[38] Joshua, the son of Nun, who stands before you, will go in there.
Give him courage, because he will lead Israel to receive the land as their inheritance.
[39] And your little ones, who you said would be captured or killed,
your children, who today do not yet know good from evil,
they will go in there.
I will give it to them, and they will own it.
[40] But you, turn around and go into the wilderness, on the way to the Red Sea.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Because of you”: Moses says God was angry with him too, because of the people. Numbers 20 tells another reason: At the water of Meribah, Moses did not act as God wanted. Both belong together: The people had made Moses so angry back then that he lost his patience.
> “Who stands before you” means: Joshua is your servant and helper.
> God’s judgment also has a good side: The children the parents were so worried about will receive the land.

---

## The attack without God (Verses 41–46)

[41] Then you answered me,
“We have sinned against the LORD.
We will go up and fight, just as the LORD our God commanded us.”
Each one of you put on his weapons of war.
And you were so proud and bold that you wanted to go up into the hill country.
[42] The LORD said to me,
“Tell them, ‘Do not go up, and do not fight, because I am not among you.
Otherwise your enemies will strike you down.’”
[43] I told you, but you did not listen.
You rebelled against the command of the LORD.
You were proud and bold, and you went up into the hill country.
[44] The Amorites, who lived in that hill country, came out against you.
They chased you the way bees do.
And they struck you down in Seir, all the way to Hormah.
[45] You came back and wept before the LORD.
But the LORD did not listen to your voice, and he did not turn his ear to you.
[46] So you stayed in Kadesh for many days, for as long as you stayed there.

> **What does this mean?**
> First the people do not want to go when God tells them to. Then they want to go no matter what when God forbids it. Both are disobedience.
> The most important sentence is: “I am not among you.” Without God, even the best weapons do not help.
> “The way bees do”: Anyone who has ever been chased by a swarm of bees knows what it is like. You can only run away.
> Verse 46 sounds strange. It means: You had to stay in Kadesh a long time, exactly as long as it took.
