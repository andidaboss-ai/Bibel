# Deuteronomy – Chapter 26
### The first fruits – a confession of faith – the covenant

---

## The basket with the first fruits (Verses 1–4)

[1] You will come into the land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance.
You will take it over and live in it.
[2] Then you shall take some of the first of all the crops of the ground
that you bring in from your land that the LORD your God gives you.
You shall put them in a basket.
And you shall go to the place that the LORD your God will choose,
to let his name live there.
[3] You shall come to the priest who is there in those days and say to him,
“Today I declare to the LORD your God
that I have come into the land
that the LORD swore to our ancestors to give us.”
[4] The priest shall take the basket from your hand
and set it down in front of the altar of the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> When the first harvest in the new land is ripe, everyone should bring a basket with the first fruits to the holy place. That is a sign of thanks: The land and everything that grows on it is a gift from God.

---

## “My father was a Syrian who was close to ruin” (Verses 5–11)

[5] Then you shall speak up before the LORD your God and say,
“My father was a Syrian who was close to ruin.
He went down into Egypt and lived there as a stranger,
with only a few people.
There he became a great nation, strong and with many people.
[6] The Egyptians treated us badly.
They oppressed us and forced us to do hard work.
[7] Then we cried out to the LORD, the God of our ancestors.
The LORD heard our voice.
He saw our misery, our hard work, and how we were oppressed.
[8] The LORD brought us out of Egypt
with a strong hand and with an outstretched arm,
with great terror, with signs, and with wonders.
[9] He has brought us to this place
and has given us this land,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
[10] Now look, I have brought the first of the crops of the ground
that you, LORD, have given me.”
Then you shall set it down before the LORD your God,
and bow down in worship before the LORD your God.
[11] And you shall be glad about all the good things
that the LORD your God has given to you and to your household,
you, and the Levite, and the foreigner who lives among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Syrian”: That is the word the WEB, our English source text, uses. The Hebrew has “Aramean.” The Arameans lived in the region of today’s Syria. This means Jacob, who lived for a long time with his uncle Laban in Aram. “Close to ruin” can also be translated as “wandering” or “homeless.”
> This prayer is like a short confession of faith of Israel. In a few sentences the whole story is told: We were homeless. We were slaves. We cried out. God heard and set us free. He gave us this land.
> Something stands out: The farmer who lives centuries later says “us” and “we.” He himself was not in Egypt. But the story of his people is his story too.
> This text is still a central part of the Jewish Passover celebration today (the Haggadah).
> And again: The joy is shared, also with the Levite and the foreigner.

## The tithe in the third year (Verses 12–15)

[12] Every third year is the year of the tithe.
When you have finished setting apart the whole tithe of your harvest in that year,
you shall give it to the Levite, to the foreigner, to the orphan, and to the widow.
Then they can eat in your towns and be full.
[13] And you shall say before the LORD your God,
“I have taken the holy share out of my house.
I have given it to the Levite, to the foreigner, to the orphan, and to the widow,
just as you commanded me in all your commandments.
I have not broken any of your commandments,
and I have not forgotten any of them.
[14] I have not eaten any of it while I was mourning.
I have not taken any of it away while I was unclean.
And I have not given any of it for the dead.
I have listened to the voice of the LORD my God.
I have done everything that you commanded me.
[15] Look down from your holy home, from heaven,
and bless your people Israel
and the land that you have given us,
as you swore to our ancestors,
a land flowing with milk and honey.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: A tithe is a tenth part.
> Every third year the tithe belongs to the poor (see Chapter 14:28–29). Afterward, people should declare before God: I really did pass it on.
> “For the dead”: Among some nations, people put food in the grave with the dead or made sacrifices for them. Israel should not do that with the holy tithe.
> At the end there is a beautiful prayer: “Look down … from heaven, and bless your people Israel.” Whoever shares with the poor may ask for God’s blessing.

## God and his people belong together (Verses 16–19)

[16] Today the LORD your God commands you to do these rules and laws.
So keep them and do them with all your heart and with all your soul.
[17] Today you have declared that the LORD is your God.
You have declared that you will walk in his ways,
keep his rules, his commandments, and his laws,
and listen to his voice.
[18] And today the LORD has declared that you are his very own people,
as he promised you,
and that you shall keep all his commandments.
[19] He will set you high above all the nations that he has made,
to bring praise, fame, and honor.
And you shall be a holy people for the LORD your God,
as he has said.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is where the large part with the individual commandments ends (Chapters 12–26).
> It is like a wedding: Both sides make a declaration. The people say: The LORD is our God. And God says: You are my people. That is the heart of the covenant.
> “High above all the nations”: This does not mean that Israel should rule over others. It should be a people in whom you can see God’s goodness, “to bring praise, fame, and honor” to God.
