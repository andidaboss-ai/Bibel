# Deuteronomy – Chapter 12
### The one place that God chooses

---

## Destroy the places of the foreign gods (Verses 1–4)

[1] These are the rules and the laws that you shall be careful to do
in the land that the LORD, the God of your fathers, has given you to own,
all the days that you live on the earth.
[2] You shall completely destroy all the places
where the nations you are driving out served their gods:
on the high mountains, on the hills, and under every green tree.
[3] You shall tear down their altars, smash their stone pillars,
and burn their Asherah poles with fire.
You shall cut down the carved images of their gods,
and you shall wipe out their name from that place.
[4] You shall not do this to the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Chapter 12, the large part with the individual commandments begins. It goes on until Chapter 26.
> The Canaanites had holy places everywhere: on mountains and under large trees. Israel should destroy these places, so that it is not led away to the foreign gods.
> “You shall not do this to the LORD your God” means: You should not worship the LORD the way the other peoples worship their gods, at any place you like.
> This is about objects and places of worship, not about people. This text does not allow anyone today to damage the houses of worship of other religions.

---

## The place that God will choose (Verses 5–12)

[5] But you shall look for the place that the LORD your God will choose out of all your tribes
to put his name there.
You shall look for the place where he lives, and you shall go there.
[6] There you shall bring your burnt offerings and your sacrifices,
your tithes and the wave offering of your hand,
your vows and your freewill offerings,
and the firstborn of your herd and of your flock.
A tithe is a tenth of what you harvest or earn. A vow is a promise to God.
[7] There you shall eat before the LORD your God.
And you shall be glad about everything you have worked for with your hands,
you and your households, because the LORD your God has blessed you in it.
[8] You shall not do what we are doing here today,
where everyone does whatever seems right to him.
[9] For you have not yet come to the rest
and to the inheritance that the LORD your God is giving you.
[10] But you will cross the Jordan and live in the land
that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.
And he will give you rest from all your enemies around you, so that you live in safety.
[11] Then you shall bring everything that I command you
to the place that the LORD your God will choose to let his name live there:
your burnt offerings, your sacrifices, your tithes, the wave offering of your hand,
and all your best vows that you promise to the LORD.
[12] And you shall be glad before the LORD your God:
you, and your sons and your daughters, your male servants and your female servants,
and the Levite who lives within your gates,
because he has no share and no inheritance with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> God will choose a place where he “lets his name live.” The central holy place is to be there. The place is not named here yet. Later it was first Shiloh and then Jerusalem with the temple.
> “To let his name live there”: God himself is everywhere and cannot be shut up in a house. But at this place, people can reach him in a special way.
> “The wave offering of your hand” is a gift that was waved back and forth before God.
> Something stands out: Worship is a festival of joy! People eat together before God. And everyone is there: children, male and female servants, and the Levites who have no land of their own. No one should be left out.

---

## Offer sacrifices only at this one place (Verses 13–19)

[13] Be careful that you do not offer your burnt offerings at any place you see.
[14] But at the place that the LORD chooses in one of your tribes,
there you shall offer your burnt offerings,
and there you shall do everything that I command you.
[15] But you may kill animals and eat meat in all your towns,
as much as you like,
according to the blessing that the LORD your God has given you.
The unclean and the clean may eat of it,
as people eat the gazelle and the deer.
[16] Only you shall not eat the blood.
You shall pour it out on the ground like water.
[17] But in your towns you may not eat
the tithe of your grain, of your new wine, or of your oil,
or the firstborn of your herd or of your flock,
or any of your vows that you promise,
or your freewill offerings, or the wave offering of your hand.
[18] You shall eat them before the LORD your God,
at the place that the LORD your God will choose:
you, your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant,
and the Levite who lives within your gates.
And you shall be glad before the LORD your God about everything you have worked for with your hands.
[19] Be careful that you do not abandon the Levite as long as you live in your land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before this, people were allowed to eat meat almost only as a sacrifice at the holy place (Leviticus 17). But when the people live spread out over the whole land, that is no longer possible. That is why it is now true: For an ordinary meal, people may also kill animals at home.
> “As people eat the gazelle and the deer”: People were allowed to eat these wild animals, but not to sacrifice them. In the same way, ordinary meat may now be eaten without being a sacrifice. That is why people who are “unclean” at the moment may also eat of it.
> But sacrifices and holy gifts belong at the one place that God chooses.
> The Levites had no land of their own. That is why the others should take care of them.

## The blood is the life (Verses 20–28)

[20] The LORD your God will make your land larger, as he has promised you.
Then you will say, “I want to eat meat,”
because you would like to eat meat.
Then you may eat meat, as much as you like.
[21] Maybe the place that the LORD your God will choose to put his name there is too far away from you.
Then you shall kill animals from your herd and from your flock, which the LORD has given you,
as I have commanded you.
And you may eat in your towns, as much as you like.
[22] You shall eat it the way the gazelle and the deer are eaten.
The unclean and the clean may eat of it alike.
[23] Only be very sure that you do not eat the blood.
For the blood is the life.
You shall not eat the life with the meat.
[24] You shall not eat it.
You shall pour it out on the ground like water.
[25] You shall not eat it,
so that it may go well with you and with your children after you,
when you do what is right in the LORD’s eyes.
[26] Only your holy things that you have, and your vows,
you shall take and go with them to the place that the LORD will choose.
[27] You shall offer your burnt offerings, the meat and the blood, on the altar of the LORD your God.
The blood of your sacrifices shall be poured out on the altar of the LORD your God,
and you may eat the meat.
[28] Pay attention to all these words that I command you, and listen to them,
so that it may go well with you and with your children after you forever,
when you do what is good and right in the eyes of the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The blood is the life”: Back then, people understood blood as the place where life is. Life belongs to God. That is why people may not eat the blood. They give it back to him by pouring it out on the ground.
> The Jewish way of killing animals for food (“kosher”) comes from this commandment: All the blood must run out of the animal. There is a similar rule in Islam too.
> The commandment shows deep respect for life, also for the life of animals.

## Do not do as the nations do (Verses 29–32)

[29] The LORD your God will cut off the nations before you that you are going to drive out.
And you will drive them out and live in their land.
[30] Then be careful that you are not caught in the trap of copying them,
after they have been destroyed before you.
And do not ask about their gods and say,
“How did these nations serve their gods? I will do the same.”
[31] You shall not do this to the LORD your God.
For they have done for their gods everything that the LORD finds disgusting and hates.
They even burn their sons and their daughters in the fire for their gods.
[32] Everything that I command you, you shall be careful to do.
You shall not add anything to it, and you shall not take anything away from it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The most disgusting thing of all: Some peoples sacrificed their own children to their gods. God hates that with all his heart. Already with Abraham, God showed that he does not want human sacrifices (Genesis 22).
> Being curious about foreign religions is not bad in itself. But Israel should not take over customs that are against God’s will, especially customs that harm people.
> In some Bibles, verse 32 is already the first verse of Chapter 13 (13:1). We follow the verse numbers of the WEB.
