# Deuteronomy – Chapter 2
### Past Edom, Moab and Ammon – the victory over Sihon

---

## Edom: Leave your brothers in peace (Verses 1–8)

[1] Then we turned and went into the wilderness, on the way to the Red Sea,
as the LORD had told me.
And for many days we went around Mount Seir.
[2] The LORD spoke to me. He said,
[3] “You have gone around this mountain long enough.
Turn toward the north.
[4] Command the people:
‘You will pass through the land of your brothers, the children of Esau, who live in Seir.
They will be afraid of you.
So be very careful.
[5] Do not start a quarrel with them.
For I will not give you any of their land,
not even enough for the sole of a foot to step on.
For I have given Mount Seir to Esau as his own.
[6] You shall buy food from them with money, so that you can eat.
You shall also buy water from them with money, so that you can drink.’”
[7] For the LORD your God has blessed you in all the work of your hands.
He has known how you walked through this great wilderness.
For these forty years the LORD your God has been with you.
You have not lacked anything.
[8] So we passed by our brothers, the children of Esau, who live in Seir.
We went away from the road through the Arabah, from Elath and from Ezion Geber.
We turned and went on along the way of the wilderness of Moab.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Edomites are descendants of Esau, the brother of Jacob. That is why Moses calls them “your brothers.”
> God did not give land only to Israel. He also gave other peoples their land. Israel should respect that. It should buy fairly and not rob.
> Verse 7 is a beautiful look back: “You have not lacked anything.” Even in the wilderness, God took care of everything.

---

## Moab: God gave them their land (Verses 9–12)

[9] The LORD said to me,
“Do not trouble Moab, and do not start a war with them.
For I will not give you any of his land as your own.
For I have given Ar to the children of Lot as their own.”
[10] (The Emim lived there before. They were a great people, with many people, and as tall as the Anakim.
[11] They are also counted as Rephaim, like the Anakim.
But the Moabites call them Emim.
[12] The Horites also lived in Seir in the past.
But the children of Esau drove them out.
They destroyed them and lived in their place,
just as Israel did to the land that it owns, which the LORD gave to them.)

> **What does this mean?**
> The Moabites come from Lot, the nephew of Abraham. So they are relatives too.
> “Ar” is a city and an area in Moab.
> The verses in parentheses are a kind of explanation that was put into the text. They tell who lived in these lands before. “Rephaim,” “Emim” and “Anakim” were names for ancient peoples of very tall people. People told many stories about them.
> The thought behind it: Other peoples also received their land from God. God is not only the God of Israel. He guides the history of all peoples.

---

## Thirty-eight years (Verses 13–15)

[13] “Now get up and cross over the brook Zered.”
And we crossed over the brook Zered.
[14] The time from when we left Kadesh Barnea until we crossed the brook Zered
was thirty-eight years.
In that time the whole generation of the men of war had died out from the camp,
as the LORD had sworn to them.
[15] And the LORD’s hand was against them,
to destroy them from the middle of the camp, until they were all gone.

> **What does this mean?**
> When they cross the brook Zered, a new part begins. The old generation has died. Now the new generation is here.
> 38 years: Add the roughly two years from leaving Egypt to Kadesh. Together that makes the 40 years in the wilderness.

---

## Ammon: No war here either (Verses 16–23)

[16] When all the men of war among the people had died out and were dead,
[17] the LORD spoke to me. He said,
[18] “Today you will pass by Ar, at the border of Moab.
[19] When you come near the people of Ammon,
do not trouble them, and do not start a quarrel with them.
For I will not give you any of the land of the people of Ammon as your own.
For I have given it to the children of Lot as their own.”
[20] (That land is also counted as a land of Rephaim.
Rephaim lived there in the past.
But the Ammonites call them Zamzummim.
[21] They were a great people, with many people, and as tall as the Anakim.
But the LORD destroyed them before Israel.
And they drove them out and lived in their place.
[22] He had done the same for the children of Esau, who live in Seir,
when he destroyed the Horites before them.
They drove them out and lived in their place, until this day.
[23] And the Avvim, who lived in villages as far as Gaza:
The Caphtorim, who came from Caphtor, destroyed them and lived in their place.)

> **What does this mean?**
> In verse 21, the WEB says “before Israel.” But the Hebrew text says “before them,” and it means the Ammonites: They drove out the Zamzummim and live in their place.
> The Ammonites are also descendants of Lot. Israel should leave them in peace.
> “Caphtor” is probably the island of Crete. The “Caphtorim” are probably the Philistines, who came from there to the coast near Gaza.

---

## God gives Sihon into Israel’s hand (Verses 24–25)

[24] “Get up, set out, and cross the valley of the Arnon.
Look! I have given Sihon the Amorite, the king of Heshbon, and his land into your hand.
Begin to take it as your own, and fight against him.
[25] Today I will begin to put terror and fear of you
on the peoples under the whole sky.
They will hear about you. They will tremble and be in great fear because of you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> With Edom, Moab and Ammon, God said: No war! With Sihon, it is different. His land belongs to the area that Israel is supposed to receive.
> The Arnon is a deep river valley that flows into the Dead Sea. It was the border between Moab and the kingdom of Sihon.

---

## Israel asks for peace (Verses 26–30)

[26] I sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon, the king of Heshbon,
with words of peace. I had them say:
[27] “Let me pass through your land.
I will stay on the highway.
I will not turn to the right or to the left.
[28] Sell me food for money, so that I can eat.
And give me water for money, so that I can drink.
Just let me pass through on foot,
[29] as the children of Esau, who live in Seir, did for me,
and the Moabites, who live in Ar,
until I cross the Jordan into the land that the LORD our God is giving us.”
[30] But Sihon, the king of Heshbon, would not let us pass through his land.
For the LORD your God had made his spirit hard and his heart stubborn,
so that he could give him into your hand, as it is today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel first offers peace. It only wants to pass through and pay for everything. Only when Sihon refuses and attacks does war come (see also Numbers 21:21–31).
> “God made his heart hard”: We know this from Pharaoh in Egypt. The Bible says with this: Even Sihon’s stubbornness is part of God’s plan. This does not mean that Sihon had no choice. Jews and Christians have often said: God makes stronger what a person already wants.

---

## The victory over Sihon (Verses 31–37)

[31] The LORD said to me,
“Look! I have begun to give Sihon and his land over to you.
Begin to take it, so that you inherit his land.”
[32] Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Jahaz.
[33] The LORD our God gave him over to us.
We struck him down, with his sons and all his people.
[34] At that time we took all his cities.
And we completely destroyed every city where people lived, with the women and the little children.
We left no one alive.
[35] Only the livestock we took as plunder for ourselves,
together with the plunder from the cities that we had taken.
[36] From Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, and from the city in the valley, all the way to Gilead,
no city was too high for us.
The LORD our God gave them all over to us.
[37] Only the land of the people of Ammon you did not come near:
the whole bank of the river Jabbok, the cities in the hill country,
and every place that the LORD our God had forbidden us.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Completely destroyed” (in Hebrew: “cherem”) means here: All the people in the cities were killed, even women and children. No one was allowed to keep any of it for themselves. It was given over completely to God.
> This is one of the hardest passages in the Bible. We must not make it sound nicer than it is. By our understanding today, this is a grave injustice.
> Jews and Christians have struggled with texts like this for a long time. Some scholars say: This language is typical of war reports in the ancient Near East, and it often exaggerates. Others say: The Bible shows honestly how brutally people fought wars back then. Many stress: These commands were tied to a one-time situation and do not apply to other times.
> One thing is very clear: No one may use this passage to justify violence against people. The Bible itself shows another way: love for the foreigner (Deuteronomy 10:19), the peace of the prophets (Isaiah 2:4), and the commandment of Jesus: “Love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44).
> Here too, Israel respects the border of Ammon. It takes only what God has allowed.
