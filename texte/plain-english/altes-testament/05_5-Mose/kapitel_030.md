# Deuteronomy – Chapter 30
### Turning back is possible – choose life!

---

## If you turn back, God will gather you again (Verses 1–10)

[1] All these things will come on you,
the blessing and the curse that I have set before you.
Then you will think about them
among all the nations where the LORD your God has driven you.
[2] You will turn back to the LORD your God
and listen to his voice,
just as I command you today,
you and your children,
with all your heart and with all your soul.
[3] Then the LORD your God will set you free from captivity
and have mercy on you.
He will turn to you again and gather you
from all the peoples where the LORD your God has scattered you.
[4] Even if your people who were driven out are at the very end of the sky,
the LORD your God will gather you from there,
and he will bring you back from there.
[5] The LORD your God will bring you into the land that your ancestors owned,
and you will own it.
He will do you good,
and he will make you more in number than your ancestors.
[6] The LORD your God will circumcise your heart
and the heart of your descendants.
Then you will love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul,
so that you may live.
[7] The LORD your God will put all these curses on your enemies,
and on those who hate you and who persecuted you.
[8] But you will turn back and listen to the voice of the LORD,
and do all his commandments that I command you today.
[9] The LORD your God will give you success in all the work of your hands,
with many children, many young animals, and good crops from your ground.
For the LORD will again be glad to do you good,
as he was glad over your ancestors.
[10] This will happen if you listen to the voice of the LORD your God
and keep his commandments and his rules
that are written in this book of the law,
and if you turn back to the LORD your God
with all your heart and with all your soul.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the heavy curses in Chapters 28 and 29, now comes a great hope: Even if everything seems lost, even if the people are scattered over the whole world, they can turn back. And then God will gather them again.
> The word “turn back” (in Hebrew “shuv”) comes up again and again in this section. It is one of the most important words of the Bible. Turning back means: turning to God again. And God also “turns” to his people again.
> Verse 6 is a great promise: God himself will “circumcise” the heart, that is, open it. In 10:16 the people were supposed to do it themselves. Here God promises: I will do it for you. The prophets Jeremiah and Ezekiel later speak of a “new heart” that God will give (Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 36:26).
> God “is glad” to do good to his people. He does not like to punish. He likes to bless.
> In all the centuries of being scattered, Jewish people have read these verses as a comfort.

## God’s word is very near (Verses 11–14)

[11] For this commandment that I command you today
is not too hard for you, and it is not too far away.
[12] It is not in heaven,
so that you would have to say,
“Who will go up to heaven for us, bring it down to us,
and tell it to us, so that we can do it?”
[13] It is also not on the other side of the sea,
so that you would have to say,
“Who will go across the sea for us, bring it to us,
and tell it to us, so that we can do it?”
[14] No, the word is very near to you.
It is in your mouth and in your heart,
so that you can do it.

> **What does this mean?**
> A wonderful comfort: God’s will is not out of reach. You do not have to climb up to heaven or travel to the end of the world to find it. You do not have to be a scholar. God’s word is very near: in your mouth when you speak it, and in your heart when you love it.
> That is also why it is so important that every person can read and understand the Bible in his or her own language.
> Paul quotes these verses and says: The word is near, namely the word about faith in Jesus Christ (Romans 10:6–10).

## Choose life! (Verses 15–20)

[15] Look, today I set before you life and good,
death and evil.
[16] For today I command you to love the LORD your God,
to walk in his ways,
and to keep his commandments, his rules, and his laws.
Then you will live and become many,
and the LORD your God will bless you in the land
that you are going into to take over.
[17] But your heart may turn away, and you may not listen.
You may let yourself be led away,
and worship other gods and serve them.
[18] Then I tell you today: You will surely die.
You will not live long in the land
that you are crossing the Jordan to go into and take over.
[19] Today I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you:
I have set before you life and death,
the blessing and the curse.
So choose life, so that you may live,
you and your descendants.
[20] Choose to love the LORD your God,
to listen to his voice, and to hold on to him.
For he is your life and gives you a long life.
Then you will live in the land
that the LORD swore to give to your ancestors,
to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the high point of the great speeches of Moses. He sets the people before a decision between two ways: life or death, blessing or curse.
> And then he says what he wishes for: “Choose life!” God forces no one. Every person has the freedom to choose. But God wishes with all his heart that we choose life.
> What does “choose life” mean? Loving God, listening to him, and holding on to him. For: “He is your life.” Whoever holds on to God holds on to the source of life.
> This sentence “Choose life” has become a guiding word for many people to this day.
