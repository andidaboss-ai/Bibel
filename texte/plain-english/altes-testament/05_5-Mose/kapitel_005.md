# Deuteronomy – Chapter 5
### The Ten Commandments

---

## The covenant is for you today (Verses 1–5)

[1] Moses called all Israel together and said to them,
“Hear, Israel, the rules and the laws that I am speaking in your ears today.
Learn them, and be careful to do them.”
[2] The LORD our God made a covenant with us at Horeb.
[3] The LORD did not make this covenant with our ancestors,
but with us, with all of us who are alive here today.
[4] The LORD spoke with you face to face on the mountain, out of the middle of the fire.
[5] (At that time I stood between the LORD and you, to tell you the LORD’s word.
For you were afraid because of the fire, and you did not go up onto the mountain.)
He said,

> **What does this mean?**
> Actually, most of the people who were at Horeb had already died. Even so, Moses says: The covenant was made with us, with you who are alive today.
> That means: God’s covenant is not only something from the past. It is for every new generation. To this day, Jews say at the Passover festival: Everyone should see himself as if he himself had come out of Egypt.
> “Face to face” means here: directly, personally. The people did not see any form of God (Chapter 4:12), but they heard his voice directly.

---

## I am the LORD your God (Verse 6)

[6] “I am the LORD your God.
I brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ten Commandments were already in Exodus 20. Here Moses repeats them for the new generation. The words are almost the same, but there are a few small differences. We point them out in the explanations.
> Again, the beginning is not a demand, but a reminder: God has set you free. The commandments are rules for free people.

---

## No other gods, no image (Verses 7–10)

[7] You shall have no other gods before me.
[8] You shall not make a carved image for yourself,
no image at all of anything
that is in heaven above, or on the earth below, or in the water under the earth.
[9] You shall not bow down to them, and you shall not serve them.
For I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God.
For those who hate me, I bring the guilt of the fathers
on the children, to the third and to the fourth generation.
[10] But to those who love me and keep my commandments,
I show my loving kindness to thousands.

> **What does this mean?**
> God alone should be God. And you should not make an image of him, because he is greater than anything we can imagine (see Chapter 4).
> The Hebrew says “to thousands.” Many understand this as “to a thousand generations.”
> The balance is important: The results of guilt reach at most four generations. But God’s kindness reaches much further. His love is greater than his anger.
> Later God says clearly: Children should not be punished for the guilt of their parents (Deuteronomy 24:16; Ezekiel 18:20). What is meant here is rather: Bad behavior often lives on in families when the children copy it.

---

## Do not misuse God’s name (Verse 11)

[11] You shall not misuse the name of the LORD your God.
For the LORD will not let anyone go unpunished who misuses his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s name is holy. You should not use it to curse, for false oaths, or to cheat people. The worst thing is to misuse God’s name for injustice and violence.

---

## The Sabbath (Verses 12–15)

[12] Keep the Sabbath day and make it holy,
as the LORD your God has commanded you.
[13] For six days you shall work and do all your work.
[14] But the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God.
On it you shall not do any work:
not you, not your son, not your daughter,
not your male servant, not your female servant,
not your ox, not your donkey, none of your livestock,
and not the foreigner who lives within your gates,
so that your male servant and your female servant can rest as well as you.
[15] Remember that you yourself were a servant in the land of Egypt.
And the LORD your God brought you out of there by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm.
That is why the LORD your God has commanded you to keep the Sabbath day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here there is a beautiful difference from Exodus 20:
> There it says “Remember the Sabbath day.” Here it says “Keep the Sabbath day.”
> There the reason is: God made the world in six days and rested on the seventh. Here the reason is: You yourself were a slave in Egypt. That is why your male servants and female servants should also be allowed to rest, “as well as you.”
> So the Sabbath is also a social commandment. No one should have to work without a break. Even the weakest people, and even the animals, have a right to rest. Nothing like that existed anywhere else back then.
> The servants mentioned here were often slaves. The WEB uses the word “servant.”

---

## Honor your parents (Verse 16)

[16] Honor your father and your mother,
as the LORD your God has commanded you,
so that your days may be long and so that it may go well with you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is meant above all for grown-up children and their old parents. Back then there were no pensions and no nursing homes. The children were supposed to take care of their parents and honor them.
> Here there is something extra: “so that it may go well with you” (verse 16). It is missing in Exodus 20:12. A society where old people are respected is good for everyone.

---

## Life, marriage, property, truth (Verses 17–20)

[17] You shall not murder.
[18] You shall not commit adultery.
Adultery means: A married person sleeps with someone who is not their husband or wife.
[19] You shall not steal.
[20] You shall not tell lies in court against your neighbor.

> **What does this mean?**
> These four short commandments protect how people live together:
> The life of every person is holy. No one may murder another person.
> Marriage should be protected. Faithfulness is important.
> You should not take away what belongs to another person.
> In court and everywhere, you should tell the truth. A lie can bring disaster on an innocent person.

---

## Do not covet (Verse 21)

[21] You shall not covet your neighbor’s wife.
To covet means: to badly want something that belongs to someone else.
And you shall not long for your neighbor’s house,
or his field, or his male servant, or his female servant,
or his ox, or his donkey,
or anything that belongs to your neighbor.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The last commandment goes deeper than all the others. It is not about deeds, but about wishes in the heart. Envy and greed are often the beginning of stealing, adultery, and even murder.
> A small difference from Exodus 20: There the house comes first, here the wife. And here the field is also named, because the people will soon own fields in the land.
> Because of this verse, Catholics and Lutherans count two commandments here: the ninth (the wife) and the tenth (the property). Others count it as one commandment.

---

## The people are afraid (Verses 22–27)

[22] The LORD spoke these words to your whole assembly on the mountain,
out of the middle of the fire, out of the cloud, and out of the thick darkness, with a loud voice.
And he added nothing more.
He wrote them on two stone tablets and gave them to me.
[23] When you heard the voice out of the middle of the darkness, while the mountain was burning with fire,
you came to me, all the leaders of your tribes and your elders.
[24] You said,
“Look! The LORD our God has shown us his glory and his greatness.
And we have heard his voice out of the middle of the fire.
Today we have seen that God speaks with a person, and the person stays alive.
[25] But now, why should we die?
For this great fire will burn us up.
If we hear the voice of the LORD our God any longer, we will die.
[26] For who of all people has heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the middle of the fire, as we have,
and stayed alive?
[27] You go near and listen to everything that the LORD our God will say.
And you tell us everything that the LORD our God tells you.
We will listen to it and do it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “He added nothing more”: God spoke the Ten Commandments directly to everyone. They are something very special. All the other commandments he gave through Moses.
> God’s nearness is overwhelming. The people are afraid and ask: Moses, be our go-between.

---

## God answers (Verses 28–33)

[28] The LORD heard what you said to me.
And the LORD said to me,
“I have heard what this people has said to you.
Everything they have said is good.
[29] Oh, if only they would always have such a heart
to fear me and keep all my commandments,
so that it would go well with them and with their children forever!
[30] Go and tell them, ‘Return to your tents.’
[31] But you, stay here and stand by me.
I will tell you all the commandments, the rules, and the laws that you shall teach them,
so that they do them in the land that I am giving them to own.”
[32] So be careful to do what the LORD your God has commanded you.
Do not turn away from it, not to the right and not to the left.
[33] Walk on the whole way that the LORD your God has commanded you,
so that you may live and that it may go well with you,
and so that you may live long in the land that you will own.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29 shows God’s heart. He sighs like a father: “Oh, if only they would always have such a heart to fear me and keep all my commandments!” God wants nothing more than for his people to be well.
> The goal of all the commandments is in verse 33: “so that you may live and that it may go well with you.” God’s commandments are like a path. Whoever stays on it will reach the goal safely.
