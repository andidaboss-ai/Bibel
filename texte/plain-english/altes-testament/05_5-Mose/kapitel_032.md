# Deuteronomy – Chapter 32
### The song of Moses

---

## Listen, heaven and earth! (Verses 1–4)

[1] Listen, you heavens, and I will speak!
Let the earth hear the words of my mouth!
[2] Let my teaching fall like rain.
Let my words come down like the dew,
like gentle rain on young grass,
like showers on the plants.
[3] For I will announce the name of the LORD.
Tell how great our God is!
[4] The Rock: His work is perfect,
for all his ways are just.
He is a faithful God who does no wrong.
He is fair and right.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the song that Moses is to teach the people (31:19). It is a poem with strong pictures. It tells the story between God and his people: God’s faithfulness, Israel’s unfaithfulness, the punishment, and in the end the rescue.
> God’s word should be like gentle rain that makes the grass grow. It should not crush, but bring life.
> “The Rock”: That is the most important picture for God in this song. It comes up again and again. A rock is firm, safe, and cannot be shaken. You can build on it. You can rely on it.

---

## An ungrateful people (Verses 5–6)

[5] They have acted wickedly against him.
They are not his children, because of their fault.
They are a twisted and crooked generation.
[6] Is this how you pay back the LORD,
you foolish people without wisdom?
Is he not your father, who bought you?
He made you, and he set you firmly in place.

> **What does this mean?**
> The contrast is great: God is perfect and faithful. The people are twisted and unfaithful.
> Here God is called “father.” That is still rare in the Old Testament. He “bought” Israel, that is, he set them free from slavery, like a father who pays to free his child.

---

## How God found his people and carried them (Verses 7–14)

[7] Remember the days of long ago.
Think about the years of many generations.
Ask your father, and he will show you.
Ask your elders, and they will tell you.
[8] When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance,
when he divided up the human race,
he set the borders of the peoples
according to the number of the people of Israel.
[9] For the LORD’s share is his people.
Jacob is the part he chose as his own.
[10] He found him in a desert land,
in an empty wilderness where the wind howls.
He put his arms around him and took care of him.
He guarded him like the pupil of his eye.
[11] Like an eagle that stirs up its nest
and hovers over its young,
he spread out his wings and took them.
He carried them on his feathers.
[12] The LORD alone led him.
No foreign god was with him.
[13] He let him ride over the heights of the earth.
He ate the crops of the field.
He let him suck honey out of the rock,
and oil out of the hard stone,
[14] butter from the cattle and milk from the flock,
with the fat of lambs,
rams of the breed of Bashan, and goats,
with the finest wheat.
And you drank wine from the blood of the grapes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Like the usual Hebrew text, the WEB, our English source text, says “according to the number of the people of Israel.” But old manuscripts from the Dead Sea and the Greek translation say: according to the number of the sons of God. Then it would mean: God divided up all the nations, but he kept his own people for himself.
> “Like the pupil of his eye”: The eye is the most sensitive part we have. We protect it at once when it is in danger. That is how God protects his people. The WEB says “the apple of his eye.” People still use this phrase today.
> The picture of the eagle (verse 11) is one of the most beautiful in the Bible: An eagle teaches its young to fly. It stirs them out of the nest. When they fall, it flies under them and catches them on its wings. That is how God led Israel through the wilderness: He challenged them, but never let them fall.
> “Honey out of the rock”: In cracks in the rocks there were sometimes wild bees. God gave his people good things even in barren places.

---

## The people grow fat and forget God (Verses 15–18)

[15] But Jeshurun grew fat and kicked.
You grew fat, thick, and well fed.
Then he left God, who made him,
and rejected the Rock who saved him.
[16] They made him jealous with foreign gods.
They made him angry with things he hates.
[17] They sacrificed to demons, not to God,
to gods that they did not know,
to new gods that had come along only recently,
gods that your ancestors did not fear.
[18] You forgot the Rock who became your father.
You forgot the God who gave birth to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Jeshurun” is a loving name for Israel. It means something like “the upright one.” The very one who should be upright becomes unfaithful. That sounds almost ironic.
> The picture: An animal that is fed well becomes wild and kicks with its hooves. In the same way, Israel becomes proud in its wealth and forgets God. We already know this danger from Chapter 8.
> Verse 18 is amazing: Here God is compared with a father who fathers a child and with a mother who gives birth. God’s love is like the love of a father and a mother at the same time.

---

## God hides his face (Verses 19–27)

[19] The LORD saw it, and he rejected them,
because his sons and his daughters had made him angry.
[20] He said, “I will hide my face from them.
I will see what their end will be.
For they are a very twisted generation,
children who are not faithful.
[21] They have made me jealous with what is not a god.
They have made me angry with their worthless idols.
So I will make them jealous with those who are not a people.
I will make them angry with a foolish nation.
[22] For a fire has been lit by my anger.
It burns down to the lowest depths of Sheol (the world of the dead).
It eats up the earth and its crops,
and it sets the foundations of the mountains on fire.
[23] I will pile disasters on them.
I will shoot all my arrows at them.
[24] They will waste away from hunger.
They will be eaten up by burning fever and bitter disease.
I will send the teeth of wild animals against them,
and the poison of snakes that crawl in the dust.
[25] Outside, the sword will take their children.
And in the rooms there will be terror
for the young man and the young woman,
for the baby at the breast and for the man with gray hair.
[26] I said I would scatter them far away.
I would wipe out the memory of them among people,
[27] if I did not fear what the enemy would do to provoke me.
Their enemies might misunderstand it.
They might say, ‘Our own power has won.
The LORD has not done all this.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Hebrew the world of the dead is called “Sheol.” Back then people imagined that the dead were in a dark place under the earth.
> Verse 21: “Those who are not a people”: Israel made God jealous with “not-gods.” So God will make Israel jealous with a “not-people.” Paul later explains it like this: God turns to the other nations, so that Israel becomes jealous and finds its way back to him (Romans 10:19; 11:11).
> Verses 26–27: God could destroy his people completely. But he does not do it. Why? Otherwise the enemies would think that they had won by their own strength. God’s name and honor are at stake. And behind this stands his faithfulness: He does not give up his people completely.

---

## The enemies do not understand it (Verses 28–35)

[28] For they are a nation without good sense.
There is no understanding in them.
[29] If only they were wise and understood this!
If only they would think about how things will end for them!
[30] How could one man chase a thousand,
and two put ten thousand to flight,
unless their Rock had sold them,
and the LORD had handed them over?
[31] For their rock is not like our Rock.
Even our enemies have to admit it.
[32] For their vine comes from the vine of Sodom
and from the fields of Gomorrah.
Their grapes are poisonous grapes.
Their bunches are bitter.
[33] Their wine is the poison of snakes,
the cruel poison of cobras.
[34] “Is this not stored up with me,
sealed up in my treasure rooms?
[35] Revenge belongs to me, and paying back,
at the time when their foot slips.
For the day of their disaster is near.
What is coming for them is rushing toward them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> It is not quite clear who is meant in verses 28–33: Israel or the enemies. Many interpreters think: first Israel, who does not understand why it is losing. Then the enemies, whose “rock,” that is, their gods, is worthless, and whose wickedness is like poisonous wine.
> Verse 30: Israel was not defeated because the enemies were so strong, but because God no longer protected his people.
> “Revenge belongs to me”: That is a very important sentence. It means: Revenge is God’s business, not the business of people. Paul quotes it and says: “Don’t seek revenge yourselves, beloved, but leave room for God’s wrath” (Romans 12:19). So this sentence actually forbids human revenge.

---

## God has mercy (Verses 36–43)

[36] For the LORD will bring justice to his people
and have mercy on his servants,
when he sees that their strength is gone
and no one is left, neither slave nor free.
[37] He will say, “Where are their gods,
the rock in which they looked for safety,
[38] the gods who ate the fat of their sacrifices
and drank the wine of their drink offerings?
Let them get up and help you!
Let them protect you!
[39] Now see that I, I am he.
There is no god beside me.
I kill, and I make alive.
I wound, and I heal.
And no one can rescue anyone from my hand.
[40] For I lift up my hand to heaven and say:
As surely as I live forever,
[41] when I sharpen my flashing sword
and my hand takes hold of it to judge,
I will take revenge on my enemies,
and I will pay back those who hate me.
[42] I will make my arrows drunk with blood.
My sword will eat flesh,
with the blood of the dead and of the prisoners,
from the heads of the enemy’s leaders.”
[43] Rejoice, you nations, with his people!
For he will avenge the blood of his servants.
He will take revenge on his enemies,
and he will make atonement for his land and for his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> The turning point: When the people are at the very end, without strength and without hope, God has mercy. The false gods did not help. But the true God helps.
> “I kill, and I make alive. I wound, and I heal”: Everything lies in God’s hand, life and death. But the goal is life and healing.
> The pictures in verses 41–42 are very harsh: God as a warrior with sword and arrows. These are pictures from the language of that time. They say: God will not let evil go unpunished. He stands on the side of the oppressed. But these pictures are no call for people to use violence. Revenge remains God’s business alone (verse 35).
> At the end there is a surprising call: “Rejoice, you nations, with his people!” The other nations too should share in the joy. Paul quotes this verse as a sign that God’s rescue is for all nations (Romans 15:10).
> The last word of the song is “atonement.” Atonement means: God takes away the guilt and makes peace again.

## The law is your life (Verses 44–47)

[44] Moses came and spoke all the words of this song
so that the people heard them,
he and Joshua the son of Nun.
[45] When Moses had finished speaking all these words to all Israel,
[46] he said to them,
“Take to heart all the words that I am warning you with today.
You shall command your children
to be careful to do all the words of this law.
[47] For this is not an empty word for you.
It is your life.
And through this word you will live a long time in the land
that you are crossing the Jordan to take over.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In verse 44 the Hebrew text does not say Joshua, but Hoshea. That was Joshua’s original name (Numbers 13:16). The WEB, our English source text, writes “Joshua.”
> “This is not an empty word for you. It is your life”: That is what Moses leaves behind for his people. God’s word is not just a collection of rules. It is the foundation of life.

---

## Moses is to climb Mount Nebo (Verses 48–52)

[48] On that same day the LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[49] “Go up into these mountains of Abarim, to Mount Nebo.
It is in the land of Moab, across from Jericho.
Look at the land of Canaan,
which I am giving to the people of Israel to own.
[50] Die on the mountain that you climb,
and be gathered to your people,
just as your brother Aaron died on Mount Hor
and was gathered to his people.
[51] This is because you were unfaithful to me among the people of Israel
at the waters of Meribah of Kadesh, in the wilderness of Zin.
You did not honor me as holy among the people of Israel.
[52] You will see the land from far away.
But you will not go into the land that I am giving to the people of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the time has come. Moses is to climb a mountain, see the land from above, and then die.
> Mount Nebo is in today’s Jordan. From its top, in clear weather, you can see far into the land, across the Jordan Valley to Jericho, and, it is said, on good days as far as the mountains of Jerusalem.
> “Be gathered to your people”: That is how the Bible speaks of death. You do not go into nothingness, but to those who have gone ahead of you.
