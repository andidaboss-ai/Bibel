# Deuteronomy – Chapter 10
### New tablets – and what God wants from you

---

## The new stone tablets (Verses 1–5)

[1] At that time the LORD said to me,
“Cut two stone tablets like the first ones,
and come up to me on the mountain.
And make an ark out of wood.
[2] I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you broke.
And you shall put them in the ark.”
[3] So I made an ark out of acacia wood,
and I cut two stone tablets like the first ones.
And I went up onto the mountain with the two tablets in my hand.
[4] He wrote on the tablets, just as it was written the first time, the Ten Commandments,
which the LORD had spoken to you on the mountain out of the middle of the fire, on the day of the assembly.
And the LORD gave them to me.
[5] I turned and came down from the mountain.
I put the tablets in the ark that I had made.
And there they are, as the LORD commanded me.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the golden calf, God gives a new beginning. The covenant is renewed. God writes the same words once more.
> That is a great message: Even after grave guilt, a new beginning is possible. God does not give up.
> The “ark” is a chest. Later it was called the “ark of the covenant,” the holiest thing in Israel (Exodus 25:10–22).

---

## An added note: Aaron’s death and the Levites (Verses 6–9)

[6] (The people of Israel traveled from Beeroth Bene Jaakan to Moserah.
There Aaron died, and there he was buried.
And his son Eleazar served as priest in his place.
[7] From there they traveled to Gudgodah, and from Gudgodah to Jotbathah, a land with brooks of water.
[8] At that time the LORD set apart the tribe of Levi
to carry the ark of the LORD’s covenant,
to stand before the LORD to serve him,
and to bless in his name, to this day.
[9] That is why Levi has no share and no inheritance with his brothers.
The LORD is his inheritance, as the LORD your God told him.)

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses in parentheses are a short explanation that was put into the speech.
> Numbers 20 and 33 say that Aaron died on Mount Hor. Here the place is called Moserah. Many interpreters think: Moserah was the area where Mount Hor is. No one knows for sure.
> Why is this here? Maybe because Moses has just told how God listened to his prayer for Aaron (Chapter 9:20). Aaron was allowed to live on and be priest, and after him his son.
> The Levites do not get their own land. But they have something better: “The LORD is his inheritance.” Their wealth is being close to God.

## God listens once more (Verses 10–11)

[10] I stayed on the mountain as the first time, 40 days and 40 nights.
And the LORD listened to me that time too.
The LORD did not want to destroy you.
[11] The LORD said to me,
“Get up, set out, and go ahead of the people.
They shall go in and take as their own the land
that I swore to their ancestors to give them.”

---

## What God wants from you (Verses 12–16)

[12] And now, Israel, what does the LORD your God ask of you?
Only this: that you fear the LORD your God,
that you walk in all his ways, that you love him,
and that you serve the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul,
[13] that you keep the LORD’s commandments and rules, which I command you today,
for your good.
[14] Look! Heaven belongs to the LORD your God, and the highest heaven,
and the earth with everything on it.
[15] Even so, the LORD took delight only in your ancestors and loved them.
And he chose their descendants after them, you, out of all peoples, as it is today.
[16] So circumcise the foreskin of your heart,
and do not be stiff-necked anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 is a wonderful summary. What does God want? Deep respect, obedience, love, and serving him with all your heart. And all of it “for your good.”
> Later the prophet Micah sums it up in a similar way: God has shown you, human being, what is good. And what does the LORD ask of you? To do what is just, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God (Micah 6:8).
> The whole universe belongs to God. And even so, he loves a small people. The great God turns to the small.
> “Circumcise the foreskin of your heart”: Circumcision was the sign of the covenant on the body (Genesis 17). But Moses says: That is not enough. The heart must also open itself to God. Away with everything that makes the heart hard and closed! Paul takes up this picture (Romans 2:29).
> “Stiff-necked” means: stubborn (see Chapter 9).

## God loves foreigners – love them too (Verses 17–22)

[17] For the LORD your God is God of gods and Lord of lords,
the great, mighty, and awesome God,
who does not favor anyone and does not take bribes.
[18] He gets justice for the orphan and the widow.
And he loves the foreigner and gives him food and clothing.
[19] So you too shall love the foreigner.
For you yourselves were foreigners in the land of Egypt.
[20] You shall fear the LORD your God. You shall serve him.
You shall hold on to him, and you shall swear by his name.
[21] He is the one you praise, and he is your God,
who has done for you these great and awesome things that your eyes have seen.
[22] Your ancestors went down into Egypt with 70 people.
And now the LORD your God has made you as many as the stars in the sky.

> **What does this mean?**
> The greatest and mightiest God cares for the weakest: for orphans, widows and foreigners. Back then these people had no protection.
> God cannot be bribed. With him it does not matter who is rich or powerful.
> Verse 19 is one of the most important commandments in the Bible: “So you too shall love the foreigner.” The reason: You yourselves were foreigners. You know what it feels like. This commandment is just as important today as it was back then.
> “Lord of lords”: In the book of Revelation, this title is used for Jesus (Revelation 19:16).
> There were 70 people at the beginning (Genesis 46:27). Now there is a great people. God has kept his promise to Abraham.
