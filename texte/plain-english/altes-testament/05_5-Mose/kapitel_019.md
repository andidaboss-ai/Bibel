# Deuteronomy – Chapter 19
### Cities of refuge – boundary stones – false witnesses

---

## Three cities of refuge in the land (Verses 1–10)

[1] The LORD your God will destroy the nations
whose land the LORD your God is giving you.
You will take their place
and live in their cities and in their houses.
[2] Then you shall set apart three cities for yourselves
in the middle of your land that the LORD your God is giving you to own.
[3] You shall prepare the roads to them.
And you shall divide the land that the LORD your God gives you as your inheritance
into three parts.
Then anyone who has killed a person can flee there.
[4] This is the rule for someone who has killed a person
and flees there to stay alive:
He killed his neighbor without meaning to,
and he did not hate him before.
[5] For example, a man goes into the forest with his neighbor to chop wood.
His hand swings the ax to cut down the tree.
The head of the ax slips off the handle and hits his neighbor, so that he dies.
That man shall flee to one of these cities and stay alive.
[6] Otherwise the avenger of blood might chase the man who killed,
while his heart is burning with anger.
He might catch him because the road is long,
and he might kill him,
even though the man did not deserve to die,
because he did not hate his neighbor before.
The avenger of blood is the relative who has the duty to punish the killer.
[7] That is why I command you:
Set apart three cities for yourselves.
[8] The LORD your God may make your land larger,
as he swore to your fathers.
He may give you all the land that he promised to give to your fathers.
[9] That will happen if you keep this whole commandment and do it,
which I command you today:
to love the LORD your God and to walk in his ways always.
Then you shall add three more cities for yourselves,
besides these three.
[10] This is so that innocent blood will not be shed
in your land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance.
Otherwise you would be guilty of that blood.

> **What does this mean?**
> We know the cities of refuge from Numbers 35. Here a clear example of an accident is given: While someone is chopping wood, the head of the ax flies off the handle and kills someone.
> The roads to the cities should be good. No one should be caught on the way only because the road is too long or too bad. Jewish teachers say: People even put up signs that said “Refuge.”
> God also protects the person who has become guilty without being at fault.

---

## No protection for murderers (Verses 11–13)

[11] But someone may hate his neighbor.
He lies in wait for him, attacks him, and strikes him so that he dies.
Then he flees into one of these cities.
[12] Then the elders of his town shall send for him and have him brought back from there.
They shall hand him over to the avenger of blood, so that he dies.
[13] You shall not feel sorry for him.
You shall remove the guilt of innocent blood from Israel.
Then things will go well for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The cities of refuge are not a hiding place for murderers. Whoever killed on purpose is handed over. Back then the death penalty applied to this (see Numbers 35).

---

## Do not move boundary stones (Verse 14)

[14] You shall not move your neighbor’s boundary stone
that the people of long ago set up.
This applies to the land that you will receive as your inheritance,
in the land that the LORD your God gives you to own.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, fields were marked with boundary stones. Whoever secretly moved a stone stole land from his neighbor. That was easy to do and hard to prove. That is why it is clearly forbidden here.

---

## Two witnesses – and the punishment for false witnesses (Verses 15–21)

[15] One witness alone shall not stand up against a man
for any wrong or for any sin that he has done.
A matter shall be settled only on the word of two witnesses,
or on the word of three witnesses.
[16] A dishonest witness may stand up against someone
to accuse him of doing wrong.
[17] Then both men who have the quarrel shall stand before the LORD,
before the priests and the judges who are there in those days.
[18] The judges shall look into it carefully.
The witness may turn out to be a false witness
who has told lies against his brother.
[19] Then you shall do to him what he planned to do to his brother.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.
[20] The rest of the people will hear about it and be afraid.
They will never again do such an evil thing among you.
[21] You shall not feel sorry:
life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

> **What does this mean?**
> A false witness gets exactly the punishment he wanted to bring on the innocent person. Whoever lies to put someone else in prison goes to prison himself. This keeps people from giving false testimony.
> “Eye for eye, tooth for tooth”: This sentence is often misunderstood, as if it called for revenge. The opposite is meant! It limits the punishment: The punishment must not be greater than the wrong. Not a life for an eye, but only an eye for an eye. Jewish teaching understood this very early as payment in money.
> Jesus goes even further: He says that you should not take revenge at all, but even turn the other cheek (Matthew 5:38–39).
