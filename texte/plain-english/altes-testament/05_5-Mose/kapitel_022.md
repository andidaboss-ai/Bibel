# Deuteronomy – Chapter 22
### Help for your neighbor – rules for marriage and the body

---

## Help your neighbor (Verses 1–4)

[1] You may see that your brother’s ox or his sheep has wandered off.
Then you shall not hide from it and look away.
You must bring it back to your brother.
[2] Your brother may not live near you,
or you may not know him.
Then you shall take the animal home to your house.
It shall stay with you until your brother comes looking for it.
Then you shall give it back to him.
[3] You shall do the same with his donkey.
You shall do the same with his coat.
You shall do the same with anything that your brother has lost and you have found.
You may not hide and look away.
[4] You may see that your brother’s donkey or his ox has fallen down on the road.
Then you shall not hide and look away.
You must help him lift them up again.

> **What does this mean?**
> “You may not hide and look away”: You may not look the other way when someone needs help. Whoever finds something must give it back. Whoever sees that someone is in trouble must help.
> Exodus 23:4–5 even says: This also applies to the animal of your enemy.
> This rule still applies today: Looking away is also a decision.

## Various rules (Verses 5–12)

[5] A woman shall not wear men’s clothing,
and a man shall not put on women’s clothing.
For the LORD your God hates it when someone does such things.
[6] On your way you may find a bird’s nest,
in a tree or on the ground,
with young birds or with eggs.
The mother bird is sitting on the young birds or on the eggs.
Then you shall not take the mother together with the young.
[7] You must let the mother go.
But you may take the young birds for yourself.
Then things will go well for you, and you will live a long time.
[8] When you build a new house,
you shall make a railing around your roof.
Then you will not bring the guilt of blood on your house
if someone falls from it.
[9] You shall not sow your vineyard with two kinds of seed.
Otherwise the whole harvest would become unclean:
the seed that you have sown and the harvest of the vineyard.
[10] You shall not plow with an ox and a donkey together.
[11] You shall not wear clothes in which wool and linen are woven together.
[12] You shall make tassels for yourself on the four corners of the coat
that you wrap around yourself.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: Back then, men and women wore very different clothing. Many interpreters think that this was mainly about pagan customs, in which people dressed up in worship for foreign gods. Others see it as a rule for keeping man and woman distinct. Jews and Christians think differently about how to apply this verse today. It is clear: It gives no one the right to look down on other people or to harm them.
> Verses 6–7: A small commandment with a great meaning: You should not exploit nature. Whoever takes the mother destroys the future. Whoever lets her fly away can harvest again. For this small commandment God promises the same as for the commandment to honor your parents: a long life.
> Verse 8: The roofs were flat. People sat and worked up there. A railing protected against accidents. Whoever does not build a railing shares the guilt if someone dies. That is an early rule for safety in building.
> Verses 9–11: Certain things should not be mixed. The exact reason is not given. Maybe it is meant to remind people of the order of creation, in which God made everything “according to its kind.” In plowing: An ox and a donkey are not equally strong. Together, the weaker animal would suffer.
> “Unclean”: That is what the WEB, our English source, means with “defiled.” The Hebrew word actually means “become holy.” It means: The harvest goes to the holy place, and you may not keep it for yourself.
> Verse 12: The tassels were meant to remind people of God’s commandments (Numbers 15:37–41). Jewish people still wear them today on the prayer shawl. Jesus also wore such a coat (Matthew 9:20).

## A false accusation against a wife (Verses 13–21)

[13] A man may marry a woman and sleep with her.
Then he hates her.
[14] He accuses her of shameful things,
and he gives her a bad name.
He says, “I married this woman.
But when I came near her,
I did not find the signs that she was a virgin.”
A virgin is a woman who has never slept with a man.
[15] Then the father and the mother of the young woman
shall take the signs that the young woman was a virgin.
They shall bring them to the elders of the town at the gate.
[16] The young woman’s father shall say to the elders,
“I gave my daughter to this man as his wife,
and now he hates her.
[17] Look, he accuses her of shameful things.
He says, ‘I did not find the signs that your daughter was a virgin.’
But here are the signs that my daughter was a virgin.”
And they shall spread out the cloth in front of the elders of the town.
[18] Then the elders of that town shall take the man and punish him.
[19] They shall make him pay a fine of one hundred shekels of silver.
They shall give it to the young woman’s father,
because the man has given a bad name to a virgin of Israel.
She shall stay his wife.
He may never send her away as long as he lives.
[20] But the thing may be true.
The signs that the young woman was a virgin may not have been found.
[21] Then they shall bring the young woman out to the door of her father’s house.
And the men of her town shall stone her with stones until she dies,
because she has done a shameful thing in Israel.
She acted like a prostitute in her father’s house.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> One hundred shekels of silver are about 2.5 pounds of silver. That was a lot of money.
> Back then, the “signs that she was a virgin” were a cloth with blood from the wedding night. Today we know: Such “proofs” are not medically reliable. Many women do not bleed the first time.
> This section protects the wife from a man who wants to get rid of her and therefore spreads lies about her. He is punished harshly and may never send her away. In this way she was provided for all her life.
> But verses 20–21 are shocking for us: A young woman is to be killed because she was no longer a virgin before marriage. By today’s understanding that is a grave injustice. Here the Bible describes an old law that placed the honor of the family very high.
> Sadly, in some places there are still so-called “honor killings” today. That is a crime. No one may use the Bible to defend it. Jesus protected a woman who was about to be stoned and said: “Is there anyone here who has never sinned? That person may throw the first stone at her” (John 8:7).

## Adultery and violence against women (Verses 22–29)

[22] A man may be found sleeping with a woman who is married to a husband.
Then both of them shall die,
the man who slept with the woman, and the woman.
So you shall remove the evil from Israel.
[23] A young woman who is a virgin may be promised in marriage to a man.
Another man meets her in the town and sleeps with her.
[24] Then you shall bring them both out to the gate of that town,
and you shall stone them with stones until they die:
the young woman, because she did not cry out for help, though she was in the town,
and the man, because he has humiliated his neighbor’s wife.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.
[25] But the man may meet the young woman who is promised in marriage out in the field.
The man forces her and sleeps with her.
Then only the man who slept with her shall die.
[26] But you shall do nothing to the young woman.
The young woman has done no sin that deserves death.
For this case is like when a man attacks his neighbor and kills him.
[27] For he met her in the field.
The young woman who was promised in marriage cried out,
but there was no one to save her.
[28] A man may meet a young woman who is a virgin
and who is not promised in marriage.
He grabs her and sleeps with her, and they are found out.
[29] Then the man who slept with her shall give the young woman’s father fifty shekels of silver.
And she shall become his wife, because he has humiliated her.
He may never send her away as long as he lives.

> **What does this mean?**
> Fifty shekels of silver are about 1¼ pounds of silver.
> Back then, being promised in marriage counted almost like a marriage.
> An important thought is in verses 25–27: If a man uses violence against a woman, she is innocent. The Bible compares rape with murder. And it says: We assume that the woman cried out for help, even if no one heard it. The guilt lies with the attacker alone.
> Verses 23–24, on the other hand, are hard to understand today: Back then, people assumed that a woman in the town could have called for help. Today we know that victims of violence often freeze with fear and cannot scream. No one may blame a victim.
> Verses 28–29 are especially hard for us: The young woman is to marry the man who used violence against her. Back then, a woman who was no longer a virgin had hardly any chance of marriage and so of being provided for. The law wanted to protect her: The man had to pay and provide for her all her life. But according to Exodus 22:16–17, the father could also refuse the marriage.
> Even so: By today’s understanding it is a grave injustice to marry a victim to the attacker. Today violence against women is punished as a serious crime. No one may use these verses to justify such a thing.

## Not the wife of your father (Verse 30)

[30] A man shall not take his father’s wife.
He shall not uncover the edge of his father’s robe.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles this verse is already the first verse of Chapter 23 (23:1). We follow the numbering of the WEB, our English source.
> It means another wife of the father, not one’s own mother, for example after the father’s death. That was forbidden (Leviticus 18:8).
> “Uncovering the edge of the father’s robe” is a picture. The robe stands for the protection that a man gives his wife (Ruth 3:9). Whoever sleeps with his father’s wife wounds his father’s honor.
