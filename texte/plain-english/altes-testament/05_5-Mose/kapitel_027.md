# Deuteronomy – Chapter 27
### The law on stones – twelve curses at Mount Ebal

---

## Large stones with the law (Verses 1–8)

[1] Moses and the elders of Israel gave the people this command:
“Keep the whole commandment that I command you today.
[2] On the day when you cross the Jordan
into the land that the LORD your God gives you,
you shall set up large stones for yourself
and cover them with plaster.
[3] When you have crossed over,
you shall write on them all the words of this law.
Then you will go into the land that the LORD your God gives you,
a land flowing with milk and honey,
as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has promised you.
[4] When you have crossed the Jordan,
you shall set up these stones on Mount Ebal,
as I command you today.
And you shall cover them with plaster.
[5] There you shall build an altar for the LORD your God,
an altar of stones.
You shall not use any iron tool on them.
[6] You shall build the altar of the LORD your God out of stones that are not cut.
And on it you shall offer burnt offerings to the LORD your God.
[7] You shall sacrifice peace offerings and eat there.
And you shall be glad before the LORD your God.
[8] And you shall write all the words of this law on the stones
very clearly.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Right after entering the land, the law is to be written down in public, on large stones, where everyone can see it. Plaster is a white paste that becomes hard. The white layer of plaster made the writing easy to read.
> “Very clearly”: God’s word should be understandable for everyone. That is also the goal of this Bible in plain language.
> Joshua did this later (Joshua 8:30–35).
> The altar is to be made of uncut stones, just as God created them (see Exodus 20:25).

---

## Today you have become God’s people (Verses 9–10)

[9] Moses and the priests, the Levites, spoke to all Israel:
“Be quiet and listen, Israel!
Today you have become the people of the LORD your God.
[10] So you shall listen to the voice of the LORD your God,
and do his commandments and his rules
that I command you today.”

---

## Blessing and curse on two mountains (Verses 11–14)

[11] On the same day Moses gave the people this command:
[12] “When you have crossed the Jordan,
these tribes shall stand on Mount Gerizim to bless the people:
Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin.
[13] And these tribes shall stand on Mount Ebal for the curse:
Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan, and Naphtali.
[14] The Levites shall speak with a loud voice
and say to all the men of Israel:

> **What does this mean?**
> The twelve tribes divide up onto two mountains that face each other. Between them lies a valley. You can imagine the voices echoing from one mountain to the other.
> The tribes on the mountain of blessing come from Jacob’s wives Leah and Rachel. On the mountain of the curse stand mainly the tribes that come from the servant women Zilpah and Bilhah, along with Reuben and Zebulun.

---

## The twelve curses (Verses 15–26)

[15] ‘Cursed is the man who makes a carved or a cast idol
and sets it up in secret.
The LORD hates it.
It is only something that a craftsman made with his hands.’
And all the people shall answer and say, ‘Amen.’
[16] ‘Cursed is anyone who dishonors his father or his mother.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[17] ‘Cursed is anyone who moves his neighbor’s boundary stone.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[18] ‘Cursed is anyone who leads a blind person the wrong way on the road.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[19] ‘Cursed is anyone who denies justice to the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[20] ‘Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his father’s wife,
because he dishonors his father’s bed.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[21] ‘Cursed is anyone who sleeps with any kind of animal.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[22] ‘Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his sister,
his father’s daughter or his mother’s daughter.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[23] ‘Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his mother-in-law.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[24] ‘Cursed is anyone who kills his neighbor in secret.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[25] ‘Cursed is anyone who takes a bribe to kill an innocent person.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’
[26] ‘Cursed is anyone who does not hold up the words of this law by doing them.’
And all the people shall say, ‘Amen.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> “He dishonors his father’s bed”: That is what the WEB, our English source text, says. The Hebrew has the same picture as in Chapter 22:30: “uncover the edge of his father’s robe.”
> “Amen” means “That is so” or “Let it be so.” All the people agree. With it, everyone says: I know what is right, and I want to keep to it.
> Something stands out: Almost all these sins happen in secret: setting up an idol “in secret,” killing “in secret,” leading a blind person the wrong way, who cannot see it. A human court would often never find out about them. But God sees it.
> Again it is about protecting the weak: the blind, foreigners, orphans, and widows.
> The last curse sums everything up. Paul quotes it and says: No one can keep the whole law perfectly. That is why all people need God’s grace. Christ carried the curse for us (Galatians 3:10–13).
