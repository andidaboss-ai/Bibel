# Deuteronomy – Chapter 13
### A warning against being led away to foreign gods

---

## False prophets (Verses 1–5)

[1] A prophet or someone who has dreams may appear among you.
He may announce a sign or a wonder to you.
[2] And the sign or the wonder that he told you about may really happen.
And he says, “Let’s follow other gods” – gods you have not known –
“and let’s serve them.”
[3] Then you shall not listen to the words of that prophet or of that dreamer.
For the LORD your God is testing you.
He wants to know whether you love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul.
[4] You shall follow the LORD your God, and you shall fear him.
You shall keep his commandments and listen to his voice.
You shall serve him and hold on to him.
[5] And that prophet or that dreamer shall be put to death.
For he has called for rebellion against the LORD your God,
who brought you out of the land of Egypt and set you free from the house of slavery.
He wanted to lead you away from the way that the LORD your God commanded you to walk in.
In this way you shall remove the evil from among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, this chapter is numbered one verse later: Verse 1 here is verse 2 there, and so on up to verse 19. We follow the verse numbers of the WEB.
> An important lesson: Even if someone does wonders, that does not yet prove that he comes from God. What matters is where he leads. Whoever leads away from God is a false prophet, even if he does impressive things. Jesus gives a very similar warning (Matthew 24:24).
> Back then, a false prophet was to be put to death. For us, that is very harsh. We need to understand: Israel back then was a people whose whole order was based on the covenant with God. Turning away from God was seen as betraying the whole people, a bit like high treason.
> Today Jews and Christians live in countries with freedom of religion. The large Jewish and Christian communities reject all violence because of faith. No one may use this passage to persecute people of another faith. Sadly, this has happened in history, and it was a grave injustice.

## Being led away in your own family (Verses 6–11)

[6] Maybe your brother, the son of your mother, or your son, or your daughter,
or the wife you hold in your arms, or your friend who is as dear to you as your own life,
tries to lead you away in secret and says,
“Let’s go and serve other gods” –
gods that you have not known, not you and not your ancestors,
[7] gods of the peoples who live around you, near you or far from you,
from one end of the earth to the other end of the earth.
[8] Then you shall not give in to him, and you shall not listen to him.
Your eye shall not pity him.
You shall not spare him, and you shall not hide him.
[9] But you shall surely kill him.
Your hand shall be the first against him to put him to death,
and after that the hands of all the people.
[10] You shall stone him with stones until he dies.
For he has tried to lead you away from the LORD your God,
who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
[11] All Israel shall hear it and be afraid.
And no one shall do such an evil thing among you anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the harshest texts in the Bible. Not even your own brother, your own child, your own wife, or your best friend is to be spared if he leads you to worship idols.
> The text shows how seriously faithfulness to God was taken back then. Love for God was to come before all other bonds.
> But it must be said clearly: By our understanding today, it is a grave injustice to kill a person because of their faith. The Jewish teachers of the Talmud already limited the death penalty with so many conditions that it could hardly be used anymore. For Christians, Jesus’ example counts: He corrected his disciples when they wanted to call down fire on a village that would not welcome him (Luke 9:54–55).
> No one may use this passage to justify violence against family members or other people.

## A city that turns away (Verses 12–18)

[12] Maybe you will hear about one of your cities that the LORD your God is giving you to live in.
[13] You hear that some wicked fellows have gone out from among you
and have led the people of their city away and said,
“Let’s go and serve other gods,” gods that you have not known.
[14] Then you shall look into it, examine it, and ask carefully.
Look! If it is true, and the matter is certain,
that such a disgusting thing has been done among you,
[15] then you shall surely strike the people of that city with the edge of the sword.
You shall completely destroy it, with everything in it, and also its livestock,
with the edge of the sword.
[16] You shall gather all its plunder in the middle of its town square.
And you shall burn the city with all its plunder with fire, as a whole offering to the LORD your God.
It shall be a heap of ruins forever.
It shall not be built again.
[17] Nothing that is devoted to destruction shall stick to your hand,
so that the LORD turns away from his burning anger,
shows you mercy, has compassion on you,
and makes you many, as he swore to your ancestors.
[18] This will happen when you listen to the voice of the LORD your God
and keep all his commandments, which I command you today,
and do what is right in the eyes of the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Wicked fellows”: The Hebrew says “sons of Belial.” That means something like “good-for-nothings” or “evil people.”
> An important point: Before anything happens, it must be examined carefully (verse 14). Rumors are not enough. This is a protection against false accusations.
> The punishment is terrible: The whole city is to be destroyed. No one may get rich from it. It is not about plunder, but about wiping out idol worship completely.
> The Jewish teachers later said: A city like that never existed and never will exist. The commandment is only there to show how serious the sin of idol worship is (Talmud, Sanhedrin 71a).
> Here too: This passage must never be used to justify violence against people.
