# Deuteronomy – Chapter 18
### Priests and Levites – no fortune-telling – a prophet like Moses

---

## What belongs to the priests and Levites (Verses 1–8)

[1] The priests and the Levites, the whole tribe of Levi,
shall have no share of land and no inheritance with Israel.
They shall eat from the offerings made by fire to the LORD,
and from the share that belongs to him.
[2] They shall have no inheritance among their brothers.
The LORD is their inheritance, as he has told them.
[3] This is what the priests have a right to receive from the people,
from those who offer a sacrifice, whether it is an ox or a sheep.
They shall give the priest the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the inner parts.
[4] You shall give him the first part of your grain, of your new wine, and of your oil,
and the first wool that you shear from your sheep.
[5] For the LORD your God has chosen him out of all your tribes
to stand and serve in the name of the LORD,
him and his sons forever.
[6] A Levite may come from one of your towns anywhere in Israel where he lives.
He may come with all the longing of his heart
to the place that the LORD will choose.
[7] Then he shall serve in the name of the LORD his God,
like all his brothers, the Levites, who stand there before the LORD.
[8] They shall get equal shares to eat,
besides what he gets from selling his family’s property.

> **What does this mean?**
> The priests and Levites have no land of their own. They live from the gifts of the people. In this way they are cared for, so that they can serve God.
> A Levite from the countryside who would like to serve at the holy place may do so. He should be treated just like the others. No one should see him as “second-class.”
> “The inner parts”: The Hebrew probably means the stomach.

---

## No fortune-telling and no magic (Verses 9–14)

[9] You will come into the land that the LORD your God gives you.
Then you shall not learn to copy the terrible practices of those nations.
[10] No one shall be found among you
who makes his son or his daughter pass through the fire.
No one shall be found among you who tries to see the future by signs,
who tells fortunes,
who reads omens, or who does magic,
[11] who casts spells,
who asks a spirit of the dead for advice,
who is a wizard,
or who tries to speak with the dead.
[12] For the LORD hates everyone who does these things.
Because of these terrible practices,
the LORD your God is driving those nations out ahead of you.
[13] You shall be without blame before the LORD your God.
[14] For these nations that you will drive out listen to people who do magic
and to people who tell the future.
But as for you, the LORD your God has not allowed you to do that.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, many nations tried to learn the future or to gain power: by reading signs, by magic, by asking the dead. Some even sacrificed their children (“make … pass through the fire”).
> God says: None of this is for you. You do not need magic. You have me. I speak to you through my word and through my prophets.
> “Without blame” means: Trust God alone, with your whole heart. Do not hold on to other powers on the side.
> An important note: These verses were terribly misused in history. In the witch hunts in Europe, tens of thousands of innocent people, most of them women, were tortured and killed. That was a grave injustice. Here the Bible forbids Israelites to practice magic. It gives no one the right to accuse other people of being “witches” and to persecute them.

## A prophet like Moses (Verses 15–19)

[15] The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me
from among you, from your brothers.
You shall listen to him.
[16] This is just what you asked of the LORD your God at Horeb,
on the day when the people were gathered.
You said,
“Do not let me hear the voice of the LORD my God again.
And do not let me see this great fire anymore,
so that I do not die.”
[17] The LORD said to me,
“What they have said is good.
[18] I will raise up for them a prophet like you
from among their brothers.
I will put my words in his mouth.
And he will tell them everything that I command him.
[19] Whoever does not listen to my words that he speaks in my name,
I will hold that person responsible.

> **What does this mean?**
> Instead of magic and fortune-telling, God promises something better: prophets. Through them God will speak to his people, just as he spoke through Moses.
> Jewish people often understand it like this: God will send prophets again and again, like Samuel, Elijah, Isaiah, and Jeremiah.
> Christians see this promise above all as pointing to Jesus. In the New Testament this verse is applied to him (Acts 3:22; John 6:14). But at the end of the book of Deuteronomy it also says: “Since then, no prophet like Moses has risen in Israel” (Deuteronomy 34:10). That is why people waited for this one prophet.

## How to recognize a false prophet (Verses 20–22)

[20] But a prophet may proudly speak a word in my name
that I have not commanded him to speak,
or he may speak in the name of other gods.
That prophet shall die.”
[21] You may say in your heart,
“How shall we know which word the LORD has not spoken?”
[22] A prophet may speak in the name of the LORD,
but the thing does not come about and does not happen.
Then it is a word that the LORD has not spoken.
The prophet has spoken it out of pride.
You shall not be afraid of him.

> **What does this mean?**
> An important sign: If a prophet announces something that does not happen, then he does not come from God.
> Together with Chapter 13 there are two tests: Does the prophet lead to God or away from him? And: Does what he says come true?
> “You shall not be afraid of him”: Today too there are people who make others afraid in the name of God, for example with predictions about the end of the world. If their words do not come true, you do not need to fear them.
> Back then, the death penalty applied to a false prophet. Today Judaism and Christianity teach: You should not believe false prophets and not follow them. Violence against them is not the way.
