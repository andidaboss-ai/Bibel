# Deuteronomy – Chapter 31
### Joshua is appointed – the law is written down

---

## Be strong and brave (Verses 1–6)

[1] Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel.
[2] He said to them,
“Today I am 120 years old.
I can no longer go out and come in.
And the LORD has said to me,
‘You will not cross this Jordan.’
[3] The LORD your God himself will cross over ahead of you.
He will destroy these nations in front of you,
and you will drive them out.
Joshua will cross over ahead of you,
as the LORD has said.
[4] The LORD will do to them what he did to Sihon and Og,
the kings of the Amorites,
and to their land, when he destroyed them.
[5] The LORD will hand them over to you.
And you shall do to them everything that I have commanded you.
[6] Be strong and brave!
Do not be afraid of them, and do not be terrified of them.
For the LORD your God is the one who goes with you.
He will not let you down, and he will not abandon you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is 120 years old. “I can no longer go out and come in” means: I can no longer lead the people. His life is coming to an end.
> But the people do not need to be afraid. Moses was not the real leader. God was. And God stays.
> “He will not let you down, and he will not abandon you”: In the letter to the Hebrews this promise is spoken to all who believe (Hebrews 13:5). It is one of the most comforting words in the Bible.

---

## Moses encourages Joshua (Verses 7–8)

[7] Moses called Joshua.
In front of all Israel he said to him,
“Be strong and brave!
For you will go with this people into the land
that the LORD swore to their ancestors to give them.
And you will give it to them as their inheritance.
[8] The LORD himself is the one who goes ahead of you.
He will be with you.
He will not let you down, and he will not abandon you.
Do not be afraid.
Do not lose heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In front of all the people, Moses gives his successor courage. So everyone knows: Joshua is the new leader, and Moses stands behind him.
> “Be strong and brave” is repeated many times in the book of Joshua (Joshua 1:6–9).

---

## The law is to be read aloud every seven years (Verses 9–13)

[9] Moses wrote down this law.
He gave it to the priests, the sons of Levi,
who carried the ark of the LORD’s covenant,
and to all the elders of Israel.
The ark is a holy chest.
[10] Moses commanded them,
“At the end of every seven years,
at the set time of the year of release,
at the Feast of Booths,
[11] all Israel will come to appear before the LORD your God
at the place that he will choose.
Then you shall read this law aloud in front of all Israel,
so that they hear it.
[12] Gather the people together,
the men, the women, and the little children,
and the foreigners who live in your towns.
Then they will hear and learn,
and they will fear the LORD your God
and be careful to do all the words of this law.
[13] And their children, who do not know it yet, will hear it.
They will learn to fear the LORD your God,
as long as you live in the land
that you are crossing the Jordan to take over.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses writes the law down. From now on, God’s word is not only in his head, but in a book. That way it can be passed on, even when Moses dies.
> Every seven years the whole law is to be read aloud in public. Back then only a few people could read. That is why reading aloud was so important.
> And again: Everyone should listen, men, women, children, and foreigners. God’s word is for everyone.
> From this came the Jewish tradition of reading a section of the Torah aloud in the synagogue every week. In the course of a year, the whole Torah is read in this way.

---

## God says ahead of time that Israel will be unfaithful (Verses 14–18)

[14] The LORD said to Moses,
“Look, the days are coming near when you must die.
Call Joshua, and come and stand in the Tent of Meeting,
so that I can appoint him to his task.”
So Moses and Joshua went and stood in the Tent of Meeting.
[15] The LORD appeared in the Tent in a pillar of cloud.
And the pillar of cloud stood over the entrance of the Tent.
[16] The LORD said to Moses,
“Look, you will soon die and join your ancestors.
And this people will get up and be unfaithful to me like a prostitute.
They will chase after the foreign gods of the land
that they are going into, to live among them.
They will leave me and break my covenant that I have made with them.
[17] On that day my anger will burn against them.
I will leave them, and I will hide my face from them.
They will be eaten up,
and many disasters and troubles will come on them.
Then on that day they will say,
‘Haven’t these disasters come on us
because our God is not among us?’
[18] On that day I will surely hide my face,
because of all the evil they have done,
because they have turned to other gods.

> **What does this mean?**
> “You will soon die and join your ancestors”: Word for word, the Hebrew says that Moses will “sleep” with his fathers. That is a gentle way of talking about death.
> “Like a prostitute”: The Bible often uses this harsh picture. God sees himself like a faithful husband. When his people run after other gods, it is like unfaithfulness in a marriage.
> “Hide my face”: God pulls back. People no longer feel that he is near. That is one of the worst experiences a person can have. Many psalms complain about it: Why do you hide your face from me? (Psalm 88:14; similar Psalm 13:1).
> It is sad: God already knows now that the people will be unfaithful. Even so, he leads them into the land. That shows his great patience and love.

## A song as a witness (Verses 19–23)

[19] “Now write down this song for yourselves
and teach it to the people of Israel.
Put it in their mouths,
so that this song will be my witness against the people of Israel.
[20] For I will bring them into the land that I swore to their ancestors,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
They will eat and be full and grow fat.
Then they will turn to other gods and serve them.
They will despise me and break my covenant.
[21] Then many disasters and troubles will come on them.
And this song will speak against them as a witness,
for their descendants will not forget it.
For I know what they are planning even today,
before I have brought them into the land that I promised them.”
[22] So on that day Moses wrote down this song
and taught it to the people of Israel.
[23] And he appointed Joshua the son of Nun to his task and said,
“Be strong and brave!
For you will bring the people of Israel into the land that I swore to them.
And I will be with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> You do not forget a song as quickly as a speech. You sing it, you learn it by heart, you pass it on to your children. That is why Moses is to write a song. It follows in Chapter 32.
> Later, when the people are in trouble, they will remember the song and understand: God warned us. But the song also ends with hope.
> In verse 23 God himself speaks to Joshua: “And I will be with you.”

---

## The book beside the ark of the covenant (Verses 24–30)

[24] Moses finished writing the words of this law in a book,
all the way to the end.
[25] Then Moses commanded the Levites,
who carried the ark of the LORD’s covenant,
[26] “Take this book of the law
and put it beside the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God,
so that it will be there as a witness against you.
[27] For I know how rebellious and stubborn you are.
Look, even today, while I am still alive with you,
you have rebelled against the LORD.
How much more will you do it after my death!
[28] Gather to me all the elders of your tribes and your officials.
Then I will speak these words so that they can hear them,
and I will call heaven and earth as witnesses against them.
[29] For I know that after my death you will become completely corrupt
and turn away from the way that I have commanded you.
And in the days to come, disaster will come on you,
because you will do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD
and make him angry by what your hands have made.”
[30] Then Moses spoke the words of this song from beginning to end,
so that the whole assembly of Israel heard them:

> **What does this mean?**
> In the ark of the covenant lay the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments. The whole book of the law is placed beside it. So it is kept in the holiest place.
> Moses speaks very honestly, almost sadly. He knows his people well. He knows how often they have rebelled against God.
> Later, in the time of King Josiah, a “book of the law” that had long been forgotten was found again in the temple. When it was read aloud, the king was shocked and renewed the covenant with God (2 Kings 22–23).
