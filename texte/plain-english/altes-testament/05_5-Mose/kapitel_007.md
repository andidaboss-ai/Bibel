# Deuteronomy – Chapter 7
### A holy people, chosen out of love

---

## No covenants with the peoples of the land (Verses 1–5)

[1] The LORD your God will bring you into the land that you are going into to take as your own.
He will drive out many nations before you:
the Hittite, the Girgashite, the Amorite, the Canaanite,
the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite –
seven nations that are greater and mightier than you.
[2] When the LORD your God gives them over to you and you strike them,
then you shall completely destroy them.
You shall make no covenant with them, and you shall not show them mercy.
[3] You shall not marry them.
You shall not give your daughter to his son,
and you shall not take his daughter for your son.
[4] For that would turn your sons away from following me, so that they serve other gods.
Then the LORD’s anger would burn against you, and he would quickly destroy you.
[5] But this is how you shall deal with them:
You shall tear down their altars, smash their stone pillars,
cut down their Asherah poles, and burn their carved images with fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> This passage is very harsh. Again it speaks of “completely destroying” (see Chapters 2 and 3). It means wiping out these peoples completely.
> The reason is given in verse 4: Israel should not be led into worshiping idols. It is about faith, not about hatred of other people. Marriages with foreigners were not forbidden as such: Moses himself had a foreign wife, and Ruth from Moab became the great-grandmother of King David.
> Something interesting: Right afterward, the text forbids marriages with these peoples. That assumes they will go on living in the land. Many interpreters see this as a hint that “completely destroying” was not meant literally as wiping them out completely, but above all as a radical separation from their idol worship. In the books of Joshua and Judges, these peoples also go on living in the land.
> Even so, this text is hard to bear. It is clear: These words belong to a certain time. They must never be used to justify violence or hatred against people of other peoples or religions.
> “Stone pillars” and “Asherah poles” were holy signs of the Canaanite religion. Asherah was a goddess.

## Why God chose Israel (Verses 6–11)

[6] For you are a holy people to the LORD your God.
The LORD your God has chosen you to be his very own people,
out of all the peoples who live on the face of the earth.
[7] The LORD did not set his love on you and choose you
because you were more in number than all the other peoples.
For you were the fewest of all peoples.
[8] But it was because the LORD loves you,
and because he wants to keep the oath that he swore to your fathers.
That is why the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand
and set you free from the house of slavery, from the hand of Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.
[9] So know this: The LORD your God himself is God, the faithful God.
He keeps his covenant and his loving kindness for a thousand generations
with those who love him and keep his commandments.
[10] But he pays back those who hate him to their face, to destroy them.
He does not wait with the one who hates him.
He pays him back to his face.
[11] So keep the commandments, the rules, and the laws that I command you today, and do them.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Holy” means: set apart for God, belonging to him.
> Why did God choose Israel of all peoples? Not because it was great, strong or especially good. It was the smallest people. God chose it simply because he loves it. Love needs no reason.
> Being chosen does not mean: We are better than others. It means: We have received a gift, and we have a task. Abraham was already told: Through you all peoples will be blessed (Genesis 12:3).
> “To pay back to his face” means: directly, personally, not only to his descendants.

## The blessing for faithfulness (Verses 12–16)

[12] If you listen to these laws and keep them and do them,
then the LORD your God will keep the covenant and the loving kindness with you
that he swore to your fathers.
[13] He will love you, bless you, and make you many.
He will bless the children of your body and the crops of your ground,
your grain, your new wine, and your oil,
the young of your cattle and the lambs of your flock,
in the land that he swore to your fathers to give you.
[14] You will be blessed more than all peoples.
Among you there will be no man and no woman who cannot have children,
and not among your livestock either.
[15] The LORD will take away all sickness from you.
He will not put on you any of the terrible diseases of Egypt that you know.
But he will put them on all those who hate you.
[16] You shall destroy all the peoples whom the LORD your God gives over to you.
Your eye shall not pity them.
And you shall not serve their gods, because that would be a trap for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises great blessing: children, good harvests, healthy animals, good health.
> This must not be misunderstood: The Bible does not say that everyone who is sick or has no children is being punished by God. The book of Job and Jesus himself speak against that idea (John 9:2–3). Here it is about the blessing for the whole people.
> Verse 16 is harsh again. The reason is at the end: The foreign gods would be a “trap.”

## Do not be afraid (Verses 17–26)

[17] Maybe you will say in your heart,
“These nations are more than I am. How can I drive them out?”
[18] Then you shall not be afraid of them.
Remember well what the LORD your God did to Pharaoh and to all Egypt:
[19] the great trials that your eyes saw,
the signs, the wonders, the mighty hand, and the outstretched arm,
with which the LORD your God brought you out.
The LORD your God will do the same to all the peoples you are afraid of.
[20] And the LORD your God will send the hornet among them,
until even those who are left and are hiding from you have died.
[21] You shall not be frightened of them.
For the LORD your God is among you, a great and awesome God.
[22] The LORD your God will drive out those nations before you little by little.
You may not destroy them all at once,
or else the wild animals would become too many for you.
[23] But the LORD your God will give them over to you.
He will throw them into great confusion, until they are destroyed.
[24] He will give their kings into your hand,
and you shall wipe out their name from under the sky.
No one will be able to stand against you, until you have destroyed them.
[25] You shall burn the carved images of their gods with fire.
You shall not covet the silver or the gold on them, and you shall not take it for yourself,
so that you are not caught in a trap by it.
For it is something the LORD your God hates.
[26] You shall not bring something hateful like that into your house,
or else you yourself will become a thing devoted to destruction, like it.
You shall hate it completely, and you shall turn away from it completely.
For it is a thing devoted to destruction.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hornet”: Maybe real insects are meant. Maybe it is a picture of fear and terror that makes the enemies run away.
> “Little by little”: God knows what is good. If the land were suddenly empty, the wild animals would spread out. God has a plan, even when it goes more slowly than we would like.
> The gold on the idols is tempting. But whoever takes it brings idol worship into his house. Something the LORD hates is something God rejects with all his heart.
> “Devoted to destruction” means here: set apart completely for God, so that it must be destroyed and no one may keep it.
