# Deuteronomy – Chapter 4
### Listen to God’s commandments – do not make an image of God

---

## Listen and do it (Verses 1–8)

[1] And now, Israel, listen to the rules and the laws that I am teaching you.
Do them, so that you may live, and go in, and take the land as your own,
which the LORD, the God of your ancestors, is giving you.
[2] You shall not add anything to the word that I command you,
and you shall not take anything away from it.
In this way you shall keep the commandments of the LORD your God, which I command you.
[3] Your eyes have seen what the LORD did because of Baal Peor.
For the LORD your God has destroyed from among you
all the men who followed Baal Peor.
[4] But you who stayed faithful to the LORD your God
are all alive today.
[5] Look! I have taught you rules and laws,
just as the LORD my God commanded me.
You shall do them in the land that you are going into to take as your own.
[6] So keep them and do them.
For this is your wisdom and your understanding in the eyes of the peoples.
They will hear about all these rules and say,
“Truly, this great nation is a wise people with understanding.”
[7] For what great nation has a god so near to them
as the LORD our God is near to us whenever we call on him?
[8] And what great nation has rules and laws as just
as all this law that I am putting before you today?

> **What does this mean?**
> Do not add anything and do not take anything away: You should not change God’s word. You should not cut it down to fit your own wishes, and you should not load it down with your own rules. That is why, in this version too, it is so important that no verse is missing.
> Baal Peor: That was the story in Numbers 25. Those who followed foreign gods back then died. Those who stayed faithful are alive.
> Verse 7 is a wonderful sentence: God is near. You can call on him at any time. You do not need a temple or magic. A simple prayer is enough.
> The commandments are not meant to box Israel in. They are wisdom. Other peoples should see: Here people live together well and justly.

---

## Do not forget what you saw at Horeb (Verses 9–14)

[9] Only be careful, and watch yourself well,
so that you do not forget the things that your eyes have seen,
and so that they do not leave your heart, all the days of your life.
Tell them to your children and to your grandchildren:
[10] Tell them about the day when you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb,
when the LORD said to me,
“Gather the people to me. I will let them hear my words,
so that they learn to fear me all the days that they live on the earth,
and so that they teach it to their children.”
[11] You came near and stood at the foot of the mountain.
The mountain burned with fire up into the middle of the sky,
with darkness, clouds, and thick darkness.
[12] The LORD spoke to you out of the middle of the fire.
You heard the sound of words, but you did not see any form.
You only heard a voice.
[13] He told you about his covenant, which he commanded you to keep: the Ten Commandments.
He wrote them on two stone tablets.
[14] At that time the LORD commanded me to teach you rules and laws,
so that you would do them in the land that you are crossing over to take as your own.

> **What does this mean?**
> Faith lives from remembering. Parents should tell their children and grandchildren what God has done. That way faith is passed on from generation to generation.
> “To fear God” does not mean to be afraid of him. It means: to have deep respect for him, to take him seriously, to honor him.
> Verse 12 is very important: The people heard God’s voice. But they did not see any form. God did not show himself as a picture. He showed himself through his word.

---

## Do not make an image (Verses 15–20)

[15] Be very careful.
For you did not see any kind of form on the day when the LORD spoke to you at Horeb out of the middle of the fire.
[16] So do not ruin yourselves by making a carved image in the shape of any figure,
an image of a man or a woman,
[17] an image of any animal that lives on the earth,
an image of any bird with wings that flies in the sky,
[18] an image of anything that creeps on the ground,
an image of any fish that lives in the water under the earth.
[19] And do not lift up your eyes to the sky
and look at the sun, the moon, and the stars, all the army of the sky,
and let yourself be led away to worship them and serve them.
The LORD your God has given them to all the peoples under the whole sky.
[20] But the LORD has taken you
and brought you out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt,
so that you would be his very own people, his inheritance, as you are today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Because God did not show any form, people should not show him in an image either. God is greater than anything people can imagine. An image would make him small. People would start to worship the image instead of God himself.
> Other peoples worshiped the sun, the moon and the stars. The Bible says: These are not gods. God made them. They are lamps in the sky, for all people (Genesis 1:14–18).
> An “iron furnace”: That is how bad the slavery in Egypt was. The Israelites suffered like metal in a glowing fire.

---

## Moses will not come along (Verses 21–24)

[21] The LORD was angry with me because of you.
He swore that I would not cross the Jordan,
and that I would not go into the good land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.
[22] I must die in this land. I may not cross the Jordan.
But you will cross over and take that good land as your own.
[23] Be careful that you do not forget the covenant of the LORD your God, which he made with you.
Do not make yourselves a carved image in the shape of anything,
as the LORD your God has forbidden you.
[24] For the LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God.

> **What does this mean?**
> “A jealous God”: That sounds strange. It means: God loves his people as much as a husband loves his wife. He does not want to share them with other gods. His love is full of passion.
> “A consuming fire”: God is holy and mighty. You cannot treat him lightly. The Letter to the Hebrews in the New Testament quotes this sentence (Hebrews 12:29).

---

## A warning and a hope (Verses 25–31)

[25] You will have children and grandchildren and will have lived in the land for a long time.
If you then ruin yourselves and make a carved image in any shape,
and do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD your God, so that you make him angry,
[26] then today I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you:
You will quickly and completely disappear from the land
that you are crossing the Jordan to take as your own.
You will not live long in it. You will be completely destroyed.
[27] The LORD will scatter you among the peoples.
And only a few of you will be left among the nations where the LORD will lead you away.
[28] There you will serve gods that are made by human hands, out of wood and stone.
They do not see, they do not hear, they do not eat, and they do not smell.
[29] But from there you will look for the LORD your God.
And you will find him when you look for him with all your heart and with all your soul.
[30] When you are in trouble and all these things have come over you,
then in the later days you will return to the LORD your God and listen to his voice.
[31] For the LORD your God is a merciful God.
He will not let you down, and he will not destroy you.
And he will not forget the covenant with your ancestors, which he swore to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses sees ahead: Israel will later be unfaithful. Then it will lose the land and be scattered among the peoples. That really happened later, when the Assyrians and the Babylonians came (about 722 and 586 BC).
> But that is not the end! Verse 29 is a promise for all times: Whoever looks for God with all their heart will find him. Even in a foreign land, even after great guilt.
> God is merciful. He never forgets his covenant. There is always a way back.

## There is no other God (Verses 32–40)

[32] Just ask about the times past, which were before you,
since the day when God created man on the earth,
and from one end of the sky to the other:
Has anything as great as this ever happened?
Has anyone ever heard of anything like it?
[33] Did a people ever hear the voice of God speaking out of the middle of the fire, as you have heard it,
and stay alive?
[34] Or has God ever tried to go and take a nation for himself out of another nation,
by trials, by signs, by wonders, by war,
by a mighty hand, by an outstretched arm, and by great terrors,
just like everything that the LORD your God did for you in Egypt before your eyes?
[35] It was shown to you so that you would know that the LORD is God.
There is no one else besides him.
[36] From heaven he let you hear his voice, to teach you.
And on earth he let you see his great fire.
And you heard his words out of the middle of the fire.
[37] Because he loved your ancestors, he chose their descendants after them.
And he brought you out of Egypt with his presence, with his great power,
[38] to drive out nations before you that are greater and mightier than you,
to bring you in and to give you their land as an inheritance, as it is today.
[39] So know it today, and take it to heart:
The LORD himself is God in heaven above and on the earth below.
There is no one else.
[40] You shall keep his rules and his commandments, which I command you today,
so that it may go well with you and with your children after you,
and so that you may live long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you for all time.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the whole Old Testament. Moses says very clearly: There is only one God. There is no other. Back then, almost all peoples believed in many gods.
> Why did God choose Israel? Not because Israel was especially great or good. But out of love (verse 37).
> “With his presence”: The Hebrew says “with his face.” God himself went along.
> And once more: The commandments are there for good, “so that it may go well with you.”

## Three cities of refuge in the east (Verses 41–43)

[41] Then Moses set apart three cities beyond the Jordan, in the east, where the sun rises.
[42] Someone who had killed his neighbor without meaning to,
and who had not hated him before, should be able to flee there.
If he fled to one of these cities, he could stay alive.
[43] These were the cities: Bezer in the wilderness, on the plain, for the Reubenites,
Ramoth in Gilead for the Gadites,
and Golan in Bashan for the Manassites.

> **What does this mean?**
> The cities of refuge were explained in Numbers 35. Here Moses chooses the three cities east of the Jordan. Later, Joshua chooses the three cities west of the Jordan (Joshua 20).

---

## The introduction to the law (Verses 44–49)

[44] This is the law that Moses put before the people of Israel.
[45] These are the testimonies, the rules, and the laws
that Moses told the people of Israel after they came out of Egypt,
[46] beyond the Jordan, in the valley opposite Beth Peor,
in the land of Sihon, the king of the Amorites, who lived at Heshbon.
Moses and the people of Israel had struck him down after they came out of Egypt.
[47] They took his land as their own, and the land of Og, the king of Bashan.
These were the two kings of the Amorites who lived beyond the Jordan, in the east, where the sun rises.
[48] The land reached from Aroer, which is on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, to Mount Siyon (also called Hermon),
[49] and all the Arabah beyond the Jordan to the east,
as far as the sea of the Arabah, below the slopes of Pisgah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a new, large part of the book begins. Moses has reminded the people of their history. Now come the commandments themselves. First come the Ten Commandments (Chapter 5).
> “Mount Siyon” is another name for Hermon. It must not be confused with Mount Zion in Jerusalem.
