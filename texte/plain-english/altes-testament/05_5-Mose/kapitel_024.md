# Deuteronomy – Chapter 24
### Divorce – protection for the poor, foreigners, orphans, and widows

---

## Divorce and marrying again (Verses 1–4)

[1] A man may take a wife and marry her.
Then she may no longer please him,
because he has found something indecent about her.
Then he shall write her a certificate of divorce.
He shall put it in her hand and send her away from his house.
[2] When she has left his house,
she may go and become another man’s wife.
[3] The second husband may also hate her.
He writes her a certificate of divorce,
puts it in her hand, and sends her away from his house.
Or the second husband who took her as his wife may die.
[4] Then her first husband, who sent her away,
may not take her back as his wife,
after she has been made unclean.
For the LORD would hate that.
You shall not bring sin on the land
that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> This law assumes that there were divorces back then. Above all, it is about protecting the woman:
> The man must give her a “certificate of divorce.” With this document she could prove that she was free and allowed to marry again. Without this letter, she could later have been accused of adultery.
> The first husband may not take her back if she has been married to another man in the meantime. This was meant to keep women from being passed back and forth like property.
> What “something indecent” means was already disputed in the time of Jesus. Some teachers said: any small reason. Others said: only adultery.
> Jesus said about this: Moses allowed divorce only because your hearts were so hard. But God’s original will is that husband and wife stay together (Matthew 19:3–9; Mark 10:2–12).
> Today many churches have different ways of dealing with divorce. What matters is this: People whose marriage has broken down deserve mercy, not contempt.

## A year for the young marriage (Verse 5)

[5] When a man has taken a new wife,
he shall not go out with the army,
and he shall not be given any other duties.
He shall be free at home for one year
and make his wife happy, the wife whom he has taken.

> **What does this mean?**
> A beautiful law: A newly married man gets one year off from military service and from other duties. The young marriage should have time to grow. And the man should “make his wife happy.” The happiness of the wife clearly matters.

---

## No millstone as a pledge (Verse 6)

[6] No one shall take a hand mill or its upper millstone as a pledge.
For that would be taking a person’s life as a pledge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever borrowed money gave the lender something as security, a “pledge.” But the hand mill could not be taken. With it the family ground the flour for its bread every day. Without the mill, the family would have gone hungry.
> What a person needs to live must not be taken away from him.

---

## Kidnapping (Verse 7)

[7] A man may be found kidnapping one of his brothers, one of the people of Israel.
He treats him as a slave or sells him.
Then that thief shall die.
So you shall remove the evil from among you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Kidnapping is one of the worst crimes. A human being is not a thing that can be stolen and sold. The story of Joseph, whom his brothers sold, shows how terrible that is (Genesis 37).
> In Exodus 21:16 this ban applies to every human being.

---

## Pay attention to the skin disease (Verses 8–9)

[8] Be careful when the disease of leprosy breaks out.
Pay close attention and do everything that the priests, the Levites, teach you.
Be careful to do it as I have commanded them.
[9] Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam
on the way when you came out of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Leprosy” was a name for various skin diseases (Leviticus 13–14). The priests were to decide who was sick and who was healthy again. This protected the community from infection.
> Miriam, the sister of Moses, got such a disease after she had spoken against Moses (Numbers 12).

---

## The pledge of a poor man (Verses 10–13)

[10] When you lend your neighbor anything,
you shall not go into his house to take a pledge from him.
[11] You shall stay standing outside.
And the man to whom you are lending shall bring the pledge out to you.
[12] If he is a poor man,
you shall not go to sleep with his pledge.
[13] You must give the pledge back to him when the sun goes down.
Then he can sleep in his coat and bless you.
And it will be counted as right for you before the LORD your God.

> **What does this mean?**
> A poor person has dignity too. The lender may not simply force his way into his house and take something. The poor person himself decides what he gives as a pledge.
> A very poor person often had only his coat as a pledge. At night he needed it as a blanket. That is why it must be given back in the evening. No one should be cold just because he is poor.

---

## Wages on the same day (Verses 14–15)

[14] You shall not take advantage of a hired worker who is poor and needy,
whether he is one of your brothers
or one of the foreigners who live in your land, in your towns.
[15] You shall give him his pay on the same day,
before the sun goes down.
For he is poor, and he is longing for it.
Otherwise he will cry out to the LORD against you,
and it will be counted as sin for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> A day laborer worked for someone else every day and lived from hand to mouth. If he did not get his pay in the evening, his family had nothing to eat.
> So: Pay wages right away! And this applies to people from the land and to foreigners alike.
> God hears the cry of those who are exploited. The letter of James in the New Testament takes up this thought (James 5:4). To this day, fair wages are a matter of faith.

---

## Each person carries his own guilt (Verse 16)

[16] Fathers shall not be put to death for their children,
and children shall not be put to death for their fathers.
Each person shall be put to death for his own sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> A very important principle of law: No one may be punished for someone else’s guilt. Among other nations, sometimes the whole family of a criminal was punished. Not in Israel.
> The prophet Ezekiel stressed this again later (Ezekiel 18:20). And this principle still applies today in every just legal system.

---

## Justice for foreigners, orphans, and widows (Verses 17–18)

[17] You shall not twist justice for the foreigner or for the orphan.
And you shall not take a widow’s clothing as a pledge.
[18] Remember that you were a slave in Egypt,
and that the LORD your God set you free from there.
That is why I command you to do this.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, foreigners, orphans, and widows had no husband, no father, and no family to stand up for them. They were easy to cheat. God clearly takes their side.
> And again the reason is: Remember that you yourself were a slave.

---

## Leave something for the poor (Verses 19–22)

[19] When you bring in the harvest from your field
and you have forgotten a bundle of grain in the field,
you shall not go back to get it.
It shall be for the foreigner, for the orphan, and for the widow.
Then the LORD your God will bless you in all the work of your hands.
[20] When you beat your olive trees to knock down the olives,
you shall not go over the branches a second time.
What is left shall be for the foreigner, for the orphan, and for the widow.
[21] When you pick the grapes in your vineyard,
you shall not go back afterward to gather the last grapes.
What is left shall be for the foreigner, for the orphan, and for the widow.
[22] Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt.
That is why I command you to do this.

> **What does this mean?**
> At harvest time you should not gather everything down to the last bit. What is left over belongs to the poor. They may come and pick it up. So they have food to eat, and with dignity, through their own work, without having to beg.
> This is exactly how Ruth, the poor widow from Moab, gathered grain in the field of Boaz (Ruth 2). That is how one of the most beautiful stories of the Bible began.
