# Deuteronomy – Chapter 33
### Moses blesses the tribes of Israel

---

## God came from Sinai (Verses 1–5)

[1] This is the blessing with which Moses, the man of God,
blessed the people of Israel before his death.
[2] He said,
“The LORD came from Sinai,
and he rose like the sun over them from Seir.
He shone out from Mount Paran.
He came from among ten thousands of holy ones.
At his right hand was a fiery law for them.
[3] Yes, he loves the people.
All his holy ones are in your hand.
They sat down at your feet.
Each one receives your words.
[4] Moses gave us a law,
an inheritance for the assembly of Jacob.
[5] He was king in Jeshurun,
when the leaders of the people gathered,
all the tribes of Israel together.

> **What does this mean?**
> Before Moses dies, he blesses the tribes, just as Jacob blessed his sons before his death (Genesis 49).
> “Man of God”: That is a title of honor for prophets. Here Moses is called this for the first time.
> God is described like the sun that rises over the mountains in the south: Sinai, Seir, Paran. He comes with his angels, the “holy ones.”
> “He loves the people”: That is what the WEB, our English source text, says. The Hebrew has “peoples,” in the plural. Perhaps the tribes of Israel are meant.
> Verses 2–5 are very hard to understand in Hebrew. That is why Bibles translate these verses in very different ways.
> “He was king in Jeshurun”: This probably means God himself. He is the real king of Israel.

---

## Reuben, Judah, and Levi (Verses 6–11)

[6] “Let Reuben live and not die.
And do not let his men be few.”
[7] And this is for Judah. He said,
“LORD, hear the voice of Judah.
Bring him to his people.
With his own hands he fought for himself.
Be his help against his enemies.”
[8] About Levi he said,
“Your Thummim and your Urim belong to your faithful man,
whom you tested at Massah,
with whom you quarreled at the waters of Meribah.
[9] He said about his father and his mother,
‘I have not seen him.’
He did not treat his brothers as his own,
and he did not know his own children.
For they have kept your word,
and they keep your covenant.
[10] They will teach Jacob your laws,
and Israel your teaching.
They will put incense before you,
and whole burnt offerings on your altar.
[11] LORD, bless his skills.
Accept the work of his hands.
Crush the hips of those who rise up against him,
of those who hate him,
so that they do not rise again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Reuben was Jacob’s firstborn, but he had lost his first place through a serious sin (Genesis 49:3–4). Moses asks: Let him live anyway, and do not let his tribe die out.
> Judah: From this tribe later comes King David. And according to the New Testament, Jesus too.
> Simeon is not named. This tribe later became part of the tribe of Judah.
> Verse 10: Incense is something that gives off a sweet smell when it burns.
> Levi: The “Urim and Thummim” were holy lots of the high priest, used to ask what God wanted (Exodus 28:30).
> Verse 9 reminds us of the story of the golden calf: The Levites took God’s side, even against their own relatives (Exodus 32:26–29). Being faithful to God mattered more to them than family.
> The task of the Levites: to teach the people and to lead worship.

---

## Benjamin and Joseph (Verses 12–17)

[12] About Benjamin he said,
“The one whom the LORD loves will live safely beside him.
He protects him all day long.
He lives between his shoulders.”
[13] About Joseph he said,
“His land is blessed by the LORD
with the best gifts of heaven, with the dew,
and with the deep water that lies below,
[14] with the best things that the sun brings out,
with the best things that the moon makes grow,
[15] with the best things of the ancient mountains,
with the best things of the hills that last forever,
[16] with the best things of the earth and everything in it,
and with the favor of the one who lived in the bush.
Let all this come on the head of Joseph,
on the top of the head of the one who was set apart from his brothers.
[17] He is like a firstborn bull, full of majesty.
His horns are the horns of the wild ox.
With them he will push the peoples,
all of them, to the ends of the earth.
They are the ten thousands of Ephraim.
They are the thousands of Manasseh.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Benjamin was Jacob’s youngest and most beloved son. “He lives between his shoulders”: Maybe that is a picture of a child whom the father carries on his shoulders. Or it means: The temple in Jerusalem later lay at the border of the land of Benjamin, “between his hills.”
> Joseph gets the richest blessing: everything good from heaven, from the earth, from the sun and the moon.
> “The one who lived in the bush”: That reminds us of the story in which God appeared to Moses in the burning bush (Exodus 3). It is the only place where Moses later mentions it again.
> “The one who was set apart from his brothers”: Joseph was sold to Egypt by his brothers (Genesis 37).
> Ephraim and Manasseh are the two sons of Joseph. They became two large tribes.

## Zebulun, Issachar, Gad, Dan, Naphtali, and Asher (Verses 18–25)

[18] About Zebulun he said,
“Be glad, Zebulun, when you go out,
and you, Issachar, in your tents.
[19] They will call the peoples to the mountain.
There they will offer the right sacrifices.
For they will draw out the riches of the seas
and the hidden treasures in the sand.”
[20] About Gad he said,
“Blessed is the one who gives Gad more room.
He lives like a lioness
and tears off the arm and the top of the head.
[21] He chose the first part for himself,
because the leader’s share was kept for him there.
He came with the heads of the people.
He carried out what the LORD said was right,
and his laws together with Israel.”
[22] About Dan he said,
“Dan is a young lion that leaps out of Bashan.”
[23] About Naphtali he said,
“Naphtali, filled with favor
and full of the LORD’s blessing,
take over the west and the south.”
[24] About Asher he said,
“Asher is blessed with children.
Let his brothers welcome him.
Let him dip his foot in oil.
[25] Your bars on the gates will be iron and bronze.
As your days are, so your strength will be.

> **What does this mean?**
> Zebulun and Issachar later lived in the north, near the sea. “The riches of the seas” and “the hidden treasures in the sand” may mean fishing, trade, and the glass that was made from sand.
> Gad received the land east of the Jordan as the “first part” (Numbers 32). Even so, he fought together with the other tribes: “He came with the heads of the people” (Deuteronomy 33:21).
> “Let him dip his foot in oil”: Asher later received a region with many olive trees. So much oil that you could bathe in it.
> “As your days are, so your strength will be”: A beautiful blessing that many people take with them as a comfort. It means: God gives you as much strength for each day as you need.

---

## No one is like God (Verses 26–29)

[26] “No one is like God, Jeshurun.
He rides across the heavens to help you,
and in his majesty across the skies.
[27] The eternal God is your home.
Underneath are the everlasting arms.
He drove out the enemy ahead of you
and said, ‘Destroy!’
[28] Israel lives in safety,
the spring of Jacob by itself,
in a land of grain and new wine.
Yes, his heavens drip with dew.
[29] How happy you are, Israel!
Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD?
He is the shield that helps you
and the sword that gives you glory.
Your enemies will bow down to you,
and you will walk on their high places.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, it is not a tribe but God himself who stands at the center.
> Verse 27 is one of the most beautiful words in the whole Bible: “The eternal God is your home. Underneath are the everlasting arms.” God is like a house in which you are safe. And when you fall, he catches you with his arms. This verse is often read at funerals and in hard times.
> “How happy you are, Israel!”: The last words of Moses to his people are not a warning, but a blessing of happiness.
