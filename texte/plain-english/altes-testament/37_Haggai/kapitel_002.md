# Haggai – Chapter 2
### The glory of this house will be greater

---

## Be strong and work! (Verses 1–9)

[1] In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came through the prophet Haggai.
It said,
[2] “Speak to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, the governor of Judah,
and to Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest,
and to the rest of the people. Say to them,
[3] ‘Who is still left among you
who saw this house in its former glory?
And how do you see it now?
Doesn’t it look like nothing to you?
[4] But now be strong, Zerubbabel,’ says the LORD.
‘Be strong, Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest.
Be strong, all you people of the land,’ says the LORD,
‘and work!
For I am with you,’ says the LORD of Armies.
[5] ‘This is the promise I made with you in my covenant
when you came out of Egypt.
And my Spirit lived among you.
Don’t be afraid!’
[6] For this is what the LORD of Armies says:
‘Once more, in a little while,
I will shake the sky and the earth,
the sea and the dry land.
[7] And I will shake all nations.
The treasures of all nations will come,
and I will fill this house with glory,’
says the LORD of Armies.
[8] ‘The silver is mine, and the gold is mine,’
says the LORD of Armies.
[9] ‘The later glory of this house
will be greater than the former glory,’
says the LORD of Armies.
‘And in this place I will give peace,’
says the LORD of Armies.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This was about the middle of October 520 BC, during the Feast of Booths.
> Verse 3: Some old people had still seen Solomon’s first temple, before it was destroyed 66 years earlier. Compared with its splendor, the new building looked poor. Many were disappointed (compare Ezra 3:12).
> Verse 5: A covenant is a binding agreement.
> Verses 4–5: God encourages them three times: “Be strong!” And: “I am with you. My Spirit is among you. Don’t be afraid!” What matters is not how splendid the building is, but that God is there.
> Verses 6–7: God will shake the whole world, and then the treasures of the nations will come to the temple. The letter to the Hebrews quotes verse 6 (Hebrews 12:26–28).
> “The treasures of all nations”: This is how the WEB translates it. Some other, older translations understand the verse as “the one whom all nations long for,” and they apply it to the Messiah. The Hebrew text can mean both.
> Verse 8: All silver and gold belong to God anyway. He can provide splendor if he wants to.
> Verse 9: The glory of the new temple will be greater than that of the old one. And: “In this place I will give peace.” Christians see here a hint that Jesus came to this temple. (Later, Herod had made it much larger and more splendid.)

## What makes things holy and what makes them unclean (Verses 10–14)

[10] On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month,
in the second year of Darius,
the word of the LORD came through the prophet Haggai.
It said,
[11] “This is what the LORD of Armies says:
Ask the priests about the law. Say,
[12] ‘Suppose someone carries holy meat in the fold of his clothes.
And with that fold he touches bread, stew, wine, oil, or any other food.
Does that food become holy?’”
The priests answered, “No.”
[13] Then Haggai said,
“Suppose someone is unclean because he touched a dead body.
If he touches any of these things,
does it become unclean?”
The priests answered, “It becomes unclean.”
[14] Then Haggai answered,
“‘That is how this people is,
and that is how this nation is before me,’ says the LORD.
‘And that is how all the work of their hands is.
What they offer there is unclean.

> **What does this mean?**
> Haggai asks the priests two questions from the laws about being clean:
> Question 1: Does something become holy when it is touched by something holy? Answer: No.
> Question 2: Does something become unclean when it is touched by something unclean? Answer: Yes.
> That means: Holiness does not spread to other things, but uncleanness does.
> Verse 14: Haggai applies this to the people: As long as the temple lay in ruins and the people neglected God, everything they did was “unclean,” even their sacrifices on the altar. A little bit of religion does not make a life holy if the heart is not in it.

## From today on I will bless you (Verses 15–19)

[15] Now please think about it, from this day and back,
before one stone was laid on another in the LORD’s temple.
[16] All through that time,
when someone came to a pile of grain of 20 measures,
there were only 10.
When someone came to the wine vat to draw out 50 measures,
there were only 20.
[17] I struck you with blight, mildew, and hail
in all the work of your hands.
And yet you did not turn to me,’
says the LORD.
[18] ‘Please think about it, from this day and back,
from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month,
since the day when the foundation of the LORD’s temple was laid.
Think about it:
[19] Is the seed still in the barn?
Yes, the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, and the olive tree
have not produced anything yet.
From today on I will bless you.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Blight and mildew are plant diseases.
> Verses 15–17: Remember how it was before: The harvest was always only half as big as expected. Diseases struck the grain. But you did not understand what God wanted to tell you.
> Verses 18–19: But now, since the day the building started again, everything will change. Nothing has been harvested yet, and the seed has not come up yet. But God promises: “From today on I will bless you.”
> This is a promise that you have to accept in faith, before you see it.
> The date (the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month) is about the middle of December 520 BC.

## Zerubbabel, the signet ring (Verses 20–23)

[20] The word of the LORD came to Haggai a second time
on the twenty-fourth day of the month.
It said,
[21] “Speak to Zerubbabel, the governor of Judah. Say,
‘I will shake the sky and the earth.
[22] I will overthrow the thrones of kingdoms.
I will destroy the power of the kingdoms of the nations.
I will overthrow the chariots and those who ride in them.
The horses and their riders will fall,
each one by the sword of his brother.
[23] On that day,’ says the LORD of Armies,
‘I will take you, Zerubbabel my servant, the son of Shealtiel,’
says the LORD,
‘and I will make you like a signet ring,
for I have chosen you,’
says the LORD of Armies.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The last message is for Zerubbabel personally.
> Verses 21–22: God will shake the great empires of the world. The mighty armies will destroy each other.
> Verse 23: Zerubbabel will be God’s “signet ring.” A signet ring was very valuable. A king used it to seal and sign his letters and laws. Whoever had the signet ring acted for the king.
> This is important: God had once rejected Zerubbabel’s ancestor, King Jehoiachin (called “Coniah” in Jeremiah). At that time God said: Even if Coniah were the signet ring on my right hand, I would still pull you off from there (Jeremiah 22:24). Now God accepts the house of David again.
> Zerubbabel himself never became king. But the promise to the house of David stayed. Zerubbabel is named in the family tree of Jesus (Matthew 1:12–13; Luke 3:27).

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Haggai?

> **What does this mean?**
> **After the exile:** The Jews had come back from Babylon, but the temple lay in ruins for almost 20 years.
> **The wrong things first:** “Is it time for you yourselves to live in your houses with fine wooden walls, while this house lies in ruins?” (1:4).
> **Think about your ways:** Whoever thinks only of himself has “a bag with holes in it” (1:6).
> **The people obey:** A rare success for a prophet. And God says: “I am with you” (1:13).
> **Be strong and don’t be afraid:** Even if the new temple is small and plain, God’s Spirit is there (2:4–5).
> **Greater glory:** “The later glory of this house will be greater than the former glory” (2:9).
> **From today on I will bless you:** God promises blessing even before people see it (2:19).
> **For today:** Haggai asks us: What place does God have in my life? Do I have time for everything, except for him?
> **What comes next?** Next comes Zechariah. He lived at the same time as Haggai and had many mysterious visions. He announces a king. This king is “humble, and riding on a donkey” (Zechariah 9:9).
