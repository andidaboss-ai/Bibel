# Haggai – Chapter 1
### Build the house of the LORD!

---

## Before we begin: Who was Haggai?

With Haggai, the last three prophets of the Old Testament begin: Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi. They lived after the exile.
What happened before: In the year 587/586 BC, the Babylonians had destroyed Jerusalem and the temple. Many people were taken away to Babylon. In the year 539 BC, the Persian king Cyrus conquered Babylon. He allowed the Jews to go back and to rebuild the temple (Ezra 1). A first group went back and laid the foundation. But then there was opposition from the neighbors, poverty, and disappointment. The work was stopped (Ezra 4).
For almost 20 years the temple lay in ruins. In the year 520 BC, in the second year of the Persian king Darius, Haggai came forward. He called on the people to finally go on building. And they listened to him! In the year 515 BC, the second temple was dedicated (Ezra 6:15).
Haggai and Zechariah are named together in the book of Ezra (Ezra 5:1; 6:14).
The book of Haggai gives very exact dates. It contains four messages within only four months. The book has 2 chapters.

---

## The people say: It is not time yet (Verses 1–4)

[1] In the second year of King Darius,
in the sixth month, on the first day of the month,
the word of the LORD came through the prophet Haggai.
It came to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, the governor of Judah,
and to Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest.
It said,
[2] “This is what the LORD of Armies says:
These people say,
‘The time has not come yet,
the time to build the house of the LORD.’”
[3] Then the word of the LORD came through the prophet Haggai.
It said,
[4] “Is it time for you yourselves
to live in your houses with fine wooden walls,
while this house lies in ruins?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The date is about August 29, 520 BC. Haggai speaks to the two leaders: Zerubbabel, the governor, and Joshua, the high priest. Zerubbabel came from King Jehoiachin, probably as his grandson. So he was from the house of David.
> Verse 2: The excuse of the people: “The time has not come yet, the time to build the house of the LORD.” There were many problems, after all: poverty, bad harvests, opposition from the neighbors.
> Verse 4: Haggai answers sharply: You have time for your own houses! You have even covered their walls with fine wood, that is, made them beautiful. But God’s house lies in ruins.
> For today: Sometimes we also say “Now is not the right time” when it is about God. But for our own things we always find time.

---

## Think about how things are going for you! (Verses 5–11)

[5] So now this is what the LORD of Armies says:
‘Think about your ways!
[6] You have planted much and brought in little.
You eat, but you do not have enough.
You drink, but you are not filled.
You put on clothes, but no one is warm.
And whoever earns wages
earns them to put into a bag with holes in it.’
[7] This is what the LORD of Armies says:
‘Think about your ways!
[8] Go up to the mountain,
bring wood,
and build the house.
Then I will take pleasure in it,
and I will be honored,’
says the LORD.
[9] ‘You looked for much,
but look, it turned out to be little.
And when you brought it home,
I blew it away.
Why?’ says the LORD of Armies.
‘Because of my house, which lies in ruins,
while each of you is busy with his own house.
[10] That is why, because of you, the sky holds back the dew,
and the earth holds back its crops.
[11] I called for a drought on the land
and on the mountains,
on the grain, on the new wine, on the oil,
and on everything the ground produces,
on people and on animals,
and on all the work of your hands.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5 and 7: “Think about your ways!” That means: Think for once about how things are going for you, and why.
> Verse 6: A very lively picture: The people work hard, but it is never enough. They eat and are not full. They earn money, but it is as if they had a purse with holes in it. The money just runs through.
> Many people know this today too: You work a lot, but at the end of the month nothing is left.
> Verse 8: God’s command is very practical: Get wood and build!
> Verses 9–11: Haggai sees the drought and the bad harvests as a sign: You have put the wrong things first. Each of you only cares about his own house.
> Important: Not every hardship in life is a punishment from God. The Bible also warns against thinking this way (the book of Job; John 9:1–3). Here the prophet says it in a certain situation to certain people.

## The people obey (Verses 12–15)

[12] Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel,
and Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest,
and all the rest of the people
obeyed the voice of the LORD their God
and the words of the prophet Haggai,
because the LORD their God had sent him.
And the people feared the LORD.
[13] Then Haggai, the LORD’s messenger,
gave the LORD’s message to the people.
He said, “I am with you,”
says the LORD.
[14] The LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel
the son of Shealtiel, the governor of Judah,
and the spirit of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest,
and the spirit of all the rest of the people.
They came and worked on the house of the LORD of Armies, their God.
[15] That was on the twenty-fourth day of the month,
in the sixth month,
in the second year of King Darius.

> **What does this mean?**
> Something rare in the Bible: A prophet speaks, and the people listen at once! All of them obey, the leaders and the whole people.
> Verse 13: God’s answer is short and strong: “I am with you.” That is the most important promise people can receive.
> Verse 14: The LORD “stirred up the spirit” of the people. He gives them new courage and new excitement. Then they begin to work.
> Verse 15: Only 23 days after Haggai’s first sermon, they begin to build.
