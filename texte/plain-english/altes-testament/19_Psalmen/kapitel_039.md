# Psalm 39
### How short a person’s life is

---

## I wanted to stay silent (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the Chief Musician. For Jeduthun. A psalm by David.
I said, “I will watch my ways,
so that I do not sin with my tongue.
I will keep my mouth closed, as with a bridle,
while the wicked are in front of me.”
[2] I was silent and said nothing.
I kept quiet, even about good things.
And my pain was stirred up.
[3] My heart grew hot inside me.
While I was thinking, the fire burned.
Then I spoke with my tongue:

> **What does this mean?**
> Jeduthun was one of the leaders of the temple music in David’s time (1 Chronicles 16:41).
> The one who prays wanted to stay silent so that he would not say anything wrong about God in front of wicked people.
> But the silence made it worse. The pain burned in him like fire. Finally he had to speak, but he does not speak to people. He speaks to God.

---

## Let me know how short my life is (Verses 4–6)

[4] “LORD, show me my end,
and how many my days will be.
Let me know how weak and passing I am.
[5] Look, you have made my days only a few hand widths long.
My lifetime is like nothing before you.
Surely every person is only a breath.”
Selah.
[6] “Surely every person walks around like a shadow.
Surely they keep busy for nothing.
A person piles up riches
and does not know who will gather them in.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one who prays thinks about how short life is. Compared to God, a human life is only “hand widths,” as short as the width of a hand.
> “A breath”: The Hebrew word is “hevel.” The same word appears often in the book of Ecclesiastes. There it says that everything is a breath, that everything passes away.
> Verse 6: People gather riches, but they do not know who will get them after they die (compare Luke 12:20).
> Thinking about death like this is not hopeless. It helps us tell what is important from what is not important (compare Psalm 90:12).

---

## My hope is in you (Verses 7–11)

[7] And now, Lord, what am I waiting for?
My hope is in you.
[8] Rescue me from all my wrongs.
Do not let foolish people make fun of me.
[9] I was silent. I did not open my mouth,
because you are the one who did it.
[10] Take your blows away from me.
I am worn out by the blows of your hand.
[11] When you correct a person
and punish him for his guilt,
you eat away his riches like a moth.
Surely every person is only a breath.”
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7 is the turning point: If everything passes away, what should I hope for? The answer: God. He alone remains.
> Verse 11: “His riches”: Some Bibles translate “his beauty” or “what he loves.” Just as a moth slowly eats a garment, so everything a person has passes away.

---

## I am a guest with you (Verses 12–13)

[12] “Hear my prayer, LORD,
and listen to my cry.
Do not be silent when you see my tears.
For I am a stranger with you,
a foreigner, as all my ancestors were.
[13] Oh, look away from me, so that I can get my strength back,
before I go away and am no more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “I am a stranger with you”: A person is only a guest on earth. He lives here only for a time, like Abraham, who lived as a stranger in the land. The earth belongs to God (Psalm 24:1).
> Verse 13: A shocking ending. In Hebrew, he asks word for word that God would look away from him. The one who prays feels God’s gaze like a burden that makes him suffer. He asks for a pause to catch his breath.
> Psalm 39 is one of the darkest psalms. It shows: Even prayers like this have a place in the Bible.
