# Psalm 139
### You know me

---

## God knows everything about me (Verses 1–6)

[1] For the music leader. A psalm by David.
LORD, you have looked deep inside me, and you know me.
[2] You know when I sit down and when I get up.
You understand my thoughts from far away.
[3] You look closely at my path and at when I lie down.
You know all my ways well.
[4] There is not a word on my tongue
that you, LORD, do not already know completely.
[5] You surround me from behind and in front.
You have put your hand on me.
[6] This knowledge is too wonderful for me.
It is too high. I cannot reach it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 139 is one of the deepest psalms about the relationship between God and a person.
> God knows me completely: what I do, what I think, what I want to say. That could make me afraid. But here it is above all a comfort: I am not unknown, not forgotten.
> Verse 5: “You have put your hand on me”: like a mother or a father who puts a protecting hand on their child.

---

## God is everywhere (Verses 7–12)

[7] Where could I go to get away from your Spirit?
Or where could I run away from your presence?
[8] If I go up into heaven, you are there.
If I make my bed in Sheol, look, you are there!
Sheol is the world of the dead.
[9] If I take the wings of the dawn
and settle down at the farthest end of the sea,
[10] even there your hand will lead me,
and your right hand will hold me.
[11] If I say, “Surely the darkness will cover me,
and the light around me will turn into night,”
[12] even the darkness is not dark to you.
The night shines like the day.
The darkness is like light to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> There is no place where God is not: not in heaven, not in the world of the dead, not at the end of the world.
> Verse 9: “The wings of the dawn”: to fly as fast as the light of the rising sun, all the way to the end of the world.
> Verses 11–12: Even in the darkest times, in depression, grief or fear, God is there. For him, the darkness is not dark.
> If you are in deep darkness right now: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Wonderfully made (Verses 13–18)

[13] For you made the deepest parts of me.
You wove me together inside my mother.
[14] I will thank you, for I am made in an amazing and wonderful way.
Your works are wonderful.
My soul knows that very well.
[15] My body was not hidden from you
when I was made in secret,
woven together with skill in the depths of the earth.
[16] Your eyes saw my body.
In your book they were all written down,
the days that were planned for me,
when not one of them had come yet.
[17] How precious your thoughts are to me, God!
How great their number is!
[18] If I tried to count them, they would be more than the sand.
When I wake up, I am still with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> God knew me even before I was born. He shaped me inside my mother, with as much skill as someone who weaves a carpet.
> Verse 14: “I am made in an amazing and wonderful way”: Every person is a wonder, no matter how they look, what they can do, or what other people say about them. This is also true for people with disabilities.
> Verse 15: “In the depths of the earth”: a poetic picture for the hidden place where a child grows.
> Verse 16: God knows my whole life, every day.
> Verse 18: God’s thoughts about me are countless. He thinks about me much more than I can count.

## Search me, God (Verses 19–24)

[19] God, if only you would kill the wicked!
Get away from me, you men who love to shed blood!
[20] For they speak against you with evil words.
Your enemies use your name for worthless things.
[21] LORD, don’t I hate those who hate you?
Am I not sad about those who rise up against you?
[22] I hate them with complete hatred.
They have become my enemies.
[23] Search me, God, and know my heart!
Test me, and know my thoughts!
[24] See if there is any evil way in me,
and lead me on the way that lasts forever!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–22 come as a surprise after such beautiful words. The person praying is full of anger about people who use violence and speak against God. He honestly tells God what he feels.
> But then something important happens: He looks at himself. “Search me … See if there is any evil way in me”. He knows: There can be evil in me too, even hatred.
> The psalm begins with “You have looked deep inside me” and ends with the request “Search me”. The person praying opens himself completely to God.
> Hatred is not a way for today. Jesus teaches us to love even our enemies (Matthew 5:44).
