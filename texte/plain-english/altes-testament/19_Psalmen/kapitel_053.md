# Psalm 53
### The fool says: There is no God

---

## No one does good (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the Chief Musician. To the tune of “Mahalath.” A contemplation by David.
The fool has said in his heart, “There is no God.”
They are rotten.
They have done disgusting evil.
There is no one who does good.
[2] God looks down from heaven on all people,
to see if there is anyone who understands,
anyone who seeks God.
[3] Every one of them has turned away.
They have all become filthy together.
There is no one who does good, no, not one.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 53 is almost the same as Psalm 14 (see there). The difference: Here it always says “God” (Elohim). In Psalm 14 it mostly says “LORD.” This fits the second book of the Psalms, which mostly says “God.”
> The fact that the same psalm appears twice shows: The book of Psalms grew together from several older collections.
> “Mahalath”: maybe a tune or an instrument. The meaning is uncertain. Some think of “sickness” or “round dance.”

---

## They were afraid where there was nothing to fear (Verses 4–6)

[4] Do those who do evil know nothing?
They eat up my people the way people eat bread.
They do not call on God.
[5] There they were filled with great fear,
where there was nothing to fear.
For God has scattered the bones
of the one who set up camp against you.
You have put them to shame,
because God has rejected them.
[6] Oh, that the salvation of Israel would come out of Zion!
When God brings his people back from captivity,
then Jacob will be full of joy, and Israel will be glad.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 is different from Psalm 14:5–6. Here it is about an enemy army that surrounds Jerusalem. Suddenly the enemies become afraid, even though there is no reason to be seen. God has made them run away. Their bones lie scattered on the field.
> Verse 6: The WEB says here “brings his people back from captivity.” The Hebrew word can also mean “turns their fortunes around” (compare Psalm 14:7).
