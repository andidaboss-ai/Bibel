# Psalm 6
### A prayer in deep trouble

---

## LORD, how long? (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. On string instruments, on the eight-stringed lyre. A psalm by David.
LORD, do not correct me in your anger.
Do not punish me in your fury.
[2] Be kind to me, LORD, for I am weak.
LORD, heal me, for my bones are shaking.
[3] My soul is also in deep distress.
But you, LORD—how long?
[4] Come back, LORD. Rescue my soul.
Save me because of your loving kindness.
[5] For in death, no one remembers you.
Who will thank you in Sheol (the world of the dead)?

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 6 is the first of the seven “psalms of repentance” (6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143). Christians pray them especially when they ask God for forgiveness.
> The person praying is sick in body and soul. “My bones are shaking”: The whole body is suffering.
> “How long?”: The sentence breaks off. The person praying has no more words. We hear this question often in the psalms.
> “Because of your loving kindness”: The person praying does not point to his own good deeds. He points to God’s faithful love.
> Verse 5: In the Old Testament, people imagined the world of the dead (“Sheol”) as a quiet, dark place. The hope of rising from the dead became clearer only later (compare Daniel 12:2).

## I am worn out from groaning (Verses 6–7)

[6] I am worn out from my groaning.
Every night I flood my bed.
I soak my couch with my tears.
[7] My eyes grow weak because of grief.
They grow old because of all my enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> A very honest picture: The person praying cries so much every night that his bed is wet. He is exhausted.
> The Bible does not hide such feelings. You may cry in front of God.
> If you feel like this right now and do not know how to go on: You are not alone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. If you are thinking about hurting yourself: In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## The LORD has heard my weeping (Verses 8–10)

[8] Go away from me, all you who do evil,
for the LORD has heard the sound of my weeping.
[9] The LORD has heard my plea for help.
The LORD accepts my prayer.
[10] May all my enemies be put to shame and filled with terror.
They will turn back. Suddenly they will be put to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Suddenly the turning point comes. The person praying is sure: God has heard my weeping!
> Three times he says: The LORD has heard, the LORD has heard, the LORD accepts. It is as if he says it to himself again and again, so that he can fully believe it.
> Many psalms of complaint have this turning point: At the beginning there is trouble, at the end there is trust. This is not because the situation has already changed. It is because the person praying knows: God has heard me.
