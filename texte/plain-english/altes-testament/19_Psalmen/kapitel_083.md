# Psalm 83
### God, do not keep silent!

---

## They make secret plans against your people (Verses 1–8)

[1] A song. A psalm by Asaph.
God, don’t keep silent!
Don’t keep silent, and don’t be still, God!
[2] For look, your enemies are in an uproar.
Those who hate you have raised their heads.
[3] They make clever plans against your people.
They plot against those you love.
[4] “Come,” they say, “let’s destroy them as a nation,
so that no one remembers the name of Israel anymore!”
[5] For they have made their plans together with one mind.
They make an alliance against you:
[6] the tents of Edom and the Ishmaelites,
Moab and the Hagrites,
[7] Gebal, Ammon, and Amalek,
Philistia with the people who live in Tyre.
[8] Assyria has also joined them.
They have helped the children of Lot.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 83 is the last of the psalms of Asaph. Many nations join together against Israel. Their goal is to wipe out the whole people.
> The nations surround Israel on all sides: Edom, Moab, and Ammon in the east (Moab and Ammon are the “children of Lot,” Genesis 19:36–38), Philistia and Tyre in the west, Assyria in the north.
> Gebal is probably a region south of the Dead Sea. The Hagrites were a people of the desert.
> Sadly, verse 4 is still relevant today: Again and again in history, people have tried to destroy the Jewish people. The psalm is a cry for help against such hatred.

---

## Do to them as you did to Midian (Verses 9–12)

[9] Do to them what you did to Midian,
what you did to Sisera and to Jabin at the Kishon River.
[10] They were destroyed at Endor.
They became like manure for the ground.
[11] Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb,
yes, all their princes like Zebah and Zalmunna,
[12] who said, “Let’s take God’s pastures for ourselves.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying remembers earlier rescues from the time of the judges:
> – Gideon defeated the Midianites and their leaders Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah, and Zalmunna (Judges 7–8).
> – Deborah and Barak defeated Sisera, the army commander of King Jabin, at the Kishon River (Judges 4–5).
> Just as God helped back then, he should help now too.

---

## So that they seek your name (Verses 13–18)

[13] My God, make them like tumbleweed,
like chaff blown by the wind.
[14] Like the fire that burns the forest,
like the flame that sets the mountains on fire,
[15] chase them with your storm
and terrify them with your wild winds.
[16] Fill their faces with shame,
so that they seek your name, LORD.
[17] Let them be put to shame and terrified forever.
Yes, let them be confused and die,
[18] so that they know that you alone, whose name is the LORD,
are the Most High over all the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Tumbleweed”: dry plants that break off from their stems and are rolled across the land by the wind.
> The request against the enemies is harsh. But verse 16 shows another goal: “so that they seek your name.” The enemies are not only to be defeated, but to come to know God.
> Verse 18: In the end, the whole world is to know that the LORD alone is God.
> As with other harsh psalms: The judgment is left to God. It is not permission for violence or hatred against other nations.
