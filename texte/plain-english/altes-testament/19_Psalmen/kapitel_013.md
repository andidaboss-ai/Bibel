# Psalm 13
### How much longer, LORD?

---

## How long? (Verses 1–2)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
How long, LORD? Will you forget me forever?
How long will you hide your face from me?
[2] How long must I struggle with my thoughts,
with sorrow in my heart every day?
How long will my enemy win over me?

> **What does this mean?**
> Four times: “How long?” The person praying feels that God has forgotten him. It has been like this for so long.
> Psalm 13 is short, but it is a pattern for many psalms of complaint: complaint – request – trust.
> It shows three sides of the trouble: God seems far away (verse 1). I myself am full of worry (the first half of verse 2). And others win over me (the second half of verse 2).

---

## Look and answer me (Verses 3–4)

[3] Look at me and answer me, LORD, my God.
Give light to my eyes,
so that I do not fall asleep in death,
[4] so that my enemy does not say, “I have beaten him,”
so that my enemies do not rejoice when I fall.

> **What does this mean?**
> The complaint turns into a request. “Give light to my eyes”: When someone is very sick or exhausted, the eyes grow dull. The person praying asks for new strength to live.
> If you feel as if God has forgotten you, and you see no way out: Get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

---

## But I trust (Verses 5–6)

[5] But I trust in your loving kindness.
My heart is full of joy because you save me.
[6] I will sing to the LORD,
because he has been good to me.

> **What does this mean?**
> “But I”: In the middle of the trouble, the person praying decides to trust God.
> He is already glad about the rescue, even though it has not come yet.
> Verse 6: He remembers: God has been good to me before. That gives hope for the future.
