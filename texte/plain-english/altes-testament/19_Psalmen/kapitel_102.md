# Psalm 102
### The prayer of a suffering person

---

## My days vanish like smoke (Verses 1–11)

[1] A prayer of a suffering person, when he is overwhelmed and pours out his complaint before the LORD.
Hear my prayer, LORD!
Let my cry come to you!
[2] Do not hide your face from me on the day of my trouble!
Turn your ear to me!
Answer me quickly on the day when I call!
[3] For my days vanish like smoke.
My bones burn like a torch.
[4] My heart is struck down like grass, and dried up,
for I forget to eat my bread.
[5] Because of my loud groaning,
my bones stick to my skin.
[6] I am like a pelican in the wilderness.
I have become like an owl in the ruins.
[7] I lie awake.
I have become like a sparrow that is alone on the roof.
[8] My enemies insult me all day long.
Those who are angry with me use my name as a curse.
[9] For I have eaten ashes like bread,
and I have mixed my drink with tears,
[10] because of your fury and your anger.
For you have picked me up and thrown me away.
[11] My days are like a long shadow in the evening.
I have dried up like grass.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 102 has a special heading: It is for every person who is “suffering” and “overwhelmed”. Anyone may use it as his or her own prayer.
> It is one of the seven psalms of repentance.
> The person praying describes deep loneliness and sickness: He forgets to eat. He is only skin and bones. He cannot sleep.
> Verses 6–7: Three pictures for loneliness: a bird in the wilderness, an owl in the ruins, a sparrow alone on the roof.
> If you feel this lonely and desperate: Please talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## But you remain (Verses 12–17)

[12] But you, LORD, will remain forever.
People will remember you in all generations.
[13] You will rise up and have mercy on Zion,
for it is time to be kind to her.
Yes, the time you have set has come.
[14] For your servants love her stones,
and they feel sorry for her dust.
[15] Then the nations will fear the name of the LORD,
and all the kings of the earth will fear your glory.
[16] For the LORD has built up Zion.
He has appeared in his glory.
[17] He has answered the prayer of the poor and helpless.
He has not looked down on their prayer.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 is the turning point: “But you, LORD, will remain forever.” People pass away, but God remains.
> Now the person praying thinks of Jerusalem (Zion), which lies in ruins. The people even love the stones and the dust of the destroyed city.
> Verse 17: God hears the prayer of those who are poor and left alone. He does not look down on it.

## For the generation to come (Verses 18–22)

[18] This will be written down for the generation to come.
A people who will be created later will praise Yah.
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.
[19] For he has looked down from his high, holy place.
From heaven, the LORD looked at the earth,
[20] to hear the groaning of the prisoners,
and to set free those who are sentenced to death.
[21] Then people will tell about the name of the LORD in Zion,
and about his praise in Jerusalem,
[22] when the peoples and the kingdoms gather together
to serve the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> How God saves should be written down, so that later generations can learn about it. This is one reason why the Bible was written.
> God looks down from heaven at the prisoners and at those sentenced to death.
> In the end, all nations gather to serve God.

## You stay the same (Verses 23–28)

[23] He has weakened my strength along the way.
He has shortened my days.
[24] I said: “My God, do not take me away in the middle of my life!
Your years go on through all generations.
[25] Long ago, you laid the foundation of the earth.
The heavens are the work of your hands.
[26] They will pass away, but you will remain.
Yes, all of them will wear out like a piece of clothing.
You will change them like a coat, and they will be changed.
[27] But you stay the same.
Your years will never end.
[28] The children of your servants will live on.
Their descendants will stand firm before you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying asks: Do not let me die in the middle of my life!
> Verses 25–27: Even heaven and earth will wear out like a piece of clothing, and one day they will be replaced. Only God always stays the same.
> The letter to the Hebrews quotes verses 25–27 and applies them to Jesus (Hebrews 1:10–12).
> Verse 28: The psalm ends with hope for the generations to come.
