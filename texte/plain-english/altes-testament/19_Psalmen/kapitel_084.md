# Psalm 84
### How lovely are your dwellings

---

## My soul longs (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. On an instrument from Gath. A psalm by the sons of Korah.
How lovely are your dwellings, LORD of Armies!
[2] My soul longs, yes, it grows weak with longing for the courts of the LORD.
My heart and my body cry out for the living God.
[3] Yes, even the sparrow has found a home,
and the swallow a nest for herself,
where she can raise her young,
near your altars, LORD of Armies,
my King and my God.
[4] Happy are those who live in your house.
They are always praising you.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 84 is a pilgrim song. A person longs to travel to the temple in Jerusalem.
> “How lovely”: how beautiful, how dearly loved.
> Verse 3 is a tender picture: Even the little birds have found a home at the temple. They build their nests close to God’s altar. The one praying almost envies them.
> Jesus says: Not one sparrow falls to the ground without your Father, and you are worth more than many sparrows (Matthew 10:29–31).
> Johannes Brahms set verses 1–2 to music in his “German Requiem.”

---

## Through the valley of Weeping (Verses 5–7)

[5] Happy are those whose strength is in you,
who have set their hearts on the pilgrim road.
[6] When they pass through the valley of Weeping,
they make it a place of springs.
Yes, the autumn rain covers it with blessings.
[7] They go from strength to strength.
Every one of them appears before God in Zion.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Who have set their hearts on the pilgrim road”: In their hearts, they are already on their way to God.
> Verse 6: The “valley of Weeping” (in Hebrew, “Baca”) may have been a dry valley on the way to Jerusalem. But for anyone who is on the way to God, even the valley of tears becomes a place full of springs.
> That is a beautiful picture for life: Even through hard times, God can give strength and blessing.
> Verse 7: “From strength to strength”: The closer they come to the goal, the stronger they become.

---

## A day in your courts (Verses 8–12)

[8] LORD God of Armies, hear my prayer!
Listen, God of Jacob!
Selah.
[9] Look, God, our shield!
Look at the face of your anointed one!
[10] For one day in your courts is better than 1,000.
I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God
than live in the tents of the wicked.
[11] For the LORD God is a sun and a shield.
The LORD gives grace and glory.
He does not hold back any good thing
from those who live without blame.
[12] LORD of Armies,
happy is the person who trusts in you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: A prayer for the king, the “anointed one.”
> Verse 10 is a well-known sentence: A single day with God is better than 1,000 days anywhere else. It is better to be a simple doorkeeper with God than a rich man among the wicked.
> The “sons of Korah” were doorkeepers at the temple themselves (1 Chronicles 9:19). They knew what they were singing about.
> Verse 11: God is “a sun and a shield”: He gives light and warmth, and he protects.
