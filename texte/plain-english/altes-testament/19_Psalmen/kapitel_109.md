# Psalm 109
### God whom I praise, do not be silent

---

## They pay back my love with hatred (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
God, you whom I praise, do not stay silent!
[2] For wicked people and liars have opened their mouths against me.
They have spoken to me with lying tongues.
[3] They have surrounded me with words of hatred.
They have fought against me for no reason.
[4] In return for my love, they are my enemies.
But I pray.
[5] They have paid me back evil for good,
and hatred for my love.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 109 is one of the hardest psalms. It contains the harshest curses in the whole book of Psalms.
> At the beginning, we see the situation: The person praying is attacked with lies by people he did good to and loved.
> Verse 4: “But I pray.” In Hebrew, it says: “But I – prayer.” His answer to hatred is not to fight back with violence. His answer is prayer.

---

## A terrible curse (Verses 6–15)

[6] Put a wicked man in charge over him!
Let an accuser stand at his right hand.
[7] When he is judged, let him be found guilty.
Let his prayer be counted as sin.
[8] Let his days be few.
Let another take his office.
[9] Let his children become orphans,
and his wife a widow.
[10] Let his children wander around as beggars.
Let them search for food far from their ruined homes.
[11] Let the man he owes money to take everything he has.
Let strangers rob him of what he worked for.
[12] Let no one show him kindness.
Let no one feel sorry for his orphans.
[13] Let his descendants be wiped out.
Let their name be erased in the next generation.
[14] Let the LORD remember the sin of his ancestors.
Do not let the sin of his mother be erased.
[15] Let their sins always be before the LORD,
so that he may wipe out the memory of them from the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are shocking. They wish the worst on the enemy and even on his family.
> There are two ways to understand these verses:
> – It is the curse of the person praying against his enemy, coming out of his deepest hurt.
> – Many interpreters think: It is a quotation. The person praying repeats what his enemies say against him. One reason for this view is that before and after this part, the psalm speaks of “they” (plural), but here it speaks of “he” (singular). Then the enemies would have spoken this curse over the person praying, and he brings it before God.
> What matters: Children and family are not guilty for what their parents did (Ezekiel 18:20). These verses do not allow anyone to wish evil on others or to do evil to them.
> Verse 8 (“Let another take his office”) is quoted by Peter when a successor for Judas is chosen (Acts 1:20).
> Jesus teaches a different way: “Bless those who curse you! Pray for those who mistreat you!” (Luke 6:28).

## He loved cursing (Verses 16–20)

[16] For he did not remember to show kindness.
Instead, he hunted down the poor and needy man,
and the one whose heart was broken, in order to kill them.
[17] Yes, he loved cursing, and it came back on him.
He did not enjoy blessing, and it stayed far from him.
[18] He put on cursing like his robe.
It soaked into his body like water,
and like oil into his bones.
[19] Let it be like the clothes that cover him,
like the belt that he always wears.
[20] This is how the LORD pays back my enemies,
those who speak evil against me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the enemy is described: He hunted down the poor and killed them. He loved cursing.
> Verses 17–19: Whoever curses others gets wrapped up in the curse himself, like in a piece of clothing. Evil falls back on the one who does it.
> Verse 20: The person praying hands everything over to the LORD. He does not take revenge himself.

---

## I am poor and needy (Verses 21–25)

[21] But you, LORD, my Lord, act for me for the sake of your name.
Because your loving kindness is good, rescue me!
[22] For I am poor and needy.
My heart is wounded inside me.
[23] I am fading away like an evening shadow.
I am shaken off like a locust.
[24] My knees are weak from fasting.
My body is thin and has no fat.
[25] I have become someone they mock.
When they see me, they shake their heads.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the person praying turns to God again. He is weak, sick and hurt.
> “Like an evening shadow”: It grows longer and paler until it disappears. That is how the person praying feels.
> “Shaken off like a locust”: worthless, simply brushed away.

---

## They may curse, but you bless (Verses 26–31)

[26] Help me, LORD, my God!
Save me because of your loving kindness,
[27] so that they will know that this is your hand,
that you, LORD, have done it.
[28] They may curse, but you bless.
When they rise up, they will be put to shame,
but your servant will be glad.
[29] Let my enemies be dressed in disgrace.
Let them cover themselves with their own shame as with a coat.
[30] I will give great thanks to the LORD with my mouth.
Yes, I will praise him among the crowd.
[31] For he stands at the right hand of the needy person,
to save him from those who want to condemn him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 28 is a turning point: “They may curse, but you bless.” Human curses have no power when God blesses.
> Verse 31 is the answer to verse 6: There, an accuser was to stand at the right hand of the person praying. Now God himself stands at the right hand of the poor, as the one who defends him.
> Psalm 109 shows: We may tell God even our deepest anger and pain. God hears that too. But judgment stays with God. And God is on the side of the poor and the hurt.
