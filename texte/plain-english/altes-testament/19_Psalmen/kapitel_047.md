# Psalm 47
### God is King over all the earth

---

## Clap your hands, all you nations! (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by the sons of Korah.
Oh, clap your hands, all you nations!
Shout to God with loud cries of joy!
[2] For the LORD Most High is awesome.
He is a great King over all the earth.
[3] He puts nations under us,
and peoples under our feet.
[4] He chooses our land for us to own,
the pride of Jacob, whom he loved.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> A joyful psalm. All nations, not only Israel, are called to clap their hands and shout for joy to God.
> God is not only the God of one people. He is King over all the earth.
> Verse 3 speaks in the language of that time about victory over other peoples. But the whole psalm invites all nations to praise God (verses 1 and 9).
> “The pride of Jacob”: the land that God gave to Israel.

---

## God has gone up (Verses 5–9)

[5] God has gone up with shouts of joy,
the LORD with the sound of a trumpet.
[6] Sing praises to God! Sing praises!
Sing praises to our King! Sing praises!
[7] For God is the King of all the earth.
Sing praises with understanding.
[8] God rules over the nations.
God sits on his holy throne.
[9] The leaders of the peoples have gathered together
as the people of the God of Abraham.
For the shields of the earth belong to God.
He is highly honored!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: “God has gone up”: like a king who goes up to his throne with shouts of joy and the sound of trumpets. Maybe the ark of the covenant was carried up to the temple in a procession.
> Christians read this psalm on Ascension Day, because Jesus also went up to God.
> Verse 9 is astonishing: The leaders of all peoples gather as “the people of the God of Abraham.” God had promised Abraham: “Through you, all the families of the earth will be blessed” (Genesis 12:3). Here it becomes reality.
> “The shields of the earth”: the powerful people and rulers of the world. They too belong to God.
