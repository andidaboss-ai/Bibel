# Psalm 90
### Teach us to count our days

---

## The fourth book of Psalms

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 90, the fourth book of Psalms begins (Psalms 90–106).
> After the end of the kingdom (Psalm 89), this book turns its eyes again to God himself as King. Many psalms call out: “The LORD reigns!” (Psalm 93; 96–99).
> The book begins with the only psalm that is linked to Moses. That reminds us of the time before the kings, when God led his people through the desert.

---

## From everlasting to everlasting you are God (Verses 1–6)

[1] A prayer by Moses, the man of God.
Lord, you have been our home
for all generations.
[2] Before the mountains were born,
before you formed the earth and the world,
from everlasting to everlasting, you are God.
[3] You turn people back to dust
and say, “Return, you children of men.”
[4] For in your eyes a thousand years
are like yesterday when it is past,
like one watch in the night.
[5] You sweep people away as they sleep.
In the morning they sprout like new grass.
[6] In the morning it sprouts and grows.
By evening it is withered and dry.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is eternal. He was already there before the mountains came to be. For people, he is like a home, a “dwelling place,” where they are safe.
> Verse 3: “Back to dust”: The Hebrew word means something crushed, like dust. People return to the dust they were made from (Genesis 3:19).
> Verse 4: For God, a thousand years are like a day. Peter quotes this in 2 Peter 3:8.
> Verses 5–6: Human life is like grass: fresh in the morning, withered in the evening.

---

## Our life is like a sigh (Verses 7–11)

[7] For we are used up by your anger.
We are terrified by your fury.
[8] You have put our sins in front of you,
our secret sins in the light of your presence.
[9] For all our days have passed away under your anger.
We bring our years to an end like a sigh.
[10] The days of our years are seventy,
or eighty if we are strong.
But even their best is only hard work and sorrow,
for it soon passes, and we fly away.
[11] Who knows the power of your anger,
your fury, as great as the fear that you deserve?

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm is honest: Life is short, and it is often full of hard work. Human guilt adds to this.
> Verse 10 is very well known: A human life lasts about 70 or 80 years. Even the best years are full of work and worry. And then it is over quickly.
> This psalm is often read at funerals.

---

## Teach us to count our days (Verse 12)

[12] So teach us to count our days,
so that we may gain a wise heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the key verse of the psalm. Whoever knows that life is short lives more mindfully and more wisely.
> “To count our days” means: to remember that our life has an end.
> It is not about being afraid of death. It is about using well the time we have, and telling the important things apart from the unimportant things.

---

## Make the work of our hands last (Verses 13–17)

[13] Turn back, LORD! How long?
Have compassion on your servants!
[14] Satisfy us in the morning with your loving kindness,
so that we may shout for joy and be glad all our days!
[15] Make us glad for as many days as you have made us suffer,
for as many years as we have seen trouble!
[16] Let your servants see your work,
and let their children see your glory!
[17] Let the kindness of the Lord our God be on us!
Make the work of our hands last for us.
Yes, make the work of our hands last!

> **What does this mean?**
> After thinking about how short life is, the request comes: God, give us joy anyway! Let us enjoy the days we have.
> Verse 15: A bold request: Give us as many good days as we had bad ones.
> Verse 17: Life is short, but what we do should not be for nothing. God is to make our work last. A beautiful prayer before any work.
