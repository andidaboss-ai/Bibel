# Psalm 42
### As the deer longs for fresh water

---

## The second book of the Psalms

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 42, the second book of the Psalms begins (Psalms 42–72).
> In this part, God is usually called “God” (Hebrew “Elohim”), and less often “LORD” (YHWH). That is why Psalms 42–83 are also called the “Elohistic Psalter.”
> Many psalms in this part come from the “sons of Korah.” They were Levites who served in the temple as singers and gatekeepers (1 Chronicles 9:19; 2 Chronicles 20:19).

---

## My soul thirsts for God (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the Chief Musician. A contemplation by the sons of Korah.
As the deer pants for streams of water,
so my soul pants for you, God.
[2] My soul thirsts for God, for the living God.
When can I come and stand before God?
[3] My tears have been my food day and night,
while people keep asking me, “Where is your God?”
[4] I remember these things,
and I pour out my soul inside me:
how I used to go with the crowd
and lead them to God’s house,
with shouts of joy and praise,
a great crowd celebrating a holy day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 42 and Psalm 43 belong together. They have the same refrain (42:5; 42:11; 43:5).
> Verse 1 is a well-known picture: A deer in the dry season searches desperately for water. That is how the one who prays longs for God.
> He is far away from the temple in Jerusalem, maybe in exile or in captivity. He can no longer go to the feasts.
> Verse 3: He cries day and night. And people mock him: “Where is your God?”
> Verse 4: He remembers the past, when he joyfully went to the temple with the crowd. This memory hurts.

---

## Why are you so downcast, my soul? (Verse 5)

[5] Why are you in such despair, my soul?
Why are you so troubled inside me?
Hope in God!
For I will still praise him
for the help and rescue of his presence.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the refrain. The one who prays talks to himself, to his own soul. He asks: Why are you so downcast?
> And then he encourages himself: Hope in God! A time will come when I can praise again.
> This is an important help in sadness: Do not only listen to your feelings. Speak to yourself and remember God’s faithfulness.

## All your waves have swept over me (Verses 6–10)

[6] My God, my soul is in despair inside me.
So I remember you from the land of the Jordan,
from the heights of Hermon, from the hill Mizar.
[7] One deep sea calls to another
at the roar of your waterfalls.
All your waves and breakers have swept over me.
[8] In the daytime the LORD will send his loving kindness.
In the night his song will be with me:
a prayer to the God of my life.
[9] I will say to God, my rock,
“Why have you forgotten me?
Why do I go around mourning,
because the enemy oppresses me?”
[10] My enemies mock me.
It feels like a sword in my bones.
They keep asking me, “Where is your God?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: The one who prays is in the north of the land, at the springs of the Jordan near Mount Hermon. There are many waterfalls there. The hill Mizar is unknown.
> Verse 7: The roar of the waterfalls becomes a picture of his trouble: Like waves, his worries crash over him. He feels as if he is drowning.
> Verse 8: In the middle of it, a ray of light: God gives loving kindness in the day and a song in the night.
> Verse 9: Again the honest question: “Why have you forgotten me?”

---

## Once more: Hope in God (Verse 11)

[11] Why are you in such despair, my soul?
Why are you so troubled inside me?
Hope in God!
For I will still praise him,
the one who helps and rescues me, and my God.

> **What does this mean?**
> The refrain comes again. The one who prays struggles with his sadness again and again. It is not a victory won once and for all. It is a struggle.
> Psalm 42 helps many people who suffer from depression or deep sadness. It shows: People of faith also know times like this. You may tell God about them.
> If you feel like this and see no way out: Get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. A doctor can also help. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
