# Psalm 110
### Sit at my right hand

---

## The king at God’s right hand (Verses 1–3)

[1] A psalm by David.
The LORD says to my Lord:
“Sit at my right hand,
until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.”
[2] The LORD will stretch out your mighty scepter from Zion.
A scepter is a royal staff. It is a sign of a king’s power.
Rule among your enemies!
[3] Your people will gladly offer themselves on the day of your power,
dressed in holy clothing.
Out of the womb of the morning,
you have the dew of your youth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 110 is the psalm that is quoted most often in the New Testament (more than 20 times).
> Verse 1: “The LORD says to my Lord”: The LORD is God. “My Lord” means the king. God invites the king to sit at his right side, the place of honor. God will put the enemies under the king’s feet. Back then, winners put their foot on the necks of those they had defeated.
> Jesus asked the teachers of the law: If David calls the Messiah “my Lord”, how can the Messiah be only David’s son? (Mark 12:35–37). Christians believe that after his resurrection, Jesus sits at God’s right hand (Acts 2:34–36; Hebrews 1:13).
> Jewish people have understood the psalm as being about the king from David’s family or about the coming Messiah.
> Verse 3 is very hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: The king is young and fresh like the dew in the morning.

---

## A priest in the order of Melchizedek (Verse 4)

[4] The LORD has sworn, and he will not change his mind:
“You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Israel, kings and priests were usually different people. Kings came from the tribe of Judah, priests from the tribe of Levi.
> But this king is also a priest, following the example of Melchizedek. Melchizedek was king of Salem (Jerusalem) and a priest at the same time. He blessed Abraham (Genesis 14:18–20).
> The letter to the Hebrews explains in detail that Jesus is this eternal priest in the order of Melchizedek (Hebrews 5–7).

---

## The king’s victory (Verses 5–7)

[5] The Lord is at your right hand.
He will crush kings on the day of his anger.
[6] He will judge among the nations.
He will pile up dead bodies.
He will crush the ruler of the whole earth.
[7] He will drink from the stream along the way.
That is why he will lift up his head.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses use the war language of that time to describe victory over all enemies. It is a picture of God’s final victory over evil. It is not a call to violence.
> Verse 7: A puzzling ending. Maybe it is a picture: The king refreshes himself at a stream on the way and goes on to victory. Maybe it reminds us of a ritual when a king was anointed at the Gihon spring (1 Kings 1:38–39).
