# Psalm 101
### A king’s promise

---

## I will live a blameless life (Verses 1–4)

[1] A psalm by David.
I will sing about loving kindness and justice.
To you, LORD, I will sing praises.
[2] I will be careful to live a blameless life.
Blameless means: no one can accuse me of doing wrong.
When will you come to me?
I will live in my house with an honest heart.
[3] I will not put anything vile in front of my eyes.
I hate what unfaithful people do.
It will not stick to me.
[4] A twisted heart will be far from me.
I will have nothing to do with evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 101 is like a king’s plan for how he will govern. He promises to rule justly, and he begins with his own life.
> “In my house”: Honesty begins at home, not only in public.
> Verse 3: “I will not put anything vile in front of my eyes”: He does not want to be influenced by bad things. This is true today too, for example in what we choose to watch.
> “When will you come to me?”: The king longs for God to be near.

---

## Who may live with me (Verses 5–8)

[5] Whoever secretly tells lies about his neighbor, I will silence.
Whoever is arrogant and proud, I will not put up with.
[6] My eyes will look for the faithful people of the land,
so that they may live with me.
Whoever lives a blameless life will serve me.
[7] Whoever cheats others will not live in my house.
Whoever tells lies will not stand before my eyes.
[8] Morning after morning, I will destroy all the wicked people of the land.
I will remove all who do evil from the LORD’s city.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king does not want people who spread lies, liars or cheaters in his government. He looks for faithful and honest servants.
> Verse 8: “Morning after morning”: Back then, kings held court in the morning. The king promises to make sure every day that there is justice and that crimes are punished.
> This is the task of a ruler with lawful authority to judge, under just laws. It does not allow any person to use violence against others on their own.
