# Psalm 89
### God’s covenant with David

---

## I will sing of the LORD’s loving kindness (Verses 1–4)

[1] A contemplative psalm by Ethan, the Ezrahite.
I will sing of the loving kindness of the LORD forever.
With my mouth I will make your faithfulness known to all generations.
[2] I say, “Love stands firm forever.
You set up the heavens. Your faithfulness is in them.”
[3] “I have made a covenant with my chosen one.
I have made a promise with an oath to David, my servant:
[4] ‘I will make your descendants firm forever,
and I will build up your throne for all generations.’”
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like Heman, Ethan was a well-known wise man (1 Kings 4:31).
> Psalm 89 remembers God’s promise to David: His family would sit on the throne forever (2 Samuel 7:12–16).
> Two words come up again and again in this psalm: “loving kindness” (or love) and “faithfulness,” seven times each. God’s loving kindness and faithfulness are the foundation for everything.
> But at the end of the psalm, a hard question is asked: What has become of this promise?

---

## Who is like the LORD? (Verses 5–18)

[5] The heavens will praise your wonders, LORD,
and your faithfulness in the assembly of the holy ones.
[6] For who in the skies can be compared to the LORD?
Who among the heavenly beings is like the LORD,
[7] a very awesome God in the council of the holy ones,
to be feared more than all who are around him?
[8] LORD God of Armies, who is mighty like you?
Yah, your faithfulness is all around you.
[9] You rule over the proud sea.
When its waves rise up, you calm them.
[10] You have crushed Rahab like someone who has been killed.
With your mighty arm you have scattered your enemies.
[11] The heavens are yours. The earth is yours too,
the world and everything in it. You have founded them.
[12] You created the north and the south.
Tabor and Hermon shout for joy at your name.
[13] You have a mighty arm.
Your hand is strong, and your right hand is lifted high.
[14] Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne.
Loving kindness and truth go ahead of you.
[15] Happy are the people who learn to cheer for you.
They walk in the light of your presence, LORD.
[16] They rejoice in your name all day long.
They are lifted up by your righteousness.
[17] For you are the glory of their strength.
By your kindness our horn will be lifted up.
[18] For our shield belongs to the LORD,
and our king to the Holy One of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> A great song of praise to God’s power. In heaven there is no one who is equal to him.
> Verse 10: Here “Rahab” is a sea monster, a picture for chaos (compare Job 26:12). Sometimes Rahab also stands for Egypt (Psalm 87:4).
> Verse 12: Tabor and Hermon are two well-known mountains in the north of Israel.
> Verse 14: God’s rule rests on righteousness and justice. Loving kindness and truth go ahead of him like servants in front of a king.
> Verse 18: The king of Israel belongs to God. He is God’s “shield,” the one who protects the people on God’s behalf.

---

## I have found David (Verses 19–29)

[19] Then you spoke in a vision to your faithful ones and said,
“I have given strength to a warrior.
I have lifted up a young man from the people.
[20] I have found David, my servant.
I have anointed him with my holy oil.
[21] My hand will stay firmly with him.
My arm will also make him strong.
[22] No enemy will demand tribute from him.
No wicked man will oppress him.
[23] I will beat down his enemies in front of him
and strike those who hate him.
[24] But my faithfulness and my loving kindness will be with him.
In my name his horn will be lifted up.
[25] I will also put his hand on the sea,
and his right hand on the rivers.
[26] He will call to me, ‘You are my Father,
my God, and the rock who saves me!’
[27] I will also make him my firstborn,
the highest of the kings of the earth.
[28] I will keep my loving kindness for him forever.
My covenant with him will stand firm.
[29] I will also make his descendants last forever,
and his throne as long as the days of heaven.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here God’s promise to David is repeated in detail. The “vision” is probably the message of the prophet Nathan (2 Samuel 7).
> God “found” David, a simple young man, and anointed him as king.
> Verses 26–27: David may call God “Father,” and God makes him the “firstborn,” the most important king in the world.
> Christians see in these words a pointer to Jesus, the Son of David, the “firstborn” (Colossians 1:15; Revelation 1:5).

---

## Even if they become unfaithful (Verses 30–37)

[30] If his children leave my law
and do not live by my rules,
[31] if they break my laws
and do not keep my commandments,
[32] then I will punish their sin with the rod,
and their guilt with beatings.
[33] But I will not completely take my loving kindness away from him.
I will not let my faithfulness fail.
[34] I will not break my covenant.
I will not change what my lips have spoken.
[35] Once and for all I have sworn by my holiness:
I will not lie to David.
[36] His descendants will last forever,
and his throne will be like the sun before me.
[37] It will stand firm forever like the moon,
the faithful witness in the sky.”
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises: Even if David’s descendants disobey, he will punish them, but he will not cancel his covenant.
> “The rod” and “beatings”: That is the language of raising children back then. Today we know that violence against children is wrong. What is meant here is: God will not overlook the guilt, but he will not reject his people.
> Verse 37: The moon, which is in the sky every night, is like a witness to God’s faithfulness.

---

## But now you have rejected him (Verses 38–45)

[38] But you have rejected him and pushed him away.
You have been angry with your anointed one.
[39] You have turned your back on the covenant with your servant.
You have thrown his crown into the dust and made it unclean.
[40] You have broken down all his walls.
You have turned his fortresses into ruins.
[41] All who pass by rob him.
He has become a joke to his neighbors.
[42] You have lifted up the right hand of his enemies.
You have made all his enemies rejoice.
[43] Yes, you turn back the edge of his sword,
and you have not helped him in battle.
[44] You have put an end to his splendor
and thrown his throne down to the ground.
[45] You have cut short the days of his youth.
You have covered him with shame.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the great break: “But you have rejected him!” The king from David’s family is defeated, his crown lies in the dust, Jerusalem is destroyed.
> This probably means the end of the kingdom of Judah, when the Babylonians conquered Jerusalem in 586 BC and took King Jehoiachin or King Zedekiah prisoner (2 Kings 24–25).
> The one praying openly accuses God: You have broken your covenant! That sounds harsh, but it shows how seriously he takes God’s promise.

---

## Where is your loving kindness from before? (Verses 46–51)

[46] How long, LORD? Will you hide yourself forever?
Will your anger burn like fire?
[47] Remember how short my time is!
How short and empty you have made the lives of all people!
[48] What man can live and never see death?
Who can save his soul from the power of Sheol?
Selah.
[49] Lord, where is your loving kindness from before,
which you swore to David in your faithfulness?
[50] Lord, remember how your servants are insulted,
how I carry in my heart the mocking of all the mighty peoples,
[51] the mocking with which your enemies have mocked, LORD,
with which they have mocked every step of your anointed one.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 49 is the great question of the psalm: “Lord, where is your loving kindness from before, which you swore to David in your faithfulness?”
> That is a question the Jewish people asked themselves for a long time after the exile. There was no longer a king from David’s family.
> The Bible’s answer is the hope for the Messiah, the coming king from David’s family. Jews are still waiting for him today. Christians believe that Jesus, the “Son of David,” is this king (Luke 1:32–33).

---

## The end of the third book of Psalms (Verse 52)

[52] Praise be to the LORD forever.
Amen and amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> This verse closes the third book of Psalms (Psalms 73–89).
> It is remarkable: After all the lament and the open questions, the book still ends with praise. Faith holds on to God, even when the answers are missing.
> With Psalm 90, the fourth book of Psalms begins.
