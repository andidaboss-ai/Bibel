# Psalm 25
### Show me your ways

---

## To you I lift up my soul (Verses 1–3)

[1] By David.
To you, LORD, I lift up my soul.
[2] My God, I have trusted in you.
Do not let me be put to shame.
Do not let my enemies win over me.
[3] Yes, no one who waits for you will be put to shame.
Those who are unfaithful for no reason will be put to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Hebrew, Psalm 25 is an alphabet poem: Each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. This made it easier to learn by heart.
> “I lift up my soul to you”: I turn my whole self toward you, the way people lift up their hands to pray.
> “Be put to shame”: to stand there embarrassed and disappointed. Whoever waits for God will not be disappointed.

---

## Teach me (Verses 4–7)

[4] Show me your ways, LORD.
Teach me your paths.
[5] Guide me in your truth, and teach me,
for you are the God who saves me.
I wait for you all day long.
[6] LORD, remember your tender mercy and your loving kindness,
for they have been there since ancient times.
[7] Do not remember the sins of my youth or the wrong things I have done.
Remember me in your loving kindness,
because you are good, LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying wants to learn from God how to live the right way.
> Verses 6–7 are a beautiful play on words: God should “remember” his loving kindness, but “not remember” my sins. God’s memory should be full of love, not full of blame.
> “The sins of my youth”: mistakes that someone made as a young person, which still weigh on them later.

---

## The LORD is good (Verses 8–11)

[8] The LORD is good and honest.
That is why he shows sinners the way.
[9] He guides the humble in what is right.
He teaches the humble his way.
[10] All the paths of the LORD are loving kindness and truth
for those who keep his covenant and his commands.
[11] For the sake of your name, LORD,
forgive my guilt, for it is great.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Because God is good, he does not give up on sinners. He shows them the way back.
> God teaches “the humble,” who admit that they do not know everything.
> Verse 11: The person praying asks for forgiveness. This is not because he has earned it. It is “for the sake of your name”: because God is merciful.

---

## The friendship of the LORD (Verses 12–15)

[12] Who is the person who fears the LORD?
The LORD will teach him the way he should choose.
[13] He will live a good and peaceful life.
His descendants will inherit the land.
[14] The friendship of the LORD is with those who fear him.
He will show them his covenant.
[15] My eyes are always on the LORD,
for he will pull my feet out of the net.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 is beautiful: God is a friend to those who honor him. The Hebrew word means a close, trusted circle in which people share secrets.
> Jesus says: “I don’t call you servants anymore. … But I have called you friends” (John 15:15).
> Verse 15: Like an animal caught in a net, the person praying needs God’s help to get free.

---

## Turn to me (Verses 16–22)

[16] Turn to me, and be kind to me,
for I am lonely and suffering.
[17] The troubles of my heart have grown great.
Oh, bring me out of my distress.
[18] Look at how I suffer and how hard things are for me.
Forgive all my sins.
[19] Look at my enemies, for there are many of them.
They hate me with a cruel hatred.
[20] Oh, guard my soul and rescue me.
Do not let me be disappointed, for I take refuge in you.
[21] Let honesty and doing what is right protect me,
for I wait for you.
[22] God, set Israel free from all its troubles.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Lonely and suffering”: The person praying feels left alone. He asks God to turn toward him.
> He brings everything to God: his fears, his struggles, his sins, his enemies.
> Verse 22: At the end, the prayer widens to the whole people. This verse stands outside the alphabet, like an afterword. The individual does not pray only for himself, but for everyone.
