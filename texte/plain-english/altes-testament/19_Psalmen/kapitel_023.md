# Psalm 23
### The LORD is my shepherd

---

## The good shepherd (Verses 1–3)

[1] A psalm by David.
The LORD is my shepherd.
I will not lack anything.
[2] He lets me lie down in green pastures.
He leads me beside still waters.
[3] He restores my soul.
He guides me on paths of righteousness.
That means: on paths that are right.
He does this for the sake of his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 23 is the best-known psalm in the Bible. Many people know it by heart. It is prayed at baptisms, weddings and funerals.
> David was a shepherd himself before he became king (1 Samuel 16:11). He knew what a good shepherd does for his sheep.
> A shepherd takes care of everything: He finds fresh grass and calm water that the sheep can drink from. He leads them on safe paths.
> “I will not lack anything”: This does not mean that I get everything I want. It means that I have everything I really need.
> “He restores my soul”: He gives me new strength and new courage.
> “For the sake of his name”: God leads me well because he is faithful. His good name depends on it.
> Later, Jesus says that he is the good shepherd, and that the good shepherd gives his life for the sheep (John 10:11).

---

## In the dark valley (Verse 4)

[4] Even when I walk through the valley of the shadow of death,
I will fear no evil, for you are with me.
Your rod and your staff comfort me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Life is not only green pastures. There are also dark valleys: sickness, grief, fear, being close to death.
> But the shepherd goes along. That is why: “I will fear no evil, for you are with me.”
> Worth noticing: Until now, David has spoken about God (“he”). Now, in the dark valley, he speaks directly to God: “You are with me.” In times of need, the relationship becomes personal.
> The rod was a club to fight off wild animals. The staff was a long shepherd’s staff to guide the sheep and bring them back. Both show: The shepherd protects and leads.

---

## The table that is set (Verses 5–6)

[5] You prepare a table for me in front of my enemies.
You anoint my head with oil.
That means: You pour oil on my head.
My cup overflows.
[6] Surely goodness and loving kindness will follow me all the days of my life.
And I will live in the LORD’s house forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the picture changes: God is no longer only the shepherd. He is also a generous host.
> “In front of my enemies”: The enemies are still there, but they cannot do anything. I am God’s guest, and I am safe.
> “You anoint my head with oil”: This is how people honored a guest back then. The oil smelled good and refreshed the guest.
> “My cup overflows”: God gives more than enough.
> Verse 6: “Goodness and loving kindness will follow me”: In Hebrew, it says more exactly: “they will chase after me.” It is not my enemies who chase me, but God’s goodness.
> “I will live in the LORD’s house forever”: For David, this was the temple. Christians and many Jews also understand it as hope for a life with God beyond death.
