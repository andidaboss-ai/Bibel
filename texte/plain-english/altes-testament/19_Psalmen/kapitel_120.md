# Psalm 120
### I am for peace

---

## A stranger among people who love to fight (Verses 1–7)

[1] A song of ascents.
That means: a song for going up to Jerusalem.
When I was in trouble, I cried out to the LORD.
He answered me.
[2] LORD, save my life from lying lips,
from a tongue that deceives!
[3] What will be given to you, and what more will be done to you,
you tongue that deceives?
[4] Sharp arrows of a warrior,
together with burning coals of juniper wood!
[5] How terrible for me that I live in Meshech,
that I live among the tents of Kedar!
[6] My soul has lived too long
with people who hate peace.
[7] I am for peace.
But when I speak, they are for war.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 120, the 15 “songs of ascents” begin (Psalms 120–134). In Hebrew, the name means: songs of going up. Pilgrims sang them when they went up to Jerusalem for the great festivals. Jerusalem lies on a mountain, so people “go up” to it.
> The journey begins far away: Meshech was in the north (in today’s Turkey). Kedar was a people in the Arabian desert. Both places stand for “very far away, in a hostile place.”
> Verses 3–4: Lies hurt like arrows and burn like hot coals. The person praying says: Whoever hurts others with lies will be hit himself. He leaves the judgment to God.
> Verse 7: A sentence that still fits today: “I am for peace. But when I speak, they are for war.” Even so, the person praying holds on to peace.
