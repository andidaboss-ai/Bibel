# Psalm 38
### A sick man confesses his guilt

---

## Your arrows have struck me (Verses 1–8)

[1] A psalm by David, for a memorial.
LORD, do not correct me in your anger.
Do not punish me in your burning rage.
[2] For your arrows have pierced me.
Your hand presses hard on me.
[3] Nothing in my body is healthy
because of your anger.
Nothing in my bones is well
because of my sin.
[4] For my guilt has gone over my head.
It is like a heavy load, too heavy for me.
[5] My wounds stink and are full of pus
because of my foolishness.
[6] I am in pain and bent over low.
I go around mourning all day long.
[7] For my waist is full of burning pain.
Nothing in my body is healthy.
[8] I am weak and badly crushed.
I groan because my heart is in anguish.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 38 is one of the seven psalms of repentance. “For a memorial”: Maybe it was sung at the “memorial offering,” or it is meant to remind God of the one who prays.
> The one who prays is very sick. He understands his sickness as a result of his guilt.
> Important: The Bible does not say that every sickness is a punishment for sin. The book of Job showed this clearly, and Jesus says it too (John 9:1–3). But this man experiences it that way for himself, and he brings it honestly before God.
> Verse 4: “My guilt has gone over my head”: like water that rises over your head. Many people who feel guilty know this feeling.

---

## My friends keep their distance (Verses 9–14)

[9] Lord, everything I long for is in front of you.
My groaning is not hidden from you.
[10] My heart is pounding. My strength leaves me.
Even the light of my eyes has left me.
[11] My loved ones and my friends stay away from me
because of my sickness.
My relatives stand far away.
[12] Those who want to take my life set traps for me.
Those who want to hurt me speak of ruin.
They think up lies all day long.
[13] But I am like a deaf man. I do not hear.
I am like a man who cannot speak,
who does not open his mouth.
[14] Yes, I am like a man who does not hear,
who has no answer in his mouth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: A comfort: God knows everything, even what I cannot put into words.
> Verse 11: This is very painful: Just when he is sick, his friends and his family keep their distance. Maybe they are afraid of catching it. Maybe they think it is his own fault.
> Verses 13–14: The one who prays does not defend himself against the attacks. He stays silent. This reminds us of the suffering servant of God in Isaiah 53:7, and of Jesus, who stayed silent before those who accused him.

---

## I hope in you (Verses 15–22)

[15] For I hope in you, LORD.
You will answer, Lord my God.
[16] For I said, “Do not let them be glad about my trouble,
or make themselves big over me when my foot slips.”
[17] For I am about to fall.
My pain is always in front of me.
[18] For I will confess my guilt.
I am sorry for my sin.
[19] But my enemies are strong and many.
There are many who hate me for no reason.
[20] Those who pay back evil for good are also against me,
because I follow what is good.
[21] Do not leave me, LORD.
My God, do not be far from me.
[22] Hurry to help me, Lord, my salvation.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: He is silent before people, but he talks with God. He expects an answer from God.
> Verse 18: He honestly confesses his guilt. That is the first step toward healing.
> The psalm does not end with an answer, but with an urgent cry for help. Not every prayer ends with a solution. Sometimes all that is left is the cry: “Do not leave me, LORD.”
