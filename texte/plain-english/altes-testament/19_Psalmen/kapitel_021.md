# Psalm 21
### Thanks for the king’s victory

---

## The king rejoices (Verses 1–7)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
The king rejoices in your strength, LORD!
How greatly he rejoices that you save him!
[2] You have given him what his heart desires.
You have not refused what his lips asked for.
Selah.
[3] For you come to meet him with rich blessings.
You put a crown of pure gold on his head.
[4] He asked you for life, and you gave it to him,
a long life, forever and ever.
[5] He has great glory because you saved him.
You give him honor and majesty.
[6] For you make him blessed forever.
You fill him with joy in your presence.
[7] For the king trusts in the LORD.
Because of the loving kindness of the Most High, nothing will shake him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 21 belongs together with Psalm 20. In Psalm 20, the people prayed for the king. In Psalm 21, they give thanks, because God has answered the prayer.
> Verse 2: “You have given him what his heart desires.” This is the direct answer to Psalm 20:4. There it said: “May he give you what your heart desires.”
> Verse 7 is the center of the psalm: The king is strong because he trusts in God, not in himself.
> Jews have also related the psalm to the Messiah, and Christians to Jesus, the King who has life “forever and ever.”

---

## God’s power against the enemies (Verses 8–13)

[8] Your hand will find all your enemies.
Your right hand will find those who hate you.
[9] When the time of your anger comes, you will make them like a blazing furnace.
The LORD will swallow them up in his fury.
The fire will burn them up.
[10] You will wipe out their descendants from the earth,
their children from among the people.
[11] For they planned evil against you.
They came up with an evil plan against you, but it cannot succeed.
[12] For you will make them turn their backs and run,
when you aim your bows at their faces.
[13] Be honored above all, LORD, in your strength.
Then we will sing and praise your power.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the psalm is about the enemies who plan evil against God and the king.
> The pictures are very harsh: fire, destruction, even the descendants. This is the language of war in the ancient Near East.
> Important: The king and the people are not the ones who take revenge here. The judgment is left to God. And it falls on those who “planned evil against you,” that is, against God.
> For readers today: These verses do not give anyone permission to persecute enemies or their children. Children are not responsible for what their parents did (Ezekiel 18:20). Jesus teaches us to love our enemies.
> The psalm does not end with the enemies, but with the praise of God.
