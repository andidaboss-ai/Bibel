# Psalm 113
### Who is like the LORD?

---

## From the rising of the sun to its setting (Verses 1–4)

[1] Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.
Praise, you servants of the LORD!
Praise the name of the LORD!
[2] Blessed be the name of the LORD,
from now on and forever!
[3] From the rising of the sun to its setting,
the name of the LORD is to be praised!
[4] The LORD is high above all nations.
His glory is above the heavens.

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 113, the “Hallel” begins (Psalm 113–118). Jewish people still sing these psalms at the great festivals today, especially at Passover. Jesus also sang them with his disciples at the Last Supper (Matthew 26:30).
> Verse 3: God should be praised all day long and everywhere in the world, from east to west.

---

## He lifts the poor out of the dust (Verses 5–9)

[5] Who is like the LORD, our God,
who sits on his throne on high,
[6] who bends down to look
at the heavens and at the earth?
[7] He lifts the poor out of the dust.
He raises the needy from the garbage heap,
[8] to seat them with princes,
with the princes of his people.
[9] He gives the woman who could not have children a home.
She becomes a happy mother of children.
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> God is so high that he even has to “bend down” to see the heavens. And yet he sees the smallest people on earth.
> Verses 7–8: God brings poor people out of their misery and seats them with princes. Hannah sang almost the same words (1 Samuel 2:8), and so did Mary in her song of praise (Luke 1:52).
> Verse 9: Back then, a woman without children was often looked down on. God gives her joy, as he did for Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Hannah.
> If you long for children and do not have any: This is not a sign that God loves you less. God’s love does not depend on having children.
