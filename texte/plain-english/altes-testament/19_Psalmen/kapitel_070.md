# Psalm 70
### Hurry to help me

---

## Come quickly, God (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. By David. As a reminder.
Hurry, God, to rescue me!
Come quickly to help me, LORD!
[2] Let those who are after my life
be disappointed and confused.
Let those who want to see me ruined
be turned back in disgrace.
[3] Let those who say, “Aha! Aha!”
turn back because of their shame.
[4] Let all those who seek you
be full of joy and glad in you.
Let those who love your salvation always say,
“Let God be praised as great!”
[5] But I am poor and needy.
Come to me quickly, God!
You are my help and my rescuer.
LORD, don’t wait!

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 70 is almost exactly the same text as Psalm 40:13–17. It was handed down as a separate, short prayer.
> “As a reminder”: Perhaps the psalm was sung at a certain sacrifice. Or it is meant to “remind” God of the one praying.
> It is a short, urgent cry for help: “Come quickly!” It begins and ends with the request that God would hurry.
> For many centuries, monks have prayed the first verse at the beginning of their times of prayer: “O God, come to my help! Lord, hurry to help me!”
