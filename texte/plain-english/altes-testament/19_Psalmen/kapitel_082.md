# Psalm 82
### God judges the judges

---

## God leads the assembly (Verses 1–4)

[1] A psalm by Asaph.
God leads the great assembly.
He judges among the gods.
[2] “How long will you judge unfairly
and take the side of the wicked?”
Selah.
[3] “Defend the weak, the poor, and the orphans!
Protect the rights of the poor and the oppressed!
[4] Rescue the weak and needy!
Set them free from the hand of the wicked!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 82 shows a court scene in heaven. God stands in the middle of an assembly of “gods.”
> Who are these “gods”? There are different explanations:
> – heavenly powers or angels who have been put in charge of the nations,
> – the gods of the other nations,
> – human judges and rulers who were supposed to judge in God’s name (compare Exodus 21:6, where “God” can also mean “the judges”).
> What God demands of them is clear: Protect the weak, the poor, the orphans! That is the standard for everyone who has power.

---

## You will die like men (Verses 5–8)

[5] They know nothing, and they understand nothing.
They walk around in darkness.
All the foundations of the earth are shaken.
[6] I said, “You are gods.
All of you are sons of the Most High.
[7] But you will die like men,
and you will fall like any one of the rulers.”
[8] Rise up, God, judge the earth,
for all the nations belong to you as your inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: Because the powerful are unjust, the whole world shakes. Injustice destroys the foundations of life together.
> Verses 6–7: They were highly honored, “sons of the Most High.” But because they did not do their job, they lose their position. They will die like ordinary people.
> Jesus quotes verse 6 in John 10:34–36, when people accuse him of making himself God.
> Verse 8: The one praying calls on God himself as judge, because the human judges have failed. In the end, God will make sure that justice is done.
