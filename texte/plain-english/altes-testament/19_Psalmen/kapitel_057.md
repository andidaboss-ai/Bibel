# Psalm 57
### In the shadow of your wings

---

## Until the disaster has passed (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the Chief Musician. To the tune of “Do Not Destroy.”
A poem by David, when he fled from Saul into the cave.
Be merciful to me, God, be merciful to me,
for my soul comes to you for safety.
Yes, I will find safety in the shadow of your wings
until the disaster has passed.
[2] I cry out to God Most High,
to God who does for me what I ask.
[3] He will send help from heaven and save me.
He scolds the one who is chasing me.
Selah.
God will send out his loving kindness and his truth.

> **What does this mean?**
> The superscription reminds us of David’s flight from Saul. David hid in a cave, and Saul came into that very cave. David could have killed him, but he spared his life (1 Samuel 24; compare 1 Samuel 22:1).
> “Do Not Destroy”: the name of a tune. It fits David, who did not want to destroy Saul.
> “In the shadow of your wings”: In the dark cave, David feels like a chick under its mother’s wings. He waits “until the disaster has passed.”

---

## Among lions (Verses 4–6)

[4] My soul is among lions.
I lie among people who breathe out fire,
people whose teeth are spears and arrows,
and whose tongue is a sharp sword.
[5] God, be honored high above the heavens!
Let your glory be over all the earth!
[6] They have set a net for my steps.
My soul is bowed down.
They dig a pit in front of me.
But they fall into the middle of it themselves.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The enemies are like wild animals. Their words are like weapons.
> Verse 5 is a refrain. In the middle of danger, David praises God. He looks away from the enemies and toward God’s greatness.
> Verse 6: Again the picture of the pit: The enemies fall into their own trap.

---

## I will wake up the dawn (Verses 7–11)

[7] My heart is steady, God. My heart is steady.
I will sing. Yes, I will sing praises.
[8] Wake up, my soul!
Wake up, lute and harp!
I will wake up the dawn.
[9] I will thank you, Lord, among the peoples.
I will sing praises to you among the nations.
[10] For your great loving kindness reaches to the heavens,
and your truth reaches to the skies.
[11] God, be honored high above the heavens!
Let your glory be over all the earth!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: “My heart is steady”: The fear has given way to firm trust.
> Verse 8: A beautiful picture: David wants to sing so early that he wakes up the dawn. He does not wait until it gets light. His praise comes before the light.
> Verses 7–11 appear again almost exactly in Psalm 108:1–5.
