# Psalm 37
### Commit your way to the LORD

---

## Do not be upset about evil people (Verses 1–2)

[1] By David.
Do not be upset because of evildoers.
Do not be jealous of those who do wrong.
[2] For they will soon be cut down like the grass.
They will dry up like green plants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 37 is a psalm of wisdom and an alphabet poem. It sounds like the advice of an old, experienced person to a young one (compare verse 25).
> The big question: Why do evil people often do so well? The answer: Do not be upset! Their success is like grass that soon dries up.

---

## Trust in the LORD (Verses 3–8)

[3] Trust in the LORD, and do good.
Live in the land, and enjoy safe pasture.
[4] Also find your joy in the LORD,
and he will give you what your heart wants.
[5] Commit your way to the LORD.
That means: Put your way into the LORD’s hands.
Trust in him too, and he will do this:
[6] He will make your righteousness shine out like light,
and your justice like the sun at noon.
[7] Be still before the LORD, and wait patiently for him.
Do not be upset because of the one who succeeds on his way,
because of the man who carries out evil plans.
[8] Stop being angry, and let go of your rage.
Do not be upset. It only leads to doing evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Also find your joy in the LORD”: When God is my greatest joy, then my wishes also become the kind that God is glad to fulfill.
> Verse 5 is very well known: “Commit your way to the LORD.” The Hebrew has a strong picture: You roll your way onto the LORD, the way you roll a heavy load onto someone else. Paul Gerhardt’s famous German hymn “Befiehl du deine Wege” is based on this verse.
> Verses 7–8: Anger and jealousy eat you up inside and can lead you to do evil yourself. It is better to become still and trust God.

---

## The humble will inherit the land (Verses 9–15)

[9] For evildoers will be cut off.
But those who wait for the LORD will inherit the land.
[10] In just a little while, the wicked will be gone.
Yes, even if you look for his place, he is not there.
[11] But the humble will inherit the land.
They will enjoy great peace.
[12] The wicked makes plans against the righteous
and grinds his teeth at him.
[13] The Lord laughs at him,
for he sees that his day is coming.
[14] The wicked have pulled out the sword
and have bent their bow.
They want to bring down the poor and needy.
They want to kill those who live honestly.
[15] Their sword will go into their own heart.
Their bows will be broken.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: “The humble will inherit the land”: Jesus takes this up in the Sermon on the Mount: “Blessed are the gentle, for they will inherit the earth” (Matthew 5:5).
> The violence of the wicked turns against them in the end (verse 15).

---

## Better a little with God (Verses 16–22)

[16] The little that a righteous person has
is better than the riches of many wicked people.
[17] For the arms of the wicked will be broken,
but the LORD holds up the righteous.
[18] The LORD knows the days of those who are blameless.
What they receive from him will last forever.
[19] They will not be put to shame in evil times.
In times of hunger they will have enough to eat.
[20] But the wicked will die.
The enemies of the LORD will be like the beauty of the fields.
They will disappear, disappear like smoke.
[21] The wicked borrow and do not pay back,
but the righteous give generously.
[22] For those whom he blesses will inherit the land.
Those whom he curses will be cut off.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Owning little with a clear conscience is better than great riches gained by doing wrong (compare Proverbs 15:16).
> Verse 20: The splendor of the wicked is like flowers in a meadow: beautiful, but soon faded. Like smoke that blows away.
> Verse 21: One difference between the wicked and the righteous: The one takes and does not give back. The other gives generously.

---

## Though he stumbles, he will not fall (Verses 23–29)

[23] The LORD makes a man’s steps firm.
He is pleased with his way.
[24] Even if he stumbles, he will not fall down,
for the LORD holds him up with his hand.
[25] I have been young, and now I am old.
Yet I have not seen the righteous left alone,
or his children begging for bread.
[26] All day long he is kind and lends to others.
His children are blessed.
[27] Turn away from evil, and do good.
Then you will live safely forever.
[28] For the LORD loves justice.
He does not leave those who are faithful to him.
They are kept safe forever,
but the children of the wicked will be cut off.
[29] The righteous will inherit the land
and live in it forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Even a righteous person sometimes stumbles, makes mistakes and has setbacks. But God holds him by the hand so that he does not fall all the way down.
> Verse 25: The old speaker tells about his experience of life. He means it honestly, but it is not a promise for every single case. There are also righteous people who are poor and suffer (like Job). The verse is meant to encourage. It is not meant to blame poor people.
> Verse 28: “The children of the wicked”: This means those who go on along the evil way of their parents. The Bible also says: Each person is responsible for their own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).

---

## God’s law in the heart (Verses 30–34)

[30] The mouth of the righteous speaks wisdom.
His tongue says what is right.
[31] The law of his God is in his heart.
None of his steps will slip.
[32] The wicked lie in wait for the righteous
and try to kill him.
[33] The LORD will not leave him in his power.
He will not let him be condemned when he is judged.
[34] Wait for the LORD, and stay on his way.
He will lift you up so that you inherit the land.
When the wicked are cut off, you will see it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: God’s teaching is not only a law on the outside. It is in the heart. Whoever loves God’s will walks safely.
> Verse 33: Even if a righteous person is condemned in a human court, God stays on his side.

---

## A man of peace has a future (Verses 35–40)

[35] I have seen the wicked in great power,
spreading out like a green tree in the soil where it grew up.
[36] But he passed away, and he was gone.
Yes, I looked for him, but he could not be found.
[37] Pay attention to the blameless man, and look at the honest one,
for there is a future for the man of peace.
[38] But those who break God’s law will all be destroyed together.
The future of the wicked will be cut off.
[39] But the salvation of the righteous comes from the LORD.
He is their stronghold in times of trouble.
[40] The LORD helps them and rescues them.
He rescues them from the wicked and saves them,
because they have come to him for safety.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 35–36: The old speaker has seen powerful tyrants come and go. They seemed like big trees, but suddenly they were gone.
> Verse 37: “There is a future for the man of peace”: Whoever makes peace has a future. This is a great encouragement.
> The psalm ends with trust: God is a stronghold in times of trouble.
