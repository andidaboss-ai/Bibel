# Psalm 35
### LORD, fight for me

---

## Fight against those who fight against me (Verses 1–8)

[1] By David.
LORD, argue against those who argue against me.
Fight against those who fight against me.
[2] Take hold of your small shield and your large shield.
Stand up and help me.
[3] Raise the spear
and block the way of those who chase me.
Say to my soul, “I am your salvation.”
Salvation means: rescue.
[4] Let those who want to take my life
be put to shame and disgraced.
Let those who plan to ruin me
be turned back and confused.
[5] Let them be like chaff in the wind,
with the LORD’s angel driving them away.
Chaff is the dry husk around the grain.
[6] Let their way be dark and slippery,
with the LORD’s angel chasing them.
[7] For they had no reason
to hide their net in a pit for me.
They had no reason
to dig a pit for my soul.
[8] Let disaster come on him by surprise.
Let the net that he has hidden catch him himself.
Let him fall into that disaster.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one who prays is being hunted for no reason. He asks God to stand up for him like a warrior, or like a lawyer in court. The Hebrew word for “argue” also means “to bring a case to court.”
> Verse 3: What he wants most is a word from God: “I am your salvation.”
> Verses 4–8 are prayers against the enemies. The one who prays does not want to strike back himself. He asks God to let the wicked fall into their own trap.

---

## Who is like you? (Verses 9–10)

[9] My soul will be full of joy in the LORD.
It will be glad because he rescues me.
[10] All my bones will say,
“LORD, who is like you?
You rescue the poor from the one who is too strong for him.
Yes, you rescue the poor and needy from the one who robs him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A short moment of trust. “All my bones”: with my whole body, with everything I am.
> God stands on the side of the weak against the strong who rob them.

---

## They pay back good with evil (Verses 11–18)

[11] Witnesses who are not honest stand up.
They ask me about things I know nothing about.
[12] They pay me back evil for good.
My soul is left all alone.
[13] But as for me, when they were sick,
I wore sackcloth.
Sackcloth was rough clothing that people wore when they were sad.
I made my soul suffer by fasting.
Fasting means: not eating, to pray more seriously.
My prayer came back into my own lap.
[14] I acted as if it were my friend or my brother.
I bowed down in sorrow,
like someone who mourns for his mother.
[15] But when I was in trouble,
they were glad and gathered together.
The attackers gathered together against me,
and I did not know it.
They tore at me and did not stop.
[16] Like godless people who make fun of others at feasts,
they ground their teeth at me.
[17] Lord, how long will you just watch?
Rescue my soul from their attacks.
Rescue my precious life from the lions.
[18] I will thank you in the great gathering.
I will praise you among many people.

> **What does this mean?**
> This hurts most of all: The people for whom the one who prays had prayed and fasted when they were sick now turn against him. They are glad about his trouble.
> Verse 13: “My prayer came back into my own lap”: This is hard to understand. Maybe: I prayed with my head bowed down to my chest. Or: My prayer did not help them; it came back to me. Or: I now pray the same prayer for myself.
> Verse 17: “How long will you just watch?”: An honest, desperate question to God.

---

## They hate me without a cause (Verses 19–26)

[19] Do not let those who are my enemies for no good reason
be glad about me.
Do not let those who hate me without a cause
wink their eyes at me.
[20] For they do not speak of peace.
Instead they make up lies
against those who live quietly in the land.
[21] Yes, they open their mouths wide against me.
They say, “Aha! Aha! We have seen it with our own eyes!”
[22] You have seen it, LORD. Do not stay silent.
Lord, do not be far from me.
[23] Wake up! Rise up to defend me, my God!
My Lord, argue my case!
[24] LORD my God, take my side,
because you are righteous.
Do not let them be glad about my trouble.
[25] Do not let them say in their heart,
“Aha! That is just how we wanted it!”
Do not let them say, “We have swallowed him up!”
[26] Let all those who are glad about my disaster
be put to shame and confused together.
Let those who make themselves big against me
be dressed in shame and disgrace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: “Those who hate me without a cause”: Jesus uses these words about himself (John 15:25).
> “Those who live quietly in the land”: peaceful, ordinary people who do not harm anyone. They are the very ones who become the target of lies.
> Verse 21: “Aha! Aha! We have seen it with our own eyes!”: The enemies laugh with glee, as if they had caught the one who prays doing something wrong.
> Verse 22: The answer: “You have seen it, LORD.” The enemies see something, but God sees everything.

---

## May the LORD be great (Verses 27–28)

[27] Let those who support my just cause
shout for joy and be glad.
Yes, let them always say,
“May the LORD be praised as great!
He is pleased when his servant is doing well.”
[28] My tongue will talk about your righteousness
and about your praise all day long.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, it is no longer about the enemies, but about the friends and about God.
> “Doing well”: The Hebrew word here is “shalom,” peace. God is glad when his children are doing well.
> Psalm 35 is a prayer for people who are bullied, lied about or treated unfairly. It allows us to tell God all of our pain and to hand the matter over to him, instead of taking revenge ourselves.
