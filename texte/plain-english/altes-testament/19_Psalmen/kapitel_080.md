# Psalm 80
### Make your face shine

---

## Shepherd of Israel, hear us! (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. To the tune of “The Lilies of the Covenant.” A psalm by Asaph.
Hear us, Shepherd of Israel,
you who lead Joseph like a flock!
You who sit on your throne above the cherubim, shine out!
[2] Before Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh,
wake up your power! Come and save us!
[3] Bring us back, God!
Make your face shine on us, and we will be saved.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 80 is a prayer for the northern kingdom of Israel (Joseph, Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin), perhaps after the Assyrians conquered it in 722 BC.
> “You who sit on your throne above the cherubim”: On the ark of the covenant there were two cherubim (figures of angels). People pictured God as a king sitting on his throne above them.
> Verse 3 is a refrain. It appears three times (verses 3, 7, and 19), and it gets stronger each time: God, God of Armies, LORD God of Armies.
> “Make your face shine”: a request from the priestly blessing (Numbers 6:25). When God looks at us kindly, we are saved.

---

## The bread of tears (Verses 4–7)

[4] LORD God of Armies,
how long will you be angry at the prayers of your people?
[5] You have fed them with the bread of tears.
You have given them tears to drink in large amounts.
[6] You make us something our neighbors fight over.
Our enemies laugh at us among themselves.
[7] Bring us back, God of Armies!
Make your face shine on us, and we will be saved.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: A strong picture: Tears have become the daily bread of the people.
> The people pray, but they feel as if God is even angry about their prayers.

---

## The vine from Egypt (Verses 8–13)

[8] You brought a vine out of Egypt.
You drove out the nations and planted it.
[9] You cleared the ground for it.
It put down deep roots and filled the land.
[10] The mountains were covered with its shade.
Its branches were like God’s cedars.
[11] It sent out its branches as far as the sea,
and its shoots as far as the River.
[12] Why have you broken down its walls,
so that all who pass by pick its fruit?
[13] The wild boar from the forest tears it up.
The wild animals of the field feed on it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel is compared to a vine. God brought it out of Egypt, planted it, and took care of it. It grew and became large, from the Mediterranean Sea to the Euphrates.
> But now the protecting wall has been torn down. Anyone can come and steal the grapes. Wild animals destroy the vineyard.
> This picture appears often in the Bible (Isaiah 5:1–7; Hosea 10:1). Later, Jesus says: “I am the true vine” (John 15:1).

---

## Look after this vine (Verses 14–19)

[14] Please come back, God of Armies!
Look down from heaven and see!
Take care of this vine,
[15] the plant that your right hand planted,
the branch that you made strong for yourself.
[16] It is burned with fire. It is cut down.
They die when you scold them.
[17] Let your hand be on the man of your right hand,
on the son of man whom you made strong for yourself.
[18] Then we will not turn away from you.
Give us new life, and we will call on your name.
[19] Bring us back, LORD God of Armies!
Make your face shine on us, and we will be saved.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying asks God: Look after your vine! You planted it yourself.
> Verse 17: “The man of your right hand” and “the son of man”: This probably means the king, or perhaps the whole people. By the way, the name “Benjamin” (verse 2) means: son of the right hand. Christians have also thought of Jesus here, who calls himself the “Son of Man” and sits at the right hand of God.
> Verse 18: The people promise: If you give us new life, we will stay faithful to you.
> The psalm ends with the refrain in its strongest form: “LORD God of Armies.”
