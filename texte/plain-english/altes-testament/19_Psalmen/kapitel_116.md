# Psalm 116
### I love the LORD, because he hears me

---

## He has turned his ear to me (Verses 1–4)

[1] I love the LORD, because he listens to my voice
and to my cries for mercy.
[2] Because he has turned his ear to me,
I will call on him as long as I live.
[3] The ropes of death were all around me.
The pains of Sheol took hold of me.
Sheol is the world of the dead.
I found trouble and sorrow.
[4] Then I called on the name of the LORD:
“LORD, please, rescue my soul!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 116 is a personal song of thanks. The person praying was deathly sick or in danger of losing his life. God saved him.
> It begins with a confession of love: “I love the LORD.” The reason: God listened.
> The psalm belongs to the “Hallel”, which is sung at Passover.

---

## Return to your rest, my soul (Verses 5–9)

[5] The LORD is gracious and righteous.
Yes, our God is merciful.
[6] The LORD protects simple people.
I was brought very low, and he saved me.
[7] Return to your rest, my soul,
for the LORD has been very good to you.
[8] For you have rescued my soul from death,
my eyes from tears,
and my feet from falling.
[9] I will walk before the LORD
in the land of the living.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Simple people”: These are plain, defenseless people who cannot help themselves. God especially takes care of them.
> Verse 7: The person praying speaks to his own soul: Come back to rest! The fear is over. God has helped.
> Verse 8: Three kinds of rescue: from death, from tears, from falling.

---

## What can I give back to the LORD? (Verses 10–14)

[10] I believed, and so I said:
“I was in deep trouble.”
[11] In my panic, I said:
“All people are liars.”
[12] What can I give to the LORD
for all the good he has done for me?
[13] I will lift up the cup of salvation,
and call on the name of the LORD.
[14] I will keep my promises to the LORD,
yes, in front of all his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: Even in trouble, the person praying did not stop believing. But he was disappointed in people: No one helped him.
> Paul quotes verse 10 from the Greek translation: “I believed, and therefore I spoke” (2 Corinthians 4:13).
> Verse 12: A beautiful question: How can I thank God for everything he has given me?
> Verse 13: The answer: I lift up the “cup of salvation”, a cup of wine at the thanksgiving celebration, and I call on God’s name. Cups of wine are still drunk at the Passover meal today. Christians also think of the cup at the Lord’s Supper.

---

## Precious is the death of his faithful people (Verses 15–19)

[15] Precious in the LORD’s eyes
is the death of his faithful people.
[16] LORD, truly, I am your servant.
I am your servant, the son of your servant girl.
You have set me free from my chains.
[17] I will offer you a sacrifice of thanks,
and call on the name of the LORD.
[18] I will keep my promises to the LORD,
yes, in front of all his people,
[19] in the courtyards of the LORD’s house,
in the middle of you, Jerusalem.
Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: “Precious in the LORD’s eyes is the death of his faithful people”: This means: The life and death of people who trust God matter to him. They are very valuable to him. He does not let them die carelessly. This is a comfort at the bedside of dying people.
> Verse 16: “The son of your servant girl”: I belong to you completely, like a child who was born in your house.
> The person praying brings his sacrifice of thanks in public, in the temple. He wants everyone to hear about God’s help.
