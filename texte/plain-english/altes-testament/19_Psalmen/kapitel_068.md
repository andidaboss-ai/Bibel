# Psalm 68
### God enters his sanctuary

---

## God rises up (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David. A song.
Let God rise up! Let his enemies be scattered!
Let those who hate him run away before him.
[2] As smoke is blown away, so blow them away.
As wax melts before the fire,
so let the wicked die before God.
[3] But let the righteous be glad.
Let them rejoice before God.
Yes, let them be full of joy and gladness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 68 is one of the most difficult psalms. Many verses are hard to understand in Hebrew. The translations often differ from one another.
> It describes a great festive procession: God marches from Sinai through the desert to his sanctuary in Jerusalem.
> Verse 1 takes up the call with which the ark of the covenant set out: “Rise up, LORD! Let your enemies be scattered” (Numbers 10:35).

---

## Father of orphans (Verses 4–6)

[4] Sing to God! Sing praises to his name!
Praise highly the one who rides on the clouds.
His name is Yah! Rejoice before him!
[5] A father of orphans and a defender of widows:
that is God in his holy home.
[6] God gives lonely people a family to live in.
He leads the prisoners out with singing.
But the rebellious live in a land burned by the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The one who rides on the clouds”: The neighboring nations called their god Baal this. The psalm says: Not Baal, but our God rides on the clouds.
> “Yah” is a short form of God’s name YHWH. People know it from the word “Hallelujah” (which means “Praise Yah”).
> Verses 5–6 are a wonderful picture: The great God cares especially for the weakest: orphans, widows, lonely people, prisoners. He is their father and their defender.
> “God gives lonely people a family”: Literally, it says “he lets them live in a house.” God gives them a home.
> Verse 6: “With singing”: Some Bibles translate this as “into happiness” or “into freedom.”

## When you marched through the wilderness (Verses 7–10)

[7] God, when you went out ahead of your people,
when you marched through the wilderness …
Selah.
[8] … the earth shook.
The sky also poured down rain before the God of Sinai,
before God, the God of Israel.
[9] You, God, sent plenty of rain.
You made your inheritance strong again when it was worn out.
[10] Your community lived there.
In your goodness, God, you provided for the poor.

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm remembers Israel’s journey through the desert after they left Egypt. It also remembers how God appeared at Mount Sinai, when the earth shook (Exodus 19:18).
> Verses 9–10: In the promised land, God gave rain and took care of the poor.

---

## Kings run away (Verses 11–14)

[11] The Lord gave the word.
Those who announced it were a great crowd.
[12] “Kings with their armies run away! They run away!”
And the woman who stays at home divides the plunder,
[13] while you sleep among the campfires:
the wings of a dove covered with silver,
her feathers with shining gold.
[14] When the Almighty scattered kings there,
it snowed on Zalmon.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are especially hard to understand.
> Verse 11: “Those who announced it”: In Hebrew, the word is in a female form here. It probably means women who spread the news of victory, like Miriam (Exodus 15:20) or Deborah (Judges 5).
> Verses 12–13: The enemies run away. The women at home divide the plunder. Verse 13 speaks of a dove: “the wings of a dove covered with silver.” It may be a precious piece of plunder, or a picture for Israel.
> Verse 13: “Among the campfires”: The Hebrew word is uncertain. Others translate “among the sheepfolds” or “in the stalls” (compare Judges 5:16).
> Verse 14: Zalmon is a mountain. Maybe the weapons of the fallen lay on the mountain, white like snow. Or it is about real snowy weather that confused the enemies.

## The mountain that God has chosen (Verses 15–18)

[15] The mountains of Bashan are mighty mountains.
The mountains of Bashan have rugged peaks.
[16] Why do you look with envy, you rugged mountains,
at the mountain where God chooses to rule?
Yes, the LORD will live there forever.
[17] The chariots of God are tens of thousands and thousands of thousands.
The Lord is among them, from Sinai into the sanctuary.
[18] You have gone up on high.
You have led away captives.
You have received gifts among people,
yes, even among the rebellious,
so that Yah God may live there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bashan is a high, impressive mountain range in the northeast (with Mount Hermon). Mount Zion in Jerusalem is much smaller. But God has chosen little Zion. The big mountains “look with envy.” God often chooses what is small (compare 1 Corinthians 1:27).
> Verse 17: God comes from Sinai into his sanctuary with a huge heavenly army.
> Verse 18: Like a king who has won a victory, God goes up to his throne, with captives and gifts.
> Paul quotes verse 18 in Ephesians 4:8 and applies it to Jesus going up into heaven. But there he says “gave gifts to people” instead of “received gifts.” Old Jewish translations also understood it this way.

## He carries our burdens (Verses 19–23)

[19] Praise be to the Lord, who carries our burdens every day,
the God who saves us.
Selah.
[20] For us, God is a God who rescues.
Escape from death belongs to the LORD, the Lord.
[21] But God will crush the heads of his enemies,
the hairy scalp of anyone who keeps on living in his guilt.
[22] The Lord said, “I will bring you back from Bashan.
I will bring you back from the depths of the sea,
[23] so that you may crush them and dip your foot in blood,
and so that the tongues of your dogs may have their share of your enemies.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19 is one of the most beautiful verses of the psalm: “Praise be to the Lord, who carries our burdens every day.” God helps us every day.
> Verse 20: God can even rescue from death.
> Verses 21–23 are very harsh. They are about enemies who will not let go of their guilt. God says: They cannot hide anywhere, not in the mountains and not in the sea (compare Amos 9:2–3).
> The pictures of blood and dogs are the war language of that time. They are not permission for violence. They mean that in the end, evil will be completely defeated. The judgment belongs to God.

## The festive procession (Verses 24–27)

[24] They have seen your processions, God,
the processions of my God, my King, into the sanctuary.
[25] The singers went in front, the musicians followed,
among the women playing tambourines:
[26] “Bless God in the congregations!
Bless the Lord in the assembly of Israel!”
[27] There is little Benjamin, their ruler,
the princes of Judah, their council,
the princes of Zebulun, and the princes of Naphtali.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now we can picture the procession: the singers in front, the musicians behind, and in between women with tambourines (hand drums).
> The tribes of Israel march along: the smallest tribe, Benjamin, at the front, then Judah from the south, Zebulun and Naphtali from the north. All Israel is united.

---

## Kings bring gifts (Verses 28–31)

[28] Your God has commanded your strength.
God, make strong what you have done for us!
[29] Because of your temple at Jerusalem,
kings will bring gifts to you.
[30] Speak sharply to the wild animal of the reeds,
to the herd of bulls with the calves of the peoples.
Trample the bars of silver under your feet.
Scatter the nations who love war!
[31] Princes will come out of Egypt.
Ethiopia will hurry to stretch out her hands to God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 30: “The wild animal of the reeds”: probably a picture for Egypt (a crocodile or a hippopotamus in the reeds of the Nile). The “bulls” are the powerful rulers.
> “Scatter the nations who love war!”: God is against those who stir up war.
> Verse 31: “Ethiopia”: In Hebrew, the name here is Cush. Cush is the land south of Egypt (today roughly Sudan). Even the most distant nations will come to God.

---

## Sing to God, you kingdoms of the earth! (Verses 32–35)

[32] Sing to God, you kingdoms of the earth!
Sing praises to the Lord!
Selah.
[33] Sing to him who rides on the highest heavens, which have been there from long ago.
Listen! He speaks with his voice, a mighty voice.
[34] Say that strength belongs to God!
His majesty is over Israel,
and his strength is in the skies.
[35] You are awesome, God, in your sanctuaries.
The God of Israel gives strength and power to his people.
Praise be to God!

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, all the kingdoms of the earth are invited to praise God.
> God is mighty in heaven, but he passes his strength on to his people.
> The psalm ends with simple praise: “Praise be to God!”
