# Psalm 28
### The LORD is my strength and my shield

---

## Do not be silent, my rock (Verses 1–5)

[1] By David.
To you, LORD, I call.
My rock, do not close your ears to me.
For if you stay silent toward me,
I will become like those who go down into the pit.
[2] Hear my voice when I beg you for help,
when I cry out to you,
when I lift up my hands toward your Most Holy Place.
[3] Do not drag me away with the wicked,
with those who do evil.
They speak of peace with their neighbors,
but there is evil in their hearts.
[4] Pay them back for what they have done,
for the evil of their deeds.
Pay them back for the work of their hands.
Bring back on them what they deserve.
[5] They do not pay attention to the works of the LORD,
or to what his hands have done.
That is why he will tear them down and not build them up.

> **What does this mean?**
> The worst thing for the person praying is when God is silent. Then he feels as if he were already dead (“in the pit”).
> “Lift up my hands toward your Most Holy Place”: This is how people prayed back then, with their hands raised toward the temple.
> Verse 3: Hypocrites speak in a friendly way, but they plan evil.
> Verse 4 asks for justice: God should give them what their own deeds deserve. The person praying does not take revenge himself.

---

## Praise the LORD (Verses 6–9)

[6] Praise be to the LORD,
because he has heard my voice when I begged for help.
[7] The LORD is my strength and my shield.
My heart trusted in him, and I was helped.
That is why my heart is full of joy.
With my song I will thank him.
[8] The LORD is their strength.
He is a fortress that saves his anointed one.
[9] Save your people, and bless your inheritance.
Be their shepherd, too, and carry them forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Again the sudden turning point: God has heard! The person praying rejoices.
> Verse 9 is a beautiful prayer for the whole people: God should be like a shepherd who carries his sheep (compare Psalm 23; Isaiah 40:11).
> Christians still pray verse 9 in worship today, for example in the old hymn of praise called the “Te Deum.” There they ask the Lord to save his people and bless his inheritance.
