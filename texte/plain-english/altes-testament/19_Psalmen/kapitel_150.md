# Psalm 150
### Let everything that breathes praise Yah!

---

## The great Hallelujah (Verses 1–6)

[1] Praise Yah!
Praise God in his holy place!
Praise him in his heavens for his powerful deeds!
[2] Praise him for his mighty deeds!
Praise him because his greatness is so wonderful!
[3] Praise him with the sound of the trumpet!
Praise him with harp and lyre!
[4] Praise him with tambourine and dancing!
Praise him with string instruments and flute!
[5] Praise him with loud cymbals!
Praise him with ringing cymbals!
[6] Let everything that has breath praise Yah!
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> The last psalm is one single shout of joy. In six verses, the word “praise” appears 13 times.
> Verses 1–2: Where should we praise God? Everywhere, in the temple and in heaven. Why? Because he has done great things and because he is great.
> Verses 3–5: With what? With all the instruments: trumpets, harps, drums, flutes, cymbals. And with dancing. The whole orchestra plays.
> Verse 6: Who should praise? Everything that breathes. Not only Israel, not only people, but all life. Every breath is a gift from God and can be praise for him.
> This is how the book of Psalms ends: not with a lament, but with praise. The way goes through many tears, but at the end there is the Hallelujah.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Psalms?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The prayer book of the Bible:** The Psalms are 150 songs and prayers. They were written over many centuries, from David until after the exile. They were the songbook of the temple in Jerusalem. To this day, Jews and Christians pray with these words, every day, all over the world.
> **Five books:** The book of Psalms is divided into five books (Psalms 1–41, 42–72, 73–89, 90–106, 107–150), like the five books of Moses. Each book ends with words of praise. At the end of the whole book, Psalm 150 stands as the great final Hallelujah.
> **There is room for all feelings:** In the Psalms there is joy and sorrow, thanks and lament, trust and despair, love and even anger and hatred. The Psalms show: We may tell God everything. We do not have to pretend to be pious before him.
> **Lament is prayer:** Almost a third of the psalms are laments. “My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?” (Psalm 22) Whoever laments still believes that God is listening. Many psalms of lament end with new trust.
> **Hard verses:** Some psalms wish evil on enemies (for example, Psalm 109 or 137). We have not left them out, because they are part of the Bible. They show honestly how hurt people can be. But they do not allow violence. Those who pray leave the judgment to God. Jesus shows the way of loving our enemies.
> **God’s word:** Psalm 1 and Psalm 119 praise God’s teaching. It is like a light on the path (Psalm 119:105).
> **The king:** Many psalms speak about the king from David’s family, the anointed one (in Hebrew “Messiah”). Jews are waiting for the Messiah. Christians believe that Jesus is this anointed one. Jesus himself prayed the Psalms, even on the cross (Psalm 22:1; 31:5).
> **Well-known words:** “The LORD is my shepherd” (Psalm 23). “Commit your way to the LORD” (Psalm 37). “Out of the depths I have cried out to you” (Psalm 130). “I look up to the hills” (Psalm 121). “Praise the LORD, my soul” (Psalm 103). These words have comforted people in every situation of life.
> **From sighing to praise:** The way of the book of Psalms goes from “Blessed is the person” (Psalm 1) through much suffering all the way to “Let everything that has breath praise Yah!” (Psalm 150).
> **What comes next?** Next comes the book of Proverbs. It is full of short, wise sayings for everyday life: about work, friendship, money, words and family. Its guiding sentence: “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” (Proverbs 1:7).
