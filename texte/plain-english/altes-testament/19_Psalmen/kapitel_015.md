# Psalm 15
### Who may live with God?

---

## The question (Verse 1)

[1] A psalm by David.
LORD, who may live in your sanctuary?
Who may live on your holy hill?

> **What does this mean?**
> People who came to the temple on Mount Zion may have asked this at the entrance: Who may go in here? Who may be close to God?
> The answer in the following verses is surprising: It is not about sacrifices or rituals. It is about how we treat other people.

---

## The answer (Verses 2–5)

[2] The one who lives without blame and does what is right,
and who speaks the truth in his heart.
[3] The one who does not tell lies about others with his tongue,
who does not do evil to his friend,
and who does not insult his neighbor.
[4] The one who looks down on a wicked person,
but who honors those who fear the LORD.
The one who keeps his promise even when it hurts him,
and does not change it.
[5] The one who does not lend his money to make a profit from others,
and does not take a bribe against an innocent person.
Whoever does these things will never be shaken.

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm names about 10 qualities, like the Ten Commandments:
> – to live honestly and do what is right,
> – to tell the truth, also in your heart,
> – not to speak badly about others,
> – not to do evil to friends,
> – not to admire evil, but to honor those who honor God,
> – to keep promises, even when it costs you something,
> – not to charge high interest (people in Israel were supposed to help each other in need without charging interest, compare Exodus 22:25),
> – not to take bribes.
> “Speaks the truth in his heart”: Honesty begins on the inside, not only with words.
> The ending is a promise: Whoever lives like this stands firm.
