# Psalm 108
### My heart is steady

---

## I will wake up the dawn (Verses 1–6)

[1] A song. A psalm by David.
My heart is steady, God.
I will sing, and I will make music with all my soul.
[2] Wake up, harp and lyre!
I will wake up the dawn.
[3] I will give thanks to you, LORD, among the nations.
I will sing praises to you among the peoples.
[4] For your loving kindness is great, higher than the heavens.
Your faithfulness reaches to the skies.
[5] Be lifted up, God, above the heavens!
Let your glory be over all the earth.
[6] Save us with your right hand and answer us,
so that the people you love may be rescued.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 108 is put together from two other psalms: Verses 1–5 come from Psalm 57:7–11, and verses 6–13 come from Psalm 60:5–12.
> In this way, older prayers were put together in a new way for a new situation. The praise from Psalm 57 and the request from Psalm 60 become one new prayer.
> “I will wake up the dawn”: The praise comes so early that it wakes up the morning.

---

## God has spoken (Verses 7–9)

[7] God has spoken from his holy place:
“I will celebrate my victory and divide up Shechem.
I will measure out the valley of Succoth.
[8] Gilead is mine. Manasseh is mine.
Ephraim is my helmet.
Judah is my scepter.
[9] Moab is my washbasin.
I will throw my sandal on Edom.
I will shout in triumph over Philistia.”

> **What does this mean?**
> See Psalm 60:6–8. God declares: The whole land belongs to me, and the neighboring nations are also in my hand.
> Verse 8: A scepter is a royal staff. It is a sign of a king’s power.
> The pictures (washbasin, sandal) are the language of that time for ruling over something and owning it.

---

## With God we will do brave deeds (Verses 10–13)

[10] Who will bring me into the strong city?
Who will lead me to Edom?
[11] God, haven’t you rejected us?
God, you do not go out with our armies.
[12] Give us help against the enemy,
for human help is worth nothing.
[13] With God we will do brave deeds,
for he is the one who will trample our enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> See Psalm 60:9–12.
> “Human help is worth nothing”: Without God, even the best human means do not help.
