# Psalm 20
### A blessing for the king

---

## May the LORD answer you (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
May the LORD answer you on the day of trouble.
May the name of the God of Jacob keep you safe up high.
[2] May he send you help from the sanctuary.
May he give you support from Zion.
[3] May he remember all your offerings
and accept your burnt offering.
Selah.
[4] May he give you what your heart desires.
May he make all your plans come true.
[5] We will shout for joy when you win the victory.
In the name of our God we will raise our flags.
May the LORD give you everything you ask for.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 20 is a prayer of blessing for the king before he goes into a hard battle. The people pray for him in the temple.
> “The name of God”: God’s name stands for who God is and for his presence. Whoever calls on God’s name calls on God himself.
> Today, too, many people pray this psalm for others, for example before an operation, an exam or a hard road ahead.

---

## Some trust in chariots (Verses 6–9)

[6] Now I know that the LORD saves his anointed one.
He will answer him from his holy heaven
with the saving strength of his right hand.
[7] Some trust in chariots, and some in horses,
but we trust in the name of the LORD our God.
[8] They have bowed down and fallen,
but we rise up and stand tall.
[9] Save us, LORD!
Let the King answer us when we call!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Suddenly one single person speaks, perhaps a priest or a prophet: “Now I know.” He is sure that God will help.
> Verse 7 is famous: Chariots and horses were the strongest weapons back then, like tanks today. But Israel does not trust in weapons. It trusts in God.
> Verse 9: “The King” can be the human king, or God himself, the true King. Some translations read: LORD, save the king! Answer us when we call!
