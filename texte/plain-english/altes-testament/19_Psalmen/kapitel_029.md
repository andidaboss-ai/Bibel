# Psalm 29
### The voice of the LORD in the storm

---

## Give glory to the LORD (Verses 1–2)

[1] A psalm by David.
Give to the LORD, you sons of the mighty,
give to the LORD glory and strength.
[2] Give to the LORD the glory that his name deserves.
Worship the LORD in holy clothing.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Sons of the mighty”: This probably means heavenly beings, angels. Even they should give glory to God.
> “In holy clothing”: in festive clothes, like the priests in the temple. Others translate: in the splendor of his holiness.

---

## The voice of the LORD (Verses 3–9)

[3] The LORD’s voice is over the waters.
The God of glory thunders.
The LORD thunders over many waters.
[4] The LORD’s voice is powerful.
The LORD’s voice is full of majesty.
[5] The LORD’s voice breaks the cedars.
Yes, the LORD smashes the cedars of Lebanon.
[6] He makes them jump like a calf,
and Lebanon and Sirion like a young wild ox.
[7] The LORD’s voice strikes with flashes of lightning.
[8] The LORD’s voice shakes the wilderness.
The LORD shakes the wilderness of Kadesh.
[9] The LORD’s voice makes the deer give birth,
and it strips the forests bare.
In his temple, everyone says, “Glory!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Seven times it says: “The LORD’s voice.” You can hear the thunder rolling.
> A thunderstorm moves across the land: from the sea (verse 3), over the mountains of Lebanon in the north (verses 5–6), to the wilderness of Kadesh in the south (verse 8).
> “Sirion” is another name for Mount Hermon.
> Israel’s neighbors believed that the god Baal sent thunder and rain. This psalm says clearly: Not Baal, but the LORD is the Lord over the storm.
> Verse 9: While the storm is raging outside, everyone in the temple calls out, “Glory!” They see God’s greatness in the storm.

---

## The LORD is King (Verses 10–11)

[10] The LORD sat on his throne above the Flood.
Yes, the LORD sits as King forever.
[11] The LORD will give strength to his people.
The LORD will bless his people with peace.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The Flood”: The same word is used here as for the great flood in the time of Noah (Genesis 6–9). God was already King over the greatest flood. He remains King forever.
> The psalm begins with God’s mighty voice in the storm and ends with a quiet word: “peace.” The mighty God gives his people rest and peace (in Hebrew, “shalom”).
