# Psalm 74
### A lament over the destroyed temple

---

## Why have you rejected us? (Verses 1–3)

[1] A contemplation by Asaph.
God, why have you rejected us forever?
Why does your anger smoke against the sheep of your pasture?
[2] Remember your community, which you bought long ago,
which you set free to be the tribe that belongs to you:
Mount Zion, where you have lived.
[3] Lift up your feet and come to the endless ruins,
to all the evil the enemy has done in the sanctuary.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 74 laments the destruction of the temple in Jerusalem. It probably means the destruction by the Babylonians in the year 586 BC (2 Kings 25:8–9).
> The people feel like a flock whose shepherd has left them.
> “Lift up your feet”: Come here and look at the ruins!

---

## They have burned your sanctuary (Verses 4–11)

[4] Your enemies have roared in the middle of your meeting place.
They have set up their own flags as signs.
[5] They acted like men swinging axes,
cutting through a thicket of trees.
[6] Now they smash all its carved woodwork
with hatchets and hammers.
[7] They have burned your sanctuary to the ground.
They have made the home of your Name unholy.
[8] They said in their hearts, “We will crush them completely.”
They have burned all the places in the land where God was worshiped.
[9] We see no miraculous signs.
There is no prophet anymore,
and none of us knows how long this will last.
[10] How long, God, will the enemy insult you?
Will the enemy speak against your name forever?
[11] Why do you hold back your hand, your right hand?
Take it out from your chest and destroy them!

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the destruction is described in detail: The enemies roar in the temple, set up their flags, smash the beautiful wood carvings with axes, and burn everything down.
> Verse 9: This is especially bad: There are no more signs from God, and no prophet who says how long it will last. God is silent.
> Verse 11: God keeps his hand hidden at his chest, inside his robe, as if he did not want to do anything. The one praying asks: Take your hand out and step in!

---

## Yet you have been my King since long ago (Verses 12–17)

[12] Yet God has been my King since long ago.
He brings rescue all over the earth.
[13] You divided the sea by your strength.
You broke the heads of the sea monsters in the waters.
[14] You crushed the heads of Leviathan.
You gave him as food to the people and creatures of the desert.
[15] You made springs and streams break open.
You dried up mighty rivers.
[16] The day is yours, and the night is yours too.
You made the light and the sun.
[17] You have set all the borders of the earth.
You have made summer and winter.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the lament, the one praying remembers: God is still the King who created the world and rescued Israel.
> Verses 13–14: Dividing the sea reminds us of the way out of Egypt. The “sea monsters” and “Leviathan” are pictures for the powers of chaos (compare Job 41). Some interpreters also think of Egypt, which was like a monster.
> Verse 14: The Hebrew word is uncertain. It can mean people who live in the desert, or wild animals of the desert. Here it says: “as food to the people and creatures of the desert.”
> Verses 16–17: Day and night, summer and winter: God created the order of the world. That is why he can create order again now.

---

## Don’t forget your poor (Verses 18–23)

[18] Remember this, LORD: the enemy has mocked you.
Foolish people have insulted your name.
[19] Don’t hand over the soul of your dove to wild animals!
Don’t forget the life of your poor people forever!
[20] Keep your covenant in mind,
for the dark places of the earth are full of places of violence.
[21] Don’t let the oppressed go back in shame!
Let the poor and needy praise your name!
[22] Rise up, God! Defend your own cause!
Remember how the foolish man mocks you all day long.
[23] Don’t forget the voices of your enemies.
The noise of those who rise up against you goes up all the time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: The people of Israel are like a helpless dove among wild animals.
> Verse 20: “Keep your covenant in mind”: A covenant is a binding agreement. God has bound himself to his people. The one praying reminds God of his promise.
> Verse 22: “Defend your own cause”: It is not only about Israel, but about God’s honor. The enemies mock God himself.
> The psalm ends without an answer. It leaves the lament open. To this day, Jews pray this psalm in memory of the destruction of the temple.
