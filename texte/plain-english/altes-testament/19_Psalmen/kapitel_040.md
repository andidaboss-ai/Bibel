# Psalm 40
### He pulled me out of the pit

---

## I waited patiently (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the Chief Musician. A psalm by David.
I waited patiently for the LORD.
He turned to me and heard my cry.
[2] He also brought me up out of a terrible pit,
out of the muddy clay.
He set my feet on a rock
and gave me a firm place to stand.
[3] He has put a new song in my mouth,
a song of praise to our God.
Many will see it and be filled with awe.
They will trust in the LORD.
[4] Blessed is the man who puts his trust in the LORD.
Blessed means: truly happy.
He does not look up to proud people,
or to those who turn aside to lies.
[5] LORD, my God, you have done many wonderful things.
You have many thoughts for us.
No one can list them all for you.
If I wanted to tell about them and speak of them,
there would be more than anyone can count.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one who prays tells about how he was rescued. He was trapped as if in a deep pit full of mud, where you cannot get out by yourself. Back then, people sometimes used empty cisterns as prisons (compare Jeremiah 38:6). A cistern is a pit dug in the ground to collect rainwater.
> God pulled him out and set him on firm rock.
> The song “40” by the Irish band U2 is based on this psalm.
> Verse 5: God’s good deeds are so many that no one can list them all.

---

## I am glad to do your will (Verses 6–10)

[6] You did not want sacrifices and offerings.
You have opened my ears.
You did not ask for burnt offerings and sin offerings.
[7] Then I said, “Look, I have come.
It is written about me in the book, in the scroll.
[8] I am glad to do your will, my God.
Yes, your law is in my heart.”
[9] I have told the good news of your righteousness
in the great gathering.
Look, I will not close my lips.
LORD, you know it.
[10] I have not hidden your righteousness in my heart.
I have told about your faithfulness and your salvation.
I have not kept your loving kindness and your truth
hidden from the great gathering.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–8: God does not first of all want animal sacrifices. He wants a heart that listens to him and is glad to do his will (compare 1 Samuel 15:22; Hosea 6:6).
> “You have opened my ears”: Word for word: “dug.” God has given him ears that hear and obey. The ancient Greek translation says here: “You prepared a body for me.”
> The letter to the Hebrews quotes verses 6–8 from the Greek translation and relates them to Jesus: Jesus came to do God’s will and to offer himself as a sacrifice (Hebrews 10:5–10).
> Verses 9–10: Whoever has experienced God’s help should not stay silent, but tell others about it.

---

## I am poor and needy (Verses 11–17)

[11] LORD, do not hold back your tender mercy from me.
Let your loving kindness and your truth always protect me.
[12] For more troubles than I can count have surrounded me.
My sins have caught up with me,
so that I cannot look up.
They are more than the hairs on my head.
My heart has failed me.
[13] LORD, please be willing to rescue me.
Hurry to help me, LORD.
[14] Let all those who want to take my life and destroy it
be put to shame and confused together.
Let those who are glad when I am hurt
be turned back and disgraced.
[15] Let those who say to me, “Aha! Aha!”
be stunned because of their shame.
[16] Let all those who seek you
be full of joy and glad in you.
Let those who love your salvation always say,
“Let the LORD be praised as great!”
[17] But I am poor and needy.
May the Lord think about me.
You are my help and the one who rescues me.
Do not wait any longer, my God.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the thanks comes a new trouble. This is honest: Life is not made up of just one rescue. New difficulties come again and again.
> Verse 12: The one who prays sees his own guilt. It is “more than the hairs on my head.”
> Verses 13–17 appear again almost exactly as Psalm 70.
> Verse 17: “But I am poor and needy. May the Lord think about me.” Even if I am small, God does not forget me.
