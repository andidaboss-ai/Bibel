# Psalm 44
### Why do you sleep, Lord?

---

## What you did in the past (Verses 1–8)

[1] For the music director. By the sons of Korah. A contemplative psalm.
God, we have heard it with our ears.
Our ancestors have told us
what you did in their days,
in the days long ago.
[2] With your hand you drove out the nations,
but you planted our ancestors in the land.
You brought trouble on the peoples,
but you let our ancestors spread out.
[3] For they did not take the land with their own sword.
Their own arm did not save them.
It was your right hand, your arm,
and the light of your face,
because you were kind to them.
[4] God, you are my King.
Give the order for victories for Jacob!
[5] With your help, we will push our enemies down.
In your name, we will trample those who rise up against us.
[6] For I will not trust in my bow.
My sword will not save me.
[7] But you have saved us from our enemies.
You have put to shame those who hate us.
[8] We have boasted about God all day long.
We will thank your name forever.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 44 is a lament of the whole people after a terrible defeat.
> It begins by remembering: In the past, God helped Israel. He gave them the land, not through their own strength, but through his grace.
> Verse 6: The people do not trust in weapons, but in God.
> The phrase “you drove out the nations” describes the taking of the land from Israel’s point of view. It is not a command for today to drive out other peoples.

---

## But now you have rejected us (Verses 9–16)

[9] But now you have rejected us and brought us to disgrace.
You no longer go out with our armies.
[10] You make us turn back and run from the enemy.
Those who hate us take our goods for themselves.
[11] You have made us like sheep that are eaten.
You have scattered us among the nations.
[12] You sell your people for nothing.
You have gained nothing from selling them.
[13] You make our neighbors insult us.
The people around us mock us and laugh at us.
[14] The nations use our name as a saying of scorn.
The peoples shake their heads at us.
[15] All day long my disgrace is in front of me.
Shame covers my face,
[16] because of the voice of the one who mocks and insults me,
because of the enemy who wants revenge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Suddenly the turn: “But now.” God seems to have left his people. They have suffered a terrible defeat. Many have been killed or taken away.
> The complaints to God are very direct: You have sold us like goods, and not even for a good price!
> The Bible allows such honest words to God, even words that accuse him.

---

## We have not forgotten you (Verses 17–22)

[17] All this has happened to us,
yet we have not forgotten you.
We have not been unfaithful to your covenant.
A covenant is a binding agreement.
[18] Our heart has not turned back.
Our steps have not left your path.
[19] Yet you have crushed us in the place where jackals live.
You have covered us with the shadow of death.
[20] If we had forgotten the name of our God,
or stretched out our hands to a foreign god,
[21] wouldn’t God find this out?
For he knows the secrets of the heart.
[22] Yes, for your sake we are killed all day long.
We are thought of as sheep to be slaughtered.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is what is special about Psalm 44: The people say, We are innocent. We have not sinned. And still we suffer. It is like Job, but for a whole people.
> Verse 22: “For your sake we are killed all day long”: They suffer precisely because they belong to God.
> Paul quotes verse 22 in Romans 8:36. Then he answers: Nothing “will be able to separate us from God’s love which is in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:39).
> Many Jews have prayed this verse in times of persecution, up to the Shoah (the Holocaust).

---

## Wake up, Lord! (Verses 23–26)

[23] Wake up! Why are you sleeping, Lord?
Get up! Do not reject us forever.
[24] Why do you hide your face?
Why do you forget our suffering and how we are oppressed?
[25] For our soul is bowed down to the dust.
Our body lies flat on the ground.
[26] Rise up and help us.
Set us free because of your loving kindness.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Why are you sleeping?”: A bold cry. Of course the one who prays knows that God does not sleep (Psalm 121:4). But that is how it feels.
> The psalm ends without an answer, only with the request: Help us, “because of your loving kindness.” Not because we have earned it, but because you are good.
