# Psalm 94
### God of vengeance, shine out!

---

## How long? (Verses 1–7)

[1] LORD, you God to whom vengeance belongs,
you God to whom vengeance belongs, shine out!
[2] Rise up, you judge of the earth!
Pay back the proud people what they deserve!
[3] LORD, how long will the wicked,
how long will the wicked celebrate their victory?
[4] They pour out proud words.
All who do evil brag.
[5] They crush your people, LORD,
and they oppress the people who belong to you.
[6] They kill the widow and the foreigner,
and they murder the orphans.
[7] They say: “Yah does not see it.
The God of Jacob does not notice it.”
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> “God to whom vengeance belongs”: This sounds harsh. It means: God makes sure that there is justice. He does not let wrong go unpunished. Because vengeance belongs to God, people do not have to take revenge themselves (compare Deuteronomy 32:35; Romans 12:19).
> The wicked do the worst things: They kill the weakest people in society, widows, foreigners and orphans. These are exactly the people God especially wants to protect.
> They think: God does not see anything.

---

## Should he not see? (Verses 8–11)

[8] Pay attention, you people without sense!
You fools, when will you become wise?
[9] He made the ear. Will he not hear?
He formed the eye. Will he not see?
[10] He disciplines the nations. Will he not punish?
He teaches people. He knows.
[11] The LORD knows people’s thoughts.
He knows that they are worthless.

> **What does this mean?**
> A clever argument: God gave people ears and eyes. How could he himself be blind and deaf? Of course he sees and hears everything.
> Verse 11: Paul quotes this verse in 1 Corinthians 3:20.

---

## Happy is the one you discipline (Verses 12–15)

[12] Happy is the person whom you discipline, Yah,
and whom you teach from your law.
[13] Then you give him rest from the bad days,
until the pit is dug for the wicked.
[14] For the LORD will not reject his people.
He will not leave the people who belong to him.
[15] For justice will become right again.
All whose hearts are upright will follow it.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s discipline is a sign of his love. Whoever knows God’s teaching finds peace inside, even in bad times.
> Verse 14 is a great promise: God does not reject his people.
> Verse 15: One day, justice will be fair again. Now it is often twisted, but it will not stay that way.

---

## When my foot was slipping (Verses 16–19)

[16] Who will stand up for me against the wicked?
Who will stand up for me against those who do evil?
[17] If the LORD had not helped me,
my soul would soon have lived in silence.
[18] When I said, “My foot is slipping!”
your loving kindness, LORD, held me up.
[19] When there were many worried thoughts inside me,
your comfort made my soul glad.

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying tells about what happened to him: No one helped him. Without God, he would have died. (“Lived in silence” means: in the world of the dead.)
> Verse 18: A beautiful picture: Just when he thought he would slip and fall, God’s loving kindness held him tight.
> Verse 19: When worries go round and round in your head, God’s comfort can make the soul glad. This is a verse for sleepless nights full of worrying.

---

## The LORD is my fortress (Verses 20–23)

[20] Can a throne of evil be your partner,
a throne that causes harm through its laws?
[21] They gather together against the life of the righteous person.
They condemn innocent people to death.
[22] But the LORD has become my high tower.
My God is the rock where I am safe.
[23] He has brought their own sin back on them.
He will destroy them in their own evil.
The LORD, our God, will destroy them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: A frightening thought: Evil rulers can even make wrong legal through laws. Sadly, this has often happened in history. But God has nothing to do with it.
> Verse 21: Innocent people are condemned by courts.
> Verses 22–23: But God is the safe fortress. Evil falls back on those who do it.
