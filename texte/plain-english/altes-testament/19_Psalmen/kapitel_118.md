# Psalm 118
### This is the day that the LORD has made

---

## His loving kindness lasts forever (Verses 1–4)

[1] Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[2] Let Israel now say:
“His loving kindness lasts forever.”
[3] Let the family of Aaron now say:
“His loving kindness lasts forever.”
[4] Let those who fear the LORD now say:
“His loving kindness lasts forever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 118 is the last psalm of the “Hallel” (113–118). It was probably a festival song for a procession to the temple. A leader called out, and the congregation answered.
> As in Psalm 115, three groups are named: the people of Israel, the priests, and all who honor God.

---

## The LORD is on my side (Verses 5–9)

[5] In my trouble, I called to Yah.
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.
Yah answered me and set me free.
[6] The LORD is on my side. I will not be afraid.
What can a human being do to me?
[7] The LORD is on my side among those who help me.
So I will look in triumph at those who hate me.
[8] It is better to find safety in the LORD
than to trust in people.
[9] It is better to find safety in the LORD
than to trust in princes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: “Set me free”: In Hebrew, it says: “into a wide place”. God leads us out of the narrow place of trouble into a wide, open space.
> Verse 6: “The LORD is on my side. I will not be afraid”: compare Hebrews 13:6.
> Verses 8–9: People, even powerful ones, can let us down. God is more reliable.

---

## In the name of the LORD (Verses 10–14)

[10] All the nations surrounded me,
but in the name of the LORD I cut them off.
[11] They surrounded me, yes, they surrounded me.
In the name of the LORD I cut them off.
[12] They swarmed around me like bees.
They burned out as fast as burning thorns.
In the name of the LORD I cut them off.
[13] You pushed me hard to make me fall,
but the LORD helped me.
[14] Yah is my strength and my song.
He has become my salvation.

> **What does this mean?**
> The king or the people were attacked from all sides, as if by a swarm of bees. But the enemies burned out quickly, like a fire of dry thorns.
> “In the name of the LORD”: not by their own strength, but with God’s help. The language of war describes being rescued from trouble. It is not a call to use violence in the name of God.
> Verse 14 quotes the song of Moses after the rescue at the Red Sea (Exodus 15:2).

---

## I will not die, but I will live (Verses 15–18)

[15] Shouts of joy and victory are heard in the tents of the righteous:
“The right hand of the LORD does mighty things.
[16] The right hand of the LORD is lifted high!
The right hand of the LORD does mighty things!”
[17] I will not die, but I will live,
and I will tell what Yah has done.
[18] Yah has punished me hard,
but he has not handed me over to death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17 is a strong confession: “I will not die, but I will live.” Martin Luther wrote this verse on the wall of his study during hard times.
> Verse 18: The person praying has been through hard times, but God did not let him die.

---

## The stone that the builders rejected (Verses 19–24)

[19] Open the gates of righteousness for me!
I will go in through them. I will give thanks to Yah.
[20] This is the gate of the LORD.
The righteous will go in through it.
[21] I will give thanks to you, for you have answered me,
and you have become my salvation.
[22] The stone that the builders rejected
has become the cornerstone.
[23] The LORD has done this.
It is wonderful in our eyes.
[24] This is the day that the LORD has made.
We will rejoice and be glad in it!

> **What does this mean?**
> The procession arrives at the temple gate: “Open the gates of righteousness for me!”
> Verse 22 is one of the most important sayings in the Bible: The builders threw away a stone as useless. But it becomes the most important stone of the whole building, the cornerstone. At first, this was a picture for Israel or for the king. The powerful looked down on them, but God chose them.
> Jesus applied this verse to himself (Matthew 21:42). The leaders of his people rejected him, and he was crucified. But God raised him from the dead. Peter says the same thing (Acts 4:11; 1 Peter 2:7).
> Verse 24: “This is the day that the LORD has made”: a well-known verse that is prayed at Easter.

---

## Blessed is the one who comes (Verses 25–29)

[25] LORD, please save us now!
LORD, please give us success now!
[26] Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the LORD!
We bless you from the house of the LORD.
[27] The LORD is God, and he has given us light.
Tie the festival sacrifice with ropes
to the horns of the altar!
[28] You are my God, and I will give thanks to you.
You are my God, and I will praise you highly.
[29] Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: “LORD, please save us now!” In Hebrew, this is “Hosanna!”
> Verse 26: “Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the LORD!” With these words, the priests welcomed the pilgrims at the temple.
> When Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey, the people shouted words from this psalm: “Hosanna to the son of David! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!” (Matthew 21:9). Christians still sing these words today at the Lord’s Supper.
> Verse 27: “The horns of the altar”: These were the corners of the altar, which stuck up like horns.
> The psalm ends the way it began: “His loving kindness lasts forever.”
