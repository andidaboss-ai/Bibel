# Psalm 111
### The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom

---

## The works of the LORD are great (Verses 1–5)

[1] Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.
I will give thanks to the LORD with my whole heart,
in the circle of upright people, and in the assembly.
[2] The works of the LORD are great.
All who enjoy them study them.
[3] What he does is full of honor and majesty.
His righteousness lasts forever.
[4] He has made sure that his wonderful works are remembered.
The LORD is gracious and merciful.
[5] He has given food to those who fear him.
He always remembers his covenant.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalms 111 and 112 are twins: Both are alphabet poems, each with 22 half-lines. Psalm 111 praises God. Psalm 112 describes the person who fears God.
> Verse 2: God’s works are worth studying. This verse is written in Latin over the entrance of a physics laboratory in Cambridge (the Cavendish Laboratory). Science and faith do not have to be enemies.
> Verse 4: “He has made sure that his wonderful works are remembered”: The festivals of Israel, especially Passover, remind people of what God has done.
> Verse 5: Food in the wilderness: the manna.

---

## His commands can be trusted (Verses 6–9)

[6] He has shown his people the power of his works
by giving them the land of other nations.
[7] The works of his hands are true and just.
All his commands can be trusted.
[8] They stand firm forever and ever.
They are made with truth and uprightness.
[9] He has sent rescue to his people.
He has set up his covenant forever.
His name is holy and awesome!

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s commands are not random. They are “made with truth and uprightness.” They are good and can be trusted.
> Verse 9: God has rescued his people, out of Egypt and later out of the exile.

---

## The beginning of wisdom (Verse 10)

[10] The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom.
All who do what he says have good understanding.
His praise lasts forever!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10 is a famous saying. It also appears in Proverbs 1:7 and 9:10.
> “The fear of the LORD” does not mean being scared of God. It means: deep respect for God, taking God seriously. Whoever takes God seriously begins to become wise.
