# Psalm 14
### The fool says: There is no God

---

## No one does good (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. By David.
The fool has said in his heart, “There is no God.”
They are rotten. They have done disgusting things.
There is no one who does good.
[2] The LORD looked down from heaven on all people,
to see if there was anyone who understood,
anyone who looked for God.
[3] They have all turned away.
All of them together have become rotten.
There is no one who does good, no, not one.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, “the fool” is not simply a person who is not smart. It is someone who lives as if there is no God. He does not necessarily think about it like a philosopher. He simply lives as if he did not have to answer to anyone.
> God looks down from heaven to see if he can find anyone who looks for him. And he finds no one.
> Paul quotes verses 1–3 in Romans 3:10–12: “There is no one righteous; no, not one.” With this, he shows that all people need God’s grace.
> Psalm 14 appears again, almost exactly the same, as Psalm 53.

---

## God is with the righteous (Verses 4–7)

[4] Do all who do evil have no understanding?
They eat up my people the way people eat bread,
and they do not call on the LORD.
[5] There they were, full of terror,
for God is with the generation of the righteous.
[6] You ruin the plans of the poor,
because the LORD is his refuge.
[7] Oh, that the salvation of Israel would come out of Zion!
When the LORD turns things around for his people,
then Jacob will be full of joy, and Israel will be glad.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: The wicked “eat up” God’s people as easily as people eat bread. They take advantage of the weak without thinking.
> But then they become afraid, because God is on the side of the righteous and the poor.
> Verse 6: The powerful can ruin the plans of the poor, but God is their refuge.
> Verse 7: A deep longing: May God come and rescue his people. “Jacob” and “Israel” are two names for the same people.
