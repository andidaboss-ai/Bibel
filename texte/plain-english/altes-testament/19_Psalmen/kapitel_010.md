# Psalm 10
### Why are you so far away, LORD?

---

## Why do you hide? (Verses 1–2)

[1] Why do you stand so far away, LORD?
Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble?
[2] In their pride, the wicked hunt down the weak.
They are caught in the evil plans they think up.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 10 has no heading. It continues Psalm 9 (see there).
> The psalm begins with an honest, painful question: Why is God so far away when people need him most? People still ask this question today.
> The second half of verse 2: “They are caught in the evil plans they think up.” This can also be read as a request to God: May God catch them in their own plans.

---

## This is how the wicked think (Verses 3–11)

[3] For the wicked person boasts about what his heart craves.
He praises the greedy and speaks against the LORD.
[4] The wicked person is so proud
that there is no room for God in his thoughts.
[5] Everything always goes well for him.
He is arrogant, and your laws are far away from his eyes.
He sneers at all his enemies.
[6] He says to himself, “Nothing will shake me.
For generations, I will have no trouble.”
[7] His mouth is full of cursing, lies and threats.
Under his tongue are trouble and evil.
[8] He lies in wait near the villages.
From his hiding places he murders innocent people.
His eyes secretly watch for the helpless.
[9] He hides in secret like a lion in its hiding place.
He lies in wait to catch the helpless.
He catches the helpless when he drags them into his net.
[10] The helpless are crushed. They collapse.
They fall under his strength.
[11] He says to himself, “God has forgotten.
He hides his face. He will never see it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here we see exactly how a wicked person thinks and acts:
> – He is proud, and there is “no room for God in his thoughts.”
> – He is successful and feels safe: Nothing can happen to me.
> – He lies and cheats.
> – He lies in wait for the weak like a wild animal hunting its prey.
> – He thinks: God does not see this.
> Paul quotes verse 7 in Romans 3:14.
> This describes oppression as it has existed at all times: The strong take advantage of the weak, and it seems as if no one steps in.

---

## Get up, LORD! (Verses 12–15)

[12] Get up, LORD! God, lift up your hand!
Do not forget the helpless.
[13] Why does the wicked person speak against God?
Why does he say to himself, “God will not make me answer for it”?
[14] But you do see trouble and grief.
You look at it closely, to take it into your hand.
You help the victim and the child without a father.
[15] Break the arm of the wicked.
As for the evil person, search out his wickedness until you find no more.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wicked person says: God does not see it. The person praying disagrees: Yes, you do see it! God sees the trouble and takes it into his hand.
> “Break the arm” is a picture: Take away his power to harm others.
> The second half of verse 15: God should search for evil until no more evil can be found. The goal is a world without injustice.

---

## The LORD is King (Verses 16–18)

[16] The LORD is King forever and ever!
The nations will disappear from his land.
[17] LORD, you have heard what the humble long for.
You will make their hearts strong.
You will listen closely
[18] to bring justice to the child without a father and to the oppressed,
so that people who are made from the earth will not spread terror anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end there is trust: The LORD is King, not the violent people.
> God hears the humble, the children without fathers and the oppressed.
> “People who are made from the earth”: Even the mightiest oppressor is only a human being made from dust. His power has an end.
> Psalms 9 and 10 together show: Faith in God’s justice and the honest complaint “Why do you stand so far away, LORD?” (Psalm 10:1) belong together.
