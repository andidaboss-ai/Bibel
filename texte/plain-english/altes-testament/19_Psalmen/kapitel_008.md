# Psalm 8
### What is a human being?

---

## How majestic is your name (Verses 1–2)

[1] For the music director. On an instrument from Gath. A psalm by David.
LORD, our Lord,
how majestic is your name in all the earth!
You have placed your glory above the heavens!
[2] From the lips of babies and little children you have built up strength,
because of your enemies,
to silence the enemy and the one who seeks revenge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 8 is a psalm of praise about creation and about human beings.
> “An instrument from Gath” (in Hebrew: “Gittith”): Gath was a city of the Philistines. Perhaps it means an instrument or a tune from Gath. No one knows for sure.
> Verse 2: A surprising thought: God does not need strong warriors. Even the babbling and laughing of little children is praise to God that puts the enemies to shame. Jesus quotes this verse when children praise him in the temple (Matthew 21:16).

---

## What is a human being? (Verses 3–8)

[3] When I look at your heavens, the work of your fingers,
the moon and the stars, which you have set in place,
[4] what is a human being, that you think of him?
What is a son of man, that you care for him?
A son of man means: a human being.
[5] For you have made him only a little lower than the angels.
You have crowned him with glory and honor.
[6] You make him ruler over the works of your hands.
You have put all things under his feet:
[7] all sheep and cattle,
yes, and the wild animals of the field,
[8] the birds of the sky, the fish of the sea,
and everything that swims along the paths of the seas.

> **What does this mean?**
> At night, David looks up at the starry sky. He feels tiny. And yet he is amazed: The great God thinks of small human beings and takes care of them!
> Verse 5: “Only a little lower than the angels”: In Hebrew, it says “Elohim.” This can mean “God” or “heavenly beings.” Some Bibles translate it here as “God.” Human beings have a high dignity.
> Verses 6–8: God has given human beings responsibility for the animals and the earth (compare Genesis 1:26–28). “To rule” here does not mean to exploit. It means to take care of things responsibly, like a good king.
> Job had asked the same question in bitterness: What is a human being, that you make so much of him and keep testing him? (Job 7:17). Psalm 8 asks it full of wonder.
> The New Testament relates these verses to Jesus, the “Son of Man,” to whom everything is made subject (Hebrews 2:6–9).

---

## How majestic is your name (Verse 9)

[9] LORD, our Lord,
how majestic is your name in all the earth!

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm ends with the same words it began with. Like a frame, the praise of God surrounds what is said about human beings.
> This shows: Human beings are great, but God is greater. Human dignity comes from God.
