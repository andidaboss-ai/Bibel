# Psalm 66
### Come and see the works of God

---

## Shout to God, all the earth! (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. A song. A psalm.
Shout to God with joy, all the earth!
[2] Sing about the glory of his name!
Give him glory and praise!
[3] Say to God: “How awesome your deeds are!
Your power is so great that your enemies bow down before you.
[4] All the earth will worship you and sing to you.
They will sing to your name.”
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> A joyful psalm of praise. The whole world is invited to praise God.

---

## He turned the sea into dry land (Verses 5–7)

[5] Come and see what God has done!
His work for the people of the earth is awesome.
[6] He turned the sea into dry land.
They walked through the river on foot.
There we rejoiced in him.
[7] He rules by his power forever.
His eyes watch the nations.
Do not let the rebellious rise up against him!
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6 reminds us of two great rescues: crossing the Red Sea (Exodus 14) and crossing the Jordan (Joshua 3).
> “There we rejoiced in him”: Even though it was long ago, the psalm says “we.” Whoever believes today is part of this story.

---

## Through fire and water (Verses 8–12)

[8] Praise our God, you peoples!
Let the sound of his praise be heard!
[9] He keeps us alive
and does not let our feet slip.
[10] For you, God, have tested us.
You have made us pure, the way silver is made pure in fire.
[11] You brought us into prison.
You put a heavy load on our backs.
[12] You let people ride over our heads.
We went through fire and through water,
but you brought us out to a place of plenty.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people have lived through hard times: captivity, oppression, humiliation.
> Verse 10: These times were like the fire in which silver is made pure. The suffering made the people pure.
> Verse 12: “Through fire and through water”: through every danger. But in the end, God leads them into plenty (compare Isaiah 43:2).

---

## I will keep my vows (Verses 13–15)

[13] I will come into your temple with burnt offerings.
I will keep my vows to you,
[14] the vows that my lips promised
and that my mouth spoke when I was in trouble.
[15] I will offer you burnt offerings of fat animals,
together with the offering of rams.
I will offer bulls and goats.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a single person speaks. When he was in trouble, he promised God something. Now he wants to keep his promise.
> He brings big, expensive sacrifices to show how thankful he is.

---

## God has heard my prayer (Verses 16–20)

[16] Come and listen, all you who fear God!
I will tell what he has done for my soul.
[17] I cried out to him with my mouth,
and my tongue praised him highly.
[18] If I had held on to sin in my heart,
the Lord would not have listened.
[19] But God has truly listened.
He has paid attention to the voice of my prayer.
[20] Praise be to God!
He has not turned away my prayer,
and he has not turned his loving kindness away from me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever has experienced God’s help wants to tell others about it.
> Verse 18: If someone holds on to a sin on purpose and does not want to let go of it, his prayer is not honest. This does not mean that only perfect people may pray. It means: We should be honest before God.
> Verse 20: The psalm ends with thanks: God has not turned away my prayer.
