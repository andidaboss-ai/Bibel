# Psalm 73
### Yet I am always with you

---

## The third book of Psalms

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 73, the third book of Psalms begins (Psalms 73–89).
> The first eleven psalms of this book (73–83) come from Asaph or from his group of singers. Asaph was a musician whom David had appointed to serve at the temple (1 Chronicles 16:4–7). Psalm 50 also comes from him.
> Many psalms in this book are dark: They lament the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple, and they ask why God allowed it.

---

## I was jealous of the wicked (Verses 1–3)

[1] A psalm by Asaph.
Surely God is good to Israel,
to those who have a pure heart.
[2] But as for me, my feet almost stumbled.
My steps had nearly slipped.
[3] For I was jealous of proud people
when I saw how well things go for the wicked.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 73 is one of the most important psalms about this question: Why do things go well for evil people and badly for good people? It is the same question as in the book of Job.
> The one praying begins with a statement of faith: God is good. But then he admits honestly: I almost lost my faith.

---

## How well things go for the wicked (Verses 4–12)

[4] For they have no struggles in their death.
Their strength stays firm.
[5] They are free from the burdens that other people carry.
They are not struck by trouble like other people.
[6] That is why pride is like a chain around their neck.
Violence covers them like clothing.
[7] Their eyes bulge with fat.
Their minds overflow with proud ideas.
[8] They mock and speak with evil intent.
In their pride they threaten to crush others.
[9] They have set their mouths in the heavens.
Their tongues walk through the earth.
[10] That is why their people turn back to them,
and they drink up water in plenty.
[11] They say, “How would God know?
Does the Most High know anything?”
[12] Look, these are the wicked.
They are always at ease, and they keep getting richer.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying describes the wicked: They are healthy, rich, and strong. They have no worries. They wear their pride like a necklace.
> Verse 9: They talk as if they could rule over heaven and earth.
> Verse 10: Many people run after them and admire them. This verse is hard to understand in Hebrew.
> Verse 11: They think: God doesn’t notice anything anyway.

---

## Was it all for nothing? (Verses 13–16)

[13] Surely I have kept my heart pure for nothing,
and I have washed my hands in innocence for nothing.
[14] For all day long I have been struck with trouble,
and I am punished every morning.
[15] If I had said, “I will talk like that,”
I would have betrayed the generation of your children.
[16] When I tried to understand this,
it was too painful for me—

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying is bitter: Was it worth it at all to live an honest life? Things go badly for me, even though I did everything right.
> Verse 15: But he does not say these doubts out loud. He does not want to shake the faith of other believers.
> Verse 16: He gets nowhere with his thinking. It only hurts.

---

## Until I went into the sanctuary (Verses 17–20)

[17] until I went into God’s sanctuary
and saw what their end will be.
[18] Surely you put them on slippery ground.
You throw them down to destruction.
[19] How suddenly they are destroyed!
Terrors sweep them away completely.
[20] As a dream disappears when someone wakes up,
so you, Lord, when you wake up, will despise their empty dreams.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17 is the turning point of the whole psalm: “until I went into God’s sanctuary.” Close to God, the one praying sees things differently.
> He realizes: The success of the wicked does not last. They stand on slippery ground. Their happiness is like a dream that disappears when you wake up.
> Sometimes we do not understand life by thinking alone, but only when we meet God.

---

## I was like a dumb animal (Verses 21–22)

[21] For my soul was bitter.
My heart was hurt.
[22] I was so foolish, and I did not understand.
I was like a dumb animal before you.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying is ashamed of his bitterness. He says honestly: I was as foolish as an animal that only looks at what it can see.

---

## Yet I am always with you (Verses 23–28)

[23] Yet I am always with you.
You have held my right hand.
[24] You will guide me with your advice,
and afterward you will take me into glory.
[25] Whom do I have in heaven?
There is no one on earth I long for besides you.
[26] My body and my heart fail,
but God is the strength of my heart,
and he is my share forever.
[27] For look, those who are far from you will die.
You have destroyed all those who are unfaithful to you.
[28] But for me, it is good to come close to God.
I have made the Lord GOD my refuge,
so that I may tell about all your works.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23 is one of the most beautiful sayings in the Bible: “Yet I am always with you.” In spite of all the doubts, in spite of the bitterness: God has held me by the hand the whole time.
> Verse 24: “And afterward you will take me into glory”: Here the hope for a life with God after death shines through.
> Verses 25–26: This is the answer to the question of the psalm. The wicked have riches. I have God. And that is worth more than anything else. Even if my body and my heart fail, God remains.
> Verse 28: “But for me, it is good to come close to God.” This is what the one praying has come to understand at the end.
