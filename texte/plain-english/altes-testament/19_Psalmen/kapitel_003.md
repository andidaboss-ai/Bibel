# Psalm 3
### A morning prayer in danger

---

## So many enemies (Verses 1–2)

[1] A psalm by David, when he ran away from his son Absalom.
LORD, how many enemies I have now!
Many people rise up against me.
[2] Many say about me,
“God will not help him.”
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The heading names the situation: David has to run away from his own son Absalom. Absalom wants to take the throne away from him (2 Samuel 15–18). This is one of the most painful moments in David’s life.
> The worst thing is not only the many enemies, but their mocking: God does not help him anymore. Job knew this, too.
> “Selah”: probably a sign for a pause in the music (see Psalm 1).
> Note: In many other Bibles, the heading is verse 1. That is why every verse there is 1 higher.

---

## But you, LORD (Verses 3–6)

[3] But you, LORD, are a shield all around me.
You are my glory.
You are the one who lifts up my head.
[4] I cry out loud to the LORD.
He answers me from his holy hill.
Selah.
[5] I lay down and slept.
I woke up, because the LORD holds me up.
[6] I will not be afraid of tens of thousands of people
who have lined up against me on every side.

> **What does this mean?**
> With “But you” comes the turning point. David no longer looks at the enemies. He looks at God.
> God is like a shield that protects him all around. “The one who lifts up my head”: When David fled, he walked with his head covered, and he wept (2 Samuel 15:30). God lifts him up again.
> Verse 5: In the middle of danger, David can sleep. This is a sign of great trust. That is why Psalm 3 is seen as a morning prayer: I have woken up. God has kept me safe.

---

## Get up, LORD! (Verses 7–8)

[7] Get up, LORD! Save me, my God!
For you have struck all my enemies on the cheek.
You have broken the teeth of the wicked.
[8] Rescue comes from the LORD.
May your blessing be on your people.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Get up, LORD!” was the call when the ark of the covenant went out into battle (Numbers 10:35).
> The enemies are compared with wild animals. When their teeth are broken, they cannot bite anymore. This is a picture: God takes away the enemies’ power to do harm. David does not strike them himself. He leaves it to God.
> Verse 8: At the end, David does not think only of himself. He blesses the whole people, even those who have just turned away from him.
