# Psalm 76
### God breaks the weapons

---

## God is known in Judah (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. On stringed instruments. A psalm by Asaph. A song.
In Judah, God is known.
His name is great in Israel.
[2] His tent is in Salem.
His home is in Zion.
[3] There he broke the flaming arrows of the bow,
the shield, the sword, and the weapons of war.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Salem” is an old name for Jerusalem (compare Genesis 14:18). It sounds like the word “shalom,” which means peace.
> In the city of peace, God breaks the weapons. That is a picture that gives hope today too.
> When they read this psalm, many people think of the rescue of Jerusalem from the Assyrians (2 Kings 19:35).

---

## The warriors are asleep (Verses 4–6)

[4] You are glorious and wonderful,
more than mountains full of prey.
[5] The brave men have been robbed.
They have fallen into their last sleep.
None of the warriors can lift their hands.
[6] When you scold them, God of Jacob,
both chariot and horse fall into a deep sleep like death.

> **What does this mean?**
> The enemies who marched against Jerusalem are defeated. They lie there like sleepers who will never wake up again.
> God needs no weapons. One word from him is enough.

---

## God rises up to judge (Verses 7–10)

[7] You, yes you, are to be feared.
Who can stand before you when you are angry?
[8] You announced judgment from heaven.
The earth was afraid and became silent
[9] when God rose up to judge,
to save all the suffering people of the earth.
Selah.
[10] Surely even human anger will praise you.
Those who escape your anger are held back.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9 shows the goal of God’s judgment: “to save all the suffering people of the earth.” God does not judge because he likes to punish, but to protect the weak.
> Verse 10 is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: In the end, even the anger of the enemies serves to show God’s greatness.

---

## Bring him gifts (Verses 11–12)

[11] Make vows to the LORD your God, and keep them!
Let all the people around him bring gifts to him, the one who is to be feared.
[12] He will cut off the spirit of princes.
The kings of the earth fear him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever has experienced God’s help should thank him and keep his promises.
> “He will cut off the spirit of princes”: This means: He breaks the pride of rulers. Even the most powerful people in the world must answer to God.
