# Psalm 24
### The King of glory comes in

---

## The earth belongs to the LORD (Verses 1–2)

[1] A psalm by David.
The earth belongs to the LORD, and everything in it,
the world and all who live in it.
[2] For he has built it on the seas
and made it stand firm on the floods.

> **What does this mean?**
> Everything belongs to God: the earth, everything on it, and all people. We are not the owners of the world. We are guests and caretakers.
> Back then, people imagined that the earth was built as if on water. God has set it on a firm foundation. The chaos of the water cannot wash it away.
> Paul quotes verse 1 in 1 Corinthians 10:26.

---

## Who may go up God’s hill? (Verses 3–6)

[3] Who may go up the LORD’s hill?
Who may stand in his holy place?
[4] The one who has clean hands and a pure heart,
who has not set his heart on lies,
and has not sworn falsely.
[5] He will receive a blessing from the LORD,
and righteousness from the God who saves him.
[6] This is the generation of those who look for him,
who look for your face—Jacob.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The same question as in Psalm 15: Who may come close to God?
> The answer: “Clean hands” (good deeds) and “a pure heart” (good intentions). The outside and the inside belong together.
> Verse 6: In Hebrew, the end is hard to understand. Some Bibles add the word “God” here. Then it means: They look for your face, God of Jacob.
> Jesus says: “Blessed are those whose hearts are pure, for they will see God” (Matthew 5:8).

---

## Open the gates wide! (Verses 7–10)

[7] Lift up your heads, you gates!
Be lifted up, you ancient doors,
and the King of glory will come in!
[8] Who is the King of glory?
The LORD, strong and mighty,
the LORD, mighty in battle.
[9] Lift up your heads, you gates!
Yes, lift them up, you ancient doors,
and the King of glory will come in!
[10] Who is this King of glory?
The LORD of Armies is the King of glory!
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here we hear a festive procession to the temple. Perhaps the psalm was sung when the ark of the covenant was brought to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6).
> A choir calls out to the gates: Open up! Lift yourselves up! The gatekeepers ask in return: Who is this King? And the choir answers: The LORD!
> The gates are so small for such a great King that they should “lift up their heads,” which means they should become bigger.
> “The LORD of Armies”: God is the Lord over all powers in heaven and on earth, over angels and stars.
> The well-known Advent hymn “Lift Up Your Heads, Ye Mighty Gates” was inspired by this psalm. It comes from Germany. Christians sing it in the time before Christmas, when they look forward to the coming of Jesus.
