# Psalm 7
### God is a righteous judge

---

## Save me from those who chase me (Verses 1–2)

[1] A meditation by David. He sang it to the LORD because of the words of Cush, the Benjamite.
LORD, my God, I take refuge in you.
Save me from all who chase me, and rescue me.
[2] Otherwise they will tear my soul apart like a lion.
They will rip it to pieces, and no one will be there to rescue me.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Meditation”: In Hebrew, it says “Shiggaion.” No one knows exactly what it means. It may be a special kind of song, or a stirred-up, passionate song.
> We do not know who Cush from the tribe of Benjamin was. He is not mentioned anywhere else. Saul came from the tribe of Benjamin. Perhaps Cush was one of Saul’s men who told lies about David.

---

## If I have done wrong (Verses 3–5)

[3] LORD, my God, if I have done this,
if there is guilt on my hands,
[4] if I have paid back evil to someone who was at peace with me
(yes, I have robbed the one who was my enemy for no reason),
[5] then let the enemy chase my soul and catch it.
Yes, let him trample my life into the ground,
and lay my honor in the dust.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> David swears an oath, much like Job (Chapter 31): If I am guilty, let disaster strike me.
> Verse 4: The words in parentheses are hard to understand. The English text we follow (the WEB) translates: “I have robbed the one who was my enemy for no reason.” But many other Bibles understand the Hebrew word as “to set free” or “to spare.” Then it means: Yes, I have even rescued the one who was my enemy for no reason. That would fit David, who spared Saul’s life two times (1 Samuel 24 and 26). Both readings are possible.

---

## Get up, LORD, and judge! (Verses 6–10)

[6] Get up, LORD, in your anger!
Rise up against the rage of my enemies!
Wake up for me! You have ordered that judgment must happen.
[7] Let the gathering of the peoples stand around you.
Rule over them from on high.
[8] The LORD judges the peoples.
Judge me, LORD, by my righteousness,
and by the honesty that is in me.
[9] Oh, let the wickedness of the wicked come to an end.
But make the righteous stand firm.
The righteous God tests their minds and hearts.
[10] My shield is with God.
He saves those whose hearts are honest.

> **What does this mean?**
> David asks God to step in as judge. He does not want to take revenge himself. He leaves the judgment to God.
> Verse 9: God tests “minds and hearts.” Word for word, it says “kidneys and heart.” Back then, people thought of the kidneys as the place of feelings and the heart as the place of thoughts. So God sees into the deepest part of a person.

---

## Whoever digs a pit (Verses 11–17)

[11] God is a righteous judge.
Yes, he is a God who is angry every day.
[12] If a person does not turn back, he will sharpen his sword.
He has bent his bow and strung it.
[13] He has also gotten deadly weapons ready for himself.
He prepares his flaming arrows.
[14] Look! He is in labor with evil.
Yes, he has become pregnant with trouble and given birth to lies.
[15] He has dug a hole,
and he has fallen into the pit that he made.
[16] The trouble he causes will come back on his own head.
His violence will come down on the top of his own head.
[17] I will thank the LORD for his righteousness.
I will sing praise to the name of the LORD Most High.

> **What does this mean?**
> God “is angry every day”: God does not shrug at evil. Injustice touches him.
> Verses 12–13: Who is “he”? It can mean God: God stands ready if the wicked person does not turn back. Or it can mean the wicked person: He prepares weapons against others. Both are possible.
> Verse 14: A picture from giving birth: The wicked person “becomes pregnant” with evil plans and “gives birth” to lies.
> Verses 15–16: Many people know this as a saying: Whoever digs a pit for others falls into it himself. The saying comes from the Bible. Proverbs 26:27 says: “Whoever digs a pit will fall into it.” Evil falls back on the one who does it.
> The psalm ends with thanks and praise.
