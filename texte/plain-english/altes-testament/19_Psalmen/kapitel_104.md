# Psalm 104
### How many are your works!

---

## Wrapped in light (Verses 1–4)

[1] Praise the LORD, my soul!
LORD, my God, you are very great.
You are dressed in honor and majesty.
[2] He wraps himself in light as in a robe.
He stretches out the heavens like a curtain.
[3] He lays the beams of his rooms on the waters.
He makes the clouds his chariot.
He walks on the wings of the wind.
[4] He makes the winds his messengers,
and flames of fire his servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 104 is a great psalm about creation. It tells in pictures how God created the world and keeps it going, much like Genesis 1.
> It begins and ends like Psalm 103: “Praise the LORD, my soul!” Psalm 103 praises God for his forgiveness. Psalm 104 praises him for his creation.
> God is dressed in light. He stretches out the sky like the cloth of a tent. Clouds and wind are like his vehicle and his servants.
> The letter to the Hebrews quotes verse 4 and applies it to the angels (Hebrews 1:7).

---

## The waters and the mountains (Verses 5–9)

[5] He set the earth on its foundations,
so that it will never be moved.
[6] You covered it with the deep waters like a coat.
The waters stood above the mountains.
[7] When you scolded them, they ran away.
At the sound of your thunder, they hurried away.
[8] The mountains rose up, and the valleys sank down,
to the place you had chosen for them.
[9] You have set a border that the waters may not cross,
so that they never cover the earth again.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the beginning, everything was covered with water (compare Genesis 1:2, 9). At God’s command, the waters pulled back, and the land and the mountains appeared.
> Verse 9: God has set a border for the sea (compare Job 38:8–11).

---

## Springs for all the animals (Verses 10–18)

[10] He sends springs into the valleys.
They flow between the mountains.
[11] They give water to every animal of the field.
The wild donkeys satisfy their thirst.
[12] The birds of the sky build their nests beside them.
They sing among the branches.
[13] He waters the mountains from his rooms above.
The earth is filled with the fruit of your works.
[14] He makes the grass grow for the cattle,
and plants for people to grow,
so that they can bring food out of the earth:
[15] wine that makes people’s hearts glad,
oil that makes their faces shine,
and bread that gives strength to their hearts.
[16] The LORD’s trees get plenty of water,
the cedars of Lebanon, which he planted.
[17] There the birds build their nests.
The stork makes its home in the cypress trees.
[18] The high mountains are for the wild goats.
The rocks are a safe place for the rock badgers.

> **What does this mean?**
> A wonderful picture: God takes care of all living things. Each one has its place: the wild donkeys at the springs, the birds in the branches, the stork in the trees, the wild goats on the mountains.
> Rock badgers are small animals that live in cracks in the rocks. They are about as big as rabbits.
> Verse 15: God does not give only what is most necessary. He also gives joy: wine for the heart, oil for beauty, bread for strength.

---

## Day and night (Verses 19–23)

[19] He made the moon to mark the seasons.
The sun knows when to go down.
[20] You make darkness, and it is night.
Then all the animals of the forest move around.
[21] The young lions roar for their prey.
They ask God for their food.
[22] The sun rises, and they slip away.
They lie down in their dens.
[23] People go out to their work,
to their labor until the evening.

> **What does this mean?**
> The moon sets the calendar (months and festivals). The sun sets day and night.
> A beautiful rhythm: At night the lions hunt. By day people work. Each has its own time.
> Even the roaring of the lions is like a prayer: “They ask God for their food.”

---

## How many are your works! (Verses 24–30)

[24] LORD, how many are your works!
You made them all with wisdom.
The earth is full of your riches.
[25] There is the sea, great and wide.
It is full of living things that no one can count,
both small and large animals.
[26] The ships sail there,
and so does Leviathan, which you formed to play there.
[27] They all wait for you
to give them their food at the right time.
[28] You give it to them, and they gather it.
You open your hand, and they are filled with good things.
[29] When you hide your face, they are frightened.
When you take away their breath, they die and turn back into dust.
[30] When you send out your Spirit, they are created.
You make the face of the ground new.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24 is the high point: “LORD, how many are your works! You made them all with wisdom.” The great variety of creation shows God’s wisdom.
> Verse 26: Leviathan is usually a picture of threatening chaos (Job 41). Here it is like a pet that God made for playing. Some people think of a whale.
> Verses 27–30: All life depends on God. When he gives his breath, the creatures live. When he takes it away, they die.
> Verse 30 is prayed at Pentecost: “When you send out your Spirit, they are created. You make the face of the ground new.”

---

## I will sing to the LORD as long as I live (Verses 31–35)

[31] Let the glory of the LORD last forever!
Let the LORD be glad about his works!
[32] He looks at the earth, and it trembles.
He touches the mountains, and they smoke.
[33] I will sing to the LORD as long as I live.
I will sing praise to my God as long as I exist.
[34] May my thoughts please him.
I will be glad in the LORD.
[35] Let sinners disappear from the earth.
Let the wicked be no more.
Praise the LORD, my soul!
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: God himself is glad about his creation (compare Genesis 1:31: “It was very good”).
> Verse 35: A surprising wish at the end: The sinners should disappear, because they spoil God’s good creation. It means: Evil should disappear from this beautiful world.
> “Praise Yah!”: In Hebrew, this is “Hallelujah!” Here this word appears for the first time in the book of Psalms. (Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.)
> Psalm 104 is an invitation to look at nature with open eyes and to discover God’s wisdom and love in it. It also shows: Creation belongs to God. We should respect it and take care of it.
