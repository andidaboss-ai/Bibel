# Psalm 48
### The city of God

---

## The LORD is great (Verses 1–3)

[1] A song. A psalm by the sons of Korah.
The LORD is great, and he is worthy of great praise
in the city of our God, on his holy mountain.
[2] Mount Zion is beautiful and high.
It is the joy of the whole earth.
It lies on the sides of the north.
It is the city of the great King.
[3] God has shown himself in her strongholds
as a safe refuge.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 48 is a “song of Zion,” a song about Jerusalem and the temple mountain, Zion.
> “On the sides of the north”: In old stories of the neighboring peoples, the gods lived on a mountain in the north. The psalm says: The true mountain of God is Zion.
> “The city of the great King”: Jesus calls Jerusalem this (Matthew 5:35).
> The city is not safe because of its walls, but because God is its refuge.

---

## The kings ran away (Verses 4–8)

[4] For look, the kings gathered together.
They marched past together.
[5] They saw it, and they were amazed.
They were terrified. They hurried away.
[6] Trembling took hold of them there,
pain like a woman giving birth.
[7] With the east wind
you break the ships of Tarshish.
[8] What we have heard, we have now seen
in the city of the LORD of Armies,
in the city of our God.
God will make it firm forever.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Enemy kings marched against Jerusalem. But when they saw the city, they became afraid and ran away. People think of the Assyrians under Sennacherib, who had to withdraw (2 Kings 19:35–36).
> Verse 7: “Ships of Tarshish” were large trading ships for long voyages. The hot east wind from the desert could destroy them. That is how quickly God can break human power.
> Verse 8: “What we have heard, we have now seen”: What our ancestors told us, we have now experienced ourselves.

---

## Walk around Zion (Verses 9–14)

[9] God, we have thought about your loving kindness
in the middle of your temple.
[10] God, your name is known to the ends of the earth,
and so is your praise.
Your right hand is full of righteousness.
[11] Let Mount Zion be glad!
Let the daughters of Judah be full of joy
because of your judgments.
[12] Walk around Zion. Go all the way around her.
Count her towers.
[13] Look closely at her walls.
Look at her palaces,
so that you can tell about it to the next generation.
[14] For this God is our God forever and ever.
He will guide us even until death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: In the temple, the people think about God’s goodness.
> “The daughters of Judah”: the towns and villages in the land of Judah.
> Verses 12–13: A walk around the city. People should look at everything carefully and tell their children about it. Faith is passed on from generation to generation.
> Verse 14: “He will guide us even until death”: God goes with us our whole life long. In Hebrew, the end of the verse is hard to read. Some translate it “forever” or “beyond death.”
