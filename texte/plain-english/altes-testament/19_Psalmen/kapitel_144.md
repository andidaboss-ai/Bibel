# Psalm 144
### What are human beings?

---

## God, my rock (Verses 1–4)

[1] By David.
Praise be to the LORD, my rock,
who trains my hands for war
and my fingers for battle.
[2] He is my loving kindness and my fortress,
my high tower and the one who rescues me,
my shield and the one in whom I find safety.
He makes my people obey me.
[3] LORD, what are human beings, that you care for them?
What is a son of man, that you think about him?
A son of man means: a human being.
[4] A human being is like a breath.
His days are like a shadow that passes by.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 144 takes up many words from Psalm 18 and Psalm 8.
> Verse 1: David was a warrior and a king. He thanks God for strength and skill in battle. That was the reality of his life back then. It is not a call to war today.
> Verses 3–4: In the middle of his praise, David is amazed: Human beings are so small and pass away so quickly, like a breath. And still, the great God cares for them (compare Psalm 8:4).

---

## Come down and rescue me (Verses 5–11)

[5] LORD, open up your heavens and come down!
Touch the mountains, and they will smoke.
[6] Throw out lightning and scatter the enemies!
Send out your arrows and chase them away!
[7] Stretch out your hand from above!
Rescue me and save me out of deep waters,
out of the hands of foreigners,
[8] whose mouths speak lies,
and whose right hand is a hand that lies.
[9] I will sing a new song to you, God.
I will sing praises to you on a lyre with 10 strings.
A lyre is a small harp.
[10] You are the one who saves kings,
who rescues David, his servant, from the deadly sword.
[11] Rescue me and save me out of the hands of foreigners,
whose mouths speak lies,
and whose right hand is a hand that lies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: God is to come as if in a mighty thunderstorm, like back then at Mount Sinai.
> Verse 8: People raised their “right hand” when they made an oath. The enemies make false oaths. Their promise is worth nothing.
> “Foreigners” here means enemy peoples who lie and cheat. It is not a statement against foreigners in general. The Bible often says: God protects strangers (for example, Psalm 146:9).

---

## Happy are the people whose God is the LORD (Verses 12–15)

[12] Then our sons will be like plants that are well cared for.
Our daughters will be like carved pillars that decorate a palace.
[13] Our barns are full,
filled with all kinds of food.
Our sheep have thousands and tens of thousands of lambs
in our fields.
[14] Our oxen will pull heavy loads.
No one breaks through our walls, no one has to go away,
and there are no cries of sorrow in our streets.
[15] Happy are the people for whom things are like this.
Happy are the people whose God is the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> A picture of peace: healthy children, full barns, many animals. No enemies break in, no one has to flee, no one cries out in sorrow.
> Verse 14: “No one breaks through our walls”: no gap broken into the city wall. “No one has to go away”: no one is taken away as a prisoner.
> Verse 15: True happiness is not only wealth. It is this: God is our God.
