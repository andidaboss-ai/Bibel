# Psalm 69
### The water is up to my neck

---

## I am sinking (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. To the tune of “Lilies.” By David.
Save me, God,
for the waters have come up to my neck!
[2] I am sinking in deep mud,
where there is nothing to stand on.
I have come into deep waters,
and the floods are washing over me.
[3] I am tired from crying out.
My throat is dry.
My eyes grow weak while I look for my God.
[4] Those who hate me without a cause
are more than the hairs on my head.
Those who want to destroy me are powerful.
They are my enemies without any reason.
I have to give back what I did not take.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The waters have come up to my neck”: Even today, people use this picture as a saying. The one praying feels as if he is drowning. He cannot find anything to hold on to.
> He has cried out until his voice gave out. He has waited until his eyes grew tired.
> Verse 4: He is hated without a cause. He is supposed to give back something he never stole. Jesus quotes: “They hated me for no reason” (John 15:25).
> If the water is up to your neck right now and you see no way out: Get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

---

## I am insulted because of you (Verses 5–12)

[5] God, you know my foolishness.
My sins are not hidden from you.
[6] Lord GOD of Armies,
don’t let those who wait for you be put to shame because of me.
God of Israel,
don’t let those who seek you be disgraced because of me.
[7] For I have been insulted because of you.
Shame has covered my face.
[8] I have become a stranger to my brothers,
a foreigner to my mother’s children.
[9] For my zeal for your house eats me up.
The insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.
[10] When I cried and fasted,
people insulted me for it.
[11] When I wore rough sackcloth as a sign of grief,
they made jokes about me.
[12] Those who sit in the city gate talk about me.
Drunkards make up songs about me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: The one praying is honest: I am not without faults. But that is not the reason for the hatred.
> Verses 7–9: He suffers because he is faithful to God. Even his own family turns away from him.
> Verse 9: “My zeal for your house eats me up”: The disciples remembered this verse when Jesus drove the traders out of the temple (John 2:17). Paul quotes the second part in Romans 15:3.
> Verse 12: “Those who sit in the city gate”: the respected people of the town. Even the drunkards make up mocking songs about him.

---

## Pull me out of the mud (Verses 13–18)

[13] But as for me, I pray to you, LORD, at a time of your favor.
God, because your loving kindness is so great,
answer me with your faithful help!
[14] Pull me out of the mud, and don’t let me sink!
Let me be rescued from those who hate me
and out of the deep waters!
[15] Don’t let the flood waters wash over me.
Don’t let the deep swallow me up.
Don’t let the pit shut its mouth over me.
[16] Answer me, LORD, for your loving kindness is good.
Turn to me in your great and tender mercy!
[17] Don’t hide your face from your servant,
for I am in trouble. Answer me quickly!
[18] Come near to my soul and set it free!
Pay the price to set me free, because of my enemies!

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying turns to God completely. He asks again and again: Answer me! Rescue me! Don’t hide!
> “Don’t let the pit shut its mouth”: like a well that is covered up, so that no one can ever get out again.

---

## I looked for comforters and found none (Verses 19–21)

[19] You know how I am insulted, my shame, and my disgrace.
All my enemies are in front of you.
[20] The insults have broken my heart,
and I am full of sadness.
I looked for someone to feel sorry for me, but there was no one.
I looked for comforters, but I found none.
[21] They also gave me poison for my food.
When I was thirsty, they gave me vinegar to drink.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: The worst part is the loneliness: No one has pity, no one comforts.
> Verse 21: Instead of help, he gets poison and vinegar. The word for “poison” probably means a bitter herb.
> The Gospels report that Jesus was given vinegar to drink on the cross (Matthew 27:34, 48; John 19:28–29). Together with Psalm 22, Psalm 69 is one of the psalms that the New Testament most often relates to the suffering of Jesus.

---

## A curse on the enemies (Verses 22–28)

[22] Let their table in front of them become a snare.
Let it become a punishment and a trap.
[23] Let their eyes be darkened, so that they cannot see.
Let their backs always be bent.
[24] Pour out your anger on them!
Let your burning anger catch up with them.
[25] Let their home be empty and ruined.
Let no one live in their tents.
[26] For they hunt down the one you have struck.
They talk about the pain of those you have wounded.
[27] Count their crimes against them, one crime after another!
Don’t let them come into your righteousness!
[28] Let them be erased from the book of life,
and let them not be written down with the righteous.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the harshest curses in the Psalms. The one praying is so hurt that he asks God to punish his enemies.
> Verse 26 shows how cruel the enemies are: Instead of having pity on the one who suffers, they kick him when he is already down.
> Paul quotes verses 22–23 in Romans 11:9–10. Peter applies verse 25 to Judas (Acts 1:20).
> Important: The one praying tells God about his anger. He does not take revenge himself. The Bible allows us to speak honestly about our anger and pain before God. But Jesus, who applied many verses of this psalm to himself, prayed for his enemies on the cross: “Father, forgive them” (Luke 23:34).
> “The book of life”: a picture for the list of those who belong to God and live (compare Exodus 32:32; Revelation 20:12).

---

## Praise is better than a bull (Verses 29–36)

[29] But I am in pain and trouble.
God, let your saving help protect me.
[30] I will praise the name of God with a song.
I will honor him greatly with thanks.
[31] That will please the LORD more than an ox,
or a bull with horns and hoofs.
[32] The humble have seen it, and they are glad.
You who seek God, let your heart live!
[33] For the LORD hears the needy.
He does not look down on his people who are prisoners.
[34] Let heaven and earth praise him,
the seas and everything that moves in them!
[35] For God will save Zion
and build the cities of Judah.
They will live there and own it.
[36] The children of his servants will also inherit it.
Those who love his name will live there.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end comes the turning point: The cry becomes praise.
> Verse 31: A song of thanks pleases God more than the most expensive animal sacrifice (compare Psalm 50:14; 51:16–17).
> Verse 33: “The LORD hears the needy.” That is the hope of all the poor and all prisoners.
> Verses 35–36: The view widens to the whole people. Perhaps the psalm was prayed in exile, when Jerusalem was destroyed, with the hope that it would be rebuilt.
