# Psalm 105
### God keeps his covenant with Abraham

---

## Remember his wonderful works (Verses 1–6)

[1] Give thanks to the LORD! Call on his name!
Tell the peoples what he has done!
[2] Sing to him, sing praises to him!
Tell about all his wonderful works!
[3] Be proud of his holy name!
Let the hearts of those who seek the LORD be glad.
[4] Seek the LORD and his strength!
Seek his face always!
[5] Remember the wonderful works he has done,
his wonders, and the judgments he has spoken,
[6] you descendants of Abraham, his servant,
you children of Jacob, his chosen ones!

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 105 tells the story of Israel from Abraham up to the time when the people entered the promised land. Unlike Psalm 78 and Psalm 106, it tells only about God’s faithfulness, not about the unfaithfulness of the people.
> Verses 1–15 also appear almost word for word in 1 Chronicles 16:8–22.
> “Seek his face always”: The relationship with God should never stop.

---

## The everlasting covenant (Verses 7–15)

[7] He is the LORD, our God.
His judgments are in all the earth.
[8] He has remembered his covenant forever,
the word he commanded for a thousand generations.
[9] It is the covenant he made with Abraham,
and the promise he swore to Isaac.
[10] He confirmed it to Jacob as a law,
to Israel as a covenant that lasts forever.
[11] He said: “I will give you the land of Canaan.
It will be your share and your inheritance.”
[12] At that time, they were only a few people,
yes, very few, and they were foreigners in the land.
[13] They moved around from nation to nation,
from one kingdom to another people.
[14] He did not allow anyone to harm them.
Yes, he warned kings because of them:
[15] “Do not touch my anointed ones!
Do not harm my prophets!”

> **What does this mean?**
> God promised Abraham, Isaac and Jacob that he would give the land of Canaan to their descendants (Genesis 12:7; 26:3; 28:13).
> When the family was still small and moved around as foreigners, God protected them. He even warned kings, for example Pharaoh and Abimelech (Genesis 12:17; 20:3).
> “My anointed ones” and “my prophets”: This means Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. In Genesis 20:7, Abraham is called a “prophet.”

---

## Joseph in Egypt (Verses 16–22)

[16] He called for a famine on the land.
He destroyed all their supplies of food.
[17] He sent a man ahead of them:
Joseph was sold as a slave.
[18] They hurt his feet with chains.
His neck was locked in iron,
[19] until the time came when what he had said happened,
and the word of the LORD proved him right.
[20] The king sent for him and set him free.
The ruler of peoples let him go free.
[21] He made him lord over his house,
and ruler over everything he owned.
[22] Joseph could give orders to his princes as he wanted,
and he taught his elders wisdom.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the story of Joseph is told in a few words (Genesis 37–45). His brothers sold him as a slave, and he ended up in prison.
> But God had a plan: Joseph became the second most powerful man in Egypt, and he saved his family from hunger.
> Verse 19: “Until the time came when what he had said happened”: Joseph had explained dreams. When they came true, he was set free.

---

## Israel in Egypt (Verses 23–36)

[23] Israel also came to Egypt.
Jacob lived as a foreigner in the land of Ham.
[24] The LORD made his people grow very large in number.
He made them stronger than their enemies.
[25] He turned the hearts of the Egyptians to hate his people,
and to make secret plans against his servants.
[26] He sent Moses, his servant,
and Aaron, whom he had chosen.
[27] They did his miraculous signs among them,
and wonders in the land of Ham.
[28] He sent darkness and made it dark.
They did not rebel against his words.
[29] He turned their waters into blood,
and he killed their fish.
[30] Their land swarmed with frogs,
even in the rooms of their kings.
[31] He spoke, and swarms of flies came,
and lice in all their land.
[32] He gave them hail instead of rain,
with lightning in their land.
[33] He struck their grapevines and their fig trees.
He broke the trees of their country.
[34] He spoke, and the locusts came,
and grasshoppers without number.
[35] They ate up every plant in their land.
They ate up the fruit of their ground.
[36] He also struck down all the firstborn in their land,
the first sons of all their strength.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jacob’s family (Israel) moved to Egypt and became a large nation there. Then the Egyptians oppressed them (Exodus 1).
> “The land of Ham”: This means Egypt (compare Psalm 78:51).
> Verse 25: “He turned the hearts of the Egyptians”: The Bible sees God’s plan even behind the hatred of the Egyptians. But this does not free the Egyptians from their own responsibility.
> Verses 28–36: The plagues are listed, but not in exactly the same order as in Exodus 7–12. Here the darkness comes first.
> Verse 28: “They did not rebel against his words”: This probably means Moses and Aaron, who faithfully carried out God’s commands.

---

## He brought them out with joy (Verses 37–45)

[37] He brought them out with silver and gold.
Among his tribes, no one was weak.
[38] Egypt was glad when they left,
because the Egyptians had become afraid of them.
[39] He spread out a cloud as a covering,
and a fire to give light at night.
[40] They asked, and he brought quails.
He filled them with bread from the sky.
[41] He opened the rock, and water gushed out.
It flowed like a river through the dry places.
[42] For he remembered his holy promise,
and Abraham, his servant.
[43] He brought his people out with joy,
his chosen ones with singing.
[44] He gave them the lands of the nations.
They took over what other peoples had worked for,
[45] so that they would keep his rules
and obey his laws.
Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> Leaving Egypt is described as a happy celebration. When the people of Israel left, they even received silver and gold from the Egyptians (Exodus 12:35–36).
> In the wilderness, God provided everything: the cloud and the fire, quails, manna and water from the rock.
> Verse 42: The reason for everything: God did not forget his promise to Abraham.
> Verse 45: The goal: The people should live in the land God gave them and follow his teaching. The land is a gift that comes with responsibility.
> The taking of the land is told from Israel’s point of view. It is not a command for today to drive other peoples out.
