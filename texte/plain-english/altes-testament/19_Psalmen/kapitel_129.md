# Psalm 129
### They have not defeated me

---

## Plowed on my back (Verses 1–8)

[1] A song of ascents.
Many times they have made me suffer since I was young.
Let Israel now say it:
[2] Many times they have made me suffer since I was young,
but they have not defeated me.
[3] The plowmen plowed on my back.
They made their furrows long.
A furrow is a long, deep line that a plow cuts into the ground.
[4] The LORD is righteous.
He has cut the ropes of the wicked.
[5] Let all those who hate Zion
be put to shame and turned back.
[6] Let them be like the grass on the roofs,
which dries up before it grows tall.
[7] The one who cuts grain cannot fill his hand with it.
The one who ties bundles of grain cannot fill his arms with it.
[8] And those who pass by do not say,
“May the blessing of the LORD be on you.
We bless you in the name of the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel looks back on its long history: Again and again, it was oppressed, from Egypt to Babylon. But it was not destroyed.
> Verse 3: A painful picture: Like a plow, the oppressors left deep wounds on the back of the people, like the marks of a whip.
> Verse 4: But God has cut the ropes that pulled the plow.
> Verses 6–8: Grass on flat clay roofs grows fast, but dries up right away. No one can harvest it. In the same way, the power of the enemies should pass away.
> Sadly, the Jewish people also suffered much persecution later, all the way to the Holocaust. This psalm bears witness: Hatred does not have the last word.
