# Psalm 63
### My soul thirsts for you

---

## In a dry land (Verses 1–4)

[1] A psalm by David, when he was in the desert of Judah.
God, you are my God. I will seek you with all my heart.
My soul thirsts for you.
My body longs for you
in a dry and tired land, where there is no water.
[2] So I have looked at you in the sanctuary,
to see your power and your glory.
The sanctuary is the holy place of God.
[3] For your loving kindness is better than life.
My lips will praise you.
[4] So I will bless you as long as I live.
I will lift up my hands in your name.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is in the desert of Judah, perhaps running away from Saul or from Absalom. It is hot and dry.
> Just as a person in the desert thirsts for water, David longs for God, with body and soul.
> Verse 3 is a deep sentence: “Your loving kindness is better than life.” God’s love is the most precious thing there is, more precious than life itself.
> In the early church, Psalm 63 was prayed every morning.

---

## In the shadow of your wings (Verses 5–8)

[5] My soul will be satisfied, as if with the richest food.
My mouth will praise you with joyful lips,
[6] when I remember you on my bed
and think about you during the watches of the night.
[7] For you have been my help.
I will shout for joy in the shadow of your wings.
[8] My soul stays close to you.
Your right hand holds me up.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the desert, David is satisfied, not by food, but by God.
> Verse 6: Even at night, when he cannot sleep, he thinks about God. “Watches of the night”: The night was divided into three watches.
> Verse 8: A beautiful picture of our relationship with God: “My soul stays close to you. Your right hand holds me up.” A person holds on to God, and God holds on to him.

---

## The king rejoices (Verses 9–11)

[9] But those who want to take my life and destroy it
will go down into the depths of the earth.
[10] They will be handed over to the power of the sword.
They will become food for jackals.
[11] But the king will rejoice in God.
Everyone who swears by him will praise him,
for the mouths of those who tell lies will be shut.

> **What does this mean?**
> The enemies who want to kill David will die themselves. Back then, this was a terrible fate: not to be buried.
> The one praying does not take revenge himself. He trusts that God will make sure that justice is done.
> Verse 11: In the end, the king rejoices in God, and the liars must be silent.
