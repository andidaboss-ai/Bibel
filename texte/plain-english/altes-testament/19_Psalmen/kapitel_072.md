# Psalm 72
### A prayer for the king

---

## Give the king your justice (Verses 1–7)

[1] By Solomon.
God, give the king your justice.
Give your righteousness to the king’s son.
[2] He will judge your people with righteousness,
and your poor people with justice.
[3] The mountains will bring well-being to the people.
The hills will bring the fruit of righteousness.
[4] He will judge fairly for the poor among the people.
He will save the children of the needy.
He will break the oppressor in pieces.
[5] They will fear you as long as the sun lasts,
and as long as the moon shines, through all generations.
[6] He will come down like rain on grass that has been cut,
like showers that water the earth.
[7] In his days the righteous will flourish.
There will be plenty of peace, until the moon is no more.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 72 is a prayer for the king, perhaps for the day he was crowned. The heading names Solomon, the son of David.
> What is a good king? He makes sure there is justice, especially for the poor and the weak. He protects them from those who oppress them.
> “Well-being” (verse 3) and “peace” (verse 7): In Hebrew, both times the word is “shalom.” That means more than “no war”: It means well-being, justice, and wholeness for everyone.
> Verse 6: A good king is like rain after the harvest: He brings new life.

---

## From sea to sea (Verses 8–11)

[8] He will also rule from sea to sea,
from the River to the ends of the earth.
[9] Those who live in the wilderness will bow before him.
His enemies will lick the dust.
[10] The kings of Tarshish and of the islands will bring tribute.
Tribute is money or goods that people pay to a ruler.
The kings of Sheba and Seba will offer gifts.
[11] Yes, all kings will fall down before him.
All nations will serve him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm wishes that the king would rule over the whole world. “The River” is the Euphrates.
> Tarshish was far in the west (perhaps Spain). Sheba and Seba were in the south (Arabia and Africa). The queen of Sheba visited Solomon (1 Kings 10).
> This went far beyond what any king in Israel really achieved. That is why Jews and Christians have understood the psalm as being about the Messiah.
> In verses 10–11, Christians think of the wise men from the East, who brought gifts to the child Jesus (Matthew 2:11).

---

## He rescues the poor (Verses 12–14)

[12] For he will rescue the needy person when he cries out,
and the poor person who has no one to help him.
[13] He will have pity on the poor and needy.
He will save the lives of the needy.
[14] He will set them free from oppression and violence.
Their blood will be precious in his eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> That is the real reason why this king is great: not his power, but his care for the poor.
> Verse 14: “Their blood will be precious in his eyes”: For a good ruler, the life of every poor person is valuable. No one is too unimportant.
> That is a standard for everyone who has power, to this day.

---

## His name lasts forever (Verses 15–17)

[15] He will live, and gold from Sheba will be given to him.
People will pray for him all the time.
They will bless him all day long.
[16] There will be plenty of grain in the land.
Its fruit sways like Lebanon.
Let it flourish and grow like the grass of the field.
[17] His name lasts forever.
His name continues as long as the sun.
People will be blessed through him.
All nations will call him blessed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Under a just king, everything thrives: the harvest, the people, the land.
> “Like Lebanon”: The heads of grain sway in the wind like the great cedar forests of Lebanon.
> Verse 17 reminds us of the promise to Abraham: “Through you, all the families of the earth will be blessed” (Genesis 12:3).

---

## The end of the second book of Psalms (Verses 18–20)

[18] Praise be to the LORD God, the God of Israel,
who alone does wonderful deeds!
[19] Praise be to his glorious name forever!
Let the whole earth be filled with his glory!
Amen and amen.
[20] This is the end of the prayers by David, the son of Jesse.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 18–19 are the praise at the end of the second book of Psalms (Psalms 42–72), just as Psalm 41:13 closes the first book.
> Verse 20: “This is the end of the prayers by David”: This was probably once the end of an older collection of David’s psalms. Later, more psalms by David were added (for example, Psalms 86, 101, 103, 108–110, 138–145).
> Jesse was David’s father.
> With Psalm 73, the third book of Psalms begins.
