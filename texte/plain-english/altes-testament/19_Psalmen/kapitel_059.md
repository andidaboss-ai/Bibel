# Psalm 59
### God is my fortress

---

## Rescue me from my enemies (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. To the tune of “Do Not Destroy.”
A poem by David, when Saul sent men,
and they watched the house to kill him.
Rescue me from my enemies, my God.
Put me in a high, safe place,
away from those who rise up against me.
[2] Rescue me from those who do evil.
Save me from violent men.
[3] For look, they lie in wait for my life.
The powerful gather together against me.
It is not because I disobeyed, and not because of my sin, LORD.
[4] I have done nothing wrong,
yet they are ready to attack me.
Rise up, look, and help me!
[5] You, LORD God of Armies, the God of Israel,
wake up to punish the nations.
Show no mercy to the wicked traitors.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The superscription reminds us of a night when Saul’s men surrounded David’s house. His wife Michal helped him escape through a window (1 Samuel 19:11–17).
> David is innocent. He has done nothing, and still he is hunted.
> “The nations”: The psalm looks beyond David’s personal enemies to all who fight against God’s people.

---

## Like dogs in the night (Verses 6–10)

[6] They come back in the evening,
howling like dogs,
and they prowl around the city.
[7] Look, they spit out words with their mouths.
Swords are on their lips.
“For,” they say, “who hears us?”
[8] But you, LORD, laugh at them.
You make fun of all the nations.
[9] Oh, my Strength, I watch for you,
for God is my high tower.
[10] My God will come to meet me with his loving kindness.
God will let me look down on my enemies in victory.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people hunting David are like stray dogs that roam the streets at night, howling. Back then, street dogs were dangerous and despised.
> They think: “Who hears us?” They believe that no one sees the wrong they do. But God hears and sees everything.
> Verse 9: Like a watchman in the night, David waits for God.

---

## Do not kill them (Verses 11–13)

[11] Do not kill them, or my people may forget.
Scatter them by your power, and bring them down,
Lord, our shield.
[12] Because of the sin of their mouths
and the words of their lips,
let them be caught in their pride,
because of the curses and lies that they speak.
[13] Destroy them in your anger.
Destroy them, so that they are no more.
Let them know that God rules in Jacob,
to the ends of the earth.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11 is surprising: “Do not kill them.” David does not want God to destroy the enemies right away. They should remain as a warning, so that the people do not forget how God rescues.
> Verse 13 then does sound harsh. But the goal is: Everyone should know that God rules. It probably means: God should destroy their power, not necessarily their lives.

---

## In the morning I will sing (Verses 14–17)

[14] Let them come back in the evening.
Let them howl like a dog
and prowl around the city.
[15] They will wander around looking for food.
They will wait all night if they do not get enough.
[16] But I will sing about your strength.
Yes, in the morning I will sing out loud about your loving kindness.
For you have been my high tower,
a safe place on the day of my trouble.
[17] To you, my Strength, I will sing praises.
For God is my high tower,
the God who shows me mercy.

> **What does this mean?**
> The enemies howl in the night, hungry and dissatisfied.
> David, on the other hand, sings in the morning. The night of fear is over.
> Verse 17: “The God who shows me mercy”: word for word, “my God of loving kindness.” God is not only strong, but also loving.
