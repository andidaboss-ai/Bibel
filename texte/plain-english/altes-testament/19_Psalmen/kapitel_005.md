# Psalm 5
### A prayer in the morning

---

## In the morning you hear my voice (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. With flutes. A psalm by David.
Listen to my words, LORD.
Pay attention to my quiet thoughts.
[2] Listen to the sound of my cry for help, my King and my God,
for I pray to you.
[3] LORD, in the morning you will hear my voice.
In the morning I will lay my requests before you,
and I will watch and wait for your answer.

> **What does this mean?**
> David begins the day with prayer. He brings his requests to God and waits eagerly for God’s answer.
> “My King”: David is a king himself, but he knows: The true King is God.
> “I will watch and wait”: like a guard who waits for the morning.

---

## God takes no pleasure in evil (Verses 4–6)

[4] For you are not a God who takes pleasure in wickedness.
Evil cannot live with you.
[5] Proud people will not stand before your eyes.
You hate all who do wrong.
[6] You will destroy those who tell lies.
The LORD hates people who are violent and who deceive others.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is holy. He cannot put up with evil.
> “You hate all who do wrong”: This is a strong statement. In the language of the Bible, “to hate” often means: to firmly reject, not to want something near you. The Bible also says that God does not want the sinner to die. He wants the sinner to turn back and live (Ezekiel 18:23).

---

## I will come into your house (Verses 7–8)

[7] But as for me, because your loving kindness is so great, I will come into your house.
I will bow down toward your holy temple with deep respect for you.
[8] LORD, lead me in your righteousness because of my enemies.
Make your way straight in front of me.

> **What does this mean?**
> David knows: I may come to God. This is not because I am so good. It is “because your loving kindness is so great.” The Hebrew word is “chesed”: God’s faithful love.
> “Toward your holy temple”: Jews still pray facing Jerusalem today.
> David asks: Show me the right way, and make it straight and smooth, with nothing to trip over.

---

## The enemies, and those who take refuge (Verses 9–12)

[9] For nothing they say can be trusted.
Their heart is full of destruction.
Their throat is an open grave.
They flatter with their tongue.
[10] God, declare them guilty.
Let them fall because of their own plans.
Drive them out because of all their many sins,
for they have rebelled against you.
[11] But let all who take refuge in you be glad.
Let them always shout for joy, because you protect them.
Let those who love your name also be joyful in you.
[12] For you will bless the righteous.
LORD, you will surround him with your kindness like a shield.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: The words of the enemies are deadly, like an open grave. Paul quotes this in Romans 3:13 to show that all people need God’s grace.
> Verse 10 asks for justice. David asks God to let the wicked fail because of their own plans. He does not take revenge himself. He leaves the judgment to God. Such prayers are called “cursing psalms” or “psalms of revenge.” They honestly show the pain of people who suffer injustice. Later, Jesus teaches us to pray even for our enemies (Matthew 5:44).
> Verses 11–12: The psalm ends with joy. God protects all who take refuge in him, as if with a large shield.
