# Psalm 9
### God judges fairly

---

## I will thank you (Verses 1–6)

[1] For the music director. To the tune of “The Death of the Son.” A psalm by David.
I will thank the LORD with my whole heart.
I will tell about all the wonderful things you have done.
[2] I will be glad and full of joy because of you.
I will sing praise to your name, Most High.
[3] When my enemies turn back,
they stumble and die in front of you.
[4] For you have stood up for my rights and my cause.
You sit on the throne and judge fairly.
[5] You have scolded the nations.
You have destroyed the wicked.
You have wiped out their name forever and ever.
[6] The enemy is caught in endless ruin.
Even the memory of the cities that you have torn down is gone.

> **What does this mean?**
> “To the tune of ‘The Death of the Son’”: This is probably the name of a well-known tune that the psalm was sung to. No one knows it today.
> Psalms 9 and 10 belong together. In Hebrew, together they form a poem based on the alphabet (an “acrostic”): Every second verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. In the old Greek and Latin Bibles, they are one single psalm. That is why, from here on, the psalm number in older Catholic and in Orthodox Bibles is often 1 lower (up to Psalm 147).
> David thanks God with his whole heart, because God has helped him against his enemies.

---

## The LORD reigns forever (Verses 7–12)

[7] But the LORD reigns forever.
He has set up his throne for judgment.
[8] He will judge the world with righteousness.
He will judge the peoples honestly.
[9] The LORD will also be a high tower for those who are oppressed,
a high tower in times of trouble.
[10] Those who know your name will put their trust in you,
for you, LORD, have not abandoned those who look for you.
[11] Sing praises to the LORD, who lives in Zion.
Tell the peoples what he has done.
[12] For the one who punishes murder remembers them.
He does not forget the cry of those who suffer.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mighty empires come and go. But God reigns forever.
> God is like a “high tower” for those who are oppressed: a safe place where they find protection.
> Verse 12: “The one who punishes murder”: God makes sure that murder and violence are not forgotten. He does not forget the victims.

---

## Be kind to me (Verses 13–16)

[13] Be kind to me, LORD.
See how I suffer because of those who hate me.
Lift me up from the gates of death,
[14] so that I can tell of all your praise.
I will rejoice in your rescue in the gates of the daughter of Zion.
[15] The nations have sunk down into the pit that they made.
Their own foot is caught in the net that they hid.
[16] The LORD has made himself known.
He has carried out judgment.
The wicked is trapped by the work of his own hands.
Meditation. Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Gates of death” and “gates of the daughter of Zion” stand opposite each other: God brings me back from the edge of death, so that I can sing his praise in Jerusalem (Zion).
> “Daughter of Zion” is a poetic name for Jerusalem and the people who live there.
> Verses 15–16: Again the same thought: The wicked are caught in their own trap (compare Psalm 7:15–16).
> “Meditation” (in Hebrew “Higgaion”): probably a sign for the music, perhaps quiet playing for thinking things over.

---

## The poor will not be forgotten (Verses 17–20)

[17] The wicked will be sent back to Sheol (the world of the dead),
all the nations that forget God.
[18] For the needy will not always be forgotten.
The hope of the poor will not be lost forever.
[19] Get up, LORD! Do not let human beings win.
Let the nations be judged in front of you.
[20] Fill them with fear, LORD.
Let the nations know that they are only human.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18 is a great promise: The poor will not be forgotten forever. Their hope will not be disappointed.
> Verse 20: Mighty nations often forget that they are “only human.” They think of themselves as gods. The psalm asks God to show them their limits.
