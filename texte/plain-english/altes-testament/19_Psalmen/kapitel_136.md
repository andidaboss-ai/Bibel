# Psalm 136
### For his loving kindness lasts forever

---

## Give thanks to the God of gods (Verses 1–3)

[1] Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[2] Give thanks to the God of gods,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[3] Give thanks to the Lord of lords,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 136 is a song sung in turns. A leader sings the first line, and the whole congregation answers each time: “for his loving kindness lasts forever.” This happens 26 times.
> The Hebrew word for “loving kindness” is “chesed.” It means faithful love that never stops.
> In Judaism, this psalm is called “the Great Hallel.” Hallel means praise. It is sung at the festival of Passover.

---

## The Creator (Verses 4–9)

[4] Give thanks to him who alone does great wonders,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[5] Give thanks to him who made the heavens with understanding,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[6] Give thanks to him who spread out the earth above the waters,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[7] Give thanks to him who made the great lights,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[8] He made the sun to rule the day,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[9] He made the moon and the stars to rule the night,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> First, God is praised as the Creator: heaven, earth, sun, moon and stars (compare Genesis 1).
> Behind every deed of God there is the same reason: his loving kindness.

---

## The one who sets free (Verses 10–22)

[10] Give thanks to him who struck down the firstborn of Egypt,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[11] He brought Israel out from among them,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[12] He did it with a strong hand and with an arm stretched out,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[13] Give thanks to him who split the Red Sea in two,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[14] He let Israel go through the middle of it,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[15] But he threw Pharaoh and his army into the Red Sea,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[16] Give thanks to him who led his people through the wilderness,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[17] Give thanks to him who struck down great kings,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[18] He killed mighty kings,
for his loving kindness lasts forever:
[19] Sihon, king of the Amorites,
for his loving kindness lasts forever,
[20] and Og, king of Bashan,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[21] He gave their land as an inheritance,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[22] He gave it as an inheritance to Israel, his servant,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the history of Israel is told: the way out of Egypt, the way through the Red Sea, the wilderness and the arrival in the land.
> The battles of that time are part of the history. They do not allow violence today. The psalm does not celebrate killing, but the rescue of a weak people.

---

## He remembers us (Verses 23–26)

[23] He remembered us when we were at the very bottom,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[24] He has set us free from our enemies,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[25] He gives food to every living creature,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[26] Oh, give thanks to the God of heaven,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, it becomes personal: God remembered “us” when things were bad for us.
> Verse 25: God’s loving kindness is not only for Israel. He gives food to every living creature, to people and animals all over the world.
> You can keep praying this psalm today with your own experiences: For everything that God has done in my life, it is true: “for his loving kindness lasts forever.”
