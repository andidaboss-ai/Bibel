# Psalm 17
### Protect me like the apple of your eye

---

## Hear my just request (Verses 1–5)

[1] A prayer by David.
Hear, LORD, my just request.
Listen to my prayer. It does not come from lying lips.
[2] Let my verdict come from you.
Let your eyes see what is fair.
[3] You have tested my heart.
You have visited me in the night.
You have examined me and found nothing.
I have decided that my mouth will not disobey.
[4] As for what people do:
because of the word of your lips,
I have kept myself away from the ways of violent people.
[5] My steps have stayed on your paths.
My feet have not slipped.

> **What does this mean?**
> David is being accused unfairly. He asks God to decide as judge.
> He is sure: God has tested me, even at night, when people are completely honest with themselves. And God has found nothing evil.
> This is not pride. David does not say that he has never sinned. He says: In this matter, I am innocent.
> Verse 4: God’s word has kept him from using violence.

---

## In the shadow of your wings (Verses 6–12)

[6] I have called to you, for you will answer me, God.
Turn your ear to me. Hear what I say.
[7] Show your wonderful loving kindness,
you who save those who take refuge at your right hand from their enemies.
[8] Protect me like the apple of your eye.
Hide me in the shadow of your wings,
[9] away from the wicked who oppress me,
from my deadly enemies who surround me.
[10] They close their hard hearts.
With their mouths they speak proudly.
[11] Now they have surrounded us wherever we step.
They are watching for a chance to throw us to the ground.
[12] He is like a lion that is hungry for its prey,
like a young lion lying in wait in its hiding place.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 has two beautiful pictures:
> – “The apple of your eye”: This means the pupil of the eye. It is the most sensitive part of the body. People protect it at once with the eyelid. That is how God protects those he loves (compare Deuteronomy 32:10).
> – “The shadow of your wings”: As a bird takes its chicks under its wings, God protects (compare Ruth 2:12; Matthew 23:37).
> “Hard hearts”: Word for word, it says “fat.” Their heart is closed up as if by fat. They feel no pity.

---

## I will see your face (Verses 13–15)

[13] Get up, LORD, face him! Throw him down!
Rescue my soul from the wicked with your sword,
[14] from people, by your hand, LORD,
from people of this world, whose share is in this life.
You fill the stomachs of those you love.
Your sons have plenty,
and they save up riches for their children.
[15] But as for me, I will see your face in righteousness.
When I wake up, I will be satisfied with seeing what you look like.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 is hard to understand in Hebrew. It can be explained in different ways. The WEB, our English source text, understands it like this: God provides richly for those he loves. Others translate: God fills the stomachs of the wicked with punishment.
> “People of this world, whose share is in this life”: people for whom only the here and now counts.
> Verse 15 is the high point: The person praying does not only want a full stomach. He wants to see God. That is what truly satisfies him.
> “When I wake up”: Perhaps the next morning. Many teachers, Jewish and Christian, also see a hope beyond death here: waking up to eternal life with God.
