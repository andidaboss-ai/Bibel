# Psalm 30
### You have turned my mourning into dancing

---

## You have healed me (Verses 1–3)

[1] A psalm. A song for the dedication of the temple. By David.
I will praise you highly, LORD, for you have lifted me up.
You have not let my enemies rejoice over me.
[2] LORD my God, I cried out to you,
and you have healed me.
[3] LORD, you have brought my soul up from Sheol.
You have kept me alive,
so that I did not go down into the pit.
Sheol is the place of the dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> The heading speaks of the “dedication of the temple.” In Hebrew, it simply says “of the house.” It can mean the temple or David’s palace. To this day, Jews pray this psalm at the festival of lights, Hanukkah. Hanukkah remembers how the temple was dedicated again.
> The person praying was very sick and close to death. God has healed him. “Lifted me up”: the way someone pulls a bucket up out of a deep well.

---

## Weeping at night, joy in the morning (Verses 4–5)

[4] Sing praise to the LORD, you who are faithful to him!
Give thanks to his holy name!
[5] For his anger lasts only a moment.
His kindness lasts a lifetime.
Weeping may stay for the night,
but joy comes in the morning.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 is one of the most comforting verses in the Bible. Suffering and grief are like a night. They are hard, but they do not last forever. The morning comes.
> God’s anger is short. His love is long.
> This does not mean that every grief is over after one night. But it does mean: Weeping does not have the last word.

---

## I thought I was safe (Verses 6–10)

[6] As for me, when things were going well, I said,
“Nothing will ever shake me.”
[7] LORD, when you were kind to me,
you made my mountain stand strong.
But when you hid your face,
I was terrified.
[8] I cried out to you, LORD.
I begged the Lord for help:
[9] “What good is it to you if I am destroyed,
if I go down into the pit?
Will the dust praise you?
Will it tell about your faithfulness?
[10] Hear me, LORD, and be kind to me.
LORD, be my helper.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying tells honestly: When things were going well for me, I became proud. I thought nothing could shake me, by my own strength.
> Then God hid himself, and suddenly everything was different. He noticed: My safety came from God, not from me.
> Verse 9: A bold argument in prayer: God, what good is it to you if I die? The dead cannot praise you anymore. Let me live, so that I can praise you!

---

## Mourning turns into dancing (Verses 11–12)

[11] You have turned my mourning into dancing.
You have taken off my sackcloth
and dressed me with joy.
Sackcloth is rough clothing that people wore when they were grieving.
[12] So my heart will sing praise to you and not be silent.
LORD my God, I will thank you forever!

> **What does this mean?**
> A beautiful picture: God takes off the rough clothing of grief (sackcloth) from the one who is mourning. He dresses him in festive clothes of joy. Mourning turns into dancing.
> The psalm ends with a promise: I will not be silent. I will thank God forever.
