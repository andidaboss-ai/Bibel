# Psalm 98
### Let the rivers clap their hands

---

## He has done wonderful things (Verses 1–3)

[1] A psalm.
Sing a new song to the LORD,
for he has done wonderful things!
His right hand and his holy arm have won salvation for him.
[2] The LORD has made known how he saves.
He has shown his righteousness openly, in front of the nations.
[3] He has remembered his loving kindness and his faithfulness
toward the people of Israel.
All the ends of the earth have seen how our God saves.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 98 is like Psalm 96. It celebrates how God saves, and the whole world has seen it.
> God has stayed faithful to his people Israel. And all the nations see it.
> Mary takes up thoughts from this psalm in her song of praise (the “Magnificat”) (Luke 1:54).

---

## Shout for joy to the King (Verses 4–6)

[4] Shout for joy to the LORD, all the earth!
Burst out and sing for joy! Yes, sing praises!
[5] Sing praises to the LORD with the harp,
with the harp and the sound of singing!
[6] With trumpets and the sound of the ram’s horn,
shout for joy before the King, the LORD!

> **What does this mean?**
> A big, loud celebration: singing, harps, trumpets and rams’ horns (in Hebrew: shofar).
> God is celebrated like a king when he is crowned.

---

## All creation shouts for joy (Verses 7–9)

[7] Let the sea roar, and everything that fills it,
the world, and those who live in it.
[8] Let the rivers clap their hands.
Let the mountains sing for joy together
[9] before the LORD, for he comes to judge the earth.
He will judge the world with righteousness,
and the peoples fairly.

> **What does this mean?**
> A happy picture: The rivers clap their hands, the mountains sing. All of nature celebrates God.
> The well-known Christmas song “Joy to the World” is based on this psalm.
> Like Psalm 96, this psalm also ends with hope: God comes and brings justice for everyone.
