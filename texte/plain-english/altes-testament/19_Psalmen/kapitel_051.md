# Psalm 51
### Create in me a clean heart, O God

---

## Have mercy on me (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David,
when Nathan the prophet came to him,
after David had slept with Bathsheba.
Have mercy on me, God,
because of your loving kindness.
Because your mercy is so great,
wipe out my wrongs.
[2] Wash all my guilt away from me.
Make me clean from my sin.
[3] For I know my wrongs.
My sin is always in front of me.
[4] Against you, and you only, I have sinned.
I have done what is evil in your eyes.
So you are right when you speak,
and you are fair when you judge.
[5] Look, I was born in guilt.
My mother was sinful when she became pregnant with me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 51 is the best-known psalm of repentance. The superscription reminds us of the worst moment in David’s life: He had committed adultery with Bathsheba and had her husband Uriah killed. The prophet Nathan confronted him with his guilt (2 Samuel 11–12).
> Note: In some Bibles, the superscription takes up two verses. That is why every verse there is 2 higher (for example, verse 10 here = verse 12 there).
> David makes no excuses. He only asks for grace. He does not deserve it. He asks because of God’s goodness and mercy.
> Verse 4: “Against you, and you only, I have sinned”: Of course David also did great wrong to Bathsheba and Uriah. But he understands: In the end, every sin is also a sin against God.
> Verse 5: This does not mean that sex or birth is sinful. David says: From the very beginning, I have been a person who tends to sin. Christians speak here of “original sin.” Jewish interpreters understand the verse rather as a strong expression of deep regret.

---

## Wash me, and I will be whiter than snow (Verses 6–9)

[6] Look, you want truth deep inside a person.
In the hidden places of my heart, you teach me wisdom.
[7] Make me pure with hyssop, and I will be clean.
Wash me, and I will be whiter than snow.
[8] Let me hear joy and gladness,
so that the bones you have broken may be full of joy.
[9] Turn your face away from my sins.
Wipe out all my guilt.

> **What does this mean?**
> God wants honesty deep inside. You cannot fool him.
> Hyssop is a small plant. Priests used a bundle of hyssop to sprinkle water or blood, to make people clean for worship (for example Leviticus 14:4–7; Numbers 19:18).
> “Whiter than snow”: That is how completely God can forgive (compare Isaiah 1:18).
> Verse 8: The guilt has broken David inside. He asks: Let me feel joy again.

---

## A clean heart (Verses 10–13)

[10] Create in me a clean heart, O God.
Make my spirit new and right again.
[11] Do not throw me away from your presence.
Do not take your Holy Spirit from me.
[12] Give me back the joy of your salvation.
Hold me up with a willing spirit.
[13] Then I will teach those who do wrong your ways.
Sinners will turn back to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10 is one of the best-known verses in the Bible. Many people sing it as a song: “Create in me a clean heart, O God.”
> “Create”: The Hebrew has the same word as in Genesis 1:1 (“God created”). Only God can create a new heart. A person cannot make himself new.
> Verse 11: David had seen how God’s Spirit left Saul (1 Samuel 16:14). He is afraid that the same thing will happen to him.
> Verse 13: Whoever has experienced forgiveness can show others the way to God.

---

## A broken heart (Verses 14–17)

[14] God, rescue me from the guilt of killing someone.
You are the God who saves me.
My tongue will sing out loud about your righteousness.
[15] Lord, open my lips.
And my mouth will tell your praise.
[16] For you do not want sacrifices,
or I would bring them.
You take no pleasure in burnt offerings.
[17] The sacrifices God wants are a broken spirit.
God, you will not turn away
a heart that is broken and sorry for its sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “The guilt of killing someone”: David is guilty of Uriah’s death. He asks God to free him from this heavy guilt.
> Verse 15: “Lord, open my lips”: To this day, many Christians and Jews begin their prayer with this sentence.
> Verses 16–17: For murder and adultery, the law had no sacrifice that could make up for the guilt. David can only bring himself: a broken heart that is sorry. And God does not turn it away.

---

## Build the walls of Jerusalem (Verses 18–19)

[18] Be good to Zion, as it pleases you.
Build the walls of Jerusalem.
[19] Then you will be pleased with the right sacrifices,
with burnt offerings and whole burnt offerings.
Then people will offer bulls on your altar.

> **What does this mean?**
> These last verses were probably added later, when Jerusalem had been destroyed (after 586 BC). The community took David’s prayer as their own prayer for forgiveness and a new beginning.
> Verse 19 does not contradict verse 16: When the heart is right, then God is also pleased with the sacrifices again.
> Psalm 51 shows: Even after the worst guilt, there is a way back to God. Not through excuses, but through honest confession and trust in God’s mercy.
