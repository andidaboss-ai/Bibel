# Psalm 50
### God judges his people

---

## God is coming (Verses 1–6)

[1] A psalm by Asaph.
The Mighty One, God, the LORD, speaks.
He calls the earth from where the sun rises to where it sets.
[2] Out of Zion, the most beautiful place of all,
God shines out.
[3] Our God is coming, and he will not stay silent.
A fire burns up everything in front of him.
A great storm rages around him.
[4] He calls to the heavens above and to the earth,
so that he can judge his people:
[5] “Gather to me those who are faithful to me,
those who have made a covenant with me by sacrifice.”
A covenant is a binding agreement.
[6] The heavens will announce his righteousness,
for God himself is judge.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Asaph was a musician and singer in David’s time (1 Chronicles 16:4–7). His name is on 12 psalms (Psalm 50 and Psalms 73–83).
> God appears as if at a great trial in court. Heaven and earth are the witnesses.
> It is not about the other nations, but about God’s own people. God tests whether they take the covenant seriously.

---

## I do not need your sacrifices (Verses 7–15)

[7] “Listen, my people, and I will speak.
Israel, I will speak as a witness against you.
I am God, your God.
[8] I do not blame you for your sacrifices.
Your burnt offerings are always in front of me.
[9] I do not need a bull from your barn,
or male goats from your pens.
[10] For every animal of the forest is mine,
and the cattle on 1,000 hills.
[11] I know all the birds of the mountains.
The wild animals of the field are mine.
[12] If I were hungry, I would not tell you,
for the world is mine, and everything in it.
[13] Do I eat the meat of bulls,
or drink the blood of goats?
[14] Bring thanks to God as your sacrifice.
Keep the promises you have made to the Most High.
[15] Call on me in the day of trouble.
I will rescue you, and you will honor me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God does not blame his people for not bringing sacrifices. They bring enough. But they think about the sacrifices in the wrong way: as if God needed them, as if they had to feed him.
> God does not need anything. The whole world belongs to him anyway.
> What God really wants: thankfulness, keeping your promises and trust in times of trouble.
> Verse 15 is a well-known promise: “Call on me in the day of trouble. I will rescue you.”

---

## A word to the hypocrites (Verses 16–21)

[16] But to the wicked God says,
“What right do you have to list my laws
and to talk about my covenant?
[17] For you hate correction,
and you throw my words behind you.
[18] When you saw a thief, you went along with him.
You joined in with people who commit adultery.
[19] “You let your mouth speak evil.
Your tongue makes up lies.
[20] You sit and speak against your brother.
You tell lies about your own mother’s son.
[21] You have done these things, and I stayed silent.
You thought that I was just like you.
But I will correct you,
and I will lay out the charges right in front of your eyes.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now God speaks to the hypocrites: They talk about God’s commandments, but they do not live by them. They steal, commit adultery, lie and speak badly about their own brothers and sisters.
> Verse 21: Because God stayed silent, they thought he was like them: that he did not care about evil. But that is a mistake. God’s patience does not mean that he does not care (compare 2 Peter 3:9).

---

## Whoever gives thanks honors me (Verses 22–23)

[22] “Now think about this, you who forget God,
or I will tear you to pieces,
and there will be no one to rescue you.
[23] Whoever brings thanks as a sacrifice honors me.
He prepares the way
on which I will show him God’s salvation.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A serious warning, but also an invitation to turn back.
> The psalm ends with a promise: Whoever thanks God and walks on the right way will experience God’s rescue.
