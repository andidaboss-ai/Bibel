# Psalm 19
### The heavens tell of God’s glory

---

## The language of creation (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
The heavens tell of the glory of God.
The sky shows the work of his hands.
[2] Day after day, they pour out speech.
Night after night, they show knowledge.
[3] There is no speech and no language
where their voice is not heard.
[4] Their voice has gone out through all the earth,
their words to the end of the world.
In the heavens, God has set up a tent for the sun.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 19 is one of the most beautiful psalms. It has two parts: God speaks through creation (verses 1–6) and through his word (verses 7–14).
> The sky is like a preacher without words. Every day and every night, it tells of God’s greatness.
> Verse 3 can be read in two ways: In every language of the world, people understand this message. Or: It is a speech without words, and yet it is heard everywhere.
> Verse 4: Paul quotes this verse in Romans 10:18.

---

## The sun (Verses 5–6)

[5] The sun is like a bridegroom coming out of his room.
It is like a strong man who is happy to run his race.
[6] It rises at one end of the heavens
and travels around to the other end.
Nothing is hidden from its heat.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many peoples back then worshiped the sun as a goddess. The psalm says: The sun is something God created. God has built a “tent” for it.
> The sun rises every morning like a shining bridegroom on his wedding day, or like a runner who starts his race full of joy.
> Verse 6: Nothing is hidden from the sun. This leads into the second part: Nothing stays hidden from God’s word either.

---

## God’s teaching is perfect (Verses 7–11)

[7] The LORD’s law is perfect.
It gives new life to the soul.
The LORD’s covenant can be trusted.
It makes simple people wise.
[8] The LORD’s instructions are right.
They make the heart glad.
The LORD’s command is pure.
It gives light to the eyes.
[9] The fear of the LORD is clean.
It lasts forever.
The LORD’s rulings are true.
All of them are right.
[10] They are worth more than gold,
yes, more than much pure gold.
They are sweeter than honey,
sweeter than honey dripping from the honeycomb.
[11] Also, your servant is warned by them.
Whoever keeps them receives a great reward.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the psalm changes from “God” to “the LORD,” from creation to God’s word. Through creation, people can see that God exists. Through his word, people learn who he is and how they can live with him.
> Six names for God’s word: law, covenant (word for word, “testimony”), instructions, command, fear, rulings. And each time, the psalm says what it does: It gives new life, makes wise, makes glad, gives light.
> God’s commandments are not a burden but a gift: worth more than gold, sweeter than honey.

---

## Forgive me my hidden faults (Verses 12–14)

[12] Who can see all his own mistakes?
Forgive me for the faults that are hidden from me.
[13] Also keep your servant from sins that are done on purpose.
Do not let them rule over me.
Then I will be honest.
I will be without blame and free from great wrongdoing.
[14] Let the words of my mouth and the thoughts of my heart
be pleasing to you, LORD, my rock and my redeemer.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever reads God’s word notices: I have faults that I do not even see myself. That is why the person praying asks for forgiveness also for the “hidden” faults.
> “Sins that are done on purpose”: sins that people commit knowingly and intentionally. The person praying asks God to keep him from them.
> Verse 14 is a well-known prayer. Many preachers say it before the sermon, and Jews pray it at the end of their main prayer: “Let the words of my mouth and the thoughts of my heart be pleasing to you.”
> “My redeemer”: In Hebrew, “Goel,” as in Job (Job 19:25). God is the one who buys me free and stands up for me.
