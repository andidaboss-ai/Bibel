# Psalm 146
### Do not trust in princes

---

## The LORD sets the prisoners free (Verses 1–10)

[1] Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God.
Praise the LORD, my soul!
[2] As long as I live, I will praise the LORD.
I will sing praises to my God as long as I am here.
[3] Do not put your trust in princes,
in a human being who cannot help.
[4] His spirit leaves him, and he goes back to the earth.
On that very day, his plans are lost.
[5] Happy is the one who has the God of Jacob as his help,
whose hope is in the LORD his God,
[6] who made heaven and earth,
the sea and everything in them,
who stays true forever,
[7] who gets justice for those who are oppressed,
who gives food to the hungry.
The LORD sets the prisoners free.
[8] The LORD opens the eyes of the blind.
The LORD lifts up those who are bent low.
The LORD loves the righteous.
[9] The LORD protects the foreigners.
He supports the orphans and the widows,
but he turns the way of the wicked upside down.
[10] The LORD will rule as King forever.
Zion, your God will rule through all generations.
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 146, the final “Hallelujah” of the book of Psalms begins. Psalms 146 to 150 all begin and end with “Praise Yah!” (in Hebrew “Hallelujah”).
> Verses 3–4: Even the most powerful politicians are only human. They die, and their plans die with them. That is why we should not put our final hope in them.
> Verses 7–9 show who God is: He is on the side of the oppressed, the hungry, the prisoners, the blind, the foreigners, the orphans and the widows. This is exactly how Jesus acted (compare Luke 4:18; 7:22).
> From this comes a task for us, too: Whoever praises God should also care for these people.
