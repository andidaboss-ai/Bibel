# Psalm 107
### Give thanks to the LORD, he rescues from every trouble

---

## The fifth book of Psalms

> **What does this mean?**
> With Psalm 107, the fifth and last book of Psalms begins (Psalms 107–150).
> It contains many songs of praise and thanks, the pilgrim songs (Psalms 120–134), the long Psalm 119 about God’s word, and at the end the great Hallelujah psalms (146–150).
> Psalm 107 connects to the end of Psalm 106. There the people asked: “Gather us from among the nations.” Here they give thanks: God has gathered us.

---

## Let those whom the LORD has rescued say so (Verses 1–3)

[1] Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[2] Let those whom the LORD has rescued say so,
those he has rescued from the hand of the enemy,
[3] and gathered from the lands,
from the east and from the west,
from the north and from the south.

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm invites everyone who has experienced God’s rescue to tell about it.
> God has brought his people back from every direction, back from the exile.

---

## Lost in the wilderness (Verses 4–9)

[4] They wandered in the wilderness, on a desert road.
They found no city to live in.
[5] They were hungry and thirsty.
Their souls grew weak inside them.
[6] Then they cried to the LORD in their trouble,
and he rescued them from their distress.
[7] He led them on a straight road,
so that they came to a city to live in.
[8] Let them praise the LORD for his loving kindness,
for the wonderful things he does for people!
[9] For he satisfies the longing soul.
He fills the hungry soul with good things.

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm tells about four groups of people in trouble. Each story has the same pattern: trouble – crying out to God – rescue – thanks.
> The first group: people who got lost in the wilderness. They are hungry and thirsty and cannot find the way. God leads them on a straight road.
> The refrain (verses 6 and 8) comes four times.

---

## In chains and darkness (Verses 10–16)

[10] Some sat in darkness and in the shadow of death.
They were prisoners in misery and in iron chains,
[11] because they had rebelled against the words of God,
and they had rejected the advice of the Most High.
[12] So he humbled their hearts with hard work.
They fell down, and no one helped them.
[13] Then they cried to the LORD in their trouble,
and he saved them from their distress.
[14] He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death.
He broke their chains apart.
[15] Let them praise the LORD for his loving kindness,
for the wonderful things he does for people!
[16] For he has broken the gates of bronze,
and cut through the bars of iron.

> **What does this mean?**
> The second group: prisoners in a dungeon. They got there through their own fault.
> But God helps them too when they cry to him. Even iron prison doors cannot stop God.
> This shows: Even someone who gets into trouble through his own mistakes may call to God.

---

## Sick because of sin (Verses 17–22)

[17] Fools suffered because of their disobedience
and because of their sins.
[18] They could not stand any kind of food.
They came close to the gates of death.
[19] Then they cry to the LORD in their trouble,
and he saves them from their distress.
[20] He sends his word and heals them.
He rescues them from their graves.
[21] Let them praise the LORD for his loving kindness,
for the wonderful things he does for people!
[22] Let them offer sacrifices of thanks,
and tell about what he has done with songs of joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> The third group: sick people who could not eat anymore and were close to death.
> Verse 20: “He sends his word and heals them”: God’s word has the power to heal. Christians think of Jesus, who healed sick people with his word (Matthew 8:8).
> Important: Not every sickness is a punishment for sin (compare John 9:1–3). The psalm tells about certain people who experienced it this way themselves.

---

## In a storm at sea (Verses 23–32)

[23] Some went out to sea in ships.
They did business on the great waters.
[24] They saw what the LORD does,
and his wonders in the deep sea.
[25] For he gives the command and stirs up a storm wind,
which lifts up the waves.
[26] They go up to the sky. They go down again to the depths.
Their courage melts away because of the danger.
[27] They sway back and forth and stagger like a drunk man.
They do not know what to do anymore.
[28] Then they cry to the LORD in their trouble,
and he brings them out of their distress.
[29] He makes the storm calm,
so that the waves are still.
[30] Then they are glad because it is calm,
and he brings them to the harbor they longed for.
[31] Let them praise the LORD for his loving kindness,
for the wonderful things he does for people!
[32] Let them praise him highly in the assembly of the people,
and praise him in the meeting of the elders.

> **What does this mean?**
> The fourth group: sailors in a storm. The ship is thrown up and down. The men do not know what to do anymore.
> God calms the storm and brings them safely into the harbor.
> The disciples experienced something like this with Jesus on the Sea of Galilee: He calmed the storm with one word (Mark 4:35–41).
> “The harbor they longed for”: a beautiful picture for the goal of life.

---

## God changes the world (Verses 33–43)

[33] He turns rivers into a desert,
and springs of water into thirsty ground.
[34] He turns fruitful land into a salty wasteland,
because of the evil of those who live there.
[35] He turns a desert into a pool of water,
and dry land into springs of water.
[36] He lets the hungry live there,
so that they can build a city to live in.
[37] They sow fields and plant vineyards,
and they gather a rich harvest.
[38] He also blesses them, so that they become very many.
He does not let their cattle become fewer.
[39] Then again they become fewer and are bowed down
by oppression, trouble and sorrow.
[40] He pours shame on princes.
He makes them wander in a wasteland without paths.
[41] But he lifts the needy out of their misery.
He makes their families grow like a flock.
[42] Upright people see it and are glad.
All the wicked have to shut their mouths.
[43] Whoever is wise will pay attention to these things.
They will think about the loving kindness of the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> God can change everything: fruitful land into desert, and desert into fruitful land. He brings down the proud and powerful, and he lifts up the poor (compare 1 Samuel 2:7–8; Luke 1:52).
> Verse 43: The psalm ends like a wise saying: Whoever is wise pays attention to God’s kindness in his own life and in history.
