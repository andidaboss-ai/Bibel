# Psalm 81
### If only my people would listen to me

---

## A festival song (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. On an instrument from Gath. By Asaph.
Sing out loud to God, our strength!
Shout for joy to the God of Jacob!
[2] Start a song, and bring the tambourine,
the lovely lyre, and the harp!
[3] Blow the trumpet at the New Moon,
at the full moon, on our feast day!
[4] For this is a rule for Israel,
a law of the God of Jacob.
[5] He set it up as a covenant in Joseph
when he went out over the land of Egypt.
I heard a language that I did not know:

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 81 begins as a joyful festival song. People celebrate with music and singing.
> Verse 3: Festivals were celebrated at the New Moon and the full moon. This probably means the Festival of Booths in the fall, which begins at the full moon (Leviticus 23:34).
> “Trumpet”: In Hebrew, the word is “shofar.” That is a ram’s horn that people blow like a trumpet. Jews still blow it today on the High Holy Days.
> Verse 5: “A language that I did not know”: Suddenly the singer hears a voice he does not know. It is God’s voice, which now speaks.

---

## I set you free (Verses 6–10)

[6] “I took the load off his shoulder.
His hands were set free from the basket.
[7] You called out in trouble, and I rescued you.
I answered you from the hidden place of thunder.
I tested you at the waters of Meribah.”
Selah.
[8] “Listen, my people, and I will warn you.
Israel, if only you would listen to me!
[9] There must be no strange god among you.
You must not worship any foreign god.
[10] I am the LORD, your God,
who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.
Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.

> **What does this mean?**
> God reminds them of how he set them free from Egypt. There the Israelites had to carry heavy baskets with clay and bricks as slaves (Exodus 1:14).
> “Meribah” means “quarreling.” There the people quarreled with God because they had no water (Exodus 17:7).
> Verses 9–10 remind us of the beginning of the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:2–3).
> “Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it”: like a young bird that opens its beak wide and is fed by its mother. God wants to give generously.

---

## But my people did not listen (Verses 11–16)

[11] But my people did not listen to my voice.
Israel wanted nothing to do with me.
[12] So I let them follow their own stubborn hearts,
so that they would live by their own plans.
[13] Oh, if only my people would listen to me!
If only Israel would walk in my ways!
[14] I would soon bring their enemies down
and turn my hand against their attackers.
[15] Those who hate the LORD would cower before him,
and their punishment would last forever.
[16] But I would feed Israel with the finest wheat.
I will satisfy you with honey from the rock.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God is disappointed: My people did not want to listen to me.
> Verse 12: God forces no one. He lets people go their own ways, even when those ways lead to disaster (compare Romans 1:24).
> Verse 13 is a longing sigh from God: “Oh, if only my people would listen to me!” God is not a cold judge. He longs for his people.
> Verse 16: God would give them the very best. “Honey from the rock”: Wild bees built their honeycombs in cracks in the rocks. It is a picture for surprising plenty.
