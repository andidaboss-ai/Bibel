# Psalm 137
### By the rivers of Babylon

---

## How can we sing in a foreign land? (Verses 1–6)

[1] By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat down.
Yes, we cried when we remembered Zion.
[2] On the willow trees in that land
we hung up our harps.
[3] For there, those who had taken us away as prisoners asked us for songs.
Those who tormented us demanded happy songs:
“Sing us one of the songs of Zion!”
[4] How can we sing the LORD’s song
in a foreign land?
[5] If I forget you, Jerusalem,
let my right hand forget its skill.
[6] Let my tongue stick to the roof of my mouth
if I do not remember you,
if I do not put Jerusalem above my greatest joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the year 587 BC, the Babylonians destroyed Jerusalem and the temple. Many people were taken away to Babylon by force (2 Kings 25).
> There they sit by the rivers and canals, far away from home. They cannot sing. Their harps hang silent on the trees.
> Verse 3: The winners make fun of them: “Sing us one of the songs of Zion!”
> Verses 5–6: An oath: I would rather no longer be able to play music or speak than forget Jerusalem. To this day, a glass is broken at Jewish weddings to remember the destruction of Jerusalem.
> This psalm is a song for all people who have lost their homeland: refugees, people driven out of their homes, people taken away by force.

---

## The cry for revenge (Verses 7–9)

[7] LORD, remember what the people of Edom did
on the day Jerusalem fell.
They said, “Tear it down! Tear it down to its foundations!”
[8] Daughter of Babylon, you will be destroyed!
Happy will be the one who pays you back
for what you have done to us.
[9] Happy will be the one
who takes your little children and smashes them against the rock.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are the hardest in the whole book of Psalms. They are shocking.
> We do not leave them out, because they are part of the Bible. But we need to understand what is happening here:
> The people had seen with their own eyes how the soldiers of Babylon killed their children. The Edomites, a neighboring people, cheered while it happened (compare Obadiah 10–14). Verse 9 is the cry of people who have lived through unimaginable horror. Their pain and their anger are so great that they wish the same thing on their enemies.
> Important: The person praying does not do it himself. He tells God about his hatred and leaves justice to God (compare Deuteronomy 32:35, where God says that taking revenge belongs to him).
> This verse is not a permission and not a command. Violence against children is always a serious crime. Jesus says: “Love your enemies … And pray for those who treat you badly and persecute you” (Matthew 5:44). And he puts children in the center and blesses them (Mark 10:13–16).
> Here the Bible shows honestly how deeply people can be hurt. Even these dark feelings may be spoken out loud before God, so that they do not turn into actions.
