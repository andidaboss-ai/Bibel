# Psalm 67
### Let all the peoples praise you

---

## May God be merciful to us (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. With stringed instruments. A psalm. A song.
May God be merciful to us and bless us!
May he make his face shine on us.
Selah.
[2] Then your way will be known on earth,
and your saving power among all nations.
[3] Let the peoples praise you, God.
Let all the peoples praise you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 takes up the blessing that the priests spoke over Israel: “The LORD bless you … The LORD make his face shine on you” (Numbers 6:24–26).
> But the psalm goes further: Israel is not blessed only for its own sake, but so that all nations come to know God. The blessing is meant to be passed on (compare Genesis 12:2–3).

---

## Let the nations be glad (Verses 4–5)

[4] Oh, let the nations be glad and sing for joy,
for you judge the peoples fairly
and guide the nations on earth.
Selah.
[5] Let the peoples praise you, God.
Let all the peoples praise you.

> **What does this mean?**
> God is a just judge and a good shepherd for all nations. That is a reason for the whole world to be glad.
> Verse 5 repeats verse 3 as a refrain.

---

## The earth gives its harvest (Verses 6–7)

[6] The earth has given its harvest.
God, our own God, will bless us.
[7] God will bless us.
All the ends of the earth will fear him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 67 is a song of thanks for the harvest. The harvest is a visible sign of God’s blessing.
> At the end, the whole world is there again: “All the ends of the earth” are to honor God.
> In many worship services, this short psalm is spoken as a prayer of blessing.
