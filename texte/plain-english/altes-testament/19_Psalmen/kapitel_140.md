# Psalm 140
### Protect me from violent people

---

## Tongues like snakes (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
LORD, rescue me from evil people!
Protect me from violent men,
[2] who plan evil in their hearts.
All the time they get together for war.
[3] They have made their tongues sharp like a snake.
The poison of a viper is under their lips.
A viper is a very poisonous snake.
Selah.
[4] LORD, keep me from the hands of the wicked!
Protect me from the violent men
who have decided to make me trip and fall.
[5] The proud have hidden a trap for me.
They have spread out ropes like a net beside the path.
They have set traps for me.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying is surrounded by enemies. They hurt him with words, as if with snake poison, and they set traps for him.
> Paul quotes verse 3 in his letter to the Romans to show how evil words can be (Romans 3:13).
> “Selah”: probably a musical sign for a pause (see Psalm 3).

---

## God gets justice for those who suffer (Verses 6–13)

[6] I said to the LORD, “You are my God.”
LORD, listen to my loud cry for help!
[7] LORD, the Lord, you are my strong help who saves me.
You have protected my head on the day of battle.
[8] LORD, do not give the wicked what they want!
Do not let their evil plans succeed,
or they will become proud.
Selah.
[9] As for the heads of those who surround me:
Let the trouble their own lips have caused cover them.
[10] Let burning coals fall on them.
Let them be thrown into the fire,
into muddy pits, where they never get up again.
[11] A person who speaks evil will not last on the earth.
Evil will hunt down the violent man to bring him down.
[12] I know that the LORD will stand up for those who suffer,
and he will get justice for the poor.
[13] Surely the righteous will thank your name.
The honest will live in your presence.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: God is like a helmet in battle.
> Verses 9–11: The person praying wishes that the evil falls back on those who do it. These are hard words. He puts them into God’s hands and does not use violence himself. For us today, this is true: Do not pay back evil with evil (Romans 12:17–21).
> Verse 12 is the heart of the psalm: God is on the side of the poor and the weak. He makes sure there is justice.
