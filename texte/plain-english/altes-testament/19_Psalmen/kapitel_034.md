# Psalm 34
### Taste and see that the LORD is good

---

## I will praise the LORD at all times (Verses 1–7)

[1] By David, when he pretended to be insane in front of Abimelech,
who drove him away, and he left.
I will praise the LORD at all times.
His praise will always be in my mouth.
[2] My soul will boast about the LORD.
The humble will hear it and be glad.
[3] Oh, praise the greatness of the LORD with me.
Let’s honor his name together.
[4] I looked for the LORD, and he answered me.
He rescued me from all my fears.
[5] Those who looked to him were glowing with joy.
Their faces will never be covered with shame.
[6] This poor man cried out, and the LORD heard him.
He saved him from all his troubles.
[7] The LORD’s angel sets up camp around those who fear him,
and he rescues them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The superscription reminds us of a story: David fled from Saul to the Philistine king in Gath. Out of fear, he pretended to be insane so that they would let him go (1 Samuel 21:10–15). There the king is called Achish. “Abimelech” was probably a title of the Philistine kings, like “Pharaoh” in Egypt.
> Psalm 34 is an alphabet poem.
> Verse 5: “Those who looked to him were glowing with joy”: Whoever looks to God has a face that shines with joy.
> Verse 7: God’s angel camps like an army all around those who trust him, and protects them.

---

## Taste and see (Verses 8–10)

[8] Oh, taste and see that the LORD is good.
Blessed is the man who comes to him for safety.
Blessed means: truly happy.
[9] Oh, fear the LORD, you who belong to him,
for those who fear him do not lack anything.
[10] The young lions lack food and go hungry.
But those who seek the LORD
will not lack any good thing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 is an invitation: Try it out! Just as you taste good food, you can experience God’s goodness. You have to experience it yourself.
> Christians often sing this verse at Holy Communion. 1 Peter 2:3 also takes it up.
> Verse 10: Even strong young lions sometimes have to go hungry. But whoever seeks God does not lack anything that is really good.

---

## Whoever loves life (Verses 11–16)

[11] Come, children, listen to me.
I will teach you the fear of the LORD.
[12] Who wants to live?
Who loves to have many days to see good things?
[13] Then keep your tongue from evil,
and keep your lips from telling lies.
[14] Turn away from evil, and do good.
Seek peace, and go after it.
[15] The LORD’s eyes are on the righteous.
His ears listen to their cry.
[16] The LORD’s face is against those who do evil.
He will wipe out the memory of them from the earth.

> **What does this mean?**
> David becomes a teacher. He gives practical advice for a good life:
> – Watch your words, do not lie.
> – Leave evil behind and do what is good.
> – “Seek peace, and go after it”: Peace does not come by itself. You have to work for it actively.
> Peter quotes verses 12–16 in his letter (1 Peter 3:10–12).

---

## Near to those with a broken heart (Verses 17–22)

[17] The righteous cry out, and the LORD hears.
He rescues them from all their troubles.
[18] The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart.
He saves those whose spirit is crushed.
[19] The righteous have many troubles,
but the LORD rescues him from all of them.
[20] He protects all his bones.
Not one of them is broken.
[21] Evil will kill the wicked.
Those who hate the righteous will be condemned.
[22] The LORD sets free the soul of his servants.
None of those who come to him for safety will be condemned.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18 is one of the most comforting verses in the Bible: God is especially near to those whose heart is broken. Whoever is sad, hurt or in despair is not far from God. God is especially close to them.
> Verse 19 is honest: The righteous also have “many troubles.” Faith does not protect us from suffering. But God rescues us out of it.
> Verse 20: John relates this verse to Jesus: On the cross, his legs were not broken, unlike the legs of the others who were crucified (John 19:36).
> Verse 21: Evil comes back on the one who does it.
> If your heart is broken right now and you need help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
