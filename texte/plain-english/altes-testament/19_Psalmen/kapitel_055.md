# Psalm 55
### Betrayed by a friend

---

## If only I had wings like a dove (Verses 1–8)

[1] For the Chief Musician. On stringed instruments. A contemplation by David.
Listen to my prayer, God.
Do not hide from me when I beg you for help.
[2] Pay attention to me, and answer me.
I am restless in my complaint, and I groan
[3] because of the voice of the enemy,
because the wicked oppress me.
For they bring suffering on me.
In their anger they hold a grudge against me.
[4] My heart is in great pain inside me.
The terrors of death have fallen on me.
[5] Fear and trembling have come over me.
Horror has overwhelmed me.
[6] I said, “Oh, if only I had wings like a dove!
Then I would fly away and be at rest.
[7] Look, then I would wander far away.
I would stay in the wilderness.”
Selah.
[8] “I would hurry to a shelter
from the stormy wind and the storm.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The one who prays is in fear of death. He is so worn out that all he wants is to get away.
> Verse 6: “If only I had wings like a dove!”: Just fly away, leave everything behind and find rest. Many people know this wish in hard times.
> The composer Felix Mendelssohn set this verse to music: “O for the wings of a dove.”
> If this wish turns into a longing not to be here at all anymore: Talk to someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

---

## Violence in the city (Verses 9–11)

[9] Confuse them, Lord, and mix up their language,
for I have seen violence and fighting in the city.
[10] Day and night they prowl around on its walls.
Evil and abuse are also inside the city.
[11] Forces of destruction are inside it.
Threats and lies never leave its streets.

> **What does this mean?**
> The city, which should be a place of safety, is full of violence, injustice and lies.
> Verse 9: “Mix up their language”: This reminds us of the tower of Babel (Genesis 11). When evil people can no longer understand each other, they cannot carry out their plans.

---

## It was my friend (Verses 12–15)

[12] For it was not an enemy who insulted me.
I could have put up with that.
It was not someone who hated me who rose up against me.
I could have hidden from him.
[13] But it was you, a man like me,
my companion and my close friend.
[14] We enjoyed sweet friendship together.
We walked with the crowd in God’s house.
[15] Let death come on them suddenly.
Let them go down alive into Sheol.
Sheol is the place of the dead.
For evil is among them, in their homes.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the deepest pain in this psalm: The betrayal does not come from an enemy, but from a close friend. Someone with whom he had prayed and worshiped God.
> Some think of Ahithophel, David’s advisor, who went over to Absalom’s side (2 Samuel 15:12, 31). Christians also think of Judas, who betrayed Jesus.
> Verse 15 is a curse that comes from deep pain. It reminds us of Korah and his followers, who were swallowed alive by the earth (Numbers 16:30–33). The one who prays tells God about his anger instead of taking revenge himself. Later, Jesus shows another way: He prays for his enemies (Luke 23:34).

---

## Evening, morning and noon (Verses 16–21)

[16] As for me, I will call to God,
and the LORD will save me.
[17] In the evening, in the morning and at noon,
I will cry out in my trouble,
and he will hear my voice.
[18] He has set my soul free and brought me peace
from the battle that was against me,
even though many are against me.
[19] God, who has ruled on his throne forever,
will hear and answer them.
Selah.
They never change, and they do not fear God.
[20] He raises his hands against his friends.
He has broken his covenant.
[21] His mouth was as smooth as butter,
but his heart was set on war.
His words were softer than oil,
yet they were swords ready to strike.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Praying three times a day: in the evening, in the morning and at noon. Later, Daniel also prayed like this (Daniel 6:10). Jews still pray three times a day today. In the Jewish calendar, the day begins in the evening.
> Verse 21: A fitting picture of a false friend: His words are soft as butter, but a sword is hidden behind them.

---

## Cast your burden on the LORD (Verses 22–23)

[22] Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will take care of you.
He will never let the righteous be shaken.
[23] But you, God, will bring them down
into the pit of destruction.
Violent and lying people
will not live out half their days.
But I will trust in you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22 is one of the best-known verses of comfort in the Bible: “Cast your burden on the LORD.” Peter takes it up: “Cast all your worries on him, because he cares for you” (1 Peter 5:7).
> The psalm ends with a simple sentence: “But I will trust in you.” Despite betrayal and violence, the one who prays holds on to God.
