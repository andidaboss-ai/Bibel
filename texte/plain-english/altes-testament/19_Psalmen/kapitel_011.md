# Psalm 11
### I find refuge with the LORD

---

## Fly away like a bird? (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. By David.
I take refuge in the LORD.
How can you say to me,
“Fly away like a bird to your mountain”?
[2] For look, the wicked bend their bows.
They put their arrows on the strings,
to shoot in the dark at those whose hearts are honest.
[3] If the foundations are destroyed,
what can the righteous do?

> **What does this mean?**
> Friends advise David: Run away! Get to safety! The situation is hopeless.
> Verse 3 is their reason: If the foundations of society are destroyed, if law and order no longer count, what can one single righteous person still do?
> But David answers: I do not need to flee to the mountains. My refuge is God.

---

## The LORD is on his throne (Verses 4–7)

[4] The LORD is in his holy temple.
The LORD’s throne is in heaven.
His eyes watch.
His eyes examine all people.
[5] The LORD examines the righteous,
but he hates the wicked and those who love violence with all his soul.
[6] He will rain burning coals on the wicked.
Fire, sulfur and a scorching wind will be what fills their cup.
[7] For the LORD is righteous.
He loves what is right.
Those who are honest will see his face.

> **What does this mean?**
> David’s answer to the question “What can the righteous do?”: He looks up. Even when everything on earth is shaking, God is still sitting on his throne. He sees everything.
> Verse 5: It says about God: “He hates the wicked and those who love violence with all his soul.” God cannot stand violence.
> Verse 6: “Fire, sulfur” reminds us of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:24). “Cup” is a picture of the fate that someone receives.
> Verse 7: The goal of those who are honest is to see God’s face, which means to be very close to God (compare Matthew 5:8: “Blessed are those whose hearts are pure, for they will see God”).
