# Psalm 41
### Happy is the one who thinks of the weak

---

## Whoever thinks of the poor (Verses 1–3)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by David.
Blessed is the one who thinks of the poor.
Blessed means: truly happy.
The LORD will rescue him on the day of trouble.
[2] The LORD will protect him and keep him alive.
He will be called blessed on the earth.
The LORD will not hand him over to what his enemies want.
[3] The LORD will support him on his sickbed.
He will make him well again from his bed of illness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Whoever takes care of the poor and weak will be taken care of by God when he himself gets into trouble.
> Verse 3: God is like a nurse who makes the sick person’s bed and helps him get up again.
> Jesus says, “Blessed are those who show mercy, for they will receive mercy” (Matthew 5:7).

---

## My enemies wait for me to die (Verses 4–9)

[4] I said, “LORD, have mercy on me!
Heal me, for I have sinned against you.”
[5] My enemies say evil things about me:
“When will he die? When will his name be forgotten?”
[6] When someone comes to visit me, he says things that are not true.
His heart collects evil.
When he goes outside, he tells it to others.
[7] All who hate me whisper together against me.
They imagine the worst for me.
[8] “A terrible disease has struck him,” they say.
“Now that he is lying down, he will never get up again.”
[9] Yes, even my close friend whom I trusted,
who ate bread with me,
has lifted up his heel against me.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one who prays is very sick. The people who visit him act as if they feel sorry for him. But outside, they speak badly about him and wait for him to die.
> Verse 9 is especially painful: Even his best friend has betrayed him. “Lifted up his heel”: like a horse that kicks, or like someone who kicks another person.
> Jesus quotes this verse at the Last Supper about Judas, who will betray him (John 13:18).

---

## Raise me up (Verses 10–12)

[10] But you, LORD, have mercy on me, and raise me up,
so that I can pay them back.
[11] This is how I know that you are pleased with me:
my enemy does not win over me.
[12] As for me, you hold me up because I am honest.
You set me in your presence forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “So that I can pay them back”: This sounds like revenge. If the one who prays is a king, it can also mean: so that I, as king, can make sure justice is done. Later, the Bible clearly teaches not to pay back evil with evil (Romans 12:17–21).
> Verse 12: The most important thing is to be in God’s presence forever, close to him.

---

## Praise at the end of the first book (Verse 13)

[13] Praise the LORD, the God of Israel,
from everlasting to everlasting!
Amen and amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> This verse is no longer part of Psalm 41 itself. It is the ending of the first book of the Psalms (Psalms 1–41).
> Each of the five books of the Psalms ends with praise like this (compare Psalm 72:18–19; 89:52; 106:48; 150).
> “Amen” means: “That is how it is! Let it be so!” It is a word of agreement and trust.
