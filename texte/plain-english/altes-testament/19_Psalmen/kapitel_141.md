# Psalm 141
### Let my prayer be like incense

---

## Guard my mouth (Verses 1–5)

[1] A psalm by David.
LORD, I have called to you. Come to me quickly!
Listen to my voice when I call to you!
[2] Let my prayer be set before you like incense,
and the lifting up of my hands like the evening sacrifice.
Incense is something that gives off a sweet smell when it burns.
[3] LORD, put a guard in front of my mouth!
Watch over the door of my lips!
[4] Do not turn my heart toward anything evil,
so that I do not do wicked things
with people who do wrong.
Do not let me eat their fine foods!
[5] If a righteous person strikes me, it is a kindness.
If he corrects me, it is like oil on my head.
Do not let my head refuse it.
But my prayer is always against evil deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Every evening in the temple, incense was burned and a sacrifice was offered. The sweet smell rose up to heaven. In the same way, my prayer should rise up to God. This verse is still sung in evening prayers today.
> Verse 3: A good request for every day: God, watch over what comes out of my mouth!
> Verse 4: The “fine foods” of the wicked: advantages and pleasures that people get when they take part in doing wrong.
> Verse 5: Honest criticism from a good person is a gift, even when it hurts. “Strikes” is a picture here for a hard correction. It does not allow violence.

---

## My eyes are on you (Verses 6–10)

[6] Their judges are thrown down from the sides of the rock.
Then they will hear my words, because they are well spoken.
[7] “As when someone plows and breaks up the ground,
so our bones are scattered at the entrance of Sheol.”
Sheol is the world of the dead.
[8] For my eyes are on you, LORD, the Lord.
I look for safety in you.
Do not leave my soul without protection!
[9] Keep me from the trap that they have set for me,
from the traps of those who do wrong!
[10] Let the wicked all fall into their own nets,
while I pass by.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7 are very hard to understand in Hebrew. The translations are very different from each other. It probably means: The unjust leaders will fall. The people suffer so much that they feel like bones lying scattered at the edge of the grave.
> Verse 8: Even so, the person praying looks to God.
> Verse 10: The wicked are to fall into their own traps, and the person praying walks on unharmed.
