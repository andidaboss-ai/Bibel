# Psalm 71
### Do not abandon me when I am old

---

## You have been my hope since I was young (Verses 1–8)

[1] In you, LORD, I find safety.
Never let me be put to shame!
[2] Rescue me because you are righteous, and set me free!
Turn your ear to me and save me!
[3] Be a rock of safety for me, where I can always go!
Give the command to save me,
for you are my rock and my fortress.
[4] My God, rescue me from the hand of the wicked,
from the hand of the unjust and cruel man!
[5] For you are my hope, Lord GOD.
I have trusted you since I was young.
[6] I have relied on you since before I was born.
You are the one who brought me out of my mother’s womb.
I will always praise you.
[7] To many people I am like a wonder,
but you are my strong place of safety.
[8] My mouth will be full of your praise
and of your honor all day long.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 71 is the prayer of an old person. He looks back on his whole life: Already as a baby, already as a young person, he trusted God.
> Verses 1–3 remind us of Psalm 31:1–3.
> Verse 7: “To many people I am like a wonder”: People are amazed at him, perhaps because he has been through so much. The word can also mean “a warning example.” But he knows: God is my place of safety.

---

## Do not abandon me when my strength fails (Verses 9–13)

[9] Don’t throw me away when I am old!
Don’t abandon me when my strength fails!
[10] For my enemies talk about me.
Those who lie in wait for my life make plans together.
[11] They say, “God has abandoned him.
Chase him and grab him, for no one will rescue him.”
[12] God, don’t be far from me!
My God, hurry to help me!
[13] Let my accusers be put to shame and destroyed.
Let those who want to harm me
be covered with disgrace and contempt.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9 is a moving prayer for old people: “Don’t abandon me when my strength fails!” In old age, people become weaker and need more help.
> The enemies think: He is old and weak. God has abandoned him. Now we can take everything from him.
> The Bible says: Old people have a special dignity (Leviticus 19:32). God does not abandon them (Isaiah 46:4: “Even into your old age I am he, and even when your hair is gray I will carry you”).

---

## I will always hope (Verses 14–18)

[14] But I will always hope.
I will praise you more and more.
[15] My mouth will tell about your righteousness
and about your saving help all day long,
even though I cannot measure it all.
[16] I will come with the mighty deeds of the Lord GOD.
I will speak about your righteousness, yours alone.
[17] God, you have taught me since I was young.
Until now I have told about your wonderful deeds.
[18] Yes, even when I am old and have gray hair, God, don’t abandon me,
until I have told the next generation about your strength,
and everyone who is still to come about your power.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: God’s good deeds are so many that no one can count them all.
> Verse 18: The old person still has a task: He wants to tell the young generation what God has done. Old people are important, because they can pass on what they have experienced.

---

## You will bring me up again (Verses 19–24)

[19] God, your righteousness also reaches up to the heavens.
You have done great things.
God, who is like you?
[20] You have let us see many bitter troubles,
but you will make me live again.
You will bring us up again from the depths of the earth.
[21] Make my honor greater,
and comfort me again!
[22] I will also praise you with the harp for your faithfulness, my God.
I sing praises to you with the lyre, Holy One of Israel.
[23] My lips will shout for joy!
My soul, which you have set free, sings praises to you!
[24] My tongue will also talk about your righteousness all day long,
for those who want to harm me
are put to shame and confused.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: The one praying has lived through many hard things. But he trusts that God will lift him up again, even “from the depths of the earth.” Many see in this a hope beyond death.
> “Holy One of Israel”: This name for God appears especially often in the book of Isaiah.
> The psalm ends with joy and singing: An old person who has trusted God all his life still praises him now.
