# Psalm 106
### We have sinned, but God stayed faithful

---

## His loving kindness lasts forever (Verses 1–5)

[1] Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God, the LORD.
Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good,
for his loving kindness lasts forever.
[2] Who can put into words the mighty acts of the LORD?
Who can tell all the praise he deserves?
[3] Happy are those who hold on to justice.
Happy is the one who does what is right at all times.
[4] Remember me, LORD, with the kindness you show to your people.
Come to me and save me,
[5] so that I may see the good things your chosen ones receive,
so that I may share the joy of your nation,
so that I may celebrate with the people who belong to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 106 is the counterpart to Psalm 105. Psalm 105 tells about God’s faithfulness. Psalm 106 honestly tells about the unfaithfulness of the people, and how God stayed faithful anyway.
> Verse 1: “For he is good, for his loving kindness lasts forever”: This is one of the most common calls of praise in the Bible.

---

## At the Red Sea (Verses 6–12)

[6] We have sinned, just like our ancestors.
We have done wrong. We have acted wickedly.
[7] Our ancestors in Egypt did not understand your wonders.
They did not remember how much loving kindness you had shown them.
Instead, they rebelled at the sea, at the Red Sea.
[8] But he saved them anyway, for the sake of his name,
to make his mighty power known.
[9] He scolded the Red Sea, and it dried up.
He led them through the deep waters as if through a desert.
[10] He saved them from the hand of the one who hated them.
He rescued them from the hand of the enemy.
[11] The waters covered their enemies.
Not one of them was left.
[12] Then they believed his words.
They sang his praise.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6 is an honest confession: “We have sinned, just like our ancestors.” The present generation stands in line with their ancestors and admits: We are no better.
> Already at the Red Sea, shortly after they left Egypt, the people doubted (Exodus 14:11–12). Even so, God saved them.
> Verse 12: After they were saved, they believed and sang the song of Moses (Exodus 15).

---

## Greed and envy in the wilderness (Verses 13–18)

[13] They soon forgot what he had done.
They did not wait for his advice.
[14] Instead, they gave in to their greedy cravings in the wilderness.
They tested God in the dry land.
[15] He gave them what they asked for,
but he sent emptiness into their souls.
[16] In the camp, they also became jealous of Moses,
and of Aaron, the LORD’s holy one.
[17] The earth opened up and swallowed Dathan.
It covered the group of Abiram.
[18] A fire broke out in their group.
The flames burned up the wicked.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: God gave them meat, as they demanded (Numbers 11). But their souls became “lean” and empty. Whoever lives only for his own wishes becomes poor inside.
> Verses 16–18: Dathan, Abiram and Korah rebelled against Moses and Aaron (Numbers 16).

---

## The golden calf (Verses 19–23)

[19] They made a calf at Horeb.
They worshiped an image made of melted metal.
[20] So they traded their glory
for the image of a bull that eats grass.
[21] They forgot God, their Savior,
who had done great things in Egypt,
[22] wonderful works in the land of Ham,
and awesome things at the Red Sea.
[23] So he said that he would destroy them.
But Moses, his chosen one, stood before him in the gap in the wall,
to turn his anger away, so that he would not destroy them.

> **What does this mean?**
> At Mount Horeb (Sinai), they made the golden calf (Exodus 32).
> Verse 20: How foolish: They traded the living God, their “glory,” for the image of an animal that eats grass!
> Verse 23: Moses stood up for the people, like a soldier who stands in a gap in the wall. It is a picture for praying for others (Exodus 32:11–14).

---

## They rejected the beautiful land (Verses 24–31)

[24] Yes, they rejected the pleasant land.
They did not believe his promise.
[25] Instead, they complained in their tents,
and they did not listen to the voice of the LORD.
[26] So he swore to them that he would strike them down in the wilderness,
[27] that he would strike down their descendants among the nations,
and scatter them in other lands.
[28] They also joined themselves to Baal Peor,
and they ate the sacrifices offered to the dead.
[29] So they made him angry with what they did.
The plague broke out among them.
[30] Then Phinehas stood up and carried out judgment,
and the plague was stopped.
[31] That was counted to him as righteousness,
for all generations to come.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24–27: The people refused to go into the promised land because they were afraid (Numbers 13–14). That is why they had to stay in the wilderness for 40 years.
> Verse 28: The people of Israel “ate the sacrifices offered to the dead.” This means sacrifices to dead idols, or sacrifices in rituals for the dead. This happened at Baal Peor. There, people of Israel worshiped a foreign god (Numbers 25).
> Verses 30–31: Phinehas, a priest, stepped in with violence at that time (Numbers 25:7–13). The Bible tells this as a judgment in the time of Moses. It does not allow anyone today to use violence in the name of God. Jesus clearly rejected violence in the name of God (Luke 9:54–55; Matthew 26:52).

---

## At Meribah (Verses 32–33)

[32] They also made him angry at the waters of Meribah,
so that things went badly for Moses because of them.
[33] For they rebelled against his Spirit,
and Moses spoke without thinking.

> **What does this mean?**
> At Meribah, even Moses lost his patience. He angrily struck the rock instead of speaking to it, as God had commanded. That is why Moses was not allowed to enter the promised land (Numbers 20:1–13).

---

## In the land: idols and child sacrifice (Verses 34–39)

[34] They did not destroy the peoples,
as the LORD had commanded them.
[35] Instead, they mixed with the nations
and learned to do what they did.
[36] They served their idols,
and the idols became a trap for them.
[37] Yes, they sacrificed their sons and their daughters to demons.
[38] They shed innocent blood,
the blood of their sons and their daughters,
whom they sacrificed to the idols of Canaan.
The land was made unclean with blood.
[39] So they made themselves unclean by what they did.
They were unfaithful in their actions, like a prostitute.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the land of Canaan, the people of Israel took over the religious customs of the nations around them. The most terrible thing: They even sacrificed their own children (compare 2 Kings 16:3; Jeremiah 7:31).
> The Bible condemns this in the strongest way. God does not want children to be sacrificed (compare Genesis 22).
> Verse 34: The command to destroy the peoples belongs to the account of how the land was taken in ancient times. It does not apply today. Back then, its main goal was to protect Israel from these cruel religions.
> “Unfaithful, like a prostitute”: The Bible often compares worshiping idols with being unfaithful in marriage to God.

---

## He remembered his covenant (Verses 40–46)

[40] Then the LORD’s anger burned against his people.
He hated the people who belonged to him.
[41] He handed them over to the nations.
Those who hated them ruled over them.
[42] Their enemies also oppressed them.
They were forced to bow under their power.
[43] Many times he rescued them,
but they kept rebelling in their plans,
and their sin brought them down.
[44] But he saw their trouble
when he heard their cry.
[45] He remembered his covenant for them.
He changed his mind, because his loving kindness is so great.
[46] He also made all those who had taken them captive
feel sorry for them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the time of the judges and the kings is summed up in a few words: Again and again the people turned away, were oppressed, cried to God and were rescued (compare Judges 2:11–19).
> Verses 44–45: In spite of everything, God heard their cry and remembered his covenant. His loving kindness was greater than their guilt.
> Verse 46: Even when they were captives (in exile), God made others feel sorry for them.

---

## Gather us from among the nations (Verses 47–48)

[47] Save us, LORD our God,
and gather us from among the nations,
so that we may give thanks to your holy name
and celebrate as we praise you!
[48] Praise the LORD, the God of Israel,
from everlasting to everlasting!
Let all the people say, “Amen.”
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 47: A prayer from the exile: Gather us again! Bring us home!
> Verse 48 is the end of the fourth book of Psalms (Psalm 90–106). Here all the people are called to answer with “Amen.”
> With Psalm 107, the fifth and last book of Psalms begins.
