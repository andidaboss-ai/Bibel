# Psalm 135
### Praise Yah, for the LORD is good!

---

## God has chosen Jacob (Verses 1–7)

[1] Praise Yah!
Yah is a short form of the name of God.
Praise the name of the LORD!
Praise him, you servants of the LORD,
[2] you who stand in the LORD’s house,
in the courtyards of the house of our God!
[3] Praise Yah, for the LORD is good!
Sing praises to his name, for that is pleasant!
[4] For Yah has chosen Jacob for himself,
Israel as his very own treasure.
[5] For I know that the LORD is great,
that our Lord is above all gods.
[6] The LORD has done whatever he wanted,
in heaven and on earth,
in the seas and in all the deep places.
[7] He makes the clouds rise from the ends of the earth.
He makes lightning together with the rain.
He brings the wind out of his storerooms.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 135 takes up many words from other psalms and from the books of Moses. It is like a bouquet of well-known verses of praise.
> Verse 4: “His very own treasure”: Israel is God’s special treasure (compare Exodus 19:5).
> Verse 7: God is in charge of the weather. The neighboring peoples believed in weather gods. The psalm says: There is only one who rules over clouds, lightning and wind.

---

## God has set his people free (Verses 8–14)

[8] He struck down the firstborn of Egypt,
both people and animals.
The firstborn is the oldest son.
[9] He sent signs and wonders into the middle of you, Egypt,
against Pharaoh and all his servants.
[10] He struck down many nations
and killed mighty kings:
[11] Sihon, king of the Amorites,
and Og, king of Bashan,
and all the kingdoms of Canaan.
[12] He gave their land as an inheritance,
as an inheritance to Israel, his people.
[13] Your name, LORD, lasts forever.
LORD, people will remember you through all generations.
[14] For the LORD will judge in favor of his people
and have compassion on his servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the history is told in a few words: the way out of Egypt (Exodus 12) and the way into the promised land (Numbers 21).
> The battles of that time are part of the history of Israel. They do not allow violence today. The psalm wants to show: God protected his weak people against great powers.
> Verse 14: God gets justice for his people and has compassion on them.

---

## Idols are dead, the LORD is alive (Verses 15–21)

[15] The idols of the nations are silver and gold,
made by human hands.
[16] They have mouths, but they cannot speak.
They have eyes, but they cannot see.
[17] They have ears, but they cannot hear.
And there is no breath in their mouths.
[18] Those who make them will become like them,
yes, everyone who trusts in them.
[19] People of Israel, praise the LORD!
Family of Aaron, praise the LORD!
[20] Family of Levi, praise the LORD!
You who have deep respect for the LORD, praise the LORD!
[21] Praise be to the LORD from Zion,
who lives in Jerusalem!
Praise Yah!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–18 are almost word for word like Psalm 115:4–8. Idols are dead. Whoever trusts in dead things becomes empty inside.
> Today, an “idol” can be anything that is more important to us than God: money, power, success.
> Verses 19–20: Everyone is called to praise: the whole people, the priests (Aaron), the temple servants (Levi) and all who honor God.
