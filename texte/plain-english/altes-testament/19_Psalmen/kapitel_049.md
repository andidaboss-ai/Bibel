# Psalm 49
### Riches do not save from death

---

## Listen, all you peoples (Verses 1–4)

[1] For the music director. A psalm by the sons of Korah.
Hear this, all you peoples.
Listen, all you who live in the world,
[2] both ordinary people and important people,
rich and poor together.
[3] My mouth will speak words of wisdom.
My heart will speak with understanding.
[4] I will turn my ear to a wise saying.
I will explain my riddle with the harp.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 49 is a psalm of wisdom. It speaks to all people, whether poor or rich, high or low.
> Its topic concerns everyone: What do riches mean in the face of death?

---

## No one can buy his way free (Verses 5–12)

[5] Why should I be afraid in evil days,
when the evil of those who follow at my heels surrounds me?
[6] They trust in their wealth.
They boast about how rich they are.
[7] But none of them can in any way set his brother free.
None of them can pay God a ransom for him.
A ransom is a price paid to set someone free.
[8] For setting a life free costs too much.
No payment is ever enough
[9] for someone to live on forever
and never see decay in the grave.
[10] For he sees that wise people die.
In the same way, the foolish and the senseless die.
They leave their wealth to others.
[11] Deep inside, they think that their houses will last forever,
and the places where they live will stay for all generations.
They name their lands after themselves.
[12] But a person does not last, even with all his riches.
He is like the animals that die.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rich people think they can buy anything. But there is one thing they cannot buy: life. No money in the world can stop death.
> Verse 10: Death comes to everyone, the wise and the foolish. And the riches are left behind for others.
> Verse 11: Some people name lands, buildings or companies after themselves to live on forever. But they also die.
> Jesus tells a parable about this: the parable of the rich farmer (Luke 12:16–21).

---

## Death is their shepherd (Verses 13–15)

[13] This is what happens to foolish people,
and to those who agree with what they say.
Selah.
[14] Like a flock of sheep, they are headed for Sheol (the world of the dead).
Death will be their shepherd.
In the morning, the honest will rule over them.
Their beauty will rot away in Sheol,
far from their great house.
[15] But God will set my soul free
from the power of Sheol,
for he will receive me.
Selah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 is a frightening picture: Death is like a shepherd who drives his flock into the place of the dead. This is the opposite of Psalm 23, where God is the good shepherd.
> Verse 15 is the high point of the psalm: No one can buy his way free. But God can! “He will receive me”: The Hebrew word is the same as for Enoch, whom God had “taken” (Genesis 5:24). Here we see a hope beyond death.

---

## He takes nothing with him (Verses 16–20)

[16] Do not be afraid when someone becomes rich,
when the splendor of his house grows.
[17] For when he dies, he will take nothing with him.
His splendor will not go down into the grave after him.
[18] While he lived, he called himself happy.
And people praise you when you do well for yourself.
[19] But he will go to the generation of his ancestors.
They will never see the light.
[20] A person who has riches but no understanding
is like the animals that die.

> **What does this mean?**
> A German saying goes: “The last shirt has no pockets.” The psalm says the same thing: When we die, we take nothing with us (compare 1 Timothy 6:7).
> Verse 20 repeats verse 12, but with one important difference: “no understanding.” Riches are not bad in themselves. What is bad is riches without understanding, when people forget that they will die and that they need God.
