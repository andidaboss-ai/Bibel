# Psalm 92
### A song for the Sabbath

---

## It is good to give thanks to the LORD (Verses 1–5)

[1] A psalm. A song for the Sabbath day.
It is a good thing to give thanks to the LORD,
and to sing praises to your name, Most High,
[2] to tell about your loving kindness in the morning,
and about your faithfulness every night,
[3] with the ten-stringed lute, with the harp,
and with the music of the lyre.
[4] For you, LORD, have made me glad through what you have done.
I will shout for joy about the works of your hands.
[5] How great are your works, LORD!
Your thoughts are very deep.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 92 is the only psalm that is clearly meant for the Sabbath. The Sabbath is the seventh day of the week, the day of rest (Exodus 20:8–11). Jewish people still pray this psalm on the Sabbath today.
> On the day of rest, there is time to thank God: in the morning for his kindness, in the evening for his faithfulness.
> Verse 5: God’s thoughts are so deep that we can never understand them completely.

---

## The wicked grow like grass (Verses 6–9)

[6] A person without sense does not know this,
and a fool does not understand it:
[7] Even if the wicked spring up like grass,
and all who do evil flourish,
they will be destroyed forever.
[8] But you, LORD, are high above forever.
[9] For look, your enemies, LORD,
look, your enemies will die.
All who do evil will be scattered.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here is the question again: Why do evil people do well? The answer: Their success is like grass. It grows quickly, but it also dries up quickly.
> Verse 8 stands exactly in the middle of the psalm: “But you, LORD, are high above forever.” This is the center.

---

## Like a palm tree (Verses 10–15)

[10] But you have lifted up my horn like the horn of a wild ox.
That means: You have made me strong.
I am anointed with fresh oil.
[11] My eyes have also seen my enemies.
My ears have heard about the wicked enemies who rise up against me.
[12] The righteous person will flourish like a palm tree.
He will grow like a cedar in Lebanon.
[13] They are planted in the LORD’s house.
They will flourish in the courtyards of our God.
[14] They will still bear fruit when they are old.
They will be full of sap and green,
[15] to show that the LORD is upright.
He is my rock, and there is nothing wrong in him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wicked are like grass (verse 7). The righteous are like palm trees and cedars (verse 12). Grass dries up quickly. Palm trees and cedars live very long and stay green.
> Verse 14 is a beautiful promise for old people: They can still bear fruit when they are old and be full of life. Old age is not an end. It can be a fruitful time.
