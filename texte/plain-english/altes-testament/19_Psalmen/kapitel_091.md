# Psalm 91
### Under the protection of the Most High

---

## Whoever lives in the shelter of the Most High (Verses 1–4)

[1] Whoever lives in the secret place of the Most High
will rest in the shadow of the Almighty.
[2] I will say about the LORD:
“He is my safe place and my fortress.
He is my God, and I trust in him.”
[3] For he will rescue you from the trap of the bird catcher,
and from the deadly plague.
[4] He will cover you with his feathers.
Under his wings you will find shelter.
His faithfulness is your shield and your wall.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 91 is one of the best-known psalms of protection. Many people pray it in danger, before a journey or when they are sick. Soldiers have carried it with them in wars.
> “In the secret place of the Most High”: It means a hiding place. With God, you are safe, like in a secret hiding place.
> Verse 3: A bird catcher set up nets and traps. God sets people free from traps.
> Verse 4: Here again is the tender picture of a mother bird who takes her chicks under her wings.

---

## You do not need to be afraid (Verses 5–8)

[5] You do not need to be afraid of the terror at night,
or of the arrow that flies by day,
[6] or of the plague that moves around in the dark,
or of the destruction that ruins at noon.
[7] 1,000 may fall at your side,
and 10,000 at your right hand,
but it will not come near you.
[8] You will only look with your eyes.
You will see how the wicked are paid back.

> **What does this mean?**
> There are dangers at every time of day: at night, by day, in the dark, at noon. With God, you are always safe.
> Verse 7: A strong picture: Many people fall all around you, but you yourself are kept safe.
> Important: This is not a promise that believers will never get sick or never die. Many faithful people have died of plagues. The psalm says: Nothing can separate us from God’s protection, not even death (compare Romans 8:38–39).

---

## He will put his angels in charge of you (Verses 9–13)

[9] For you have made the LORD your safe place,
and the Most High your home.
[10] No evil will happen to you.
No plague will come near your home.
[11] For he will put his angels in charge of you,
to guard you in all your ways.
[12] They will carry you in their hands,
so that you do not hit your foot against a stone.
[13] You will step on the lion and the cobra.
You will trample the young lion and the snake under your feet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12 are very well known: God sends angels who protect us. This is where the idea of a “guardian angel” comes from.
> These verses appear when Jesus is tempted: The devil quotes them and tells Jesus to throw himself down from the temple. Jesus answers: “Do not test the Lord your God” (Matthew 4:5–7). That means: We should not put God to the test.
> This shows: We must not misuse God’s promises by putting ourselves in danger on purpose.

---

## I will rescue him (Verses 14–16)

[14] “Because he has set his love on me,
I will rescue him.
I will put him in a high, safe place,
because he knows my name.
[15] He will call to me, and I will answer him.
I will be with him in trouble.
I will rescue him and honor him.
[16] I will fill him with a long life,
and I will show him my salvation.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end, God himself speaks. He gives seven promises: to rescue, to put in a safe place, to answer, to be with him, to set free, to honor, to fill with life.
> Verse 15 is especially comforting: “I will be with him in trouble.” God does not promise that there will be no trouble. But he promises to be there in the trouble.
