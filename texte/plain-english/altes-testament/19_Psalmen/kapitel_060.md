# Psalm 60
### With God we will do brave deeds

---

## You have rejected us (Verses 1–5)

[1] For the music director. To the tune of “The Lily of the Covenant.” A teaching poem by David,
when he fought against Aram Naharaim and Aram Zobah,
and Joab returned and killed 12,000 men of Edom in the Valley of Salt.
God, you have rejected us.
You have broken us down. You have been angry.
Make us whole again.
[2] You have made the land shake. You have torn it open.
Heal its cracks, for it is shaking.
[3] You have made your people see hard things.
You have made us drink wine that makes us stagger.
[4] You have given a banner to those who fear you,
so that they can raise it because of the truth.
Selah.
[5] Save us with your right hand, and answer us,
so that the people you love may be rescued.

> **What does this mean?**
> The superscription reminds us of David’s wars against the Arameans and the Edomites (2 Samuel 8; 1 Chronicles 18). 2 Samuel 8:13 and 1 Chronicles 18:12 speak of 18,000 dead. The numbers in old traditions sometimes differ from each other.
> Note: In some Bibles, the superscription takes up two verses. That is why every verse there is 2 higher.
> In the middle of the war, Israel has suffered a terrible defeat. It feels like an earthquake that tears the land apart.
> “Wine that makes us stagger”: wine that makes you drunk, so that you sway back and forth. A picture of confusion and helplessness.

---

## God has spoken (Verses 6–8)

[6] God has spoken from his holy place:
“I will win the victory.
I will divide up Shechem
and measure out the valley of Succoth.
[7] Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine.
Ephraim is the helmet that protects my head.
Judah is my scepter.
A scepter is a royal staff, a sign of a king’s power.
[8] Moab is my washbasin.
I will throw my sandal on Edom.
I shout in triumph over Philistia.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God answers with a promise: The whole land belongs to me.
> Shechem, Succoth, Gilead, Manasseh, Ephraim and Judah: These are areas of Israel. They are like God’s equipment: Ephraim is his helmet, Judah is his ruler’s staff.
> The neighboring peoples are compared with household objects: Moab is the “washbasin” in which you wash your feet. “Throwing a sandal on Edom” was a sign of claiming ownership (compare Ruth 4:7).
> This is the picture language of that time. It means: God is the Lord over all peoples.

---

## Human help is worth nothing (Verses 9–12)

[9] Who will bring me into the strong city?
Who has led me to Edom?
[10] God, haven’t you rejected us?
You do not go out with our armies, God.
[11] Give us help against the enemy,
for human help is worth nothing.
[12] With God we will do brave deeds,
for he is the one who will trample our enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The strong city”: probably Petra or Bozrah, the strongly fortified capital of Edom.
> Verse 11: “Human help is worth nothing”: Without God, even the best army does not help.
> Verse 12: With God, courage is possible. Verses 5–12 appear again almost exactly in Psalm 108:6–13.
