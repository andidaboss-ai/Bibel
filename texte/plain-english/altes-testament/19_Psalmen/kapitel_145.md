# Psalm 145
### I will honor you, my God and King

---

## No one can measure God’s greatness (Verses 1–7)

[1] A psalm of praise by David.
I will honor you highly, my God, the King!
I will praise your name forever and ever.
[2] Every day I will praise you.
I will praise your name highly forever and ever.
[3] The LORD is great, and he deserves great praise!
No one can measure how great he is.
[4] One generation will praise your works to the next.
They will tell about your mighty deeds.
[5] I will think deeply about the glorious splendor of your majesty,
about your wonderful works.
[6] People will speak about the power of your awesome deeds.
I will tell about your greatness.
[7] They will gladly tell everyone how very good you are,
and they will sing about your righteousness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 145 is the last alphabet poem in the book of Psalms: In Hebrew, each verse begins with the next letter of the alphabet.
> It is the only psalm that is called a “psalm of praise” (in Hebrew “tehillah”) in its heading. The Hebrew name of the whole book of Psalms comes from this word: “Tehillim”, songs of praise.
> In Judaism, Psalm 145 is prayed three times a day. The Talmud says: Whoever prays it every day belongs to the world to come.
> Verse 4: Faith is passed on from generation to generation: from parents to children, from grandparents to grandchildren.

## The LORD is good to all (Verses 8–13)

[8] The LORD is kind and merciful.
He is slow to become angry and rich in loving kindness.
[9] The LORD is good to all.
His tender mercy is over everything he has made.
[10] Everything you have made will thank you, LORD.
Your holy people will praise you highly.
[11] They will speak about the glory of your kingdom
and talk about your power,
[12] to let all people know about his mighty deeds
and the glorious splendor of his kingdom.
[13] Your kingdom is a kingdom that lasts forever.
Your rule lasts through all generations.
The LORD is faithful in all his words,
and loving in all his deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 is the great statement that God himself said to Moses (Exodus 34:6). It comes up again and again in the Bible.
> Verse 9: “Good to all”: God’s goodness is not only for one people, but for all people and for all of creation.
> Verse 13: In the standard Hebrew text, the verse with the letter “Nun” is missing. It has been kept in an old handwritten copy from Qumran and in the Greek translation: “The LORD is faithful in all his words …” The World English Bible, which this book is based on, includes it in verse 13. So the alphabet is complete.

## The LORD lifts up those who are bent low (Verses 14–21)

[14] The LORD holds up all who fall,
and lifts up all who are bent low.
[15] The eyes of all look to you and wait,
and you give them their food at the right time.
[16] You open your hand
and satisfy the wishes of every living thing.
[17] The LORD is righteous in all his ways
and kind in all his works.
[18] The LORD is near to all who call on him,
to all who call on him in truth.
[19] He gives those who have deep respect for him what they wish for.
He also hears their cry and saves them.
[20] The LORD protects all who love him,
but he will destroy all the wicked.
[21] My mouth will speak the praise of the LORD.
Let all people praise his holy name
forever and ever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: God is there for those who fall and who are bent low. He helps them get up again.
> Verses 15–16 are a well-known prayer before meals: “The eyes of all look to you and wait, and you give them their food at the right time. You open your hand and satisfy the wishes of every living thing.”
> Verse 18: God is near. You do not have to search far for him. An honest prayer is enough.
> Verse 20: God will put an end to evil. That is a comfort for the victims of injustice.
> The word “all” appears very often in this psalm. God’s goodness reaches far.
