# Psalm 132
### A home for the LORD

---

## David’s oath (Verses 1–10)

[1] A song of ascents.
LORD, remember David
and all his suffering,
[2] how he made an oath to the LORD
and a solemn promise to the Mighty One of Jacob:
[3] “I will surely not go into my house
and not get into my bed.
[4] I will not let my eyes sleep
or let my eyelids rest,
[5] until I find a place for the LORD,
a home for the Mighty One of Jacob.”
[6] Look, we heard about it in Ephrathah.
We found it in the fields of Jaar.
[7] “Let’s go into his home.
Let’s worship at the stool for his feet.”
[8] Get up, LORD, and come to your resting place,
you and the ark of your strength!
[9] Let your priests be dressed in righteousness.
Let your holy people shout for joy!
[10] For the sake of your servant David,
do not turn away from your anointed one!

> **What does this mean?**
> The psalm remembers how King David brought the ark of the covenant to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6). The ark of the covenant was a chest with the stone tablets of the law in it. It was the sign that God is with his people.
> David did not want to rest until God had a place in Jerusalem.
> Verse 6: Ephrathah is an area near Bethlehem. “Jaar” probably means Kiriath Jearim, where the ark stood for a long time (1 Samuel 7:1–2).
> Verse 7: The “stool for his feet” is the ark of the covenant. People imagined: God sits on his throne, unseen, above it, and the ark is like his footstool.
> Verse 8 has almost the same words that Solomon prayed when the temple was dedicated (2 Chronicles 6:41–42).

---

## God’s oath for David and Zion (Verses 11–18)

[11] The LORD has made an oath to David in truth.
He will not turn away from it:
“I will put one of your own children
on your throne.
[12] If your children keep my covenant
and my teaching that I will teach them,
then their children will also sit on your throne
forever.”
[13] For the LORD has chosen Zion.
He wanted it as his home.
[14] “This is my resting place forever.
I will live here, because I have wanted it.
[15] I will richly bless her food.
I will give her poor people enough bread to eat.
[16] I will also dress her priests in salvation.
Her holy people will shout loudly for joy.
[17] There I will make a horn grow for David.
I have set up a lamp for my anointed one.
[18] I will dress his enemies in shame,
but on him, his crown will shine.”

> **What does this mean?**
> First David made an oath (verse 2). Now God makes an oath (verse 11). God’s promise is even greater: David’s family is to rule forever (compare 2 Samuel 7).
> Verse 15: God cares in a special way for the poor. They are to have enough to eat.
> Verse 17: “I will make a horn grow for David”: A new, strong king is to grow out of David’s family. The “lamp” is a picture that David’s family will not die out.
> Jews are still waiting today for the Messiah from David’s family. Christians believe that this promise came true in Jesus, who is called “Son of David” (Luke 1:32–33).
