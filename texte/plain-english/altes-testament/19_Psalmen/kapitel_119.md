# Psalm 119
### The great song about God’s word

---

## Aleph: Happy are those who live by God’s teaching (Verses 1–8)

[1] Blessed are those whose ways are without blame,
who live by the law of the LORD.
Blessed means: truly happy.
[2] Blessed are those who keep his rules,
who look for him with their whole heart.
[3] Yes, they do nothing wrong.
They walk in his ways.
[4] You have given us your instructions,
so that we obey them fully.
[5] Oh, I wish my ways were firm,
so that I would obey your rules!
[6] Then I would not be put to shame
when I look at all your commandments.
[7] I will thank you with an honest heart
when I learn your right judgments.
[8] I will keep your rules.
Do not leave me all alone!

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 119 is the longest psalm and the longest chapter in the Bible: 176 verses.
> It is a skillfully made alphabet poem. It has 22 sections, as many as there are letters in the Hebrew alphabet. In each section, all eight verses begin with the same letter: in the first section with Aleph, in the second with Beth, and so on. The headings name these letters.
> Almost every verse speaks about God’s word. In Hebrew, eight different words are used for it. In this book, they appear as words like law (in Hebrew “Torah”, which means teaching), rules, teachings, instructions, commandments, rulings, word and promise. They all mean the same thing: what God has told us so that we can live well.
> This psalm is not a dry law book. It is a love song to God’s word and to God himself. The person praying is happy about God’s word. He longs for it. He looks for comfort in it, even in suffering and persecution.
> Verses 1–3 remind us of Psalm 1: Happy is the person who lives by God’s teaching.

## Beth: How can a young person keep their way pure? (Verses 9–16)

[9] How can a young man keep his way pure?
By living by your word.
[10] I have looked for you with my whole heart.
Do not let me wander away from your commandments!
[11] I have hidden your word in my heart,
so that I do not sin against you.
[12] Praise be to you, LORD!
Teach me your rules!
[13] With my lips I have told about
all the rulings that you have spoken.
[14] I have been glad about the way of your teachings,
as glad as about great riches.
[15] I will think deeply about your instructions
and pay attention to your ways.
[16] I will enjoy your rules.
I will not forget your word.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9 is a question that many young people think about: How do I live the right way? The answer: by God’s word.
> Verse 11: “I have hidden your word in my heart”: Whoever knows God’s words by heart and carries them in their heart has them along in hard situations.
> Verse 14: For the person praying, God’s word is as valuable as great riches.

## Gimel: Open my eyes (Verses 17–24)

[17] Do good to your servant!
Then I will live and obey your word.
[18] Open my eyes,
so that I can see the wonderful things in your law!
[19] I am a stranger on the earth.
Do not hide your commandments from me!
[20] My soul is worn out with longing
for your rulings at all times.
[21] You have scolded the proud, who are cursed,
who wander away from your commandments.
[22] Take shame and scorn away from me,
because I have kept your rules.
[23] Even when princes sit together and say bad things about me,
your servant will think deeply about your rules.
[24] Yes, your rules are my joy.
They are my advisers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A beautiful prayer before reading the Bible: “Open my eyes”. We need God’s help to understand the depth of his word.
> Verse 19: “I am a stranger on the earth”: Life is like a journey in a foreign country. God’s commandments are like a map.
> Verse 24: God’s words are like good advisers.

---

## Daleth: My soul lies in the dust (Verses 25–32)

[25] My soul lies low in the dust.
Give me new life, as your word promises!
[26] I told you about my ways, and you answered me.
Teach me your rules!
[27] Help me understand the teaching of your instructions!
Then I will think deeply about your wonderful works.
[28] My soul is tired with sorrow.
Make me strong, as your word promises!
[29] Keep me away from the way of lies!
Be kind and give me your law!
[30] I have chosen the way of truth.
I have put your rulings in front of my eyes.
[31] I hold on tight to your rules, LORD.
Do not let me be put to shame!
[32] I run on the path of your commandments,
because you have set my heart free.

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying is discouraged and tired. He asks: Give me new life, make me strong through your word.
> Verse 32: “You have set my heart free”: God’s commandments are not chains. They set us free. Whoever loves them is glad to run on this path.

---

## He: Teach me, LORD (Verses 33–40)

[33] Teach me, LORD, the way of your rules!
I will keep them to the end.
[34] Give me understanding, and I will keep your law.
Yes, I will obey it with my whole heart.
[35] Lead me on the path of your commandments,
because I find joy in them.
[36] Turn my heart toward your rules,
and not toward selfish gain!
[37] Turn my eyes away from looking at worthless things!
Give me new life in your ways!
[38] Keep your promise to your servant,
so that people will have deep respect for you!
[39] Take away the shame that I am afraid of,
because your rulings are good.
[40] Look, I long for your instructions!
Give me new life because you are righteous!

> **What does this mean?**
> This section is made up almost only of requests: Teach me, give me understanding, lead me, turn my heart, turn my eyes away.
> Verse 36: A request for today, too: My heart should not hang on money, but on God.
> Verse 37: “Turn my eyes away from looking at worthless things”: In a world full of pictures and screens, this is a very fitting prayer.

---

## Vav: I will speak in front of kings (Verses 41–48)

[41] LORD, let your loving kindness come to me, too.
Let your saving help come to me, as your word promises.
[42] Then I will have an answer for the one who insults me,
because I trust in your word.
[43] Do not take the word of truth away from my mouth,
because I put my hope in your rulings.
[44] So I will obey your law all the time,
forever and ever.
[45] I will walk in freedom,
because I have looked for your instructions.
[46] I will also speak about your rules in front of kings,
and I will not be put to shame.
[47] I will enjoy your commandments,
because I love them.
[48] I reach out my hands for your commandments, which I love.
I will think deeply about your rules.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: “I will walk in freedom”: In Hebrew, it says “in a wide space”. God’s commandments do not lead into a narrow place, but into freedom.
> Verse 46: The person praying has the courage to speak about God even in front of powerful people.
