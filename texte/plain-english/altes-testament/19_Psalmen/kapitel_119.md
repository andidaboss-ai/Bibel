# Psalm 119
### The great song about God’s word

---

## Aleph: Happy are those who live by God’s teaching (Verses 1–8)

[1] Blessed are those whose ways are without blame,
who live by the law of the LORD.
Blessed means: truly happy.
[2] Blessed are those who keep his rules,
who look for him with their whole heart.
[3] Yes, they do nothing wrong.
They walk in his ways.
[4] You have given us your instructions,
so that we obey them fully.
[5] Oh, I wish my ways were firm,
so that I would obey your rules!
[6] Then I would not be put to shame
when I look at all your commandments.
[7] I will thank you with an honest heart
when I learn your right judgments.
[8] I will keep your rules.
Do not leave me all alone!

> **What does this mean?**
> Psalm 119 is the longest psalm and the longest chapter in the Bible: 176 verses.
> It is a skillfully made alphabet poem. It has 22 sections, as many as there are letters in the Hebrew alphabet. In each section, all eight verses begin with the same letter: in the first section with Aleph, in the second with Beth, and so on. The headings name these letters.
> Almost every verse speaks about God’s word. In Hebrew, eight different words are used for it. In this book, they appear as words like law (in Hebrew “Torah,” which means teaching), rules, teachings, instructions, commandments, rulings, word and promise. They all mean the same thing: what God has told us so that we can live well.
> This psalm is not a dry law book. It is a love song to God’s word and to God himself. The person praying is happy about God’s word. He longs for it. He looks for comfort in it, even in suffering and persecution.
> Verses 1–3 remind us of Psalm 1: Happy is the person who lives by God’s teaching.

## Beth: How can a young person keep their way pure? (Verses 9–16)

[9] How can a young man keep his way pure?
By living by your word.
[10] I have looked for you with my whole heart.
Do not let me wander away from your commandments!
[11] I have hidden your word in my heart,
so that I do not sin against you.
[12] Praise be to you, LORD!
Teach me your rules!
[13] With my lips I have told about
all the rulings that you have spoken.
[14] I have been glad about the way of your teachings,
as glad as about great riches.
[15] I will think deeply about your instructions
and pay attention to your ways.
[16] I will enjoy your rules.
I will not forget your word.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9 is a question that many young people think about: How do I live the right way? The answer: by God’s word.
> Verse 11: “I have hidden your word in my heart”: Whoever knows God’s words by heart and carries them in their heart has them along in hard situations.
> Verse 14: For the person praying, God’s word is as valuable as great riches.

## Gimel: Open my eyes (Verses 17–24)

[17] Do good to your servant!
Then I will live and obey your word.
[18] Open my eyes,
so that I can see the wonderful things in your law!
[19] I am a stranger on the earth.
Do not hide your commandments from me!
[20] My soul is worn out with longing
for your rulings at all times.
[21] You have scolded the proud, who are cursed,
who wander away from your commandments.
[22] Take shame and scorn away from me,
because I have kept your rules.
[23] Even when princes sit together and say bad things about me,
your servant will think deeply about your rules.
[24] Yes, your rules are my joy.
They are my advisers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A beautiful prayer before reading the Bible: “Open my eyes.” We need God’s help to understand the depth of his word.
> Verse 19: “I am a stranger on the earth”: Life is like a journey in a foreign country. God’s commandments are like a map.
> Verse 24: God’s words are like good advisers.

## Daleth: My soul lies in the dust (Verses 25–32)

[25] My soul lies low in the dust.
Give me new life, as your word promises!
[26] I told you about my ways, and you answered me.
Teach me your rules!
[27] Help me understand the teaching of your instructions!
Then I will think deeply about your wonderful works.
[28] My soul is tired with sorrow.
Make me strong, as your word promises!
[29] Keep me away from the way of lies!
Be kind and give me your law!
[30] I have chosen the way of truth.
I have put your rulings in front of my eyes.
[31] I hold on tight to your rules, LORD.
Do not let me be put to shame!
[32] I run on the path of your commandments,
because you have set my heart free.

> **What does this mean?**
> The person praying is discouraged and tired. He asks: Give me new life, make me strong through your word.
> Verse 32: “You have set my heart free”: God’s commandments are not chains. They set us free. Whoever loves them is glad to run on this path.

## He: Teach me, LORD (Verses 33–40)

[33] Teach me, LORD, the way of your rules!
I will keep them to the end.
[34] Give me understanding, and I will keep your law.
Yes, I will obey it with my whole heart.
[35] Lead me on the path of your commandments,
because I find joy in them.
[36] Turn my heart toward your rules,
and not toward selfish gain!
[37] Turn my eyes away from looking at worthless things!
Give me new life in your ways!
[38] Keep your promise to your servant,
so that people will have deep respect for you!
[39] Take away the shame that I am afraid of,
because your rulings are good.
[40] Look, I long for your instructions!
Give me new life because you are righteous!

> **What does this mean?**
> This section is made up almost only of requests: Teach me, give me understanding, lead me, turn my heart, turn my eyes away.
> Verse 36: A request for today, too: My heart should not hang on money, but on God.
> Verse 37: “Turn my eyes away from looking at worthless things”: In a world full of pictures and screens, this is a very fitting prayer.

## Vav: I will speak in front of kings (Verses 41–48)

[41] LORD, let your loving kindness come to me, too.
Let your saving help come to me, as your word promises.
[42] Then I will have an answer for the one who insults me,
because I trust in your word.
[43] Do not take the word of truth away from my mouth,
because I put my hope in your rulings.
[44] So I will obey your law all the time,
forever and ever.
[45] I will walk in freedom,
because I have looked for your instructions.
[46] I will also speak about your rules in front of kings,
and I will not be put to shame.
[47] I will enjoy your commandments,
because I love them.
[48] I reach out my hands for your commandments, which I love.
I will think deeply about your rules.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: “I will walk in freedom”: In Hebrew, it says “in a wide space.” God’s commandments do not lead into a narrow place, but into freedom.
> Verse 46: The person praying has the courage to speak about God even in front of powerful people.

## Zayin: Your word is my comfort in my suffering (Verses 49–56)

[49] Remember your word to your servant,
because you have given me hope.
[50] This is my comfort in my suffering:
Your word has given me new life.
[51] The proud make fun of me very much,
but I do not turn away from your law.
[52] I remember your rulings from long ago, LORD,
and I have found comfort.
[53] Anger has taken hold of me
because of the wicked people who turn away from your law.
[54] Your rules have been my songs
in the house where I live.
[55] I have remembered your name in the night, LORD,
and I obey your law.
[56] This is my way:
I keep your instructions.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 50: In hard times, God’s word is a comfort that gives new life.
> Verse 54: For the person praying, God’s commandments are like songs. He likes to sing them in the place where he lives.

## Heth: The LORD is my share (Verses 57–64)

[57] The LORD is my share.
I promised to obey your words.
[58] I looked for your kindness with my whole heart.
Be merciful to me, as your word promises!
[59] I thought about my ways,
and I turned my steps toward your rules.
[60] I will hurry and not wait
to obey your commandments.
[61] The ropes of the wicked tie me up,
but I will not forget your law.
[62] At midnight I will get up to thank you
for your right rulings.
[63] I am a friend of all who have deep respect for you,
of those who keep your instructions.
[64] The earth is full of your loving kindness, LORD.
Teach me your rules!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 57: “The LORD is my share”: God himself is the greatest treasure (compare Psalm 16:5).
> Verse 59: The person praying has thought about his life and has turned back to God.
> Verse 62: He even gets up in the middle of the night to thank God.
> Verse 63: Whoever loves God finds friends in all who love him too.

## Teth: It was good for me that I suffered (Verses 65–72)

[65] You have treated your servant well,
as your word promised, LORD.
[66] Teach me good judgment and knowledge,
because I believe in your commandments.
[67] Before I suffered, I went the wrong way.
But now I keep your word.
[68] You are good, and you do good.
Teach me your rules!
[69] The proud have spread lies about me.
But I will keep your instructions with my whole heart.
[70] Their heart is as dull as fat.
But I find joy in your law.
[71] It was good for me that I suffered,
so that I would learn your rules.
[72] The law that you have spoken is worth more to me
than thousands of pieces of gold and silver.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 67 and 71: The person praying tells how hard times brought him closer to God. This is his personal experience. It does not mean that God sends every suffering, or that every suffering is good.
> Verse 68: “You are good, and you do good”: a simple, strong statement of faith.
> Verse 72: God’s word is worth more than a great treasure.

## Yodh: Your hands have made me (Verses 73–80)

[73] Your hands have made me and shaped me.
Give me understanding, so that I can learn your commandments!
[74] Those who have deep respect for you will see me and be glad,
because I have put my hope in your word.
[75] LORD, I know that your judgments are right,
and that you have let me suffer because you are faithful.
[76] Please let your loving kindness comfort me,
as you promised your servant.
[77] Let your tender mercy come to me, so that I may live,
because your law is my joy.
[78] Let the proud be put to shame,
because they have brought me down unfairly.
I will think deeply about your instructions.
[79] Let those who have deep respect for you turn to me.
They will know your rules.
[80] Let my heart be without blame in keeping your rules,
so that I am not put to shame.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 73: God created me. That is why he also knows best how I should live.
> Verse 75: Even in hard times, the person praying trusts: God means well with me.

## Kaph: Like a wineskin in the smoke (Verses 81–88)

[81] My soul is worn out with longing for your saving help.
I hope in your word.
[82] My eyes grow tired looking for your word.
I say, “When will you comfort me?”
[83] I have become like a wineskin in the smoke.
I do not forget your rules.
[84] How many days does your servant have left?
When will you judge those who chase after me to hurt me?
[85] The proud have dug pits for me.
That is against your law.
[86] All your commandments can be trusted.
They chase after me unfairly. Help me!
[87] They almost wiped me off the earth,
but I did not turn away from your instructions.
[88] Keep me alive because of your loving kindness.
Then I will obey the rules that you have spoken.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the darkest section of the psalm. The person praying is waiting and longing for help. He is being persecuted and is almost at the end.
> Verse 83: “Like a wineskin in the smoke”: A wineskin is a bag made of leather that holds wine. In the smoke of the huts, these bags became black, dry and cracked. That is how the person praying feels: dried out and old.
> Even so, he holds on to God’s word.

## Lamedh: Your word stands firm in heaven (Verses 89–96)

[89] LORD, your word stands firm in heaven forever.
[90] Your faithfulness lasts for all generations.
You have set the earth in its place, and it stays.
[91] Your laws remain to this day,
because all things serve you.
[92] If your law had not been my joy,
I would have died in my suffering.
[93] I will never forget your instructions,
because with them you have given me new life.
[94] I belong to you. Save me,
because I have looked for your instructions.
[95] The wicked have waited for me, to destroy me.
I will pay attention to your rules.
[96] I have seen that even the most perfect things have a limit,
but your commands have no limits.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the dark section comes the turning point: God’s word stands firm like heaven. It does not change.
> Verse 92: God’s word has saved the life of the person praying. Without this support, he would have broken down.
> Verse 94: “I belong to you”: a short, deep prayer.
> Verse 96: Everything human has limits, even the very best. God’s word has no end.

## Mem: Oh, how I love your law! (Verses 97–104)

[97] Oh, how I love your law!
I think about it all day long.
[98] Your commandments make me wiser than my enemies,
because your commandments are always with me.
[99] I have more understanding than all my teachers,
because I think deeply about your teachings.
[100] I understand more than the old people,
because I have kept your instructions.
[101] I have kept my feet away from every evil way,
so that I can keep your word.
[102] I have not turned away from your rulings,
because you have taught me.
[103] How sweet your promises taste to me,
sweeter than honey in my mouth!
[104] Through your instructions I gain understanding.
That is why I hate every false way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 97 is the high point of love: “Oh, how I love your law!”
> Verses 99–100: God’s word makes people wiser than the smartest teachers. Wisdom does not depend only on education or age, but on God’s word.
> Verse 103: God’s word tastes sweet like honey (compare Psalm 19:10).

## Nun: Your word is a lamp to my feet (Verses 105–112)

[105] Your word is a lamp for my feet
and a light for my path.
[106] I have made a promise with an oath, and I have made it firm:
I will obey your right rulings.
[107] I am suffering very much.
Give me new life, LORD, as your word promises!
[108] Please accept the offerings that my mouth gives you freely, LORD.
Teach me your rulings!
[109] My life is always in my hand,
but I will not forget your law.
[110] The wicked have set a trap for me,
but I have not wandered away from your instructions.
[111] I have taken your teachings as my inheritance forever,
because they are the joy of my heart.
[112] I have set my heart on doing what your rules say,
forever, to the very end.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 105 is one of the best-known verses in the Bible. God’s word is like a lamp in the night. Back then, people had small oil lamps. They did not shine very far, but far enough for the next step. In the same way, God’s word often does not show the whole way, but it shows the next step.
> Verse 109: “My life is always in my hand”: This means: My life is always in danger.
> Verse 108: “The offerings that my mouth gives you freely”: Prayers and songs are like offerings to God.

## Samekh: You are my hiding place and my shield (Verses 113–120)

[113] I hate people whose hearts are divided,
but I love your law.
[114] You are my hiding place and my shield.
I hope in your word.
[115] Go away from me, you people who do evil,
so that I can keep the commandments of my God!
[116] Hold me up, as your word promises, so that I may live!
Do not let me be put to shame in my hope!
[117] Hold me up, and I will be safe.
I will always pay attention to your rules.
[118] You reject all who wander away from your rules,
because their lies are useless.
[119] You throw away all the wicked people of the earth like dross.
That is why I love your teachings.
[120] My body trembles with fear of you.
I am afraid of your judgments.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 113: “People whose hearts are divided”: people who think one way at one time and another way at another time. They are half for God and half against him.
> Verse 114: God is a safe hiding place.
> Verse 119: “Dross”: the worthless leftovers that remain when metal is melted. They are thrown away.

## Ayin: It is time to act (Verses 121–128)

[121] I have done what is fair and right.
Do not leave me to the people who oppress me!
Oppress means: to treat people cruelly and unfairly.
[122] Make sure that your servant is well!
Do not let the proud oppress me!
[123] My eyes grow tired looking for your saving help
and for your right word.
[124] Treat your servant with your loving kindness!
Teach me your rules!
[125] I am your servant. Give me understanding,
so that I may know your teachings!
[126] It is time to act, LORD,
because they break your law.
[127] That is why I love your commandments more than gold,
yes, more than pure gold.
[128] That is why I think that all your instructions are right.
I hate every false way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 126: The person praying calls out to God: Now it is time for you to step in! People trample your law.
> Verse 127: Just when others look down on God’s commandments, the person praying loves them all the more.

## Pe: When your words come in, there is light (Verses 129–136)

[129] Your teachings are wonderful.
That is why my soul keeps them.
[130] When your words come in, they give light.
They give understanding to simple people.
[131] I opened my mouth wide and gasped for air,
because I longed for your commandments.
[132] Turn to me and be merciful to me,
as you always are to those who love your name!
[133] Make my steps firm in your word!
Do not let any evil rule over me!
[134] Set me free from people who oppress me.
Then I will keep your instructions.
[135] Let your face shine on your servant!
Teach me your rules!
[136] Streams of tears run down from my eyes,
because people do not keep your law.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 130: When God’s word “comes in” to a person, it becomes bright, like when you open a door and light comes in. Even simple people become wise through it.
> Verse 131: Like a person gasping for air, the person praying longs for God’s word.
> Verse 136: The person praying cries because others ignore God’s teaching. He is not proud. He is sad.

[137] You are righteous, LORD.
Your judgments are right.
[138] You have given your rules in righteousness.
They can be fully trusted.
[139] My zeal wears me out,

Zeal means: a strong, burning love for something.
[140] Your promises have been tested very carefully,
and your servant loves them.
[141] I am small and looked down on.
I do not forget your instructions.
[142] Your righteousness lasts forever.
Your law is truth.
[143] Trouble and fear have taken hold of me.
Your commandments are my joy.
[144] Your teachings are right forever.
Give me understanding, so that I may live!

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 140: God’s promises are like metal that has been tested in fire: real and pure.
> Verse 141: “I am small and looked down on”: To the world, the person praying is not important. But he has a great treasure: God’s word.
> Verse 143: Even in the middle of trouble and fear, God’s word can give joy.

## Shin: Those who love your law have great peace (Verses 161–168)

[161] Princes have chased after me for no reason,
but my heart stands in awe of your words.
[162] I am glad about your word,
like someone who finds a great treasure taken in war.
[163] I hate lies, and I find them disgusting.
I love your law.
[164] Seven times a day I praise you,
because of your right rulings.
[165] Those who love your law have great peace.
Nothing makes them stumble.
[166] I have hoped for your saving help, LORD.
I have done what your commandments say.
[167] My soul has kept your teachings.
I love them very much.
[168] I have obeyed your instructions and your teachings,
because you see all my ways.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the World English Bible, this section is called “Sin and Shin,” because the Hebrew letter has two ways of being pronounced.
> Verse 162: Discovering God’s word is like finding a treasure.
> Verse 164: “Seven times a day”: again and again, all the time. The seven prayer times in many monasteries are based on this.
> Verse 165: “Those who love your law have great peace”: a beautiful promise.

## Tav: I have wandered like a lost sheep (Verses 169–176)

[169] Let my cry come before you, LORD!
Give me understanding, as your word promises!
[170] Let my prayer for help come before you!
Rescue me, as you promised!
[171] Let praise pour out from my lips,
because you teach me your rules.
[172] Let my tongue sing about your word,
because all your commandments are right.
[173] Let your hand be ready to help me,
because I have chosen your instructions.
[174] I have longed for your saving help, LORD.
Your law is my joy.
[175] Let my soul live, so that I may praise you!
Let your rulings help me!
[176] I have wandered like a lost sheep.
Look for your servant,
because I do not forget your commandments.

> **What does this mean?**
> The last section sums everything up: crying out, asking for help, praising, singing.
> Verse 176 is an amazing ending. After 175 verses full of love for God’s word, the person praying admits: “I have wandered like a lost sheep. Look for your servant.”
> This shows: Even someone who loves God’s word is not perfect. In the end, all of us depend on God looking for us and finding us.
> Jesus tells the parable of the good shepherd who looks for the lost sheep until he finds it (Luke 15:4–7).
