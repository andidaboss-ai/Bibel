# Psalm 79
### Jerusalem lies in ruins

---

## The nations have broken into your inheritance (Verses 1–4)

[1] A psalm by Asaph.
God, the nations have come into your inheritance.
They have made your holy temple unclean.
They have turned Jerusalem into piles of ruins.
[2] They have given the dead bodies of your servants
as food to the birds of the sky.
They have given the flesh of your faithful people to the animals of the earth.
[3] They have poured out their blood like water all around Jerusalem.
There was no one to bury them.
[4] We have become a joke to our neighbors.
The people around us mock us and laugh at us.

> **What does this mean?**
> Like Psalm 74, Psalm 79 is a lament over the destruction of Jerusalem, probably by the Babylonians in 586 BC.
> The description is shocking: The temple has been made unclean, the city lies in ruins, the dead lie unburied in the streets. For the people back then, that was the most terrible thing of all.
> To this day, laments like this are a prayer for people who live through war and destruction.

---

## How long, LORD? (Verses 5–7)

[5] How long, LORD?
Will you be angry forever?
Will your jealousy burn like fire?
[6] Pour out your anger on the nations that do not know you,
on the kingdoms that do not call on your name.
[7] For they have eaten up Jacob
and destroyed his homeland.

> **What does this mean?**
> The one praying knows: The destruction also has to do with the guilt of his own people. That is why he asks: How much longer will you be angry?
> Verses 6–7 ask God to punish those who destroyed the city. Jeremiah 10:25 has almost the same words.

---

## Forgive us for the sake of your name (Verses 8–10)

[8] Don’t hold the sins of our ancestors against us!
Let your tender mercy come quickly to meet us,
for we are in great need.
[9] Help us, God who saves us, for the glory of your name!
Rescue us, and forgive our sins for the sake of your name!
[10] Why should the nations say, “Where is their God?”
Let the nations know, while we watch,
that you pay back those who poured out the blood of your servants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–9: The people confess their guilt and ask for forgiveness. They do not point to anything good they have done, but to God’s name and honor.
> Verse 10: The nations mock: “Where is their God?” It is about God’s reputation in the world.
> “You pay back those who poured out the blood”: The one praying leaves justice to God. He does not take revenge himself.

---

## Hear the groaning of the prisoners (Verses 11–13)

[11] Let the groaning of the prisoners come before you!
By your great power, keep alive those who have been sentenced to death!
[12] Lord, pay back our neighbors seven times over, right into their laps,
for the insults with which they have insulted you!
[13] Then we, your people and the sheep of your pasture,
will thank you forever.
We will tell of your praise to all generations.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: A prayer for the prisoners and for those who were taken away, who have been sentenced to death.
> Verse 12: “Seven times over, right into their laps”: completely, fully. The mocking was not aimed only at Israel, but at God himself.
> Verse 13: In spite of everything, the psalm ends with a promise: We will praise you, also for the generations to come.
