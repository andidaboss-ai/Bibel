# Nehemiah – Chapter 2
### Nehemiah goes to Jerusalem

---

## Nehemiah before the king (Verses 1–8)

[1] It was the month of Nisan,
in the twentieth year that Artaxerxes was king.
Wine was set before the king.
I picked up the wine and gave it to him.
I had never been sad in front of him before.
[2] So the king asked me,
“Why do you look so sad?
You are not sick.
This must be sadness of the heart.”
Then I was very afraid.
[3] I said to the king,
“May the king live forever!
How could I not look sad?
The city where my ancestors are buried lies in ruins.
Its gates have been burned down.”
[4] The king asked me,
“What do you want?”
Then I prayed to the God of heaven.
[5] And I said to the king,
“If it pleases the king,
and if you are happy with me, your servant,
please send me to Judah,
to the city where my ancestors are buried.
Then I can rebuild it.”
[6] The queen was sitting next to the king.
The king asked me,
“How long will your journey take?
When will you come back?”
The king was willing to send me,
and I gave him a date.
[7] I also said to the king,
“If it pleases the king,
please give me letters for the governors of the land beyond the River.
Then they will let me travel through their lands until I reach Judah.
[8] Please also give me a letter for Asaph,
who is in charge of the king’s forest.
Then he will give me wood for beams.
I need beams for the gates of the fortress next to the temple,
for the city wall,
and for the house where I will live.”
The king gave me what I asked for,
because the good hand of my God was on me.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month Nisan is March/April. Nehemiah has prayed and waited for four months.
> You were not allowed to look sad before a Persian king. That could count as discontent or treason. That is why Nehemiah becomes afraid.
> Nehemiah is wise: he speaks of the city “where my ancestors are buried.” Every king understood that you honor the graves of your ancestors. He does not say the name Jerusalem. The king had earlier had the building of this city stopped (Ezra 4:21).
> Verse 4 is one of the shortest prayers of the Bible: between the king’s question and his answer, Nehemiah prays silently. You can pray at any time, even in the middle of a conversation.
> Nehemiah is well prepared. He knows exactly what he needs: letters for the journey and wood for the building. Faith and good planning belong together.
> The “king’s forest” was maybe in Lebanon, where cedars grew.

## Arrival and the first opponents (Verses 9–10)

[9] Then I came to the governors of the land beyond the River.
I gave them the king’s letters.
The king had also sent army officers and horsemen with me.
[10] Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah, the Ammonite servant, heard about it.
They were very upset
that someone had come to help the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unlike Ezra (Ezra 8:22), Nehemiah travels with soldiers. That is not a contradiction. God can protect in different ways.
> Sanballat was the governor of Samaria. His name is also found in a letter that was discovered in Elephantine in Egypt. “Horonite” probably means: he came from Beth Horon.
> Tobiah was probably a high official in the area of Ammon, east of the Jordan. “Servant” was maybe a title, like “servant of the king.” Or it is meant to belittle him.
> These men become Nehemiah’s main opponents. They do not want Jerusalem to become strong again.

---

## Nehemiah inspects the wall by night (Verses 11–16)

[11] So I came to Jerusalem.
I stayed there for three days.
[12] Then I got up in the night.
I took a few men with me.
I had not told anyone what my God had put into my heart to do for Jerusalem.
I had no animal with me except the one I was riding.
[13] At night, I went out through the Valley Gate.
I went toward the Jackal’s Well and then to the Dung Gate.
I looked closely at the walls of Jerusalem.
They were broken down,
and the gates had been burned.
[14] Then I went on to the Spring Gate and to the King’s Pool.
But there was no room for my animal to get through.
[15] So during the night I went up along the brook and looked at the wall.
Then I turned back.
I went in again through the Valley Gate.
That is how I came back.
[16] The officials did not know where I had gone or what I had done.
I had not yet told anything to the Jews:
not to the priests, the nobles, the officials,
or the others who would do the work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah goes about it wisely. First, he looks at everything calmly, secretly at night. Only when he has a plan does he speak with the others.
> The rubble lies so thick that in some places his animal cannot get through.
> The “brook” is the Kidron Valley east of Jerusalem.
> Nehemiah probably goes around the southern part of the city, the old City of David.

---

## “Let’s rise up and build!” (Verses 17–20)

[17] Then I said to them,
“You see the bad situation that we are in.
Jerusalem lies in ruins.
Its gates have been burned.
Come, let’s rebuild the wall of Jerusalem!
Then we will no longer be a disgrace.”
[18] I told them how the hand of my God had been good to me.
I also told them what the king had said to me.
They said, “Let’s rise up and build!”
And they got ready for this good work with courage.
[19] But Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah, the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arabian heard about it.
They laughed at us and looked down on us.
They said,
“What are you doing?
Are you rebelling against the king?”
[20] I answered them,
“The God of heaven will give us success.
We are his servants.
We will start building.
But you have no share in Jerusalem,
no right to it,
and nothing there that people will remember you by.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah says “we”: “the bad situation that we are in.” Although he has only just come, he puts himself on the same level as the people.
> He gives them courage by telling how God has already helped. The people answer with enthusiasm: “Let’s rise up and build!”
> At once, mockery and threats come. They accuse them of rebellion against the king. That is the same tactic as in Ezra 4.
> Geshem was a powerful Arab ruler. A silver bowl that was found in Egypt probably names the same man: “Geshem, king of Kedar.”
> Nehemiah does not let himself be frightened. He answers clearly: God will give us success. You have nothing to say here.
