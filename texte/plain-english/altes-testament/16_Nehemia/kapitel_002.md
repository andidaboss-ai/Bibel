# Nehemiah – Chapter 2
### Nehemiah goes to Jerusalem

---

## Nehemiah before the king (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king,
wine was before him.
I picked up the wine and gave it to the king.
I had never been sad in his presence before.
[2] The king said to me,
“Why is your face sad, since you are not sick?
This is nothing else but sorrow of heart.”
Then I was very much afraid.
[3] I said to the king,
“May the king live forever!
Why shouldn’t my face be sad,
when the city, the place of my fathers’ tombs, lies in ruins,
and its gates have been burned up with fire?”
[4] Then the king said to me,
“What do you ask for?”
So I prayed to the God of heaven.
[5] I said to the king,
“If it pleases the king,
and if your servant has found favor in your sight,
please send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers’ tombs,
so that I may build it.”
[6] The king said to me (the queen was also sitting by him),
“How long will your journey take? When will you return?”
So it pleased the king to send me,
and I set a time for him.
[7] I also said to the king,
“If it pleases the king,
let letters be given me to the governors beyond the River,
so that they let me pass through until I come to Judah,
[8] and a letter to Asaph the keeper of the king’s forest,
so that he gives me timber to make beams
for the gates of the fortress by the temple,
for the wall of the city,
and for the house that I will live in.”
The king gave me what I asked for,
because the good hand of my God was on me.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month Nisan is March/April. Nehemiah has prayed and waited for four months.
> You were not allowed to look sad before a Persian king. That could count as discontent or treason. That is why Nehemiah becomes afraid.
> Nehemiah is wise: he speaks of the city of “my fathers’ tombs”. Every king understood that you honor the graves of your ancestors. He does not say the name Jerusalem. The king had earlier had the building of this city stopped (Ezra 4:21).
> Verse 4 is one of the shortest prayers of the Bible: between the king’s question and his answer, Nehemiah prays silently. You can pray at any time, even in the middle of a conversation.
> Nehemiah is well prepared. He knows exactly what he needs: letters for the journey and wood for the building. Faith and good planning belong together.
> The “king’s forest” was maybe in Lebanon, where cedars grew.

## Arrival and the first opponents (Verses 9–10)

[9] Then I came to the governors beyond the River
and gave them the king’s letters.
The king had sent army officers and horsemen with me.
[10] Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard of it.
It upset them very much
that a man had come to seek the good of the children of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Unlike Ezra (Ezra 8:22), Nehemiah travels with soldiers. That is not a contradiction. God can protect in different ways.
> Sanballat was the governor of Samaria. His name is also found in a letter that was discovered in Elephantine in Egypt. “Horonite” probably means: he came from Beth Horon.
> Tobiah was probably a high official in the area of Ammon, east of the Jordan. “Servant” was maybe a title, like “servant of the king”. Or it is meant to belittle him.
> These men become Nehemiah’s main opponents. They do not want Jerusalem to become strong again.

---

## Nehemiah inspects the wall by night (Verses 11–16)

[11] So I came to Jerusalem and was there three days.
[12] I got up in the night, I and a few men with me.
I didn’t tell anyone what my God had put into my heart to do for Jerusalem.
There was no animal with me except the animal that I rode on.
[13] I went out by night through the Valley Gate
toward the Jackal’s Well, and then to the Dung Gate.
I inspected the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down,
and its gates, which had been burned up with fire.
[14] Then I went on to the Spring Gate and to the King’s Pool,
but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.
[15] Then I went up in the night along the brook and inspected the wall.
I turned back, went in through the Valley Gate, and so returned.
[16] The rulers didn’t know where I had gone or what I had done.
I had not yet told it to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles,
nor to the rulers, nor to the rest who did the work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah goes about it wisely. First he looks at everything calmly, secretly at night. Only when he has a plan does he speak with the others.
> The rubble lies so thick that in some places his animal cannot get through.
> The “brook” is the Kidron Valley east of Jerusalem.
> Nehemiah probably goes around the southern part of the city, the old City of David.

---

## “Let’s rise up and build!” (Verses 17–20)

[17] Then I said to them,
“You see the bad situation that we are in,
how Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates are burned with fire.
Come, let’s build up the wall of Jerusalem,
so that we will no longer be a disgrace.”
[18] I told them about the hand of my God, which had been good to me,
and also about the words that the king had spoken to me.
They said, “Let’s rise up and build.”
So they strengthened their hands for the good work.
[19] But Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arabian heard it.
They made fun of us and looked down on us, and said,
“What is this thing that you are doing?
Will you rebel against the king?”
[20] Then I answered them,
“The God of heaven will give us success.
So we, his servants, will get up and build.
But you have no share, no right, and no memorial in Jerusalem.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah says “we”: “the bad situation that we are in”. Although he has only just come, he puts himself on the same level as the people.
> He gives them courage by telling how God has already helped. The people answer with enthusiasm: “Let’s rise up and build!”
> At once mockery and threats come. They accuse them of rebellion against the king. That is the same tactic as in Ezra 4.
> Geshem was a powerful Arab ruler. A silver bowl that was found in Egypt probably names the same man: “Geshem, king of Kedar”.
> Nehemiah does not let himself be frightened. He answers clearly: God will give us success. You have nothing to say here.
