# Nehemiah – Chapter 6
### Traps for Nehemiah – and the wall is finished

---

## The invitation to Ono (Verses 1–4)

[1] Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of our enemies heard
that I had built the wall,
and that there was no gap left in it.
(But up to that time I had not yet set up the doors in the gates.)
[2] Then Sanballat and Geshem sent word to me:
“Come! Let’s meet together in the villages in the plain of Ono.”
But they planned to harm me.
[3] I sent messengers to them and said,
“I am doing a great work, so that I can’t come down.
Why should the work stop while I leave it and come down to you?”
[4] They sent to me four times like this,
and I answered them the same way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the opponents try a trick. Ono was about 25 miles northwest of Jerusalem, far away from the protection of the city. There they could easily have captured or killed Nehemiah.
> Nehemiah’s answer is famous: “I am doing a great work, so that I can’t come down.” He does not let himself be distracted from his task.
> He keeps to his answer, even the fourth time. He does not let himself be worn down.

---

## The open letter (Verses 5–9)

[5] Then Sanballat sent his servant to me in the same way a fifth time,
with an open letter in his hand.
[6] In it was written:
“It is reported among the nations, and Gashmu says it,
that you and the Jews plan to rebel.
That is why you are building the wall.
And according to these reports, you want to be their king.
[7] You have also appointed prophets to announce about you in Jerusalem:
‘There is a king in Judah!’
Now this will be reported to the king, according to these words.
So come now, and let’s talk it over together.”
[8] Then I sent to him and said,
“None of the things you say have happened.
You are making them up out of your own heart.”
[9] For they all wanted to make us afraid.
They thought,
“Their hands will become too weak for the work, so that it will not be done.”
But now, strengthen my hands.

> **What does this mean?**
> An “open letter” was not sealed. Anyone could read it. That way the rumors were meant to spread.
> Gashmu is another form of the name Geshem.
> The lie is dangerous: whoever rebelled against the Persian king was punished severely.
> Nehemiah answers briefly and clearly: this is made up. He does not get into a long defense.
> At the end of verse 9 he prays again in the middle of the report: “Strengthen my hands!” A short prayer in need.

---

## The false prophet (Verses 10–14)

[10] I went to the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah, the son of Mehetabel,
who had shut himself in at his home.
He said,
“Let us meet together in God’s house, inside the temple,
and let’s shut the doors of the temple.
For they will come to kill you.
Yes, in the night they will come to kill you.”
[11] I said,
“Should a man like me run away?
And who is there like me who would go into the temple to save his life?
I will not go in.”
[12] I saw clearly that God had not sent him.
He had spoken this prophecy against me
because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.
[13] He was hired so that I would be afraid, do what he said, and sin.
Then they would have something for an evil report about me,
so that they could put me to shame.
[14] “My God, remember Tobiah and Sanballat for these deeds of theirs,
and also the prophetess Noadiah
and the rest of the prophets who wanted to make me afraid.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now comes the most dangerous trap: a false prophet from his own people. He acts as if he wants to save Nehemiah.
> But Nehemiah sees through him for two reasons:
> – A leader who runs away would discourage the people.
> – Nehemiah was not a priest. He was not allowed to go into the inside of the temple (Numbers 18:7). If he had done it, he would have sinned against God’s law.
> A true prophet of God would never tell someone to sin. That is how Nehemiah recognizes that Shemaiah had been bought.
> A prophetess, Noadiah, also belonged to the opponents. So there were several false prophets.
> Nehemiah does not take revenge himself. He leaves his enemies to God.

---

## The wall is finished (Verses 15–16)

[15] So the wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of Elul,
in 52 days.
[16] When all our enemies heard of it,
all the nations around us were afraid,
and they lost their confidence.
For they saw that this work had been done by our God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only 52 days! In spite of mockery, threats, exhaustion and traps. The month Elul is August/September. So the wall was finished in the fall of 445 BC.
> The enemies who wanted to frighten others now become afraid themselves. For they see: this cannot only be the work of people. God has worked here.
> The Jewish historian Josephus later writes of two years and four months. Maybe he meant the whole construction with towers and gates.

## Tobiah’s connections (Verses 17–19)

[17] Also in those days the nobles of Judah sent many letters to Tobiah,
and Tobiah’s letters came to them.
[18] For many in Judah were bound to him by oath,
because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah the son of Arah.
And his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam the son of Berechiah.
[19] They also spoke of his good deeds before me
and reported my words to him.
And Tobiah sent letters to make me afraid.

> **What does this mean?**
> Through marriages Tobiah had close connections in Jerusalem. Meshullam the son of Berechiah had even helped build the wall (Chapter 3:4, 30).
> Some in Jerusalem played a double game: they praised Tobiah to Nehemiah and passed Nehemiah’s words on to Tobiah.
> Even after the building of the wall the struggle goes on. This time with letters and intimidation.
