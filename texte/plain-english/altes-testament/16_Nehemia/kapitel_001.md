# Nehemiah – Chapter 1
### Nehemiah hears bad news

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Nehemiah?

The book of Nehemiah continues the book of Ezra. Originally both were one single book.
Nehemiah was a Jew who lived at the Persian royal court. He was cupbearer to King Artaxerxes (Artaxerxes I). That was a position of trust.
In the year 445 before Christ Nehemiah hears that the walls of Jerusalem still lie in ruins. He asks the king to let him go to Jerusalem to build them again.
The book tells:
– Chapters 1–7: Nehemiah and the people build the wall again in only 52 days, in spite of mockery, threats and opposition.
– Chapters 8–10: Ezra reads the law to the people. The people confess their guilt and make a covenant with God.
– Chapters 11–13: People settle in Jerusalem again, the wall is dedicated, and Nehemiah puts things in order.
Large parts are written using “I”, like a diary. They are called the “Nehemiah memoir”.
Nehemiah prays very often, sometimes only very briefly, in the middle of a conversation. He combines prayer and hard work.

## The news from Jerusalem (Verses 1–4)

[1] The words of Nehemiah the son of Hacaliah.
In the month Chislev, in the twentieth year,
I was in Susa the palace.
[2] Hanani, one of my brothers, came with some men from Judah.
I asked them about the Jews who had escaped,
who were left from the captivity,
and about Jerusalem.
[3] They said to me,
“Those who are left from the captivity there in the province
are in great trouble and shame.
The wall of Jerusalem is broken down,
and its gates are burned with fire.”
[4] When I heard these words,
I sat down and wept, and mourned for several days.
And I fasted and prayed before the God of heaven.

> **What does this mean?**
> The month Chislev is November/December. The “twentieth year” is the twentieth year of the reign of King Artaxerxes, that is 445 before Christ (Chapter 2:1).
> Susa was one of the capitals of the Persian Empire, in today’s Iran. The story of Esther also takes place there.
> Hanani was maybe really Nehemiah’s own brother (compare Chapter 7:2).
> The wall had already been destroyed by the Babylonians more than 140 years before. Maybe a later attempt to build it again had failed (Ezra 4:23). At that time a city without a wall was without protection and was seen as a disgrace.
> Nehemiah lives in wealth at the royal court. But the suffering of his people does not leave him cold. He weeps, fasts and prays.

## Nehemiah’s prayer (Verses 5–11)

[5] I said,
“Please, LORD, the God of heaven,
the great and awesome God,
who keeps covenant and loving kindness with those who love him and keep his commandments:
[6] let your ear now listen and your eyes be open,
so that you hear the prayer of your servant,
which I pray before you at this time, day and night,
for the children of Israel your servants.
I confess the sins of the children of Israel,
which we have sinned against you.
Yes, I and my father’s house have sinned.
[7] We have acted very wickedly against you.
We have not kept the commandments, the rules, and the laws
which you commanded your servant Moses.
[8] Please remember the word that you commanded your servant Moses:
‘If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples.
[9] But if you return to me, and keep my commandments and do them,
then even if your scattered people were at the farthest end of the heavens,
I will gather them from there
and bring them to the place that I have chosen,
to make my name dwell there.’
[10] Now these are your servants and your people,
whom you have set free by your great power and by your strong hand.
[11] Lord, please let your ear listen now to the prayer of your servant,
and to the prayer of your servants who love to fear your name.
Please give your servant success today,
and let him find mercy in the sight of this man.”
At that time I was cupbearer to the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah’s prayer is an example:
> – He begins with praise: God is great and faithful.
> – He confesses the guilt, his own too: “Yes, I and my father’s house have sinned.”
> – He reminds God of his promises (Deuteronomy 30:1–5).
> – He asks for help in concrete terms.
> He prays “day and night”, for four months (from Chislev to Nisan, Chapter 2:1). He waits patiently for the right moment.
> “This man” is the king. For God even the most powerful king in the world is only “a man”.
> A cupbearer handed the king his wine and tested it first to see whether it was poisoned. He was a close confidant of the king and often had great influence.
