# Nehemiah – Chapter 7
### Guards for the city and the list of those who returned

---

## Order for Jerusalem (Verses 1–4)

[1] When the wall was built and I had set up the doors,
and the gatekeepers, the singers, and the Levites had been appointed,
[2] I put my brother Hanani,
and Hananiah the commander of the fortress, in charge of Jerusalem.
For he was a faithful man and feared God more than many others.
[3] I said to them,
“Don’t let the gates of Jerusalem be opened until the sun is hot.
While the guards are still standing there, let them shut the doors and bar them.
And appoint watches of the people who live in Jerusalem,
each one at his post, each one near his own house.”
[4] The city was wide and large,
but there were few people in it,
and the houses had not been built.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah appoints faithful men to be in charge. The most important quality: Hananiah “feared God more than many others”.
> The gates are to be opened only when the sun is high. Then everyone is awake, and no enemy can break in by surprise at dawn.
> A new problem: the city is large, but almost empty. A wall alone does not yet make a living city. It needs people. The solution comes in Chapter 11.

---

## The record is found (Verses 5–7)

[5] My God put it into my heart
to gather together the nobles, the rulers, and the people,
so that they could be listed by their families.
I found the book of the family records of those who came up at first,
and I found this written in it:
[6] These are the people of the province
who went up out of the captivity of those who had been carried away,
whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away.
They returned to Jerusalem and to Judah, each to his own city.
[7] They came with Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Azariah, Raamiah, Nahamani,
Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispereth, Bigvai, Nehum, and Baanah.
The number of the men of the people of Israel:

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah finds the old list of the first people who returned, almost 100 years old. It appears almost exactly the same in Ezra 2.
> The list helps to find out which families belong where. So later it can be decided who is to move to Jerusalem.
> Here twelve leaders are named, in Ezra 2:2 only eleven. The twelve recall the twelve tribes of Israel.
> In this chapter some numbers and names are a little different from Ezra 2. When long lists were copied, small differences easily happened.

## Those without proof (Verses 61–65)

[61] These were the ones who went up from Tel Melah, Tel Harsha, Cherub, Addon, and Immer.
But they could not show their families or their ancestry,
whether they were of Israel:
[62] the descendants of Delaiah, the descendants of Tobiah, the descendants of Nekoda: 642.
[63] Of the priests:
the descendants of Hobaiah, the descendants of Hakkoz,
the descendants of Barzillai.
He had married one of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite
and was called by their name.
[64] These searched for their family records, but couldn’t find them.
So they were not allowed to serve and were removed from the priesthood.
[65] The governor told them not to eat of the most holy things
until a priest stood up to serve with Urim and Thummim.

> **What does this mean?**
> As in Ezra 2:59–63: whoever could not prove his ancestry was still allowed to come along. Only the service as priest was postponed until God himself would give clarity.
> “Urim and Thummim” were lots with which the high priest asked for God’s will.

## The total number and the gifts (Verses 66–73)

[66] The whole assembly together was 42,360,
[67] besides their male servants and their female servants,
of whom there were 7,337.
They had 245 singing men and singing women.
[68] Their horses were 736, their mules 245,
[69] their camels 435, their donkeys 6,720.
[70] Some of the heads of the families gave to the work.
The governor gave to the treasury 1,000 darics of gold, about 18 pounds,
50 basins, and 530 priests’ garments.
[71] Some of the heads of the families gave into the treasury for the work
20,000 darics of gold, about 370 pounds,
and 2,200 minas of silver, about 2,750 pounds.
[72] What the rest of the people gave was 20,000 darics of gold,
and 2,000 minas of silver, about 2,500 pounds,
and 67 priests’ garments.
[73] So the priests, the Levites, the gatekeepers, the singers,
some of the people, the temple servants, and all Israel lived in their cities.
When the seventh month had come,
the people of Israel were in their cities.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Darics” were Persian gold coins of about 0.3 ounces. Silver: the Bible says “2,200 minas” and “2,000 minas”. A mina is about 1¼ pounds.
> The list of gifts is more detailed here than in Ezra 2:68–69. The governor gave too. Everyone gave according to his means.
> The end of verse 73 is almost exactly the same sentence as Ezra 3:1. It leads over to the great assembly in the seventh month (Chapter 8).
