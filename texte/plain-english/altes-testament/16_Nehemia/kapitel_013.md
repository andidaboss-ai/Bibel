# Nehemiah – Chapter 13
### Nehemiah’s second term in office

---

## The law about Ammonites and Moabites (Verses 1–3)

[1] On that day they read in the book of Moses so that the people could hear.
And it was found written in it
that an Ammonite and a Moabite should never come into the assembly of God,
[2] because they didn’t meet the children of Israel with bread and with water,
but hired Balaam against them to curse them.
However, our God turned the curse into a blessing.
[3] When they had heard the law,
they separated all the mixed crowd from Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The law is in Deuteronomy 23:3–5. The story of Balaam is in Numbers 22–24.
> “God turned the curse into a blessing”: Balaam was supposed to curse Israel, but he had to bless it. God can turn even evil into good.
> How does this law fit with Ruth the Moabite, who became David’s great-grandmother? Jewish interpretation later said: the law applies to men from Moab and Ammon, not to women. Others say: whoever turns completely to the God of Israel, like Ruth, belongs. The Bible itself keeps both side by side.
> The prophet Isaiah says later: even foreigners who hold on to the LORD are welcome in God’s house, “for my house will be called a house of prayer for all peoples” (Isaiah 56:3–7).

---

## Tobiah in the temple (Verses 4–9)

[4] Before this, Eliashib the priest,
who was appointed over the rooms of the house of our God,
and who was related to Tobiah,
[5] had prepared a great room for him.
Before that, they had stored there the grain offerings, the frankincense, the objects,
and the tithes of the grain, the new wine, and the oil,
which were given by command to the Levites, the singers, and the gatekeepers,
and the offerings for the priests.
[6] But during all this I was not in Jerusalem.
For in the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon
I had gone to the king.
After some time I asked the king for permission to leave,
[7] and I came to Jerusalem.
Then I understood the evil that Eliashib had done for Tobiah,
by preparing a room for him in the courts of God’s house.
[8] It made me very angry.
So I threw all of Tobiah’s household goods out of the room.
[9] Then I gave orders, and they cleansed the rooms.
And I brought back into them the objects of God’s house,
with the grain offerings and the frankincense.

> **What does this mean?**
> After twelve years (433 before Christ) Nehemiah had gone back to the king. Later he came to Jerusalem a second time.
> While he was away, a lot had gone wrong. The worst thing: the high priest Eliashib had given Nehemiah’s enemy Tobiah a place to live in the temple!
> Nehemiah acts firmly: he throws Tobiah’s furniture out. That recalls Jesus, who drove the traders out of the temple (John 2:13–17).
> Artaxerxes is called “king of Babylon” here, because he also ruled over Babylon.

---

## The Levites are provided for again (Verses 10–14)

[10] I also found out that the portions of the Levites had not been given to them.
So the Levites and the singers, who did the work,
had each run away to his own field.
[11] Then I argued with the rulers and said,
“Why is God’s house forsaken?”
I gathered the Levites together and put them back in their places.
[12] Then all Judah brought the tithe of the grain, the new wine, and the oil
to the storerooms.
[13] I made treasurers over the storerooms:
Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, and of the Levites, Pedaiah.
Next to them was Hanan the son of Zaccur, the son of Mattaniah.
For they were considered faithful,
and their task was to hand out the portions to their brothers.
[14] Remember me, my God, for this.
Don’t wipe out the good deeds that I have done
for the house of my God and for its services.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people had promised: “We will not abandon the house of our God” (Chapter 10:39). But they had stopped giving the tithe. The Levites had to go back to their fields to survive.
> Nehemiah’s question is still important today: “Why is God’s house forsaken?”
> He again appoints faithful men. Faithfulness is the most important thing when you handle what belongs to others.

## The Sabbath (Verses 15–22)

[15] In those days I saw men in Judah treading winepresses on the Sabbath,
bringing in sheaves, and loading donkeys
with wine, grapes, figs, and all kinds of loads,
which they brought into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day.
I warned them on the day they sold food.
[16] Some men of Tyre also lived there.
They brought in fish and all kinds of goods
and sold them on the Sabbath to the people of Judah, and in Jerusalem.
[17] Then I argued with the nobles of Judah and said to them,
“What is this evil thing that you are doing?
You are making the Sabbath day unholy!
[18] Didn’t your fathers do the same?
And didn’t our God bring all this disaster on us and on this city?
Yet you are bringing more anger on Israel by making the Sabbath unholy.”
[19] When it began to get dark at the gates of Jerusalem before the Sabbath,
I commanded that the doors should be shut,
and that they should not be opened until after the Sabbath.
I set some of my servants over the gates,
so that no load would be brought in on the Sabbath day.
[20] So the traders and sellers of all kinds of goods
camped outside Jerusalem once or twice.
[21] Then I warned them and said to them,
“Why are you staying around the wall?
If you do it again, I will lay hands on you.”
From that time on they didn’t come on the Sabbath any more.
[22] I commanded the Levites to make themselves clean
and to come and guard the gates,
to keep the Sabbath day holy.
Remember me for this also, my God,
and spare me according to the greatness of your loving kindness.

> **What does this mean?**
> The promise about the Sabbath (Chapter 10:31) had also been broken. On the day of rest people worked and traded as on any other day.
> The Sabbath is a gift of God: a day of rest for everyone, also for servants, foreigners and animals (Deuteronomy 5:12–15). When trade decides everything, this rest is lost.
> The Sabbath begins on Friday evening, when it gets dark. That is why Nehemiah shuts the gates when darkness falls.
> At the end Nehemiah does not only pray “Remember me”, but also: “Spare me.” He knows that he himself needs God’s grace.

## The mixed marriages (Verses 23–29)

[23] In those days I also saw Jews who had married women of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab.
[24] Half of their children spoke the language of Ashdod,
and could not speak the Jews’ language,
but only the language of one people or another.
[25] I argued with them, cursed them,
struck some of them, and pulled out their hair.
And I made them swear by God:
“You shall not give your daughters to their sons,
nor take their daughters for your sons or for yourselves.
[26] Didn’t Solomon king of Israel sin because of such things?
Among many nations there was no king like him.
He was loved by his God,
and God made him king over all Israel.
Yet foreign women made even him sin.
[27] Shall we then listen to you and do all this great evil,
to be unfaithful to our God by marrying foreign women?”
[28] One of the sons of Joiada, the son of Eliashib the high priest,
was son-in-law to Sanballat the Horonite.
So I chased him away from me.
[29] Remember them, my God,
because they have made the priesthood unclean,
and the covenant of the priesthood and of the Levites.

> **What does this mean?**
> The third promise from Chapter 10 had also been broken.
> Nehemiah’s concern: the children could no longer speak Hebrew. So they could no longer understand God’s word either. Faith was in danger of being lost within one generation.
> He reminds them of Solomon: even the wisest king was led into idol worship by foreign wives (1 Kings 11:1–8).
> Nehemiah reacts very violently. He hits people and pulls out their hair. The Bible reports this honestly, without praising it directly. Today Jews and Christians say: violence is no way to force faith. Nehemiah too was a human being with faults.
> A grandson of the high priest had married the daughter of Nehemiah’s archenemy Sanballat. That was especially bad, because by law the high priest was only allowed to marry a woman from Israel (Leviticus 21:14).
> On the question of mixed marriages, see also the detailed explanation on Ezra 10.

## Nehemiah’s last prayer (Verses 30–31)

[30] So I cleansed them from all foreigners,
and I set the duties for the priests and for the Levites, each one in his work,
[31] and for the wood offering at set times, and for the first fruits.
Remember me, my God, for good.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book ends with a short prayer: “Remember me, my God, for good!” Nehemiah has done a lot for God and the people. But at the end he does not trust in what he has achieved, but in God remembering him.
> The end is not triumphant. The people have broken their promises. Nehemiah had to clean up again. That shows: people alone cannot keep the covenant with God for good. That is why the prophets speak of a “new covenant”, in which God writes his law on the heart (Jeremiah 31:31–34). Christians see this new covenant fulfilled in Jesus.

## Looking back: What have we read in the book of Nehemiah?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A man who prays and acts:** Nehemiah, the cupbearer of the Persian king, hears about the misery of Jerusalem. He weeps, fasts and prays. Then he bravely asks the king and goes to Jerusalem.
> **The wall (Chapters 1–7):** Everyone helps to build: priests and goldsmiths, men and women. In spite of mockery, threats, exhaustion and traps, the wall is finished in only 52 days. “Each one did the work with one of his hands and held his weapon with the other.”
> **Justice (Chapter 5):** Nehemiah fights for the poor, who are exploited by their rich brothers. He himself gives up his privileges.
> **God’s word (Chapters 8–10):** Ezra reads the law aloud. The people weep, then they celebrate: “The joy of the LORD is your strength.” A great prayer of repentance tells the whole story of God’s faithfulness. The people sign a covenant.
> **The dedication (Chapters 11–12):** People live in Jerusalem again. Two choirs walk along the wall. The joy is heard far away.
> **The failure (Chapter 13):** When Nehemiah is away, the people break their promises. Nehemiah has to clean up again.
> **What we learn:** Praying and working belong together. God helps with great tasks. But walls and promises alone do not change hearts. That needs God’s grace.
> **Short prayers:** Nehemiah often prays very briefly, in the middle of everyday life: “Strengthen my hands!” “Remember me, my God, for good!” We can pray like that today too.
> **What comes next?** The book of Esther tells a story from the same time, but not in Jerusalem. It takes place at the court of the Persian king in Susa.
