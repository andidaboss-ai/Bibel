# Nehemiah – Chapter 4
### Building with the sword at your side

---

## Mockery and prayer (Verses 1–6)

[1] Sanballat heard that we were building the wall.
He became angry and very upset.
He made fun of the Jews.
[2] He spoke in front of his brothers and the army of Samaria.
He said, “What are these weak Jews doing?
Do they want to make their city strong?
Do they want to offer sacrifices?
Do they think they will finish in one day?
Can they bring the stones back to life out of the piles of rubble?
The stones are burned!”
[3] Tobiah the Ammonite was standing next to him.
He said, “Look at what they are building!
If a fox climbed up on it, he would break down their stone wall.”
[4] “Hear us, our God!
People look down on us.
Let their insults come back on their own heads.
Let them be robbed and taken away as prisoners to a foreign land.
[5] Don’t cover up their guilt.
Don’t wipe out their sin from before you.
They have insulted the builders.”
[6] So we built the wall.
The whole wall was joined together up to half its height.
The people had a mind to work.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles verses 1–6 of this chapter are at the end of Chapter 3 (verses 33–38). That is why the verses of Chapter 4 there are shifted by six: verse 7 here is verse 1 there, and so on.
> The opponents first try mockery. Mockery can be very discouraging.
> Nehemiah does not answer with mockery of his own. He prays. His prayer is harsh: he asks God to punish the mockers. There are such prayers in the Psalms too. You may tell God honestly how angry and hurt you are. Nehemiah leaves the punishment to God and does not take revenge himself. Jesus goes even further: “Pray for those who treat you badly and persecute you” (Matthew 5:44).
> Verse 6: “The people had a mind to work”, word for word “a heart for work”. In spite of the mockery they go on working with all their heart.

## Threats and exhaustion (Verses 7–12)

[7] Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabians, the Ammonites, and the people of Ashdod heard the news:
The repair of the walls of Jerusalem was going forward.
The gaps in the wall were starting to be filled.
Then they became very angry.
[8] They all made a secret plan together.
They wanted to come and fight against Jerusalem
and cause confusion among us.
[9] But we prayed to our God.
And because of them we set up guards against them day and night.
[10] The people of Judah said,
“The strength of those who carry the loads is giving out.
There is so much rubble.
We are not able to build the wall.”
[11] Our enemies said,
“They will not know or see anything
until we come in among them and kill them.
Then we will stop the work.”
[12] The Jews who lived near the enemies came to us.
They told us ten times, from all places,
“Wherever you turn, they will attack us.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the enemies come from all sides: Samaria in the north, the Arabs in the south, the Ammonites in the east, and Ashdod (the Philistines) in the west. Jerusalem is surrounded.
> Verse 9 shows Nehemiah’s way: to pray AND to set up guards. Faith and caution belong together.
> Then exhaustion comes from inside: “We are not able to build the wall.” The rubble seems endless. And the Jews from the towns around spread fear, “ten times”, that is, again and again.
> Discouragement comes from outside and from inside. Both are dangerous.

## Nehemiah encourages the people (Verses 13–15)

[13] So I set guards in the lowest places behind the wall, in the open places.
I placed the people there by families.
They had their swords, their spears, and their bows.
[14] I looked at everything.
Then I stood up and spoke to the nobles, the leaders, and the rest of the people.
I said, “Don’t be afraid of them!
Remember the Lord.
He is great and awesome.
Fight for your brothers, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your houses.”
[15] Our enemies heard that we knew about their plan.
God had made their plan fail.
Then all of us went back to the wall.
Each one went back to his work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nehemiah sets the people up by families. Everyone defends those he loves.
> His words are a strong encouragement: think of God, who is greater than your enemies. And think of your families.
> This was about defense, not attack. The people protected their families and their city.
> The attack did not happen at all. When the enemies noticed that their plan was known, they gave up.

## Building with one hand, keeping watch with the other (Verses 16–23)

[16] From that day on, half of my servants did the work.
The other half held the spears, the shields, the bows, and the armor.
The leaders stood behind all the people of Judah.
[17] The people who built the wall and the people who carried loads worked like this:
With one hand each one did the work.
With the other hand he held his weapon.
[18] Each of the builders wore his sword at his side while he built.
The man who blew the trumpet stayed next to me.
[19] I said to the nobles, the leaders, and the rest of the people,
“The work is big and spread out over a wide area.
We are spread out along the wall, far from one another.
[20] When you hear the sound of the trumpet,
come together to us at that place.
Our God will fight for us.”
[21] So we did the work.
Half of the people held the spears
from the first light of morning until the stars came out.
[22] At that time I also said to the people,
“Each man and his servant should stay overnight inside Jerusalem.
Then they can guard us at night and work during the day.”
[23] So none of us took off our clothes:
not I, not my brothers, not my servants,
and not the men of the guard who followed me.
Each one took his weapon with him to the water.

> **What does this mean?**
> This picture is famous: building with one hand, holding the weapon with the other. It stands for people who go on working under threat.
> Christians have often applied this picture to spiritual life: we build God’s kingdom and at the same time stay watchful (compare Ephesians 6:10–18).
> Nehemiah has a good plan: a trumpeter is always with him. When there is danger somewhere, everyone comes there. No one stands alone.
> “Our God will fight for us”: Nehemiah trusts in God, but he also does everything he can himself.
> Nehemiah himself sets a good example. He does not even take off his clothes at night.
> The end of verse 23 is hard to understand in Hebrew. It probably means: everyone always had his weapon at hand, even when fetching water.
