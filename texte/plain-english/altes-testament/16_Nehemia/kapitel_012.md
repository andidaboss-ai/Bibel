# Nehemiah – Chapter 12
### The dedication of the wall

---

## The priests and Levites of the first return (Verses 1–9)

[1] These are the priests and the Levites who went up
with Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua:
Seraiah, Jeremiah, Ezra,
[2] Amariah, Malluch, Hattush,
[3] Shecaniah, Rehum, Meremoth,
[4] Iddo, Ginnethoi, Abijah,
[5] Mijamin, Maadiah, Bilgah,
[6] Shemaiah, Joiarib, Jedaiah,
[7] Sallu, Amok, Hilkiah, and Jedaiah.
These were the heads of the priests and of their brothers in the days of Jeshua.
[8] The Levites were Jeshua, Binnui, Kadmiel, Sherebiah, Judah,
and Mattaniah, who was in charge of the songs of thanks, he and his brothers.
[9] Also Bakbukiah and Unno, their brothers,
were close to them in their duties.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the book jumps back to the first return, almost 100 years earlier (Ezra 2).
> The Ezra in verse 1 is not the Ezra from Ezra 7. He is a priest with the same name.
> “Close to them”: the Hebrew says “opposite them”. The singers probably stood opposite each other in two choirs and sang in turns.

---

## The high priests (Verses 10–11)

[10] Jeshua became the father of Joiakim,
Joiakim became the father of Eliashib,
Eliashib became the father of Joiada,
[11] Joiada became the father of Jonathan,
and Jonathan became the father of Jaddua.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the list of the high priests over six generations, from about 538 to after 400 before Christ.
> Eliashib was high priest in the time of Nehemiah (Chapter 3:1; 13:4). “Jonathan” is called “Johanan” in verse 22. His name is also found in a letter from Elephantine in Egypt.

---

## The priestly families under Joiakim (Verses 12–21)

[12] In the days of Joiakim these were the priests, the heads of families:
of Seraiah, Meraiah; of Jeremiah, Hananiah;
[13] of Ezra, Meshullam; of Amariah, Jehohanan;
[14] of Malluchi, Jonathan; of Shebaniah, Joseph;
[15] of Harim, Adna; of Meraioth, Helkai;
[16] of Iddo, Zechariah; of Ginnethon, Meshullam;
[17] of Abijah, Zichri; of Miniamin, of Moadiah, Piltai;
[18] of Bilgah, Shammua; of Shemaiah, Jehonathan;
[19] of Joiarib, Mattenai; of Jedaiah, Uzzi;
[20] of Sallai, Kallai; of Amok, Eber;
[21] of Hilkiah, Hashabiah; of Jedaiah, Nethanel.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the next generation each priestly family had a new head. The list shows: the service went on, from generation to generation.
> With “Miniamin” the name of the head is missing in the Hebrew text.

---

## The records and the Levites (Verses 22–26)

[22] As for the Levites,
in the days of Eliashib, Joiada, Johanan, and Jaddua,
the heads of families were written down,
and also the priests, in the reign of Darius the Persian.
[23] The descendants of Levi, heads of families,
were written in the book of the chronicles,
until the days of Johanan the son of Eliashib.
[24] The heads of the Levites were Hashabiah, Sherebiah, and Jeshua the son of Kadmiel,
with their brothers close to them,
to praise and give thanks,
as David the man of God had commanded,
section next to section.
[25] Mattaniah, Bakbukiah, Obadiah, Meshullam, Talmon, and Akkub were gatekeepers,
keeping watch at the storerooms of the gates.
[26] These lived in the days of Joiakim the son of Jeshua, the son of Jozadak,
and in the days of Nehemiah the governor,
and of Ezra the priest and scribe.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Darius the Persian” is probably Darius II (423–404 before Christ). So this note was added somewhat later.
> “Section next to section”: the choirs stood next to each other or opposite each other and sang in turns, as David had ordered (1 Chronicles 25).

---

## Getting ready for the dedication (Verses 27–30)

[27] At the dedication of the wall of Jerusalem,
they searched for the Levites in all their places,
to bring them to Jerusalem to celebrate the dedication with joy,
with thanksgiving and with singing,
with cymbals, stringed instruments, and harps.
[28] The sons of the singers gathered together
from the plain around Jerusalem
and from the villages of the Netophathites,
[29] also from Beth Gilgal and from the fields of Geba and Azmaveth,
for the singers had built villages for themselves around Jerusalem.
[30] The priests and the Levites made themselves clean,
and they made the people, the gates, and the wall clean.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wall is not only a building. It is dedicated to God. That is why there is a cleansing: first the priests themselves, then the people, then the gates and the wall.

---

## Two choirs on the wall (Verses 31–42)

[31] Then I brought the princes of Judah up on the wall,
and appointed two great choirs who gave thanks and went in procession.
One went to the right on the wall, toward the Dung Gate.
[32] After them went Hoshaiah with half of the princes of Judah,
[33] and Azariah, Ezra, and Meshullam,
[34] Judah, Benjamin, Shemaiah, Jeremiah,
[35] and some of the priests’ sons with trumpets:
Zechariah the son of Jonathan, the son of Shemaiah, the son of Mattaniah,
the son of Micaiah, the son of Zaccur, the son of Asaph,
[36] and his brothers, Shemaiah, Azarel, Milalai, Gilalai, Maai, Nethanel, Judah, and Hanani,
with the musical instruments of David the man of God.
And Ezra the scribe went in front of them.
[37] At the Spring Gate they went straight up
by the stairs of David’s city, at the ascent of the wall,
above David’s house, as far as the Water Gate in the east.
[38] The other choir of those who gave thanks went the opposite way,
and I followed them, with half of the people,
on the wall above the Tower of the Ovens, as far as the Broad Wall,
[39] and above the Gate of Ephraim, by the Old Gate, by the Fish Gate,
the tower of Hananel, and the tower of Hammeah, as far as the Sheep Gate.
And they stopped at the Gate of the Guard.
[40] So the two choirs of those who gave thanks stood in God’s house,
and I, and half of the rulers with me,
[41] and the priests Eliakim, Maaseiah, Miniamin, Micaiah, Elioenai, Zechariah, and Hananiah, with trumpets,
[42] and Maaseiah, Shemaiah, Eleazar, Uzzi, Jehohanan, Malchijah, Elam, and Ezer.
The singers sang loudly, with Jezrahiah their leader.

> **What does this mean?**
> What a picture: two great choirs walk on the wall around the city in opposite directions. One is led by Ezra, the other by Nehemiah. They meet at the temple.
> The wall Tobiah mocked, saying “a fox … would break down their stone wall” (Chapter 4:3), now carries a whole procession.
> The way of the choirs follows the list in Chapter 3, only in the opposite direction.

---

## Great joy (Verse 43)

[43] They offered great sacrifices that day and rejoiced,
for God had made them rejoice with great joy.
The women and the children also rejoiced,
so that the joy of Jerusalem was heard far away.

> **What does this mean?**
> The word “joy” comes five times in this verse (in Hebrew). It is the high point of the book.
> Everyone rejoices: men, women and children. And the joy is so loud that it is heard far away (compare Ezra 3:13).

---

## Providing for the temple service (Verses 44–47)

[44] On that day men were appointed over the rooms for the treasures,
for the offerings, for the first fruits, and for the tithes.
They were to gather into them from the fields of the cities
the portions that the law had set for the priests and Levites.
For Judah rejoiced over the priests and over the Levites who served.
[45] They carried out the duties of their God and the duties of cleansing,
and so did the singers and the gatekeepers,
as David and Solomon his son had commanded.
[46] For long ago, in the days of David and Asaph,
there was a leader of the singers,
and there were songs of praise and thanksgiving to God.
[47] In the days of Zerubbabel and in the days of Nehemiah,
all Israel gave the portions for the singers and the gatekeepers,
as each day required.
They set apart what was for the Levites,
and the Levites set apart what was for the descendants of Aaron.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people gladly provide for those who serve in the temple. “Judah rejoiced over the priests and over the Levites.”
> The order of worship continues what David and Solomon began. The old heritage lives on.
