# Nehemiah – Chapter 12
### The dedication of the wall

---

## The priests and Levites of the first return (Verses 1–9)

[1] Here are the priests and the Levites who came up to Jerusalem
with Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and with Jeshua:
Seraiah, Jeremiah, Ezra,
[2] Amariah, Malluch, Hattush,
[3] Shecaniah, Rehum, Meremoth,
[4] Iddo, Ginnethoi, Abijah,
[5] Mijamin, Maadiah, Bilgah,
[6] Shemaiah, Joiarib, Jedaiah,
[7] Sallu, Amok, Hilkiah, and Jedaiah.
In the days of Jeshua, these were the leaders of the priests and of their brothers.
[8] The Levites were Jeshua, Binnui, Kadmiel, Sherebiah, Judah, and Mattaniah.
Mattaniah and his brothers were in charge of the songs of thanks.
[9] Their brothers Bakbukiah and Unno stood close to them during the service.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the book jumps back to the first return, almost 100 years earlier (Ezra 2).
> The Ezra in verse 1 is not the Ezra from Ezra 7. He is a priest with the same name.
> “Close to them”: the Hebrew says “opposite them”. The singers probably stood opposite each other in two choirs and sang in turns.

---

## The high priests (Verses 10–11)

[10] Jeshua became the father of Joiakim,
Joiakim became the father of Eliashib,
Eliashib became the father of Joiada,
[11] Joiada became the father of Jonathan,
and Jonathan became the father of Jaddua.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the list of the high priests over six generations, from about 538 to after 400 BC.
> Eliashib was high priest in the time of Nehemiah (Chapter 3:1; 13:4). “Jonathan” is called “Johanan” in verse 22. His name is also found in a letter from Elephantine in Egypt.

---

## The priestly families under Joiakim (Verses 12–21)

[12] In the days of Joiakim, these priests were the heads of the families:
for Seraiah: Meraiah; for Jeremiah: Hananiah;
[13] for Ezra: Meshullam; for Amariah: Jehohanan;
[14] for Malluchi: Jonathan; for Shebaniah: Joseph;
[15] for Harim: Adna; for Meraioth: Helkai;
[16] for Iddo: Zechariah; for Ginnethon: Meshullam;
[17] for Abijah: Zichri; for Miniamin; for Moadiah: Piltai;
[18] for Bilgah: Shammua; for Shemaiah: Jehonathan;
[19] for Joiarib: Mattenai; for Jedaiah: Uzzi;
[20] for Sallai: Kallai; for Amok: Eber;
[21] for Hilkiah: Hashabiah; for Jedaiah: Nethanel.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the next generation each priestly family had a new head. The list shows: the service went on, from generation to generation.
> With “Miniamin” the name of the head is missing in the Hebrew text.

---

## The records and the Levites (Verses 22–26)

[22] In the days of Eliashib, Joiada, Johanan, and Jaddua,
the heads of the Levite families were written down.
The heads of the priest families were written down too,
during the rule of Darius the Persian.
[23] The heads of the families of the descendants of Levi were written in the book of the chronicles.
The list goes until the days of Johanan the son of Eliashib.
[24] The leaders of the Levites were Hashabiah, Sherebiah, and Jeshua the son of Kadmiel.
Their brothers stood close to them.
They praised God and gave thanks,
as David, the man of God, had commanded.
One group answered the other group, section next to section.
[25] Mattaniah, Bakbukiah, Obadiah, Meshullam, Talmon, and Akkub were gatekeepers.
They kept watch at the storerooms by the gates.
[26] All these served in the days of Joiakim the son of Jeshua, the son of Jozadak.
They also served in the days of Nehemiah the governor
and of Ezra the priest and scribe.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Darius the Persian” is probably Darius II (423–404 BC). So this note was added somewhat later.
> “Section next to section”: the choirs stood next to each other or opposite each other and sang in turns, as David had ordered (1 Chronicles 25).

---

## Getting ready for the dedication (Verses 27–30)

[27] Then the wall of Jerusalem was dedicated to God.
For this they looked for the Levites in all the places where they lived.
They brought them to Jerusalem to celebrate the dedication with joy.
They gave thanks and sang songs
with cymbals, stringed instruments, and harps.
[28] The singers came together
from the area around Jerusalem
and from the villages of the Netophathites.
[29] They also came from Beth Gilgal and from the fields of Geba and Azmaveth.
The singers had built villages for themselves around Jerusalem.
[30] The priests and the Levites made themselves pure for God.
Then they made the people, the gates, and the wall pure.

> **What does this mean?**
> The wall is not only a building. It is dedicated to God. That is why there is a cleansing: first the priests themselves, then the people, then the gates and the wall.

---

## Two choirs on the wall (Verses 31–42)

[31] Then I brought the leaders of Judah up onto the wall.
I set up two large choirs.
They gave thanks and walked in a procession.
The first choir went to the right on the wall, toward the Dung Gate.
[32] Behind them walked Hoshaiah with half of the leaders of Judah,
[33] and Azariah, Ezra, and Meshullam,
[34] Judah, Benjamin, Shemaiah, Jeremiah,
[35] and some of the priests’ sons with trumpets:
Zechariah the son of Jonathan, the son of Shemaiah, the son of Mattaniah,
the son of Micaiah, the son of Zaccur, the son of Asaph,
[36] and his brothers Shemaiah, Azarel, Milalai, Gilalai, Maai, Nethanel, Judah, and Hanani.
They had the musical instruments of David, the man of God.
Ezra the scribe walked in front of them.
[37] At the Spring Gate they went straight up the stairs of the City of David,
where the wall goes up.
They went above David’s palace, as far as the Water Gate in the east.
[38] The other choir of those who gave thanks went the opposite way.
I followed them with half of the people.
We walked on the wall above the Tower of the Ovens, as far as the Broad Wall,
[39] and above the Gate of Ephraim, by the Old Gate, by the Fish Gate,
by the tower of Hananel and the tower of Hammeah, as far as the Sheep Gate.
They stopped at the Gate of the Guard.
[40] Then both choirs of those who gave thanks took their places in God’s house.
I did the same, and so did half of the leaders who were with me.
[41] The priests Eliakim, Maaseiah, Miniamin, Micaiah, Elioenai, Zechariah, and Hananiah were there with trumpets,
[42] and also Maaseiah, Shemaiah, Eleazar, Uzzi, Jehohanan, Malchijah, Elam, and Ezer.
The singers sang loudly.
Jezrahiah was their leader.

> **What does this mean?**
> What a picture: two great choirs walk on the wall around the city in opposite directions. One is led by Ezra, the other by Nehemiah. They meet at the temple.
> The wall Tobiah mocked, saying “a fox … would break down their stone wall” (Chapter 4:3), now carries a whole procession.
> The way of the choirs follows the list in Chapter 3, only in the opposite direction.

---

## Great joy (Verse 43)

[43] On that day they offered many sacrifices.
They were full of joy,
because God had given them great joy.
The women and the children were full of joy too.
The joy in Jerusalem could be heard far away.

> **What does this mean?**
> The word “joy” comes five times in this verse (in Hebrew). It is the high point of the book.
> Everyone rejoices: men, women and children. And the joy is so loud that it is heard far away (compare Ezra 3:13).

---

## Providing for the temple service (Verses 44–47)

[44] On that day men were put in charge of the storerooms.
The treasures, the wave offerings, the first fruits, and the tithes were kept there.
A tithe is a tenth of the harvest.
The law set a share for the priests and the Levites.
The men were to gather these shares from the fields around the cities and bring them into the storerooms.
The people of Judah were glad about the priests and about the Levites who served.
[45] The priests and Levites did the work their God had given them.
They took care of the cleansing.
The singers and the gatekeepers did their work too.
They did everything as David and his son Solomon had commanded.
[46] Long ago, in the days of David and Asaph,
there was a leader of the singers.
There were songs of praise and songs of thanks to God.
[47] In the days of Zerubbabel and in the days of Nehemiah,
all Israel gave the singers and the gatekeepers their shares,
as they needed them each day.
They set apart a share for the Levites.
And the Levites set apart a share for the descendants of Aaron.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people gladly provide for those who serve in the temple. “The people of Judah were glad about the priests and about the Levites.”
> The order of worship continues what David and Solomon began. The old heritage lives on.
