# Nehemiah – Chapter 3
### Everyone helps to build

---

## From the Sheep Gate to the Old Gate (Verses 1–5)

[1] Eliashib the high priest and his relatives, the priests, got to work.
They built the Sheep Gate.
They made it holy and put its doors in place.
They also made the wall holy as far as the Tower of Hammeah
and the Tower of Hananel.
[2] The men of Jericho built the next section.
Zaccur, the son of Imri, built the section after that.
[3] The sons of Hassenaah built the Fish Gate.
They put in its beams.
They put its doors, bolts, and bars in place.
[4] Meremoth, the son of Uriah, the son of Hakkoz, repaired the next section.
Meshullam, the son of Berechiah, the son of Meshezabel, repaired the section after that.
Zadok, the son of Baana, repaired the next one.
[5] The people of Tekoa repaired the next section.
But their nobles would not bend their necks to do the Lord’s work.

> **What does this mean?**
> This chapter is a list: who built which piece of the wall? It begins in the north at the Sheep Gate and goes once around the city, counterclockwise.
> The high priest begins. The leaders set a good example.
> The Sheep Gate was near the temple. The sheep for the offerings were probably brought in there. Later the pool of Bethesda, where Jesus healed a sick man, was also near the Sheep Gate (John 5:2).
> Verse 5 is honest: the nobles of Tekoa thought they were too fine for the work. But the ordinary people of Tekoa even built two sections (verse 27).
> “The Lord’s work”: the Hebrew says “the work of their lords.” This means either God or Nehemiah as governor.

---

## From the Old Gate to the Valley Gate (Verses 6–13)

[6] Joiada, the son of Paseah, and Meshullam, the son of Besodeiah, repaired the Old Gate.
They put in its beams.
They put its doors, bolts, and bars in place.
[7] Next to them, Melatiah from Gibeon and Jadon from Meronoth made repairs.
They were men from Gibeon and Mizpah.
They repaired the residence of the governor of the land beyond the River.
[8] Next to them, Uzziel, the son of Harhaiah, made repairs.
He was one of the goldsmiths.
Next to him, Hananiah made repairs.
He was one of the perfume makers.
They made Jerusalem strong as far as the Broad Wall.
[9] Next to them, Rephaiah, the son of Hur, made repairs.
He was the ruler of half the district of Jerusalem.
[10] Next to them, Jedaiah, the son of Harumaph, made repairs across from his house.
Next to him, Hattush, the son of Hashabneiah, made repairs.
[11] Malchijah, the son of Harim, and Hasshub, the son of Pahathmoab,
repaired another section and the Tower of the Ovens.
[12] Next to them, Shallum, the son of Hallohesh, made repairs.
He was the ruler of half the district of Jerusalem.
His daughters helped him.
[13] Hanun and the people of Zanoah repaired the Valley Gate.
They built it and put its doors, bolts, and bars in place.
They also repaired 1,000 cubits of the wall, about 1,500 feet,
as far as the Dung Gate.

> **What does this mean?**
> People from all trades help to build: goldsmiths, perfume makers, district rulers. Everyone helps, even people who are not builders.
> Verse 12 is remarkable: Shallum builds together with his daughters. Women worked on building the wall too.
> Many build “across from their house.” Each one takes care of the piece that is closest to him. So the work is well divided, and everyone has a reason to be careful.
> The Bible says “1,000 cubits.” A cubit is about 18 inches. Here the wall was probably less damaged.

---

## From the Dung Gate to the water (Verses 14–16)

[14] Malchijah, the son of Rechab, repaired the Dung Gate.
He was the ruler of the district of Beth Haccherem.
He built the gate and put its doors, bolts, and bars in place.
[15] Shallun, the son of Colhozeh, repaired the Spring Gate.
He was the ruler of the district of Mizpah.
He built the gate and put a roof over it.
He put its doors, bolts, and bars in place.
He also repaired the wall of the Pool of Shelah, next to the king’s garden.
He repaired it as far as the stairs that go down from David’s city.
[16] After him, Nehemiah, the son of Azbuk, made repairs.
He was the ruler of half the district of Beth Zur.
He repaired the wall up to the place across from the tombs of David,
up to the pool that had been built,
and up to the house of the warriors.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Dung Gate led to the Valley of Hinnom, where the city’s garbage was taken.
> The pool of Shelah is probably the pool of Siloam. Later Jesus sent a blind man there to wash (John 9:7).
> This Nehemiah, the son of Azbuk, is not the same as Nehemiah the governor.
> The “tombs of David” were in the old City of David. Today no one knows exactly where.

## The Levites and priests (Verses 17–22)

[17] After him, the Levites made repairs.
Rehum, the son of Bani, made repairs.
Next to him, Hashabiah made repairs for his district.
He was the ruler of half the district of Keilah.
[18] After him, their relatives made repairs.
Bavvai, the son of Henadad, the ruler of half the district of Keilah, made repairs.
[19] Next to him, Ezer, the son of Jeshua, the ruler of Mizpah, repaired another section.
It was across from the way up to the armory, where the wall turns.
An armory is a building where weapons are kept.
[20] After him, Baruch, the son of Zabbai, worked earnestly and repaired another section.
It went from the place where the wall turns
to the door of the house of Eliashib the high priest.
[21] After him, Meremoth, the son of Uriah, the son of Hakkoz, repaired another section.
It went from the door of Eliashib’s house to the end of Eliashib’s house.
[22] After him, the priests from the surrounding area made repairs.

> **What does this mean?**
> Baruch works “earnestly.” He is the only one of whom this is said directly. God sees who puts in a special effort.
> Meremoth builds two sections (verses 4 and 21). He was the priest to whom Ezra had handed over the treasures (Ezra 8:33).
> “Bavvai”: some manuscripts have “Binnui,” as in verse 24.

## As far as the Ophel (Verses 23–27)

[23] After them, Benjamin and Hasshub made repairs across from their house.
After them, Azariah, the son of Maaseiah, the son of Ananiah,
made repairs next to his own house.
[24] After him, Binnui, the son of Henadad, repaired another section.
It went from Azariah’s house to the place where the wall turns, at the corner.
[25] Palal, the son of Uzai, made repairs across from the place where the wall turns.
He also repaired the tower that sticks out from the upper palace of the king,
next to the courtyard of the guard.
After him, Pedaiah, the son of Parosh, made repairs.
[26] (The temple servants lived on the Ophel hill.
They lived as far as the place across from the Water Gate on the east side,
and as far as the tower that sticks out.)
[27] After him, the people of Tekoa repaired another section.
It went from across from the great tower that sticks out
to the wall of the Ophel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Ophel is the hill between the old City of David and the temple mount.
> The “Water Gate” was probably called that because the way to the Gihon spring led from there. On the square in front of the Water Gate, Ezra later read the law to the people (Chapter 8:1).
> The people of Tekoa build a second section, although their nobles did not help (verse 5).

---

## From the Horse Gate back to the Sheep Gate (Verses 28–32)

[28] The priests made repairs above the Horse Gate.
Each of them repaired the section across from his own house.
[29] After them, Zadok, the son of Immer, made repairs across from his own house.
After him, Shemaiah, the son of Shecaniah, made repairs.
He was the guard of the East Gate.
[30] After him, Hananiah, the son of Shelemiah,
and Hanun, the sixth son of Zalaph, repaired another section.
After him, Meshullam, the son of Berechiah, made repairs across from his room.
[31] After him, Malchijah made repairs.
He was one of the goldsmiths.
He repaired the wall as far as the house of the temple servants and the merchants,
across from the Gate of Hammiphkad,
and as far as the ascent of the corner.
[32] Between the ascent of the corner and the Sheep Gate,
the goldsmiths and the merchants made repairs.

> **What does this mean?**
> The circle closes: the list ends again at the Sheep Gate, where it began.
> “Gate of Hammiphkad” means something like “Gate of Inspection”. Maybe soldiers were inspected there, or goods were checked.
> “Ascent of the corner”: the Hebrew word can also mean “upper room,” that is, a room up at the corner of the wall.
> The message of this chapter: a great work succeeds when everyone lends a hand. Priests and merchants, men and women, people from the city and from the country. Each in his place. And every name is known to God.
