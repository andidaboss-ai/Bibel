# Nehemiah – Chapter 8
### Ezra reads the law aloud

---

## The great assembly at the Water Gate (Verses 1–8)

[1] All the people came together as one man.
They gathered in the open square in front of the Water Gate.
They asked Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses.
The LORD had given this law to Israel.
[2] So Ezra the priest brought the law to the assembly.
Men and women were there, and everyone who could listen and understand.
It was the first day of the seventh month.
[3] Ezra read from the book in the open square in front of the Water Gate.
He read from early morning until noon.
The men and the women and everyone who could understand were there.
All the people listened closely to the book of the law.
[4] Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform.
They had made it for this purpose.
On his right stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah.
On his left stood Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, and Meshullam.
[5] Ezra opened the book where all the people could see it.
He was standing higher than all the people.
When he opened it, all the people stood up.
[6] Then Ezra praised the LORD, the great God.
All the people lifted up their hands and answered, “Amen, Amen!”
They bowed their heads.
They worshiped the LORD with their faces to the ground.
[7] The Levites helped the people understand the law.
Among them were Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah,
Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, and Pelaiah.
The people stayed in their places.
[8] They read clearly from the book, from the law of God.
They explained the meaning.
So the people understood what was read.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important moments in the book of Nehemiah. The people themselves ask Ezra to read the law aloud. They are hungry for God’s word.
> The first day of the seventh month is a feast day (today: Rosh Hashanah, the Jewish New Year).
> Men and women and all the children who can understand listen. God’s word is for everyone.
> They listen for six hours, from morning until midday.
> When Ezra opens the book, the people stand up, out of reverence for God’s word. To this day people in synagogues and churches stand up when the Bible is read aloud.
> Verse 8 is important: it is not enough only to read God’s word aloud. It must also be explained, so that people understand it. Maybe the Hebrew text was also translated into Aramaic, because after the exile many no longer understood Hebrew well. That is exactly what this plain-language Bible is about too.

## “The joy of the LORD is your strength” (Verses 9–12)

[9] Then Nehemiah the governor, Ezra the priest and scribe,
and the Levites who taught the people spoke to all the people.
They said, “Today is a holy day for the LORD your God.
Don’t be sad, and don’t cry.”
They said this because all the people were crying when they heard the words of the law.
[10] Then he said to them,
“Go home.
Eat rich food and drink sweet drinks.
Send some of it to people who have nothing ready.
Today is a holy day for our Lord.
Don’t be sad.
The joy of the LORD is your strength.”
[11] The Levites calmed all the people.
They said, “Be quiet, because the day is holy.
Don’t be sad.”
[12] Then all the people went home.
They ate and drank and sent food to others.
They celebrated with great joy.
They had understood the words that were told to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> When the people hear the law, they weep. They notice how far they are from God’s will.
> But Nehemiah and Ezra say: today is a feast day! Today is not a day for weeping, but for celebrating.
> “The joy of the LORD is your strength”: this is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible. Some Bibles translate it as “joy in the LORD”. Both are possible: joy about God gives strength. And God’s own joy about his people gives strength. The strength for a life with God does not come from feelings of guilt, but from joy.
> Important: “Send some of it to people who have nothing ready.” A feast includes not forgetting the poor. No one is to go hungry on a feast day.
> Verse 12: they celebrate a great feast of joy. “They had understood the words that were told to them.” Understanding leads to joy.

## The feast of booths (Verses 13–18)

[13] On the second day the heads of the families of all the people came together with Ezra the scribe.
The priests and the Levites came too.
They wanted to study the words of the law.
[14] In the law they found what the LORD had commanded through Moses:
The people of Israel should live in booths during the feast in the seventh month.
Booths are simple huts made of branches.
[15] And they should announce this in all their cities and in Jerusalem:
“Go out to the mountains.
Get olive branches, wild olive branches, myrtle branches, palm branches,
and branches of leafy trees.
Make booths with them, as it is written.”
[16] So the people went out and brought branches.
They made booths for themselves.
Each one made a booth on the roof of his house,
or in their courtyards, or in the courtyards of God’s house,
or in the open square of the Water Gate,
or in the open square of the Gate of Ephraim.
[17] The whole assembly of those who had come back from captivity made booths.
They lived in the booths.
The people of Israel had not done this since the days of Joshua the son of Nun.
There was very great joy.
[18] He read from the book of the law of God every day,
from the first day to the last day.
They kept the feast for seven days.
On the eighth day there was a special holy meeting, as the law commands.

> **What does this mean?**
> The leaders want to learn even more. While reading, they discover the feast of booths (Leviticus 23:33–43).
> The feast of booths recalls the 40 years in the wilderness, when the people lived in tents and God provided for them. For those who had returned it had a special meaning: they too had gone through a kind of wilderness and had experienced God’s care.
> The people of Israel “had not done this since the days of Joshua”: the feast had already been celebrated (Ezra 3:4). But not like this, with the whole people in booths. This way of celebrating was completely new.
> To this day Jews celebrate the feast of booths (Sukkot) and build huts out of branches.
> Reading the Bible is not boring, but leads to “very great joy”.
