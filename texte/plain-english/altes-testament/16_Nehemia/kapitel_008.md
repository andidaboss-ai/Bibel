# Nehemiah – Chapter 8
### Ezra reads the law aloud

---

## The great assembly at the Water Gate (Verses 1–8)

[1] All the people gathered together as one man
in the open square in front of the Water Gate.
They asked Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses,
which the LORD had commanded to Israel.
[2] Ezra the priest brought the law before the assembly,
men and women, and all who could listen with understanding,
on the first day of the seventh month.
[3] He read from it in the open square in front of the Water Gate,
from early morning until midday,
before the men and the women and those who could understand.
And all the people listened closely to the book of the law.
[4] Ezra the scribe stood on a platform of wood,
which they had made for this purpose.
Beside him on his right stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah,
and on his left Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, and Meshullam.
[5] Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people,
for he was standing above all the people.
When he opened it, all the people stood up.
[6] Then Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God.
All the people answered, “Amen, Amen,”
and lifted up their hands.
They bowed their heads
and worshiped the LORD with their faces to the ground.
[7] Also Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah,
Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah, and the Levites
helped the people to understand the law.
And the people stayed in their places.
[8] They read in the book, in the law of God, clearly,
and they explained the meaning,
so that the people understood what was read.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important moments in the book of Nehemiah. The people themselves ask Ezra to read the law aloud. They are hungry for God’s word.
> The first day of the seventh month is a feast day (today: Rosh Hashanah, the Jewish New Year).
> Men and women and all the children who can understand listen. God’s word is for everyone.
> They listen for six hours, from morning until midday.
> When Ezra opens the book, the people stand up, out of reverence for God’s word. To this day people in synagogues and churches stand up when the Bible is read aloud.
> Verse 8 is important: it is not enough only to read God’s word aloud. It must also be explained, so that people understand it. Maybe the Hebrew text was also translated into Aramaic, because after the exile many no longer understood Hebrew well. That is exactly what this plain-language Bible is about too.

## “The joy of the LORD is your strength” (Verses 9–12)

[9] Nehemiah, who was the governor,
Ezra the priest and scribe,
and the Levites who taught the people
said to all the people,
“Today is holy to the LORD your God.
Don’t mourn or weep.”
For all the people wept when they heard the words of the law.
[10] Then he said to them,
“Go your way.
Eat the fat, drink the sweet,
and send portions to him for whom nothing is prepared,
for today is holy to our Lord.
Don’t be sad, for the joy of the LORD is your strength.”
[11] So the Levites calmed all the people and said,
“Be quiet, for the day is holy.
Don’t be sad.”
[12] All the people went their way
to eat, to drink, to send portions, and to celebrate with great joy,
because they had understood the words that were told to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> When the people hear the law, they weep. They notice how far they are from God’s will.
> But Nehemiah and Ezra say: today is a feast day! Today is not a day for weeping, but for celebrating.
> “The joy of the LORD is your strength”: this is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible. Some Bibles translate it as “joy in the LORD”. Both are possible: joy about God gives strength. And God’s own joy about his people gives strength. The strength for a life with God does not come from feelings of guilt, but from joy.
> Important: “Send portions to him for whom nothing is prepared.” A feast includes not forgetting the poor. No one is to go hungry on a feast day.
> Verse 12: they celebrate a great feast of joy, “because they had understood the words that were told to them”. Understanding leads to joy.

## The feast of booths (Verses 13–18)

[13] On the second day the heads of the families of all the people,
the priests, and the Levites gathered together with Ezra the scribe,
to study the words of the law.
[14] They found written in the law
that the LORD had commanded through Moses
that the children of Israel should live in booths
during the feast of the seventh month,
[15] and that they should announce it in all their cities and in Jerusalem:
“Go out to the mountains,
and get olive branches, branches of wild olive, myrtle branches, palm branches,
and branches of leafy trees,
to make booths, as it is written.”
[16] So the people went out and brought them,
and made themselves booths,
each one on the roof of his house,
in their courtyards, in the courts of God’s house,
in the open square of the Water Gate,
and in the open square of the Gate of Ephraim.
[17] The whole assembly of those who had come back out of the captivity
made booths and lived in the booths.
For since the days of Joshua the son of Nun until that day,
the children of Israel had not done so.
And there was very great joy.
[18] Day by day, from the first day to the last day,
he read in the book of the law of God.
They kept the feast for seven days,
and on the eighth day there was a solemn assembly, according to the rule.

> **What does this mean?**
> The leaders want to learn even more. While reading, they discover the feast of booths (Leviticus 23:33–43).
> The feast of booths recalls the 40 years in the wilderness, when the people lived in tents and God provided for them. For those who had returned it had a special meaning: they too had gone through a kind of wilderness and had experienced God’s care.
> “Since the days of Joshua … had not done so”: the feast had already been celebrated (Ezra 3:4). But not like this, with the whole people in booths. This way of celebrating was completely new.
> To this day Jews celebrate the feast of booths (Sukkot) and build huts out of branches.
> Reading the Bible is not boring, but leads to “very great joy”.
