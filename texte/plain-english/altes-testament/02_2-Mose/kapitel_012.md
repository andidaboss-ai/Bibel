# Exodus – Chapter 12
### The first Passover – the going out from Egypt

---

## A new calendar (Verses 1–2)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt.
He said,
[2] “This month will be the beginning of months for you.
It will be the first month of the year for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Being set free is so important that a new year begins with it. The life of the people starts completely new.
> This month is in the spring, in March or April. Later it was called “Nisan.”

---

## The Passover lamb (Verses 3–11)

[3] “Speak to the whole community of Israel.
Tell them this:
On the tenth day of this month, every man must take a lamb for his family,
one lamb for each household.
[4] If a household is too small for a whole lamb,
then he must share one with his nearest neighbor.
Count by the number of people.
Figure out how much lamb you need by how much each person can eat.
[5] Your lamb must be without any defect, a male, one year old.
You may take it from the sheep or from the goats.
[6] Keep it until the fourteenth day of this month.
Then the whole gathered community of Israel must kill it in the evening.

[7] “Take some of the blood.
Put it on the two doorposts and on the beam above the door of the houses where you eat it.
[8] That night you must eat the meat, roasted over the fire.
Eat it with unleavened bread and with bitter herbs.
Unleavened bread is bread made without yeast.
[9] Do not eat it raw or boiled in water.
Eat it roasted over the fire, with its head, its legs and its inner parts.
[10] Do not leave any of it until the morning.
Whatever is left until the morning, you must burn in the fire.

[11] “This is how you must eat it:
with your belt around your waist, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand.
Eat it in a hurry.
It is the LORD’s Passover.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Passover” (in Hebrew “Pesach”) means “passing over” or “sparing.” God passes over the houses of the Israelites and spares them.
> Every family is to join the celebration. Small and poor families too: They join together with their neighbors.
> The blood on the door is a sign of protection.
> Unleavened bread is bread without sourdough or yeast. It is flat and quick to make. There is no time to wait for the dough to rise.
> The bitter herbs are a reminder of the bitter life in slavery (Exodus 1:14).
> People eat dressed and ready to travel, with shoes on their feet. Very soon they will set out into freedom.

---

## God’s judgment and the sign of the blood (Verses 12–14)

[12] “For on that night I will go through the land of Egypt.
I will strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both people and animals.
And I will bring judgment on all the gods of Egypt.
I am the LORD.
[13] The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are.
When I see the blood, I will pass over you.
No plague will come on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.

[14] “This day will be a day of remembering for you.
You must celebrate it as a festival for the LORD.
Celebrate it as a festival through all your generations.
This is a rule forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> The plagues were also a judgment on the gods of Egypt. They showed: These gods cannot help.
> The blood of the lamb saves. Death passes by the house with the blood.
> To this day, Jews celebrate the Passover festival every year, for more than 3,000 years, generation after generation.
> For Christians, this festival has a special meaning: Jesus died exactly at the time of the Passover festival. Before that, he celebrated the Passover meal with his disciples – the Last Supper. In the New Testament, Jesus is called “our Passover lamb” (1 Corinthians 5:7). Christians believe: His blood saves from death.

---

## The festival of unleavened bread (Verses 15–20)

[15] “For seven days you must eat unleavened bread.
On the very first day you must remove all yeast from your houses.
For whoever eats bread made with yeast from the first day to the seventh day
must be cut off from Israel.
[16] On the first day you must have a holy gathering,
and on the seventh day a holy gathering too.
No kind of work may be done on these days.
Only the food that everyone must eat may be made by you.

[17] “Keep the festival of unleavened bread.
For on this very day I brought your armies out of the land of Egypt.
So you must keep this day through all your generations.
This is a rule forever.

[18] “In the first month, from the evening of the fourteenth day of the month,
you must eat unleavened bread until the evening of the twenty-first day of the month.
[19] For seven days there must be no yeast in your houses.
For whoever eats anything made with yeast must be cut off from the community of Israel,
whether he is a foreigner or someone born in the land.
[20] Eat nothing made with yeast.
In all the places where you live, you must eat unleavened bread.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Besides the Passover meal on the one night, there is a festival that lasts seven days. For a whole week, people eat only flat bread without yeast. Jews call it “matzah.”
> In the Bible, yeast often stands for something old that spreads everywhere. Before the new beginning comes, the old is cleared away. To this day, Jewish families clean the whole house before the Passover festival and look for every crumb of bread made with yeast.
> “To be cut off” means: to no longer belong to the community of the people.

---

## Moses passes on the instructions (Verses 21–28)

[21] Then Moses called all the elders of Israel.
He said to them,
“Go and pick out lambs for your families.
And kill the Passover lamb.
[22] Take a bunch of hyssop.
Dip it in the blood that is in the bowl.
Then put some of the blood from the bowl on the beam above the door and on the two doorposts.
None of you may go out of the door of his house until the morning.

[23] “For the LORD will go through the land to strike the Egyptians.
When he sees the blood on the beam above the door and on the two doorposts,
the LORD will pass over the door.
He will not let the destroyer come into your houses to strike you.

[24] “You must keep this.
It is a rule for you and for your sons forever.
[25] When you come to the land that the LORD will give you, as he has promised,
you must keep this service.
[26] When your children ask you,
‘What does this service mean to you?’
[27] then you must say,
‘It is the sacrifice of the LORD’s Passover.
He passed over the houses of the people of Israel in Egypt
when he struck the Egyptians.
He spared our houses.’”
Then the people bowed their heads and worshiped.

[28] The people of Israel went and did so.
They did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.

> **What does this mean?**
> Hyssop is a small plant with many leaves. People could use it like a brush.
> The “destroyer” is a picture for death that goes through the land.
> The children are to ask. And the parents are to tell. To this day, at the Passover festival, the youngest child asks: “Why is this night different from all other nights?” Then the adults tell the story of how God set his people free.
> Faith is passed on by telling what God has done.

---

## The last plague (Verses 29–36)

[29] At midnight the LORD struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt,
from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sat on his throne,
to the firstborn of the prisoner who was in the dungeon,
and all the firstborn of the livestock.
[30] Pharaoh got up in the night, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians.
And there was a great cry in Egypt.
For there was not a house where no one was dead.

[31] That same night Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron.
He said,
“Get up! Get out from among my people, both you and the people of Israel!
Go, serve the LORD, as you have said!
[32] Take your flocks and your herds too, as you have said.
Go!
And bless me too!”

[33] The Egyptians pushed the people to leave the land quickly.
For they said,
“We will all be dead!”
[34] So the people took their dough before yeast had made it rise.
They wrapped their bowls for making dough in their clothes and carried them on their shoulders.

[35] The people of Israel did what Moses had said.
They asked the Egyptians for jewelry of silver, jewelry of gold, and clothing.
[36] The LORD had made the Egyptians kind toward the people.
So the Egyptians gave them what they asked for.
In this way they took the riches of the Egyptians.

> **What does this mean?**
> What God announced now happens. In every house in Egypt, people mourn – in the palace too. Pharaoh loses his own son.
> Now Pharaoh finally gives in. He gives everything he refused before: Everyone may go, with all the livestock.
> “And bless me too!” – The proud Pharaoh, who thought he was a god, asks the slaves for their blessing.
> The Israelites must leave so quickly that the dough has no time to rise. The unleavened bread is a reminder of this.

---

## Setting out (Verses 37–42)

[37] The people of Israel traveled from Rameses to Succoth.
There were about 600,000 men on foot, not counting the children.
[38] A large mixed crowd of other people went up with them too.
There were also flocks and herds, a very large number of livestock.
[39] From the dough that they had brought out of Egypt, they baked flat cakes without yeast.
For the dough had not risen.
They had been driven out of Egypt.
They could not wait, and they had not made any food for the journey.

[40] The people of Israel had lived in Egypt for 430 years.
[41] At the end of the 430 years, on that very day,
all the armies of the LORD went out of the land of Egypt.
[42] It is a night when the LORD kept watch, to bring them out of the land of Egypt.
This is the LORD’s night.
All the people of Israel must keep watch on this night through all their generations.

> **What does this mean?**
> 600,000 men – with women and children, that would be more than two million people. That is a huge number. Some experts think that the Hebrew word for “thousand” could also mean “family” or “group” here. Then there would have been fewer people. Others take the number literally.
> A “large mixed crowd of other people” goes with them. They were probably Egyptians and other slaves who joined them. From the very beginning, God’s people are open to people from other nations.
> 430 years – a long time. God had already told Abraham that his descendants would live in a foreign land for a long time (Genesis 15:13).
> God “kept watch” on this night. That is why the Israelites are to stay awake on this night and celebrate.

---

## Who may celebrate the Passover? (Verses 43–51)

[43] The LORD said to Moses and Aaron,
“This is the rule for the Passover:
No foreigner may eat of it.
[44] But every slave who was bought for money may eat of it once you have circumcised him.
[45] A foreigner who is only staying with you and a hired worker may not eat of it.
[46] The Passover lamb must be eaten in one house.
You must not carry any of the meat outside the house.
And you must not break any of its bones.
[47] The whole community of Israel must keep it.

[48] “A stranger may live with you as a foreigner and may want to keep the Passover for the LORD.
Then all the males in his family must be circumcised.
After that he may come and keep it.
He will be like someone born in the land.
But no one who is not circumcised may eat of it.
[49] The same law is for the one who is born in the land and for the foreigner who lives among you.”

[50] All the people of Israel did so.
They did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.
[51] On that very day, the LORD brought the people of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover is a festival for the people of the covenant. Whoever wants to celebrate it belongs to the people – through circumcision, the sign of the covenant with Abraham.
> But foreigners may join too. When they turn to the God of Israel, they are like people born in the land. “The same law is for the one who is born in the land and for the foreigner.” These are great words: equal rights for all.
> “Do not break any of its bones” – the Gospel of John remembers this when it tells about the death of Jesus: None of his bones were broken (John 19:36).
