# Exodus – Chapter 34
### New tablets – God says who he is – Moses’ shining face

---

## Two new tablets (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Cut two stone tablets like the first ones.
I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you broke.
[2] Be ready by the morning.
In the morning, come up Mount Sinai
and stand before me there on the top of the mountain.
[3] No one shall come up with you.
No one shall be seen anywhere on the mountain.
And do not let the flocks or the herds graze in front of that mountain.”
[4] So Moses cut two tablets of stone like the first ones.
Moses got up early in the morning and went up Mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him.
And he took the two stone tablets in his hand.

> **What does this mean?**
> God gives the people a second chance. The covenant is made new.
> This time, Moses has to cut the stone tablets himself. But God writes the words again.

---

## God says who he is (Verses 5–9)

[5] The LORD came down in the cloud.
He stood with him there and called out the name of the LORD.
[6] The LORD passed by in front of him and called out,
“The LORD! The LORD!
A God who is full of mercy and kindness,
slow to become angry, and rich in loving kindness and faithfulness.
[7] He keeps loving kindness for thousands.
He forgives wrongdoing, disobedience and sin.
But he will not simply let the guilty go free.
He brings the wrongdoing of the fathers on the children and on the grandchildren,
on the third and on the fourth generation.”
[8] Moses quickly bowed his head to the ground and worshiped.
[9] He said,
“Lord, if I have now found favor in your eyes,
then please, Lord, go among us.
Yes, these are a stiff-necked people.
But forgive our wrongdoing and our sin.
And take us as your own.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the whole Old Testament. God himself says who he is. These words are repeated many times in the Bible (for example, Psalm 103:8; Jonah 4:2).
> The first thing God says about himself is: full of mercy, kind, patient, full of love and faithfulness. And this comes right after the golden calf!
> “Loving kindness for thousands” and punishment up to the “third and fourth generation” – the love is much, much greater than the punishment.
> The punishment up to the third and fourth generation means: The results of guilt go on in families. Children often suffer because of what their parents did wrong. Back then, three or four generations often lived together in one house. Later the Bible says clearly: Each person is responsible only for his own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).
> “Loving kindness” stands for the Hebrew word “chesed.” It means a faithful love that keeps its promise.
> Now Moses no longer says “these people,” but “us.” He takes the side of the people completely.

## God makes the covenant new (Verses 10–17)

[10] He said,
“Look, I am making a covenant.
In front of all your people I will do wonders,
such as have never been done in all the earth or in any nation.
All the people among whom you live shall see the work of the LORD.
For what I will do with you is an awesome thing.

[11] Keep what I command you today.
Look, I will drive out the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites in front of you.
[12] Be careful not to make a covenant with the people who live in the land where you are going.
Otherwise it will become a trap among you.
[13] Instead, you shall break down their altars,
smash their holy stone pillars
and cut down their Asherah poles.
[14] For you shall worship no other god.
For the LORD, whose name is ‘Jealous,’ is a jealous God.

[15] “Do not make a covenant with the people who live in the land.
Otherwise they will give themselves to their gods like prostitutes and offer sacrifices to their gods.
And someone will invite you, and you will eat from his sacrifice.
[16] And you will take their daughters as wives for your sons.
Then their daughters will give themselves to their gods like prostitutes,
and they will make your sons give themselves to their gods like prostitutes too.

[17] “Do not make any idols of melted metal for yourselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Asherah poles” were wooden poles that were set up for the goddess Asherah. Asherah was a goddess of fertility in Canaan.
> Israel shall not get involved with the gods of the other peoples. God compares this to unfaithfulness in a marriage. That is why the hard picture of prostitution is used here. God and his people are like husband and wife. Whoever serves other gods breaks faith.
> “Jealous” is even a name of God. God loves his people with all his passion and does not want to share them with anyone.
> This is not about hating people from other nations. It is about faith: Israel shall stay faithful to the one God only. Later, for example, Ruth, a woman from Moab, marries into Israel, because she turns to Israel’s God (Ruth 1:16).
> “Idols of melted metal” – verse 17 is a direct reminder of the golden calf.

## The feasts and the gifts (Verses 18–26)

[18] “Keep the feast of unleavened bread.
For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you,
at the set time in the month of Abib.
For in the month of Abib you came out of Egypt.

[19] “Everything that is born first from its mother is mine.
That includes all the male firstborn of your livestock, of cattle and of sheep.
[20] Buy back the firstborn of a donkey with a lamb.
If you will not buy it back, then break its neck.
Buy back all the firstborn of your sons.
No one shall come before me with empty hands.

[21] “You shall work for six days, but on the seventh day you shall rest.
Even in the time of plowing and in the time of harvest you shall rest.

[22] “Keep the feast of weeks, with the first fruits of the wheat harvest.
And keep the feast of harvest at the end of the year.
[23] Three times a year all your men shall come before the Lord GOD, the God of Israel.
[24] For I will drive out nations in front of you and make your land larger.
No one will want to take your land
when you go up three times a year to come before the LORD your God.

[25] “Do not offer the blood of my sacrifice together with bread made with yeast.
And do not leave any of the sacrifice of the feast of the Passover until the morning.
[26] “Bring the very first of the first fruits of your fields to the house of the LORD your God.
“Do not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.”

> **What does this mean?**
> We already know these commandments from Chapters 13 and 23. They are repeated here, because the covenant is being made new.
> “Buy back” means: You give something else in its place, for example a lamb for the donkey. A donkey could not be sacrificed. And people were never sacrificed. For every firstborn son, a gift was given instead.
> The Sabbath rest also counts in the time of harvest, when there is the most work. Especially then, people should trust God.
> “No one will want to take your land” – God promises: When all the men go to the feast, God watches over their land. No one will attack it.
> You can read more about the feasts and the young goat in the explanations for Exodus 23.

## Forty days on the mountain (Verses 27–28)

[27] The LORD said to Moses,
“Write down these words.
For on the basis of these words I have made a covenant with you and with Israel.”
[28] Moses was there with the LORD for 40 days and 40 nights.
He did not eat any bread or drink any water.
And he wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the Ten Commandments.

> **What does this mean?**
> For the second time, Moses is on the mountain for 40 days. He fasts completely, without food and drink. That is more than a person can normally stand. God himself keeps him alive.
> Here it is not completely clear who writes on the tablets. In verse 1, God had said that he himself would write. Deuteronomy 10:4 also says: God wrote.
> Later, Jesus also fasted for 40 days in the desert (Matthew 4:2).

## Moses’ face shines (Verses 29–35)

[29] Moses came down from Mount Sinai.
He had the two tablets of the covenant in his hand.
When he came down from the mountain, Moses did not know that the skin of his face was shining,
because he had been speaking with God.
[30] Aaron and all the people of Israel saw Moses.
And look, the skin of his face was shining.
So they were afraid to come near him.
[31] Moses called to them.
Then Aaron and all the leaders of the community came back to him,
and Moses spoke to them.
[32] After that, all the people of Israel came near.
And he gave them all the commandments that the LORD had spoken with him on Mount Sinai.

[33] When Moses had finished speaking with them, he put a veil over his face.
[34] But whenever Moses went in before the LORD to speak with him, he took the veil off until he came out.
Then he came out and told the people of Israel what he had been commanded.
[35] The people of Israel saw Moses’ face, that the skin of Moses’ face was shining.
Then Moses put the veil over his face again, until he went in to speak with God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses was so close to God that some of God’s light stayed on him. He himself does not even notice it.
> The Hebrew word for “shine” is related to the word for “horn.” So an old Latin translation wrote that Moses had “horns.” That is why some old pictures and statues show Moses with horns, for example the famous statue by Michelangelo in Rome. But what is meant is rays of light.
> Moses does not wear the veil when he speaks with God or passes on God’s words. He wears it afterward, in everyday life.
> In the New Testament, Paul writes about this story (2 Corinthians 3:7–18). He says: Whoever turns to God has the veil taken away. And people who look at God are changed more and more themselves.
