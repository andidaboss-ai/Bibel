# Exodus – Chapter 18
### Jethro visits Moses and gives him good advice

---

## A family visit in the desert (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses’ father-in-law, heard about everything God had done for Moses and for Israel, his people.
He heard how the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.
[2] Moses had sent his wife Zipporah away before.
Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took her in
[3] with her two sons.
The name of one son was Gershom.
For Moses had said,
“I have lived as a foreigner in a foreign land.”
[4] The name of the other was Eliezer.
For Moses had said,
“My father’s God was my help.
He saved me from Pharaoh’s sword.”

[5] Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, came to Moses in the wilderness with Moses’ sons and his wife.
Moses was camped at the mountain of God.
[6] Jethro said to Moses,
“I, your father-in-law Jethro, have come to you,
with your wife and her two sons.”

[7] Moses went out to meet his father-in-law.
He bowed down and kissed him.
They asked each other how they were.
Then they went into the tent.
[8] Moses told his father-in-law everything the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel’s sake.
He told him about all the troubles they had met on the way,
and how the LORD had saved them.

> **What does this mean?**
> At some point, Moses had sent his wife and his sons back to her father. Maybe to protect them during the dangerous time in Egypt.
> Now Jethro brings the family back together.
> “Eliezer” means “My God is help.”
> The two men meet each other with great respect and warmth. And Moses tells what God has done – the good things and also the troubles.

---

## Jethro praises God (Verses 9–12)

[9] Jethro was glad about all the good things the LORD had done for Israel.
He was glad that the LORD had saved them from the hand of the Egyptians.
[10] Jethro said,
“Blessed be the LORD!
He has saved you from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of Pharaoh.
He has freed the people from the power of the Egyptians.
[11] Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods.
That became clear when they treated the people so proudly.”

[12] Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, brought a burnt offering and other sacrifices for God.
And Aaron came with all the elders of Israel to eat a meal with Moses’ father-in-law before God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jethro is not an Israelite. He is a priest from Midian. But when he hears what God has done, he praises the God of Israel.
> “Now I know” – Jethro sees what Pharaoh did not want to see: The LORD is greater than all gods.
> A foreigner brings the first sacrifice after leaving Egypt, and the elders of Israel eat with him. God’s people are open to people from other nations who come to know God.

---

## Moses works too much (Verses 13–18)

[13] On the next day, Moses sat down to judge the people.
And the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.
[14] Moses’ father-in-law saw everything he was doing for the people.
He said,
“What is this that you are doing for the people?
Why do you sit here all alone,
while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?”

[15] Moses said to his father-in-law,
“Because the people come to me to ask God for guidance.
[16] When they have a dispute, they come to me.
Then I decide between a man and his neighbor.
And I teach them God’s rules and his laws.”

[17] Moses’ father-in-law said to him,
“What you are doing is not good.
[18] You will completely wear yourself out, both you and the people who are with you.
For this task is too heavy for you.
You cannot do it alone.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses does everything alone. From morning until evening, he listens to disputes. And the people have to wait all day.
> Jethro sees it from the outside and says honestly: “What you are doing is not good.”
> This sounds very modern: Whoever wants to do everything alone burns out. And the others suffer from it too.

---

## Jethro’s advice (Verses 19–23)

[19] “Now listen to me.
I will give you advice, and may God be with you.
You must stand for the people before God.
Bring their cases to God.
[20] Teach them the rules and the laws.
Show them the way they must walk and the work they must do.

[21] “But choose capable men from all the people.
They must fear God.
They must be honest men who hate dishonest gain.
Put them over the people as leaders:
leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens.
[22] Let them judge the people at all times.
They must bring every big case to you.
But they must decide every small case themselves.
So it will be easier for you.
And they will carry the load together with you.
[23] If you do this, and God commands you to,
then you will be able to keep going.
And all these people will go home in peace.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jethro suggests: Share the work. Others decide the small cases. Only the hard ones come to Moses.
> He also says what good leaders should be like: They must fear God, be honest and not take bribes. This is still true today for everyone who has responsibility.
> “To fear God” means: to take God seriously and to answer to him.
> Jethro is modest. He says: Do it like this – if God commands you to. God has the last word.

---

## Moses listens to the advice (Verses 24–27)

[24] Moses listened to his father-in-law and did everything he had said.
[25] Moses chose capable men from all Israel.
He made them heads over the people:
leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens.
[26] They judged the people at all times.
They brought the hard cases to Moses.
But they decided every small case themselves.

[27] Then Moses let his father-in-law leave.
And Jethro went back to his own land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is the great leader who speaks with God. Still, he is humble enough to listen to the advice of his father-in-law.
> Good ideas may also come from the outside, from people of another nation and with a different background.
> Before God gives the people his law at Sinai, an order for fair judges is already set up. Law and justice belong to the foundation of a people.
