# Exodus – Chapter 6
### God’s promise – the family of Moses and Aaron

---

## “I am the LORD” (Verses 1–8)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Now you will see what I will do to Pharaoh.
A strong hand will force him to let them go.
Yes, a strong hand will force him to drive them out of his land.”

[2] God spoke to Moses.
He said to him,
“I am the LORD.
[3] I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob as God Almighty.
But by my name, the LORD, I did not make myself known to them.
[4] I also made my covenant with them.
I promised to give them the land of Canaan,
the land where they traveled and lived as foreigners.
[5] I have also heard the groaning of the people of Israel,
whom the Egyptians keep as slaves.
And I have remembered my covenant.

[6] “So tell the people of Israel,
‘I am the LORD.
I will bring you out from under the heavy work of the Egyptians.
I will free you from their slavery.
I will rescue you with an outstretched arm and with great judgments.
[7] I will take you as my own people.
I will be your God.
Then you will know that I am the LORD your God,
who brings you out from under the heavy work of the Egyptians.
[8] I will bring you into the land that I swore to give to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.
I will give it to you as your own.
I am the LORD.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses had accused God (Chapter 5). God does not answer with an explanation, but with promises.
> Verse 3 is hard to understand: The fathers already used the name “the LORD” in the book of Genesis. Many interpreters understand it like this: The fathers knew the name, but they had not yet experienced what it means. Only now, as he frees his people from Egypt, does God show who he really is: the God who is there and who saves.
> At the beginning and at the end of this speech is the same sentence: “I am the LORD.” Everything God promises stands on this foundation.
> God promises seven things: to bring out, to free, to rescue, to take as his people, to be their God, to bring them into the land, to give them the land. To this day, Jews remember these promises at the Passover festival with four cups of wine.
> In Hebrew, “swore” here is literally: “lifted up my hand.” That means: made a solemn promise.

## No one listens (Verses 9–13)

[9] Moses told all this to the people of Israel.
But they did not listen to Moses.
For they had lost all hope because of the cruel slavery.

[10] Then the LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[11] “Go in and speak to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.
Tell him to let the people of Israel go out of his land.”
[12] But Moses said to the LORD,
“Look, the people of Israel have not listened to me.
How then will Pharaoh listen to me,
when I have uncircumcised lips?”

[13] Then the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron.
He gave them a command for the people of Israel and for Pharaoh, the king of Egypt:
They were to bring the people of Israel out of the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Israelites can no longer hear the good news. They are too worn out and too full of despair. When someone suffers very much, sometimes they cannot accept hope anymore.
> This is understandable. God does not blame them for it.
> Moses literally says: “I have uncircumcised lips.” This is an old picture and means: I am clumsy at speaking.

## The families of Reuben, Simeon and Levi (Verses 14–25)

[14] These are the heads of their families:
The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel:
Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.
These are the families of Reuben.
[15] The sons of Simeon:
Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar,
and Shaul, the son of a Canaanite woman.
These are the families of Simeon.

[16] These are the names of the sons of Levi, by their generations:
Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.
Levi lived 137 years.
[17] The sons of Gershon:
Libni and Shimei, by their families.
[18] The sons of Kohath:
Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel.
Kohath lived 133 years.
[19] The sons of Merari:
Mahli and Mushi.
These are the families of the Levites, by their generations.

[20] Amram married Jochebed, his father’s sister.
She gave birth to Aaron and Moses.
Amram lived 137 years.
[21] The sons of Izhar:
Korah, Nepheg, and Zichri.
[22] The sons of Uzziel:
Mishael, Elzaphan, and Sithri.

[23] Aaron married Elisheba.
She was the daughter of Amminadab and the sister of Nahshon.
She gave birth to Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.
[24] The sons of Korah:
Assir, Elkanah, and Abiasaph.
These are the families of the Korahites.
[25] Eleazar, Aaron’s son, married one of the daughters of Putiel.
She gave birth to Phinehas.
These are the heads of the families of the Levites, by their families.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the exciting story, there is suddenly a family list. It shows: Moses and Aaron are not just anyone. They have a family history. They come from Levi, a son of Jacob.
> The list begins with Reuben and Simeon, the oldest sons of Jacob, and then comes to Levi. With Levi, it becomes detailed, because Moses and Aaron and later all the priests come from this tribe.
> Moses’ parents are Amram and Jochebed. Jochebed was Amram’s aunt. Later God forbade such marriages (Leviticus 18:12).
> Some names will become important later: Korah will lead a rebellion against Moses (Numbers 16). Nadab and Abihu die because they do not honor God (Leviticus 10). Phinehas will become a brave priest.
> Nahshon later became the leader of the tribe of Judah and an ancestor of King David.

## Moses and Aaron (Verses 26–30)

[26] These are the Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said,
“Bring the people of Israel out of the land of Egypt, in order, by their armies.”
[27] They are the ones who spoke to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt,
to bring the people of Israel out of Egypt.
These are that Moses and Aaron.

[28] On the day when the LORD spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,
[29] the LORD said to Moses,
“I am the LORD.
Tell Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, everything that I tell you.”
[30] But Moses said to the LORD,
“Look, I have uncircumcised lips.
How will Pharaoh listen to me?”

> **What does this mean?**
> After the list, the story goes back exactly to where it stopped: to Moses’ doubts.
> “By their armies” means here: the people, in order like a great army. The slaves are not to leave as scattered refugees, but as God’s people.
