# Exodus – Chapter 13
### The firstborn belong to God – the pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire

---

## All the firstborn belong to God (Verses 1–2)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[2] “Set apart for me every firstborn.
Among the people of Israel, everything that comes first out of its mother’s womb belongs to me,
both people and animals.
It is mine.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Egypt, the firstborn died. The firstborn of Israel were spared. So now they are to belong to God in a special way.
> “To set apart” means: to give something to God so that it belongs to him.

---

## Remember this day (Verses 3–10)

[3] Moses said to the people,
“Remember this day,
the day you came out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
For the LORD brought you out from this place by the strength of his hand.
No bread made with yeast may be eaten.
[4] Today you are going out, in the month of Abib.

[5] “The LORD will bring you into the land of the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.
He swore to your ancestors that he would give it to you,
a land flowing with milk and honey.
Then you must keep this service in this month.
[6] For seven days you must eat bread without yeast.
And on the seventh day there will be a festival for the LORD.
[7] You must eat bread without yeast for all seven days.
No bread made with yeast may be seen among you.
No yeast may be seen among you anywhere in your land.

[8] “On that day you must tell your son,
‘It is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.’
[9] It will be for you like a sign on your hand
and like a reminder between your eyes,
so that the law of the LORD may be in your mouth.
For the LORD brought you out of Egypt with a strong hand.
[10] So you must keep this rule at its set time every year.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Abib” is the old name of the month. It means “young ears of grain.” It is the time in the spring when the grain grows. Later the month was called “Nisan.”
> Important: The father does not say “what God did for our ancestors,” but “what the LORD did for **me**.” Every generation is to celebrate being set free as if they themselves had been there.
> “A sign on your hand and between your eyes” means: You must always think of it, in everything you do and see. Devout Jews take this literally too, to this day. At morning prayer they tie small boxes with Bible verses to their arm and forehead. They are called “tefillin.”

---

## The firstborn are bought back (Verses 11–16)

[11] “The LORD will bring you into the land of the Canaanite,
as he swore to you and to your ancestors.
He will give it to you.
[12] Then you must set apart for the LORD everything that comes first out of its mother’s womb.
Every firstborn of your animals that is male belongs to the LORD.
[13] You must buy back every firstborn donkey with a lamb.
If you do not buy it back, you must break its neck.
And you must buy back every firstborn among your sons.

[14] “In the future your son will ask you, ‘What does this mean?’
Then you must tell him,
‘The LORD brought us out of Egypt by the strength of his hand, out of the house of slavery.
[15] Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go.
Then the LORD killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt,
both the firstborn of people and the firstborn of livestock.
That is why I sacrifice to the LORD every male that comes first out of its mother’s womb.
But I buy back every firstborn of my sons.’
[16] It will be like a sign on your hand and like a reminder between your eyes.
For the LORD brought us out of Egypt by the strength of his hand.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “To buy back” means: to get something back by giving something else in its place.
> A donkey was thought of as an “unclean” animal. People were not allowed to sacrifice it. So they give a lamb in its place.
> Very important: People are **never** sacrificed. The firstborn sons are always bought back. God does not want human sacrifices (see also Genesis 22).
> To this day, Jews have a celebration when a firstborn son is one month old. It is called “Pidyon HaBen,” which means “buying back the son.”
> Jesus too, as a firstborn, was presented to God in the temple (Luke 2:22–23).

## God chooses the way (Verses 17–22)

[17] Pharaoh had let the people go.
But God did not lead them on the road through the land of the Philistines, even though that way was near.
For God said,
“The people might change their minds when they see war.
Then they would go back to Egypt.”
[18] So God led the people around, on the road through the wilderness to the Red Sea.
The people of Israel went up out of the land of Egypt ready for battle.

[19] Moses took the bones of Joseph with him.
For Joseph had made the people of Israel swear an oath.
He had said,
“God will surely come to help you.
Then you must carry my bones up from here with you.”

[20] They set out from Succoth.
They camped in Etham, at the edge of the wilderness.
[21] The LORD went ahead of them:
by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them on their way,
and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light.
So they could travel by day and by night.
[22] The pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night did not leave the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> The shortest way to Canaan went along the coast. But there were Egyptian forts there and the warlike Philistines. God knows his people. After slavery, they are not yet ready for a fight. So he chooses a longer, but safer way.
> Sometimes God’s way is a detour. But he has a good reason.
> Moses keeps the promise that Joseph was given more than 400 years before (Genesis 50:25). Joseph’s faith that his people would return comes true.
> The pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire show: God is among his people. He leads. He protects. He is always there, by day and by night.
