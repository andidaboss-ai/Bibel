# Exodus – Chapter 2
### Moses is born and saved

---

## The child in the basket (Verses 1–10)

[1] A man from the family of Levi married a woman.
She was a daughter of Levi too.
[2] The woman became pregnant and gave birth to a son.
She saw that he was a fine child.
So she hid him for three months.
[3] When she could not hide him any longer, she took a basket made of papyrus.
She coated it with tar and with pitch.
Then she put the child in it.
And she laid the basket in the reeds at the bank of the river.
[4] His sister stood a little way off.
She wanted to see what would happen to him.

[5] Then Pharaoh’s daughter came down to the river to bathe.
Her young women walked along the riverbank.
She saw the basket among the reeds.
She sent her servant to get it.
[6] She opened it and saw the child.
The baby was crying.
She felt sorry for him.
She said,
“This is one of the Hebrews’ children.”

[7] Then his sister said to Pharaoh’s daughter,
“Should I go and call a nurse for you from the Hebrew women?
She can nurse the child for you.”
A nurse is a woman who breastfeeds a baby.
[8] Pharaoh’s daughter said to her,
“Go.”
So the girl went and called the child’s mother.
[9] Pharaoh’s daughter said to her,
“Take this child away and nurse him for me.
I will pay you for it.”
So the woman took the child and nursed him.

[10] The child grew.
Then she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter.
And he became her son.
She named him Moses.
She said,
“Because I drew him out of the water.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses comes from the family of Levi. Later we learn the names of his parents: Amram and Jochebed (Chapter 6:20). His sister is called Miriam.
> In Hebrew, the word for “basket” is the same word as “ark” in the story of Noah. Like Noah in his ark, Moses is saved from death in the water in his little “box.”
> Papyrus is a reed plant that grows by the Nile. People also made paper from it.
> Again it is women who save a life: the mother, the sister – and even Pharaoh’s daughter, who does not obey her own father.
> What a turn: The mother gets her own child back – and is even paid for it!
> The name “Moses” probably comes from Egyptian and means “son” or “born.” In Hebrew it sounds like “to draw out.” Later Moses himself will “draw” his people out of Egypt.

## Moses kills an Egyptian (Verses 11–15)

[11] In those days, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his brothers.
He saw how hard they had to work.
He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his brothers.
[12] He looked this way and that way.
When he saw that no one was there, he killed the Egyptian.
And he hid him in the sand.

[13] On the next day he went out again.
Two Hebrew men were fighting with each other.
He said to the one who was in the wrong,
“Why are you hitting your neighbor?”
[14] The man said,
“Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?
Do you plan to kill me, the way you killed the Egyptian?”
Then Moses was afraid.
He said, “So people know about this.”

[15] When Pharaoh heard about it, he tried to kill Moses.
But Moses fled from Pharaoh.
He went to live in the land of Midian.
There he sat down by a well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses grew up at Pharaoh’s court. But he knows that he is a Hebrew. He calls the slaves “his brothers.”
> He sees injustice and wants to help. But he does it on his own and with violence. He kills a person and hides the body.
> The Bible does not praise this. Moses wants justice, but he goes the wrong way. He first has to learn to listen to God.
> Midian is a desert region east of Egypt, probably in the northwest of today’s Saudi Arabia. The Midianites came from Abraham and Keturah (Genesis 25:2).

## Moses in Midian (Verses 16–22)

[16] The priest of Midian had seven daughters.
They came to the well and drew water.
They filled the troughs to give water to their father’s flock.
A trough is a long, open box for water.
[17] Then shepherds came and drove them away.
But Moses stood up and helped them.
He gave water to their flock.

[18] When they came to Reuel, their father, he asked,
“Why have you come back so early today?”
[19] They said,
“An Egyptian saved us from the shepherds.
He even drew water for us and gave water to the flock.”
[20] He said to his daughters,
“Where is he?
Why did you leave the man there?
Call him, so that he can eat with us.”

[21] Moses was glad to stay with the man.
And the man gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as his wife.
[22] She gave birth to a son.
Moses named him Gershom.
He said,
“I have lived as a foreigner in a foreign land.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Again there is a meeting at a well, like with Rebekah and with Rachel. And again it leads to a wedding.
> Moses stands up for the weak again. This time without violence.
> The women think Moses is an Egyptian, because he is dressed like one.
> The priest of Midian has two names: Reuel and Jethro (Chapter 3:1).
> “Gershom” sounds like “a foreigner there.” Moses feels he has no home: In Egypt he was a Hebrew, in Midian he is a foreigner.

## God hears his people cry (Verses 23–25)

[23] Many years went by.
Then the king of Egypt died.
The people of Israel groaned because of their slavery.
They cried out for help.
And their cry because of the slavery went up to God.
[24] God heard their groaning.
And God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.
A covenant is a binding agreement.
[25] God saw the people of Israel.
And God understood.

> **What does this mean?**
> These three verses are the turning point. Here it says “God” four times: God hears. God remembers his covenant. God sees. God understands.
> The people suffered for a long time. It seemed as if God was far away. But God has not forgotten anything. He has heard every cry.
> “God understood” means more than: He had information. It means: He knows their suffering from the inside. He feels with them.
> This message is still true today for all who are oppressed: God hears your cry.
