# Exodus – Chapter 10
### Locusts and darkness

---

## A warning about the locusts (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go to Pharaoh.
For I have made his heart hard, and the heart of his servants,
so that I may show these signs of mine among them.
[2] And so that you may tell your son and your grandson
what I have done to Egypt,
and the signs that I have done among them.
Then you will know that I am the LORD.”

[3] So Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said to him,
“The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says this:
‘How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?
Let my people go, so that they may serve me.
[4] If you refuse to let my people go,
look: Tomorrow I will bring locusts into your country.
[5] They will cover the surface of the ground,
so that no one will be able to see the ground.
They will eat what is left over for you, what escaped the hail.
And they will eat every tree that grows for you in the field.
[6] Your houses will be full of them,
and the houses of all your servants and the houses of all the Egyptians.
Neither your ancestors nor their ancestors have seen anything like it,
from the day they came onto the earth until today.’”
Then Moses turned and went out from Pharaoh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here God explains why he does all this: The story is to be told again and again, by parents to children and grandchildren. To this day, Jewish families tell this story to their children every year at the Passover festival.
> Locusts are large insects. They fly in huge swarms with millions of animals and eat up everything green in a short time. To this day, swarms of locusts are a feared plague in Africa and in the Middle East.
> “To humble yourself before me” means: to become humble, to admit that God is greater.

---

## The officials grow impatient (Verses 7–11)

[7] Pharaoh’s servants said to him,
“How long will this man be a trap for us?
Let the men go, so that they may serve the LORD, their God.
Do you still not understand that Egypt is ruined?”

[8] So Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh.
He said to them,
“Go, serve the LORD, your God.
But who exactly will go?”
[9] Moses said,
“We will go with our young and with our old.
We will go with our sons and with our daughters,
with our flocks and with our herds.
For we must hold a festival for the LORD.”

[10] Pharaoh said to them,
“The LORD will have to be with you if I ever let you go with your little children!
Look, you clearly have evil plans!
[11] No, not like this!
You men go and serve the LORD.
That is what you want after all!”
Then they were driven out from Pharaoh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now even Pharaoh’s own officials push him. They see: Egypt is being ruined. But Pharaoh stays stubborn.
> Pharaoh wants to let only the men go. The women and children are to stay behind as a guarantee. Then the men would surely come back.
> Moses insists: Everyone belongs. Young and old, men and women. A festival for God is a festival for the whole family.
> Pharaoh means “The LORD will have to be with you” in a mocking way. He does not believe in it.

---

## The eighth plague: locusts (Verses 12–20)

[12] The LORD said to Moses,
“Reach out your hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts,
so that they come up over the land of Egypt
and eat every plant in the land, everything that the hail has left.”
[13] So Moses reached out his staff over the land of Egypt.
And the LORD made an east wind blow over the land all that day and all night.
When it was morning, the east wind had brought the locusts.

[14] The locusts came up over the whole land of Egypt.
They settled down in every part of Egypt.
It was a terrible swarm.
There had never been so many locusts before, and there will never be so many again.
[15] They covered the surface of the whole ground, so that the land became dark.
They ate every plant in the land and all the fruit on the trees that the hail had left.
In the whole land of Egypt, nothing green was left, no tree and no plant of the field.

[16] Then Pharaoh quickly called for Moses and Aaron.
He said,
“I have sinned against the LORD, your God, and against you.
[17] Now please forgive my sin just this once more.
And pray to the LORD, your God, that he will at least take this deadly disaster away from me.”
[18] So Moses went out from Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD.
[19] Then the LORD sent a very strong west wind.
It picked up the locusts and drove them into the Red Sea.
Not one locust was left in all of Egypt.

[20] But the LORD made Pharaoh’s heart hard.
He did not let the people of Israel go.

> **What does this mean?**
> What the hail left behind, the locusts now eat. Egypt is bare. A famine is coming.
> Pharaoh admits his guilt – again only because he is in trouble.
> In the Hebrew original, the sea is called the “Sea of Reeds.” Many Bibles say “Red Sea.” Later the Israelites will go through this sea.

---

## The ninth plague: darkness (Verses 21–29)

[21] The LORD said to Moses,
“Reach out your hand toward the sky.
Then darkness will come over the land of Egypt,
a darkness that people can feel.”
[22] So Moses reached out his hand toward the sky.
And there was thick darkness in the whole land of Egypt for three days.
[23] People could not see one another.
For three days, no one got up from his place.
But all the people of Israel had light where they lived.

[24] Then Pharaoh called for Moses.
He said,
“Go, serve the LORD.
Only your flocks and your herds must stay here.
Your little children may go with you too.”
[25] Moses said,
“You must also give us animals for sacrifices and burnt offerings,
so that we can sacrifice them to the LORD, our God.
[26] Our livestock must also go with us.
Not one hoof may be left behind.
For we must take some of them to serve the LORD, our God.
And we do not know what we must use to serve the LORD until we get there.”
A burnt offering is an animal that is completely burned on the altar for God.

[27] But the LORD made Pharaoh’s heart hard.
He would not let them go.
[28] Pharaoh said to Moses,
“Get away from me!
Be careful never to see my face again!
For on the day you see my face, you will die!”
[29] Moses said,
“You have spoken well.
I will never see your face again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The highest god of the Egyptians was the sun god Ra. Pharaoh was thought to be his son. Now the sun is gone for three days. The sun god has no power.
> But the Israelites have light. That is a strong picture: In the middle of darkness, God’s people have light.
> Again Pharaoh wants to bargain: Now all the people may go, but the livestock must stay. Without livestock they could not survive in the desert – and would have to come back.
> “Not one hoof” means: not one single animal.
> Pharaoh threatens Moses with death. Moses answers calmly: Fine, we will not see each other again. The conversation is over. Now the last plague comes.
