# Exodus – Chapter 8
### Frogs, lice and flies

---

## The second plague: frogs (Verses 1–15)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go to Pharaoh and tell him,
‘The LORD says this:
“Let my people go, so that they may serve me.
[2] If you refuse to let them go,
look: I will strike your whole country with frogs.
[3] The river will swarm with frogs.
They will come up and come into your house,
into your bedroom and onto your bed,
into the houses of your servants and onto your people,
into your ovens and into your bowls for making dough.
[4] The frogs will come up onto you,
onto your people and onto all your servants.”’”

[5] The LORD said to Moses,
“Tell Aaron,
‘Reach out your hand with your staff over the rivers, over the streams, and over the pools.
Make frogs come up onto the land of Egypt.’”
[6] So Aaron reached out his hand over the waters of Egypt.
And the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt.
[7] But the magicians did the same thing with their secret arts.
They too made frogs come up onto the land of Egypt.

[8] Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron.
He said,
“Pray to the LORD that he takes the frogs away from me and from my people.
Then I will let the people go, so that they can offer sacrifices to the LORD.”
[9] Moses said to Pharaoh,
“You may have the honor of choosing the time when I should pray for you, for your servants, and for your people.
Then the frogs will be gone from you and from your houses.
They will stay only in the river.”
[10] Pharaoh said,
“Tomorrow.”
Moses said,
“Let it be as you say.
Then you will know that there is no one like the LORD, our God.
[11] The frogs will leave you, your houses, your servants, and your people.
They will stay only in the river.”

[12] Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh.
And Moses cried out to the LORD about the frogs that he had brought on Pharaoh.
[13] The LORD did what Moses asked.
The frogs died in the houses, in the courtyards, and in the fields.
[14] People gathered them together in heaps.
And the land stank.
[15] But when Pharaoh saw that he had some relief, he made his heart hard.
He did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, the verses of this chapter have different numbers. There, the beginning (verses 1–4) is still part of Chapter 7. This Bible follows the numbering of the WEB, our English source text.
> Frogs everywhere: in bed, in the oven, in the dough. A disgusting, annoying plague. The Egyptians worshiped a goddess with the head of a frog. She stood for having many children and good harvests. Now frogs become a plague.
> The magicians can make even more frogs appear – but not one single frog goes away because of them. Only God can end the plague.
> Moses lets Pharaoh choose the time. So no one can say it was by chance.
> As soon as the trouble is over, Pharaoh forgets his promise. People today know this too: When they are in trouble, they pray. Afterward, everything is forgotten.

## The third plague: lice (Verses 16–19)

[16] The LORD said to Moses,
“Tell Aaron,
‘Reach out your staff and strike the dust of the earth.
Then it will become lice in the whole land of Egypt.’”
[17] They did so.
Aaron reached out his hand with his staff and struck the dust of the earth.
Then there were lice on people and on animals.
All the dust of the earth became lice in the whole land of Egypt.

[18] The magicians tried with their secret arts to make lice too.
But they could not.
There were lice on people and on animals.
[19] Then the magicians said to Pharaoh,
“This is God’s finger.”
But Pharaoh’s heart stayed hard.
He did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Hebrew word can mean lice, tiny biting gnats, or fleas. It means tiny pests that crawl and bite everywhere.
> This plague comes without a warning.
> Now the magicians are at their end. They admit: “This is God’s finger.” That means: Here a power is at work that is greater than all human skill.
> Pharaoh’s own advisers see it. Only Pharaoh does not want to see it.

## The fourth plague: flies (Verses 20–32)

[20] The LORD said to Moses,
“Get up early in the morning and stand before Pharaoh.
Look, he will be coming out to the water.
Tell him,
‘The LORD says this:
“Let my people go, so that they may serve me.
[21] If you do not let my people go,
look: I will send swarms of flies on you, on your servants, on your people, and into your houses.
The houses of the Egyptians will be full of swarms of flies,
and so will the ground they stand on.
[22] But on that day I will set apart the land of Goshen, where my people live.
There will be no swarms of flies there.
By this you will know that I am the LORD on the earth.
[23] I will make a difference between my people and your people.
This sign will happen tomorrow.”’”

[24] The LORD did so.
Terrible swarms of flies came into the house of Pharaoh and into the houses of his servants.
In the whole land of Egypt, the land was ruined by the swarms of flies.

[25] Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron.
He said,
“Go and offer sacrifices to your God here in the land!”
[26] Moses said,
“That would not be right.
For what we sacrifice to the LORD, our God, is disgusting to the Egyptians.
Look, if we sacrifice before their eyes what is disgusting to the Egyptians,
will they not throw stones at us until we die?
[27] We will go on a journey of three days into the wilderness.
There we will sacrifice to the LORD, our God, as he will command us.”

[28] Pharaoh said,
“I will let you go, so that you may sacrifice to the LORD, your God, in the wilderness.
Only do not go very far away.
Pray for me.”
[29] Moses said,
“Look, I am leaving you now.
I will pray to the LORD that the swarms of flies leave Pharaoh, his servants, and his people tomorrow.
Only Pharaoh must not trick us again
by not letting the people go to sacrifice to the LORD.”

[30] Moses went out from Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD.
[31] The LORD did what Moses asked.
He took the swarms of flies away from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people.
Not one fly was left.
[32] But Pharaoh made his heart hard this time too.
He did not let the people go.

> **What does this mean?**
> New in this plague: God makes a difference. The land of Goshen, where the Israelites live, is spared. This shows: It is not by chance. God is on the side of his people.
> Pharaoh begins to bargain: First “offer sacrifices to your God here in the land,” then “only do not go very far away.” He wants to give in a little, but not completely.
> The Israelites sacrificed sheep, goats and cattle. Some of these animals were holy in Egypt. A sacrifice before the eyes of the Egyptians would have caused great anger.
> “Pray for me.” – The mighty Pharaoh needs the prayers of his slaves.
