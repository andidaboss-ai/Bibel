# Exodus – Chapter 20
### The Ten Commandments

---

## I am the LORD your God (Verses 1–2)

[1] Then God spoke all these words:
[2] “I am the LORD your God.
I brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now come the Ten Commandments. They are among the best-known words in the world. They are found here and once again in Deuteronomy 5.
> Very important: the commandments do not begin with an order, but with a reminder: “I brought you out of the land of Egypt.” God set them free. First comes God’s rescue, then come the commandments. The commandments are not chains. They show how a free person can live well.
> Jews count this sentence as the first of the “Ten Words.” For in Hebrew, the Bible actually calls the commandments “the ten words” (Exodus 34:28).
> The commandments speak to “you” – one person. God speaks to each person personally.

---

## No other gods (Verses 3–6)

[3] You must have no other gods besides me.

[4] You must not make an idol for yourself.
You must not make any image at all of anything
that is in the sky above, or on the earth below, or in the water under the earth.
[5] You must not bow down to them or serve them.
For I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God.
For those who hate me, I bring the guilt of the fathers on the children,
to the third and fourth generation.
[6] But to thousands of those who love me and keep my commandments,
I show my loving kindness.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first commandment: God alone is to be God. Nothing else is to be more important to us than he is – not money, not power, not success.
> The ban on images: God cannot be locked up in a picture or a statue. He is greater than anything we can imagine. Back then, the peoples made statues of their gods and worshiped them.
> Christians count the commandments in different ways. Catholics and Lutherans put verses 3–6 together as **one** commandment. Reformed churches, Orthodox churches and Jews count the ban on images as a separate commandment. That is why the commandments are numbered differently in different churches.
> “Jealous” here means: God loves his people so much that he does not want to share them with other gods – just as a person in a marriage does not want to share their partner.
> The comparison is important: the results of guilt reach four generations at most. But God’s loving kindness reaches thousands. God’s love is much greater than his anger.

---

## Do not misuse God’s name (Verse 7)

[7] You must not misuse the name of the LORD your God.
For the LORD will not let anyone go unpunished who misuses his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s name is holy. People should not use it carelessly, for example to curse, to swear falsely or to cheat others.
> The worst is when people use God’s name to justify wrong or to oppress others.

---

## The Sabbath (Verses 8–11)

[8] Remember the Sabbath day and keep it holy.
[9] You are to work for six days and do all your work.
[10] But the seventh day is a Sabbath for the LORD your God.
On that day you must not do any work:
not you, not your son, not your daughter,
not your male servant, not your female servant, not your livestock,
and not the foreigner who lives in your towns.
[11] For in six days the LORD made the sky and the earth, the sea and everything in them.
And on the seventh day he rested.
That is why the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: A Sabbath is a day of rest.
> The Sabbath is a gift: a free day every week.
> What is special: it is for **everyone**. Not only for the head of the house, but also for children, servants, foreigners and even for the animals. No one may be exploited.
> For people who had just been slaves and had to toil every day, this was revolutionary.
> For Jews, the Sabbath is Saturday. Most Christians celebrate Sunday as their day of rest, because Jesus rose from the dead on a Sunday.

---

## Honor father and mother (Verse 12)

[12] Honor your father and your mother.
Then you will live a long time in the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

> **What does this mean?**
> This commandment is mainly for grown-up children. They are to respect their old parents and take care of them when they become weak.
> Father and mother are named equally. Both are to be honored.
> This is the first commandment with a promise: a long life in the land.

---

## Do not murder (Verse 13)

[13] You must not murder.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every human life is holy, because every person is made in God’s image (Genesis 1:27; 9:6).
> The Hebrew word mainly means: to kill a person on purpose and unjustly.
> Jesus made this commandment go even deeper: hatred and anger in the heart against another person are already the beginning of it (Matthew 5:21–22).

---

## Do not break your marriage (Verse 14)

[14] You must not commit adultery.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: To commit adultery means: to have sex with someone else’s husband or wife, or to be unfaithful to your own husband or wife.
> Marriage is a covenant of faithfulness between husband and wife. Whoever breaks the marriage destroys this trust.
> This commandment protects marriage, the family and the children.

---

## Do not steal (Verse 15)

[15] You must not steal.

> **What does this mean?**
> Every person has a right to what belongs to them.
> Ancient Jewish teachers said: this commandment above all also forbids stealing a **person** – that is, kidnapping and slavery.

---

## Do not lie (Verse 16)

[16] You must not give false testimony against your neighbor.

> **What does this mean?**
> First of all, the commandment means: you must not lie in court. With false testimony, you can destroy another person’s life.
> But it also applies in everyday life: you should not speak badly about others, spread rumors or tell lies about them.

---

## Do not covet (Verse 17)

[17] You must not covet your neighbor’s house.
You must not covet your neighbor’s wife,
or his male servant, or his female servant, or his ox, or his donkey,
or anything that belongs to your neighbor.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “To covet” means: to badly want to have something that belongs to someone else. Envy and greed.
> This commandment goes deeper than all the others. It is not only against actions, but against what happens in the heart. For envy and greed in the heart later lead to stealing, adultery and even murder.
> Catholics and Lutherans divide this verse into two commandments: the 9th and the 10th commandment. In this way, they also get to 10.

---

## The people are afraid (Verses 18–21)

[18] All the people saw the thunder and the lightning,
heard the sound of the trumpet and saw the mountain smoking.
When the people saw it, they trembled and stayed far away.
[19] They said to Moses,
“You speak with us, and we will listen.
But do not let God speak with us,
or we will die.”

[20] Moses said to the people,
“Do not be afraid.
God has come to test you,
so that you will have awe of him and not sin.”
[21] So the people stayed far away.
But Moses went near the thick darkness where God was.

> **What does this mean?**
> God’s voice is so mighty that the people cannot bear it. They ask Moses to be the go-between for them and God.
> “Do not be afraid” and “have awe” – both belong together. You do not need to be afraid of God, as if he were a tyrant. But you should take him seriously. Then you live in a way that does not harm others.

---

## Instructions for the altar (Verses 22–26)

[22] The LORD said to Moses,
“Tell the people of Israel this:
‘You yourselves have seen that I spoke with you from heaven.
[23] You must not make gods of silver or gods of gold to set beside me.
You must never make such gods for yourselves.
[24] Make an altar of earth for me.
On it you are to sacrifice your burnt offerings and your peace offerings,
your sheep and your cattle.
In every place where I cause my name to be remembered,
I will come to you and I will bless you.
[25] If you make me an altar of stones,
do not build it from cut stones.
For if you use your tool on it, you make it unclean.
[26] And do not go up to my altar by steps,
so that your private parts are not seen there.’

> **What does this mean?**
> God spoke, not through a picture. That is why the people need no statues of gods.
> The altar is to be very simple: made of earth or of stones that have not been cut. No splendor, no artwork. Not people, but God is to be at the center.
> “Peace offerings” were sacrifices in which one part was given to God and one part was eaten together, like a feast with God.
> “In every place … I will come to you and I will bless you” – God is not only at Sinai. He comes everywhere people honor him.
> Back then, people wore long robes without underwear. Anyone who climbed steps could be seen from below. Worship is to be done with dignity.
