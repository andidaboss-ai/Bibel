# Exodus – Chapter 30
### Altar of incense, offering, basin, anointing oil and incense

---

## The altar of incense (Verses 1–10)

[1] “Make an altar to burn incense on.
Make it of acacia wood.
[2] It shall be about 18 inches long and about 18 inches wide.
So it shall be square.
And it shall be about 3 feet high.
Its horns shall be one piece with it.
[3] Cover it with pure gold:
its top, its sides all around and its horns.
And make a gold molding all around it.
[4] Make two gold rings for it under its molding.
Make them on its two sides, across from each other.
They shall hold the poles for carrying it.
[5] Make the poles of acacia wood and cover them with gold.

[6] Put the altar in front of the veil that is by the ark of the covenant,
in front of the mercy seat that is over the covenant.
There I will meet with you.
[7] Aaron shall burn sweet-smelling incense on it every morning.
When he takes care of the lamps, he shall burn it.
[8] And when Aaron lights the lamps in the evening, he shall burn it too.
So there shall always be incense before the LORD, through all your generations.
[9] Do not offer any strange incense on it,
no burnt offering and no meal offering.
And do not pour any drink offering on it.
[10] Once a year, Aaron shall make atonement on its horns.
With the blood of the sin offering of atonement, he shall make atonement for it once a year, through all your generations.
It is most holy to the LORD.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: one cubit long and wide, two cubits high. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The altar of incense is small. It stands in the Holy Place, right in front of the veil to the Most Holy Place.
> Incense is a mixture of resins and spices. When you sprinkle it on glowing coals, sweet-smelling smoke rises up.
> In the Bible, rising smoke is a picture for prayer. In Psalm 141:2, someone asks God to let his prayer come before God like incense. In the morning and in the evening the sweet smell rises – just like the prayers of the people rise to God.
> “Strange incense” is incense that God did not command. A story in Leviticus 10 shows how serious that is.
> “Once a year” means the great Day of Atonement, Yom Kippur (Leviticus 16).

## The offering at the census (Verses 11–16)

[11] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[12] “You will take a census of the people of Israel.
A census means: counting the people.
When you count them, each man shall give the LORD a ransom for his life.
A ransom is a price paid to set a life free.
Then no plague will come on them when you count them.
[13] Everyone who passes over to those who are counted shall give this:
half a shekel, by the weight of the shekel of the sanctuary.
A shekel is 20 gerahs.
Half a shekel shall be an offering to the LORD.
[14] Everyone who passes over to those who are counted, who is 20 years old or older, shall give the offering to the LORD.
[15] The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less, than the half shekel,
when you give the LORD’s offering to make atonement for your lives.
[16] Take the atonement money from the people of Israel.
Use it for the service of the Tent of Meeting.
It shall be a reminder of the people of Israel before the LORD,
to make atonement for your lives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Half a shekel is about one fifth of an ounce of silver. A “gerah” is an even smaller weight, about one fiftieth of an ounce.
> The men who were counted were 20 years old or older. They were the men who could go to war. Whoever was counted “passed over” to those who had already been counted.
> Back then, people believed: Counting a people is dangerous. Life belongs to God, not to the king or the leader. That is why everyone shall give a small gift – as a sign: My life belongs to God. Later, the Bible tells how King David counts the people, and a plague comes because of it (2 Samuel 24).
> Important: The rich and the poor pay exactly the same. Before God, every life is worth the same.
> Later, part of the sanctuary was built with this money (Exodus 38:25–28). In the time of Jesus, Jews paid a temple tax like this every year (Matthew 17:24).

## The basin (Verses 17–21)

[17] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[18] “Also make a basin of bronze with a base of bronze, for washing.
Put it between the Tent of Meeting and the altar.
And put water in it.
[19] Aaron and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet in it.
[20] When they go into the Tent of Meeting, they shall wash with water, so that they do not die.
They shall also wash when they come near the altar to serve, to burn an offering made by fire to the LORD.
[21] So they shall wash their hands and their feet, so that they do not die.
This shall be a law forever for them,
for Aaron and for his descendants, through all their generations.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Before the priests serve God, they wash. With their hands they do their work. With their feet they walk their way. Both shall be clean.
> Washing is a sign: Whoever comes to God should not just come any way at all, but prepared and cleansed.
> In the New Testament, Jesus washes his disciples’ feet (John 13). And in baptism, washing with water becomes a sign of a new beginning with God.

## The holy anointing oil (Verses 22–33)

[22] The LORD also spoke to Moses.
He said,
[23] “Also take the finest spices:
liquid myrrh, about 12 pounds;
sweet-smelling cinnamon, half as much, about 6 pounds;
and sweet-smelling cane, about 6 pounds;
[24] and cassia, about 12 pounds, by the weight of the shekel of the sanctuary.
Also take about 1 gallon of olive oil.
[25] Make it into a holy anointing oil,
a perfume mixed with the skill of a perfume maker.
It shall be a holy anointing oil.

[26] Use it to anoint
the Tent of Meeting, the ark of the covenant,
[27] the table and all its things, the lamp stand and its tools, the altar of incense,
[28] the altar of burnt offering with all its tools, and the basin with its base.
[29] Make them holy, so that they are most holy.
Whatever touches them will be holy.
[30] Anoint Aaron and his sons too, and make them holy,
so that they can serve me as priests.

[31] Speak to the people of Israel.
Say, ‘This shall be a holy anointing oil for me, through all your generations.
[32] It shall not be poured on the body of an ordinary person.
And do not make any oil like it with the same mixture.
It is holy. It shall be holy to you.
[33] Whoever mixes any oil like it,
or whoever puts any of it on a stranger,
shall be cut off from his people.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the weights are given like this: 500 shekels of myrrh, 250 shekels of cinnamon, 250 shekels of cane, 500 shekels of cassia, and a hin of oil. A shekel is about 0.4 ounces. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> Myrrh is a precious, sweet-smelling resin from a bush. Cassia is a kind of cinnamon. The cane is a sweet-smelling plant, maybe sweet flag.
> This oil has a very special smell. No one may copy it for himself. It belongs only to God. So the people learn: There are things that are set apart completely for God.
> “A stranger” here means: someone who is not a priest.
> “Cut off from his people” – this is word for word what the Hebrew says. It is not completely clear what this meant. Maybe the person was thrown out of the community. Maybe it means that God himself punishes the person.

## The holy incense (Verses 34–38)

[34] The LORD said to Moses,
“Take sweet spices: gum resin, onycha and galbanum.
Take these sweet spices with pure frankincense.
There shall be the same weight of each.
[35] Make incense out of it, a perfume mixed with the skill of a perfume maker.
It shall be seasoned with salt, pure and holy.
[36] Beat some of it into very fine powder.
Put some of it in front of the covenant in the Tent of Meeting, where I will meet with you.
It shall be most holy to you.
[37] Do not make any incense with the same mixture for yourselves.
It shall be holy to you, for the LORD.
[38] Whoever makes any like it, to enjoy its smell,
shall be cut off from his people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The incense is made of four ingredients. Frankincense is a sweet-smelling resin from a tree that grows in Arabia and Africa. Galbanum is a resin from a plant. “Onycha” is probably the shell lid of a sea snail, which smells sweet when it burns.
> Here too: This smell is only for God. No one shall copy it at home for himself.
> To this day, incense is burned in many churches. It is a reminder of this service in the sanctuary and of prayer that rises up to God.
