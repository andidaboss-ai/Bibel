# Exodus – Chapter 19
### At Mount Sinai

---

## Arriving at the mountain of God (Verses 1–2)

[1] In the third month after the people of Israel had left the land of Egypt,
on that very day, they came into the wilderness of Sinai.
[2] They had set out from Rephidim and had come to the wilderness of Sinai.
They set up camp there in the wilderness.
Israel camped in front of the mountain.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the people have reached the goal of the first stage: Mount Sinai. This is where God appeared to Moses in the burning bush. This is where God promised: “You will serve God on this mountain” (Exodus 3:12).
> The people will stay at Sinai for almost a year. Everything told from here to the end of Exodus, in the whole book of Leviticus and at the beginning of Numbers happens here.
> It is not certain exactly where Mount Sinai is. Many people think of a high mountain in the south of the Sinai Peninsula, the “Mountain of Moses” (Jebel Musa).

---

## God’s offer (Verses 3–8)

[3] Moses went up to God.
The LORD called to him from the mountain,
“This is what you are to say to the house of Jacob,
and what you are to tell the people of Israel:
[4] ‘You have seen what I did to the Egyptians.
You have seen how I carried you on eagles’ wings and brought you to myself.
[5] Now, if you will truly listen to my voice and keep my covenant,
then you will be my very own treasure out of all the peoples.
For the whole earth is mine.
[6] You will be a kingdom of priests for me and a holy nation.’
These are the words you are to speak to the people of Israel.”

[7] Moses came and called together the elders of the people.
He told them all these words that the LORD had commanded him.
[8] All the people answered together,
“We will do everything the LORD has said.”
And Moses brought the people’s answer back to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> First God reminds them of what he has done. Only then does he say what he wishes from the people. With God, the gift always comes first, then the task.
> “Carried on eagles’ wings” – a beautiful picture. An eagle carries its young safely when they are learning to fly. In the same way, God has carried his people through all dangers.
> God offers a covenant. Israel is to be his “very own treasure” – like a precious treasure. But not because the other peoples do not matter to God: “For the whole earth is mine.”
> “A kingdom of priests” means: the whole people is to stand between God and the world. It is to show the other peoples who God is. In the New Testament, this word is applied to all Christians: “a royal priesthood, a holy nation” (1 Peter 2:9).
> The people say yes right away. Whether they will keep their promise will soon become clear.

---

## Getting ready to meet God (Verses 9–15)

[9] The LORD said to Moses,
“Look, I am coming to you in a thick cloud.
The people are to hear when I speak with you.
Then they will believe you forever.”
And Moses told the LORD what the people had said.

[10] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go to the people.
Make them holy today and tomorrow.
They are to wash their clothes.
[11] They are to be ready on the third day.
For on the third day the LORD will come down on Mount Sinai in front of all the people.
[12] Mark a boundary for the people all around the mountain.
Tell them,
‘Be careful not to go up the mountain or even touch its edge.
Anyone who touches the mountain must be put to death.
[13] No hand is to touch that person.
He must be stoned or shot with arrows.
Whether animal or human, he must not live.’
Only when the trumpet sounds a long blast may they come up to the mountain.”

[14] So Moses went down from the mountain to the people.
He made the people holy, and they washed their clothes.
[15] He said to the people,
“Be ready by the third day.
Do not have sex with a woman.”

> **What does this mean?**
> You do not meet a king in dirty clothes. How much more is this true for God! The people are to get ready on the inside and on the outside.
> “To make holy” or “to get ready” means: to prepare for meeting God.
> The boundary around the mountain shows: God is near, but he is also holy and powerful. Nobody can just come near him however they like.
> The “trumpet” here is a ram’s horn (in Hebrew “shofar”). It is a wind instrument made from the horn of a male sheep. It is still blown on Jewish holidays today.
> That men and women are not to sleep with each other during this time does not mean that sex is bad. Back then, it was a sign of special preparation and focus.

---

## God comes down on the mountain (Verses 16–25)

[16] On the third day, when morning came, there was thunder and lightning.
A thick cloud lay on the mountain.
And there was the sound of a very loud trumpet.
All the people in the camp trembled.
[17] Moses led the people out of the camp to meet God.
They stood at the foot of the mountain.

[18] All of Mount Sinai was covered in smoke,
because the LORD had come down on it in fire.
Its smoke went up like the smoke of a furnace.
And the whole mountain shook violently.
[19] The sound of the trumpet grew louder and louder.
Moses spoke, and God answered him with a voice.

[20] The LORD came down on Mount Sinai, on the top of the mountain.
The LORD called Moses to the top of the mountain,
and Moses went up.
[21] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go down and warn the people.
They must not push through to the LORD to stare at him.
Otherwise many of them will die.
[22] The priests who come near to the LORD must also make themselves holy.
Otherwise the LORD will break out against them.”

[23] Moses said to the LORD,
“The people cannot come up Mount Sinai.
For you yourself warned us,
‘Mark a boundary around the mountain and make it holy.’”
[24] The LORD said to him,
“Go down!
Then come back up, and bring Aaron with you.
But do not let the priests and the people push through to come up to the LORD.
Otherwise he will break out against them.”
[25] So Moses went down to the people and told them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Thunder, lightning, fire, smoke, a shaking mountain and a trumpet sound that grows louder and louder: meeting God is overwhelming. The people tremble.
> God is not a harmless buddy. He is holy and mighty, like a fire. And yet he comes down to speak with his people.
> Moses is the go-between. He goes up and down again and again. He brings God’s words to the people and the people’s words to God.
> The “priests” here probably means the firstborn or the elders who offered sacrifices back then. The actual priests from Aaron’s family are appointed only later.
