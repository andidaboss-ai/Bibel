# Exodus – Chapter 3
### The burning bush

---

## A bush that burns but does not burn up (Verses 1–6)

[1] Moses was taking care of the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law.
Jethro was the priest of Midian.
Moses led the flock to the far side of the wilderness.
The wilderness is a dry, empty land.
So he came to God’s mountain, to Horeb.
[2] There the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the middle of a bush.
Moses looked.
The bush was burning with fire.
But the bush did not burn up.
[3] Moses said,
“I will go over now and see this great sight.
Why does the bush not burn up?”

[4] The LORD saw that Moses came over to look.
Then God called to him out of the middle of the bush,
“Moses! Moses!”
Moses said, “Here I am.”
[5] God said,
“Do not come closer.
Take off your sandals.
For the place where you are standing is holy ground.”
[6] He also said,
“I am the God of your father,
the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.”
Then Moses hid his face.
For he was afraid to look at God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is now a simple shepherd in the desert. He is about 80 years old (see Chapter 7:7). He probably did not expect anything great to happen in his life anymore.
> Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai.
> A fire that burns but does not burn up – a sign that God is present. God is like a fire: full of power, but he does not destroy.
> Moses is curious. God calls him by his name, twice – just as with Abraham and Jacob.
> “Holy ground”: Where God is, an ordinary place becomes special. Taking off your shoes is a sign of deep respect. In many cultures, people still take off their shoes today when they enter a holy place.

## God has seen the misery (Verses 7–10)

[7] The LORD said,
“I have surely seen the misery of my people who are in Egypt.
I have heard them cry out because of their slave drivers.
Yes, I know their pain.
[8] So I have come down to save them from the hand of the Egyptians.
I will bring them up out of that land to a good and wide land,
to a land flowing with milk and honey:
to the place of the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.

[9] “Now the cry of the people of Israel has come to me.
I have also seen how the Egyptians oppress them.
[10] So come now.
I will send you to Pharaoh.
Bring my people, the people of Israel, out of Egypt.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God says: I have seen, I have heard, I know the pain, I have come down, I will save. God does not stay in heaven. He comes down to those who suffer.
> “A land flowing with milk and honey” – that means: a rich, fertile land where there is plenty of everything. Milk from the herds, honey from the bees or from dates.
> And then the surprise: God wants to save – and for this he sends Moses. God often acts through people.

---

## “Who am I?” (Verses 11–12)

[11] Moses said to God,
“Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh?
And that I should bring the people of Israel out of Egypt?”
[12] God said,
“I will certainly be with you.
This will be the sign for you that I have sent you:
When you have brought the people out of Egypt,
you will serve God on this mountain.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Earlier, Moses wanted to help his people by his own strength. Now that God calls him, he feels too small.
> God does not answer, “You can do it.” He says, “**I** will certainly be with you.” What matters is not who Moses is, but who goes with him.
> The sign is strange: It comes only when everything is done. Moses first has to trust and set out.

## God’s name (Verses 13–15)

[13] Moses said to God,
“Look, I will come to the people of Israel and tell them,
‘The God of your fathers has sent me to you.’
Then they will ask me, ‘What is his name?’
What should I tell them?”

[14] God said to Moses,
“I AM WHO I AM.”
And he said,
“Tell the people of Israel this:
‘I AM has sent me to you.’”

[15] God also said to Moses,
“Tell the people of Israel this:
‘The LORD, the God of your fathers,
the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob,
has sent me to you.’
This is my name forever.
By this name I am to be remembered in all generations.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important passages in the whole Bible: God tells his name.
> “I AM WHO I AM” can also be translated: “I will be who I will be” or “I am there as the one who I am there.” The name means: God is always there. He is there for his people. But he cannot be pinned down or locked in. He remains a mystery.
> From this “I AM” comes God’s name “YHWH” (probably pronounced “Yahweh”). In this Bible it is written as “the LORD.”
> Out of deep respect, Jews do not say this name out loud. Instead they say “Adonai” (my Lord) or “HaShem” (the Name).
> In the Gospel of John, Jesus says “I am” several times: “I am the light of the world,” “I am the good shepherd.” Christians see in this a hint of this name of God.

## God’s plan (Verses 16–22)

[16] “Go and gather the elders of Israel.
Tell them,
‘The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, has appeared to me.
He said,
“I have surely come to look after you.
I have seen what is being done to you in Egypt.
[17] And I have said:
I will bring you up out of the misery of Egypt
to the land of the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite,
to a land flowing with milk and honey.”’

[18] “They will listen to you.
Then you and the elders of Israel will go to the king of Egypt.
You will tell him,
‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us.
Now please let us go on a journey of three days into the wilderness.
There we want to offer sacrifices to the LORD, our God.’

[19] “But I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go,
not even when a strong hand forces him.
[20] So I will reach out my hand.
I will strike Egypt with all my wonders that I will do there.
After that he will let you go.

[21] “I will make the Egyptians kind toward this people.
When you go, you will not go with empty hands.
[22] Every woman will ask her neighbor, and the woman who stays in her house,
for jewelry of silver, jewelry of gold, and clothing.
You will put them on your sons and on your daughters.
So you will take the riches of the Egyptians.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Elders were the experienced, respected men who led a people or a town.
> God honestly tells Moses ahead of time: It will not be easy. Pharaoh will refuse.
> The first request is modest: only three days into the desert to worship God. Pharaoh will refuse even that. This shows how hard he is.
> The Israelites are not to leave with empty hands. After so many years of unpaid slave labor, this is like late wages. God had already promised this to Abraham: After this, they will leave that land with great riches (Genesis 15:14).
