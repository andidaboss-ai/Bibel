# Exodus – Chapter 9
### Disease of the livestock, boils and hail

---

## The fifth plague: the livestock die (Verses 1–7)

[1] Then the LORD said to Moses,
“Go to Pharaoh and tell him,
‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says this:
“Let my people go, so that they may serve me.
[2] If you refuse to let them go and still hold on to them,
[3] look: The hand of the LORD will come on your livestock in the field,
on the horses, on the donkeys, on the camels, on the herds of cattle, and on the flocks of sheep and goats.
A very terrible disease will come.
[4] But the LORD will make a difference between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt.
Of everything that belongs to the children of Israel, not one animal will die.”’”

[5] The LORD set a time.
He said,
“Tomorrow the LORD will do this in the land.”
[6] On the next day the LORD did it.
All the livestock of Egypt died.
But of the livestock of the children of Israel, not one animal died.
[7] Pharaoh sent people to check.
Not even one animal of the Israelites’ livestock had died.
But Pharaoh’s heart stayed stubborn.
He did not let the people go.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the livestock are struck. They were the wealth of Egypt. Here too, the Egyptians worshiped gods in the form of animals, for example a holy bull.
> Again God makes a difference. Pharaoh even checks it himself – and it is true. Still he does not give in.
> “All the livestock” here probably means: the livestock in the fields. For in the plague of hail, livestock are mentioned again.

---

## The sixth plague: boils (Verses 8–12)

[8] The LORD said to Moses and to Aaron,
“Take handfuls of ashes from a furnace.
Let Moses throw them up toward the sky before Pharaoh’s eyes.
[9] Then they will become fine dust over the whole land of Egypt.
And on people and on animals they will become boils that break open into blisters,
in the whole land of Egypt.”

[10] So they took ashes from a furnace and stood before Pharaoh.
Moses threw them up toward the sky.
Then people and animals got boils that broke open into blisters.
[11] The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils.
For the magicians had the boils, just like all the Egyptians.

[12] But the LORD made Pharaoh’s heart hard.
He did not listen to them, just as the LORD had told Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> A boil is a swollen, painful spot on the skin that is full of pus.
> Ashes from a furnace – perhaps from an oven where bricks were baked. The Israelites were tormented with bricks. Now the punishment comes out of this oven.
> The magicians cannot even stand anymore. They are sick themselves.
> For the first time, it says clearly here: “The LORD made Pharaoh’s heart hard.” Before this, Pharaoh did it himself, five times. Now God confirms his decision.

---

## The seventh plague: hail (Verses 13–35)

[13] The LORD said to Moses,
“Get up early in the morning, stand before Pharaoh, and tell him,
‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says this:
“Let my people go, so that they may serve me.
[14] For this time I will send all my plagues against you yourself, against your officials, and against your people.
By this you will know that there is no one like me in all the earth.
[15] By now I could have reached out my hand
and struck you and your people with a deadly disease.
Then you would have been wiped off the earth.
[16] But this is the very reason why I have let you stand:
to show you my power,
and so that my name may be made known in all the earth.
[17] You still set yourself up proudly against my people and will not let them go.
[18] Look: Tomorrow at about this time I will make a very terrible hail fall.
There has never been hail like it in Egypt, from the day Egypt was founded until now.
[19] So now send word.
Bring your livestock and everything you have in the field into a safe place.
The hail will fall on every person and every animal that is in the field and is not brought home.
And they will die.”’”

[20] Some of Pharaoh’s servants feared the word of the LORD.
They quickly brought their servants and their livestock into the houses.
[21] But whoever did not respect the word of the LORD
left his servants and his livestock in the field.

[22] The LORD said to Moses,
“Reach out your hand toward the sky.
Then there will be hail in the whole land of Egypt,
on people, on animals, and on every plant of the field, in the whole land of Egypt.”
[23] Moses reached out his staff toward the sky.
And the LORD sent thunder and hail.
Lightning flashed down to the earth.
The LORD made hail rain on the land of Egypt.
[24] So there was very heavy hail, and lightning flashed in the middle of the hail.
There had never been anything like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.
[25] In the whole land of Egypt, the hail struck down everything that was in the field, both people and animals.
The hail struck down every plant of the field.
And it broke every tree of the field.
[26] Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, there was no hail.

[27] Then Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron.
He said to them,
“This time I have sinned.
The LORD is in the right.
I and my people are in the wrong.
[28] Pray to the LORD!
There has been enough of this mighty thunder and hail.
I will let you go.
You do not have to stay any longer.”

[29] Moses said to him,
“As soon as I have gone out of the city, I will spread out my hands to the LORD.
Then the thunder will stop, and there will be no more hail.
By this you will know that the earth belongs to the LORD.
[30] But as for you and your servants, I know that you do not yet fear the LORD God.”

[31] The flax and the barley were struck down.
For the barley was ripe, and the flax was in bloom.
[32] But the wheat and the spelt were not struck down.
For they had not yet come up.

[33] Moses left Pharaoh and went out of the city.
He spread out his hands to the LORD.
Then the thunder and the hail stopped.
And the rain no longer poured down on the earth.
[34] When Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunder had stopped, he sinned even more.
He and his servants made their hearts hard.
[35] Pharaoh’s heart stayed hard.
He did not let the children of Israel go, just as the LORD had said through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> This plague is special. God warns ahead of time and gives advice: Bring people and animals to a safe place. God does not want people to die.
> And some Egyptians listen to him! In the middle of Egypt, there are people who take God’s word seriously. God saves everyone who listens to him – no matter which people they come from.
> For the first time, Pharaoh says: “I have sinned.” But Moses sees through him: This is not real regret. Pharaoh is only afraid.
> Flax is a plant that people make linen from. Barley and wheat are grains. Spelt is an old kind of wheat.
> It is important that the wheat and the spelt are not struck down yet: So something is still left – for the next plague, the locusts.
> The apostle Paul quotes verse 16 in the New Testament (Romans 9:17).
