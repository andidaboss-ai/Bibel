# Exodus – Chapter 29
### The priests are made holy – the daily offering

---

## Getting ready (Verses 1–3)

[1] “This is what you shall do to them to make them holy, so that they can serve me as priests:
Take one young bull and two rams without any defect.
[2] Also take bread without yeast,
cakes without yeast mixed with oil,
and wafers without yeast spread with oil.
Make them of fine wheat flour.
[3] Put them into one basket.
Bring them in the basket, together with the bull and the two rams.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it describes how Aaron and his sons are put into their office. The ceremony lasts seven days. Leviticus 8 tells how it really takes place.
> “Consecrate” means: to set apart for God and make holy.

---

## Washing, dressing and anointing (Verses 4–9)

[4] “Bring Aaron and his sons to the door of the Tent of Meeting.
Wash them with water.
[5] Take the clothes.
Put the tunic on Aaron, and the robe of the ephod, the ephod and the breastplate.
And tie the skillfully woven band of the ephod around him.
[6] Put the turban on his head.
And put the holy crown on the turban.
[7] Then take the anointing oil.
Pour it on his head and anoint him.

[8] “Bring his sons too.
Put tunics on them.
[9] Tie belts around them, around Aaron and his sons.
And tie headbands on them.
The priesthood shall be theirs by a law forever.
In this way, you shall consecrate Aaron and his sons.

> **What does this mean?**
> First they are washed. Whoever wants to serve God must be clean – on the outside and on the inside.
> The “holy crown” is the golden plate on the forehead with the words “Holy to the LORD” (Exodus 28:36).
> Only Aaron has oil poured over him. It is a sign of his special office as high priest.

---

## The sin offering: the bull (Verses 10–14)

[10] “Bring the bull in front of the Tent of Meeting.
Aaron and his sons shall lay their hands on the head of the bull.
[11] Then kill the bull before the LORD, at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[12] Take some of the bull’s blood and put it on the horns of the altar with your finger.
Pour out all the rest of the blood at the base of the altar.
[13] Take all the fat that covers the inner organs,
the cover of the liver, the two kidneys and the fat on them.
Burn them on the altar.
[14] But burn the meat of the bull, its skin and its dung with fire outside the camp.
It is a sin offering.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sacrifices seem strange to us today. Back then, they were the way people came before God.
> When Aaron and his sons lay their hands on the animal’s head, they show: This animal stands in our place. It carries our guilt.
> The priests are also people who make mistakes. Before they stand before God for others, they need forgiveness themselves.
> A “sin offering” is an offering through which guilt is forgiven.
> The fat was seen as the best part of the animal. It belongs to God.
> The New Testament reminds us that Jesus also died “outside the gate” (Hebrews 13:11–12).

---

## The burnt offering: the first ram (Verses 15–18)

[15] “Also take the one ram.
Aaron and his sons shall lay their hands on its head.
[16] Kill the ram.
Take its blood and sprinkle it all around on the altar.
[17] Cut the ram into its pieces.
Wash its inner organs and its legs.
Put them with its other pieces and with its head.
[18] Then burn the whole ram on the altar.
It is a burnt offering to the LORD.
It is a pleasant smell, an offering made by fire to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> In a burnt offering, the whole animal is burned. Nothing is left for the people. It is a sign of giving oneself completely: We belong completely to you, God.
> “A pleasant smell to the LORD” is a picture. It means that God gladly accepts the offering.

---

## The offering of consecration: the second ram (Verses 19–28)

[19] “Take the other ram.
Aaron and his sons shall lay their hands on its head.
[20] Then kill the ram.
Take some of its blood and put it
on the tip of Aaron’s right ear and on the tip of his sons’ right ear,
on the thumb of their right hand and on the big toe of their right foot.
Sprinkle the rest of the blood all around on the altar.
[21] Take some of the blood that is on the altar, and some of the anointing oil.
Sprinkle it on Aaron and on his clothes,
and on his sons and on his sons’ clothes with him.
Then he and his clothes, and his sons and his sons’ clothes with him, will be holy.

[22] “Also take some of the ram’s fat:
the fat tail, the fat that covers the inner organs,
the cover of the liver, the two kidneys and the fat on them,
and the right thigh.
For it is a ram of consecration.
[23] Also take one loaf of bread, one cake of bread made with oil and one wafer
out of the basket of bread without yeast that stands before the LORD.
[24] Put all of this into Aaron’s hands and into his sons’ hands.
Wave them before the LORD as a wave offering.
[25] Then take them from their hands.
Burn them on the altar, on top of the burnt offering,
as a pleasant smell before the LORD.
It is an offering made by fire to the LORD.

[26] “Take the breast of Aaron’s ram of consecration.
Wave it before the LORD as a wave offering.
It shall be your share.
[27] Make holy the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the wave offering,
which were waved and raised up from the ram of consecration,
from what is for Aaron and for his sons.
[28] It shall be Aaron’s and his sons’ share forever from the people of Israel.
For it is a wave offering.
It shall be a wave offering from the people of Israel, from the sacrifices of their peace offerings.
It is their wave offering to the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The blood is put on the ear, the hand and the foot, each time on the right side. This means: The priest shall listen to God with his ear, do God’s work with his hand, and walk in God’s ways with his foot. The whole person belongs to God.
> In a “wave offering,” the gift is waved back and forth in front of the altar. This is a sign: We give it to God – and God gives it back to us.
> Part of the offerings is food for the priests. In this way, God takes care of those who serve him.
> The “fat tail” belongs to a special kind of sheep in the Middle East. This sheep stores a lot of fat in its tail.

---

## The clothes for those who come after (Verses 29–30)

[29] “Aaron’s holy clothes shall belong to his sons after him.
They shall be anointed and consecrated in them.
[30] The son who becomes priest in his place shall wear them for seven days,
when he comes into the Tent of Meeting to serve in the holy place.

---

## The holy meal (Verses 31–34)

[31] “Take the ram of consecration and boil its meat in a holy place.
[32] Aaron and his sons shall eat the meat of the ram and the bread in the basket,
at the door of the Tent of Meeting.
[33] They shall eat the things by which atonement was made for them,
to consecrate them and make them holy.
But a stranger shall not eat any of it,
because these things are holy.
[34] If any of the meat of consecration or any of the bread is left over until the morning,
then burn what is left with fire.
It shall not be eaten,
because it is holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the end of the consecration, there is a meal together. It is like a feast with God.
> “Atonement” means: What separates God and people is taken away. The relationship is whole again.
> “A stranger” here means: someone who does not belong to the family of priests.

---

## Seven days of consecration (Verses 35–37)

[35] “Do this to Aaron and to his sons, exactly as I have commanded you.
Consecrate them for seven days.
[36] Every day, offer a bull as a sin offering for atonement.
Cleanse the altar when you make atonement for it.
Anoint it to make it holy.
[37] For seven days, make atonement for the altar and make it holy.
Then the altar will be most holy.
Whatever touches the altar will be holy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Seven days – like the seven days of creation. With the sanctuary, something completely new begins.
> Even the altar is cleansed and made holy. Everything that serves God shall be holy.

---

## The daily offering (Verses 38–42)

[38] “This is what you shall offer on the altar:
two lambs a year old, every day, all the time.
[39] Offer one lamb in the morning,
and offer the other lamb in the evening.
[40] With the one lamb, offer about 2 quarts of fine flour mixed with about 1 quart of oil from beaten olives,
and about 1 quart of wine as a drink offering.
[41] Offer the other lamb in the evening.
Do the same with it as with the grain offering and the drink offering of the morning.
It is a pleasant smell, an offering made by fire to the LORD.
[42] It shall be a burnt offering all the time, through all your generations,
at the door of the Tent of Meeting before the LORD.
There I will meet with you, to speak to you there.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the amounts are given like this: a tenth of an ephah of flour, a fourth of a hin of oil and a fourth of a hin of wine. A hin is about 1 gallon.
> An offering every morning and every evening. The whole day begins with God and ends with God. To this day, Jews pray at these times. Many Christians also have a morning prayer and an evening prayer.

---

## God lives in the middle of his people (Verses 43–46)

[43] “There I will meet with the people of Israel.
And the place will be made holy by my glory.
[44] I will make the Tent of Meeting and the altar holy.
I will also make Aaron and his sons holy, so that they can serve me as priests.
[45] I will live among the people of Israel.
And I will be their God.
[46] Then they will know that I am the LORD their God,
who brought them out of the land of Egypt,
so that I might live among them.
I am the LORD their God.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are the goal of everything: “I will live among the people of Israel. And I will be their God.”
> God did not only free Israel from Egypt so that it would be free. He freed it so that he could live with it. Freedom leads to friendship with God.
> In the New Testament it says about Jesus that the Word became flesh and lived among us (John 1:14). Flesh here means: a human being. In Greek, the word for “lived” really means: to live in a tent. And at the very end of the Bible it says that God’s home is with people, and that he will live with them (Revelation 21:3). There, too, the Greek uses the word for tent.
