# Exodus – Chapter 11
### The last plague is announced

---

## One more plague (Verses 1–3)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt.
After that he will let you go from here.
When he lets you go, he will even drive you out completely.
[2] Speak now to the people.
Let every man ask his neighbor, and every woman her neighbor,
for jewelry of silver and jewelry of gold.”
[3] The LORD made the Egyptians kind toward the people.
And the man Moses himself was very great in the land of Egypt,
in the eyes of Pharaoh’s servants and in the eyes of the people.

> **What does this mean?**
> God reminds them of his promise from Chapter 3: The Israelites are not to leave with empty hands.
> Interesting: By now the Egyptians have great respect for Moses and the Israelites. They have seen who the true God is. Only Pharaoh does not want to admit it.

---

## The announcement (Verses 4–8)

[4] Moses said,
“The LORD says this:
‘At about midnight I will go out through the middle of Egypt.
[5] Then every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die,
from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sits on his throne,
to the firstborn of the female slave who works at the hand mill,
and all the firstborn of the livestock.
[6] There will be a great cry in the whole land of Egypt.
There has never been such a cry, and there will never be one like it again.
[7] But against the people of Israel, not even a dog will bark or move its tongue,
neither against people nor against animals.
By this you will know that the LORD makes a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.
[8] All these servants of yours will come down to me.
They will bow down to me and say,
“Get out, you and all the people who follow you!”
And after that I will go out.’”
Then Moses went out from Pharaoh, burning with anger.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the most terrible plague. Many readers ask: How can God do something like this?
> The Bible tells it like this: Pharaoh was the first to have the Hebrew babies killed (Chapter 1). God warned him: “Israel is my son, my firstborn. … So I will kill your son, your firstborn” (Chapter 4:22–23). Pharaoh had the chance to turn around nine times. He did not use it.
> The plague strikes everyone, from the king’s family to the poorest slave woman at the mill. Through his stubbornness, Pharaoh brings endless suffering on his own people.
> The Bible does not make this look nice. It shows: Whoever refuses God again and again and oppresses others brings death on himself and on others.
> To this day, at the Passover festival, many Jews let a drop of wine fall from their cup for each plague as they tell the story. This shows: We are glad about our freedom – but we also grieve over the suffering of the Egyptians.
> “Not even a dog will bark” means: The Israelites stay completely undisturbed.
> Moses is angry – because of Pharaoh’s stubbornness and because of the suffering that will now come.

---

## Summary (Verses 9–10)

[9] The LORD said to Moses,
“Pharaoh will not listen to you.
So my wonders will become even more in the land of Egypt.”
[10] Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh.
But the LORD made Pharaoh’s heart hard.
He did not let the people of Israel go out of his land.

> **What does this mean?**
> These two verses are like a look back at the nine plagues. Everything has happened just as God announced.
> In the next chapter the last plague happens – and the people of Israel celebrate the Passover festival for the first time.
