# Exodus – Chapter 22
### Laws for living together (2): stealing, property and protecting the weak

---

## Stealing (Verses 1–4)

[1] “If a man steals an ox or a sheep, and kills it or sells it,
he must pay back five oxen for one ox and four sheep for one sheep.
[2] If a thief is caught breaking in,
and he is hit so that he dies,
then the one who killed him is not guilty of bloodshed.
[3] But if the sun has already risen,
then he is guilty of bloodshed.
The thief must pay back what he stole.
If he has nothing, he is to be sold as a slave to pay for what he stole.
[4] If the stolen animal is found alive with him, whether ox, donkey or sheep,
he must pay back double.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles, the verse numbering is different here: verse 1 is still Exodus 21:37 there.
> Anyone who steals must give back more than he took. An ox is more valuable and important for work in the field, so it has to be paid back five times.
> There were no prison sentences back then. Anyone who could not pay had to work off his debt.
> The difference between night and day is interesting: in the dark, you do not know whether a burglar is dangerous. You may defend yourself. But in daylight, you can see that he is only a thief, and you may not simply kill him. Even a thief’s life is protected.

---

## Damage to fields (Verses 5–6)

[5] “Someone may let his animals graze in a field or a vineyard,
and they may eat in another man’s field.
Then he must make up for it with the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.

[6] “If a fire breaks out and spreads to thorn bushes,
so that stacked bundles of grain, standing grain or a whole field burn up,
then the one who started the fire must make up for the loss.

> **What does this mean?**
> Anyone who causes damage to another person, even without meaning to, must pay for the damage. And not with the worst, but with the best.

---

## Goods given for safekeeping (Verses 7–13)

[7] “Someone may give his neighbor money or goods to keep safe.
If they are stolen from the neighbor’s house
and the thief is found, the thief must pay back double.
[8] If the thief is not found,
the owner of the house must come before God.
There it will be found out whether he himself has taken his neighbor’s goods.
[9] In every quarrel about property –
whether it is about an ox, a donkey, a sheep, clothing
or anything lost about which someone says, ‘This is mine’ –
the case of both people is to be brought before God.
The one whom God finds guilty must pay back double to his neighbor.

[10] “Someone may give his neighbor a donkey, an ox, a sheep or any other animal to look after.
It may die, or be injured, or be driven away, without anyone seeing it.
[11] Then both of them are to swear an oath by the LORD
that the neighbor has not taken the other man’s goods.
The owner must accept this.
And the neighbor does not have to pay anything back.
[12] But if it was stolen from him,
he must pay it back to the owner.
[13] If it was torn to pieces by a wild animal,
he is to bring what is left as proof.
He does not have to pay for an animal that was torn to pieces.

> **What does this mean?**
> There were no banks back then. People gave valuables or animals to a neighbor to keep safe.
> If something goes missing and no one knows who is to blame, the matter is brought “before God.” This probably means: before the judges or priests at the sanctuary. There people have to tell the truth under a holy oath.
> A shepherd cannot help it if a wild animal attacks. But he has to bring proof. Remember: Jacob complained to Laban that he even had to replace animals that were torn to pieces (Genesis 31:39). This law is fairer.
> In some Bibles, the verse numbers in this chapter are often shifted by one.

## Borrowed goods (Verses 14–15)

[14] “If someone borrows something from his neighbor,
and it is injured or dies while the owner is not there,
he must surely pay for it.
[15] If the owner was there, he does not have to pay for it.
If it was rented, the loss is covered by the rent.

---

## Seducing a young woman (Verses 16–17)

[16] “A man may talk a young woman into sleeping with him.
She is a virgin and not promised to anyone in marriage.
Then he must pay the bride price for her and marry her.
[17] If her father firmly refuses to give her to him as a wife,
he must still pay as much money as the bride price for a virgin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: A bride price is money that the groom gives to the bride’s family.
> Back then, it was very hard for a woman who was no longer a virgin to find a husband. Without a husband, she had hardly any future in that society.
> This law protects the young woman: the man may not simply leave her. He has to take responsibility.
> But the father can also forbid the marriage, for example if the man is a bad person. Then the man still has to pay.

---

## Deeds that deserve death (Verses 18–20)

[18] “You must not let a sorceress live.
[19] Anyone who has sex with an animal must be put to death.
[20] Anyone who sacrifices to any god other than the LORD alone must be completely destroyed.

> **What does this mean?**
> These laws are very harsh for us today. They are aimed at things that were connected with faith in foreign gods back then: magic, sexual rituals with animals and sacrifices to other gods.
> These verses have been terribly misused in history. In the Middle Ages and in early modern times, many thousands of women were burned as “witches” because of verse 18. That was a great injustice. These women were innocent.
> Jews and Christians no longer use these death penalties today. Jesus showed that God does not want to destroy sinners, but to save them.

---

## Protection for foreigners, widows and orphans (Verses 21–24)

[21] “You must not mistreat a foreigner or oppress him.
For you yourselves were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

[22] “You must not take advantage of any widow or orphan.
[23] If you do take advantage of them and they cry out to me,
I will surely hear their cry.
[24] Then my anger will burn,
and I will kill you with the sword.
Then your own wives will become widows, and your children orphans.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most important commandments in the Bible. It comes up again and again, more than 30 times.
> Foreigners, widows and orphans were the weakest people in society. They had no one to protect them.
> The reason given is moving: you know what it is like to be a foreigner. You yourselves were oppressed. So do not oppress anyone.
> God himself takes the side of the weak. He hears their cry – just as he heard the cry of Israel in Egypt. Anyone who oppresses them will have to deal with God.

---

## Protection for the poor (Verses 25–27)

[25] “If you lend money to one of my people, a poor person who lives among you,
do not treat him like a moneylender would.
You must not charge him interest.
[26] If you take your neighbor’s coat as a pledge that he will pay you back,
give it back to him before the sun goes down.
[27] For it is his only covering.
It is the coat for his body.
What would he sleep in?
When he cries out to me, I will hear him.
For I am gracious.

> **What does this mean?**
> Anyone who is poor and has to borrow money should not be taken advantage of on top of that. No interest for the poor!
> A poor person often had nothing but his coat. During the day he wore it, at night he covered himself with it. Nobody may take this coat away from him overnight.
> “What would he sleep in?” – God thinks very concretely about the suffering of the poor. He wants us to think about it too.

---

## Respect for God and for the leaders (Verses 28–31)

[28] “You must not speak evil against God.
And you must not curse a leader of your people.

[29] “Do not delay in giving me some of your harvest and of the juice from your presses.
You are to give me the firstborn of your sons.
[30] Do the same with your cattle and your sheep.
The young animal is to stay with its mother for seven days.
On the eighth day you are to give it to me.

[31] “You are to be holy people for me.
So you must not eat any meat from an animal torn to pieces by wild animals in the field.
Throw it to the dogs.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “presses” are the presses for wine and olive oil. From everything people harvest, they are to give something to God first, as thanks.
> “To give the firstborn” does not mean to sacrifice him. Firstborn sons are bought back (see Exodus 13:13).
> The young animal may stay with its mother for a week. Even with animals, people are to act with compassion.
> “To be holy” means: to live differently from the others, to belong completely to God. This shows even in what you eat. Meat from animals torn to pieces had not been drained of blood and was therefore seen as unclean.
