# Exodus – Chapter 28
### The clothes of the priests

---

## Aaron and his sons become priests (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Bring your brother Aaron and his sons with him near to you, from among the people of Israel.
They shall serve me as priests:
Aaron and his sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.
[2] Make holy clothes for your brother Aaron,
to give him glory and beauty.
[3] Speak to all who have a wise heart,
whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom.
They shall make Aaron’s clothes,
to make him holy, so that he can serve me as a priest.

[4] “These are the clothes that they shall make:
a breastplate, an ephod, a robe, a fitted tunic, a turban and a sash.
They shall make holy clothes for your brother Aaron and his sons,
so that he can serve me as a priest.
[5] They shall use the gold,
and the blue, purple and scarlet cloth, and the fine linen.

> **What does this mean?**
> A priest is a person who stands between God and people. He brings the people’s offerings before God and speaks God’s blessing over the people.
> Aaron becomes the first high priest. His sons become priests. Later, only their descendants are allowed to be priests.
> The clothes shall give “glory and beauty.” Whoever serves before God should show it on the outside too.
> Interesting: Here the skill of a craftsman is called a gift of God’s Spirit. Art and craft are gifts from God.

---

## The ephod (Verses 6–14)

[6] “They shall make the ephod of gold,
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen, the work of a skillful worker.
[7] It shall have two shoulder straps fastened to its two ends.
So it is held together.
[8] The skillfully woven band on it shall be made the same way and be one piece with it,
of gold, of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.

[9] “Take two onyx stones.
Engrave the names of the people of Israel on them:
[10] six of their names on the one stone,
and the names of the other six on the other stone,
in the order of their birth.
[11] An engraver of stone shall engrave the names of the people of Israel on the two stones, the way a seal is engraved.
Put the stones in settings of gold.
[12] Put the two stones on the shoulder straps of the ephod.
They shall be stones of remembrance for the people of Israel.
So Aaron shall carry their names before the LORD on his two shoulders, as a reminder.

[13] “Make settings of gold,
[14] and two chains of pure gold.
Make them like braided cords.
Fasten the braided chains to the settings.

> **What does this mean?**
> The ephod is a kind of apron or vest. It is worn over the robe. It is held up by straps over the shoulders.
> Verse 11: A seal is a stamp with a name on it.
> On his shoulders, Aaron carries the names of the twelve tribes. This is a beautiful picture: The priest carries the whole people on his shoulders before God. He forgets no one.

---

## The breastplate (Verses 15–30)

[15] “Make a breastplate of judgment, the work of a skillful worker.
Make it the same way as the ephod:
of gold, of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.
[16] It shall be square and folded double.
It shall be about 9 inches long and about 9 inches wide.

[17] “Set stones in it, in four rows:
In the first row a ruby, a topaz and a beryl.
[18] In the second row a turquoise, a sapphire and an emerald.
[19] In the third row a jacinth, an agate and an amethyst.
[20] In the fourth row a chrysolite, an onyx and a jasper.
They shall be set in gold settings.
[21] There shall be twelve stones, one for each name of the people of Israel.
A name shall be engraved on each stone, the way a seal is engraved.
They shall be for the twelve tribes.

[22] “Make chains for the breastplate like braided cords of pure gold.
[23] Make two rings of gold for the breastplate.
Put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.
[24] Put the two braided chains of gold through the two rings at the ends of the breastplate.
[25] Fasten the other two ends of the two chains to the two settings.
Put them on the front of the shoulder straps of the ephod.

[26] “Make two more rings of gold.
Put them on the two ends of the breastplate, on its inside edge, next to the ephod.
[27] And make two more rings of gold.
Put them low on the front of the two shoulder straps of the ephod,
close to the place where they are joined, above the woven band of the ephod.
[28] They shall tie the breastplate by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord.
So it stays on the woven band of the ephod,
and the breastplate cannot swing away from the ephod.

[29] “So Aaron shall carry the names of the people of Israel on the breastplate of judgment, on his heart,
when he goes into the holy place.
It shall always be a reminder before the LORD.
[30] Put the Urim and the Thummim into the breastplate of judgment.
They shall be on Aaron’s heart when he goes in before the LORD.
So Aaron shall always carry the judgment of the people of Israel on his heart before the LORD.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible gives the size as “a span.” A span is the distance from the thumb to the little finger when the hand is spread out. It is about 9 inches.
> Today no one knows for sure which precious stones are meant. The names are translations of old Hebrew words. Different Bibles name different stones here.
> Twelve precious stones for the twelve tribes. Each tribe is precious and special, like a jewel.
> The priest carries the names of the people on his shoulders (strength) and on his heart (love). He brings the people, with everything that moves them, before God.
> No one knows exactly what the “Urim and Thummim” were. They were probably two lots or stones. With them, people could ask what God wanted in difficult questions. The words probably mean “lights and perfections.”

## The robe with bells (Verses 31–35)

[31] “Make the robe that is worn under the ephod all of blue cloth.
[32] There shall be a hole for the head in the middle of it.
There shall be a woven edge around the hole,
like the neck hole of a coat of armor, so that it does not tear.
[33] On its hem, make pomegranates
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth, all around the hem.
And put bells of gold between them, all around:
[34] a golden bell and a pomegranate,
a golden bell and a pomegranate,
all around the hem of the robe.
[35] Aaron shall wear it when he serves.
Its sound shall be heard when he goes into the holy place before the LORD, and when he comes out,
so that he does not die.

> **What does this mean?**
> The pomegranate is a fruit with many seeds. It was a sign of new life and of many children.
> The bells ring with every step. So the people outside hear: The priest is inside and is doing his service. He is alive.
> “So that he does not die” – that sounds hard. It shows how serious it is to meet the holy God. You do not simply go to God without being announced – just as you would not with a king.

## The golden plate on the forehead (Verses 36–38)

[36] “Make a plate of pure gold.
Engrave on it, the way a seal is engraved:
‘HOLY TO THE LORD.’
[37] Fasten it with a blue cord to the turban.
It shall be on the front of the turban.
[38] It shall be on Aaron’s forehead.
So Aaron carries the guilt that may stick to the holy things,
which the people of Israel make holy in all their holy gifts.
It shall always be on his forehead,
so that the LORD accepts them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The WEB, our English source text, says “sash” in verse 37. But the Hebrew text means the turban, the covering for the head, because the plate sits on the forehead. That is why it says “turban” here.
> “Holy to the LORD” – these words show: The priest belongs completely to God. And he stands in the place of the whole people, who are meant to belong to God.
> People make mistakes, even when they want to give God a gift. The priest carries these mistakes before God, so that God still accepts the gifts with kindness.

## The other clothes (Verses 39–43)

[39] “Weave the tunic of fine linen.
Make a turban of fine linen.
And make a sash, the work of an embroiderer.

[40] “Make tunics for Aaron’s sons too.
Make sashes for them, and make headbands for them.
They shall give them glory and beauty.
[41] Put them on your brother Aaron and on his sons with him.
Anoint them, ordain them and make them holy,
so that they can serve me as priests.

[42] “Make linen pants for them to cover their naked bodies.
They shall reach from the waist to the thighs.
[43] Aaron and his sons shall wear them
when they go into the Tent of Meeting,
or when they come near the altar to serve in the holy place.
Otherwise they will carry guilt and die.
This shall be a law forever, for Aaron and for his descendants after him.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Anoint” means: to pour sweet-smelling oil over someone. It is a sign that God has chosen someone for a special task. Later, kings were anointed too. The Hebrew word for “anointed one” is “Messiah.” In Greek it is “Christ.”
> The priests shall be dressed with dignity and decency. In many other religions of that time, there were rituals with nakedness. Not in Israel.
