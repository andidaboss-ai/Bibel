# Exodus – Chapter 25
### God’s home in the middle of his people: the ark, the table and the lamp stand

---

## Gifts for the sanctuary (Verses 1–9)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[2] “Speak to the children of Israel.
Tell them to collect an offering for me.
Take my offering from everyone whose heart makes him willing to give.
[3] This is the offering that you shall take from them:
gold, silver and bronze,
[4] blue, purple and scarlet cloth,
fine linen and goats’ hair,
[5] rams’ skins dyed red, sea cow hides and acacia wood,
[6] oil for the light,
spices for the anointing oil and for the sweet incense,
[7] onyx stones, and stones to be set
for the ephod and for the breastplate.

[8] Let them make me a sanctuary.
Then I will live among them.
[9] You shall make it exactly like everything I show you:
the pattern of the tabernacle and the pattern of all its furniture.
That is how you shall make it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now a long part begins (Chapters 25–31 and 35–40). It is about the “sanctuary.” It is also called the “tabernacle” or the “Tent of Meeting.” It is a large tent that can be carried. In it, God wants to live in the middle of his people.
> This is the great goal of leaving Egypt: God does not want to stay far away on a mountain. He wants to be in the middle of his people and travel with them through the desert.
> No one is forced to give anything. God only wants gifts that come from the heart.
> The valuable things probably came from Egypt: When the Israelites left, the Egyptians had given them gold, silver and clothes (Exodus 12:35).
> Purple and scarlet were very expensive dyes. Cloth for kings was dyed with them.
> It is not certain what “sea cows” means here. Maybe the hides of dugongs, which live in the Red Sea. Other Bibles say “fine leather” or “dolphin skins.”
> Acacias are trees that grow in the desert. Their wood is very hard and lasts a long time.
> The ephod and the breastplate are part of the high priest’s clothes (Chapter 28).

## The ark of the covenant (Verses 10–16)

[10] “They shall make an ark of acacia wood.
It shall be about 3 feet 9 inches long, about 2 feet 3 inches wide and about 2 feet 3 inches high.
[11] Cover it with pure gold, inside and outside.
And make a gold molding all around it.
[12] Cast four gold rings for it.
Put them on its four feet:
two rings on one side and two rings on the other side.
[13] Make poles of acacia wood and cover them with gold.
[14] Put the poles through the rings on the sides of the ark, so that the ark can be carried with them.
[15] The poles shall stay in the rings of the ark.
They shall never be taken out.
[16] Put the covenant that I will give you into the ark.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the measurements are given in cubits: two and a half cubits long, one and a half cubits wide and high. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> The ark (also called the “ark of the covenant”) is a chest. It is the most important object in the sanctuary.
> In the ark are the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments. They are the “testimony” of the covenant. A covenant is a firm agreement.
> The poles always stay in the rings. God’s presence is always ready to move on with the people.

## The cover with the cherubim (Verses 17–22)

[17] Make a mercy seat of pure gold.
It is the cover of the ark.
It shall be about 3 feet 9 inches long and about 2 feet 3 inches wide.
[18] Make two cherubim of hammered gold.
Put them at the two ends of the mercy seat.
[19] Make one cherub at one end and one cherub at the other end.
The cherubim shall be one piece with the mercy seat.
[20] The cherubim shall spread out their wings upward
and cover the mercy seat with their wings.
Their faces shall be turned toward each other.
They shall look toward the mercy seat.

[21] Put the mercy seat on top of the ark.
And put the covenant that I will give you inside the ark.
[22] There I will meet with you.
From above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubim on the ark of the covenant,
I will tell you everything that I command you for the children of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> In English, the cover is called the “mercy seat.” In Hebrew it is called “kapporet.” The word is connected with “making peace” and “covering guilt.” On the great Day of Atonement, blood is sprinkled here, so that the people’s guilt is forgiven (Leviticus 16).
> Cherubim (one is called a cherub) are mighty heavenly beings with wings. They also guard the way to the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:24).
> The space between the cherubim is like an empty throne. God himself sits on it, but no one can see him. There is no image of God – only an empty place for him.
> “There I will meet with you” – this is the heart of the sanctuary. Here God speaks with Moses.

## The table for the bread (Verses 23–30)

[23] “Make a table of acacia wood.
It shall be about 3 feet long, about 18 inches wide and about 2 feet 3 inches high.
[24] Cover it with pure gold and make a gold molding all around it.
[25] Make a rim all around it, about 3 inches wide.
And make a gold molding all around this rim.
[26] Make four gold rings for the table.
Put the rings at the four corners, on its four legs.
[27] The rings shall be close to the rim.
The poles for carrying the table shall go through them.
[28] Make the poles of acacia wood and cover them with gold.
The table shall be carried with them.
[29] Also make its dishes, its spoons, its ladles and its bowls for pouring out drink offerings.
Make them of pure gold.
[30] Always put the bread of the presence on the table before me.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: two cubits long, one cubit wide, one and a half cubits high. A hand width is about 3 inches.
> The “bread of the presence” (word for word: “bread of the face”) was twelve loaves, one for each tribe (Leviticus 24:5–9). They always lay before God. They show: The whole people always stands before God’s face. And it is God who gives his people bread.

## The golden lamp stand (Verses 31–40)

[31] “Make a lamp stand of pure gold.
The lamp stand shall be made of hammered gold.
Its base, its shaft, its cups, its buds and its flowers shall be one piece with it.
[32] Six branches shall go out from its sides:
three branches on one side and three branches on the other side.
[33] On one branch there shall be three cups shaped like almond blossoms, each with a bud and a flower.
And on the next branch there shall also be three cups shaped like almond blossoms, each with a bud and a flower.
It shall be like this for all six branches that go out from the lamp stand.
[34] On the lamp stand itself there shall be four cups shaped like almond blossoms, with their buds and their flowers.
[35] There shall be a bud under each pair of branches, one piece with the lamp stand:
a bud under the first two branches,
a bud under the next two branches
and a bud under the last two branches.
It shall be like this for the six branches that go out from the lamp stand.
[36] The buds and the branches shall be one piece with it.
All of it shall be one piece of hammered, pure gold.

[37] Make seven lamps for it.
They shall light its lamps so that they give light to the space in front of it.
[38] Its snuffers and its snuff dishes shall also be of pure gold.
[39] The lamp stand and all these tools shall be made from a talent of pure gold.
[40] Make sure that you make everything exactly like the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The lamp stand has seven branches. In Hebrew it is called the “menorah.” To this day, it is one of the most important signs of Judaism. It is even on the coat of arms of the State of Israel.
> The lamp stand looks like an almond tree in bloom. In the land of Israel, the almond tree is the first tree to bloom each year, while it is still winter. Maybe the lamp stand is also a reminder of the tree of life in the Garden of Eden.
> A talent is an old weight, about 75 pounds. That much pure gold for just one lamp stand!
> “Hammered gold” means: The gold is beaten into shape with a hammer. The whole lamp stand is made of one single piece.
> Snuffers are used to cut off the burned wicks of the lamps.
> Again and again it says: exactly like the pattern that God showed. People did not invent the sanctuary themselves. It is a copy of something in heaven (Hebrews 8:5).
