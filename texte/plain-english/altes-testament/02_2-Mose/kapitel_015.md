# Exodus – Chapter 15
### The song by the sea – water in the desert

---

## The song of Moses (Verses 1–5)

[1] Then Moses and the people of Israel sang this song to the LORD.
They said,
“I will sing to the LORD,
for he has won a glorious victory.
He has thrown the horse and its rider into the sea.
[2] The LORD is my strength and my song.
He has become my salvation.
This is my God, and I will praise him.
He is my father’s God, and I will honor him.
[3] The LORD is a warrior.
The LORD is his name.
[4] He has thrown Pharaoh’s chariots and his army into the sea.
His best officers have sunk in the Red Sea.
[5] The deep waters cover them.
They went down into the depths like a stone.

> **What does this mean?**
> After being saved, the people sing. This is one of the oldest songs in the Bible. In Hebrew it is written as poetry.
> Whoever has been saved wants to give thanks and celebrate. Faith and music belong together.
> In verse 2, the short form “Yah” stands for God. It is also in the word “Hallelujah” – that means: “Praise Yah! Praise the LORD!”
> “The LORD is a warrior” means here: He fought for his people when they could not defend themselves (Exodus 14:14).

---

## God’s strong hand (Verses 6–12)

[6] “Your right hand, LORD, is glorious in power.
Your right hand, LORD, smashes the enemy to pieces.
[7] In your great majesty, you throw down those who rise up against you.
You send out your anger.
It burns them up like straw.
[8] With the blast of your nostrils, the waters were piled up.
The floods stood up like a wall.
The deep waters became solid in the heart of the sea.

[9] “The enemy said,
‘I will chase them. I will catch up with them.
I will divide the loot.
I will get all I want from them.
I will draw my sword.
My hand will destroy them.’
[10] You blew with your wind.
The sea covered them.
They sank like lead in the mighty waters.

[11] “Who is like you, LORD, among the gods?
Who is like you, glorious in holiness,
awesome in praises, doing wonders?
[12] You reached out your right hand.
The earth swallowed them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The song paints big pictures: God’s “blast of your nostrils” is the wind that divided the sea.
> The enemy is full of greed and self-confidence: “I will chase them. I will catch up with them.” Again and again he says “I” and “my.” But one single breath from God is enough, and it is all over.
> “Who is like you, LORD, among the gods?” – The gods of Egypt could do nothing. No other god is like the LORD. To this day, this sentence is spoken in Jewish prayer: “Mi chamocha.”

---

## God leads his people (Verses 13–18)

[13] “In your faithful love, you have led the people that you have set free.
In your strength you have guided them to your holy dwelling.
[14] The peoples have heard it. They tremble.
Pain has taken hold of the people who live in Philistia.
[15] Then the chiefs of Edom were terrified.
Trembling takes hold of the mighty men of Moab.
All the people who live in Canaan have lost their courage.
[16] Terror and dread fall on them.
Because of the greatness of your arm, they are as still as a stone,
until your people pass by, LORD,
until the people you have bought for yourself pass by.
[17] You will bring them in and plant them on the mountain that belongs to you,
the place, LORD, that you have made for yourself to live in,
the holy place, Lord, that your hands have built.
[18] The LORD will reign forever and ever.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the song looks ahead: God will lead his people through the desert and bring them into the promised land.
> Philistia, Edom, Moab and Canaan are the peoples whose lands Israel will pass on the way.
> The “mountain that belongs to you” is probably Mount Zion in Jerusalem, where the temple will later stand.
> The song ends with the most important sentence: “The LORD will reign forever and ever.” It is not Pharaoh who is king, but God.

---

## Miriam dances (Verses 19–21)

[19] For Pharaoh’s horses went into the sea with his chariots and his horsemen.
And the LORD brought the waters of the sea back over them.
But the people of Israel walked on dry land through the middle of the sea.

[20] Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand.
A tambourine is a small hand drum.
All the women went out after her with tambourines and with dancing.
[21] Miriam sang to them,
“Sing to the LORD,
for he has won a glorious victory.
He has thrown the horse and its rider into the sea.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Miriam is the sister who once watched over her little brother by the Nile (Exodus 2:4). Now she is an old woman – and she dances.
> She is called a “prophetess.” She is one of the first women in the Bible with this title. God speaks through women too.
> The women celebrate with drums and dancing. People may show their joy about God’s rescue with their whole body.

## Bitter water becomes sweet (Verses 22–27)

[22] Then Moses led Israel on from the Red Sea.
They went out into the wilderness of Shur.
For three days they walked through the wilderness and found no water.
[23] Then they came to Marah.
But they could not drink the water of Marah, because it was bitter.
That is why the place was called Marah.
Marah means “bitter.”
[24] The people complained against Moses.
They said,
“What are we supposed to drink?”

[25] Moses cried out to the LORD.
The LORD showed him a piece of wood.
Moses threw it into the water.
Then the water became sweet.
There the LORD gave them a rule and a law.
And there he tested them.
[26] He said,
“Listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God.
Do what is right in his eyes.
Pay attention to his commandments, and keep all his rules.
If you do this, I will not put on you any of the diseases that I put on the Egyptians.
For I am the LORD who heals you.”

[27] Then they came to Elim.
There were 12 springs of water and 70 palm trees there.
They camped there by the water.

> **What does this mean?**
> Just a short time ago, they were celebrating. Three days later they are already complaining. The desert is hard. Thirst is a real need.
> In the Bible, “to complain” here means: to grumble and be unhappy.
> Moses does the right thing: He brings the need to God.
> “I am the LORD who heals you” – a beautiful name for God. God wants to heal, not to make people sick.
> After the bitter water comes Elim: an oasis with 12 springs and 70 palm trees. Twelve like the twelve tribes, 70 like the 70 descendants of Jacob. God provides enough for everyone.
