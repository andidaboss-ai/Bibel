# Exodus – Chapter 36
### The building begins: curtains, boards and veils

---

## The people must be stopped (Verses 1–7)

[1] “Bezalel and Oholiab shall work
together with every man who has a wise heart,
in whom the LORD has put wisdom and understanding.
So they know how to do all the work for the service of the sanctuary,
exactly as the LORD has commanded.”

[2] Moses called Bezalel and Oholiab
and every man with a wise heart, in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom,
everyone whose heart moved him to come to the work and do it.
[3] They received from Moses the whole offering
that the children of Israel had brought for the work of the sanctuary, so that they could build it.
But the people kept bringing freewill offerings to him every morning.

[4] So all the skilled men who did all the work of the sanctuary came.
Each one came from the work that he was doing.
[5] They said to Moses,
“The people are bringing much more than enough for the work that the LORD commanded us to do.”
[6] So Moses gave a command.
They had it announced through the whole camp:
“No man and no woman shall make anything more for the offering for the sanctuary!”
So the people were stopped from bringing more.
[7] For the materials they had were enough for all the work.
They were even too much.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is a wonderful passage: The people are so glad to give that they have to be stopped. It is probably one of the few collections in history that had to end because the gifts were too large.
> When people give from the heart, there is more than enough.

## The inner curtains of linen (Verses 8–13)

[8] All the men with a wise heart among the workers made the tabernacle with ten curtains.
They were made of fine twisted linen and of blue, purple and scarlet cloth.
Cherubim were woven into them, the work of a skillful worker.
[9] Each curtain was about 42 feet long and about 6 feet wide.
All the curtains were the same size.
[10] He joined five curtains to each other,
and he joined the other five curtains to each other too.
[11] He made loops of blue cloth on the edge of the last curtain of the first set.
He did the same on the edge of the last curtain of the second set.
[12] He made 50 loops on the one curtain,
and 50 loops on the edge of the curtain of the second set.
The loops were exactly opposite each other.
[13] He made 50 clasps of gold.
He joined the curtains to each other with the clasps.
So the tabernacle became one whole.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: 28 cubits long, 4 cubits wide. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> “He” here is probably Bezalel, the leader of the work. But it also means all those who worked with him.
> From here on, everything is done exactly as it was commanded in Chapter 26.

---

## The curtains of goats’ hair and the skins (Verses 14–19)

[14] He made curtains of goats’ hair as a covering over the tabernacle, like a tent roof.
He made eleven of these curtains.
[15] Each curtain was about 45 feet long and about 6 feet wide.
The eleven curtains were all the same size.
[16] He joined five curtains together into one set,
and six curtains into another set.
[17] He made 50 loops on the edge of the last curtain of the first set,
and 50 loops on the edge of the last curtain of the second set.
[18] He made 50 clasps of bronze to join the tent together, so that it became one whole.
[19] He made a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red.
And above that, he made a covering of sea cow hides.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: 30 cubits long, 4 cubits wide.
> As in Chapter 26: Over the beautiful linen curtains lie a covering of goats’ hair and two coverings of skins and hides. They protect against sun and rain.

## The boards (Verses 20–30)

[20] He made the boards for the tabernacle of acacia wood. They stood upright.
[21] Each board was about 15 feet long and about 2 feet 3 inches wide.
[22] Each board had two tenons, joined to each other.
He made all the boards of the tabernacle this way.
[23] He made the boards for the tabernacle: 20 boards for the south side.
[24] He made 40 sockets of silver under the 20 boards:
two sockets under one board for its two tenons,
and two sockets under the next board for its two tenons.

[25] For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, he made 20 boards
[26] and their 40 sockets of silver:
two sockets under one board and two sockets under the next board.
[27] For the back of the tabernacle, toward the west, he made six boards.
[28] And he made two boards for the corners of the tabernacle at the back.
[29] They were double at the bottom, and in the same way they went all the way to the top, up to the first ring.
He did this with both of them, at the two corners.
[30] So there were eight boards with their sockets of silver, 16 sockets:
two sockets under each board.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: 10 cubits long, one and a half cubits wide.
> The boards form the walls of the sanctuary. You can read more about this in the explanation for Exodus 26.

## The bars (Verses 31–34)

[31] He made bars of acacia wood:
five for the boards on one side of the tabernacle,
[32] five bars for the boards on the other side of the tabernacle,
and five bars for the boards at the back, toward the west.
[33] He made the middle bar so that it went through the middle of the boards from one end to the other.
[34] He covered the boards with gold.
He made gold rings on them for the bars to go through.
And he covered the bars with gold.

---

## The two veils (Verses 35–38)

[35] He made the veil of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.
Cherubim were woven into it, the work of a skillful worker.
[36] He made four pillars of acacia wood for it and covered them with gold.
Their hooks were of gold.
And he cast four sockets of silver for them.

[37] He made a screen for the door of the tent
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen,
the work of an embroiderer.
[38] He also made its five pillars with their hooks.
He covered their capitals and their bands with gold.
And their five sockets were of bronze.

> **What does this mean?**
> The first veil separates the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place. The second curtain hangs at the entrance of the tent.
> A “capital” is the top part of a pillar. It is often decorated.
