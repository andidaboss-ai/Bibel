# Exodus – Chapter 17
### Water from the rock – the fight against Amalek

---

## No water in Rephidim (Verses 1–7)

[1] The whole community of the people of Israel set out from the wilderness of Sin.
They traveled from place to place, as the LORD commanded.
Then they camped in Rephidim.
But there was no water there for the people to drink.
[2] So the people quarreled with Moses.
They said,
“Give us water to drink!”
Moses said to them,
“Why are you quarreling with me?
Why are you testing the LORD?”
[3] But the people were very thirsty for water there.
So the people complained against Moses.
They said,
“Why have you brought us up out of Egypt?
Do you want to kill us, our children, and our livestock with thirst?”

[4] Then Moses cried out to the LORD,
“What should I do with these people?
They are almost ready to throw stones at me!”
[5] The LORD said to Moses,
“Walk on ahead of the people.
Take the elders of Israel with you.
Also take in your hand the staff that you struck the Nile with, and go.
[6] Look, I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb.
Strike the rock.
Then water will come out of it, so that the people can drink.”
Moses did so before the eyes of the elders of Israel.

[7] He named the place Massah and Meribah.
For the people of Israel had quarreled there.
And they had tested the LORD.
They had said,
“Is the LORD among us or not?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Again there is no water. Again there is a quarrel. The situation becomes dangerous: The people even want to throw stones at Moses.
> Thirst is a real need. But the people do not ask, “Help us, God!” They ask with distrust: “Is the LORD among us or not?” The Bible calls this: testing God.
> Moses strikes with the same staff that he used to turn the Nile into blood. Now the staff brings life.
> “Massah” means “testing.” “Meribah” means “quarreling.” The names remind people of what happened there.
> The apostle Paul sees in the rock that gives water a picture of Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4).

## The fight against Amalek (Verses 8–13)

[8] Then Amalek came and fought against Israel in Rephidim.
[9] Moses said to Joshua,
“Choose men for us, and go out to fight against Amalek.
Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill
with God’s staff in my hand.”
[10] So Joshua did as Moses had told him, and fought against Amalek.
Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.

[11] As long as Moses held up his hand, Israel was winning.
When he let his hand down, Amalek was winning.
[12] But Moses’ hands grew tired.
So they took a stone and put it under him.
He sat on it.
Aaron and Hur held up his hands, one on one side, and the other on the other side.
So his hands stayed steady until the sun went down.
[13] And Joshua defeated Amalek and his people with the sword.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Amalekites were a desert people. They came from Esau (Genesis 36:12). They attack the worn-out people. Later we learn: They attacked on purpose the weak and tired people at the end of the line (Deuteronomy 25:18).
> Here we meet Joshua for the first time. Later he will take Moses’ place as leader.
> Moses lifts his hands – probably in prayer. The victory does not come from the strength of the fighters, but from God.
> A beautiful picture: Moses is tired. He cannot go on. Then Aaron and Hur help him and hold up his arms. Even a great leader needs people who support him. No one has to do everything alone.
> Hur is a close friend of Moses. According to Jewish tradition, he was Miriam’s husband.

## An altar to remember (Verses 14–16)

[14] The LORD said to Moses,
“Write this in a book, so that it will be remembered.
And tell it clearly to Joshua:
I will completely wipe out the memory of Amalek from under the sky.”
[15] Moses built an altar.
He named it “The LORD our Banner.”
[16] He said,
“Yah has sworn:
‘The LORD will be at war with Amalek from generation to generation.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here, for the first time in the Bible, it says that something is to be written down. God’s acts are not to be forgotten.
> A “banner” is a flag or a pole that soldiers gather around. Moses says: The place where we gather is God himself. He is our sign of victory.
> Later, Amalek became a picture for the evil that attacks God’s people again and again.
> In Hebrew, verse 16 is very hard to understand. Literally, it speaks of something like a hand on the throne of Yah. Many Bibles understand it as a solemn oath, as here: “Yah has sworn.” “Yah” is a short form of God’s name.
