# Exodus – Chapter 31
### The craftsmen, the Sabbath and the two stone tablets

---

## Bezalel and Oholiab (Verses 1–11)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[2] “Look, I have called Bezalel by name.
He is the son of Uri, the son of Hur, from the tribe of Judah.
[3] I have filled him with the Spirit of God,
with wisdom, with understanding and with knowledge,
and with skill for every kind of work.
[4] So he can plan works of art
and work with gold, with silver and with bronze.
[5] He can cut stones and set them,
carve wood and do every kind of work.

[6] And look, I myself have chosen Oholiab to work with him.
He is the son of Ahisamach, from the tribe of Dan.
And I have put wisdom into the hearts of all who have a wise heart.
So they can make everything that I have commanded you:
[7] the Tent of Meeting, the ark of the covenant,
the mercy seat on it and all the furniture of the Tent,
[8] the table and its things, the pure lamp stand with all its things, the altar of incense,
[9] the altar of burnt offering with all its tools, the basin and its base,
[10] the finely made clothes –
the holy clothes for Aaron the priest,
and the clothes of his sons for their service as priests –
[11] the anointing oil and the sweet-smelling incense for the holy place.
They shall do everything exactly as I have commanded you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bezalel is the first person in the Bible of whom it says that he is filled with the Spirit of God. And for what? For art and craft!
> This shows: Gifts for designing, building and decorating come from God. Not only priests and prophets serve God, but also craftsmen and artists.
> Bezalel comes from Judah, a large and important tribe. Oholiab comes from Dan, a small tribe. The two of them work together.
> Hur is probably the man who held up Moses’ arms together with Aaron (Exodus 17:12).

## The Sabbath as a sign (Verses 12–17)

[12] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[13] “Also speak to the children of Israel.
Say, ‘You shall surely keep my Sabbaths.
For the Sabbath is a sign between me and you, through all your generations.
By it you shall know that I am the LORD who makes you holy.
[14] So keep the Sabbath, because it is holy to you.
Everyone who treats it as unholy shall surely be put to death.
For whoever does any work on it shall be cut off from among his people.
[15] Work shall be done for six days.
But the seventh day is a Sabbath, a day of complete rest, holy to the LORD.
Whoever does any work on the Sabbath day shall surely be put to death.
[16] So the children of Israel shall keep the Sabbath.
They shall celebrate the Sabbath through all their generations, as a covenant forever.
[17] It is a sign between me and the children of Israel forever.
For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth.
And on the seventh day he rested and was refreshed.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the middle of the instructions for the sanctuary comes the commandment about the Sabbath. That is no accident. It means: Even the holy work on the sanctuary must rest on the Sabbath. Resting with God is more important than any work, even more important than work for God.
> The Sabbath is a “sign,” like a wedding ring between God and his people. It shows: We belong together.
> “Was refreshed” – in Hebrew, this word means: “caught his breath.” That is a strong picture. God himself rests after his work. If even God rests, then people need rest all the more.
> The death penalty for working on the Sabbath shocks us today. It shows how seriously the Sabbath was taken back then: It was the heart of the covenant. In later Judaism, the teachers set up very high hurdles, so that such punishments were hardly ever carried out. Jesus later said that the Sabbath was made for people, not people for the Sabbath (Mark 2:27).
> To this day, Jews celebrate the Sabbath from Friday evening to Saturday evening. Most Christians celebrate Sunday as a day of rest, because Jesus rose from the dead on a Sunday.

## The two stone tablets (Verse 18)

[18] God finished speaking with Moses on Mount Sinai.
Then he gave Moses the two tablets of the covenant.
They were tablets of stone, written with God’s finger.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the many instructions end that God gave Moses on the mountain (since Chapter 25).
> “Written with God’s finger” means: These words come straight from God. Moses did not make them up.
> But while Moses was up on the mountain, something terrible happened down in the camp. The next chapter tells about it.
