# Exodus – Chapter 35
### The people bring their gifts

---

## First the Sabbath (Verses 1–3)

[1] Moses gathered the whole community of the people of Israel and said to them,
“These are the words that the LORD has commanded you to do:
[2] ‘Work shall be done for six days.
But the seventh day shall be a holy day for you,
a Sabbath, a day of complete rest to the LORD.
Whoever does any work on it shall be put to death.
[3] Do not light a fire in any of your homes on the Sabbath day.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the building of the sanctuary begins. Chapters 35 to 40 tell how everything is made exactly the way God commanded in Chapters 25 to 31. That is why many verses sound almost the same as there. The repetition shows: Now the people really do what God said.
> At the very beginning, there is the Sabbath again. Even the work for the sanctuary shall rest on the Sabbath.
> Do not light a fire – back then, making a fire was hard work. To this day, many Jews do not light a fire on the Sabbath. That is why, for example, there are lights that are lit before the Sabbath begins.
> You can read more about the death penalty for work on the Sabbath in the explanation for Exodus 31.

---

## Whoever is glad to give shall give (Verses 4–9)

[4] Moses said to the whole community of the people of Israel,
“This is what the LORD has commanded:
[5] ‘Collect an offering for the LORD from among you.
Whoever has a willing heart shall bring it as the LORD’s offering:
gold, silver and bronze,
[6] blue, purple and scarlet cloth, fine linen, goats’ hair,
[7] rams’ skins dyed red, sea cow hides, acacia wood,
[8] oil for the light,
spices for the anointing oil and for the sweet incense,
[9] onyx stones, and stones to be set for the ephod and for the breastplate.

> **What does this mean?**
> No one is forced. Only those who have a “willing heart” shall give. God does not want gifts that are forced.
> This list is the same as in Exodus 25:3–7.

---

## Whoever has skill shall build (Verses 10–19)

[10] “‘Let every man among you who has a wise heart come
and make everything that the LORD has commanded:
[11] the tabernacle, its tent and its covering,
its clasps, its boards, its bars, its pillars and its sockets,
[12] the ark and its poles, the mercy seat, and the veil that hides it,
[13] the table with its poles and all its things, and the bread of the presence,
[14] the lamp stand for the light with its things, its lamps and the oil for the light,
[15] the altar of incense with its poles, the anointing oil, the sweet incense,
and the screen for the door, at the door of the tabernacle,
[16] the altar of burnt offering with its grating of bronze, its poles and all its tools,
the basin and its base,
[17] the hangings of the courtyard, its pillars and their sockets,
and the screen for the gate of the courtyard,
[18] the tent pegs of the tabernacle, the tent pegs of the courtyard and their ropes,
[19] the finely made clothes for serving in the holy place –
the holy clothes for Aaron the priest and the clothes of his sons –
for their service as priests.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here everything that must be built is listed. It is a lot of work. One person alone cannot do it. Everyone who is able to do something shall help.
> A “wise heart” here means: skill and ability in craft and art.

## The people give more than enough (Verses 20–29)

[20] Then the whole community of the people of Israel left Moses.
[21] And they came back:
everyone whose heart moved him,
and everyone whose spirit made him willing.
They brought the LORD’s offering
for the work on the Tent of Meeting, for all its service and for the holy clothes.
[22] They came, men and women, all who had a willing heart.
They brought brooches, earrings, signet rings and armbands, all kinds of gold jewelry.
Every man brought the LORD an offering of gold.

[23] Everyone who had blue, purple or scarlet cloth,
fine linen, goats’ hair, rams’ skins dyed red or sea cow hides brought them.
[24] Everyone who wanted to give an offering of silver or bronze brought it as the LORD’s offering.
And everyone who had acacia wood for any of the work brought it.

[25] All the women who had a wise heart spun thread with their hands.
They brought what they had spun:
the blue, the purple and the scarlet thread and the fine linen.
[26] And all the women whose heart moved them and who had skill spun the goats’ hair.

[27] The leaders brought the onyx stones and the stones to be set for the ephod and for the breastplate,
[28] and the spices and the oil for the light, for the anointing oil and for the sweet incense.
[29] So the people of Israel brought a freewill offering to the LORD.
Every man and woman whose heart made them willing
brought something for all the work that the LORD had commanded through Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> What a difference from Chapter 32! There the people gave their gold for a golden calf. Now they give their gold for God’s sanctuary.
> Everyone takes part: men and women, leaders and ordinary people. Each person gives what he has and what he can do.
> The women are named in a special way. They spin the threads for the cloth. Without their work there would be no curtains and no priests’ clothes.
> Where did the people get so much gold and jewelry in the desert? When they left, the Egyptians had given them jewelry (Exodus 12:35–36).

## Bezalel and Oholiab (Verses 30–35)

[30] Moses said to the people of Israel,
“Look, the LORD has called Bezalel by name,
the son of Uri, the son of Hur, from the tribe of Judah.
[31] He has filled him with the Spirit of God,
with wisdom, with understanding and with knowledge,
and with skill for every kind of work,
[32] so that he can plan works of art
and work with gold, with silver and with bronze,
[33] cut stones and set them, carve wood,
and do every kind of skillful work.
[34] And he has put it into his heart to teach others,
both him and Oholiab, the son of Ahisamach, from the tribe of Dan.
[35] He has filled them with wisdom of heart to do every kind of work:
the work of the engraver, of the skillful worker, and of the embroiderer
in blue, purple and scarlet thread and in fine linen,
and the work of the weaver.
They can do every kind of work and plan works of art.”

> **What does this mean?**
> What is new here: Bezalel and Oholiab can not only do good work themselves. God has also given them the gift of teaching others. So many people can work along with them.
> Good teachers are a gift. Knowledge and skill should be passed on.
