# Exodus – Chapter 23
### Laws for living together (3): justice, times of rest and festivals

---

## Justice in court (Verses 1–3)

[1] “You must not spread a false report.
Do not join forces with a guilty person by being a lying witness.
[2] You must not follow a crowd to do evil.
When you speak as a witness in court, do not side with the majority and so twist justice.
[3] You must also not favor a poor man in his case.

> **What does this mean?**
> In court, only the truth counts. Not the majority, not the pressure of the crowd.
> “Follow a crowd to do evil” – this is still a great danger today: people go along because everyone else is going along. The Bible says: stay with the truth, even if you are alone.
> Surprisingly, a poor person is not to be favored either. Justice means that everyone is treated the same – poor and rich.

---

## Help even your enemy (Verses 4–5)

[4] “If you come across your enemy’s ox or donkey wandering off,
you must be sure to bring it back to him.
[5] You may see the donkey of someone who hates you lying under its load.
Do not leave him alone with it.
You must be sure to help him with it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here we already find something of the command to love your enemies, which Jesus teaches later (Matthew 5:44).
> Even if you are enemies with someone: help him when he is in need. And the animal is not to blame for the quarrel.
> Maybe helping each other with the donkey even leads to making peace.

---

## No injustice in court (Verses 6–9)

[6] “You must not deny justice to the poor among you in their cases.
[7] Stay far away from a false charge.
Do not put the innocent and the righteous to death.
For I will not declare the guilty innocent.
[8] You must not take a bribe.
For a bribe makes those who can see blind,
and it twists the words of those who are in the right.
[9] You must not oppress a foreigner.
You know how a foreigner feels,
for you yourselves were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

> **What does this mean?**
> The poor must not be favored (verse 3) – but they must not be treated unfairly either (verse 6). Equal justice for all.
> Righteous means: living right before God.
> A bribe makes people blind. Anyone who receives money no longer sees the truth. This is still true today in politics, business and the courts.
> Once again the foreigner is protected: “You know how a foreigner feels.” Literally it says: you know the heart of a foreigner. Anyone who was once a foreigner should have compassion.

---

## A year of rest and a day of rest (Verses 10–13)

[10] “For six years you are to plant your land and bring in its harvest.
[11] But in the seventh year you are to let it rest and not farm it.
Then the poor among your people can eat from it.
And what they leave, the wild animals may eat.
Do the same with your vineyard and your olive grove.

[12] “For six days you are to do your work.
But on the seventh day you are to rest.
Then your ox and your donkey can rest too.
And the son of your female servant and the foreigner can catch their breath.

[13] “Be careful to do everything I have told you.
Do not call on the names of other gods.
Do not even let their names be heard from your mouth.

> **What does this mean?**
> It is not only people who need rest, but the land too. Every seven years a field is not to be farmed. This is called the “Sabbath year.” Today we know: this is also good for the soil.
> And what grows by itself belongs to the poor and even to the animals. God’s care is for everyone.
> For the day of rest, the weakest are especially kept in mind here: the animals, the children of servants, the foreigners. They are to be able to “catch their breath.”

---

## Three great festivals a year (Verses 14–19)

[14] “Three times a year you are to hold a festival for me.
[15] Keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread.
For seven days you are to eat bread without yeast, as I commanded you.
Keep it at the set time in the month of Abib,
for in that month you came out of Egypt.
No one is to come before me with empty hands.
[16] Also keep the Festival of Harvest,
when you harvest the first crops of what you planted in the field.
And keep the Festival of Ingathering at the end of the year,
when you gather in the crops of your work from the field.
[17] Three times a year all your males are to come before the Lord GOD.

[18] “You must not offer the blood of my sacrifice together with bread made with yeast.
And the fat of my festival sacrifice must not be left overnight until the morning.
[19] Bring the very best of the first crops of your land to the house of the LORD your God.
You must not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.

> **What does this mean?**
> Israel is to celebrate three great festivals. All three are connected with farming and with what God has done:
> The Festival of Unleavened Bread (Passover) in spring reminds them of the exodus from Egypt.
> The Festival of Harvest in early summer is the festival of the wheat harvest. Later it is called the “Festival of Weeks” (Shavuot). At this time of year, Christians celebrate Pentecost.
> The Festival of Ingathering in the fall is the festival at the end of the harvest, when fruit and grapes are brought in. Later it is called the “Festival of Shelters” (Sukkot).
> “Not to come with empty hands” means: whoever celebrates brings God a gift of thanks from what he has received.
> The ban on boiling a young goat in its mother’s milk may be aimed at a pagan custom. But it also shows compassion: the milk that is meant to give life is not to serve death. From this verse, Jews later developed the rule not to eat meat and milk together.

## God promises his protection (Verses 20–26)

[20] “Look, I am sending an angel ahead of you.
He will protect you on the way
and bring you to the place I have prepared.
[21] Pay attention to him and listen to his voice.
Do not rebel against him,
for he will not forgive your disobedience,
because my name is in him.
[22] But if you truly listen to his voice and do everything I say,
then I will be an enemy to your enemies
and an opponent to your opponents.

[23] “For my angel will go ahead of you.
He will bring you to the Amorite, the Hittite, the Perizzite, the Canaanite, the Hivite and the Jebusite.
And I will wipe them out.
[24] You must not bow down to their gods or serve them.
Do not do what they do.
Instead, you must completely tear them down and smash their sacred pillars.

[25] “You are to serve the LORD your God.
Then he will bless your bread and your water.
And I will take sickness away from among you.
[26] In your land no woman will lose her baby before birth or be unable to have children.
I will let you live out the full number of your days.

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises to go with his people and protect them along the whole way. The angel is God’s messenger. “My name is in him” means: God himself is present in him.
> The “sacred pillars” were standing stones where the peoples in Canaan worshiped their gods.
> The peoples in Canaan are to be wiped out. This is a very difficult passage. It becomes important in the books of Joshua and Judges. The Bible gives the reason that these peoples worshiped cruel gods – some even sacrificed children. Israel was not to be drawn into this idol worship. Many interpreters stress: this was a one-time task in a certain time. It never justifies violence against other peoples today.
> God’s blessing is very concrete: bread, water, health, children and a long life.

## Little by little (Verses 27–33)

[27] “I will send terror of me ahead of you.
I will throw into confusion all the peoples you come to.
I will make all your enemies turn their backs and run from you.
[28] I will send the hornet ahead of you.
It will drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite and the Hittite from in front of you.
[29] But I will not drive them out from in front of you in a single year.
Otherwise the land would become a wasteland,
and the wild animals would become too many for you.
[30] Little by little I will drive them out from in front of you,
until you have become many and can take the land as your own.

[31] “I will set your borders from the Red Sea to the sea of the Philistines,
and from the wilderness to the River.
For I will hand over the people who live in the land to you,
and you will drive them out from in front of you.
[32] You must not make a covenant with them or with their gods.
[33] They must not live in your land,
or they will make you sin against me.
For if you serve their gods, it will surely become a trap for you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> God promises to fight for his people himself. He sends terror and the hornet ahead. Hornets are large, dangerous wasps. Maybe this is also a picture of a panic that makes the peoples run away.
> God is wise: if the land were suddenly empty, it would grow wild. That is why it happens step by step.
> The “sea of the Philistines” is the Mediterranean Sea. The “River” means the Euphrates. The land was this large only for a short time later, in the time of King David and Solomon.
> The greatest danger for Israel was not the weapons of the other peoples, but their gods. Whoever binds himself to them loses himself.
