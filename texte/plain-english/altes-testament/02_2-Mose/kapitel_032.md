# Exodus – Chapter 32
### The golden calf

---

## The people make a golden calf (Verses 1–6)

[1] The people saw that Moses was taking a long time to come down from the mountain.
So the people gathered around Aaron and said to him,
“Come, make us gods that will go in front of us!
For this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt –
we do not know what has happened to him.”
[2] Aaron said to them,
“Take off the gold rings that are in the ears of your wives, your sons and your daughters.
And bring them to me.”
[3] So all the people took off the gold rings that were in their ears and brought them to Aaron.
[4] Aaron took what they handed him.
He shaped it with an engraving tool and made it into a calf of melted metal.
Then they said, “Israel, these are your gods, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!”

[5] When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of the calf.
And Aaron announced, “Tomorrow there shall be a feast to the LORD!”
[6] The next day, they got up early.
They offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings.
Then the people sat down to eat and to drink.
And after that they got up to play.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses has already been on the mountain for 40 days (Exodus 24:18). The people become impatient. They want something they can see and touch.
> In Egypt and in Canaan, a calf or young bull was a sign of strength and of new life. Many peoples worshiped gods in the form of a bull.
> Very shocking: They say that this calf freed them from Egypt. And Aaron calls the feast a “feast to the LORD.” They do not want to leave God completely. But they make an image of him – exactly what God has just forbidden (Exodus 20:4).
> Only a few weeks after they heard God’s voice and promised to do everything he said (Exodus 24:3), they break the covenant.
> “To play” – the Hebrew word can also mean wild partying and dancing.

## God is angry – Moses prays for the people (Verses 7–14)

[7] The LORD said to Moses,
“Go, get down!
For your people, whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt, have done evil.
[8] They have quickly turned away from the way that I commanded them.
They have made themselves a calf of melted metal.
They have bowed down to it and have offered sacrifices to it.
And they have said, ‘Israel, these are your gods, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.’”
[9] The LORD also said to Moses,
“I have seen these people.
Look, they are a stiff-necked people.
[10] So now leave me alone!
Let my anger burn hot against them, so that I can destroy them.
But I will make you into a great nation.”

[11] But Moses begged the LORD his God. He said,
“LORD, why should your anger burn hot against your people?
You brought them out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a mighty hand.
[12] Why should the Egyptians say,
‘He brought them out to do them harm.
He wanted to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth’?
Turn away from your burning anger.
Do not bring this harm on your people.
[13] Remember Abraham, Isaac and Israel, your servants.
You swore to them by your own self and said to them,
‘I will make your descendants as many as the stars of the sky.
And I will give all this land that I have spoken of to your descendants.
They shall own it forever.’”
[14] So the LORD turned away from the harm that he had said he would do to his people.

> **What does this mean?**
> God says to Moses “your people,” not “my people.” It sounds as if God wants to separate himself from Israel.
> “A stiff-necked people” is a picture from farming: a cow or ox that makes its neck stiff and will not be led. It means: stubborn and willful.
> God offers Moses a new beginning with him – like back then with Abraham. But Moses says no. He stands up for the people.
> Moses gives three reasons: They are your people. What will the Egyptians think of you? And remember your promises to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
> “So the LORD turned away from the harm” – this is an amazing passage. A person’s prayer matters to God. It changes something. Here the Bible shows: God does not want to destroy. He waits for someone to stand up for the guilty.

## Moses breaks the tablets (Verses 15–20)

[15] Moses turned and went down from the mountain.
He had the two tablets of the covenant in his hand.
The tablets were written on both sides, on the front and on the back.
[16] The tablets were the work of God.
And the writing was God’s writing, engraved on the tablets.

[17] Joshua heard the noise of the people as they shouted.
He said to Moses, “There is the noise of war in the camp!”
[18] Moses answered,
“It is not the sound of people shouting because they have won.
And it is not the sound of people crying because they have lost.
I hear the noise of people singing.”

[19] As soon as Moses came near the camp, he saw the calf and the dancing.
Then Moses became very angry.
He threw the tablets out of his hands and broke them at the foot of the mountain.
[20] He took the calf that they had made and burned it with fire.
He ground it into powder, scattered it on the water,
and made the people of Israel drink it.

> **What does this mean?**
> The tablets were God’s own handwriting. Moses breaks them. This is a sign: The covenant is broken. The people have broken it already, even before the tablets arrive.
> The calf is burned, ground up and scattered on the water. The people have to drink it. So they learn: This “god” is nothing. It can be destroyed and swallowed. And they themselves have to “swallow” what their deed brings.

## Aaron’s excuse (Verses 21–24)

[21] Moses said to Aaron,
“What did these people do to you, that you have brought such a great sin on them?”
[22] Aaron answered,
“Do not let my lord become angry.
You know the people. They are set on doing evil.
[23] They said to me,
‘Make us gods that will go in front of us.
For this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt –
we do not know what has happened to him.’
[24] So I said to them,
‘Whoever has any gold, take it off.’
So they gave it to me.
I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Aaron’s excuse is almost silly: “I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf.” As if it had made itself. But he shaped it himself (verse 4).
> People often talk like this when they have made mistakes: The others are to blame, and it just happened. Here the Bible shows honestly: Aaron, the high priest, failed too.

## The Levites and the punishment (Verses 25–29)

[25] Moses saw that the people were out of control.
For Aaron had let them lose control, so that their enemies could laugh at them.
[26] Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp and called out,
“Whoever is on the LORD’s side, come to me!”
And all the sons of Levi gathered around him.
[27] He said to them,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says,
‘Every man, put his sword on his side.
Go back and forth from gate to gate through the camp.
And every man, kill his brother, every man his friend, and every man his neighbor.’”
[28] The sons of Levi did what Moses said.
That day about 3,000 men of the people died.
[29] Moses said,
“Set yourselves apart for the LORD today.
For every man was against his son and against his brother.
So he will give you a blessing today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the hardest passages in the Bible. 3,000 men are killed, by their own relatives.
> We have to understand it from its own time. The covenant with God had only just been made. Worshiping idols put the life of the whole people in danger. The Bible sees it as betrayal, as bad as treason in a war. The punishment probably fell on those who did not want to stop.
> Still, this story remains shocking. Jews and Christians have thought about it for a long time. It is not an instruction to use violence in God’s name today. Jesus rejected violence in God’s name (Matthew 26:52).
> The tribe of Levi takes God’s side completely. That is why the Levites later become the servants at the sanctuary (Numbers 3).

## Moses wants to stand up for the people (Verses 30–35)

[30] The next day, Moses said to the people,
“You have committed a great sin.
Now I will go up to the LORD.
Maybe I can make atonement for your sin.”
[31] Moses went back to the LORD and said,
“Oh, these people have committed a great sin.
They have made themselves gods of gold.
[32] But now, please, forgive their sin!
And if not, then please erase me from your book that you have written.”
[33] The LORD said to Moses,
“Whoever has sinned against me, I will erase him from my book.
[34] Now go!
Lead the people to the place that I have told you about.
Look, my angel will go in front of you.
But on the day when I punish, I will punish them for their sin.”
[35] And the LORD struck the people,
because of what they did with the calf that Aaron made.

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is ready to give his own life for the people. “Erase me from your book” means: I would rather be lost myself than let my people be lost.
> The “book” is a picture for the book of life. The names of the people who belong to God are written in it.
> God does not accept the offer. Each person is responsible for his own guilt.
> Christians see a pointer to Jesus here: He did what Moses offered. He gave his life for the guilt of others. Paul also said something similar to Moses about his own people (Romans 9:3).
> It does not say how God “struck” the people. Maybe it was a disease.
