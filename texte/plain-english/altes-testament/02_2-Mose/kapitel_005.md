# Exodus – Chapter 5
### Moses before Pharaoh – the work gets even harder

---

## “Let my people go!” (Verses 1–5)

[1] After that, Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said,
“The LORD, the God of Israel, says this:
‘Let my people go, so that they may hold a festival for me in the wilderness.’”
[2] Pharaoh said,
“Who is the LORD, that I should listen to him and let Israel go?
I do not know the LORD.
And I will not let Israel go.”

[3] They said,
“The God of the Hebrews has met with us.
Please let us go on a journey of three days into the wilderness.
There we want to offer sacrifices to the LORD, our God.
Otherwise he may strike us with a deadly disease or with the sword.”

[4] The king of Egypt said to them,
“Moses and Aaron, why are you taking the people away from their work?
Get back to your work!”
[5] Pharaoh said,
“Look, the people of the land are now many.
And you want them to rest from their work!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Let my people go!” – This sentence became famous. Later it became the cry of many oppressed people, for example the Black slaves in America (“Let my people go”).
> Pharaoh thinks he himself is a god. For him, the God of the slaves is a nobody: “Who is the LORD … I do not know the LORD.” In the next chapters he will get to know him.
> Pharaoh thinks only about how much work the slaves can do. For him, they are not people, only workers.

---

## Bricks without straw (Verses 6–14)

[6] That same day, Pharaoh gave a command to the slave drivers of the people and to their foremen.
He said,
[7] “Do not give the people straw to make bricks anymore, as you did before.
Let them go and gather straw for themselves.
[8] But they must still make the same number of bricks as before.
Do not let them make even one less.
For they are lazy.
That is why they cry out, ‘Let’s go and offer sacrifices to our God.’
[9] Make the work heavier for the men.
Keep them busy with their work.
Then they will not pay attention to lies.”

[10] So the slave drivers of the people and their foremen went out.
They said to the people,
“Pharaoh says this:
‘I will not give you straw.
[11] Go yourselves and get straw wherever you can find it.
But your work will not become any less.’”
[12] So the people spread out through the whole land of Egypt.
They gathered stubble to use as straw.
[13] The slave drivers kept pushing them.
They said,
“Finish your work every day, just as when you had straw!”

[14] Pharaoh’s slave drivers had put Israelite foremen over the people.
These foremen of the people of Israel were beaten.
They were asked,
“Why have you not made as many bricks as before, yesterday and today?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bricks were made from clay, water and chopped straw. The straw made the bricks stronger.
> Now the Israelites have to find the straw themselves – and still make just as many bricks. That is impossible. Pharaoh wants to wear them down on purpose.
> “Stubble” is the short pieces of stalk that are left in the field after the harvest.
> The foremen were Israelites who had to watch over their own people. They are caught in the middle – and they are beaten when their people cannot do the work.

---

## The foremen complain (Verses 15–21)

[15] Then the foremen of the people of Israel went to Pharaoh.
They cried out to him,
“Why do you treat your servants like this?
[16] Your servants are not given any straw.
And still they tell us, ‘Make bricks!’
And look: Your servants are beaten.
But the fault is with your own people.”

[17] But Pharaoh said,
“You are lazy! You are lazy!
That is why you say, ‘Let’s go and offer sacrifices to the LORD.’
[18] Now go and work!
You will not be given any straw.
But you must still deliver the same number of bricks!”

[19] Then the foremen of the people of Israel saw that they were in trouble.
For they had been told,
“You must not make one brick less each day!”
[20] When they came out from Pharaoh, they met Moses and Aaron.
Moses and Aaron were standing by the way.
[21] The foremen said to them,
“May the LORD look at you and judge you!
You have made Pharaoh and his servants hate us.
It is as if we stink to them.
You have put a sword in their hand to kill us!”

> **What does this mean?**
> The foremen ask Pharaoh for justice. But Pharaoh only mocks them.
> “Lazy” – this is how an oppressor talks about people he exploits.
> Now the Israelites blame Moses and Aaron. Just a short time ago they were worshiping God. Now everything has become worse. Their hope is gone.
> Many people know this: Sometimes things first get worse before they get better.

---

## Moses complains to God (Verses 22–23)

[22] Then Moses turned to the LORD again and said,
“Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people?
Why did you send me at all?
[23] Ever since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has treated this people even worse.
And you have not rescued your people at all!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is in despair. He did what God wanted – and everything has become worse.
> He brings his disappointment straight to God. He even accuses God: “Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people?”
> This is allowed. In the Bible, people may tell God about their pain, their anger and their disappointment. An honest prayer of complaint is a prayer too.
> In the next chapter, God answers him.
