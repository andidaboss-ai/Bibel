# Exodus – Chapter 7
### Aaron’s staff – the first plague: blood

---

## Moses and Aaron get their task (Verses 1–7)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Look, I have made you like God to Pharaoh.
And Aaron, your brother, will be your prophet.
[2] You will speak everything that I command you.
And Aaron, your brother, will speak to Pharaoh,
so that he lets the people of Israel go out of his land.

[3] “But I will make Pharaoh’s heart hard.
And I will do many signs and wonders in the land of Egypt.
[4] Pharaoh will not listen to you.
So I will lay my hand on Egypt.
I will bring my armies, my people, the people of Israel, out of the land of Egypt
with great judgments.
[5] Then the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD,
when I reach out my hand against Egypt
and bring the people of Israel out from among them.”

[6] Moses and Aaron did so.
They did just as the LORD had commanded them.
[7] Moses was 80 years old, and Aaron was 83 years old, when they spoke to Pharaoh.

> **What does this mean?**
> A prophet is someone who passes on God’s words to others. Here Moses passes on God’s words to Aaron, and Aaron tells them to Pharaoh.
> Pharaoh thinks he himself is a god. Now an old shepherd stands before him and speaks with the power of the true God.
> Again and again it says: The Egyptians will “know that I am the LORD.” Remember: Pharaoh had said, “I do not know the LORD” (Exodus 5:2). Now he will get to know him.
> Moses is 80 years old! For God, it is never too late to use someone.

---

## The staff becomes a snake (Verses 8–13)

[8] The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron.
He said,
[9] “When Pharaoh says to you, ‘Do a miracle!’
then tell Aaron,
‘Take your staff and throw it down before Pharaoh,
and it will become a snake.’”

[10] So Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh.
They did as the LORD had commanded.
Aaron threw down his staff before Pharaoh and before his servants.
And it became a snake.

[11] Then Pharaoh also called for his wise men and his sorcerers.
The magicians of Egypt did the same thing with their secret arts.
[12] Each of them threw down his staff, and the staffs became snakes.
But Aaron’s staff swallowed up their staffs.

[13] But Pharaoh’s heart stayed hard.
He did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Egyptian magicians can copy the trick. But Aaron’s staff eats up their staffs. This shows: God’s power is greater than all the magic of Egypt.
> Still, Pharaoh is not impressed. A hard heart does not see even a miracle.
> Later the magicians are called “Jannes and Jambres” (2 Timothy 3:8). But their names are not in this chapter.

---

## The first plague: The water turns into blood (Verses 14–25)

[14] The LORD said to Moses,
“Pharaoh’s heart is stubborn.
He refuses to let the people go.
[15] Go to Pharaoh in the morning.
Look, he will be going out to the water.
Stand on the bank of the river to meet him.
Take in your hand the staff that turned into a snake.
[16] Tell him,
‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you.
He says, “Let my people go, so that they may serve me in the wilderness.
But until now you have not listened.”
[17] The LORD says, “By this you will know that I am the LORD:
Look, I will strike the water in the river with the staff that is in my hand.
Then it will turn into blood.
[18] The fish in the river will die.
And the river will stink.
The Egyptians will not be able to stand drinking water from the river.”’”

[19] The LORD said to Moses,
“Tell Aaron,
‘Take your staff and reach out your hand over the waters of Egypt:
over their rivers, over their streams, over their pools, and over all their ponds of water.
Then they will become blood.
There will be blood in the whole land of Egypt,
even in containers of wood and containers of stone.’”

[20] Moses and Aaron did so, just as the LORD had commanded.
He lifted up the staff and struck the water in the river,
before the eyes of Pharaoh and before the eyes of his servants.
Then all the water in the river turned into blood.
[21] The fish in the river died.
The river stank.
The Egyptians could not drink water from the river.
There was blood in the whole land of Egypt.

[22] But the magicians of Egypt did the same thing with their secret arts.
So Pharaoh’s heart stayed hard.
He did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.
[23] Pharaoh turned and went into his house.
He did not take even this to heart.
[24] All the Egyptians dug around the river for water to drink.
For they could not drink the water of the river.

[25] Seven days went by after the LORD had struck the river.

> **What does this mean?**
> Now the 10 plagues begin. A plague is a great disaster that comes over a land.
> The river is the Nile. The Nile was the heart of Egypt. The Egyptians worshiped it as a god. God shows: The Nile is not a god. I am the Lord over it.
> Remember: Pharaoh had ordered the Hebrew babies to be thrown into the Nile (Exodus 1:22). Now the Nile itself turns into blood.
> Pharaoh simply goes home. He has enough to drink after all. He does not care about the suffering of his people.
> Some researchers try to explain the plagues by natural events, for example red algae in the Nile. The Bible tells them as acts of God. What matters is not how it happened, but what it shows: God is mightier than Pharaoh and his gods.
