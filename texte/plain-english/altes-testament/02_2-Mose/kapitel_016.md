# Exodus – Chapter 16
### Manna and quail

---

## The people are hungry (Verses 1–3)

[1] They set out from Elim.
The whole community of the people of Israel came to the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai.
That was on the fifteenth day of the second month after they had left the land of Egypt.
[2] In the wilderness, the whole community of the people of Israel complained against Moses and against Aaron.
[3] The people of Israel said to them,
“We wish we had died by the LORD’s hand in the land of Egypt!
There we sat by the pots of meat.
There we ate as much bread as we wanted.
But you have brought us out into this wilderness
to kill this whole crowd with hunger!”

> **What does this mean?**
> One month after leaving Egypt, the food they brought is used up. The people are hungry.
> And right away they remember Egypt only as the land of the “pots of meat.” The slavery, the whips, the dead children – all forgotten.
> There is still a saying in German about longing for “the meat pots of Egypt.” It means: People long for a time that is past and forget how bad it really was.

---

## God promises bread from heaven (Verses 4–12)

[4] Then the LORD said to Moses,
“Look, I will make bread rain down from the sky for you.
The people must go out every day and gather as much as they need for that day.
In this way I will test them, to see whether they will live by my law or not.
[5] On the sixth day they must prepare what they bring in.
It will be twice as much as they gather on the other days.”

[6] Moses and Aaron said to all the people of Israel,
“This evening you will know that the LORD has brought you out of the land of Egypt.
[7] And in the morning you will see the glory of the LORD.
For he has heard you complaining against the LORD.
Who are we, that you complain against us?”
[8] Moses said,
“The LORD will give you meat to eat in the evening,
and in the morning bread, as much as you want.
For the LORD has heard you complaining against him.
Who are we?
You are not complaining against us, but against the LORD.”

[9] Moses said to Aaron,
“Tell the whole community of the people of Israel,
‘Come close to the LORD, for he has heard your complaining.’”
[10] While Aaron was speaking to the whole community of the people of Israel, they looked toward the wilderness.
And there the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.
[11] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[12] “I have heard the complaining of the people of Israel.
Tell them,
‘In the evening you will eat meat.
And in the morning you will be filled with bread.
Then you will know that I am the LORD your God.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> God does not scold them. He answers their complaining with a gift.
> But he also wants them to learn something: The people are to take only as much as they need each day. They are to learn to trust God anew every day.
> “The glory of the LORD” is God’s visible presence, here as a bright shining in the cloud.

---

## Quail and manna (Verses 13–21)

[13] In the evening, quail came and covered the camp.
And in the morning there was dew all around the camp.
[14] When the dew was gone,
there was something small and round on the surface of the wilderness,
as small as frost on the ground.
[15] The people of Israel saw it.
They said to one another,
“What is it?”
For they did not know what it was.
Moses said to them,
“It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat.

[16] “This is what the LORD has commanded:
‘Everyone must gather as much of it as he needs to eat.
Take an omer for each person,
by the number of people.
Everyone must gather for those who are in his tent.’”
[17] The people of Israel did so.
Some gathered more, some less.
[18] When they measured it with an omer,
the one who gathered much had nothing left over.
And the one who gathered little did not have too little.
Each one had gathered as much as he needed to eat.

[19] Moses said to them,
“No one may leave any of it until the morning.”
[20] But they did not listen to Moses.
Some of them left some of it until the morning.
Then it was full of worms and stank.
And Moses was angry with them.
[21] So they gathered it morning by morning, each one as much as he needed to eat.
When the sun grew hot, it melted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Quail are small birds. To this day, they fly over the Sinai Peninsula in large flocks and land on the ground, worn out. Then they are easy to catch.
> “What is it?” in Hebrew is “Man hu?” That is where the name “manna” comes from.
> An omer is an old measure, about 2 quarts.
> The miracle: Everyone has exactly as much as he needs. Whoever gathers much does not have too much. Whoever gathers little does not have too little. The apostle Paul quotes this sentence when he calls people to share with others (2 Corinthians 8:15).
> Whoever stores food because he does not trust God finds out: It spoils. You cannot hoard God’s care.
> Later Jesus teaches people to pray to God for their daily bread. The manna is a reminder of this: new every day.

## The manna and the Sabbath (Verses 22–30)

[22] On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for each person.
Then all the leaders of the community came and told Moses.
[23] He said to them,
“This is what the LORD has said:
‘Tomorrow is a day of rest, a holy Sabbath for the LORD.
Bake what you want to bake, and boil what you want to boil.
And put aside everything that is left over and keep it until the morning.’”
[24] They put it aside until the morning, as Moses had ordered.
And it did not stink, and there were no worms in it.

[25] Moses said,
“Eat it today.
For today is a Sabbath for the LORD.
Today you will not find any in the field.
[26] For six days you must gather it.
But the seventh day is the Sabbath.
On that day there will be none.”

[27] On the seventh day, some of the people still went out to gather.
But they found none.
[28] The LORD said to Moses,
“How long will you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?
[29] Look, the LORD has given you the Sabbath.
That is why on the sixth day he gives you bread for two days.
Everyone must stay in his place.
No one may leave his place on the seventh day.”
[30] So the people rested on the seventh day.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the word “Sabbath” appears for the first time in the Bible. It means “rest” or “stopping.”
> God rested on the seventh day of creation (Genesis 2:2). Now he gives his people this day of rest.
> For people who were slaves until recently and had to work hard every day, this is a huge gift: a day off, every week!
> Before the Sabbath, the manna even keeps for two days. God makes sure that no one has to work on the day of rest.
> But some people cannot let go. They go out to gather anyway. Even people who have been set free must first learn to rest.

---

## A jar of manna to remember (Verses 31–36)

[31] The house of Israel called it “manna.”
It was white, like coriander seed.
And it tasted like thin cakes made with honey.

[32] Moses said,
“This is what the LORD has commanded:
‘Fill an omer with it and keep it for your descendants through all generations.
They are to see the bread that I fed you with in the wilderness,
when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.’”
[33] Moses said to Aaron,
“Take a jar and put a full omer of manna in it.
Put it before the LORD, so that it may be kept through all your generations.”
[34] Aaron put it in front of the Testimony, so that it would be kept,
just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

[35] The people of Israel ate the manna for 40 years,
until they came to a land where people lived.
They ate the manna until they came to the border of the land of Canaan.
[36] An omer is one tenth of an ephah.

> **What does this mean?**
> Coriander is a spice with small, round seeds.
> The manna is not to be forgotten. A jar of it is kept, so that later generations can see: This is how God provided for us.
> The “Testimony” is the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, which are later kept in the ark of the covenant. So Moses writes this story with a later time in mind.
> For 40 years, every day, God provides for his people – in spite of all their complaining.
> An ephah is a larger dry measure, about 6 gallons.
> In the New Testament, Jesus says that he is the bread of life. And he compares himself with the manna, the bread from heaven (John 6:31–35).
