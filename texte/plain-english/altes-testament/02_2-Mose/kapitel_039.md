# Exodus – Chapter 39
### The priests’ clothes – and everything is finished

---

## The ephod (Verses 1–7)

[1] From the blue, purple and scarlet cloth they made finely made clothes for serving in the holy place.
They made the holy clothes for Aaron, as the LORD commanded Moses.
[2] He made the ephod of gold,
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.
[3] They beat the gold into thin sheets and cut it into threads.
So they could weave it into the blue, purple and scarlet cloth and into the fine linen,
the work of a skillful worker.
[4] They made shoulder straps for it, joined together.
It was held together at its two ends.
[5] The skillfully woven band on it, used to tie it on, was one piece with it and made the same way:
of gold, of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

[6] They worked the onyx stones and set them in settings of gold.
They engraved the names of the people of Israel on them, the way a seal is engraved.
[7] He put them on the shoulder straps of the ephod.
They were stones of remembrance for the people of Israel,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here it shows how gold thread is made: The gold is beaten very thin and then cut into fine strips.
> Again and again this chapter says: “as the LORD commanded Moses.” This sentence comes seven times here. In the Bible, seven is the number of completeness. Everything is done exactly the way God wanted it.
> You can read more about the clothes in the explanation for Exodus 28.

---

## The breastplate (Verses 8–21)

[8] He made the breastplate, the work of a skillful worker, the same way as the ephod:
of gold, of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.
[9] It was square. They made the breastplate folded double.
It was about 9 inches long and about 9 inches wide, folded double.
[10] They set four rows of stones in it:
In the first row a ruby, a topaz and a beryl.
[11] In the second row a turquoise, a sapphire and an emerald.
[12] In the third row a jacinth, an agate and an amethyst.
[13] In the fourth row a chrysolite, an onyx and a jasper.
They were set in gold settings.
[14] There were 12 stones, one for each name of the people of Israel.
A name was engraved on each stone, the way a seal is engraved, for the 12 tribes.

[15] They made chains for the breastplate like braided cords of pure gold.
[16] They made two settings of gold and two rings of gold.
They put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.
[17] They put the two braided chains of gold through the two rings at the ends of the breastplate.
[18] They fastened the other two ends of the two chains to the two settings.
And they put them on the front of the shoulder straps of the ephod.

[19] They made two more rings of gold.
They put them on the two ends of the breastplate, on its inside edge, next to the ephod.
[20] And they made two more rings of gold.
They put them low on the front of the two shoulder straps of the ephod,
close to the place where they are joined, above the woven band of the ephod.
[21] They tied the breastplate by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord.
So it stayed on the woven band of the ephod, and the breastplate could not come loose from the ephod,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible gives the size as “a span.” A span is about 9 inches.
> The 12 stones stand for the 12 tribes of Israel. The priest carries them on his heart before God.

---

## The robe and the other clothes (Verses 22–31)

[22] He made the robe that is worn under the ephod, of woven work, all of blue cloth.
[23] The robe had an opening in the middle, like the neck opening of a coat of armor.
There was an edge around the opening, so that it would not tear.
[24] On the hem of the robe they made pomegranates
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of twisted linen.
[25] They made bells of pure gold.
They put the bells between the pomegranates, all around the hem of the robe, between the pomegranates:
[26] a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate,
all around the hem of the robe, for serving,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

[27] They made the tunics of fine linen, of woven work, for Aaron and for his sons,
[28] the turban of fine linen,
the linen headbands of fine linen,
the linen pants of fine twisted linen,
[29] and the sash of fine twisted linen and of blue, purple and scarlet cloth,
the work of an embroiderer,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

[30] They made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold.
They wrote words on it, the way a seal is engraved:
“HOLY TO THE LORD.”
[31] They tied a blue cord to it, to fasten it on the turban above,
as the LORD commanded Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> The bells on the hem, the golden plate on the forehead with “Holy to the LORD” – everything is finished now. You can read more about it in the explanation for Exodus 28.

## The work is finished (Verses 32–43)

[32] So all the work on the tabernacle, the Tent of Meeting, was finished.
The people of Israel did everything as the LORD had told Moses. That is what they did.
[33] They brought the tabernacle to Moses:
the tent with all its furniture,
its clasps, its boards, its bars, its pillars and its sockets,
[34] the covering of rams’ skins dyed red,
the covering of sea cow hides,
the veil that hangs as a screen,
[35] the ark of the covenant with its poles, the mercy seat,
[36] the table with all its things, and the bread of the presence,
[37] the pure lamp stand with its lamps, the lamps set in a row,
with all its things and the oil for the light,
[38] the golden altar, the anointing oil, the sweet incense,
the screen for the door of the Tent,
[39] the bronze altar with its grating of bronze, its poles and all its tools,
the basin and its base,
[40] the hangings of the courtyard, its pillars and its sockets,
the screen for the gate of the courtyard, its ropes and its tent pegs,
and all the tools for the service of the tabernacle, for the Tent of Meeting,
[41] the finely made clothes for serving in the holy place,
the holy clothes for Aaron the priest and the clothes of his sons
for their service as priests.

[42] The people of Israel did all the work exactly as the LORD had told Moses.
[43] Moses looked at all the work.
And look: They had done it as the LORD had commanded. That is what they had done.
And Moses blessed them.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are a reminder of the story of creation. There it says: “God looked at everything he had made. And look: It was very good” (Genesis 1:31). And God blessed the seventh day (Genesis 2:3).
> Here Moses looks at all the work. It is done well. And Moses blesses the people.
> Just as God made the world as a home for people, people have now made a home for God.
