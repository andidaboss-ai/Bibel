# Exodus – Chapter 1
### Israel is oppressed in Egypt

---

## Before we begin: What is the book of Exodus?

The book of Exodus is also called “the Second Book of Moses.”
The name “Exodus” means “going out.”
It tells how God frees his people Israel from slavery in Egypt.
The main person is Moses.
God leads the people through the sea and through the desert to Mount Sinai.
There God makes a covenant with his people and gives them the Ten Commandments.
At the end, the people build a holy tent, where God lives among them.
For Jews, the going out from Egypt is still the most important event in their history. They celebrate it every year at the Passover festival.

---

## A family becomes a people (Verses 1–7)

[1] These are the names of the sons of Israel who came to Egypt.
Each man came with Jacob, together with his household:
[2] Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah,
[3] Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin,
[4] Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.
[5] All the people who came from Jacob were 70 people.
Joseph was already in Egypt.

[6] Then Joseph died.
All his brothers died too, and all the people of that generation.
[7] But the people of Israel had many children.
They grew in number very much.
They became very many and very strong.
The land was full of them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book of Exodus goes on right where the book of Genesis ends. Again the sons of Jacob are named.
> God had promised Abraham: Your descendants will be as many as the stars. In Egypt this comes true. 70 people become a great people.
> The words here remind us of God’s blessing at creation: “Have many children, and become many!” (Genesis 1:28). God’s blessing is at work – even in a foreign land.

---

## A new king (Verses 8–14)

[8] Then a new king came to power over Egypt.
He did not know about Joseph.
[9] He said to his people,
“Look! The people of the people of Israel are more and stronger than we are.
[10] Come, let’s be clever with them.
Otherwise they will become even more.
And when a war breaks out, they will join our enemies.
Then they will fight against us and leave the land.”

[11] So the Egyptians put slave drivers over them.
They were to oppress them with heavy work.
The Israelites built storage cities for Pharaoh: Pithom and Raamses.
[12] But the more the Egyptians oppressed them, the more they grew in number.
And the more they spread out.
The Egyptians began to dread the people of Israel.

[13] The Egyptians forced the people of Israel to work without mercy.
[14] They made their lives bitter with hard work:
with mortar and with bricks,
and with all kinds of work in the field.
In all this work, they forced them without mercy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many years have passed. The new Pharaoh “did not know about Joseph.” That means: He no longer remembers that an Israelite once saved Egypt from hunger. Or he does not care.
> Pharaoh is afraid of the foreigners in his land. Fear turns into oppression. Sadly, this is still a well-known pattern today: People make a minority into a scapegoat.
> The Israelites become slaves. They have to make bricks out of clay and build cities with them.
> But God still lets his people grow. The oppression brings about the opposite of what Pharaoh wants.

---

## The brave midwives (Verses 15–21)

[15] The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives.
The name of one was Shiphrah.
The name of the other was Puah.
[16] He said,
“When you help the Hebrew women give birth, watch them on the birth stool.
If it is a son, kill him.
But if it is a daughter, she may live.”

[17] But the midwives feared God.
They did not do what the king of Egypt had commanded them.
They let the baby boys live.
[18] Then the king of Egypt called for the midwives.
He said to them,
“Why have you done this? Why have you let the boys live?”
[19] The midwives said to Pharaoh,
“The Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women.
They are strong.
They give birth before the midwife comes to them.”

[20] God was good to the midwives.
And the people grew in number and became very strong.
[21] Because the midwives feared God, he gave them families of their own.

> **What does this mean?**
> A midwife is a woman who helps at a birth.
> Pharaoh orders a cruel mass murder of babies. But two women refuse – in the middle of the most powerful kingdom in the world.
> They have more respect for God than for the king. This is the first act of nonviolent resistance in the Bible.
> Shiphrah and Puah are named. The name of the Pharaoh is not given. For the Bible, these brave women are more important than the powerful king.
> It is not quite clear whether they are lying or telling the truth. What is clear: God is on their side, because they save lives.

---

## Pharaoh’s command (Verse 22)

[22] Then Pharaoh commanded all his people,
“Throw every son who is born into the river.
But let every daughter live.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Now it gets even worse. Pharaoh orders all Egyptians to kill the Hebrew boys.
> The river is the Nile. For the Egyptians, the Nile was a god who gives life. Now it is to become a place of death.
> But it is in the Nile that God will save a child: Moses. The next chapter tells about it.
