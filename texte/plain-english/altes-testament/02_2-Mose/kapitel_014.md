# Exodus – Chapter 14
### The way through the sea

---

## Trapped between the sea and the desert (Verses 1–4)

[1] The LORD spoke to Moses.
He said,
[2] “Tell the people of Israel to turn back.
They must camp in front of Pihahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, in front of Baal Zephon.
You must camp across from it, by the sea.
[3] Then Pharaoh will say about the people of Israel,
‘They are lost in the land.
The wilderness has shut them in.’
[4] I will make Pharaoh’s heart hard, and he will chase after them.
Then I will show my glory through Pharaoh and through all his army.
And the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD.”
They did so.

> **What does this mean?**
> God lets the Israelites go into a dead end on purpose: the sea in front of them, the desert behind them. From a human point of view, a bad plan.
> But God has a plan: Pharaoh is to think that it will be easy for him. Then God will show who is really mighty.
> Today no one knows for sure exactly where the places Pihahiroth, Migdol and Baal Zephon were.

---

## Pharaoh chases after them (Verses 5–9)

[5] The king of Egypt was told that the people had fled.
Then Pharaoh and his servants changed their minds about the people.
They said,
“What have we done?
We have let Israel go, and now they no longer serve us!”
[6] Pharaoh got his chariot ready.
He took his army with him.
[7] He took 600 chosen chariots
and all the other chariots of Egypt,
with officers over all of them.

[8] The LORD made the heart of Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, hard.
And Pharaoh chased after the people of Israel.
But the people of Israel went out with a raised hand.
[9] The Egyptians chased after them.
All of Pharaoh’s horses and chariots, his horsemen, and his army caught up with them.
The Israelites were camping by the sea,
beside Pihahiroth, in front of Baal Zephon.

> **What does this mean?**
> As soon as the slaves are gone, Pharaoh regrets it. He thinks only about the workers he has lost. Grief over his son has not changed him.
> Back then, chariots were the strongest weapon. They were fast and dangerous. Against people on foot, they could hardly be beaten.
> “With a raised hand” means: boldly, confidently, like winners.

---

## “Do not be afraid!” (Verses 10–14)

[10] When Pharaoh came near, the people of Israel looked up.
They saw the Egyptians marching after them.
The people of Israel became very afraid.
They cried out to the LORD.
[11] They said to Moses,
“Were there no graves in Egypt?
Is that why you have taken us away to die in the wilderness?
Why have you done this to us, bringing us out of Egypt?
[12] Did we not tell you this in Egypt?
We said, ‘Leave us alone, so that we can serve the Egyptians.’
It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness!”

[13] Moses said to the people,
“Do not be afraid!
Stand still and see how the LORD will save you today.
For you will never again see the Egyptians whom you have seen today.
[14] The LORD will fight for you.
And you must be still.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The fear is huge. In front of them is the sea, behind them the strongest army in the world. The people see no way out.
> In their panic they even want to go back into slavery. Better to be unfree and alive than free and dead. This is human and understandable.
> “Were there no graves in Egypt?” – a bitter mockery. Egypt was famous for its huge tombs, the pyramids.
> Moses’ answer is one of the best-known sentences in the Bible: “The LORD will fight for you. And you must be still.” Sometimes you cannot do anything yourself. Then you may trust that God will act.

---

## The sea divides (Verses 15–22)

[15] The LORD said to Moses,
“Why are you crying out to me?
Tell the people of Israel to go forward.
[16] And you: Lift up your staff.
Reach out your hand over the sea and divide it.
Then the people of Israel will go through the middle of the sea on dry ground.
[17] And look: I myself will make the hearts of the Egyptians hard, and they will go in after them.
Then I will show my glory through Pharaoh and through all his army,
through his chariots and through his horsemen.
[18] Then the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD,
when I have shown my glory through Pharaoh, through his chariots, and through his horsemen.”

[19] Then the angel of God, who went in front of the camp of Israel, moved and went behind them.
The pillar of cloud also moved from in front of them and stood behind them.
[20] It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel.
There was the cloud and the darkness.
Yet it gave light in the night.
So the one camp did not come near the other all night.

[21] Moses reached out his hand over the sea.
Then the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind all night.
He made the sea into dry land.
The waters were divided.
[22] The people of Israel went into the middle of the sea on dry ground.
The waters were like a wall for them, on their right and on their left.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Why are you crying out to me? … go forward.” – There is a time to pray and a time to act. Now the Israelites must take the first step.
> The pillar of cloud becomes a shield. For the Egyptians it is dark, for the Israelites it is bright. The same presence of God means protection for some and a barrier for others.
> God uses a wind, as at creation and after the flood (Genesis 1:2; 8:1). Again God makes dry land in the middle of the water. It is like a new creation: Slaves become a free people.

---

## The Egyptians drown (Verses 23–31)

[23] The Egyptians chased after them.
All of Pharaoh’s horses, his chariots, and his horsemen went in after them into the middle of the sea.
[24] In the last watch of the night, just before morning, the LORD looked down on the Egyptian army from the pillar of fire and of cloud.
And he threw the Egyptian army into confusion.
[25] He made the wheels come off their chariots,
so that they could only drive them with great trouble.
Then the Egyptians said,
“Let’s flee from Israel!
For the LORD is fighting for them against the Egyptians!”

[26] The LORD said to Moses,
“Reach out your hand over the sea.
Then the waters will come back over the Egyptians, over their chariots, and over their horsemen.”
[27] Moses reached out his hand over the sea.
When morning came, the sea went back to its full strength.
The Egyptians fled right into it.
So the LORD threw the Egyptians into the middle of the sea.
[28] The waters came back and covered the chariots and the horsemen,
the whole army of Pharaoh that had gone in after them into the sea.
Not one of them was left.

[29] But the people of Israel walked on dry land through the middle of the sea.
The waters were like a wall for them, on their right and on their left.
[30] So on that day the LORD saved Israel from the hand of the Egyptians.
And Israel saw the Egyptians lying dead on the seashore.
[31] Israel saw the great power that the LORD had used against the Egyptians.
Then the people feared the LORD.
And they believed in the LORD and in his servant Moses.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, the night was divided into watches. The last watch of the night was just before sunrise.
> Now even the Egyptians say: “The LORD is fighting for them.” At last they see who the true God is – but too late.
> Pharaoh wanted to drown the Israelites’ babies in the water (Exodus 1:22). Now his own army drowns in the water.
> The freedom is complete. The slaves are free. The oppressors can never chase them again.
> The people see and believe. To this day, this crossing of the sea is the great sign for Israel that God saves.
> Christians also see in it a picture of baptism: through the water into new life (1 Corinthians 10:1–2).
