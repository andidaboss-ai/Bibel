# Exodus – Chapter 27
### The altar for burnt offerings, the courtyard and the oil for the lamp stand

---

## The altar for burnt offerings (Verses 1–8)

[1] “Make the altar of acacia wood.
It shall be about 7 feet 6 inches long and about 7 feet 6 inches wide.
So the altar shall be square.
It shall be about 4 feet 6 inches high.
[2] Make horns on its four corners.
The horns shall be one piece with the altar.
Cover it with bronze.
[3] Also make its pots for taking away the ashes,
and its shovels, its basins, its meat hooks and its fire pans.
Make all its tools of bronze.

[4] “Make a grating of bronze for the altar, like a net.
On the net, make four bronze rings at its four corners.
[5] Put the grating under the ledge that goes around the altar, down below.
The net shall reach halfway up the altar.
[6] Make poles for the altar, poles of acacia wood, and cover them with bronze.
[7] The poles shall be put into the rings.
When the altar is carried, the poles shall be on its two sides.
[8] Make the altar hollow, out of planks.
They shall make it just as it was shown to you on the mountain.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: 5 cubits long and wide, 3 cubits high. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> This altar stands outside in the courtyard, in front of the entrance of the sanctuary. The animal sacrifices are burned on it.
> The “horns” are points on the four corners. People saw them as very holy. Someone who was being hunted could hold on to the horns of the altar and ask for protection (1 Kings 1:50).
> The altar is hollow inside and has poles for carrying. So it can be taken along on the journey through the desert.
> Here everything is made of bronze, not of gold. The farther away from the Most Holy Place, the simpler the materials become.

## The courtyard (Verses 9–19)

[9] “Make the courtyard of the tabernacle.
For the south side, there shall be hangings of fine twisted linen for the courtyard, about 150 feet long for this one side.
[10] Its pillars shall be 20, with 20 sockets of bronze.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands shall be of silver.
[11] It shall be the same on the north side:
hangings about 150 feet long, with 20 pillars and 20 sockets of bronze.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands shall be of silver.

[12] “For the width of the courtyard on the west side, there shall be hangings about 75 feet long,
with ten pillars and ten sockets.
[13] On the east side, toward the sunrise, the courtyard shall also be about 75 feet wide.
[14] On one side of the gate there shall be hangings about 22 feet 6 inches long,
with three pillars and three sockets.
[15] And on the other side there shall be hangings, also about 22 feet 6 inches long,
with three pillars and three sockets.
[16] For the gate of the courtyard there shall be a screen about 30 feet long.
It shall be made of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer.
It shall have four pillars and four sockets.

[17] “All the pillars around the courtyard shall be joined with bands of silver.
Their hooks shall be of silver, and their sockets of bronze.
[18] The courtyard shall be about 150 feet long and about 75 feet wide all along.
The hangings shall be about 7 feet 6 inches high, made of fine twisted linen.
And their sockets shall be of bronze.
[19] All the tools of the tabernacle that are needed for its service,
all its tent pegs and all the tent pegs of the courtyard shall be of bronze.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: The courtyard is 100 cubits long and 50 cubits wide. The hangings are 5 cubits high. The gate is 20 cubits wide, and the parts on each side of it are 15 cubits each.
> The courtyard is a large open space with a fence around the sanctuary. It is about as big as four tennis courts.
> The white linen hangings were more than 7 feet high. No one could see in from the outside. So in the middle of the camp, there was a holy area that was set apart.
> There is only one entrance, on the east side. It is especially colorful and beautiful.

## The oil for the lamp stand (Verses 20–21)

[20] “Command the people of Israel to bring you pure olive oil.
It shall be made from beaten olives, for the light.
With it, a lamp shall burn all the time.
[21] It shall be in the Tent of Meeting, outside the veil that is in front of the covenant.
There Aaron and his sons shall keep the lamp burning before the LORD from evening until morning.
This is a law forever, for all the generations of the people of Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The best oil is used for the lamps: The olives are not pressed hard. They are only beaten lightly. So the oil is very pure and burns brightly, without much smoke.
> “Tent of Meeting” is another name for the sanctuary. There God and people meet.
> The light shall burn every night. To this day, an “eternal light” burns in every synagogue. An eternal light also burns in Catholic churches. It shows: God is present here.
