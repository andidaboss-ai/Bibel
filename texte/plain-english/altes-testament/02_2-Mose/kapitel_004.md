# Exodus – Chapter 4
### God gives Moses signs – Moses goes back to Egypt

---

## Three signs (Verses 1–9)

[1] Moses answered,
“But they will not believe me.
They will not listen to me.
They will say, ‘The LORD has not appeared to you.’”
[2] The LORD said to him,
“What is that in your hand?”
Moses said,
“A staff.”
[3] The LORD said,
“Throw it on the ground.”
Moses threw it on the ground.
Then it became a snake.
And Moses ran away from it.
[4] The LORD said to Moses,
“Reach out your hand and take it by the tail.”
Moses reached out his hand and took hold of it.
Then it became a staff in his hand again.
[5] “This is so that they may believe that the LORD has appeared to you,
the God of their fathers,
the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.”

[6] The LORD also said to him,
“Now put your hand inside your cloak.”
Moses put his hand inside his cloak.
When he took it out, his hand had leprosy.
It was as white as snow.
[7] The LORD said,
“Put your hand inside your cloak again.”
Moses put his hand inside his cloak again.
When he took it out of his cloak, it was healthy again, like the rest of his body.

[8] “If they do not believe you and do not listen to the first sign,
they will believe the second sign.
[9] And if they do not believe even these two signs and do not listen to you,
then take some water from the river and pour it on the dry land.
The water that you take from the river will become blood on the dry land.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses still has doubts. God is patient and gives him signs that he can show the Israelites.
> The staff was a simple shepherd’s staff – the tool of his daily work. God makes something special out of it. God uses what we already have in our hands.
> In Egypt, the snake was a sign of Pharaoh’s power. Moses grabs it by the tail. This shows: God is stronger than the power of Egypt.
> Leprosy was a feared skin disease. God can make people sick and heal them.
> The river is the Nile. For the Egyptians, the Nile was holy. When its water turns into blood, it shows: God is Lord over the gods of Egypt too.

---

## “I am not good with words” (Verses 10–17)

[10] Moses said to the LORD,
“Oh, Lord, I am not good with words.
I was not good with words before,
and I am not good with words now, since you have spoken to your servant.
I speak slowly, and my tongue is slow.”

[11] The LORD said to him,
“Who made the human mouth?
Who makes a person mute or deaf, seeing or blind?
Is it not I, the LORD?
[12] So go now.
I will be with your mouth.
I will teach you what to say.”

[13] But Moses said,
“Oh, Lord, please send someone else.”

[14] Then the LORD became very angry with Moses.
He said,
“What about your brother Aaron, the Levite?
I know that he can speak well.
And look: He is already coming out to meet you.
When he sees you, he will be glad in his heart.
[15] You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth.
I will be with your mouth and with his mouth.
I will teach you what to do.
[16] He will speak to the people for you.
He will be a mouth for you.
And you will be like God to him.
[17] Take this staff in your hand.
With it you will do the signs.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses keeps finding new excuses. Maybe he stuttered. Maybe after 40 years in the desert he had forgotten the Egyptian language.
> God’s answer: I made you. I know your weaknesses. I will help you.
> When Moses then simply says “Send someone else,” God becomes angry. Behind the excuses is the fear of trusting God.
> But God still gives in and sends help: Moses’ brother Aaron. God takes our weaknesses into account.
> “You will be like God to him” means: Moses receives the message from God and passes it on to Aaron, the way God speaks to a prophet.

---

## Moses goes back to Egypt (Verses 18–23)

[18] Moses went back to Jethro, his father-in-law.
He said to him,
“Please let me go back to my brothers in Egypt.
I want to see whether they are still alive.”
Jethro said to Moses,
“Go in peace.”

[19] The LORD said to Moses in Midian,
“Go back to Egypt.
For all the men who wanted to kill you are dead.”
[20] So Moses took his wife and his sons.
He put them on a donkey and went back to the land of Egypt.
Moses took God’s staff in his hand.

[21] The LORD said to Moses,
“When you go back to Egypt, do all the wonders before Pharaoh that I have put in your hand.
But I will make his heart hard.
Then he will not let the people go.
[22] Tell Pharaoh,
‘The LORD says:
Israel is my son, my firstborn.
[23] I have told you,
“Let my son go, so that he may serve me.”
But you have refused to let him go.
So I will kill your son, your firstborn.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses is respectful. He asks his father-in-law for permission.
> The shepherd’s staff is now called “God’s staff.” Through God’s task, a simple object has become something special.
> “I will make his heart hard” – for many readers this is a difficult passage. Does it mean that Pharaoh has no choice? In the next chapters, it often also says: Pharaoh made his heart hard **himself**. Many interpreters understand it like this: Pharaoh decides against God again and again. And God lets him stay with his decision and confirms it.
> “Israel is my son, my firstborn” – God loves his people the way a father loves his child. Pharaoh will not set God’s son free. So he will lose his own son. That is the last and worst of the ten plagues (Chapter 12).

## A mysterious night (Verses 24–26)

[24] On the way, at a place where they stayed for the night, the LORD met Moses.
He wanted to kill him.
[25] Then Zipporah took a sharp flint stone.
She cut off the foreskin of her son.
She threw it at his feet and said,
“You really are a bridegroom of blood to me.”
[26] So the LORD left him alone.
Then she said, “You are a bridegroom of blood.”
She said this because of the circumcision.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is one of the most mysterious passages in the whole Bible. No one can explain it with complete certainty.
> The most common explanation: Moses had not circumcised his son, even though circumcision was the sign of the covenant with God (Genesis 17). Whoever is to lead God’s people must take God’s covenant seriously himself.
> Zipporah, a foreign woman, sees the danger and acts quickly. She saves Moses’ life – just like the midwives, his mother, his sister and Pharaoh’s daughter did before.
> Whose feet she touches – those of Moses or those of the son – is not quite clear in the text. In Hebrew, “feet” can also be a modest word for the private parts.
> “Bridegroom of blood” was perhaps an old expression that had to do with circumcision. Today no one knows exactly what it means.

## Moses and Aaron with the people (Verses 27–31)

[27] The LORD said to Aaron,
“Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.”
So Aaron went.
He met Moses at God’s mountain and kissed him.
[28] Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD, who had sent him.
He also told him about all the signs the LORD had commanded him to do.

[29] Then Moses and Aaron went.
They gathered all the elders of the children of Israel.
[30] Aaron spoke all the words that the LORD had spoken to Moses.
And he did the signs before the eyes of the people.
[31] The people believed.
They heard that the LORD had come to look after the children of Israel,
and that he had seen their misery.
Then they bowed their heads and worshiped.

> **What does this mean?**
> The brothers meet at God’s mountain, where God met Moses in the burning bush.
> Moses was afraid that no one would believe him. But exactly the opposite happens: The people believe and worship God.
> After long years of oppression, the people finally hear: God has not forgotten us. He has seen our suffering. That is the best news anyone could bring them.
