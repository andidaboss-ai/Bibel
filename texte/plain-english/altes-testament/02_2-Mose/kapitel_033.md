# Exodus – Chapter 33
### Will God still go with them? Moses wants to see God’s glory

---

## “I will not go up among you” (Verses 1–6)

[1] The LORD said to Moses,
“Leave. Go up from here,
you and the people that you brought up out of the land of Egypt.
Go to the land that I promised to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob with an oath.
I said, ‘I will give it to your descendants.’
[2] I will send an angel in front of you.
And I will drive out the Canaanite, the Amorite, the Hittite, the Perizzite, the Hivite and the Jebusite.
[3] Go to a land flowing with milk and honey.
But I will not go up among you myself,
because you are a stiff-necked people.
Otherwise I might destroy you on the way.”

[4] When the people heard this bad news, they mourned.
And no one put on his jewelry.
[5] For the LORD had said to Moses,
“Tell the people of Israel,
‘You are a stiff-necked people.
If I went up among you for just one moment, I would destroy you.
So now take off your jewelry.
Then I will know what to do with you.’”
[6] So the people of Israel took off their jewelry, from Mount Horeb on.

> **What does this mean?**
> God keeps his promise: The people will get the land. But God himself does not want to go with them. Only an angel shall lead them.
> At first, that does not sound so bad. But the people understand: The land without God is worth nothing. That is why they mourn.
> God says: Being close to me would be dangerous for you. A holy God and a people that turns against him – that does not fit together.
> Taking off jewelry is a sign of sadness and of being sorry. After all, they had made the golden calf out of jewelry.
> Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai.

---

## The Tent of Meeting outside the camp (Verses 7–11)

[7] Moses used to take the tent and set it up outside the camp, far away from the camp.
He called it “the Tent of Meeting.”
Everyone who wanted to seek the LORD went out to the Tent of Meeting, which was outside the camp.
[8] Whenever Moses went out to the Tent, all the people got up.
Each person stood at the door of his tent.
And they watched Moses until he had gone into the Tent.
[9] When Moses went into the Tent, the pillar of cloud came down and stood at the door of the Tent.
And the LORD spoke with Moses.
[10] All the people saw the pillar of cloud standing at the door of the Tent.
Then all the people got up and worshiped, each person at the door of his tent.
[11] The LORD spoke to Moses face to face,
as a man speaks to his friend.
Then Moses went back into the camp.
But his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, did not leave the Tent.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is not yet the large sanctuary that Chapters 25–31 describe. That one is built later. Here it is a simpler tent, where Moses meets God.
> The tent now stands outside the camp. This shows: God has moved away. Whoever seeks him must go out.
> “As a man speaks to his friend” – that is how close Moses is to God. “Face to face” here means: very directly and as close friends. Verse 20 says that Moses cannot really see God’s face.
> Joshua stays in the tent. Already now he is learning to be close to God. Later he will take Moses’ place as leader.

## Moses asks: Go with us! (Verses 12–17)

[12] Moses said to the LORD,
“Look, you tell me, ‘Bring up these people.’
But you have not let me know whom you will send with me.
Yet you have said, ‘I know you by name,
and you have found favor in my eyes.’
[13] So if I have found favor in your eyes,
then please show me your way now.
Then I can know you and find favor in your eyes.
And remember: These people are your people.”
[14] He answered, “My presence will go with you.
And I will give you rest.”
[15] Moses said to him,
“If your presence does not go with me, then do not take us up from here.
[16] For how else will people know that I and your people have found favor in your eyes?
Is it not because you go with us?
That is what makes me and your people different from all the peoples on the face of the earth.”
[17] The LORD said to Moses,
“I will also do this thing that you have asked.
For you have found favor in my eyes, and I know you by name.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses does not give up. He reminds God: “These people are your people.” In Chapter 32 God had said “your people,” meaning: the people of Moses. Moses gives them back to God.
> “My presence will go with you” means: I myself will go with you, not just an angel. Word for word, the Hebrew says: “My face will go with you.”
> Moses says: Without you, we do not even want to start out. What makes Israel special is not the land or power. What makes Israel special is that God goes with it.
> And God lets himself be asked. Again the Bible shows: Moses’ prayer changes something.

---

## Moses wants to see God’s glory (Verses 18–23)

[18] Moses said, “Please show me your glory!”
[19] He answered,
“I will make all my goodness pass in front of you.
And I will call out the name of the LORD before you.
I will be kind to whom I will be kind.
And I will show mercy to whom I will show mercy.”
[20] He also said,
“You cannot see my face.
For no one can see me and live.”
[21] The LORD also said,
“Look, there is a place near me. Stand on the rock.
[22] While my glory passes by, I will put you in a crack in the rock.
And I will cover you with my hand until I have passed by.
[23] Then I will take my hand away.
And you will see my back.
But my face shall not be seen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses wishes for the greatest thing a person can wish for: to see God himself.
> God answers with his “goodness.” This shows: God’s glory is, above all, his goodness.
> “I will be kind to whom I will be kind” means: God’s kindness is a gift. No one can earn it or force it. God gives it freely.
> No one can see God’s face. God is too great and too holy for our eyes. But God protects Moses with his hand and shows him as much as he can bear.
> “You will see my back” – maybe this means: Often we only recognize God afterward, by the traces he has left behind.
> The New Testament says that no one has ever seen God, but that the only Son, who is very close to the Father, has made him known (John 1:18). Christians believe: In Jesus, you can see what God is like.
