# Exodus – Chapter 38
### Altar for burnt offerings, basin, courtyard – and what everything cost

---

## The altar for burnt offerings (Verses 1–7)

[1] He made the altar of burnt offering of acacia wood.
It was square: about 7 feet 6 inches long and about 7 feet 6 inches wide.
And it was about 4 feet 6 inches high.
[2] He made its horns on its four corners.
Its horns were one piece with it.
And he covered it with bronze.
[3] He made all the tools of the altar:
the pots, the shovels, the basins, the forks and the fire pans.
He made all its tools of bronze.
[4] He made a grating of bronze for the altar, like a net.
It was under the ledge that goes around the altar, down below.
It reached halfway up.
[5] He cast four rings for the four corners of the bronze grating.
They were to hold the poles.
[6] He made the poles of acacia wood and covered them with bronze.
[7] He put the poles into the rings on the sides of the altar, to carry it with them.
He made it hollow, out of planks.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: 5 cubits long and wide, 3 cubits high. A cubit is about 18 inches.
> All of this was commanded in Chapter 27. Now it is done exactly that way.

---

## The basin made of mirrors (Verse 8)

[8] He made the basin of bronze and its base of bronze.
He made them from the mirrors of the women who served at the door of the Tent of Meeting.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then, there were no mirrors made of glass. Mirrors were made of bronze. The bronze was polished so brightly that people could see themselves in it.
> The women give away their mirrors. For them, this was something valuable and personal. It becomes the basin in which the priests wash.
> Here it is mentioned in passing that women served at the entrance of the sanctuary. The Bible does not say what exactly their task was. They are also mentioned in 1 Samuel 2:22.

## The courtyard (Verses 9–20)

[9] He made the courtyard.
On the south side, the hangings of the courtyard were made of fine twisted linen, about 150 feet long.
[10] Their pillars were 20, and their sockets 20, of bronze.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands were of silver.
[11] On the north side it was also about 150 feet,
with 20 pillars and 20 sockets of bronze.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands were of silver.
[12] On the west side there were hangings about 75 feet long,
with ten pillars and ten sockets.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands were of silver.
[13] On the east side, toward the sunrise, it was also about 75 feet.
[14] The hangings for one side were about 22 feet 6 inches long,
with three pillars and three sockets.
[15] And it was the same on the other side:
On both sides of the gate of the courtyard there were hangings about 22 feet 6 inches long,
with three pillars and three sockets.
[16] All the hangings around the courtyard were of fine twisted linen.

[17] The sockets for the pillars were of bronze.
The hooks of the pillars and their bands were of silver.
Their capitals were covered with silver.
All the pillars of the courtyard had silver bands.
[18] The screen for the gate of the courtyard was the work of an embroiderer,
of blue, purple and scarlet cloth and of fine twisted linen.
It was about 30 feet long and about 7 feet 6 inches high, just as high as the hangings of the courtyard.
[19] Their pillars were four, and their sockets four, of bronze.
Their hooks were of silver.
And the covering of their capitals and their bands were of silver.
[20] All the tent pegs of the tabernacle and of the courtyard around it were of bronze.

> **What does this mean?**
> In cubits: The courtyard is 100 cubits long and 50 cubits wide. The hangings next to the gate are 15 cubits long each. The gate is 20 cubits wide and 5 cubits high.
> A “capital” is the top part of a pillar.
> You can read more about the courtyard in the explanation for Exodus 27.

## The accounts: gold, silver and bronze (Verses 21–31)

[21] This is the list of the materials for the tabernacle, the Tabernacle of the Testimony.
They were counted at the command of Moses,
for the service of the Levites, under the leadership of Ithamar, the son of Aaron the priest.
[22] Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, from the tribe of Judah,
made everything that the LORD had commanded Moses.
[23] With him was Oholiab, the son of Ahisamach, from the tribe of Dan.
He was an engraver and a skillful worker,
and an embroiderer in blue, purple and scarlet thread and in fine linen.

[24] All the gold that was used for the work on the whole sanctuary, the gold of the offering,
was about 2,200 pounds, by the weight of the shekel of the sanctuary.

[25] The silver from those of the community who were counted was about 7,540 pounds, by the weight of the shekel of the sanctuary.
[26] That was a beka for each person, that is, half a shekel by the weight of the shekel of the sanctuary,
from everyone who passed over to those who were counted, 20 years old or older.
There were 603,550 men.
[27] With about 7,500 pounds of silver, they cast the sockets of the sanctuary and the sockets of the veil:
100 sockets from this amount of silver, each socket about 75 pounds.
[28] From the other silver, about 43 pounds, he made the hooks for the pillars.
He covered their capitals and made bands for them.

[29] The bronze of the offering was about 5,300 pounds.
[30] With it he made the sockets for the door of the Tent of Meeting,
the bronze altar, the bronze grating for it and all the tools of the altar,
[31] the sockets around the courtyard, the sockets of the gate of the courtyard,
all the tent pegs of the tabernacle and all the tent pegs around the courtyard.

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Bible, the weights are given in talents and shekels. A talent is about 75 pounds. A shekel is about 0.4 ounces.
> Gold: 29 talents and 730 shekels. Silver: 100 talents and 1,775 shekels. Bronze: 70 talents and 2,400 shekels. 100 sockets from 100 talents, one talent for each socket. The other 1,775 shekels for hooks and bands.
> A “beka” is half a shekel, about one fifth of an ounce of silver. This is the offering from Exodus 30:13. The numbers add up exactly: 603,550 half shekels are 100 talents and 1,775 shekels.
> All together, about one ton of gold, more than three tons of silver and more than two tons of bronze. The sanctuary was a great and precious work.
> Here the Bible gives an exact account. Money that was given for God should be handled honestly and openly.
> The number 603,550 also appears in the great census in Numbers 1:46.
