# James – Chapter 4
### Come near to God

---

## Where conflict comes from (Verses 1–10)

[1] Where do the wars and fights among you come from?
Don’t they come from your desires
that fight in your bodies?
[2] You want something
and don’t get it.
You murder and are jealous
and still can’t get anything.
You fight and make war.
You don’t have,
because you don’t ask.
[3] You ask,
but you don’t receive,
because you ask with wrong motives.
You only want to spend it on your pleasures.
[4] You adulterers and adulteresses!
Don’t you know
that friendship with the world is hostility toward God?
So whoever wants to be a friend of the world
makes himself an enemy of God.
[5] Or do you think
that the Scripture says in vain,
“The Spirit
who lives in us
yearns jealously”?
[6] But he gives more grace.
That is why it says,
“God resists the proud,
but gives grace to the humble.”
[7] So be subject to God.
Resist the devil,
and he will flee from you.
[8] Come near to God,
and he will come near to you.
Wash your hands,
you sinners.
Purify your hearts,
you double-minded.
[9] Lament,
mourn
and weep.
Let your laughter be turned to mourning
and your joy to gloom.
[10] Humble yourselves before the Lord,
and he will lift you up.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Conflict and war begin in the heart: with jealousy, greed and the wish to have more and more.
> Verse 2: “You murder” is probably a drastic picture. Jesus says: Hatred and anger are already like murder (compare Matthew 5:21–22).
> Verse 4: “Adulterers”: In the Scriptures of Israel this is a picture for people who become unfaithful to God, as in a marriage. “The world” here means: a life that only asks about power, money and pleasure.
> Verse 5: We don’t know exactly which passage of Scripture is meant here. The verse is also translated in different ways.
> Verse 6 quotes Proverbs 3:34.
> Verse 8: A wonderful promise: When we come near to God, he comes to meet us.
> Verse 9: This is about true regret for one’s own wrongdoing, not about Christians never being allowed to laugh.

---

## Don’t judge one another (Verses 11–12)

[11] Don’t speak against one another,
brothers.
Whoever speaks against his brother
and judges his brother
speaks against the law
and judges the law.
But if you judge the law,
you are not a doer of the law,
but a judge.
[12] There is only one lawgiver and judge.
He is able to save and to destroy.
Who are you
to judge your neighbor?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: Only God is judge. We are not to condemn others or speak badly about them. This recalls the words of Jesus: “Don’t judge” (compare Matthew 7:1).
> That doesn’t mean that you may not name wrongdoing. But we are not to look down on other people.

---

## If the Lord wills (Verses 13–17)

[13] Listen,
you
who say,
“Today or tomorrow let’s travel to this or that city,
stay there a year,
trade
and make a profit.”
[14] You don’t know
what your life will be like tomorrow.
For what is your life?
You are like a vapor
that appears for a little time
and then vanishes away.
[15] Instead you ought to say,
“If the Lord wills,
we will live
and do this or that.”
[16] But now you glory in your boasting.
All such boasting is evil.
[17] So whoever knows
what is good
and doesn’t do it,
for him it is sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–15: Making plans is not wrong. But we are not to act as if we had everything in our hands. Life is precious and fragile.
> Verse 15: “If the Lord wills”: Many Christians still say or write this today when they plan something.
> Verse 17: An important sentence: Not only doing evil is sin, but also not doing good, although you could.
