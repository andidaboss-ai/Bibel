# James – Chapter 5
### Patience and prayer

---

## A warning to the rich (Verses 1–6)

[1] Now listen, you rich people!
Cry and howl,
because terrible troubles are coming on you.
[2] Your riches have rotted.
Moths have eaten your clothes.
[3] Your gold and your silver have rusted.
Their rust will be evidence against you.
It will eat your flesh like fire.
You have stored up treasure in the last days.
[4] Look!
You cheated the workers who mowed your fields.
You kept back their pay.
That pay cries out against you.
The cries of the people who harvested your crops
have reached the ears of the Lord of Armies.
[5] You have lived in luxury on the earth.
You have enjoyed your pleasures.
You have fattened your hearts,
like animals on a day of slaughter.
[6] You have condemned the righteous one,
and you have murdered him.
He does not fight back against you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–6: Very harsh words against rich people who gathered their riches at the expense of others. They recall the prophets of Israel, for example Amos, who stood up for the poor.
> Verse 4: Keeping back fair wages from workers is a serious injustice. Deuteronomy 24:14–15 already says this. God hears the cries of those who are exploited.
> “Lord of Armies”: A name of God from the Scriptures of Israel. It shows God’s power.
> For today: Exploitation, starvation wages and unfair working conditions exist today too, in rich countries and worldwide. Christians are to stand up for fair wages.
> Verse 6: “The righteous one”: This probably means poor, innocent people who are oppressed by the powerful.

## Be patient (Verses 7–12)

[7] So, brothers, be patient until the Lord comes.
Look at the farmer.
He waits for the precious crop of the land.
He is patient with it,
until it has received the early rain and the late rain.
[8] You too must be patient.
Make your hearts strong,
because the Lord will soon come.
[9] Brothers, don’t complain against one another,
so that you will not be judged.
Look, the judge is standing at the door!
[10] Brothers, the prophets spoke in the name of the Lord.
Take them as an example of how to suffer and how to keep going.
[11] Look, we call the people who endured blessed.
You have heard how Job kept going.
And you have seen how things turned out for him in the end through the Lord.
The Lord is full of compassion and mercy.
[12] But most of all, my brothers, don’t swear.
Do not swear by heaven,
or by the earth,
or with any other oath.
Let your “yes” be “yes,”
and your “no” be “no.”
Then you will not fall into hypocrisy.
Hypocrisy means: pretending to be better than you are.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The farmer in the land of Israel waited for two rainy seasons: the early rain in the fall and the late rain in the spring. Without them there was no harvest. In the same way Christians are to wait patiently for God.
> Verse 11: Job suffered a lot. He also told God his complaint and his anger. In the end God gave him good things again (Job 42). God is merciful.
> Verse 12: This recalls the words of Jesus in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:33–37). Christians are to be so honest that they need no oath.
> That is why some Christians, for example Mennonites and Quakers, still take no oath today. In many countries you can make a solemn affirmation in court instead of an oath.

## Pray for one another (Verses 13–20)

[13] Is anyone among you suffering?
He should pray.
Is anyone happy?
He should sing songs of praise.
[14] Is anyone among you sick?
He should call for the elders of the church.
They should pray over him.
And they should anoint him with oil in the name of the Lord.
To anoint means: to rub or pour oil on someone.
[15] The prayer of faith will heal the sick person.
The Lord will raise him up.
If he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.
[16] Confess your sins to one another.
And pray for one another, so that you may be healed.
The prayer of a righteous person who keeps on praying is very powerful.
[17] Elijah was a person like us.
He prayed hard that it would not rain.
And it did not rain on the earth for three years and six months.
[18] Then he prayed again.
And the sky gave rain,
and the earth produced its crops.
[19] My brothers, someone among you may wander away from the truth.
And someone else may bring him back.
[20] Then he should know this:
Whoever brings a sinner back from his wrong way
will save that person’s soul from death.
And he will cover many sins.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Praying in suffering, singing in joy: There are words before God for every situation in life.
> Verses 14–15: The elders are to pray for the sick and anoint them with oil. At that time oil was also a medicine. The Catholic and the Orthodox churches still celebrate the anointing of the sick as a sacrament today. In many Protestant churches too there are prayers and anointings for the sick.
> Very important: Sickness is not a punishment for sin. And prayer does not replace a doctor. Whoever is sick should go to the doctor and may also pray. If someone doesn’t get well, it is not because their faith is too weak.
> Verse 16: Confessing faults to one another and praying for one another can bring healing. Later confession developed from this.
> Verses 17–18: The story of Elijah is in 1 Kings 17–18. Elijah was a completely ordinary person, and yet God answered his prayer.
> Verses 19–20: The letter ends with a task: Take care of one another. If someone goes off the way, lovingly help them back.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter of James?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Hearing and doing:** God’s word is not only to be heard, but also lived (chapter 1).
> **True religion:** Caring for orphans, widows and all the weak (chapter 1).
> **Not favoring anyone:** Poor and rich are equal before God. Love your neighbor as yourself (chapter 2).
> **Faith and deeds:** Faith without deeds is dead. Paul and James complement each other (chapter 2).
> **The power of words:** The tongue is small, but powerful. Words can build up or destroy (chapter 3).
> **Wisdom from above:** It is peaceful, gentle and merciful (chapter 3).
> **Humility:** Come near to God, and he will come near to you. Don’t judge one another (chapter 4).
> **Justice for the poor:** Harsh words against rich people who cheat workers out of their wages (chapter 5).
> **Patience and prayer:** For those who suffer, the cheerful and the sick. Praying for one another (chapter 5).
> **For today:** Faith is practical. It shows itself in how we treat other people, especially the weak.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the first letter of Peter. It encourages Christians who suffer because of their faith.
