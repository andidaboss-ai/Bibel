# James – Chapter 1
### Hearing and doing

---

## Before we begin: The letter of James

The letter names itself a letter of James. According to an old tradition this is James, the brother of Jesus. He was an important leader of the church in Jerusalem (Acts 15 and Galatians 1:19). Many scholars think that the letter was written later in the spirit of James. Others consider James himself the author.
The letter is addressed to “the 12 tribes which are in the Dispersion.” This probably means Christians who lived outside the land of Israel, above all Christians of Jewish background.
The letter is very practical. It recalls the wisdom books of the Bible, for example Proverbs, and the Sermon on the Mount of Jesus.
The big theme is: Faith shows itself in deeds. The letter stands up strongly for the poor and warns the rich.
At the time of the Reformation people argued about this letter, because it says that faith without works is dead. Today most Christians see: Paul and James complement each other.
The letter of James has 5 chapters.

## Joy in trials (Verses 1–8)

[1] James,
a servant of God
and of the Lord Jesus Christ,
greets the 12 tribes which are in the Dispersion.
[2] My brothers,
count it all joy
when you fall into various temptations.
[3] For you know:
When your faith is tested,
it produces endurance.
[4] Let endurance finish its work,
so that you may be perfect and complete,
lacking in nothing.
[5] But if any of you lacks wisdom,
let him ask God for it.
God gives to all gladly
and without blaming.
Then it will be given to him.
[6] But let him ask in faith,
without doubting.
For whoever doubts
is like a wave of the sea,
driven by the wind and tossed back and forth.
[7] Such a person shouldn’t think
that he will receive anything from the Lord.
[8] He is a double-minded man,
unstable in all his ways.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “The 12 tribes”: Israel was made up of 12 tribes. The letter speaks to the Christians as part of God’s people. “The Dispersion” means: living scattered in other lands, outside the land of Israel.
> Verse 2: The WEB says “temptations.” Here the Greek word means above all trials and hard times.
> Verses 2–4: Hard times are not nice. But they can make us stronger. “Joy” doesn’t mean that you have to think suffering is good.
> Verse 5: God gives gladly and doesn’t blame us when we ask him for help.
> Verses 6–8: This is about people who don’t want to decide whether to trust God. “Double-minded” means: torn between two ways.
> Important: Doubts and questions are part of faith. Many people in the Bible doubted too. God is patient with us.

## Poor and rich (Verses 9–11)

[9] Let the brother in humble circumstances
boast in his high position,
[10] and the rich in his being made low.
For like a flower in the grass he will pass away.
[11] For the sun rises with the scorching wind
and withers the grass.
Its flower falls,
and the beauty of its appearance perishes.
So the rich man will also fade away in his pursuits.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: Before God the poor are not worth less. They have a “high position.” And riches don’t last.
> Verses 10–11: The picture of the grass and the flower comes from Isaiah 40:6–8.

---

## Temptation does not come from God (Verses 12–18)

[12] Blessed is the person
who endures temptation.
For when he has been approved,
he will receive the crown of life.
The Lord promised it to those
who love him.
[13] Let no one say
when he is tempted,
“I am tempted by God.”
For God can’t be tempted by evil,
and he himself tempts no one.
[14] But each one is tempted
when he is drawn away and enticed
by his own desire.
[15] Then when the desire has become pregnant,
it gives birth to sin.
And when sin is full grown,
it produces death.
[16] Don’t be deceived,
my beloved brothers.
[17] Every good gift
and every perfect gift
is from above,
from the Father of lights.
With him there is no change
and no shadow cast by turning.
[18] By his own will he gave birth to us by the word of truth,
so that we would be a kind of first fruits of his creatures.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: God doesn’t want us to do evil. He tempts no one.
> Verses 14–15: Evil deeds often start small, with a wish. If you give in to it, it can grow.
> Verse 17: All good comes from God. “Father of lights”: God made the sun, the moon and the stars. But unlike them he doesn’t change.
> Verse 18: “First fruits”: The first fruit of the harvest belonged to God. Christians are like a beginning of the new creation.

## Not only hearing, but doing (Verses 19–27)

[19] So,
my beloved brothers:
Let every person be quick to hear,
slow to speak
and slow to anger.
[20] For human anger doesn’t produce the righteousness of God.
[21] So put away all filthiness
and all the wickedness that overflows.
Receive with humility the word
that is planted in you
and that is able to save your souls.
[22] But be doers of the word,
not only hearers.
Otherwise you deceive yourselves.
[23] For if anyone only hears the word
but doesn’t do it,
he is like a man
who looks at his face in a mirror.
[24] He looks at himself,
goes away
and right away forgets
what he looked like.
[25] But whoever looks into the perfect law of freedom
and stays with it,
who is not a forgetful hearer
but a doer,
will be blessed in what he does.
[26] If anyone thinks
that he is religious,
but doesn’t keep his tongue in check,
he deceives his own heart.
His religion is worthless.
[27] Pure and spotless religion before God,
the Father,
is this:
to visit orphans and widows in their need
and to keep oneself unstained by the world.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Good advice for every day: first listen, then speak. And don’t get angry quickly. Verse 20: Righteousness means: living right before God.
> Verses 22–24: The picture of the mirror: Whoever hears God’s word but changes nothing is like someone who sees himself in a mirror and then forgets what he looks like.
> Verse 25: “The perfect law of freedom”: God’s teaching sets us free. It is not a prison, but a way to a good life. The psalms see it this way too (for example Psalm 119).
> Verse 27: True faith shows itself in how we treat the weakest. At that time these were above all orphans and widows.
> For today: These are all people who have no support: people who are alone, children without a family, refugees, the poor, the lonely.
