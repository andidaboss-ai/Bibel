# James – Chapter 2
### Faith shows itself in deeds

---

## Don’t favor anyone (Verses 1–13)

[1] My brothers,
don’t hold the faith of our glorious Lord Jesus Christ
while favoring some people over others.
[2] Imagine:
A man with a gold ring
and in fine clothing comes into your synagogue.
And a poor man in dirty clothing also comes in.
[3] And you pay special attention to the one in the fine clothing
and say,
“Sit here in a good place.”
But to the poor man you say,
“Stand there,”
or,
“Sit by my footstool.”
[4] Haven’t you then made distinctions among yourselves
and become judges with evil thoughts?
[5] Listen,
my beloved brothers:
Didn’t God choose those
who are poor in this world
to be rich in faith
and to inherit the Kingdom
that he promised to those
who love him?
[6] But you have dishonored the poor man.
Isn’t it the rich
who oppress you
and drag you before the courts?
[7] Don’t they blaspheme the honorable name
by which you are called?
[8] However, if you fulfill the royal law,
as the Scripture says,
“You shall love your neighbor as yourself,”
you do well.
[9] But if you favor some people over others,
you commit sin.
And the law convicts you as wrongdoers.
[10] For whoever keeps the whole law,
but stumbles in one point,
has become guilty of all.
[11] For he who said,
“Do not commit adultery,”
also said,
“Do not commit murder.”
Now if you do not commit adultery,
but murder,
you have become a breaker of the law.
[12] So speak and so act
as people
who will be judged by the law of freedom.
[13] For judgment is without mercy to him
who has shown no mercy.
Mercy triumphs over judgment.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: The WEB says “synagogue.” The word simply means “gathering.” The first Christians of Jewish background often called their gatherings that.
> Verses 1–4: No one in the church is to be favored or treated worse because of money or clothing. All are equal before God.
> Verse 5: God is on the side of the poor. They are just as precious to him as everyone else.
> Verse 8 quotes Leviticus 19:18: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.” Jesus called this commandment one of the two most important commandments (Mark 12:31).
> Verse 11 quotes the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:13–14).
> Verse 13: “Mercy triumphs over judgment”: A great saying. Whoever is merciful may hope for God’s mercy themselves.
> For today: Today too people are treated worse because of poverty, background or appearance. That is not to happen in the church.

## Faith without deeds is dead (Verses 14–26)

[14] What good is it,
my brothers,
if someone says
he has faith,
but he has no deeds?
Can that faith save him?
[15] If a brother or a sister
has nothing to wear
and not enough food for the day,
[16] and one of you says to them,
“Go in peace,
be warmed and filled!”
but you don’t give them
what the body needs,
what good is that?
[17] In the same way faith,
if it has no deeds,
is dead in itself.
[18] Yes,
someone will say,
“You have faith,
and I have deeds.”
Show me your faith without deeds,
and I will show you my faith by my deeds.
[19] You believe
that God is one.
You do well.
The demons also believe that
and tremble.
[20] But do you want to know,
you foolish person,
that faith without deeds is dead?
[21] Wasn’t Abraham
our father
made righteous by deeds
when he offered up his son Isaac on the altar?
[22] You see:
Faith worked together with his deeds.
And by the deeds faith was made perfect.
[23] So the Scripture was fulfilled
which says,
“Abraham believed God,
and it was counted to him as righteousness.”
And he was called the friend of God.
[24] So you see:
A person is made righteous by deeds,
and not only by faith.
[25] In the same way, wasn’t Rahab
the prostitute
also made righteous by deeds
when she received the messengers
and sent them out another way?
[26] For as the body without the spirit is dead,
so faith without deeds is also dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–16: A very clear example: Nice words don’t help a hungry person. He needs food and clothing.
> Verse 19: “God is one”: This is the confession of faith of Israel, the *Shema Israel* (Deuteronomy 6:4). Jews pray it every day to this day. James says: The confession is right. But it must also be visible in life.
> Verse 23 quotes Genesis 15:6. Abraham is called “friend of God” in Isaiah 41:8.
> Verse 24: Paul writes: A person is made righteous by faith, not by works of the law (Romans 3:28). That sounds like a contradiction. But the two mean different things: Paul says that we can’t earn salvation. James says that true faith always leads to good deeds. Both belong together.
> Verse 25: Rahab is again an example (compare Hebrews 11:31). She protected Israel’s messengers.
> Verse 26: Faith and deeds belong together like body and spirit.
