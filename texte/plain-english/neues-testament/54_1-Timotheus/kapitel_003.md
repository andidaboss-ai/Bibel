# 1 Timothy – Chapter 3
### Those who carry responsibility in the church

---

## The overseers of the church (Verses 1–7)

[1] This is a trustworthy saying:
Someone who wants to be an overseer in the church
desires a good work.
[2] So the overseer must be without reproach,
the husband of one wife,
sober,
sensible,
respectable,
hospitable
and good at teaching.
[3] He must not be a drinker,
not violent,
not greedy for money,
but gentle,
not quarrelsome
and not covetous.
[4] He must manage his own household well
and guide his children to obey with all respect.
[5] For if someone doesn’t know how to manage his own household,
how could he take care of God’s assembly?
[6] He must not be new in the faith.
Otherwise he might become puffed up
and fall into the same condemnation as the devil.
[7] He must also have a good reputation with those outside the church.
Otherwise he will fall into disgrace
and into the snare of the devil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Overseer means: a leader of the church.
> Hospitable means: welcoming guests gladly.
> Verse 1: “Overseer” (Greek *episkopos*): Later the word *bishop* came from this word. At that time it probably meant a leader of a local church.
> Verses 2–7: The list shows: For leadership, character matters most, not power or wealth. Leaders are to be gentle, honest, not violent and not greedy for money.
> Verse 2: “The husband of one wife” is understood in different ways: Some think he should be married only once. Others think he should be faithful to his wife and not have several wives. In the Catholic Church priests today live unmarried. In Orthodox and Protestant churches pastors may be married.
> Verse 4: The leader is to raise his children with respect. That does not mean with violence. Violence against children is never right.
> Very important: These rules are meant to protect the church. Whoever has responsibility in the church must never misuse it. If you have experienced abuse in a church, you can get help. In the US, call the RAINN hotline at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, call NAPAC at 0808 801 0331.

## The servants of the church (Verses 8–13)

[8] In the same way, the servants must be respectable,
not double-tongued,
not addicted to much wine
and not greedy for money.
[9] They must hold the mystery of the faith
with a pure conscience.
[10] They too must first be tested.
Then let them serve
if they are blameless.
[11] Their wives in the same way must be respectable,
not slanderers,
sober
and faithful in all things.
[12] The servants must be husbands of one wife
and manage their children and their own households well.
[13] For those who have served well
gain a good standing
and great boldness in the faith in Christ Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> Double-tongued means: saying one thing to one person and something else to another.
> Verse 8: “Servants” (Greek *diakonos*): Later the word *deacon* came from this. Deacons took care of the poor and the sick, for example.
> Verse 11: The WEB says “Their wives.” But the Greek word can also simply mean *women*. Then this would be about women who were themselves servants of the church. Phoebe too is called a servant of the church (Romans 16:1). Interpreters understand the verse in different ways.
> Verse 13: Whoever serves others gains respect and courage in faith. Serving is no small task.

---

## The mystery of faith (Verses 14–16)

[14] I am writing these things to you,
although I hope
to come to you soon.
[15] But if I am delayed,
you should know
how people ought to behave in God’s house.
That is the assembly of the living God,
the pillar and foundation of the truth.
[16] Without question,
the mystery of godliness is great:
God was revealed in the flesh,
justified in the spirit,
seen by angels,
preached among the nations,
believed on in the world,
received up in glory.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The church is like a house and a family of God. It holds up the truth the way a pillar holds up a roof.
> Verse 16 is probably an old song of the first Christians about Jesus. In six short lines it tells his path: He became human, God confirmed him, angels saw him, he was preached and believed, and he was taken up into heaven.
> The WEB says “God was revealed in the flesh.” Many old manuscripts say *He who was revealed in the flesh*. In both cases Jesus Christ is meant.
