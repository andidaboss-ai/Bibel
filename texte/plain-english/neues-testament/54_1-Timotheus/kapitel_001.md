# 1 Timothy – Chapter 1
### Christ came to save sinners

---

## Before we begin: The first letter to Timothy

Timothy was a close coworker of Paul. His mother was Jewish and a Christian, his father a Greek (Acts 16:1). Paul calls him his *true child in faith*.
The letter calls itself a letter from Paul to Timothy. Timothy has stayed in Ephesus to lead the church there. The letter gives him advice: how to deal with false teachings, how worship is celebrated and which people should carry responsibility in the church.
The two letters to Timothy and the letter to Titus are together called the *Pastoral Letters*, that is, letters for shepherds of the church. Many scholars today think that they were written later by a student of Paul, in Paul’s spirit. Others consider Paul himself to be the author.
**An important note:** In this letter there are rules about women, slaves and other groups. They reflect the society of that time. Christians and churches understand them in different ways today. We explain this fairly and honestly in the boxes.
The first letter to Timothy has 6 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
an apostle of Jesus Christ
by the command of God
our Savior
and of Christ Jesus
our hope,
[2] writes to Timothy,
his true child in faith:
Grace,
mercy
and peace
from God the Father
and from Christ Jesus our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “God our Savior”: This name for God appears often in the Pastoral Letters.
> Verse 2: “True child in faith”: Timothy is not Paul’s own son. But Paul led him to faith and guides him like a father.

---

## Warning against false teachings (Verses 3–11)

[3] I urged you
to stay at Ephesus
when I went on to Macedonia.
You were to command certain people
not to teach a different doctrine
[4] and not to pay attention to myths
and endless family trees.
These cause disputes
rather than serving God’s plan,
which is in faith.
[5] But the goal of this command is love
from a pure heart,
a good conscience
and sincere faith.
[6] Some have wandered away from these things
and turned to empty talk.
[7] They want to be teachers of the law.
But they understand neither
what they are saying
nor the things
they so confidently claim.
[8] But we know:
The law is good
if a person uses it properly.
[9] You must know this:
The law is not made for a righteous person,
but for the lawless and disobedient,
for the ungodly and sinners,
for the unholy and corrupt,
for those
who kill their fathers or their mothers,
for murderers,
[10] for the sexually immoral,
for homosexuals,
for slave-traders,
for liars,
for perjurers,
and for anything else
that is contrary to sound doctrine.
[11] This is according to the Good News of the glory of the blessed God,
which was entrusted to me.

*doctrine* = teaching
*perjurers* = people who lie under oath

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Myths and endless family trees”: Some teachers busied themselves with speculation and long lists of ancestors. This led to disputes, but not to a better life.
> Verse 5 is the heart of it: The goal of all teaching is love.
> Verse 8: “The law is good”: The law that God gave Israel is good and holy (compare Romans 7:12). This is about people who misuse it.
> Verses 9–10: This list roughly follows the Ten Commandments: honoring parents, not killing, not committing adultery, not stealing (“slave-traders” stole people), not giving false testimony.
> Verse 10: The WEB translates a difficult Greek word as “homosexuals.” It is the same word as in 1 Corinthians 6:9 (*arsenokoitai*). It means roughly: men who sleep with men. The word *homosexual* has only existed since the 19th century. That is why other Bibles translate it in very different ways.
> Churches understand this passage in different ways today. Some see in it a lasting command against same-sex sexuality. Others say: What was meant was exploitation and abuse as it existed at that time, for example in connection with human trafficking and slavery (the next word in the list), not loving, faithful partnerships as we know them today.
> Very important: This text gives no one the right to despise people, to insult them or to use violence against them. Every human being has the same dignity before God. If you experience exclusion or violence because of your sexual orientation, you can get help. Young LGBTQ people can contact The Trevor Project. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verse 10: “Slave-traders”: Whoever kidnaps and sells people commits a grave injustice. That is also true today of human trafficking and forced labor.

## God’s grace for Paul (Verses 12–17)

[12] I thank him
who gave me strength:
Christ Jesus our Lord.
For he counted me faithful
and appointed me to his service,
[13] although I used to be a blasphemer,
a persecutor
and a violent man.
But I obtained mercy
because I did it in ignorance,
in unbelief.
[14] The grace of our Lord overflowed,
together with the faith and love
that are in Christ Jesus.
[15] The saying is trustworthy
and deserves
to be accepted by all:
Christ Jesus came into the world
to save sinners.
And of them I am the worst.
[16] But for this very reason I obtained mercy:
In me first
Jesus Christ wanted to show all his patience,
as an example for all
who were going to believe in him
and receive eternal life.
[17] Now to the King eternal,
immortal,
invisible,
the only God,
who alone is wise,
be honor and glory
forever and ever.
Amen.

*blasphemer* = someone who mocks or insults God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Paul once persecuted Christians (Acts 8:3 and 9:1–2). He doesn’t hide this.
> Verse 15 is one of the most important sentences of the letter: Jesus came to save sinners. No one is too bad for God’s grace.
> Verse 16: If God forgave even a persecutor like Paul, then every person can have hope.
> Verse 17: A short song of praise to God. He is eternal, immortal and invisible.

## The good fight (Verses 18–20)

[18] I give you this instruction,
my child Timothy,
according to the prophecies
that were spoken about you before.
With their help you are to fight the good fight.
[19] Hold on to faith and a good conscience.
Some have pushed away a good conscience
and have made a shipwreck of their faith.
[20] Among them are Hymenaeus and Alexander.
I handed them over to Satan,
so that they may learn
not to blaspheme anymore.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: “The good fight” is a picture. It means standing up for the faith, not violence against people.
> Verse 19: “A shipwreck of their faith”: Whoever no longer takes their conscience seriously can lose their faith like a ship that sinks.
> Verse 20: Being handed over to Satan probably means: being excluded from the church (compare 1 Corinthians 5:5). The goal was not punishment, but that they would turn back and learn. The door to return stays open.
