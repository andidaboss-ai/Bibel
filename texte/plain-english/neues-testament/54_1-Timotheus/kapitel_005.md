# 1 Timothy – Chapter 5
### Caring for one another in the church

---

## How to treat one another (Verses 1–2)

[1] Don’t speak harshly to an older man,
but encourage him as a father.
Treat the younger men as brothers,
[2] the older women as mothers,
the younger women as sisters,
in all purity.

> **What does this mean?**
> The church is like a family. Everyone is to treat one another with respect, like fathers, mothers, brothers and sisters.
> “In all purity”: In dealing with younger women everything is to be proper. No one may take advantage of their position.

---

## Caring for widows (Verses 3–16)

[3] Honor widows
who are truly widows.
[4] But if a widow has children or grandchildren,
they should first learn
to show godliness toward their own family
and to repay their parents.
For this pleases God.
[5] A widow
who is truly a widow and all alone
has set her hope on God.
She continues in requests and prayers night and day.
[6] But she
who lives only for pleasure
is dead
while she lives.
[7] Command these things too,
so that they may be without reproach.
[8] But if anyone doesn’t provide for his relatives,
and especially for his own household,
he has denied the faith.
He is worse than an unbeliever.
[9] Let no one be put on the list of widows
who is under sixty years old,
and she must have been the wife of one man.
[10] She must be known for good works:
if she has brought up children,
if she has welcomed strangers,
if she has washed the saints’ feet,
if she has helped people in need
and if she has devoted herself to every good work.
[11] But refuse younger widows.
For when their desires draw them away from Christ,
they want to marry.
[12] Then they bring judgment on themselves,
because they have broken their first pledge.
[13] Besides, they learn
to be idle
and go about from house to house.
And not only idle,
but they are also gossips and busybodies,
meddling in other people’s affairs
and saying
things they ought not.
[14] So I want
the younger widows to marry,
have children,
manage the household
and give the opponent no reason for insults.
[15] For some have already turned away
and follow Satan.
[16] If any man or woman who believes has widows in the family,
they should help them.
The church should not be burdened,
so that it can help those
who are truly widows.

> **What does this mean?**
> Pledge means: a solemn promise.
> Verse 3: Widows had a very hard life at that time. There was no pension and no social security. Without a husband or children they were often poor. The church took care of them. That was an early social welfare system.
> Verses 4 and 8: Children and grandchildren are to take care of their old parents. That is a command of love and recalls the commandment *Honor your father and mother*.
> Verses 9–10: Apparently there was a “list of widows.” These widows were provided for by the church and probably also had tasks: praying, welcoming guests, helping the sick.
> Verses 11–15: These verses reflect the ideas of that time. Younger widows who were put on the list probably promised not to marry again. The letter thinks: That is too hard for younger women. So they should rather marry again.
> Important: Today women have other possibilities. Whether a woman marries, has children or lives alone is her own decision. A woman is not worth less if she lives alone or has no children.
> Verse 13: The judgment on “gossips” applies to men just as much as to women.
> For today: Christians are to care for old, poor and lonely people, in the family and in the church.

## The elders of the church (Verses 17–25)

[17] The elders
who lead well
should be counted worthy of double honor,
especially those
who work hard at preaching and teaching.
[18] For the Scripture says:
“You shall not muzzle the ox
when it treads out the grain.”
And:
“The laborer
is worthy of his wages.”
[19] Don’t accept an accusation against an elder,
except on the word of two or three witnesses.
[20] Those who sin
you are to correct in front of everyone,
so that the others may also be afraid.
[21] I solemnly command you before God,
before the Lord Jesus Christ
and before the chosen angels:
Keep these things without prejudice.
Do nothing out of partiality.
[22] Don’t lay hands on anyone too quickly.
Don’t share in other people’s sins.
Keep yourself pure.
[23] Don’t drink only water any longer,
but use a little wine,
for your stomach’s sake
and because you are often sick.
[24] Some people’s sins are obvious.
They go ahead of them to judgment.
With others they only come to light later.
[25] In the same way, good works are also obvious.
And those
that are not
can’t stay hidden either.

> **What does this mean?**
> Muzzle means: to tie an animal’s mouth shut.
> Treads out the grain means: walks on grain to separate the kernels from the stalks.
> Verse 17: “Double honor” probably also means pay. Whoever works for the church should be able to live from it.
> Verse 18: The first sentence quotes Deuteronomy 25:4 (compare also 1 Corinthians 9:9). The second sentence is a saying of Jesus (Luke 10:7).
> Verse 19: Two or three witnesses: This rule comes from Deuteronomy 19:15. It protects against false accusations.
> Very important: This rule must not lead to abuse by leaders being covered up. Victims of abuse often have no witnesses. What they say must be taken seriously. Verses 20–21 say clearly: Whoever sins is held accountable, without partiality, even if he is a leader. You can get help. In the US, call the RAINN hotline at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, call NAPAC at 0808 801 0331.
> Verse 22: Laying on hands here means: appointing someone to an office. That should not happen too quickly.
> Verse 23: A personal piece of advice to Timothy. At that time wine was also considered a medicine, and the water was often not clean. For today: Whoever has problems with alcohol should rather do without it completely. Help is available at addiction counseling services. In the US, call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. In the UK, call Drinkline at 0300 123 1110.
> Verses 24–25: Not everything is visible right away. But God sees everything, the bad and the good.
