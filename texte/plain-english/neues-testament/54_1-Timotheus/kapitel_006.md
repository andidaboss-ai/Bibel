# 1 Timothy – Chapter 6
### Being content and doing good

---

## A word to the slaves (Verses 1–2)

[1] All
who are slaves under the yoke
are to count their own masters worthy of all honor,
so that the name of God and the teaching are not insulted.
[2] Those who have believing masters
must not look down on them
because they are brothers.
But rather they are to serve them all the more,
because those
who benefit from their service
are believers and beloved.
Teach and urge these things.

*yoke* = a wooden bar put on animals so they can pull loads; here a picture for slavery

> **What does this mean?**
> At the time of the letter there were very many slaves in the Roman Empire. There were slaves and masters in the churches too.
> Verse 1: The letter wanted to avoid the young church being seen as a danger to public order and being persecuted.
> Verse 2: Even when slave and master were both Christians and so “brothers,” slavery continued. That is hard to understand. But the word “brothers” already showed: Before God they are equal.
> Very important: Slavery is a grave injustice. These verses were later misused to justify slavery. That was wrong. Many Christians fought against slavery. Today there is still human trafficking and forced labor. Christians should stand up against it. If you know about trafficking or forced labor, you can get help. In the US, call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888. In the UK, call the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.

## True godliness and the danger of money (Verses 3–10)

[3] If anyone teaches a different doctrine
and doesn’t hold to sound words,
the words of our Lord Jesus Christ,
and to the teaching
that fits with godliness,
[4] he is conceited
and understands nothing.
He is obsessed with arguments and word battles.
From these come envy,
strife,
insults,
evil suspicions
[5] and constant friction among people
whose minds are corrupt
and who lack the truth.
They think
that godliness is a means
of making a profit.
Keep away from such people.
[6] But godliness with contentment
is great gain.
[7] For we brought nothing into the world,
and we certainly can’t carry anything out.
[8] If we have food and clothing,
we will be content with that.
[9] But those
who are determined to be rich
fall into temptation
and a snare
and many foolish and harmful desires.
These plunge people into ruin
and destruction.
[10] For the love of money
is a root of all kinds of evil.
Some, in their greed for it,
have been led astray from the faith.
And they have pierced themselves with many sorrows.

*contentment* = being satisfied with what you have
*snare* = a trap

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: There were teachers who wanted to make money from the faith. That exists today too. Whoever promises that God will make you rich if you donate money is misusing the faith.
> Verses 6–8: Contentment is a great treasure. Whoever has enough to live on is rich.
> Verse 7: A thought that recalls Job 1:21: We come into the world with nothing and leave with nothing.
> Verse 10: Money itself is not evil, but the love of money, that is, greed.
> Important: These verses are no reproach to people in poverty who want more so that they can live well. Whoever doesn’t have enough to live on may seek help. And Christians are to help make sure that all people have enough.

## Fight the good fight (Verses 11–16)

[11] But you,
man of God,
flee these things.
Pursue righteousness,
godliness,
faith,
love,
perseverance
and gentleness.
[12] Fight the good fight of faith.
Take hold of the eternal life
to which you were called.
For this you made the good confession before many witnesses.
[13] I command you before God,
who gives life to all things,
and before Christ Jesus,
who made the good confession before Pontius Pilate:
[14] Keep the commandment
without spot and without blame
until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[15] He will show this at the right time,
he who is the blessed and only Ruler,
the King of kings
and Lord of lords.
[16] He alone is immortal.
He lives in light
that no one can approach.
No human being has seen him,
and no one can see him.
To him be honor and eternal power.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Gentleness is at the end of the list. The “fight” of faith is fought with gentleness, not with violence.
> Verse 12: “Fight the good fight of faith” is a picture from sports. It means standing up for the faith. The “good confession” probably means the confession of faith at baptism.
> Verse 13: Jesus stood by his mission before Pilate (John 18:37). He is the example for all who confess their faith.
> Verses 15–16: “The King of kings”: No emperor and no ruler stands above God. At that time the Roman emperor let himself be worshiped as a god. The letter says: This honor belongs to God alone.

## A word to the rich (Verses 17–19)

[17] Command those who are rich in this world:
They must not be arrogant.
They must not set their hope on uncertain riches,
but on the living God.
He richly provides us with everything to enjoy.
[18] They are to do good
and be rich in good works.
They are to give gladly
and be ready to share.
[19] In this way they lay up for themselves a good foundation
for the future,
so that they may take hold of eternal life.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Wealth is uncertain. It can be lost quickly. Only God is certain.
> Verse 17: “Everything to enjoy”: God is glad to give us the good things of life. Enjoying them is no sin.
> Verse 18: Whoever has much also has much responsibility. The rich are to share.
> For today: This also applies to rich countries. Justice between rich and poor in the world is a Christian concern.

---

## Conclusion (Verses 20–21)

[20] Timothy,
guard
what has been entrusted to you.
Turn away from the empty chatter
and the arguments of what
is falsely called “knowledge.”
[21] Some have professed it
and so have wandered from the faith.
Grace be with you.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: “What has been entrusted to you”: the Good News of Jesus. Timothy is to guard it like a precious treasure.
> “Knowledge” (Greek *gnosis*): Some teachers claimed to have special, secret knowledge. Later a movement grew out of this that is called *Gnosticism*.

## Looking back: What have we read in the first letter to Timothy?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Christ saves sinners:** Jesus came into the world to save sinners. Paul too, the former persecutor, obtained mercy (chapter 1).
> **God wants all people to be saved:** That is why Christians are to pray for all people (chapter 2).
> **Difficult verses about women:** Churches understand chapter 2 in very different ways. Man and woman are of equal worth before God.
> **Good leaders:** Whoever carries responsibility needs good character. Power must not be misused (chapters 3 and 5).
> **God’s creation is good:** Food, the body and marriage are gifts from God (chapter 4).
> **Caring for one another:** for widows, the elderly and those in need (chapter 5).
> **Contentment instead of greed:** The love of money leads to ruin. The rich are to share (chapter 6).
> **Important note:** The rules about slaves reflect the time back then. Slavery is a grave injustice.
> **For today:** Faith shows itself in everyday life: in honesty, care, modesty and love.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the second letter to Timothy. It is like a farewell letter from Paul in prison.
