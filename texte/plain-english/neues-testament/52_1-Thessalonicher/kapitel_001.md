# 1 Thessalonians – Chapter 1
### An example for everyone

---

## Before we begin: The first letter to the Thessalonians

Thessalonica is today’s Thessaloniki in Greece. At that time it was a large port city and the capital of the Roman province of Macedonia.
Paul came to Thessalonica with Silas and Timothy and founded a church there (Acts 17:1–9). After a short time he had to leave the city because of unrest.
Paul is worried about the young church. He sends Timothy to it. When Timothy brings good news, Paul writes this letter, probably from Corinth.
The letter is probably the oldest letter of Paul that we have, and so one of the oldest writings of the New Testament. It was written around AD 50.
An important theme is hope: What about the Christians who have already died? Paul comforts the church with the hope that Jesus will come again.
**Important note:** In chapter 2 there are very harsh words about *the Jews*. In the box we explain why these verses must never be used against Jews.
The first letter to the Thessalonians has 5 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verse 1)

[1] Paul,
Silvanus
and Timothy
write to the church of the Thessalonians
in God the Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ:
Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Silvanus is the same man who is called *Silas* in Acts. Silas is the short form. He and Timothy were there when the church was founded.

---

## Thanks for the church (Verses 2–10)

[2] We always give thanks to God for all of you
when we remember you in our prayers.
[3] Without ceasing we remember before our God and Father
your work of faith,
your labor of love
and your perseverance of hope
in our Lord Jesus Christ.
[4] Brothers
loved by God:
We know
that you are chosen.
[5] For our Good News came to you
not in word only,
but also in power,
in the Holy Spirit
and with full certainty.
You know
how we lived among you
for your sake.
[6] And you became imitators of us
and of the Lord.
You received the word in much affliction,
with joy of the Holy Spirit.
[7] So you became an example
to all who believe in Macedonia and in Achaia.
[8] For from you the word of the Lord has spread,
not only in Macedonia and Achaia.
In every place people have heard
about your faith toward God.
We don’t need to say anything more about it.
[9] For they themselves report about us
how you welcomed us
and how you turned to God from idols
to serve a living and true God
[10] and to wait for his Son from heaven.
God raised him from the dead:
Jesus,
who rescues us from the anger to come.

> **What does this mean?**
> Affliction means: great trouble and suffering.
> Idols means: images of false gods.
> Verse 3: Faith, love and hope: For Paul these three always belong together (compare 1 Corinthians 13:13). Here they are named together for the first time. Faith shows itself in deeds, love takes effort, hope gives perseverance.
> Verse 5: The Good News is not only a teaching. It has power and changes people.
> Verse 6: “In much affliction”: The Christians in Thessalonica had opponents from the beginning and faced hostility (Acts 17:5–9). Still they had joy.
> Verse 7: Achaia was the Roman province in the south of Greece, with Corinth as its capital.
> Verse 9: Most Christians in Thessalonica had worshiped other gods before. They turned to the God of Israel, “a living and true God.”
> Verse 10: The first Christians strongly hoped that Jesus would come again soon. “The anger to come” means God’s judgment on evil. Jesus rescues from it. Christians don’t need to be afraid of the judgment.
