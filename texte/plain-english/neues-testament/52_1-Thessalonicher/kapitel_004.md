# 1 Thessalonians – Chapter 4
### Hope for those who have died

---

## A life that pleases God (Verses 1–8)

[1] Finally,
brothers:
We beg and urge you in the Lord Jesus.
You learned from us
how you ought to live
to please God.
And that is how you are living.
Keep growing in it more and more.
[2] For you know
what instructions we gave you through the Lord Jesus.
[3] For this is the will of God:
that you live a holy life.
Keep away from sexual immorality.
[4] Each one of you should know
how to control his own body
in holiness and honor,
[5] not in the passion of lust,
like the nations
who don’t know God.
[6] No one should take advantage of
a brother or sister in this matter
or wrong them.
For the Lord punishes all these things.
We already told you and warned you about this before.
[7] For God did not call us to uncleanness,
but to a holy life.
[8] So whoever rejects this
doesn’t reject a human being,
but God.
He has also given you his Holy Spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 3–5: In the society of that time it was often normal for men to have sex with slave women, prostitutes or other women outside marriage. Paul says: Christians live differently.
> Verse 4: The Greek word can mean *body*, but also *vessel*. Some interpreters think it means your own wife. The WEB translates “body.”
> Verse 6 is very important: Sexuality must never be used to take advantage of others or to wrong them. God stands on the side of those who are wronged.
> For today: Sexual violence and abuse are a grave injustice. Whoever has experienced this is not to blame. You can get help. In the US, call the RAINN hotline at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, call NAPAC at 0808 801 0331 or the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247.

## Love one another and work quietly (Verses 9–12)

[9] About love for the brothers and sisters
we don’t need to write to you.
For you yourselves are taught by God
to love one another.
[10] And you are already doing this
toward all the brothers in all Macedonia.
But we urge you,
brothers:
Keep growing in it more and more.
[11] Make it your ambition
to lead a quiet life,
to mind your own business
and to work with your own hands,
just as we told you.
[12] In this way you are to live properly
toward those
who are outside the church
and not depend on anyone.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “Taught by God”: God himself has put love for one another into the hearts of the Christians.
> Verse 11: Some in the church had probably stopped working, perhaps because they thought Jesus would come again soon anyway. Paul says: Go on living calmly and work.
> Verse 12: An orderly life is also a good witness to people outside the church.
> Important: This is no reproach to people who can’t work because they are sick, old or unemployed. The church is to help them.

## Hope for those who have died (Verses 13–18)

[13] But we don’t want you to be uninformed,
brothers,
about those
who have fallen asleep,
so that you don’t grieve
like the rest,
who have no hope.
[14] For we believe
that Jesus died and rose again.
In the same way God will bring with him those
who have fallen asleep in Jesus.
[15] For this we tell you by the word of the Lord:
We
who are alive,
who are left until the coming of the Lord,
will in no way go before those
who have fallen asleep.
[16] For the Lord himself will come down from heaven,
with a shout,
with the voice of the archangel
and with God’s trumpet.
And first the dead
in Christ will rise.
[17] Then we
who are alive and are left
will be caught up together with them in the clouds
to meet the Lord in the air.
And so we will be with the Lord forever.
[18] So comfort one another with these words.

*archangel* = a leading angel

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Some Christians in Thessalonica had died. The others were afraid: Have those who died now missed the return of Jesus? Paul comforts them.
> “Fallen asleep” is a gentle way of saying *died*. It expresses hope: Whoever sleeps will wake up again.
> Important: Paul doesn’t say: *You should not grieve.* He says: “don’t grieve like the rest, who have no hope.” Grief is allowed and important. Jesus too wept when his friend Lazarus had died (John 11:35). If you need support in your grief, you can find it, for example, with a grief counselor. If your grief becomes too heavy, in the US call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 14: The reason for hope is the resurrection of Jesus.
> Verse 15: At that time Paul expected that he would still live to see the return of Jesus (“we who are alive”).
> Verses 16–17: Paul uses pictures: shout, trumpet and clouds. This is how people at that time described a king coming into a city. The inhabitants go out to meet him, to welcome him and to enter the city with him.
> Christians understand verse 17 in different ways. Some expect a *rapture* of believers before a great time of trouble at the end of time. Most churches understand the verse like this: When Jesus comes again, everyone, the living and the dead, will be united with him forever.
> Verse 18: The purpose of these verses is comfort, not fear.
