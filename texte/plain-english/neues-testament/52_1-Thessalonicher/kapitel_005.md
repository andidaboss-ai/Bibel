# 1 Thessalonians – Chapter 5
### Children of light

---

## The day of the Lord (Verses 1–11)

[1] About the times and the seasons,
brothers,
we don’t need to write anything to you.
[2] For you yourselves know very well:
The day of the Lord comes
like a thief in the night.
[3] When people are saying,
“Peace and safety,”
then sudden destruction will come on them,
like birth pains on a pregnant woman.
And they will in no way escape.
[4] But you,
brothers,
are not in darkness,
so that the day could surprise you like a thief.
[5] You are all children of light
and children of the day.
We don’t belong to the night
nor to darkness.
[6] So let’s not sleep
like the rest do,
but let’s stay awake and be sober.
[7] For those who sleep
sleep in the night.
And those who get drunk
get drunk in the night.
[8] But we belong to the day.
So let’s be sober.
Let’s put on the breastplate of faith and love,
and for a helmet, the hope of salvation.
[9] For God didn’t appoint us to anger,
but to receive salvation
through our Lord Jesus Christ.
[10] He died for us,
so that we should live together with him,
whether we wake or sleep.
[11] So encourage one another
and build each other up,
just as you are already doing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sober means: clear-headed, not drunk.
> A breastplate is a piece of armor that protects the chest.
> Verses 1–2: No one knows when Jesus will come again. So you should not calculate dates either. Jesus himself used the picture of the thief (Matthew 24:43–44).
> Verse 3: “Peace and safety” was a slogan of the Roman Empire. Paul says: True safety is not given by the emperor, but by God.
> Verses 5–8: Christians are to live awake and sober, as if it were already day. That means: attentive, sensible, not indifferent.
> Verse 8: As in Ephesians 6, the *armor* is a picture: Faith, love and hope protect us. It is no call to violence.
> Verse 10: “Whether we wake or sleep” here means: whether we are alive or have died. No one is separated from Christ, not even by death.
> Verse 9: This is the heart of it: God wants to save, not to condemn. This section is not meant to make you afraid, but to give you courage.

## How Christians live together (Verses 12–22)

[12] But we ask you,
brothers:
Respect those
who labor among you,
who lead you in the Lord
and admonish you.
[13] Honor them very highly in love
because of their work.
Be at peace among yourselves.
[14] We urge you,
brothers:
Warn those
who live in a disorderly way.
Encourage the fainthearted.
Support the weak.
Be patient toward all.
[15] See to it
that no one returns evil for evil to anyone.
But always pursue what is good,
for one another and for all.
[16] Always rejoice.
[17] Pray without ceasing.
[18] In everything give thanks.
For this is the will of God
in Christ Jesus for you.
[19] Don’t quench the Spirit.
[20] Don’t despise prophecies.
[21] Test all things,
and hold firmly to what is good.
[22] Keep away from every kind of evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Fainthearted means: fearful, easily discouraged.
> Quench means: to put out, like a fire.
> Verses 12–13: The church already had people who carried responsibility. They deserve respect because they work hard. But they too must not misuse their power.
> Verse 14: Every person needs something different: Some need a clear word, others encouragement, others support. And everyone needs patience.
> Verse 15: Not returning evil for evil: Jesus taught this (Matthew 5:38–48). It applies “for all,” not only to Christians.
> Verses 16–18: Three short sentences: joy, prayer, thanks. “Pray without ceasing” doesn’t mean saying prayers all day long. It means: being connected with God in all of life.
> Verse 18: “In everything give thanks” doesn’t mean having to be thankful for every bad thing. It means: Even in hard times there is reason to give thanks, because God is with us.
> Verses 19–21: The Holy Spirit works in the church. You should not despise prophetic words, but test them. Not everything that someone says in God’s name is right.

## Blessing and farewell (Verses 23–28)

[23] May the God of peace himself
make you completely holy.
May your whole spirit,
soul and body
be kept
blameless
at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[24] He who calls you
is faithful.
He will also do it.
[25] Brothers,
pray for us too.
[26] Greet all the brothers with a holy kiss.
[27] I solemnly command you by the Lord:
Have this letter read to all the holy brothers.
[28] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ
be with you.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Spirit, soul and body: God wants to make the whole person whole, not only the soul. The body matters to God too.
> Verse 24: A word of comfort: We don’t have to manage everything. God is faithful and completes what he has begun.
> Verse 26: The “holy kiss” was a sign of greeting among Christians, like a hug or a handshake today.
> Verse 27: At that time many people could not read. That is why the letter was read aloud in worship. That way everyone could hear it.

## Looking back: What have we read in the first letter to the Thessalonians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The oldest letter of Paul:** We have probably read the oldest surviving letter of Paul, written around AD 50.
> **Faith, love and hope:** These three shape the young church (chapters 1 and 5).
> **An example:** The church in Thessalonica held on to the faith in spite of hostility and became an example for many (chapter 1).
> **How Paul worked:** Gentle like a mother, encouraging like a father, honest and unselfish (chapter 2).
> **A difficult passage:** The harsh words in 2:14–16 must never be used against Jews. Paul himself was a Jew. Antisemitism is sin.
> **Worry and joy:** Paul worries about the church and rejoices at the good news from Timothy (chapter 3).
> **Hope for those who have died:** Whoever has died in Christ is not lost. We may grieve, but with hope (chapter 4).
> **Children of light:** Living awake and sober, encouraging one another, not returning evil for evil, rejoicing, praying and giving thanks (chapter 5).
> **For today:** Death does not have the last word. That is why Christians can comfort one another.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the second letter to the Thessalonians. It deals once more with the return of Jesus.
