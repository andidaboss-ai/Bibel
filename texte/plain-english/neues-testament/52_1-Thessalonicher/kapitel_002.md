# 1 Thessalonians – Chapter 2
### How Paul worked in Thessalonica

---

## How we came to you (Verses 1–12)

[1] You yourselves know,
brothers:
Our visit to you was not in vain.
[2] Before that we had suffered in Philippi
and had been shamefully treated there.
You know that.
Still, trusting in our God, we grew bold.
We told you the Good News of God,
even though we faced much conflict.
[3] For our appeal doesn’t come from error,
nor from impure motives,
nor from deception.
[4] Rather, God has tested us
and entrusted us with the Good News.
That is why we speak this way:
not to please people,
but God,
who tests our hearts.
[5] For we never used words of flattery,
as you know.
And we didn’t secretly act out of greed.
God is our witness.
[6] We didn’t seek glory from people,
neither from you nor from others.
Yet as apostles of Christ
we could have claimed authority.
[7] But we were gentle among you,
like a nursing mother cherishes her own children.
[8] That is how much we longed for you.
We wanted to give you not only the Good News of God,
but also our own lives.
For you had become very dear to us.
[9] You remember,
brothers,
our labor and toil.
We worked night and day,
so that we would not be a burden to any of you.
That is how we preached the Good News of God to you.
[10] You are witnesses,
and God also,
how holy,
righteous
and blameless
we were toward you who believe.
[11] For you know:
As a father does his own children,
so we urged each one of you,
encouraged you
and pleaded with you
[12] to live in a way
worthy of God.
He calls you into his own Kingdom
and glory.

> **What does this mean?**
> Flattery means: praise that is not honest, given to win someone over.
> Verse 2: In Philippi Paul and Silas were beaten and thrown into prison (Acts 16:22–24).
> Verses 3–6: At that time many traveling preachers and speakers went through the cities to win money and fame. Paul says: We were not like that.
> Verse 7: Paul compares himself to a mother nursing her baby. And in verse 11 to a father. He cared for the church with a mother’s and a father’s love.
> Verse 9: Paul worked as a tentmaker (Acts 18:3). He didn’t want to live at anyone’s expense.
> For today: Whoever has responsibility in the church should be gentle, honest and unselfish. Power must not be misused.

## You received God’s word (Verses 13–16)

[13] That is why we also thank God without ceasing:
You heard from us the word of God’s message.
And you accepted it not as the word of men,
but as what
it truly is:
God’s word.
It works in you
who believe.
[14] For you,
brothers,
became imitators of the assemblies of God in Judea
that are in Christ Jesus.
For you suffered the same things from your own countrymen
as they did from the Jews
[15] who killed both the Lord Jesus
and their own prophets
and drove us out.
They don’t please God
and are against all people.
[16] They forbid us
to speak to the nations
so that they may be saved.
So they always fill up the measure of their sins.
But wrath has come on them to the uttermost.

> **Important note**
> Verses 14–16 are among the most difficult verses in the New Testament. In history they were terribly misused to despise, persecute and kill Jews. That was a grave injustice. These verses must never be used against Jews.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paul himself was a Jew. The churches in Judea (verse 14) that he praises consisted of Jews. Jesus, his disciples and the first Christians were Jews.
> Here Paul speaks in anger about a certain group in Judea that persecuted the Christians at that time and had driven him out. He doesn’t mean the whole Jewish people. The Greek word can also mean *the Judeans*, that is, the people living in Judea. And the sentence can be read this way: *from those Jews who …*, so only from certain people.
> Verse 15: Jesus was crucified by the Romans. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. The claim that *the Jews* killed Jesus is false and has caused endless suffering.
> Verse 16: What Paul means by “wrath has come on them to the uttermost” is unclear. Some interpreters think that verses 15–16 were added later by someone else, perhaps after the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70. Others consider them words of Paul in a moment of great anger.
> Later Paul wrote very differently: God has not rejected his people. God does not take back his gifts and his calling. All Israel will be saved (Romans 11:1–2, 26, 29).
> For today: Antisemitism is sin. Christians must stand up against hatred of Jews everywhere.

## Paul longs for the church (Verses 17–20)

[17] But we,
brothers,
were separated from you for a short time,
in person,
but not in heart.
All the more we tried
to see you again,
with great longing.
[18] For we wanted to come to you.
I,
Paul,
wanted to more than once.
But Satan hindered us.
[19] For who is our hope,
our joy
and our crown of rejoicing
that we are proud of?
Isn’t it you,
before our Lord Jesus
at his coming?
[20] For you are our glory and our joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Paul had to leave Thessalonica quickly because of unrest (Acts 17:10). He feels like a father whose children were taken away.
> Verse 18: Paul doesn’t say exactly what hindered him. He sees in it the work of evil.
> Verse 19: “At his coming”: Here for the first time is the word for the return of Jesus. It is an important theme in this letter.
> Verse 20: Paul is proud of the church, not of himself.
