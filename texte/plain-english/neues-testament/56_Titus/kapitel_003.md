# Titus – Chapter 3
### Saved by God’s kindness

---

## Gentle toward all people (Verses 1–2)

[1] Remind them
to be subject to rulers and authorities,
to be obedient
and to be ready for every good work.
[2] They are to speak evil of no one,
not to be quarrelsome,
but gentle,
showing all humility toward all people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Christians are to keep the order of the state and do good for society (compare Romans 13).
> Important: This doesn’t mean that you have to do everything the state demands. If a state commands injustice, you must obey God rather than people (Acts 5:29).
> Verse 2: “Toward all people”: Gentleness is not only for Christians, but for everyone.

---

## God’s kindness has saved us (Verses 3–8)

[3] For we too were once foolish,
disobedient
and led astray.
We were slaves to all kinds of desires and pleasures.
We lived in malice and envy,
were hateful
and hated one another.
[4] But then the kindness of God our Savior
and his love toward mankind appeared.
[5] He saved us,
not because of right deeds
that we ourselves had done,
but because he is merciful.
He saved us through the washing of new birth
and renewing by the Holy Spirit.
[6] He poured out the Spirit on us richly
through Jesus Christ our Savior,
[7] so that, being made right with God by his grace,
we might become heirs
according to the hope of eternal life.
[8] This saying is trustworthy.
I want
you to insist on these things firmly,
so that those
who have believed God
may be careful
to do good works.
These things are good and useful for people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “We too”: Paul includes himself. No one is better than others by nature.
> Verse 4: “His love toward mankind”: The Greek word for this is *philanthropia*. Our word *philanthropy*, love for people, comes from it. God is a friend of people.
> Verse 5: Salvation is God’s gift out of mercy. “The washing of new birth” means baptism. Through it a new life begins.
> Verses 4–7 are a short summary of the whole faith: God loves people, he saves by grace, he gives the Holy Spirit and the hope of eternal life.
> Verse 8: We are not saved by good works. But whoever is saved is to do good.

---

## Avoid quarrels (Verses 9–11)

[9] But avoid foolish arguments,
family trees,
strife
and disputes about the law.
For they are useless and empty.
[10] After a first and second warning,
avoid a person
who causes divisions.
[11] You know
that such a person is twisted and sinful.
He has condemned himself.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Quarreling about little things leads nowhere. That is also true today in churches.
> Verse 10: Whoever causes division again and again gets two warnings. Only then should you part from him. Patience comes before strictness.

---

## Last instructions and greetings (Verses 12–15)

[12] When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you,
do your best
to come to me at Nicopolis.
For I have decided
to spend the winter there.
[13] Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos
on their journey quickly,
so that they lack nothing.
[14] Our people too are to learn
to do good works,
to help in urgent needs,
so that they will not be unfruitful.
[15] All
who are with me
greet you.
Greet those
who love us in faith.
Grace be with you all.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Nicopolis was probably a city on the west coast of Greece.
> Verse 13: Apollos was a well-known preacher (Acts 18:24–28). Zenas was a “lawyer,” either an expert in the Jewish law or a legal expert.
> Verse 14: Good works are very practical: helping people in need.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to Titus?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Good leaders:** Titus is to appoint leaders on Crete who have good character (Chapter 1).
> **A difficult passage:** The harsh words in chapter 1 must not be used against Jews or against any people. Sweeping judgments about people are wrong.
> **For every age:** Advice for old and young, women and men. Some roles reflect the time back then (Chapter 2).
> **God’s grace has appeared:** It brings salvation to all people and helps us live well (Chapter 2).
> **God loves people:** He saves not because of our deeds, but out of mercy (Chapter 3).
> **Good works:** Whoever is saved is to help others in their need (Chapter 3).
> **For today:** God is a friend of people. That is why Christians too are to be kind to everyone.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the short letter to Philemon. It is about a runaway slave who has become a brother.
