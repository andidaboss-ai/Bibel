# Titus – Chapter 1
### Good leaders for the churches on Crete

---

## Before we begin: The letter to Titus

Titus was a coworker of Paul. He was not a Jew, but a Greek (Galatians 2:3). Paul entrusted him with important tasks, for example in Corinth (2 Corinthians 7 and 8).
According to the letter, Paul left Titus behind on the island of Crete. Titus is to put the young churches in order there and appoint leaders.
Together with the two letters to Timothy, the letter to Titus belongs to the *Pastoral Letters*. Many scholars think that a student of Paul wrote them, in Paul’s spirit. Others consider Paul himself to be the author.
The great theme is: God’s grace has appeared. It changes life. Christians are to do good.
**Important note:** In chapter 1 there are harsh words about “those of the circumcision,” about “Jewish fables” and about the people of Crete. In the boxes we explain why these words must not be used against Jews or against any people.
The letter to Titus has 3 chapters.

## Greeting (Verses 1–4)

[1] Paul,
a servant of God
and an apostle of Jesus Christ,
for the faith of God’s chosen ones
and the knowledge of the truth
which leads to godliness,
[2] in hope of eternal life.
God,
who can’t lie,
promised it before time began.
[3] But in his own time he revealed his word
in the message
with which I was entrusted
by the command of God our Savior.
[4] Paul writes to Titus,
his true child in a common faith:
Grace,
mercy
and peace
from God the Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ our Savior.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “God, who can’t lie”: You can rely on God’s promises.
> Verse 4: “In a common faith”: Paul was a Jew, Titus was a Greek. Faith in Christ connects them.

---

## Who can be a church leader (Verses 5–9)

[5] I left you in Crete
so that you would set in order
what was still lacking
and appoint elders in every city,
as I directed you.
[6] An elder must be blameless,
the husband of one wife.
He must have children
who believe
and who are not accused of wild or unruly behavior.
[7] For the overseer must be blameless,
as God’s steward.
He must not be self-pleasing,
not quick-tempered,
not a drinker,
not violent
and not greedy for dishonest gain.
[8] But he must be hospitable,
a lover of good,
sensible,
fair,
holy
and self-controlled.
[9] He must hold to the trustworthy word
as it was taught.
Then he can encourage with sound teaching
and correct those
who contradict.

> **What does this mean?**
> Steward means: someone who manages things for the owner.
> Verses 5–9: Similar to 1 Timothy 3. For leaders, character counts: not quick-tempered, not violent, not greedy for money, but hospitable, fair and self-controlled.
> Verses 5 and 7: “Elders” and “overseer” probably mean the same people here: the leaders of the local church.
> Verse 6: The children are not to live in a “wild or unruly” way. That doesn’t mean that parents are to blame for everything their adult children do.

## Warning against false teachers (Verses 10–16)

[10] For there are many unruly people,
empty talkers and deceivers,
especially those of the circumcision.
[11] They must be silenced.
They upset whole families
and teach
things they ought not,
for the sake of dishonest gain.
[12] One of them,
a prophet of their own,
said:
“Cretans are always liars,
evil beasts
and lazy gluttons.”
[13] This testimony is true.
So correct them sharply,
so that they may be sound in the faith
[14] and not pay attention to Jewish fables
and commandments of people
who turn away from the truth.
[15] To the pure, all things are pure.
But to those who are defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure.
Instead, both their mind and their conscience are defiled.
[16] They claim
that they know God,
but by their deeds they deny him.
They are detestable,
disobedient
and unfit for any good work.

> **Important note**
> Gluttons means: people who eat far too much.
> Defiled means: made unclean.
> Verses 10–16 contain harsh words. They must never be used against Jews or against the people of any nation.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “Those of the circumcision” were probably teachers within the Christian church who themselves came from Judaism. They demanded certain rules from the Christians from the nations. It is an argument among Christians, as in the letter to the Galatians.
> Verse 14: “Jewish fables”: This probably means certain speculations and legends that these teachers spread. It is no judgment about the Jewish faith or the holy Scriptures of Israel. Paul himself was a Jew and held the Scriptures of Israel in high esteem (compare 2 Timothy 3:15–16).
> Verse 12: The quotation probably comes from the Greek poet Epimenides, who himself came from Crete. It was a well-known saying at that time.
> Very important: Sweeping judgments about the people of a nation are wrong. No people is “always” evil or dishonest. Prejudice against people because of where they come from hurts their dignity. God loves all people equally.
> Verse 15: “To the pure, all things are pure”: similar to what Jesus says: Not what comes from outside makes a person unclean, but what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:15).
> Verse 16: Faith shows itself in deeds, not only in words.
