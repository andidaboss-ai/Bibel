# Titus – Chapter 2
### God’s grace has appeared

---

## Advice for old and young (Verses 1–8)

[1] But you, say
what fits sound teaching.
[2] The older men are to be sober,
respectable,
sensible,
sound in faith,
in love
and in perseverance.
[3] In the same way, the older women are to behave
as is fitting for saints.
They must not be slanderers
and not be enslaved to much wine.
They are to be teachers of that which is good,
[4] so that they may train the young women
to love their husbands
and to love their children,
[5] to be sensible,
pure,
workers at home,
kind
and in subjection to their own husbands,
that God’s word may not be blasphemed.
[6] In the same way, urge the younger men
to be sensible.
[7] In all things show yourself an example of good works.
In your teaching show integrity,
seriousness
and incorruptibility,
[8] and sound speech
that can’t be condemned.
Then the opponent will be ashamed,
because he has nothing bad to say about us.

*incorruptibility* = not letting yourself be bribed or led astray

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–6: There is advice for each group: older men, older women, young women and young men. All are to be “sensible.”
> Verse 3: Older women are to be “teachers of that which is good.” Women had an important task as teachers of other women.
> Verses 4–5: These roles reflect the family order of that time. The reason is given: “that God’s word may not be blasphemed.” The young church did not want to give offense in its surroundings.
> Today many Christians understand marriage as a partnership of two people with equal rights. Others hold on to a special responsibility of the husband. All stress: It is about love, not about power.
> Very important: These verses give no one the right to rule over their spouse or to use violence. Violence in marriage is a serious wrong and a crime. You can get help. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. Men can also call the Men’s Advice Line at 0808 801 0327. In an emergency, call 911 in the US or 999 in the UK.

## A word to the slaves (Verses 9–10)

[9] Urge servants
to be in subjection to their own masters.
They are to please them in all things,
not talk back,
[10] not steal,
but show complete faithfulness,
so that they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior
in all things.

> **What does this mean?**
> The WEB says *servants* here. Slaves are meant.
> Verse 10: It is remarkable: Slaves too can “adorn the teaching of God.” They count as valuable witnesses of the faith, not just as property.
> Very important: Slavery is a grave injustice. These verses were later misused to justify slavery. That was wrong. Today there is still human trafficking and forced labor. Christians should stand up against it. If you know about trafficking or forced labor, you can get help. In the US, call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888. In the UK, call the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.

## The grace of God has appeared (Verses 11–15)

[11] For the grace of God has appeared.
It brings salvation to all people.
[12] It trains us
to say no to ungodliness and worldly desires
and to live sensibly,
justly
and godly in this present age.
[13] We are waiting for the blessed hope:
the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior,
Jesus Christ.
[14] He gave himself for us,
to set us free from all wrongdoing
and to purify for himself a people for his own possession,
a people eager
to do good works.
[15] Say these things.
Encourage
and correct
with all authority.
Let no one look down on you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11 is the heart of the letter: God’s grace is for all people.
> Verse 12: Grace is like a teacher. It helps us to live well. Grace is not only forgiveness, but also strength for a new start.
> Verse 13: “Our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ”: Many interpreters understand this to mean that Jesus is called God here. Others translate: *the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ*.
> Verse 14: Christ gave himself in order to “purify for himself a people for his own possession.” These words come from the Scriptures of Israel (compare Exodus 19:5). God chose Israel. Through Christ people from all nations are added. Israel is not replaced by this (compare Romans 11:1–2, 29).
