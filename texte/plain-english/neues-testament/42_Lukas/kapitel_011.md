# Luke – Chapter 11
### “Lord, teach us to pray”

---

## The Lord’s Prayer (Verses 1–4)

[1] One time Jesus was praying in a certain place.
When he finished,
one of his disciples said to him,
“Lord, teach us to pray,
just as John taught his disciples.”
[2] Jesus said to them,
“When you pray, say:
‘Our Father in heaven,
may your name be kept holy.
May your Kingdom come.
May your will be done
on earth as it is in heaven.
[3] Give us each day
the bread we need for the day.
[4] Forgive us our sins,
because we also forgive everyone
who owes us something.
Don’t bring us into temptation.
But save us from the evil one.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Lord’s Prayer is the most important prayer of Christians. It unites all churches. A longer form is in Matthew 6:9–13.
> Jesus prays himself, and the disciples want to pray the way he does. He teaches them to call God “Father.” That shows closeness and trust.
> Many of the requests have roots in Jewish prayer, for example in the Kaddish, which asks that God’s great name be made holy and that his kingdom come. Jesus prays as a Jew.
> The oldest manuscripts of Luke have a shorter form. It begins simply with Father, and it does not have the words “Our,” “in heaven,” “May your will be done …” and “But save us from the evil one.” The WEB, our English source text, has the longer form.
> “From the evil one”: The WEB means evil as a person. It can also be understood as evil in general.
> “Don’t bring us into temptation”: God does not want to lead anyone into evil (James 1:13). What is meant is: Keep us safe in hard tests.
> For today: You don’t need beautiful words to pray. You may tell God everything, as you would a good father or a good mother.

## The friend at midnight (Verses 5–13)

[5] Then Jesus said to them,
“Imagine this:
One of you has a friend.
You go to him at midnight and say,
‘Friend, lend me three loaves of bread.
[6] A friend of mine has come to me from a trip.
I have nothing to give him to eat.’
[7] And your friend answers from inside the house,
‘Don’t bother me!
The door is already locked.
My children are in bed with me.
I can’t get up and give you anything.’
[8] I tell you,
he may not get up and give him the bread
because he is his friend.
But he will get up because the man keeps on asking
and does not give up.
And he will give him as much as he needs.
[9] So I tell you:
Keep asking, and you will receive.
Keep looking, and you will find.
Keep knocking, and the door will be opened for you.
[10] For everyone who asks receives.
Everyone who looks finds.
And the door will be opened for everyone who knocks.
[11] Which of you fathers would give your son a stone
when he asks for bread?
If he asks for a fish,
would you give him a snake instead?
[12] If he asks for an egg,
would you give him a scorpion?
[13] Even though you are evil,
you know how to give good gifts to your children.
So your Father in heaven will give even more.
He will give the Holy Spirit
to those who ask him!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then hospitality was a sacred duty. Leaving a guest without food was not possible. That is why the man goes to his neighbor in the middle of the night.
> In a simple house the whole family slept in one room. Getting up would have woken everyone.
> The man does not give up. He asks again and again. Older Bibles call this “persistence.”
> Jesus says: If even a tired neighbor helps, how much more will God help, who loves us. You may keep on praying.
> Verse 11: The words about bread and a stone are missing in some old manuscripts.
> Verse 13: “Even though you are evil” means: You are not perfect. Even so, parents do good things for their children. God is much better still.
> Only Luke says: God gives “the Holy Spirit.” That is the best gift.
> Important: Not every prayer is answered the way we wish. That does not mean someone believed too little. God still hears.
> Sadly, there are parents who do not do good things for their children. Whoever experiences violence at home can get help. In the US, call or text Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111.

## Jesus and Beelzebul (Verses 14–26)

[14] Jesus was driving out a demon
that made a man unable to speak.
When the demon had gone out,
the man spoke.
The crowds were amazed.
[15] But some of them said,
“He drives out demons with the help of Beelzebul,
the ruler of the demons.”
[16] Others wanted to test Jesus.
They asked him for a sign from heaven.
[17] But Jesus knew what they were thinking.
He said to them,
“Every kingdom that fights against itself
will be destroyed.
A family that fights against itself
will fall apart.
[18] If Satan fights against himself,
how can his kingdom last?
I say this because you say
that I drive out demons with the help of Beelzebul.
[19] If I drive out demons with Beelzebul’s help,
then whose help do your children use to drive them out?
So your children will be your judges.
[20] But I drive out demons by God’s finger.
If that is so,
then God’s Kingdom has come to you.
[21] When a strong man with all his weapons
guards his own house,
his things are safe.
[22] But someone stronger than he may attack him
and defeat him.
Then the stronger man takes away all the weapons
the strong man trusted in.
And he divides up the things he has taken.
[23] Whoever is not with me
is against me.
Whoever does not gather with me
scatters.
[24] An unclean spirit goes out of a person.
Then it travels through dry places
and looks for a place to rest.
But it finds none.
Then it says,
‘I will go back to my house that I came out of.’
[25] It comes back
and finds the house swept clean and in order.
[26] Then it goes and gets seven other spirits
that are even more evil than itself.
They go in and live there.
In the end that person is worse off
than he was at first.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Beelzebul” was a name for the leader of the evil spirits. The name may come from an old idol (compare 2 Kings 1:2).
> Jesus shows: The accusation makes no sense. Satan would not fight against himself.
> Verse 19: “Your children” are Jewish people who at that time also cast out evil spirits. Jesus acknowledges them.
> Verse 20: “God’s finger” reminds us of the miracles in Egypt (Exodus 8:19; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 8:15). God himself is at work through Jesus.
> Verses 21–22: The “strong man” is Satan. The one who is “stronger” is Jesus. He defeats the power of evil.
> Verses 24–26: It is not enough that evil is gone. The “house” must not stay empty. It should be filled with good, with God’s Spirit.
> Important: Mental illnesses and disabilities such as being unable to speak are not possession. People back then explained many kinds of suffering this way. Today we know more about illnesses.

## Who is truly blessed? (Verses 27–28)

[27] While Jesus was saying these things,
a woman in the crowd called out loudly to him,
“Blessed is the mother who gave birth to you
and who nursed you!”
[28] But Jesus said,
“No, blessed are the people
who hear the word of God and obey it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells this little scene. The woman praises Jesus’ mother. Back then that was great praise.
> Here Jesus says “No.” The WEB says “On the contrary.” But the Greek word can also mean “rather” or “yes, but.” Jesus does not reject the praise for his mother. He says: It is even more important to hear God’s word and to live by it.
> Mary herself is the best example of this (Luke 1:38,45; 2:19). She heard God’s word and kept it.

## The sign of Jonah (Verses 29–32)

[29] The crowds grew bigger and bigger.
Jesus began to say,
“The people of this generation are evil.
They want to see a sign.
But no sign will be given to them
except the sign of Jonah the prophet.
[30] Jonah was a sign for the people of Nineveh.
In the same way, the Son of Man will be a sign
for this generation.
[31] On judgment day the Queen of the South
will stand up with the people of this generation.
She will condemn them.
She came from the ends of the earth
to listen to the wisdom of Solomon.
And look:
Someone greater than Solomon is here!
[32] On judgment day the men of Nineveh
will stand up with this generation.
They will condemn it.
For they turned away from their sins
when Jonah preached to them.
And look:
Someone greater than Jonah is here!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonah was a prophet. He preached in Nineveh, a large city that did not belong to Israel. The people there turned back to God (Jonah 3).
> The “Queen of the South” is the Queen of Sheba. She traveled far to hear King Solomon (1 Kings 10:1–13).
> Both were non-Jews. They listened to God’s messengers. Jesus says: Whoever hears Jesus now and does not turn back has fewer excuses than they did.
> “This generation”: Jesus speaks as a Jewish prophet to people of his time who did not want to accept him. This is not a judgment on the Jewish people for all time.

## The light within you (Verses 33–36)

[33] “No one lights a lamp
and puts it in a cellar
or under a basket.
He puts it on a lampstand.
Then people who come in can see the light.
[34] Your eye is the lamp of your body.
When your eye is good,
your whole body is full of light.
But when your eye is evil,
your body is full of darkness.
[35] So be careful
that the light in you is not darkness.
[36] Your whole body may be full of light,
with no part of it dark.
Then it will be completely full of light,
as when a lamp shines brightly on you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then people believed: The eye lets light into a person, like a window.
> A “good” eye means: a clear, honest, generous way of looking. An “evil” eye means: envy, greed, a twisted way of looking.
> For today: What matters is how I look at the world and at other people.

## Jesus criticizes Pharisees and lawyers (Verses 37–54)

> **An important note first**
> In this section Jesus speaks very harsh words. Later they were often misused to despise and persecute Jews. That is a grave injustice.
> Here Jesus speaks as a Jew to other Jews, like the prophets of Israel. He even eats with a Pharisee. His criticism is aimed at hypocrisy and injustice, which exist in every religion, including among Christians.
> The Pharisees were devout people who wanted to live out God’s commandments in everyday life. Later rabbinic Judaism grew out of them. Today the church teaches: The Jewish people are loved by God and not rejected (Romans 11:1–2,29; *Nostra Aetate* 1965).

[37] While Jesus was speaking,
a Pharisee invited him to eat with him.
So Jesus went in
and sat down at the table.
[38] The Pharisee was surprised
that Jesus did not wash first before the meal.
[39] The Lord said to him,
“You Pharisees clean the outside of cups and plates.
But inside you are full of greed and evil.
[40] You fools!
The one who made the outside
also made the inside, didn’t he?
[41] Give what is inside to the poor.
Then everything will be clean for you.
[42] How terrible it will be for you Pharisees!
You give God a tenth of your mint and rue
and of every herb in your garden.
But you ignore justice and the love of God.
You should do these things,
without leaving the other things undone.
[43] How terrible it will be for you Pharisees!
You love the most important seats in the synagogues.
You love it when people greet you with respect in the marketplaces.
[44] How terrible it will be for you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!
You are like graves that no one can see.
People walk over them
without knowing it.”
[45] One of the experts in the law said to Jesus,
“Teacher, when you say these things,
you insult us too.”
[46] Jesus said,
“How terrible it will be for you experts in the law too!
You put heavy loads on people
that are hard to carry.
But you yourselves will not lift one finger
to help them carry these loads.
[47] How terrible it will be for you!
You build tombs for the prophets.
But it was your ancestors who killed them.
[48] So you show that you agree
with what your ancestors did.
They killed the prophets,
and you build their tombs.
[49] That is why the wisdom of God said,
‘I will send them prophets and apostles.
They will kill some of them
and persecute others.’
[50] So the people of this generation will be held responsible
for the blood of all the prophets
that has been spilled since the world was made,
[51] from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zachariah.
Zachariah was killed between the altar and the temple.
Yes, I tell you,
this generation will be held responsible for it all.
[52] How terrible it will be for you experts in the law!
You have taken away the key of knowledge.
You did not go in yourselves.
And you stopped others who were trying to go in.”
[53] Jesus said these things to them.
Then the scribes and the Pharisees became very angry with him.
They began to ask him hard questions about many things.
[54] They were waiting to trap him.
They wanted to catch him saying something wrong,
so that they could accuse him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 38: Devout Jews washed their hands before eating, as a sign of purity before God. It was not a matter of hygiene, but a religious custom.
> Verses 39–41: Jesus says: Outward purity is good. But the heart is more important. Whoever shares with the poor is pure inside.
> Verse 42: The “tithe” was an offering for the temple and the poor: a tenth of the harvest (Deuteronomy 14:22). Mint and rue are garden herbs. Jesus does not say that tithing is wrong: “You should do these things, without leaving the other things undone” (11:42). But justice and love are more important. The prophets said this too (Micah 6:8).
> Verse 44: Whoever touched a grave became unclean (Numbers 19:16). Jesus says: Whoever is a hypocrite makes others unclean without their noticing. The words “scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites” (11:44) are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> The “experts in the law” (in older Bibles: lawyers) knew the law of Moses very well. They were similar to the scribes.
> Verses 47–48: Many prophets were persecuted in the history of Israel (for example Jeremiah). The Bible of Israel itself tells this honestly.
> Verse 51: Abel is the first person who was murdered (Genesis 4:8). Zachariah was stoned in the temple courtyard (2 Chronicles 24:20–22). In the Hebrew Bible the Book of Chronicles comes at the end. So “from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zachariah” (11:51) means: from the beginning to the end of the Holy Scriptures.
> Verses 50–51: “This generation”: This is the language of the prophets. It is not a judgment on the whole Jewish people. The Bible says clearly: Each person is responsible only for their own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).
> Verse 52: The “key of knowledge” is the right understanding of the Holy Scriptures.
> For today: These words are above all a warning to everyone who teaches and leads in the church: No hypocrisy, no heavy burdens for others, no obstacles on the way to God.
