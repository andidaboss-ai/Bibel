# Luke – Chapter 11
### “Lord, teach us to pray”

---

## The Lord’s Prayer (Verses 1–4)

[1] Once Jesus was praying in a certain place.
When he had finished,
one of his disciples said to him,
“Lord, teach us to pray,
just as John also taught his disciples.”
[2] He said to them,
“When you pray,
say,
‘Our Father in heaven,
may your name be kept holy.
May your Kingdom come.
May your will be done
on earth, as it is in heaven.
[3] Give us day by day
our daily bread.
[4] Forgive us our sins,
for we ourselves also forgive everyone
who owes us something.
Bring us not into temptation,
but deliver us from the evil one.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Lord’s Prayer is the most important prayer of Christians. It unites all churches. A longer form is in Matthew 6:9–13.
> Jesus prays himself, and the disciples want to pray the way he does. He teaches them to call God “Father.” That shows closeness and trust.
> Many of the requests have roots in Jewish prayer, for example in the Kaddish, which asks that God’s great name be made holy and that his kingdom come. Jesus prays as a Jew.
> The oldest manuscripts of Luke have a shorter form. It begins simply with Father, and it does not have the words “Our,” “in heaven,” “May your will be done …” and “but deliver us from the evil one.” The WEB has the longer form.
> “From the evil one”: The WEB means evil as a person. It can also be understood as evil in general.
> “Bring us not into temptation”: God does not want to lead anyone into evil (James 1:13). What is meant is: Keep us safe in hard tests.
> For today: You don’t need beautiful words to pray. You may tell God everything, as you would a good father or a good mother.

## The friend at midnight (Verses 5–13)

[5] He said to them,
“Imagine this:
One of you has a friend.
You go to him at midnight
and say,
‘Friend, lend me three loaves of bread.
[6] For a friend of mine has come to me from a journey,
and I have nothing
to set before him.’
[7] And he answers from inside,
‘Don’t bother me.
The door is already shut,
and my children are with me in bed.
I can’t get up
and give it to you.’
[8] I tell you,
he may not get up
and give it to him
because he is his friend.
But because of his persistence,
he will get up
and give him as many as he needs.
[9] I tell you,
keep asking,
and it will be given you.
Keep seeking,
and you will find.
Keep knocking,
and it will be opened to you.
[10] For everyone who asks
receives.
He who seeks
finds.
To him who knocks
it will be opened.
[11] Which of you fathers
will give his son a stone
if he asks for bread?
Or if he asks for a fish,
will he give him a snake instead of a fish?
[12] Or if he asks for an egg,
will he give him a scorpion?
[13] If you then, being evil,
know how to give good gifts to your children,
how much more will your heavenly Father
give the Holy Spirit
to those who ask him?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then hospitality was a sacred duty. Leaving a guest without food was not possible. That is why the man goes to his neighbor in the middle of the night.
> In a simple house the whole family slept in one room. Getting up would have woken everyone.
> Persistence: not giving up, asking again and again.
> Jesus says: If even a tired neighbor helps, how much more will God help, who loves us. You may keep on praying.
> Verse 11: The words about bread and a stone are missing in some old manuscripts.
> Verse 13: “Being evil” means: You are not perfect. Even so, parents do good things for their children. God is much better still.
> Only Luke says: God gives “the Holy Spirit.” That is the best gift.
> Important: Not every prayer is answered the way we wish. That does not mean someone believed too little. God still hears.
> Sadly, there are parents who do not do good things for their children. Whoever experiences violence at home can get help. In the US, call or text Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111.

## Jesus and Beelzebul (Verses 14–26)

[14] Jesus was casting out a demon,
and it was mute.
When the demon had gone out,
the mute man spoke.
The crowds were amazed.
[15] But some of them said,
“He casts out demons by Beelzebul,
the prince of the demons.”
[16] Others wanted to test him.
They asked him for a sign from heaven.
[17] But he knew their thoughts
and said to them,
“Every kingdom divided against itself
is brought to ruin.
A house divided against itself
falls.
[18] If Satan also is divided against himself,
how will his kingdom stand?
For you say
that I cast out demons by Beelzebul.
[19] But if I cast out demons by Beelzebul,
by whom do your children cast them out?
So they will be your judges.
[20] But if I cast out demons by God’s finger,
then God’s Kingdom has come to you.
[21] When the strong man, fully armed,
guards his own house,
his goods are safe.
[22] But when someone stronger attacks him
and overcomes him,
he takes from him all his armor
in which he trusted,
and divides his plunder.
[23] He who is not with me
is against me.
He who doesn’t gather with me
scatters.
[24] When the unclean spirit has gone out of the man,
he passes through dry places,
seeking rest.
When he finds none,
he says,
‘I will go back to my house
from which I came out.’
[25] When he returns,
he finds it swept and put in order.
[26] Then he goes
and takes seven other spirits
more evil than himself.
They enter in and live there.
The last state of that man
becomes worse than the first.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Beelzebul” was a name for the leader of the evil spirits. The name may come from an old idol (compare 2 Kings 1:2).
> Jesus shows: The accusation makes no sense. Satan would not fight against himself.
> Verse 19: “Your children” are Jewish people who at that time also cast out evil spirits. Jesus acknowledges them.
> Verse 20: “God’s finger” reminds us of the miracles in Egypt (Exodus 8:19; in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 8:15). God himself is at work through Jesus.
> Verses 21–22: The “strong man” is Satan. The one who is “stronger” is Jesus. He defeats the power of evil.
> Verses 24–26: It is not enough that evil is gone. The “house” must not stay empty. It should be filled with good, with God’s Spirit.
> Important: Mental illnesses and disabilities such as being unable to speak are not possession. People back then explained many kinds of suffering this way. Today we know more about illnesses.

## Who is truly blessed? (Verses 27–28)

[27] As Jesus was saying these things,
a woman in the crowd called out loudly,
“Blessed is the womb that bore you,
and the breasts which nursed you!”
[28] But he said,
“On the contrary,
blessed are those who hear the word of God
and keep it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells this little scene. The woman praises Jesus’ mother. Back then that was great praise.
> The WEB says “On the contrary.” But the Greek word can also mean “rather” or “yes, but.” Jesus does not reject the praise for his mother. He says: It is even more important to hear God’s word and to live by it.
> Mary herself is the best example of this (Luke 1:38,45; 2:19). She heard God’s word and kept it.

## The sign of Jonah (Verses 29–32)

[29] More and more people were gathering around him.
Then he began to say,
“This is an evil generation.
It looks for a sign.
But no sign will be given to it
except the sign of Jonah the prophet.
[30] For just as Jonah became a sign to the people of Nineveh,
so the Son of Man will also be a sign
to this generation.
[31] The Queen of the South
will rise up in the judgment with the people of this generation
and will condemn them.
For she came from the ends of the earth
to hear the wisdom of Solomon.
And look:
One greater than Solomon is here.
[32] The men of Nineveh
will stand up in the judgment with this generation
and will condemn it.
For they repented
at the preaching of Jonah.
And look:
One greater than Jonah is here.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jonah was a prophet. He preached in Nineveh, a large city that did not belong to Israel. The people there turned back to God (Jonah 3).
> The “Queen of the South” is the Queen of Sheba. She traveled far to hear King Solomon (1 Kings 10:1–13).
> Both were non-Jews. They listened to God’s messengers. Jesus says: Whoever hears Jesus now and does not turn back has fewer excuses than they did.
> “This generation”: Jesus speaks as a Jewish prophet to people of his time who did not want to accept him. This is not a judgment on the Jewish people for all time.

## The light within you (Verses 33–36)

[33] “No one lights a lamp
and puts it in a cellar
or under a basket.
Instead he puts it on a stand,
so that those who come in may see the light.
[34] The lamp of the body is the eye.
When your eye is good,
your whole body is full of light.
But when it is evil,
your body is also full of darkness.
[35] So see to it
that the light that is in you isn’t darkness.
[36] If your whole body is full of light,
having no part dark,
it will be wholly full of light,
as when the lamp with its bright shining gives you light.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then people believed: The eye lets light into a person, like a window.
> A “good” eye means: a clear, honest, generous way of looking. An “evil” eye means: envy, greed, a twisted way of looking.
> For today: What matters is how I look at the world and at other people.

## Jesus criticizes Pharisees and lawyers (Verses 37–54)

> **An important note first**
> In this section Jesus speaks very harsh words. Later they were often misused to despise and persecute Jews. That is a grave injustice.
> Here Jesus speaks as a Jew to other Jews, like the prophets of Israel. He even eats with a Pharisee. His criticism is aimed at hypocrisy and injustice, which exist in every religion, including among Christians.
> The Pharisees were devout people who wanted to live out God’s commandments in everyday life. Later rabbinic Judaism grew out of them. Today the church teaches: The Jewish people are loved by God and not rejected (Romans 11:1–2,29; *Nostra Aetate* 1965).

[37] As Jesus was speaking,
a Pharisee invited him to dine with him.
Jesus went in
and sat at the table.
[38] The Pharisee saw this
and was surprised
that Jesus had not first washed himself before dinner.
[39] The Lord said to him,
“You Pharisees clean the outside of the cup and of the platter,
but your inner part is full of greed and wickedness.
[40] You foolish ones!
Didn’t he who made the outside
make the inside also?
[41] But give to the needy as gifts
those things which are within.
And then
all things will be clean to you.
[42] But woe to you Pharisees!
For you tithe mint and rue
and every herb.
But you bypass justice
and God’s love.
You ought to have done these,
and not to have left the other undone.
[43] Woe to you Pharisees!
For you love the best seats in the synagogues
and the greetings in the marketplaces.
[44] Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!
For you are like hidden graves.
People walk over them
and don’t know it.”
[45] One of the lawyers answered him,
“Teacher, in saying this
you insult us also.”
[46] Jesus said,
“Woe to you lawyers also!
For you load people with burdens
that are difficult to carry.
But you yourselves won’t even lift one finger
to help carry those burdens.
[47] Woe to you!
For you build the tombs of the prophets,
and your fathers killed them.
[48] So you testify
that you agree with the works of your fathers.
For they killed them,
and you build their tombs.
[49] That is why the wisdom of God also said,
‘I will send them prophets and apostles.
Some of them they will kill and persecute.’
[50] So the blood of all the prophets,
which was shed from the foundation of the world,
will be required of this generation,
[51] from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zachariah,
who died between the altar and the sanctuary.
Yes, I tell you,
it will be required of this generation.
[52] Woe to you lawyers!
For you took away the key of knowledge.
You didn’t enter in yourselves,
and those who were entering in,
you hindered.”
[53] As he said these things to them,
the scribes and the Pharisees became terribly angry.
They began
to question him about many things.
[54] They lay in wait for him
and tried to catch him in something he might say,
so that they could accuse him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 38: Devout Jews washed their hands before eating, as a sign of purity before God. It was not a matter of hygiene, but a religious custom.
> Verses 39–41: Jesus says: Outward purity is good. But the heart is more important. Whoever shares with the poor is pure inside.
> Verse 42: The “tithe” was an offering for the temple and the poor: a tenth of the harvest (Deuteronomy 14:22). Mint and rue are garden herbs. Jesus does not say that tithing is wrong: “You ought to have done these, and not to have left the other undone” (11:42). But justice and love are more important. The prophets said this too (Micah 6:8).
> Verse 44: Whoever touched a grave became unclean (Numbers 19:16). Jesus says: Whoever is a hypocrite makes others unclean without their noticing. The words “scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites” (11:44) are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> “Lawyers” were experts in the law of Moses, similar to the scribes.
> Verses 47–48: Many prophets were persecuted in the history of Israel (for example Jeremiah). The Bible of Israel itself tells this honestly.
> Verse 51: Abel is the first person who was murdered (Genesis 4:8). Zachariah was stoned in the temple courtyard (2 Chronicles 24:20–22). In the Hebrew Bible the Book of Chronicles comes at the end. So “from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zachariah” (11:51) means: from the beginning to the end of the Holy Scriptures.
> Verses 50–51: “This generation”: This is the language of the prophets. It is not a judgment on the whole Jewish people. The Bible says clearly: Each person is responsible only for their own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).
> Verse 52: The “key of knowledge” is the right understanding of the Holy Scriptures.
> For today: These words are above all a warning to everyone who teaches and leads in the church: No hypocrisy, no heavy burdens for others, no obstacles on the way to God.
