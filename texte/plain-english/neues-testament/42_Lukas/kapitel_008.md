# Luke – Chapter 8
### He who has ears to hear, let him hear

---

## Women travel with Jesus (Verses 1–3)

[1] Soon after that, Jesus traveled through towns and villages.
He preached and told the good news of God’s Kingdom.
The twelve were with him.
[2] Some women were also with him.
Jesus had healed them from evil spirits and sicknesses.
Mary, who was called Magdalene, was one of them.
Seven demons had gone out of her.
[3] Joanna was with him too.
She was the wife of Chuzas,
who managed Herod’s household.
Susanna was there,
and many other women.
These women helped Jesus and his disciples
with their own money and goods.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke says this so clearly: Not only twelve men traveled with Jesus, but also many women. They truly belonged to the group.
> The women provided for the group with their own money. Without them Jesus could not have traveled around like this.
> Mary Magdalene came from Magdala on the Sea of Galilee. She was one of the most important companions of Jesus. Later she is the first witness of the resurrection (Luke 24:10; John 20:11–18).
> Important: The Bible nowhere says that Mary Magdalene was a prostitute. That is a later guess. She is also not the woman from Luke 7. “Seven demons” means: She had suffered greatly. We do not know exactly from what.
> Chuzas managed Herod’s household. Such a man was called a steward: a man who manages a household or property for someone.
> Joanna belongs to Herod’s court. This shows: People from the upper class also followed Jesus.
> Verse 3: Some manuscripts say that they served him instead of them.

## The parable of the farmer and the seed (Verses 4–15)

[4] A large crowd came together.
People came to Jesus from every town.
Then he told them a parable.
[5] “A farmer went out to plant his seed.
As he scattered it,
some seed fell on the path.
People stepped on it,
and the birds ate it up.
[6] Some seed fell on rock.
It began to grow.
But soon it dried up,
because it had no water.
[7] Some seed fell among thorn bushes.
The thorns grew up with it
and choked the plants.
[8] Some seed fell on good soil.
It grew and produced a crop,
one hundred times as much as was planted.”
When Jesus had said this,
he called out,
“Whoever has ears to hear should listen!”
[9] His disciples asked him,
“What does this parable mean?”
[10] He said,
“God lets you know the secrets of his Kingdom.
But to the others I speak in parables.
So ‘they look, but they do not see.
They hear, but they do not understand.’
[11] This is what the parable means:
The seed is the word of God.
[12] The seed on the path
is like people who hear the word.
But then the devil comes
and takes the word out of their hearts.
He does not want them to believe and be saved.
[13] The seed on the rock
is like people who hear the word
and accept it with joy.
But they have no roots.
They believe for a while.
But when a time of temptation comes,
they turn away.
[14] The seed that fell among the thorns
is like people who hear the word.
But as they go on,
worries, riches, and the pleasures of life choke them.
So their fruit never becomes ripe.
[15] The seed on the good soil
is like people who hear the word
with an honest and good heart.
They hold on to it tightly.
They do not give up,
and they produce fruit.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then people often sowed first and plowed afterward. So seed also fell on paths, on rocky ground and among thorns.
> Jesus explains the parable himself: The seed is the word of God. The different kinds of ground are different people who hear it.
> Verse 10 freely quotes Isaiah 6:9. That sounds harsh. It does not mean that God shuts people out on purpose. Parables invite people in. Whoever is open and asks questions, like the disciples, understands more. Whoever closes themselves off stays outside.
> Verse 13: Luke says “temptation,” which can also mean a test. Hard times can put faith to the test.
> Verse 14: Worries, riches and pleasures can choke the word. In Luke, how we deal with possessions is a big theme.
> Verse 15: Only Luke says that they “do not give up.” Older Bibles say “with perseverance.” Faith grows slowly, like a plant.
> For today: Everyone can ask: Which kind of ground am I right now? Ground can also change.

## The light on the stand (Verses 16–18)

[16] “No one lights a lamp
and then covers it with a bowl
or puts it under a bed.
He puts it on a lampstand.
Then people who come in can see the light.
[17] Everything that is hidden will be made known.
Everything that is secret
will be found out and come into the light.
[18] So be careful how you listen!
Whoever has something will be given more.
But whoever has nothing will lose
even what he thinks he has.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The word of God is like a light. It should not be hidden, but should shine.
> Verse 18: “Whoever has something will be given more” is not a rule about money here. It is about hearing: Whoever takes in God’s word understands more and more. Whoever does not take it in loses even the little he thinks he has.
> For today: What matters is not only whether you hear, but how.

## Jesus’ true family (Verses 19–21)

[19] The mother and brothers of Jesus came to see him.
But they could not get near him
because of the crowd.
[20] Someone told Jesus,
“Your mother and your brothers are standing outside.
They want to see you.”
[21] But he answered them,
“My mother and my brothers
are the people who hear God’s word and do it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus does not reject his family. But he says: Everyone who hears God’s word and lives by it belongs to his family.
> In Luke, Mary herself is an example of this: She hears God’s word and says yes (Luke 1:38; 2:19).
> The churches understand Jesus’ “brothers” in different ways. Protestant Christians usually see them as his brothers by birth. Catholic Christians usually understand them as cousins or close relatives. Orthodox Christians often think of sons of Joseph from an earlier marriage.

## Jesus calms the storm (Verses 22–25)

[22] One day Jesus got into a boat with his disciples.
He said to them,
“Let’s go across to the other side of the lake.”
So they started out.
[23] While they were sailing,
Jesus fell asleep.
A strong wind came down on the lake.
Water came into the boat.
They were in great danger.
[24] The disciples went to Jesus and woke him up.
They said,
“Master, Master, we are going to die!”
Jesus got up.
He gave orders to the wind and the wild waves.
They stopped,
and everything became calm.
[25] Then he said to the disciples,
“Where is your faith?”
They were afraid and amazed.
They said to each other,
“Who is this man?
He gives orders even to the winds and the water,
and they obey him!”

> **What does this mean?**
> On the Sea of Galilee strong storms can come up suddenly. The winds rush down from the mountains.
> Jesus sleeps, calm and full of trust. The disciples are terrified of dying.
> Verse 25: In Luke, Jesus only asks: “Where is your faith?” That is a gentle question, not an accusation.
> In the Old Testament it is God who calms the sea (Psalm 107:29). That is why the disciples ask who this man is (verse 25).
> For today: Being afraid is human. In times of need you may cry out, like the disciples.

## Jesus heals a man in the country of the Gadarenes (Verses 26–39)

[26] Jesus and his disciples came to the land of the Gadarenes.
It is across the lake from Galilee.
[27] Jesus got out of the boat onto the shore.
There a man from the town came to meet him.
The man had had demons for a long time.
He wore no clothes.
He did not live in a house.
He lived among the graves in the tombs.
[28] When he saw Jesus,
he screamed and fell down in front of him.
He shouted with a loud voice,
“What do you want with me,
Jesus, Son of the Most High God?
I beg you, don’t torture me!”
[29] He said this because Jesus had ordered the unclean spirit
to come out of the man.
The unclean spirit had often taken control of him.
People had guarded him.
They had tied him up with chains and fetters.
But he broke the chains.
And the demon drove him out into lonely places.
[30] Jesus asked him,
“What is your name?”
He said,
“Legion.”
He said this because many demons had gone into him.
[31] The demons begged Jesus
not to send them into the abyss.
[32] A large herd of pigs was there,
eating on the hillside.
The demons begged Jesus
to let them go into the pigs.
Jesus let them.
[33] The demons came out of the man
and went into the pigs.
The herd ran down the steep bank into the lake
and drowned.
[34] The men who took care of the pigs saw what had happened.
They ran away.
They told the news in the town and in the countryside.
[35] People went out to see what had happened.
They came to Jesus.
They found the man whom the demons had left.
He was sitting at the feet of Jesus,
clothed and in his right mind.
And the people were afraid.
[36] Those who had seen it told the others
how the man with the demons had been healed.
[37] Then all the people from the area of the Gadarenes
asked Jesus to go away from them.
For they were very afraid.
So Jesus got into the boat
and went back.
[38] The man whom the demons had left
begged Jesus to let him go with him.
But Jesus sent him away and said,
[39] “Go back home.
Tell people about the great things God has done for you.”
So the man went away.
He told people in the whole town
about the great things Jesus had done for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The area was on the eastern shore of the lake. Many non-Jews lived there. That is why there were pigs there, which counted as unclean for Jews.
> The WEB, our English source text, says “Gadarenes.” Many old manuscripts say Gerasenes, others Gergesenes. The same area is meant.
> The man is completely cast out: without clothes, without a house, chained, among the graves. Jesus gives him back his dignity. In the end he sits at Jesus’ feet “clothed and in his right mind,” like a student.
> Fetters: chains for the feet.
> “Legion” was a Roman army unit with several thousand soldiers. The name shows: The man is ruled by a huge, destructive power.
> Verse 31: The “abyss” was thought of as a place where evil powers are locked up.
> Verse 37: The people are afraid of so much power. And they have lost their pigs. For some people, possessions matter more than a person who has been healed.
> Verse 39: The healed man becomes Jesus’ first messenger among non-Jews. He tells what “God” has done, and by this he means what “Jesus” has done.
> Important: Back then people often explained severe mental suffering by demons. Mental illnesses are not possession and not a sin. People with such illnesses need respect, medical help and community, not chains.

## The daughter of Jairus and a sick woman (Verses 40–56)

[40] Jesus came back across the lake.
The crowd welcomed him,
because they had all been waiting for him.
[41] A man named Jairus came.
He was a leader of the synagogue.
He fell down at the feet of Jesus.
He begged Jesus to come to his house.
[42] For he had only one daughter.
She was about twelve years old,
and she was dying.
Jesus went with him.
The crowds pushed against Jesus from all sides.
[43] A woman was there who had been bleeding for twelve years.
She had spent all her money on doctors.
But no one could heal her.
[44] She came up behind Jesus
and touched the fringe of his cloak.
At once her bleeding stopped.
[45] Jesus asked,
“Who touched me?”
Everyone said, “Not me.”
Peter and those with him said,
“Master, the crowds are pushing and pressing against you,
and you say, ‘Who touched me?’”
[46] But Jesus said,
“Someone did touch me.
I felt power go out of me.”
[47] The woman saw that she could not stay hidden.
She came, shaking with fear,
and fell down in front of Jesus.
In front of all the people she told him
why she had touched him.
And she told how she had been healed at once.
[48] Jesus said to her,
“Daughter, be encouraged.
Your faith has made you well.
Go in peace.”
[49] Jesus was still speaking
when someone came from the house of the synagogue leader.
He said to Jairus,
“Your daughter is dead.
Don’t bother the Teacher anymore.”
[50] Jesus heard this.
He said to Jairus,
“Don’t be afraid.
Only believe,
and she will be healed.”
[51] Jesus came to the house.
He did not let anyone go in with him
except Peter, John, and James,
and the girl’s father and mother.
[52] All the people were crying and mourning for her.
But Jesus said,
“Don’t cry.
She is not dead.
She is sleeping.”
[53] The people laughed at him,
because they knew that she was dead.
[54] But Jesus sent them all outside.
He took the girl by the hand
and called out,
“Child, get up!”
[55] Her spirit came back,
and at once she got up.
Jesus told them to give her something to eat.
[56] Her parents were amazed.
But Jesus ordered them
not to tell anyone what had happened.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two stories are woven into each other. Both times it is about twelve years: The girl is twelve years old. The woman has been sick for twelve years.
> Jairus is a respected man. A ruler of the synagogue led the worship services. In his need he falls down before Jesus. Here Luke tells of a Jewish leader who trusts Jesus.
> According to the law, the sick woman counted as unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27). For twelve years she was shut out, poor and alone. That is why she comes secretly from behind.
> Verse 43: The words “She had spent all her money on doctors” are missing in some old manuscripts. In verse 45 the words “and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” and the words “and those with him” are also missing in some manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verse 44: The “fringe” was the tassels on the cloak that devout Jews wore (Numbers 15:38–39).
> Verses 47–48: Jesus does not want to shame her. He wants everyone to know: She is healed and clean again. He calls her “Daughter.” She belongs again.
> Verse 52: “She is not dead. She is sleeping”: For Jesus, death is not the last word. That is why Christians sometimes speak of death as a kind of sleep.
> Verse 55: Jesus thinks of something very practical: The girl needs something to eat.
> Important: Whoever stays sick, or whose child dies, has not believed too little. God is close even in suffering and grief. Parents who have lost a child can find help, for example, from The Compassionate Friends (compassionatefriends.org in the US, tcf.org.uk in the UK). If you are in crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
