# Luke – Chapter 8
### He who has ears to hear, let him hear

---

## Women travel with Jesus (Verses 1–3)

[1] Soon afterward Jesus went through cities and villages.
He preached
and brought the good news of God’s Kingdom.
The twelve were with him,
[2] and certain women
who had been healed of evil spirits and illnesses:
Mary who was called Magdalene,
from whom seven demons had gone out;
[3] and Joanna, the wife of Chuzas,
Herod’s steward;
and Susanna;
and many others.
They served them from their own possessions.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke says this so clearly: Not only twelve men traveled with Jesus, but also many women. They truly belonged to the group.
> The women provided for the group with their own money. Without them Jesus could not have traveled around like this.
> Mary Magdalene came from Magdala on the Sea of Galilee. She was one of the most important companions of Jesus. Later she is the first witness of the resurrection (Luke 24:10; John 20:11–18).
> Important: The Bible nowhere says that Mary Magdalene was a prostitute. That is a later guess. She is also not the woman from Luke 7. “Seven demons” means: She had suffered greatly. We do not know exactly from what.
> Steward: a man who manages a household or property for someone.
> Joanna belongs to Herod’s court. This shows: People from the upper class also followed Jesus.
> Verse 3: Some manuscripts say that they served him instead of them.

## The parable of the farmer and the seed (Verses 4–15)

[4] A great crowd came together.
People were coming to him from every city.
Then he told them a parable:
[5] “The farmer went out
to sow his seed.
As he sowed,
some fell along the road.
It was trampled under foot,
and the birds of the sky ate it up.
[6] Some seed fell on the rock.
As soon as it grew,
it withered away,
because it had no moisture.
[7] Some fell among the thorns.
The thorns grew with it
and choked it.
[8] Some fell into the good ground.
It grew
and produced one hundred times as much fruit.”
As he said these things,
he called out,
“He who has ears to hear, let him hear!”
[9] Then his disciples asked him,
“What does this parable mean?”
[10] He said,
“To you it is given
to know the mysteries of God’s Kingdom.
But to the rest it is given in parables,
so that ‘seeing they may not see,
and hearing they may not understand.’
[11] Now the parable is this:
The seed is the word of God.
[12] Those along the road
are those who hear.
Then the devil comes
and takes away the word from their heart,
so that they may not believe and be saved.
[13] Those on the rock
are those who receive the word with joy
when they hear it.
But they have no root.
They believe for a while.
But in time of temptation
they fall away.
[14] What fell among the thorns,
these are those who have heard.
But as they go on their way they are choked
by cares, riches, and pleasures of life.
They bring no fruit to maturity.
[15] Those in the good ground
are those who hear the word
with an honest and good heart.
They hold it tightly
and produce fruit with perseverance.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then people often sowed first and plowed afterward. So seed also fell on paths, on rocky ground and among thorns.
> Jesus explains the parable himself: The seed is the word of God. The different kinds of ground are different people who hear it.
> Verse 10 freely quotes Isaiah 6:9. That sounds harsh. It does not mean that God shuts people out on purpose. Parables invite people in. Whoever is open and asks questions, like the disciples, understands more. Whoever closes themselves off stays outside.
> Verse 13: Luke says “temptation,” which can also mean a test. Hard times can put faith to the test.
> Verse 14: Worries, riches and pleasures can choke the word. In Luke, how we deal with possessions is a big theme.
> Verse 15: Only Luke says “with perseverance.” Perseverance means not giving up. Faith grows slowly, like a plant.
> For today: Everyone can ask: Which kind of ground am I right now? Ground can also change.

## The light on the stand (Verses 16–18)

[16] “No one lights a lamp
and covers it with a container
or puts it under a bed.
Instead he puts it on a stand,
so that those who come in may see the light.
[17] For nothing is hidden
that will not be revealed.
And nothing is secret
that will not be known and come to light.
[18] So be careful
how you hear.
For whoever has,
to him will be given.
And whoever doesn’t have,
from him will be taken away even
what he thinks he has.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The word of God is like a light. It should not be hidden, but should shine.
> Verse 18: “Whoever has, to him will be given” is not a rule about money here. It is about hearing: Whoever takes in God’s word understands more and more. Whoever does not take it in loses even the little he thinks he has.
> For today: What matters is not only whether you hear, but how.

## Jesus’ true family (Verses 19–21)

[19] His mother and brothers came to him.
But because of the crowd
they could not get near him.
[20] Some people told him,
“Your mother and your brothers are standing outside
and want to see you.”
[21] But he answered them,
“My mother and my brothers
are these who hear the word of God and do it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus does not reject his family. But he says: Everyone who hears God’s word and lives by it belongs to his family.
> In Luke, Mary herself is an example of this: She hears God’s word and says yes (Luke 1:38; 2:19).
> The churches understand Jesus’ “brothers” in different ways. Protestant Christians usually see them as his brothers by birth. Catholic Christians usually understand them as cousins or close relatives. Orthodox Christians often think of sons of Joseph from an earlier marriage.

## Jesus calms the storm (Verses 22–25)

[22] One day
Jesus got into a boat with his disciples.
He said to them,
“Let’s go over to the other side of the lake.”
So they set out.
[23] As they sailed,
he fell asleep.
A wind storm came down on the lake.
They were taking on dangerous amounts of water.
[24] They came to him,
woke him up,
and said,
“Master, Master, we are dying!”
He woke up
and rebuked the wind
and the raging water.
They stopped,
and it was calm.
[25] He said to them,
“Where is your faith?”
They were afraid
and amazed,
and they said to one another,
“Who is this then?
He commands even the winds and the water,
and they obey him!”

> **What does this mean?**
> On the Sea of Galilee strong storms can come up suddenly. The winds rush down from the mountains.
> Jesus sleeps, calm and full of trust. The disciples are terrified of dying.
> Verse 25: In Luke, Jesus only asks: “Where is your faith?” That is a gentle question, not an accusation.
> In the Old Testament it is God who calms the sea (Psalm 107:29). That is why the disciples ask who this man is (verse 25).
> For today: Being afraid is human. In times of need you may cry out, like the disciples.

## Jesus heals a man in the country of the Gadarenes (Verses 26–39)

[26] They arrived at the country of the Gadarenes.
It is opposite Galilee.
[27] When Jesus stepped ashore,
a man from the city met him.
He had had demons for a long time.
He wore no clothes
and didn’t live in a house,
but in the tombs.
[28] When he saw Jesus,
he cried out,
fell down before him,
and said with a loud voice,
“What do I have to do with you,
Jesus, you Son of the Most High God?
I beg you,
don’t torment me!”
[29] For Jesus was commanding the unclean spirit
to come out of the man.
The unclean spirit had often seized the man.
He had been kept under guard
and bound with chains and fetters.
But he broke the bonds apart
and was driven by the demon into the desert.
[30] Jesus asked him,
“What is your name?”
He said,
“Legion.”
For many demons had entered into him.
[31] They begged Jesus
not to command them
to go into the abyss.
[32] There was a large herd of pigs feeding on the mountain there.
The demons begged him
to allow them
to enter into the pigs.
He allowed them.
[33] The demons came out of the man
and entered into the pigs.
The herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake
and drowned.
[34] When those who fed the pigs saw
what had happened,
they fled
and told it in the city and in the country.
[35] People went out
to see what had happened.
They came to Jesus
and found the man
from whom the demons had gone out.
He was sitting at Jesus’ feet,
clothed and in his right mind.
And they were afraid.
[36] Those who had seen it
told them
how the man who had been possessed by demons
was healed.
[37] All the people of the country of the Gadarenes
asked Jesus
to leave them.
For they were very much afraid.
So he got into the boat
and went back.
[38] The man from whom the demons had gone out
begged Jesus
to let him go with him.
But Jesus sent him away and said,
[39] “Return to your house,
and declare what great things God has done for you.”
He went away
and told throughout the whole city
what great things Jesus had done for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The area was on the eastern shore of the lake. Many non-Jews lived there. That is why there were pigs there, which counted as unclean for Jews.
> The WEB says “Gadarenes.” Many old manuscripts say Gerasenes, others Gergesenes. The same area is meant.
> The man is completely cast out: without clothes, without a house, chained, among the graves. Jesus gives him back his dignity. In the end he sits at Jesus’ feet “clothed and in his right mind,” like a student.
> Fetters: chains for the feet.
> “Legion” was a Roman army unit with several thousand soldiers. The name shows: The man is ruled by a huge, destructive power.
> Verse 31: The “abyss” was thought of as a place where evil powers are locked up.
> Verse 37: The people are afraid of so much power. And they have lost their pigs. For some people, possessions matter more than a person who has been healed.
> Verse 39: The healed man becomes Jesus’ first messenger among non-Jews. He tells what “God” has done, and by this he means what “Jesus” has done.
> Important: Back then people often explained severe mental suffering by demons. Mental illnesses are not possession and not a sin. People with such illnesses need respect, medical help and community, not chains.

## The daughter of Jairus and a sick woman (Verses 40–56)

[40] When Jesus returned,
the crowd welcomed him.
For they had all been waiting for him.
[41] A man named Jairus came.
He was a ruler of the synagogue.
He fell down at Jesus’ feet
and begged him
to come into his house.
[42] For he had an only daughter.
She was about twelve years old
and she was dying.
As Jesus went,
the crowds pressed against him.
[43] There was a woman
who had had a flow of blood for twelve years.
She had spent all her living on physicians,
but no one could heal her.
[44] She came up behind him
and touched the fringe of his cloak.
Immediately the flow of her blood stopped.
[45] Jesus said,
“Who touched me?”
When everyone denied it,
Peter and those with him said,
“Master, the crowds press and push against you,
and you say,
‘Who touched me?’”
[46] But Jesus said,
“Someone did touch me.
For I felt
that power has gone out of me.”
[47] When the woman saw
that she could not stay hidden,
she came trembling,
fell down before him,
and told him in front of all the people
why she had touched him
and how she had been healed immediately.
[48] He said to her,
“Daughter, cheer up.
Your faith has made you well.
Go in peace.”
[49] While he was still speaking,
someone came from the house of the ruler of the synagogue
and said,
“Your daughter is dead.
Don’t trouble the Teacher.”
[50] When Jesus heard it,
he answered him,
“Don’t be afraid.
Only believe,
and she will be healed.”
[51] When he came to the house,
he didn’t allow anyone to go in
except Peter, John, James,
and the father and mother of the child.
[52] Everyone was weeping and mourning for her.
But he said,
“Don’t weep.
She isn’t dead,
but sleeping.”
[53] They laughed at him,
because they knew
that she was dead.
[54] But he put them all outside.
He took her by the hand
and called out,
“Child, arise!”
[55] Her spirit returned,
and she got up immediately.
He commanded
that something be given to her to eat.
[56] Her parents were amazed.
But he commanded them
to tell no one
what had happened.

> **What does this mean?**
> Two stories are woven into each other. Both times it is about twelve years: The girl is twelve years old. The woman has been sick for twelve years.
> Jairus is a respected man. A ruler of the synagogue led the worship services. In his need he falls down before Jesus. Here Luke tells of a Jewish leader who trusts Jesus.
> According to the law, the sick woman counted as unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27). For twelve years she was shut out, poor and alone. That is why she comes secretly from behind.
> Verse 43: The words “she had spent all her living on physicians” are missing in some old manuscripts. In verse 45 the words “and you say, ‘Who touched me?’” and the words “and those with him” are also missing in some manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verse 44: The “fringe” was the tassels on the cloak that devout Jews wore (Numbers 15:38–39).
> Verses 47–48: Jesus does not want to shame her. He wants everyone to know: She is healed and clean again. He calls her “Daughter.” She belongs again.
> Verse 52: “She isn’t dead, but sleeping”: For Jesus, death is not the last word. That is why Christians sometimes speak of death as a kind of sleep.
> Verse 55: Jesus thinks of something very practical: The girl needs something to eat.
> Important: Whoever stays sick, or whose child dies, has not believed too little. God is close even in suffering and grief. Parents who have lost a child can find help, for example, from The Compassionate Friends (compassionatefriends.org in the US, tcf.org.uk in the UK). If you are in crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
