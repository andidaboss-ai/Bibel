# Luke – Chapter 1
### Two children are announced

---

## Before we begin: The Gospel of Luke

The Gospel of Luke is the longest of the four Gospels. It is the first part of a two-part work. The second part is the Book of Acts. Both books are addressed to a man named Theophilus.
According to old church tradition, it was written by Luke, a doctor and companion of the apostle Paul (Colossians 4:14; Philemon 24). We do not know this for sure. The book does not name its author. It was probably written between AD 80 and 90.
Luke writes carefully and in a well-ordered way. As he says himself, he did research first (1:1–4). He probably knew the Gospel of Mark and other traditions as well.
Luke especially shows: Jesus turns to the poor, the sick, women, sinners and foreigners. God’s salvation is for all people.
Many famous texts are found only in Luke: the Christmas story with the manger and the shepherds, the Magnificat (the song of Mary), the parables of the Good Samaritan and the lost son, the story of Zacchaeus and the disciples on the road to Emmaus.
An important note: Luke begins and ends in the temple in Jerusalem. The story of Jesus is deeply rooted in the history and faith of Israel. Debates with other Jewish groups were arguments within Judaism. They must never be used against Jews.
The Gospel has 24 chapters.

## A foreword to Theophilus (Verses 1–4)

[1] Many have already tried
to write an account of the events
that have been fulfilled among us.
[2] They wrote it down
just as it was handed on to us
by those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses
and servants of the word.
[3] So it seemed good to me also
to trace everything carefully from the start
and to write it out for you in order,
most excellent Theophilus.
[4] Then you will know
the certainty of the things
you have been taught.

> **What does this mean?**
> Luke begins like a historian of his time, with a foreword.
> He says: There are already other accounts. I have done careful research and talked with eyewitnesses.
> Eyewitnesses: people who saw something with their own eyes.
> “Theophilus” means “friend of God.” He was probably a respected man, perhaps a supporter who had the book copied and shared. The name can also be understood as a way of speaking to all readers who love God.
> Luke wants faith to stand on firm ground.

## The birth of John is announced (Verses 5–25)

[5] In the days of Herod, the king of Judea,
there was a priest named Zacharias.
He belonged to the priestly division of Abijah.
His wife was descended from Aaron,
and her name was Elizabeth.
[6] They were both righteous before God.
They kept all the commandments and rules of the Lord
and lived blamelessly.
[7] But they had no child,
because Elizabeth could not have children.
And they were both very old.
[8] One time Zacharias was serving as priest before God,
because it was his division’s turn.
[9] According to the custom of the priests,
the lot fell to him
to go into the temple of the Lord
and burn incense.
[10] The whole crowd of people was praying outside
at the hour of incense.
[11] Then an angel of the Lord appeared to him,
standing on the right side of the altar of incense.
[12] When Zacharias saw him,
he was troubled,
and fear fell on him.
[13] But the angel said to him,
“Don’t be afraid, Zacharias,
because your prayer has been heard.
Your wife, Elizabeth, will bear you a son,
and you shall call his name John.
[14] You will have joy and gladness,
and many will rejoice at his birth.
[15] For he will be great in the sight of the Lord.
He will drink no wine and no strong drink.
He will be filled with the Holy Spirit,
even from his mother’s womb.
[16] He will turn many of the people of Israel
to the Lord their God.
[17] He will go before him
in the spirit and power of Elijah,
‘to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children,’
and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just.
So he will prepare a people
who are ready for the Lord.”
[18] Zacharias said to the angel,
“How can I be sure of this?
For I am an old man,
and my wife is very old.”
[19] The angel answered him,
“I am Gabriel.
I stand in the presence of God.
I was sent
to speak to you
and to bring you this good news.
[20] Listen:
You will be silent
and not able to speak
until the day these things happen.
For you didn’t believe my words,
which will be fulfilled at the right time.”
[21] The people were waiting for Zacharias
and were surprised
that he stayed so long in the temple.
[22] When he came out,
he could not speak to them.
Then they realized
that he had seen a vision in the temple.
He kept making signs to them
and remained mute.
[23] When the days of his service were over,
he went home.
[24] After these days his wife Elizabeth became pregnant.
She kept herself hidden for five months
and said,
[25] “The Lord has done this for me.
In these days he has looked at me
and taken away my disgrace
among people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Herod” here is Herod the Great. He ruled until 4 BC.
> There were 24 divisions of priests. Each division served one week in the temple twice a year (compare 1 Chronicles 24:10). A priest could usually offer incense only once in his life. For Zacharias this was a special day.
> Incense: a substance that gives off a sweet-smelling smoke when it is burned. In the temple it was offered to God.
> Zacharias and Elizabeth are devout, righteous Jews. Luke begins the Gospel right in the middle of Israel’s faith, in the temple.
> Verses 7 and 25: Back then, not having children was often seen as a disgrace. Important: Being childless is not a punishment from God and not a disgrace. Elizabeth was “righteous before God” (verse 6), even without a child. People who cannot have the children they long for are loved by God just as much.
> Verse 13: “John” means “God is gracious.”
> Verse 17 quotes Malachi 4:6 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles Malachi 3:24). John will be like the prophet Elijah.
> “Gabriel” means “God is strong.” He also appears in the Book of Daniel (Daniel 8:16; 9:21).
> Verse 20: For a time Zacharias cannot speak. That is a sign in this special story. Important: This does not mean that people who cannot speak are being punished. Being mute or having a disability is not a punishment for doubt.

## The birth of Jesus is announced (Verses 26–38)

[26] In the sixth month
the angel Gabriel was sent from God
to a city of Galilee
named Nazareth,
[27] to a virgin.
She was engaged to a man
whose name was Joseph.
He was from the family of David.
The virgin’s name was Mary.
[28] The angel came in to her and said,
“Rejoice, you highly favored one!
The Lord is with you.
Blessed are you among women!”
[29] When she saw him,
she was greatly troubled by what he said.
She wondered
what kind of greeting this might be.
[30] The angel said to her,
“Don’t be afraid, Mary,
for you have found favor with God.
[31] You will become pregnant
and give birth to a son.
You shall name him ‘Jesus.’
[32] He will be great
and will be called the Son of the Most High.
The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David.
[33] He will reign over the house of Jacob forever.
There will be no end to his Kingdom.”
[34] Mary said to the angel,
“How can this be,
since I am a virgin?”
[35] The angel answered her,
“The Holy Spirit will come on you,
and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.
Therefore the holy one
who is born from you
will also be called the Son of God.
[36] And listen:
Elizabeth your relative
has also become pregnant with a son in her old age.
People said she could not have children.
Now she is in her sixth month.
[37] For nothing spoken by God
is impossible.”
[38] Mary said,
“Here I am, the servant of the Lord.
Let it be done to me according to your word.”
Then the angel left her.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nazareth was a small, unimportant village. Mary was a young, simple woman, probably still very young. God does not choose the powerful, but the little ones.
> Being “engaged” back then was already a firm bond, almost like being married. But the couple did not yet live together.
> “Jesus” is the Greek form of the Hebrew name “Yeshua” or “Joshua.” It means: “God saves.”
> Verses 32–33: God had promised David that his kingship would last forever (2 Samuel 7:12–16).
> Verses 34–35: Christians believe that Jesus was conceived through the Holy Spirit, without a human father. This is called the “virgin birth.” It is in the Christian creed. Some Christians today understand it more as a picture. For everyone it shows: Jesus comes entirely from God.
> Overshadow: to cover like a shadow. Here it means that God’s power will rest on Mary.
> Verse 38: Mary freely says yes. God does not force her. She asks questions and then decides. For Christians, Mary is an example of faith. In the Catholic and Orthodox churches she is especially honored. Protestant Christians honor her as the mother of Jesus and an example in faith.

## Mary visits Elizabeth (Verses 39–45)

[39] In those days Mary set out
and hurried into the hill country,
to a city of Judah.
[40] She went into the house of Zacharias
and greeted Elizabeth.
[41] When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting,
the baby leaped in her womb.
And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.
[42] She called out with a loud voice,
“Blessed are you among women,
and blessed is the fruit of your womb!
[43] Why am I so favored,
that the mother of my Lord should come to me?
[44] For when I heard your greeting,
the baby leaped in my womb for joy!
[45] Blessed is she who believed!
For what the Lord has told her
will be fulfilled.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Two pregnant women meet: an old one and a young one. Both have experienced something unbelievable with God. They share their joy.
> Even before he is born, the baby John “leaped for joy.” Later John will point to Jesus.
> Womb: the place in a mother’s body where a baby grows.
> Elizabeth calls Mary “the mother of my Lord.” Words from verse 42 are part of the Catholic prayer “Hail Mary.”
> Verse 45: Mary is called blessed because she believed. This is different from Zacharias (verse 20).

## The song of Mary – the Magnificat (Verses 46–56)

[46] Mary said,
“My soul magnifies the Lord.
[47] My spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior,
[48] for he has looked at the humble state of his servant.
From now on,
all generations will call me blessed.
[49] For he who is mighty has done great things for me.
Holy is his name.
[50] His mercy is for generations and generations
on those who fear him.
[51] He has shown strength with his arm.
He has scattered the proud
in the plans of their hearts.
[52] He has put down princes from their thrones
and has lifted up the lowly.
[53] He has filled the hungry with good things.
He has sent the rich away empty.
[54] He has helped Israel, his servant,
and remembered his mercy,
[55] as he promised our fathers,
Abraham and his offspring forever.”
[56] Mary stayed with Elizabeth about three months.
Then she returned home.

> **What does this mean?**
> The song of Mary is named after its first word in the Latin Bible: “Magnificat” (“magnifies”). To this day it is sung every evening in many churches.
> Magnifies: praises, makes great.
> The song is very much like the song of Hannah (1 Samuel 2:1–10) and many psalms. Mary prays completely in the language of Israel’s faith.
> The song is revolutionary: God brings down the powerful and lifts up the lowly. He fills the hungry. God is on the side of the poor and oppressed. This idea runs through the whole Gospel of Luke.
> Verses 54–55: God stays faithful to his people Israel, as he promised Abraham.
> For today: The Magnificat encourages all who are made to feel small. God sees them.

## John is born (Verses 57–66)

[57] The time came for Elizabeth to give birth,
and she gave birth to a son.
[58] Her neighbors and relatives heard
how the Lord had shown her great mercy.
And they rejoiced with her.
[59] On the eighth day they came
to circumcise the child.
They wanted to name him after his father: Zacharias.
[60] But his mother said,
“Not so!
He will be called John.”
[61] They said to her,
“There is no one among your relatives
who is called by this name.”
[62] They made signs to his father
to ask what he wanted the child to be called.
[63] He asked for a writing tablet
and wrote,
“His name is John.”
They were all amazed.
[64] Immediately his mouth was opened
and his tongue was freed,
and he spoke and praised God.
[65] Fear came on all who lived around them.
All through the hill country of Judea
people talked about these things.
[66] All who heard about it
kept it in their hearts and said,
“What then will this child be?”
For the hand of the Lord was with him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Circumcision on the eighth day is a sign of the covenant between God and Israel (Genesis 17:12). To this day Jewish boys are circumcised on the eighth day. At that time the child receives his name.
> Circumcise: to cut away the foreskin of a boy’s penis. Covenant: a binding agreement.
> Elizabeth stands firm. Against what the relatives expect, she gives the child the name that God has chosen.
> Verse 62: People make signs to Zacharias. Perhaps he could not hear either. The Greek word in verse 22 can mean “mute” or “deaf.”
> As soon as Zacharias confirms the name, he can speak again. His first word is praise to God.

## The song of Zacharias – the Benedictus (Verses 67–80)

[67] His father Zacharias was filled with the Holy Spirit
and prophesied:
[68] “Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel,
for he has visited and redeemed his people.
[69] He has raised up a horn of salvation for us
in the house of his servant David,
[70] as he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets
long ago:
[71] salvation from our enemies
and from the hand of all who hate us.
[72] So he shows mercy to our fathers
and remembers his holy covenant,
[73] the oath which he swore to Abraham our father.
[74] He grants to us
that we, rescued from the hand of our enemies,
should serve him without fear,
[75] in holiness and righteousness before him
all the days of our life.
[76] And you, child,
will be called a prophet of the Most High.
For you will go before the Lord
to prepare his ways,
[77] to give his people knowledge of salvation
by the forgiveness of their sins.
[78] This comes from the tender mercy of our God.
Through it the dawn from on high will visit us,
[79] to shine on those
who sit in darkness and the shadow of death,
and to guide our feet into the way of peace.”
[80] The child grew and became strong in spirit.
He lived in the desert
until the day
he appeared in public to Israel.

> **What does this mean?**
> The song of Zacharias is named after its first word in the Latin Bible: “Benedictus” (“blessed”). To this day it is prayed every morning in many churches.
> Redeemed: set free, rescued.
> Verse 69: “A horn of salvation.” In the Bible the horn is a picture of power and strength (compare Psalm 18:2; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 18:3).
> Zacharias praises God, who is faithful to his people Israel and keeps his covenant with Abraham.
> Verses 76–79: Zacharias speaks to his little son: You will prepare the way for the Lord. “The dawn from on high” means the Messiah.
> Verse 79: “The way of peace”: God’s salvation leads to peace.
> Verse 80: John lives in the desert until he is grown up and appears in public (Chapter 3).
