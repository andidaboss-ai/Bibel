# Luke – Chapter 21
### Be watchful

---

## The poor widow’s gift (Verses 1–4)

[1] Jesus looked up
and saw the rich people
who were putting their gifts into the treasury.
[2] He also saw a poor widow.
She put in two small brass coins.
[3] He said,
“Truly I tell you,
this poor widow put in more
than all of them.
[4] For all these gave gifts for God
from their abundance.
But she, out of her poverty,
put in all
that she had to live on.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the temple there were boxes for donations. The treasury: the place where these offering boxes stood. The two small coins were the smallest kind of coin. They were worth almost nothing.
> Jesus looks closely. For God what counts is not the size of the gift, but the heart. The widow gives everything.
> Just before this, Jesus warned against scribes: “They take away the houses of widows” (Luke 20:47). Some interpreters also hear a complaint in this: A system that makes poor widows give their last coins is not just.
> Important: No one should pressure people to give more than they can. Groups that take advantage of people financially act against God’s will.
> For today: Even those who have little can give a lot. And: We should see the poor as Jesus sees them.

## Jesus speaks about the destruction of the temple (Verses 5–19)

[5] Some were talking about the temple.
They said
how beautifully it was decorated with fine stones and gifts.
Then Jesus said,
[6] “The days will come
when of all these things which you see,
not one stone will be left on another.
Everything will be thrown down.”
[7] They asked him,
“Teacher, when will these things be?
And what is the sign
that these things are about to happen?”
[8] He said,
“Watch out
that you don’t get led astray!
For many will come in my name
and say,
‘I am he,’
and,
‘The time is at hand.’
Don’t follow them!
[9] When you hear of wars and disturbances,
don’t be terrified.
For these things must happen first.
But the end won’t come immediately.”
[10] Then he said to them,
“Nation will rise against nation,
and kingdom against kingdom.
[11] There will be great earthquakes,
and in various places famines and plagues.
There will be terrors
and great signs from heaven.
[12] But before all these things,
they will arrest you and persecute you.
They will hand you over to synagogues
and to prisons.
They will bring you before kings and governors
for my name’s sake.
[13] It will turn out as a testimony for you.
[14] So settle it in your hearts
not to worry beforehand
about how to answer.
[15] For I will give you words and wisdom.
None of your opponents will be able
to stand against them
or contradict them.
[16] You will be handed over even by parents, brothers, relatives, and friends.
And they will have some of you put to death.
[17] You will be hated by everyone
for my name’s sake.
[18] But not a hair of your head will perish.
[19] By your endurance
you will win your lives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple in Jerusalem was a huge, magnificent building. Herod the Great had it splendidly enlarged. In the year AD 70 the Romans destroyed it.
> Important: For the Jewish people the destruction of the temple was a terrible disaster. To this day Jews remember it with grief, on the fast day of Tisha B’Av. Christians should respect this grief.
> Verses 8–9: Jesus warns: Don’t follow false saviors. And don’t let wars and disasters throw you into panic. They are not a sign that the end is coming right away.
> Important: Groups that bind people to themselves through fear of the end of the world misuse these words. Advice is available, for example, from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.
> Verse 12: The first Christians were persecuted. The Book of Acts tells about this. At that time the first Christians were themselves still Jews. These were conflicts within the Jewish people and with the Roman authorities.
> Important: Later, for many centuries, Christians persecuted Jews. This text must never be used to put the blame on Jews.
> Verses 18–19: “Not a hair of your head will perish”: Even if disciples suffer or die, they are safe with God.
> For today: Today too, Christians are persecuted in some countries, just like people of other religions. Freedom of religion is a human right for everyone.

## The siege of Jerusalem (Verses 20–24)

[20] “But when you see
Jerusalem surrounded by armies,
then know
that its destruction is at hand.
[21] Then let those who are in Judea
flee to the mountains.
Let those who are in the city
leave it.
And let those who are in the country
not go into the city.
[22] For these are days of vengeance,
so that all things which are written
may be fulfilled.
[23] Woe to those who are pregnant
and to those
who nurse infants in those days!
For there will be great distress in the land
and wrath to this people.
[24] They will fall by the edge of the sword
and will be led captive into all the nations.
And Jerusalem will be trampled down by the Gentiles
until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Luke describes very exactly what happened in the Jewish War: The Romans surrounded Jerusalem and conquered the city in the year AD 70. Many people died, many were made slaves.
> “Woe to those who are pregnant”: Jesus has compassion on those who suffer the most. It is a lament, not a curse.
> Important: The words “days of vengeance” and “wrath to this people” were terribly misused in church history. People said the Jews were being punished “rightly.” That is wrong and has contributed to hatred and persecution. Jesus speaks as a Jewish prophet about his own people, with tears (Luke 19:41). The prophets of Israel lamented in a similar way, for example Jeremiah.
> Verse 24: “Until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled”: This time has an end. God’s story with Jerusalem and with Israel goes on (compare Romans 11:25–26).
> “The Gentiles” here means the Romans.

## The coming of the Son of Man (Verses 25–28)

[25] “There will be signs in the sun, moon, and stars.
And on the earth the nations will be in anxiety,
confused by the roaring of the sea and the waves.
[26] People will faint for fear
and for expectation of the things
which are coming on the world.
For the powers of the heavens will be shaken.
[27] Then they will see the Son of Man
coming in a cloud
with power and great glory.
[28] But when these things begin to happen,
look up
and lift up your heads,
because your redemption is near.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus uses pictures from the books of the prophets (for example Isaiah 13:10; Joel 2:30–31, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 3:3–4; Daniel 7:13–14). They describe God making the whole world new.
> These pictures are not exact predictions. They want to show: In the end God comes, and evil does not have the last word.
> Verse 28: While others are afraid, the disciples are to look up. For them the coming of Jesus is not a threat, but freedom. Redemption: being set free.
> For today: Whoever is afraid of the future may lift up their head. God holds the world in his hand.

## The parable of the fig tree (Verses 29–33)

[29] He told them a parable:
“Look at the fig tree
and all the trees.
[30] When they are already budding,
you see it
and know by yourselves
that the summer is already near.
[31] In the same way, you also,
when you see these things happening,
know that God’s Kingdom is near.
[32] Most certainly I tell you,
this generation will not pass away
until all things are accomplished.
[33] Heaven and earth will pass away,
but my words will by no means pass away.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Israel many trees lose their leaves in winter. When they get new leaves, you know: Summer is coming soon.
> Verse 32 is hard to understand. Some interpreters connect “this generation” with the destruction of Jerusalem, which happened while many of the listeners were still alive. Others understand it as “humanity” or “this kind of people.” What is certain: Jesus wants us to be ready at all times.
> Verse 33: Jesus’ words remain valid, even when everything else passes away.

## Be watchful! (Verses 34–38)

[34] “So be careful!
Don’t let your hearts be loaded down
with wild partying, drunkenness,
and the worries of this life.
Otherwise that day will come on you suddenly,
[35] like a trap.
For it will come on all those
who live on the face of the whole earth.
[36] Therefore be watchful all the time and pray,
so that you may be counted worthy
to escape all these things
that will happen,
and to stand before the Son of Man.”
[37] Every day Jesus was teaching in the temple.
Every night he would go out
and spend the night on the mountain
that is called Olivet.
[38] And all the people came early in the morning to him in the temple
to hear him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 34: Not only partying and alcohol, but also everyday worries can make the heart “loaded down.” Then you forget what really matters.
> Important: Whoever struggles with alcohol or other addictions is not a bad person. Addiction is an illness. Help is available from addiction counseling services and self-help groups. In the US, call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. In the UK, call Frank at 0300 123 6600.
> Verse 36: “Be watchful all the time and pray”: That is the attitude Jesus expects from his disciples.
> Verses 37–38: Luke shows once more: All the people come early in the morning to hear Jesus. The Jewish people love Jesus and listen to him.
