# Luke – Chapter 21
### Be watchful

---

## The poor widow’s gift (Verses 1–4)

[1] Jesus looked up.
He saw rich people putting their gifts
into the offering boxes in the temple.
[2] He also saw a poor widow.
She put in two small brass coins.
[3] Jesus said,
“I tell you the truth:
This poor widow has put in more than all the others.
[4] All these people gave gifts to God
from what they had left over.
But she is poor,
and she put in everything she had to live on.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the temple there were boxes for donations. The treasury: the place where these offering boxes stood. The two small coins were the smallest kind of coin. They were worth almost nothing.
> Jesus looks closely. For God what counts is not the size of the gift, but the heart. The widow gives everything.
> Just before this, Jesus warned against scribes: “They take away the houses of widows” (Luke 20:47). Some interpreters also hear a complaint in this: A system that makes poor widows give their last coins is not just.
> Important: No one should pressure people to give more than they can. Groups that take advantage of people financially act against God’s will.
> For today: Even those who have little can give a lot. And: We should see the poor as Jesus sees them.

## Jesus speaks about the destruction of the temple (Verses 5–19)

[5] Some people were talking about the temple.
They talked about how beautiful it was,
with its fine stones and the gifts given to God.
Then Jesus said,
[6] “You see all this.
But the time will come
when not one stone will be left on top of another.
Every stone will be thrown down.”
[7] They asked him,
“Teacher, when will this happen?
And what will be the sign
that it is about to happen?”
[8] Jesus said,
“Be careful that no one fools you.
Many will come and use my name.
They will say, ‘I am he!’
And they will say, ‘The time has come!’
Don’t follow them.
[9] You will hear about wars and riots.
Don’t be afraid.
These things must happen first.
But the end will not come right away.”
[10] Then Jesus said to them,
“One nation will fight against another nation.
One kingdom will fight against another kingdom.
[11] There will be great earthquakes.
In many places there will be famines and diseases.
Terrible things will happen,
and there will be great signs from heaven.
[12] But before all this happens,
people will arrest you and persecute you.
They will hand you over to the synagogues
and put you in prison.
They will bring you before kings and governors
because of my name.
[13] This will be your chance to tell them about me.
[14] So make up your minds
not to worry ahead of time
about how you will defend yourselves.
[15] I will give you words and wisdom.
None of your enemies will be able to stand against them
or prove them wrong.
[16] Even your parents, brothers, relatives, and friends
will hand you over.
They will have some of you killed.
[17] Everyone will hate you
because of my name.
[18] But not one hair on your head will be lost.
[19] If you stay strong and do not give up,
you will win your lives.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple in Jerusalem was a huge, magnificent building. Herod the Great had it splendidly enlarged. In the year AD 70 the Romans destroyed it.
> Important: For the Jewish people the destruction of the temple was a terrible disaster. To this day Jews remember it with grief, on the fast day of Tisha B’Av. Christians should respect this grief.
> Verses 8–9: Jesus warns: Don’t follow false saviors. And don’t let wars and disasters throw you into panic. They are not a sign that the end is coming right away.
> Important: Groups that bind people to themselves through fear of the end of the world misuse these words. Advice is available, for example, from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.
> Verse 12: The first Christians were persecuted. The Book of Acts tells about this. At that time the first Christians were themselves still Jews. These were conflicts within the Jewish people and with the Roman authorities.
> Important: Later, for many centuries, Christians persecuted Jews. This text must never be used to put the blame on Jews.
> Verses 18–19: “Not one hair on your head will be lost”: Even if disciples suffer or die, they are safe with God.
> For today: Today too, Christians are persecuted in some countries, just like people of other religions. Freedom of religion is a human right for everyone.

## The siege of Jerusalem (Verses 20–24)

[20] “You will see armies all around Jerusalem.
Then you will know
that the time has come for it to be destroyed.
[21] Then the people in Judea must run away to the mountains.
The people in the city must get out.
The people in the country must not go into the city.
[22] For these are the days of punishment.
Then everything that is written will come true.
[23] How terrible it will be in those days for women who are pregnant!
How terrible for mothers who are nursing babies!
For there will be great trouble in the land.
God’s anger will come on this people.
[24] They will be killed by the sword.
They will be taken away as prisoners to all the nations.
And the Gentiles will trample on Jerusalem
until the times of the Gentiles are over.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Luke describes very exactly what happened in the Jewish War: The Romans surrounded Jerusalem and conquered the city in the year AD 70. Many people died, many were made slaves.
> “How terrible it will be in those days for women who are pregnant!”: Jesus has compassion on those who suffer the most. It is a lament, not a curse.
> Important: The words “days of punishment” and “God’s anger will come on this people” (in older Bibles: “days of vengeance” and “wrath to this people”) were terribly misused in church history. People said the Jews were being punished “rightly.” That is wrong and has contributed to hatred and persecution. Jesus speaks as a Jewish prophet about his own people, with tears (Luke 19:41). The prophets of Israel lamented in a similar way, for example Jeremiah.
> Verse 24: “Until the times of the Gentiles are over”: This time has an end. God’s story with Jerusalem and with Israel goes on (compare Romans 11:25–26).
> “The Gentiles” here means the Romans.

## The coming of the Son of Man (Verses 25–28)

[25] “There will be signs in the sun, the moon, and the stars.
On the earth the nations will be full of fear.
They will not know what to do
because of the roaring of the sea and the waves.
[26] People will faint with fear.
They will be afraid of what is going to happen to the world.
For the powers in the heavens will be shaken.
[27] Then people will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud
with power and great glory.
[28] When these things begin to happen,
stand up straight and lift up your heads!
For soon you will be set free.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus uses pictures from the books of the prophets (for example Isaiah 13:10; Joel 2:30–31, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 3:3–4; Daniel 7:13–14). They describe God making the whole world new.
> These pictures are not exact predictions. They want to show: In the end God comes, and evil does not have the last word.
> Verse 28: While others are afraid, the disciples are to look up. For them the coming of Jesus is not a threat, but freedom. Older Bibles call this “redemption”: being set free.
> For today: Whoever is afraid of the future may lift up their head. God holds the world in his hand.

## The parable of the fig tree (Verses 29–33)

[29] Then Jesus told them a parable.
“Look at the fig tree and all the other trees.
[30] When they begin to grow new leaves,
you see it.
And you know by yourselves
that summer is near.
[31] It is the same with you.
When you see these things happening,
you know that God’s Kingdom is near.
[32] I tell you for certain:
This generation will not pass away
before all these things happen.
[33] Heaven and earth will pass away.
But my words will never pass away.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Israel many trees lose their leaves in winter. When they get new leaves, you know: Summer is coming soon.
> Verse 32 is hard to understand. Some interpreters connect “this generation” with the destruction of Jerusalem, which happened while many of the listeners were still alive. Others understand it as “humanity” or “this kind of people.” What is certain: Jesus wants us to be ready at all times.
> Verse 33: Jesus’ words remain valid, even when everything else passes away.

## Be watchful! (Verses 34–38)

[34] “Be careful!
Don’t let your hearts be loaded down
with wild partying and getting drunk,
and with the worries of this life.
Otherwise that day may come on you suddenly,
[35] like a trap.
For it will come on everyone
who lives on the whole earth.
[36] So be watchful all the time and pray.
Pray that God will find you worthy
to escape all these things that are going to happen.
And pray that you will be able to stand before the Son of Man.”
[37] Every day Jesus taught in the temple.
Every night he went out
and stayed on the mountain called Olivet.
[38] Early every morning all the people came to the temple
to listen to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 34: Not only partying and alcohol, but also everyday worries can make the heart “loaded down.” Then you forget what really matters.
> Important: Whoever struggles with alcohol or other addictions is not a bad person. Addiction is an illness. Help is available from addiction counseling services and self-help groups. In the US, call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. In the UK, call Frank at 0300 123 6600.
> Verse 36: “Be watchful all the time and pray”: That is the attitude Jesus expects from his disciples.
> Verses 37–38: Luke shows once more: All the people come early in the morning to hear Jesus. The Jewish people love Jesus and listen to him.
