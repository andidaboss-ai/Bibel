# Luke – Chapter 5
### People who leave everything

---

## The great catch of fish (Verses 1–11)

[1] One day Jesus was standing by the lake of Gennesaret.
A crowd of people was pushing in around him.
They wanted to hear the word of God.
[2] Jesus saw two boats at the shore of the lake.
The fishermen had gotten out of them.
They were washing their nets.
[3] Jesus got into one of the boats.
It belonged to Simon.
Jesus asked him to go out a little way from the shore.
Then Jesus sat down in the boat
and taught the crowds.
[4] When he had finished speaking,
he said to Simon,
“Go out into the deep water.
Let down your nets to catch some fish.”
[5] Simon answered him,
“Master, we worked hard all night
and caught nothing.
But because you say so,
I will let down the nets.”
[6] They did it.
And they caught so many fish
that their nets began to tear.
[7] They waved to their partners in the other boat
to come and help them.
The partners came.
They filled both boats with fish,
so full that the boats began to sink.
[8] When Simon Peter saw this,
he fell down at Jesus’ knees.
He said,
“Go away from me, Lord!
I am a sinful man.”
[9] Simon was amazed at the great catch of fish.
All the men with him were amazed too.
[10] James and John were amazed as well.
They were the sons of Zebedee
and Simon’s partners.
Jesus said to Simon,
“Don’t be afraid.
From now on you will be catching people alive.”
[11] They brought their boats to the shore.
They left everything
and followed Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> The lake of Gennesaret is another name for the Sea of Galilee.
> Simon (Peter) is an experienced fisherman. Fish were usually caught at night. Fishing in deep water during the day was unusual. Even so, Simon trusts Jesus’ word.
> Verse 8: Simon is frightened. He senses how holy Jesus is, and how small he himself is. The prophet Isaiah felt something similar (Isaiah 6:5).
> Verse 10: “Don’t be afraid.” Jesus does not send Simon away, but calls him. The WEB says “catching people alive.” The Greek word means “to catch alive”: to win people for life.
> Only Luke tells about this great catch of fish when the disciples are called. A similar story is in John 21:1–11, after the resurrection.
> For today: Even someone who feels unworthy can be called by Jesus.

## Jesus heals a man with leprosy (Verses 12–16)

[12] One time Jesus was in one of the towns.
There was a man there whose body was full of leprosy.
When he saw Jesus,
he bowed down with his face to the ground.
He begged him,
“Lord, if you want to,
you can make me clean.”
[13] Jesus reached out his hand
and touched him.
He said,
“I want to. Be clean!”
At once the leprosy left the man.
[14] Jesus ordered him not to tell anyone.
He said,
“Go and show yourself to the priest.
Bring the offering for your cleansing,
as Moses commanded.
That will show them that you are healed.”
[15] But the news about Jesus spread even more.
Great crowds came together.
They wanted to hear him
and to be healed of their sicknesses.
[16] But Jesus went away to lonely places
and prayed.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Full of leprosy”: Luke stresses how seriously ill the man was. “Leprosy” was a name for various skin diseases. Anyone who had it had to live apart from other people (Leviticus 13:45–46).
> Jesus touches him. Otherwise no one did that. He gives him health and a place in the community again.
> Verse 14: Jesus sends him to the priest, as the law of Moses requires (Leviticus 14). Jesus respects the law.
> Verse 16: In Luke, Jesus often prays, especially when a lot is going on.
> For today: No one should be shut out because of an illness.

## Jesus heals a paralyzed man (Verses 17–26)

[17] One day Jesus was teaching.
Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there.
They had come from every village in Galilee and Judea,
and from Jerusalem.
The power of the Lord was with Jesus,
so that he could heal people.
[18] Some men came carrying a man on a mat.
The man was paralyzed.
That means: He could not move his body.
The men wanted to bring him in
and lay him down in front of Jesus.
[19] But there were too many people.
They could not find a way to bring him in.
So they went up on the roof.
They let the man down on his mat through the tiles,
into the middle of the crowd, right in front of Jesus.
[20] Jesus saw their faith.
He said to the man,
“Friend, your sins are forgiven.”
[21] The scribes and the Pharisees began to think,
“Who is this man?
He is insulting God!
Only God can forgive sins!”
[22] But Jesus knew what they were thinking.
He answered them,
“Why are you thinking these things in your hearts?
[23] Which is easier to say:
‘Your sins are forgiven,’
or ‘Get up and walk’?
[24] I want you to know
that the Son of Man has authority on earth
to forgive sins.”
Then he said to the paralyzed man,
“I tell you,
get up, pick up your mat,
and go home!”
[25] At once the man got up in front of them.
He picked up the mat he had been lying on.
He went home and praised God.
[26] Everyone was amazed,
and they praised God.
They were filled with fear.
They said,
“We have seen amazing things today.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Luke speaks of “tiles.” He describes the houses the way his readers knew them. In Galilee houses usually had roofs made of clay (compare Mark 2:4).
> The friends don’t give up. Jesus sees “their faith.” The trust of other people can also help someone.
> Verse 20: Jesus speaks of forgiveness first. That does not mean the paralysis was a punishment. Illness and disability are not punishments from God.
> The scribes think Jesus is blaspheming. Blasphemy means: words that insult God.
> Verse 21: The scribes are right that only God can forgive sins. That is why what Jesus does is so astonishing. Their question is meant seriously.
> Verse 25: The healed man and everyone who sees it praise God. In Luke people often praise God when he does great things.

## Jesus calls Levi (Verses 27–32)

[27] After this Jesus went out.
He saw a tax collector named Levi.
Levi was sitting at the tax office.
Jesus said to him,
“Follow me!”
[28] Levi got up.
He left everything
and followed Jesus.
[29] Levi gave a great feast for Jesus in his house.
A large crowd of tax collectors and other people
were eating with them.
[30] The Pharisees and their scribes
complained to the disciples of Jesus.
They said,
“Why do you eat and drink
with tax collectors and sinners?”
[31] Jesus answered them,
“Healthy people do not need a doctor.
Sick people do.
[32] I have not come to call good people.
I have come to call sinners,
so that they turn away from their sins.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Tax collectors collected customs and taxes. Many of them demanded too much. That is why they were disliked and counted as sinners.
> Levi leaves “everything.” In Luke, letting go of possessions is very important.
> Eating together meant: We belong together. Jesus eats with people whom others avoid.
> Verse 30: The Pharisees wanted to keep God’s commandments very carefully, including about food. Their question is meant seriously.
> Verse 32: Only Luke adds that sinners are to turn away from their sins (“to repentance”). Jesus accepts sinners so that they can begin a new life.
> For today: The church should be a place for everyone, especially for those who feel shut out.

## The question about fasting (Verses 33–39)

[33] Some people said to Jesus,
“John’s disciples often fast and pray.
The disciples of the Pharisees do the same.
But your disciples eat and drink.”
[34] Jesus said to them,
“The friends of the bridegroom are with him at the wedding.
Can you make them fast while he is with them?
[35] But the time will come
when the bridegroom will be taken away from them.
In those days they will fast.”
[36] Jesus also told them a parable.
“No one tears a piece from new clothes
to patch old clothes.
If he does, he ruins the new clothes.
And the new piece will not match the old clothes.
[37] No one pours new wine into old wineskins.
If he does, the new wine will burst the skins.
The wine will run out,
and the skins will be ruined.
[38] New wine must be poured into new wineskins.
Then both are kept safe.
[39] And no one who has drunk old wine
wants new wine right away.
He says,
‘The old is better.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Fasting means: going without food for a time in order to focus completely on God. Devout Jews often fasted on certain days.
> Jesus compares his time to a wedding. At a wedding people celebrate. Jesus is the “bridegroom.”
> Verse 35: Jesus hints at his death. Then there will be times of fasting again.
> Wineskins: bags made of animal skin for keeping wine. Old ones are no longer stretchy and can burst.
> Verses 36–38: Jesus’ message is so new that it needs new forms.
> Only Luke tells verse 39. With a smile he shows: Many people prefer what they are used to. New things have a hard time. That is human.
> Important: The parable is not a judgment that Judaism is “old and bad.” Jesus himself lived as a Jew.
> Verse 39: Instead of “The old is better,” some manuscripts say that the old is good. A few manuscripts leave out the whole of verse 39.
