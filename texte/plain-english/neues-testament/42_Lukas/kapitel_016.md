# Luke – Chapter 16
### God or money

---

## The clever manager (Verses 1–13)

[1] Jesus also said to his disciples,
“There was a rich man who had a manager.
People told the rich man
that his manager was wasting his money and property.
[2] So the rich man called the manager and said to him,
‘What is this I hear about you?
Show me the records of your work.
You cannot be my manager any longer.’
[3] The manager thought to himself,
‘What shall I do now?
My master is taking my job away from me.
I am not strong enough to dig.
And I am ashamed to beg.
[4] I know what I will do!
Then people will welcome me into their homes
when I lose my job.’
[5] So the manager called in each person
who owed his master something.
He asked the first one,
‘How much do you owe my master?’
[6] The man said,
‘A hundred batos of oil.’
The manager said to him,
‘Here is your bill.
Sit down quickly and write fifty.’
[7] Then he asked another one,
‘How much do you owe?’
The man said,
‘A hundred cors of wheat.’
The manager said to him,
‘Here is your bill.
Write eighty.’
[8] The master praised the dishonest manager,
because he had acted cleverly.
The children of this world
are more clever with people like themselves
than the children of the light are.
[9] I tell you,
use unjust mammon to make friends for yourselves.
Then, when you fail,
your friends will welcome you into the eternal homes.
[10] Whoever can be trusted with very little
can also be trusted with much.
Whoever is dishonest with very little
is also dishonest with much.
[11] You may not have been faithful with unjust mammon.
Then who will trust you with the true riches?
[12] You may not have been faithful with what belongs to someone else.
Then who will give you what belongs to you?
[13] No servant can serve two masters.
He will hate one and love the other.
Or he will be loyal to one and look down on the other.
You cannot serve both God and Mammon.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This parable is hard to understand. Jesus does not praise cheating. He praises the manager for thinking cleverly about his future.
> Verses 6–7: The WEB names “batos” and “cors.” These were units of measure. A batos of oil was several gallons, a cor of wheat was a large amount, many pounds. These are big quantities.
> Some interpreters think: Perhaps the manager crossed out his own share, or hidden interest. Taking interest from fellow Jews was forbidden by the law (Deuteronomy 23:19; in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 23:20). But this is not certain.
> Verse 8: The “children of this world” are people who think only of this world. The “children of the light” are those who belong to God. Jesus says: Worldly people are often more determined than devout people.
> Verse 9: “Mammon” is an Aramaic word for money and possessions. Jesus calls it “unjust” because money is often tied up with injustice. Money should be used to do good and help others.
> Verse 13: Money can become like a god that you serve. Jesus says: Decide. God should come first.
> For today: Money is not evil. But it must not rule my life. Whoever has fallen into financial trouble or debt can find help from nonprofit debt counseling services.

## The law and God’s Kingdom (Verses 14–18)

[14] The Pharisees heard all these things.
They loved money.
So they made fun of Jesus.
[15] Jesus said to them,
“You try to make yourselves look good in front of people.
But God knows your hearts.
What people think is very important
is disgusting to God.
[16] The law and the prophets were until John.
Since that time the Good News of God’s Kingdom is told,
and everyone is forcing his way into it.
[17] But it is easier for heaven and earth to disappear
than for the smallest stroke of a pen in the law to drop out.
[18] Any man who divorces his wife
and marries another woman
commits adultery.
And a man who marries a woman
who has been divorced by her husband
commits adultery.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Here Luke says of certain Pharisees that they loved money.
> Important: This is not a judgment on all Pharisees or on the Jewish people. Many Pharisees lived simple, devout lives. The love of money is a danger for all people, including Christians and churches.
> Verse 15: God does not look at the outside, but at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). Older Bibles say: an “abomination” to God, something God finds disgusting.
> Verse 16: With John the Baptizer a new time begins. “Everyone is forcing his way into it” is understood in different ways: Many people badly want to get into God’s Kingdom. Or: You have to try with all your strength.
> Verse 17: God’s law stays valid. Jesus does not cancel the Torah. That is important for the relationship between Christians and Jews.
> Verse 18: Back then a man could easily divorce his wife. The woman was then often left without protection or support. With these words Jesus especially protects women. Adultery means breaking the faithfulness of a marriage.
> Today the churches deal with divorce in different ways. The Catholic Church holds that a marriage cannot be dissolved, but it has a process for declaring a marriage null. Protestant and Orthodox churches allow a new marriage under certain circumstances.
> Important: No one has to stay in a marriage where they experience violence. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).

## The rich man and poor Lazarus (Verses 19–31)

[19] “There was a rich man.
He wore purple clothes and fine linen.
Every day he lived in luxury.
[20] A poor man named Lazarus
was laid at the rich man’s gate.
Lazarus was a beggar.
His body was full of sores.
[21] He wanted so much to eat
the crumbs that fell from the rich man’s table.
Even the dogs came and licked his sores.
[22] Then the beggar died.
The angels carried him to Abraham’s side.
The rich man also died
and was buried.
[23] In Hades the rich man was in great pain.
He looked up
and saw Abraham far away,
with Lazarus at his side.
[24] He called out,
‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me!
Send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water
and cool my tongue.
I am suffering terribly in this fire!’
[25] But Abraham said,
‘My son, remember:
During your life you received good things.
And in the same way, Lazarus received bad things.
But now he is comforted here,
and you are suffering.
[26] And besides all this,
there is a deep valley between us and you.
No one can cross it.
People who want to go from here to you cannot do it.
And no one can come over from there to us.’
[27] The rich man said,
‘Then I beg you, father,
send Lazarus to my father’s house.
[28] I have five brothers.
He should warn them,
so that they will not also come to this place of pain.’
[29] But Abraham said to him,
‘They have Moses and the prophets.
They should listen to them.’
[30] The rich man said,
‘No, father Abraham!
But if someone comes to them from the dead,
then they will turn away from their sins.’
[31] Abraham said to him,
‘If they don’t listen to Moses and the prophets,
they will not be persuaded
even if someone rises from the dead.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells this story. The poor man has a name: Lazarus. It means “God helps.” The rich man has no name.
> Purple was very expensive. Only kings and very rich people wore purple.
> Verse 21: Dogs counted as unclean. The poor man is so weak that he cannot defend himself against them. Sores: open wounds on the skin.
> The rich man does nothing bad to Lazarus. But he overlooks him every day, right at his door. That is his guilt.
> “Abraham’s side” (in older Bibles: Abraham’s bosom) is a picture of the place of honor at God’s feast, very close to Abraham.
> Verse 23: “Hades” is the place of the dead. Here Jesus uses pictures that people back then knew. The parable does not want to describe exactly what it is like after death. It wants to warn: How I live now matters.
> Verses 29–31: “Moses and the prophets” are the Holy Scriptures of Israel. They already say everything you need to know about justice and the poor (for example Deuteronomy 15:7–11).
> Verse 31: Christians think of Jesus’ resurrection here. Even that does not convince everyone.
> For today: Who is lying at my door? Whom do I overlook? Poverty is not far away; it is often very close.
