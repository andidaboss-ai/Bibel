# Luke – Chapter 16
### God or money

---

## The clever manager (Verses 1–13)

[1] Jesus also said to his disciples,
“There was a rich man who had a manager.
Someone accused the manager
of wasting his master’s possessions.
[2] The master called him
and said to him,
‘What is this that I hear about you?
Give an accounting of your management.
For you can no longer be manager.’
[3] The manager thought to himself,
‘What will I do?
My lord is taking the management position away from me.
I don’t have the strength to dig.
I am ashamed to beg.
[4] I know what I will do,
so that when I am removed from management,
people will welcome me into their houses.’
[5] He called each one of his lord’s debtors to him.
He said to the first,
‘How much do you owe my lord?’
[6] He said,
‘A hundred batos of oil.’
The manager said to him,
‘Take your bill,
sit down quickly,
and write fifty.’
[7] Then he said to another,
‘How much do you owe?’
He said,
‘A hundred cors of wheat.’
He said to him,
‘Take your bill,
and write eighty.’
[8] His lord praised the dishonest manager
because he had acted wisely.
For the children of this world are,
in their own generation, wiser
than the children of the light.
[9] I tell you,
make for yourselves friends
by means of unrighteous mammon,
so that when you fail,
they may receive you into the eternal tents.
[10] He who is faithful in a very little
is faithful also in much.
He who is dishonest in a very little
is also dishonest in much.
[11] If then you have not been faithful with unrighteous mammon,
who will trust you with the true riches?
[12] If you have not been faithful
with what belongs to someone else,
who will give you
what is your own?
[13] No servant can serve two masters.
Either he will hate the one
and love the other,
or he will hold to one
and despise the other.
You aren’t able to serve God
and Mammon.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This parable is hard to understand. Jesus does not praise cheating. He praises the manager for thinking cleverly about his future.
> Verses 6–7: The WEB names “batos” and “cors.” These were units of measure. A batos of oil was several gallons, a cor of wheat was a large amount, many pounds. These are big quantities.
> Some interpreters think: Perhaps the manager crossed out his own share, or hidden interest. Taking interest from fellow Jews was forbidden by the law (Deuteronomy 23:19; in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 23:20). But this is not certain.
> Verse 8: The “children of this world” are people who think only of this world. The “children of the light” are those who belong to God. Jesus says: Worldly people are often more determined than devout people.
> Verse 9: “Mammon” is an Aramaic word for money and possessions. Jesus calls it “unrighteous” because money is often tied up with injustice. Money should be used to do good and help others.
> Verse 13: Money can become like a god that you serve. Jesus says: Decide. God should come first.
> For today: Money is not evil. But it must not rule my life. Whoever has fallen into financial trouble or debt can find help from nonprofit debt counseling services.

## The law and God’s Kingdom (Verses 14–18)

[14] The Pharisees, who were lovers of money,
also heard all these things,
and they mocked Jesus.
[15] He said to them,
“You are the ones
who justify yourselves in the sight of men.
But God knows your hearts.
For what is highly valued among people
is an abomination in the sight of God.
[16] The law and the prophets
were until John.
From that time the Good News of God’s Kingdom is preached,
and everyone is forcing his way into it.
[17] But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away
than for one tiny stroke of a pen in the law to fall.
[18] Everyone who divorces his wife
and marries another
commits adultery.
He who marries one
who is divorced from a husband
commits adultery.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Here Luke says of certain Pharisees that they loved money.
> Important: This is not a judgment on all Pharisees or on the Jewish people. Many Pharisees lived simple, devout lives. The love of money is a danger for all people, including Christians and churches.
> Verse 15: God does not look at the outside, but at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). An abomination is something God finds disgusting.
> Verse 16: With John the Baptizer a new time begins. “Everyone is forcing his way into it” is understood in different ways: Many people badly want to get into God’s Kingdom. Or: You have to try with all your strength.
> Verse 17: God’s law stays valid. Jesus does not cancel the Torah. That is important for the relationship between Christians and Jews.
> Verse 18: Back then a man could easily divorce his wife. The woman was then often left without protection or support. With these words Jesus especially protects women. Adultery means breaking the faithfulness of a marriage.
> Today the churches deal with divorce in different ways. The Catholic Church holds that a marriage cannot be dissolved, but it has a process for declaring a marriage null. Protestant and Orthodox churches allow a new marriage under certain circumstances.
> Important: No one has to stay in a marriage where they experience violence. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).

## The rich man and poor Lazarus (Verses 19–31)

[19] “There was a rich man.
He was dressed in purple and fine linen
and lived in luxury every day.
[20] A beggar named Lazarus
was laid at his gate.
He was full of sores.
[21] He longed to be fed
with the crumbs
that fell from the rich man’s table.
Yes, even the dogs came
and licked his sores.
[22] The beggar died.
He was carried away by the angels to Abraham’s bosom.
The rich man also died
and was buried.
[23] In Hades he was in torment.
He lifted up his eyes
and saw Abraham far off,
and Lazarus at his bosom.
[24] He cried out,
‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me!
Send Lazarus,
so that he may dip the tip of his finger in water
and cool my tongue!
For I am in anguish in this flame.’
[25] But Abraham said,
‘Son, remember:
In your lifetime you received your good things,
and Lazarus in the same way bad things.
But now he is comforted here,
and you are in anguish.
[26] Besides all this,
between us and you
there is a great gulf fixed.
Those who want to pass from here to you
are not able to.
And no one may cross over from there to us.’
[27] He said,
‘Then I ask you, father,
send him to my father’s house,
[28] for I have five brothers.
He should warn them,
so that they won’t also come into this place of torment.’
[29] But Abraham said to him,
‘They have Moses and the prophets.
Let them listen to them.’
[30] He said,
‘No, father Abraham!
But if one goes to them from the dead,
they will repent.’
[31] Abraham said to him,
‘If they don’t listen to Moses and the prophets,
neither will they be persuaded
if one rises from the dead.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells this story. The poor man has a name: Lazarus. It means “God helps.” The rich man has no name.
> Purple was very expensive. Only kings and very rich people wore purple.
> Verse 21: Dogs counted as unclean. The poor man is so weak that he cannot defend himself against them. Sores: open wounds on the skin.
> The rich man does nothing bad to Lazarus. But he overlooks him every day, right at his door. That is his guilt.
> “Abraham’s bosom” is a picture of the place of honor at God’s feast, very close to Abraham.
> Verse 23: “Hades” is the place of the dead. Here Jesus uses pictures that people back then knew. The parable does not want to describe exactly what it is like after death. It wants to warn: How I live now matters. Anguish: very great pain.
> Verses 29–31: “Moses and the prophets” are the Holy Scriptures of Israel. They already say everything you need to know about justice and the poor (for example Deuteronomy 15:7–11).
> Verse 31: Christians think of Jesus’ resurrection here. Even that does not convince everyone.
> For today: Who is lying at my door? Whom do I overlook? Poverty is not far away; it is often very close.
