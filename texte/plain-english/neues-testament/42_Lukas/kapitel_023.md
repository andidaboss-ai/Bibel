# Luke – Chapter 23
### “Father, forgive them”

---

> **An important note first**
> In the history of the church the story of Jesus’ suffering was often misused to insult Jews as “Christ killers.” This led to terrible persecution and violence. That is grave guilt.
> In 1965 the Catholic Church declared: What happened in Jesus’ suffering may not be blamed on all the Jews living at that time, nor on the Jews of today (*Nostra Aetate*). The Protestant churches have also clearly taken a stand against hostility toward Jews.
> Jesus was crucified by the Romans. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. Those involved were a few powerful people from the temple leadership together with the Roman governor Pilate. Jesus, his mother, the disciples and the women who wept for him were Jews.
> Christians believe: Jesus died for the sins of all people. No group of people may be made the guilty ones.

---

## Jesus before Pilate (Verses 1–7)

[1] The whole assembly rose up
and brought Jesus before Pilate.
[2] They began
to accuse him,
saying,
“We found
that this man is stirring up our nation.
He forbids
paying taxes to Caesar.
And he says
that he himself is Christ, a king.”
[3] Pilate asked him,
“Are you the King of the Jews?”
Jesus answered him,
“So you say.”
[4] Pilate said to the chief priests and the crowds,
“I find no basis for a charge
against this man.”
[5] But they insisted,
saying,
“He stirs up the people.
He teaches throughout all Judea,
beginning from Galilee even to this place.”
[6] When Pilate heard Galilee mentioned,
he asked
if the man was a Galilean.
[7] When he found out
that Jesus was under Herod’s rule,
he sent him to Herod.
Herod was also in Jerusalem during those days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea from about AD 26 to 36. Other ancient sources describe him as harsh and cruel.
> Verse 2: The charges are political: rebellion, refusing to pay taxes, claiming the title of king. The Romans dealt harshly with such things. The charge about the taxes is false (compare Luke 20:25).
> Verse 4: For the first time Pilate says that Jesus is innocent. Luke has him say this three times (verses 4, 14, 22).
> Verse 7: Herod Antipas ruled over Galilee. He was in Jerusalem for the Passover feast.

---

## Jesus before Herod (Verses 8–12)

[8] When Herod saw Jesus,
he was very glad.
For he had wanted to see him for a long time,
because he had heard many things about him.
He hoped
to see some miracle done by him.
[9] He asked him many questions.
But Jesus gave him no answers.
[10] The chief priests and the scribes stood there
and accused him fiercely.
[11] Herod and his soldiers
treated Jesus with contempt
and mocked him.
They dressed him in splendid clothing
and sent him back to Pilate.
[12] That very day Herod and Pilate became friends.
Before that they had been enemies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells that Jesus stood before Herod.
> Herod had had John the Baptizer killed. He is only curious. He wants to see a show. That is why Jesus is silent.
> Verse 11: The splendid clothing is mockery: They dress him up as a king.
> Verse 12: The powerful become friends, at the cost of an innocent man.

---

## Pilate condemns Jesus (Verses 13–25)

[13] Pilate called together the chief priests,
the rulers,
and the people.
[14] He said to them,
“You brought this man to me
as one who stirs up the people.
I have examined him in front of you.
I found no basis for a charge against this man
concerning those things
of which you accuse him.
[15] Neither has Herod,
for I sent you to him.
He has done nothing
worthy of death.
[16] So I will have him whipped
and release him.”
[17] For he had to release one prisoner to them
at the feast.
[18] But they all cried out together,
“Away with this man!
Release to us Barabbas!”
[19] Barabbas had been thrown into prison
for a revolt in the city
and for murder.
[20] Pilate spoke to them again,
because he wanted to release Jesus.
[21] But they shouted,
“Crucify! Crucify him!”
[22] He said to them the third time,
“Why?
What evil has this man done?
I have found nothing in him
worthy of death.
So I will have him whipped
and release him.”
[23] But they kept pressing him with loud voices,
asking
that he be crucified.
And their voices and the voices of the chief priests won.
[24] Pilate decided
that what they asked for should be done.
[25] He released the man
who had been thrown into prison for rebellion and murder,
the one they asked for.
But he handed Jesus over to their will.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: We do not know exactly who stood there and shouted. It was a crowd in Jerusalem, led by the temple leaders. Shortly before, “all the people” hung on Jesus’ every word (Luke 19:48; 21:38). And right afterward many people from the crowds mourn for Jesus (verses 27 and 48).
> Important: A crowd in one city on one morning is not “the Jewish people.” No one may conclude from this that all Jews are guilty.
> Verse 17: Many old manuscripts do not have this verse. The WEB has it. We know of such a custom, releasing a prisoner at the feast, only from the Gospels.
> Pilate thinks Jesus is innocent and has him crucified anyway. He had the power and bears the responsibility. A governor who condemns an innocent man to keep the peace acts in a cowardly and unjust way.
> Barabbas was a rebel against the Romans. The crowd chooses a violent man instead of Jesus, who brings peace.
> For today: Today too, innocent people are condemned because the powerful seek their own advantage. Christians should stand up for justice.

## The way to the cross (Verses 26–32)

[26] When they led Jesus away,
they grabbed a man named Simon.
He was from Cyrene
and was coming in from the country.
They laid the cross on him
to carry it behind Jesus.
[27] A great crowd of the people followed Jesus,
including women
who mourned and wept for him.
[28] Jesus turned to them
and said,
“Daughters of Jerusalem,
don’t weep for me,
but weep for yourselves
and for your children.
[29] For the days are coming
in which they will say,
‘Blessed are the barren,
the wombs that never bore,
and the breasts that never nursed.’
[30] Then they will begin
to tell the mountains,
‘Fall on us!’
and tell the hills,
‘Cover us.’
[31] For if they do these things in the green tree,
what will be done in the dry?”
[32] Two others,
two criminals,
were also led away with Jesus
to be put to death.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cyrene was a city in North Africa, in what is now Libya. Many Jews lived there. Simon is forced to carry the cross. He becomes a picture of everyone who follows Jesus (compare Luke 9:23).
> Verse 27: Many people from the crowds and many women weep for Jesus. Only Luke tells this. The Jewish people mourn for Jesus.
> Verses 28–31: Even on the way to his death Jesus thinks of others. He foresees the suffering that will come over Jerusalem in the war with the Romans. He speaks with compassion. Barren: unable to have children.
> Verse 30 is a reminder of Hosea 10:8.
> Verse 31: A proverb. It probably means: If even the innocent one has to suffer like this, how bad will it be in the hard time to come?

## Jesus is crucified (Verses 33–43)

[33] They came to the place
that is called “The Skull.”
There they crucified him
and the criminals,
one on the right
and the other on the left.
[34] Jesus said,
“Father, forgive them,
for they don’t know what they are doing.”
They divided his clothes among them
by casting lots.
[35] The people stood watching.
The rulers also mocked him,
saying,
“He saved others.
Let him save himself,
if this is the Christ of God,
his chosen one!”
[36] The soldiers also mocked him.
They came to him,
offered him vinegar,
[37] and said,
“If you are the King of the Jews,
save yourself!”
[38] There was also an inscription over him,
written in letters of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew:
“THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.”
[39] One of the criminals
who was hanging there
insulted him,
saying,
“If you are the Christ,
save yourself and us!”
[40] But the other rebuked him
and said,
“Don’t you even fear God?
You are under the same sentence.
[41] And we justly,
for we receive
what our deeds deserve.
But this man has done nothing wrong.”
[42] And he said to Jesus,
“Lord, remember me
when you come into your Kingdom.”
[43] Jesus said to him,
“Assuredly I tell you,
today you will be with me in Paradise.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The Skull” (in Latin “Calvary,” in Aramaic “Golgotha”) was a place of execution outside the city walls of Jerusalem.
> Crucifixion was one of the cruelest punishments of the Romans. It was meant for slaves and rebels.
> Verse 34: “Father, forgive them”: Jesus prays for those who are killing him. These words are missing in some old manuscripts. But they fit Jesus completely (Luke 6:27–28) and also Stephen (Acts 7:60).
> Important: If Jesus himself asks forgiveness for his executioners, then no Christian has the right to blame anyone for his death.
> Verse 34: The dividing of the clothes is a reminder of Psalm 22:18 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 22:19). Casting lots: a way of deciding by chance, like throwing dice.
> Verse 35: “The people stood watching.” Luke separates the people from the mockers.
> Verse 38: The words about the letters of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verses 39–43: Only Luke tells about the criminal who asks for mercy. He can no longer make anything right. But he trusts Jesus. And Jesus promises him: “today you will be with me in Paradise.”
> “Paradise” is a word for God’s garden, the place of fellowship with God.
> For today: It is never too late to turn to Jesus.

## Jesus dies (Verses 44–49)

[44] It was now about the sixth hour.
Darkness came over the whole land
until the ninth hour.
[45] The sun was darkened,
and the curtain of the temple was torn in two.
[46] Jesus cried with a loud voice,
“Father, into your hands
I commit my spirit!”
Having said this,
he breathed his last.
[47] The centurion saw
what had happened.
He praised God
and said,
“Certainly this was a righteous man.”
[48] And all the crowds
that had come together
to see this
saw what had happened.
They beat their chests
and returned home.
[49] All who knew Jesus
stood at a distance,
watching these things,
including the women
who had followed him from Galilee.

> **What does this mean?**
> The sixth hour is about 12 noon, the ninth hour about 3 in the afternoon.
> Verse 45: The curtain in the temple separated the Most Holy Place from the rest of the temple. Christians often understand its tearing like this: Through Jesus’ death the way to God is open for everyone.
> Verse 46: Jesus prays with words from Psalm 31:5 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 31:6). Devout Jews also prayed this prayer in the evening before going to sleep. Jesus dies trusting his Father.
> Verse 47: A Roman officer recognizes: Jesus was innocent and righteous. Centurion: a Roman officer in charge of about a hundred soldiers.
> Verse 48: The people from the crowds beat their chests, a sign of grief and regret. Once again Luke shows: The people mourn for Jesus.
> Verse 49: The women stay, even when it is dangerous. They will be the first witnesses of the resurrection.
> For today: Whoever is dying or has to say goodbye may pray like Jesus, with the words “into your hands I commit my spirit.”

## Jesus is buried (Verses 50–56)

[50] There was a man named Joseph.
He was a member of the council,
a good and righteous man.
[51] He had not agreed
with their decision and what they did.
He was from Arimathaea,
a city of the Jews,
and was waiting for God’s Kingdom.
[52] This man went to Pilate
and asked for Jesus’ body.
[53] He took it down from the cross,
wrapped it in a linen cloth,
and laid him in a tomb
that was cut in stone.
No one had ever been laid there.
[54] It was the day of the Preparation,
and the Sabbath was drawing near.
[55] The women
who had come with Jesus out of Galilee
followed after.
They saw the tomb
and how his body was laid.
[56] Then they went back
and prepared spices and ointments.
On the Sabbath they rested,
as the commandment says.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 50–51: Joseph of Arimathaea is a Jewish member of the council. He did not agree with what happened. He honors Jesus with a dignified burial. This shows: The council was not united.
> People who were crucified were often not buried. Joseph stands up for Jesus, even though that was dangerous.
> Verse 54: “The day of the Preparation” is the Friday on which everything is made ready for the Sabbath. The Sabbath begins on Friday evening at sunset.
> Verse 56: The women keep the Sabbath, as God’s law says (Exodus 20:8–11). They live as devout Jewish women. Ointments: fragrant oils for the body of the dead.
> For today: Burying a person with dignity and mourning for them is a work of love. People who are grieving can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
