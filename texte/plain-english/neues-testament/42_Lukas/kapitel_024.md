# Luke – Chapter 24
### He is alive!

---

## The empty tomb (Verses 1–12)

[1] On the first day of the week,
at early dawn,
the women and some others came to the tomb.
They brought the spices
which they had prepared.
[2] They found the stone rolled away from the tomb.
[3] They went in,
but they didn’t find the Lord Jesus’ body.
[4] They were very confused about this.
Then suddenly
two men stood by them
in dazzling clothing.
[5] The women were terrified
and bowed their faces down to the earth.
The men said to them,
“Why do you seek the living among the dead?
[6] He isn’t here,
but is risen.
Remember
what he told you
when he was still in Galilee.
[7] He said
that the Son of Man must be handed over into the hands of sinful men,
be crucified,
and on the third day rise again.”
[8] Then they remembered his words.
[9] They returned from the tomb
and told all these things to the eleven
and to all the rest.
[10] They were Mary Magdalene,
Joanna,
and Mary the mother of James.
The other women who were with them
also told these things to the apostles.
[11] But these words seemed like nonsense to the apostles,
and they didn’t believe them.
[12] But Peter got up
and ran to the tomb.
He bent down
and looked in.
He saw only the strips of linen lying there.
Then he went home,
wondering about
what had happened.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The first day of the week” is Sunday. That is why Christians hold their worship services on Sunday: It is the day of the resurrection. Easter is the most important feast of Christians.
> The women want to anoint the dead Jesus. They are not expecting a resurrection. But the tomb is empty.
> Verse 5: “Why do you seek the living among the dead?” That is the Easter message.
> The women are the first witnesses of the resurrection. Back then the testimony of women often counted for little in court. Even so, all the Gospels tell that women were the first at the empty tomb. That suggests that this is how it was handed down.
> Verse 11: The apostles do not believe the women. They think it is nonsense. That is told honestly.
> Verse 12 is missing in one old manuscript. Almost all others have it.
> For today: Whoever has doubts is in good company. Even the apostles could not believe it at first.

## The disciples on the road to Emmaus (Verses 13–35)

[13] That very day two of them
were going to a village named Emmaus.
It was sixty stadia from Jerusalem.
[14] They were talking with each other
about all these things which had happened.
[15] While they were talking and discussing,
Jesus himself came near
and went with them.
[16] But their eyes were kept
from recognizing him.
[17] He said to them,
“What are you talking about as you walk?
And why are you sad?”
[18] One of them,
named Cleopas,
answered him,
“Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem
who doesn’t know
the things which have happened there in these days?”
[19] He said to them,
“What things?”
They said to him,
“The things about Jesus the Nazarene.
He was a prophet,
mighty in deed and word
before God and all the people.
[20] The chief priests and our rulers
handed him over
to be condemned to death,
and crucified him.
[21] But we were hoping
that it was he
who would redeem Israel.
And besides all this,
it is now the third day
since these things happened.
[22] Also, some women of our group
amazed us.
They were at the tomb early in the morning.
[23] They didn’t find his body.
They came and said
that they had also seen a vision of angels,
who said
that he was alive.
[24] Some of us went to the tomb
and found it
just as the women had said.
But they didn’t see him.”
[25] He said to them,
“Foolish people!
How slow your hearts are
to believe all
that the prophets have spoken!
[26] Didn’t the Christ have to suffer these things
and enter into his glory?”
[27] Beginning from Moses and from all the prophets,
he explained to them
the things about himself in all the Scriptures.
[28] They came near the village
where they were going.
He acted
as if he would go further.
[29] But they urged him,
saying,
“Stay with us,
for it is almost evening,
and the day is almost over.”
So he went in
to stay with them.
[30] When he had sat down at the table with them,
he took the bread,
gave thanks,
broke it,
and gave it to them.
[31] Then their eyes were opened,
and they recognized him.
And he vanished out of their sight.
[32] They said to one another,
“Weren’t our hearts burning within us
while he spoke to us along the way
and opened the Scriptures to us?”
[33] That very hour they got up
and returned to Jerusalem.
There they found the eleven gathered together,
and those who were with them.
[34] They were saying,
“The Lord is risen indeed,
and has appeared to Simon!”
[35] And the two told
what had happened along the way,
and how they had recognized him
when he broke the bread.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Luke tells this story. It is one of the most beautiful in the Bible.
> Verse 13: Sixty stadia are about 7 miles (11 kilometers). Some manuscripts have one hundred sixty stadia. Where exactly Emmaus was is not certain.
> Verse 16: The two are so sad and disappointed that they do not recognize Jesus.
> Verse 18: Cleopas is one of the two. Luke does not give the name of the other. Some interpreters think it was his wife.
> Verse 20: The two disciples are Jews themselves. They speak of “our rulers.” What is meant is certain leaders, not the people. Important: No guilt of the Jews may be drawn from this.
> Verse 21: “We were hoping”: Their hope seems to have died. Many people know this feeling. Redeem: set free.
> Verse 27: Jesus explains the Holy Scriptures of Israel to them, the Old Testament. Without them you cannot understand Jesus.
> Verses 30–31: They recognize him when he breaks the bread, as at the Last Supper (Luke 22:19). To this day Christians experience this: In worship, in hearing the Scriptures and in the Lord’s Supper, Jesus is near.
> Verse 34: Jesus appeared to Simon Peter. Paul also speaks of this (1 Corinthians 15:5). Peter, who had denied him, is one of the first he meets.
> For today: Jesus walks with us, even when we do not recognize him. The request of the two, to stay with them because it is almost evening, is to this day a well-loved prayer and the theme of many hymns.

## Jesus appears to the disciples (Verses 36–49)

[36] While they were still talking about this,
Jesus himself stood among them
and said to them,
“Peace be to you.”
[37] But they were terrified
and filled with fear.
They thought
they were seeing a spirit.
[38] He said to them,
“Why are you troubled?
Why do doubts arise in your hearts?
[39] See my hands and my feet:
It is truly me.
Touch me and see.
For a spirit doesn’t have flesh and bones,
as you see that I have.”
[40] When he had said this,
he showed them his hands and his feet.
[41] They still couldn’t believe it for joy,
and they wondered.
Then he said to them,
“Do you have anything here to eat?”
[42] They gave him a piece of broiled fish
and some honeycomb.
[43] He took it
and ate in front of them.
[44] He said to them,
“This is what I told you
while I was still with you:
All things must be fulfilled
which are written about me
in the law of Moses,
the prophets,
and the psalms.”
[45] Then he opened their minds,
so that they might understand the Scriptures.
[46] He said to them,
“Thus it is written:
The Christ had to suffer
and to rise from the dead on the third day.
[47] And in his name
it must be preached to all the nations
that they should turn back to God
and that their sins are forgiven.
Begin at Jerusalem.
[48] You are witnesses of these things.
[49] I am sending on you
what my Father promised.
But wait in the city of Jerusalem
until you are clothed with power from on high.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 36: “Peace be to you” is the Jewish greeting “shalom.” It is still spoken in worship services today.
> Verses 39–43: Luke stresses: The risen Jesus is not a ghost. He has a body, he can be touched, he eats. And yet he is different: He appears and disappears. The resurrection is a mystery that goes beyond our understanding.
> Verse 40 is missing in one old manuscript. Verse 42: The words about the honeycomb are missing in most old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verse 44: The law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms: These are the three parts of the Holy Scriptures of Israel. Jesus reads the whole Bible of Israel as a witness to himself.
> Verse 47: The message is to go to all nations, but to begin in Jerusalem. The Book of Acts, the second part of Luke’s work, tells about this.
> Verse 49: What the Father promised is the Holy Spirit. He comes at Pentecost (Acts 2). “Clothed with power from on high” means: filled with God’s strength.

## Jesus is taken up into heaven (Verses 50–53)

[50] Jesus led them out as far as Bethany.
He lifted up his hands
and blessed them.
[51] While he blessed them,
he withdrew from them
and was carried up into heaven.
[52] They worshiped him
and returned to Jerusalem with great joy.
[53] They were continually in the temple,
praising and blessing God.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the Ascension of Jesus. Christians celebrate it forty days after Easter (compare Acts 1:3–11).
> Verses 50–51: The last thing the disciples see of Jesus: He blesses them. With this blessing they go into the future.
> Verses 51–52: The words about being carried up into heaven and about worshiping him are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> “Heaven” is not a place above the clouds. It means: Jesus is with God, in God’s reality. From there he is near to all people.
> Verse 52: The disciples are not sad, but full of joy.
> Verse 53: The Gospel ends where it began: in the temple in Jerusalem (Luke 1:8–9). The disciples keep praying in the temple as devout Jews.
> “Amen” means: So it is, or: So be it. It is missing in many old manuscripts.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Gospel of Luke?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A careful report:** Luke wanted “to trace everything carefully from the start” (1:3). He names emperors and rulers and places Jesus’ story within world history. He writes for Theophilus and for everyone who loves God.
> **Christmas:** In Luke we find the story of Mary’s yes, the birth in Bethlehem, the manger, the shepherds and the angels: “a Savior is born to you” (2:11). Also the songs of Mary, Zechariah and Simeon, which are still sung today.
> **Good news for the poor:** In Nazareth Jesus reads from Isaiah that God has anointed him “to preach good news to the poor” (4:18). Luke shows Jesus on the side of the poor, the sick, tax collectors, sinners, Samaritans and foreigners.
> **Women:** In Luke women play a big role: Mary and Elizabeth, the prophetess Anna, the women who travel with Jesus and support him, Martha and Mary, the widows. Women are the first witnesses of the resurrection.
> **Famous parables:** Only Luke tells of the Good Samaritan, the lost son, the rich farmer, the rich man and poor Lazarus, the Pharisee and the tax collector.
> **Prayer and the Holy Spirit:** In Luke Jesus prays before all the important moments. And he promises the Holy Spirit.
> **Mercy and forgiveness:** Zacchaeus, the woman who anoints Jesus’ feet, the criminal on the cross. And on the cross Jesus prays: “Father, forgive them” (23:34). The Son of Man came “to seek and to save” what was lost (19:10).
> **An important note:** Luke begins and ends in the temple in Jerusalem. Jesus, Mary, the disciples and the first Christians lived as Jews. Again and again Luke shows how “all the people” listen to Jesus and mourn for him. No text of this Gospel may be used against Jews.
> **For today:** Like the disciples on the road to Emmaus, we too can meet Jesus: when we hear the Holy Scriptures and share the bread.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the Gospel of John. It is different from the other three Gospels. It begins with the Word that was there in the beginning. John tells of great signs of Jesus and of long speeches in which Jesus says who he is: the bread of life, the light of the world, the good shepherd.
