# Luke – Chapter 2
### The birth of Jesus

---

## Jesus is born in Bethlehem (Verses 1–7)

[1] In those days an order went out from Caesar Augustus:
All the world should be enrolled.
[2] This was the first enrollment.
It took place when Quirinius was governor of Syria.
[3] Everyone went to be enrolled,
each to his own city.
[4] Joseph also went up
from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth,
into Judea, to David’s city,
which is called Bethlehem.
For he was of the house and family of David.
[5] He went to be enrolled
together with Mary,
who was engaged to him
and was expecting a child.
[6] While they were there,
the time came for her to give birth.
[7] She gave birth to her firstborn son.
She wrapped him in bands of cloth
and laid him in a feeding trough,
because there was no room for them in the inn.

> **What does this mean?**
> “All the world” here means the whole Roman Empire.
> Caesar Augustus ruled the Roman Empire from 27 BC to AD 14. An “enrollment” (a census) was mainly used to collect taxes.
> Census: counting and listing all the people in a country.
> Verse 2: Quirinius was governor of Syria from AD 6. Other sources tell of a census in that year. But Jesus was probably born before the death of Herod (Luke 1:5), that is, before 4 BC. These dates do not fit together exactly. Scholars explain this in different ways.
> Bethlehem is the city of King David (1 Samuel 16). The prophet Micah had announced that a ruler would come from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 5:1).
> “Firstborn” means: the first child. This does not yet say whether Mary had more children later. Christians understand this in different ways.
> Verse 7: The WEB says “inn.” The Greek word can also mean “guest room.” Perhaps there was no more room in the guest room of a house, and Mary had to give birth where the animals were kept.
> Feeding trough: a box that holds food for animals, also called a manger.
> The Son of God is not born in a palace, but in poverty, in a feeding trough.
> For today: God is with the poor, the homeless and those for whom there is no room.

## The shepherds and the angels (Verses 8–20)

[8] In the same area there were shepherds in the field.
They were keeping watch over their flock at night.
[9] Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood by them,
and the glory of the Lord shone around them.
They were terrified.
[10] The angel said to them,
“Don’t be afraid!
For I bring you good news of great joy
which will be to all the people.
[11] For today in David’s city
a Savior is born to you,
who is Christ the Lord.
[12] This is the sign to you:
You will find a baby
wrapped in strips of cloth,
lying in a feeding trough.”
[13] Suddenly there was with the angel
a great crowd of the heavenly army.
They were praising God and saying,
[14] “Glory to God in the highest,
on earth peace,
good will toward men.”
[15] When the angels had gone away from them into the sky,
the shepherds said to one another,
“Let’s go to Bethlehem now
and see this thing that has happened,
which the Lord has made known to us.”
[16] They hurried there
and found Mary and Joseph
and the baby
lying in the feeding trough.
[17] When they saw it,
they told everyone
what they had been told about this child.
[18] All who heard it
were amazed at the things
the shepherds told them.
[19] But Mary kept all these sayings
and pondered them in her heart.
[20] The shepherds went back.
They glorified and praised God
for all the things they had heard and seen.
It was all
just as they had been told.

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then shepherds were simple, poor people. They were often looked down on. They are the very ones who hear the good news first.
> Verse 11: The angel calls the child “Savior,” “Christ” (Messiah) and “Lord.” At that time the Roman emperor also claimed these titles for himself. Luke says: The true Savior is this poor child.
> Verse 14: The WEB says: “on earth peace, good will toward men.” Many old manuscripts have a different form here. Then it means: peace on earth among the people God loves.
> Verse 19: Mary thinks about it. She does not yet understand everything, but she keeps it in her heart. Pondered: thought about something deeply.
> This story is the heart of the Christmas celebration. Christians all over the world read it at Christmas.

## Jesus is presented in the temple (Verses 21–24)

[21] When eight days had passed
and the child was to be circumcised,
he was given the name Jesus.
The angel had given him this name
before he was conceived in the womb.
[22] When the days of their purification according to the law of Moses were over,
they brought him up to Jerusalem
to present him to the Lord.
[23] For it is written in the law of the Lord,
“Every male who opens the womb
shall be called holy to the Lord.”
[24] And they came to offer a sacrifice,
as it says in the law of the Lord,
“A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Like every Jewish boy, Jesus is circumcised on the eighth day (Genesis 17:12). His parents carefully keep the commandments of the law of Moses.
> Verse 22: According to the law of Moses, a woman offered a sacrifice in the temple 40 days after the birth of a son (Leviticus 12:2–8). Purification: becoming clean again according to religious rules. That was a religious custom. Important: Giving birth is nothing dirty or shameful. Women are not worth less after giving birth.
> Verse 23 reminds us of Exodus 13:2,12: The firstborn son belongs to God. The words about the male who opens the womb mean the first child born to a mother.
> Verse 24: Two doves or pigeons were the sacrifice for poor families. Those who had more money offered a lamb (Leviticus 12:8). So Mary and Joseph were poor.

## Simeon in the temple (Verses 25–35)

[25] There was a man in Jerusalem
whose name was Simeon.
He was righteous and devout
and was waiting for the comfort of Israel.
And the Holy Spirit was on him.
[26] The Holy Spirit had shown him
that he would not die
before he had seen the Lord’s Christ.
[27] Led by the Spirit,
he came into the temple.
When the parents brought in the child Jesus
to do for him what the law required,
[28] he took him into his arms,
praised God, and said,
[29] “Master, now you are letting your servant go in peace,
according to your word.
[30] For my eyes have seen your salvation,
[31] which you have prepared before all peoples:
[32] a light for revelation to the nations,
and the glory of your people Israel.”
[33] Joseph and his mother were amazed
at the things that were said about him.
[34] Simeon blessed them
and said to Mary, his mother,
“This child is appointed
for the falling and the rising of many in Israel.
He will be a sign
that people speak against.
[35] Yes, a sword will pierce through your own soul too.
So the thoughts of many hearts will be revealed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Simeon is an old, devout Jew. All his life he has been waiting for the Messiah.
> Verse 33: The WEB says “Joseph and his mother.” Many old manuscripts say: his father and his mother.
> Verses 29–32: His song is named after its Latin beginning: “Nunc dimittis” (“Now you are letting go”). To this day it is prayed in the evening in many churches.
> Verse 32: Jesus is a light for all nations and the glory of Israel. Both belong together. This reminds us of Isaiah 42:6 and 49:6.
> Verses 34–35: Simeon foretells: People will be divided over Jesus. And Mary will suffer great pain. Christians think of Mary standing at the cross.

## Anna in the temple (Verses 36–38)

[36] There was also a prophetess named Anna,
the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Asher.
She was very old.
After her wedding she had lived with her husband for seven years.
[37] Then she had been a widow
for about eighty-four years.
She did not leave the temple,
but worshiped God night and day with fasting and prayer.
[38] At that very hour she came up,
gave thanks to the Lord,
and spoke about the child to all
who were waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> Anna is a prophetess, a woman through whom God speaks. Luke often names women and men side by side: Simeon and Anna, Zacharias and Elizabeth.
> Verse 37: The sentence can also be understood to mean that Anna was 84 years old. The WEB understands it to mean that she had been a widow for about 84 years.
> Anna tells everyone about the child. She is one of the first women to spread the good news.
> For today: Old people are precious. Simeon and Anna are examples of faithful waiting and of a living hope in old age.

## Back to Nazareth (Verses 39–40)

[39] When they had done everything
that the law of the Lord required,
they went back to Galilee,
to their own city, Nazareth.
[40] The child grew and became strong in spirit.
He was filled with wisdom,
and the grace of God was upon him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus grows up in a devout Jewish family that keeps God’s commandments.
> Matthew also tells about the escape to Egypt (Matthew 2:13–23). Luke does not mention it. The Gospels tell about Jesus’ childhood in different ways.
> Verse 40: Jesus grows like every child, in body and spirit.

## Twelve-year-old Jesus in the temple (Verses 41–52)

[41] Every year his parents went to Jerusalem for the feast of the Passover.
[42] When he was twelve years old,
they went up again, as was the custom for the feast.
[43] When the days of the feast were over
and they were going home,
the boy Jesus stayed behind in Jerusalem.
Joseph and his mother didn’t know it.
[44] They thought he was with the traveling group.
They went a day’s journey
and looked for him among their relatives and friends.
[45] When they didn’t find him,
they went back to Jerusalem
to look for him.
[46] After three days they found him in the temple.
He was sitting in the middle of the teachers,
listening to them
and asking them questions.
[47] All who heard him
were amazed at his understanding and his answers.
[48] When his parents saw him,
they were astonished.
His mother said to him,
“Son, why have you treated us this way?
Your father and I were anxiously looking for you.”
[49] He said to them,
“Why were you looking for me?
Didn’t you know
that I must be in my Father’s house?”
[50] But they didn’t understand
what he meant.
[51] Then he went down with them
and came to Nazareth.
He obeyed them.
And his mother kept all these sayings in her heart.
[52] Jesus grew in wisdom and stature,
and in favor with God and people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Many Jews made a pilgrimage to Jerusalem for the Passover. People traveled in large groups with family and neighbors.
> Pilgrimage: a journey to a holy place.
> At twelve or thirteen, a Jewish boy took on religious responsibility. Today this is called “Bar Mitzvah.”
> Jesus sits with the teachers in the temple, listens and asks questions. The Jewish teachers take the boy seriously and are amazed at him.
> Verse 48: Mary and Joseph were very afraid. Parents know this fear. Mary says honestly how she feels.
> Verse 49: Jesus’ first words in the Gospel of Luke: He calls God “my Father.” He knows to whom he belongs.
> Verse 51: Even so, he obeys his parents and goes home with them.
> Verse 52: Jesus is fully human. He grows and learns like every child. Stature: height, size.
