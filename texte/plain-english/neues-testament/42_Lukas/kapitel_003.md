# Luke – Chapter 3
### John the Baptizer and the baptism of Jesus

---

## John the Baptizer appears (Verses 1–6)

[1] This happened in the fifteenth year of the rule of Tiberius Caesar.
At that time Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea.
Herod was tetrarch of Galilee.
His brother Philip was tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis.
Lysanias was tetrarch of Abilene.
[2] Annas and Caiaphas were the high priests.
At that time God spoke his word to John, the son of Zacharias.
John was in the wilderness.
[3] John went through the whole area around the Jordan River.
He told the people to turn away from their sins and be baptized.
Then God would forgive their sins.
[4] This is what is written in the book of Isaiah the prophet:
“A voice calls out in the wilderness,
‘Prepare the way for the Lord.
Make his paths straight.
[5] Every valley will be filled.
Every mountain and every hill will be made low.
The crooked roads will become straight.
The rough roads will become smooth.
[6] All people will see how God saves.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Like a historian, Luke names the exact rulers. The 15th year of Emperor Tiberius was about AD 28 or 29.
> “Herod” here is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. Tetrarch: a ruler over part of a country.
> Annas had been high priest earlier. His son-in-law Caiaphas held the office at this time (compare John 18:13). But Annas still had great influence.
> The word of God comes to John as it came to the prophets in the Old Testament.
> To turn away from your sins (repentance) means: turning around, changing your life and turning back to God.
> Verses 4–6 quote Isaiah 40:3–5. Only Luke also quotes: “All people will see how God saves” (3:6). Word for word it says “all flesh.” That means all people. This is a favorite thought of Luke: God’s salvation is for everyone.

---

## John calls people to turn back (Verses 7–18)

[7] Crowds of people came out to John to be baptized.
He said to them,
“You children of poisonous snakes!
Who warned you to run away from God’s coming anger?
[8] Show by the way you live that you have turned away from your sins!
Don’t start telling yourselves,
‘Abraham is our father.’
I tell you, God can make children for Abraham
out of these stones!
[9] The ax is already lying at the roots of the trees.
Every tree that does not grow good fruit
will be cut down and thrown into the fire.”
[10] The crowds asked John,
“Then what should we do?”
[11] He answered them,
“If you have two coats,
give one to a person who has none.
If you have food,
do the same.”
[12] Tax collectors also came to be baptized.
They asked him,
“Teacher, what should we do?”
[13] He said to them,
“Don’t collect more money
than you are supposed to collect.”
[14] Soldiers also asked him,
“And what about us? What should we do?”
He said to them,
“Don’t use force to take money from anyone.
Don’t accuse anyone falsely.
Be happy with your pay.”
[15] The people were waiting and hoping.
They all wondered in their hearts about John.
Could he be the Christ?
[16] John answered them all,
“I baptize you with water.
But someone is coming who is stronger than I am.
I am not even good enough
to untie the straps of his sandals.
He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire.
[17] He has his winnowing fork in his hand.
He will clean up his threshing floor completely.
He will gather the wheat into his barn.
But he will burn up the chaff in a fire
that never goes out.”
[18] John said many other things to encourage the people.
He told them the Good News.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Children of poisonous snakes” is the harsh language of the prophets. John wants to shake people awake.
> Verse 8: John says: It is not enough to belong to the people of Abraham. God wants a changed life. This is not criticism of the Jewish people, but a call to turn back, like the call of Israel’s prophets. John himself was a Jew.
> Only Luke tells verses 10–14. John gives very practical answers: Share with the poor. Be honest at work. Don’t misuse power.
> Tax collectors: men who collected taxes for the Romans. Many of them took more than they should.
> Striking: John does not tell the tax collectors and soldiers to give up their jobs. They are to do them fairly.
> Verse 16: “Baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire”: Fire is a picture of God’s cleansing power. Christians also think of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came like fire (Acts 2:3).
> Verse 17: A winnowing fork is a kind of shovel. When winnowing, people threw the threshed grain into the wind with a shovel. The light chaff (the husks) blew away, and the heavy grain fell down. A picture of God’s judgment.
> For today: Verse 11 is a clear task: Whoever has more should share with those who have nothing.

## John is arrested (Verses 19–20)

[19] John also told Herod the tetrarch that he was wrong.
He told him he was wrong to take Herodias, his brother’s wife.
He told him he was wrong in all the other evil things he had done.
[20] Then Herod did one more evil thing:
He locked John up in prison.

> **What does this mean?**
> John criticizes the powerful Herod. Herod had married his brother’s wife, which was forbidden by the law of Moses (Leviticus 18:16).
> Rebuked: told off sharply for doing wrong.
> Luke already tells about the arrest here, even though it happened later. In this way he finishes the story of John before the story of Jesus begins.
> John is an example of courage: He tells the powerful the truth.

---

## Jesus is baptized (Verses 21–22)

[21] All the people were being baptized.
Jesus was baptized too.
While he was praying,
the sky opened.
[22] The Holy Spirit came down on him.
The Spirit had a body like a dove.
And a voice came from the sky:
“You are my Son. I love you.
I am very pleased with you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus is baptized in the middle of all the people. He stands with the people.
> Only Luke mentions: Jesus is praying. In Luke, Jesus often prays, especially before important events.
> God’s voice says: “You are my Son. I love you.” This reminds us of Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1.
> In verse 22 some old manuscripts have the words of Psalm 2:7: You are my son. Today I have become your father. The WEB, our English source text, follows the other reading.

## The family tree of Jesus (Verses 23–38)

[23] Jesus was about thirty years old
when he began to teach.
People thought that he was the son of Joseph.
Joseph was the son of Heli,
[24] the son of Matthat,
the son of Levi,
the son of Melchi,
the son of Jannai,
the son of Joseph,
[25] the son of Mattathias,
the son of Amos,
the son of Nahum,
the son of Esli,
the son of Naggai,
[26] the son of Maath,
the son of Mattathias,
the son of Semein,
the son of Joseph,
the son of Judah,
[27] the son of Joanan,
the son of Rhesa,
the son of Zerubbabel,

the son of Neri,
[28] the son of Melchi,

[29] the son of Jose,
the son of Eliezer,
the son of Jorim,
the son of Matthat,
the son of Levi,
[30] the son of Simeon,
the son of Judah,
the son of Joseph,
the son of Jonan,
the son of Eliakim,
[31] the son of Melea,
the son of Menan,
the son of Mattatha,
the son of Nathan,
the son of David,
[32] the son of Jesse,
the son of Obed,
the son of Boaz,
the son of Salmon,
the son of Nahshon,
[33] the son of Amminadab,
the son of Aram,
the son of Hezron,
the son of Perez,
the son of Judah,
[34] the son of Jacob,
the son of Isaac,
the son of Abraham,
the son of Terah,
the son of Nahor,
[35] the son of Serug,
the son of Reu,
the son of Peleg,
the son of Eber,
the son of Shelah,
[36] the son of Cainan,
the son of Arphaxad,
the son of Shem,
the son of Noah,
the son of Lamech,
[37] the son of Methuselah,
the son of Enoch,
the son of Jared,
the son of Mahalaleel,
the son of Cainan,
[38] the son of Enos,
the son of Seth,
the son of Adam,
the son of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> At about thirty years old, Jesus begins his public work. The priests also began their service at thirty (Numbers 4:3), and David became king at thirty (2 Samuel 5:4).
> “People thought that he was the son of Joseph”: Luke has told us that Jesus was conceived through the Holy Spirit (1:35). By law, Jesus counted as Joseph’s son.
> Matthew also has a family tree (Matthew 1:1–17). It begins with Abraham and goes down to Jesus. Luke goes from Jesus back to Adam and to God. He shows: Jesus belongs to all of humanity, not only to one people.
> The two family trees are very different between David and Joseph. In Matthew the line goes through Solomon, in Luke through Nathan, another son of David. There are different explanations for this. One old explanation is: Luke gives the family tree of Mary. Others think of brother-in-law marriages or adoption. No one knows for sure.
> Verse 36: A “Cainan” between Arphaxad and Shelah is not in the Hebrew Bible (Genesis 11:12), but it is in the old Greek translation of the Old Testament.
> Verse 38: “The son of God”: Adam was created directly by God. Jesus is the new human being, like a new Adam.
> The names are spelled as in the WEB. In other Bibles some names are spelled a little differently.
