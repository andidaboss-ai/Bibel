# Luke – Chapter 3
### John the Baptizer and the baptism of Jesus

---

## John the Baptizer appears (Verses 1–6)

[1] It was in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar.
Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea.
Herod was tetrarch of Galilee,
and his brother Philip was tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis.
Lysanias was tetrarch of Abilene.
[2] Annas and Caiaphas were high priests.
Then the word of God came to John, the son of Zacharias,
in the wilderness.
[3] He went into all the region around the Jordan,
preaching the baptism of repentance
for the forgiveness of sins.
[4] It is written in the book of the words of Isaiah the prophet:
“The voice of one crying in the wilderness,
‘Make ready the way of the Lord.
Make his paths straight.
[5] Every valley will be filled.
Every mountain and hill will be brought low.
The crooked will become straight,
and the rough ways smooth.
[6] All flesh will see God’s salvation.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Like a historian, Luke names the exact rulers. The 15th year of Emperor Tiberius was about AD 28 or 29.
> “Herod” here is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. Tetrarch: a ruler over part of a country.
> Annas had been high priest earlier. His son-in-law Caiaphas held the office at this time (compare John 18:13). But Annas still had great influence.
> The word of God comes to John as it came to the prophets in the Old Testament.
> Repentance: turning around, changing your life and turning back to God.
> Verses 4–6 quote Isaiah 40:3–5. Only Luke also quotes: “All flesh will see God’s salvation” (3:6). “All flesh” means all people. This is a favorite thought of Luke: God’s salvation is for everyone.

---

## John calls people to turn back (Verses 7–18)

[7] John said to the crowds
who came out
to be baptized by him,
“You offspring of vipers!
Who warned you
to flee from the anger to come?
[8] Produce fruits that show
you have changed your lives!
And don’t begin to say among yourselves,
‘We have Abraham for our father.’
For I tell you,
God is able to raise up children to Abraham
from these stones!
[9] Even now the ax lies at the root of the trees.
Every tree that doesn’t produce good fruit
is cut down and thrown into the fire.”
[10] The crowds asked him,
“What then must we do?”
[11] He answered them,
“He who has two coats,
let him give to him who has none.
He who has food,
let him do likewise.”
[12] Tax collectors also came
to be baptized.
They said to him,
“Teacher, what must we do?”
[13] He said to them,
“Collect no more
than what you are told to collect.”
[14] Soldiers also asked him,
“What about us? What must we do?”
He said to them,
“Take money from no one by violence,
don’t accuse anyone falsely,
and be content with your wages.”
[15] The people were full of expectation.
They all wondered in their hearts
whether John might be the Christ.
[16] John answered them all,
“I baptize you with water.
But one is coming who is mightier than I.
I am not worthy
to loosen the strap of his sandals.
He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire.
[17] His winnowing fan is in his hand.
He will thoroughly clean his threshing floor
and gather the wheat into his barn.
But he will burn up the chaff with fire
that can never be put out.”
[18] With many other words he urged the people
and preached the good news to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Offspring of vipers” is the harsh language of the prophets. Vipers are poisonous snakes. John wants to shake people awake.
> Verse 8: John says: It is not enough to belong to the people of Abraham. God wants a changed life. This is not criticism of the Jewish people, but a call to turn back, like the call of Israel’s prophets. John himself was a Jew.
> Only Luke tells verses 10–14. John gives very practical answers: Share with the poor. Be honest at work. Don’t misuse power.
> Tax collectors: men who collected taxes for the Romans. Many of them took more than they should.
> Striking: John does not tell the tax collectors and soldiers to give up their jobs. They are to do them fairly.
> Verse 16: “Baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire”: Fire is a picture of God’s cleansing power. Christians also think of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came like fire (Acts 2:3).
> Verse 17: When winnowing, people threw the threshed grain into the wind with a shovel. The light chaff (the husks) blew away, and the heavy grain fell down. A picture of God’s judgment.
> For today: Verse 11 is a clear task: Whoever has more should share with those who have nothing.

## John is arrested (Verses 19–20)

[19] Herod the tetrarch was rebuked by John
because of Herodias, his brother’s wife,
and because of all the evil things he had done.
[20] Then Herod added this to all of it:
He shut John up in prison.

> **What does this mean?**
> John criticizes the powerful Herod. Herod had married his brother’s wife, which was forbidden by the law of Moses (Leviticus 18:16).
> Rebuked: told off sharply for doing wrong.
> Luke already tells about the arrest here, even though it happened later. In this way he finishes the story of John before the story of Jesus begins.
> John is an example of courage: He tells the powerful the truth.

---

## Jesus is baptized (Verses 21–22)

[21] When all the people were being baptized,
Jesus was baptized too.
And as he was praying,
the sky was opened.
[22] The Holy Spirit came down on him in a bodily form
like a dove.
And a voice came out of the sky:
“You are my beloved Son.
In you I am well pleased.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus is baptized in the middle of all the people. He stands with the people.
> Only Luke mentions: Jesus is praying. In Luke, Jesus often prays, especially before important events.
> God’s voice says: “You are my beloved Son.” This reminds us of Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1.
> In verse 22 some old manuscripts have the words of Psalm 2:7: You are my son. Today I have become your father. The WEB follows the other reading.

## The family tree of Jesus (Verses 23–38)

[23] Jesus was about thirty years old
when he began to teach.
He was, as people thought, the son of Joseph.
Joseph was the son of Heli,
[24] the son of Matthat,
the son of Levi,
the son of Melchi,
the son of Jannai,
the son of Joseph,
[25] the son of Mattathias,
the son of Amos,
the son of Nahum,
the son of Esli,
the son of Naggai,
[26] the son of Maath,
the son of Mattathias,
the son of Semein,
the son of Joseph,
the son of Judah,
[27] the son of Joanan,
the son of Rhesa,
the son of Zerubbabel,
the son of Shealtiel,
the son of Neri,
[28] the son of Melchi,
the son of Addi,
the son of Cosam,
the son of Elmodam,
the son of Er,
[29] the son of Jose,
the son of Eliezer,
the son of Jorim,
the son of Matthat,
the son of Levi,
[30] the son of Simeon,
the son of Judah,
the son of Joseph,
the son of Jonan,
the son of Eliakim,
[31] the son of Melea,
the son of Menan,
the son of Mattatha,
the son of Nathan,
the son of David,
[32] the son of Jesse,
the son of Obed,
the son of Boaz,
the son of Salmon,
the son of Nahshon,
[33] the son of Amminadab,
the son of Aram,
the son of Hezron,
the son of Perez,
the son of Judah,
[34] the son of Jacob,
the son of Isaac,
the son of Abraham,
the son of Terah,
the son of Nahor,
[35] the son of Serug,
the son of Reu,
the son of Peleg,
the son of Eber,
the son of Shelah,
[36] the son of Cainan,
the son of Arphaxad,
the son of Shem,
the son of Noah,
the son of Lamech,
[37] the son of Methuselah,
the son of Enoch,
the son of Jared,
the son of Mahalaleel,
the son of Cainan,
[38] the son of Enos,
the son of Seth,
the son of Adam,
the son of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> At about thirty years old, Jesus begins his public work. The priests also began their service at thirty (Numbers 4:3), and David became king at thirty (2 Samuel 5:4).
> “As people thought, the son of Joseph”: Luke has told us that Jesus was conceived through the Holy Spirit (1:35). By law, Jesus counted as Joseph’s son.
> Matthew also has a family tree (Matthew 1:1–17). It begins with Abraham and goes down to Jesus. Luke goes from Jesus back to Adam and to God. He shows: Jesus belongs to all of humanity, not only to one people.
> The two family trees are very different between David and Joseph. In Matthew the line goes through Solomon, in Luke through Nathan, another son of David. There are different explanations for this. One old explanation is: Luke gives the family tree of Mary. Others think of brother-in-law marriages or adoption. No one knows for sure.
> Verse 36: A “Cainan” between Arphaxad and Shelah is not in the Hebrew Bible (Genesis 11:12), but it is in the old Greek translation of the Old Testament.
> Verse 38: “The son of God”: Adam was created directly by God. Jesus is the new human being, like a new Adam.
> The names are spelled as in the WEB. In other Bibles some names are spelled a little differently.
