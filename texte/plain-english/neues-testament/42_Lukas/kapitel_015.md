# Luke – Chapter 15
### Lost and found

---

## The lost sheep (Verses 1–7)

[1] All the tax collectors and sinners
were coming to Jesus to listen to him.
[2] The Pharisees and the scribes complained.
They said,
“This man welcomes sinners
and even eats with them.”
[3] So Jesus told them this parable.
[4] “Suppose one of you has 100 sheep,
and one of them gets lost.
Won’t he leave the 99 sheep in the wilderness?
Won’t he go and look for the lost sheep
until he finds it?
[5] When he finds it,
he puts it on his shoulders.
He is very happy.
[6] He comes home.
He calls his friends and neighbors together.
He says to them,
‘Be happy with me!
I have found my sheep that was lost!’
[7] I tell you,
it is the same in heaven.
There is more joy in heaven
over one sinner who turns away from his sins
than over 99 good people
who do not need to turn away from their sins.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: The devout teachers are upset that Jesus eats with “sinners.” Back then eating together meant: We belong together. Jesus answers with three parables.
> The Pharisees and scribes wanted to keep God’s commandments faithfully. Their concern was meant seriously. Jesus wants to win them over, not condemn them. The parables are also about them, especially the older brother.
> A shepherd counted his sheep every evening. A sheep on its own was in great danger.
> In the Old Testament God himself is the shepherd who looks for what is lost (Ezekiel 34:11–16; Psalm 23).
> Verse 5: The shepherd does not scold. He carries the sheep home, full of joy.
> Verse 7: “99 good people who do not need to turn away from their sins”: This sounds almost a little ironic. Who doesn’t need to turn back?
> For today: No person is worth too little to God. He looks for every single one.

## The lost coin (Verses 8–10)

[8] “Or suppose a woman has 10 silver coins called drachmas,
and she loses one of them.
Won’t she light a lamp
and sweep the house?
Won’t she search carefully
until she finds the coin?
[9] When she finds it,
she calls her friends and neighbors together.
She says,
‘Be happy with me!
I have found the coin that I lost!’
[10] I tell you,
it is the same with the angels of God.
They are full of joy
over one sinner who turns away from his sins.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A drachma was a silver coin. It was worth about one day’s pay. For a poor woman that was a lot of money.
> The houses had hardly any windows and were dark. That is why she needs a lamp.
> After the shepherd, Jesus tells about a woman. In this parable a woman stands for God, who searches. That is remarkable.
> Verse 9: In the Greek, the words for friends and neighbors here are female: She calls together her women friends and neighbors.
> For today: God searches with great effort. And there is a celebration in heaven when someone finds their way home.

## The lost son and his brother (Verses 11–32)

[11] Jesus said,
“A man had two sons.
[12] The younger son said to his father,
‘Father, give me my share of your property.’
So the father divided his property between his two sons.
[13] A few days later the younger son packed up everything he had.
He traveled to a country far away.
There he wasted his money on wild living.
[14] He spent everything he had.
Then there was a terrible famine in that country.
There was not enough food.
The son began to go hungry.
[15] He went to work for a man in that country.
The man sent him into his fields to feed pigs.
[16] The son was so hungry
that he wanted to eat the pods the pigs were eating.
But no one gave him anything.
[17] Then he came to his senses.
He said,
‘My father’s hired workers have more than enough food.
But here I am, dying of hunger!
[18] I will get up and go back to my father.
I will say to him,
“Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you.
[19] I am not good enough anymore
to be called your son.
Let me be like one of your hired workers.”’
[20] So the son got up
and went back to his father.
He was still a long way off
when his father saw him.
His father felt deep love and pity for him.
He ran to his son,
threw his arms around his neck,
and kissed him.
[21] The son said to him,
‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you.
I am not good enough anymore
to be called your son.’
[22] But the father said to his servants,
‘Bring out the best robe and put it on him.
Put a ring on his finger
and sandals on his feet.
[23] Bring the calf we have been fattening.
Kill it.
Let’s eat and celebrate!
[24] For this son of mine was dead,
and now he is alive again.
He was lost,
and now he is found.’
And they began to celebrate.
[25] The older son was out in the field.
He came back
and came near the house.
Then he heard music and dancing.
[26] He called one of the servants
and asked him what was going on.
[27] The servant said to him,
‘Your brother has come home.
Your father has killed the fattened calf,
because your brother has come back safe and healthy.’
[28] But the older brother became angry.
He did not want to go in.
So his father came out
and begged him to come in.
[29] But the older son answered his father,
‘Look! For so many years I have served you.
I have never disobeyed your orders.
But you never gave me even a young goat,
so that I could celebrate with my friends.
[30] But this son of yours wasted your money on prostitutes.
And when he comes home,
you kill the fattened calf for him!’
[31] The father said to him,
‘Son, you are always with me.
Everything I have is yours.
[32] But we had to celebrate and be glad.
For this brother of yours was dead,
and now he is alive again.
He was lost,
and now he is found.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> This parable is one of the best known in the Bible. Only Luke tells it. It is often called the “parable of the lost son,” sometimes also the parable of the loving father, because the father is the main character.
> Verse 12: Asking for your inheritance while your father was still alive was a deep insult back then. It was almost like saying: I wish you were dead. Even so, the father gives in.
> Verse 13: “Wild living” means: a wild, wasteful life.
> Verses 15–16: For Jews, pigs counted as unclean (Leviticus 11:7). The son could hardly sink any lower. The “pods” were probably the fruit of the carob tree, which was used as animal feed.
> Verse 17: “He came to his senses”: He sees honestly how things stand with him. That is the beginning of turning back.
> Verse 20: The father waits and keeps watch. He runs to meet his son. For an older man back then, running was almost undignified. But love is greater than dignity.
> Verse 21: The father does not even let the son finish the speech he had prepared. Some old manuscripts add here: “Let me be like one of your hired workers.”
> Verse 22: Robe, ring and sandals are signs: You are fully my son again. Slaves went barefoot, sons wore sandals. The fattened calf was kept for a special feast.
> Verses 25–32: The older brother is faithful, but bitter. He does not say “my brother,” but “this son of yours.” The father goes out to him too and invites him. He loves both sons.
> Verse 30: Only the older brother says that the younger son lived with prostitutes. Verse 13 only says “wild living.”
> The parable ends openly: Will the older brother go in and celebrate too? Jesus leaves the question open for the listeners, including the Pharisees and scribes from verse 2.
> Important: The older brother is not a picture of the Jews, and the younger is not a picture of Christians. Both can be found in every person: the one who runs away, and the one who does not want to join the celebration.
> For today: God waits for everyone who comes back. No one is too far away. And: Be glad with others when someone else receives grace.
