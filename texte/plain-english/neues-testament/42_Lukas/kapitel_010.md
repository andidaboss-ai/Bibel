# Luke – Chapter 10
### Who is my neighbor?

---

## Jesus sends out 70 disciples (Verses 1–16)

[1] After this the Lord chose 70 other disciples.
He sent them out two by two ahead of him.
They went to every town and place
where he was going to come.
[2] He said to them,
“There is a big harvest,
but there are only a few workers.
So pray to the Lord of the harvest.
Ask him to send out workers into his harvest.
[3] Go now!
I am sending you out like lambs among wolves.
[4] Don’t take a money bag.
Don’t take a traveler’s bag or sandals.
Don’t stop to greet anyone on the way.
[5] When you go into a house,
first say,
‘Peace be with this house.’
[6] If a son of peace lives there,
your peace will stay with him.
If not,
your peace will come back to you.
[7] Stay in that same house.
Eat and drink what they give you.
For a worker deserves his pay.
Don’t move from house to house.
[8] When you go into a town
and the people welcome you,
eat what they put in front of you.
[9] Heal the sick people there.
Tell them,
‘God’s Kingdom has come near to you.’
[10] But sometimes you will go into a town
where the people do not welcome you.
Then go out into its streets and say,
[11] ‘We wipe off even the dust of your town
that sticks to us.
We wipe it off against you.
But know this:
God’s Kingdom has come near to you.’
[12] I tell you,
on that day it will be easier for Sodom
than for that town.
[13] How terrible it will be for you, Chorazin!
How terrible it will be for you, Bethsaida!
The great miracles that were done in you
could have been done in Tyre and Sidon.
Then the people there would have turned away from their sins long ago.
They would have sat in sackcloth and ashes.
[14] But on judgment day it will be easier for Tyre and Sidon
than for you.
[15] And you, Capernaum,
you are lifted up to heaven.
But you will be brought down to Hades.
[16] Whoever listens to you
listens to me.
Whoever rejects you
rejects me.
And whoever rejects me
rejects the one who sent me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Not only the Twelve, but many more people are sent out. The WEB, our English source text, says 70. Many old manuscripts say 72. Both numbers remind us of the list of nations in Genesis 10: The message is to reach all nations.
> “Two by two”: No one is to travel alone. Two can help each other. And two witnesses were considered trustworthy.
> Verse 4: “Don’t stop to greet anyone on the way”: Back then greetings were long and complicated. The messengers are not to let themselves be held up. It is not unfriendliness.
> Verse 6: “A son of peace”: someone who is open to God’s peace.
> Verse 7: “A worker deserves his pay” means: Whoever works has earned his pay. This sentence is quoted again later in 1 Timothy 5:18.
> Verses 12–15: In the Bible, Sodom, Tyre and Sidon were cities known as godless. Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum were on the Sea of Galilee. Jesus had done a lot there. The cry “How terrible it will be for you” (in the WEB: “woe”) is a serious lament, as with the prophets.
> “Sackcloth and ashes”: signs of mourning and being sorry. Sackcloth was rough cloth. Hades: the realm of the dead.
> Important: The messengers are not to punish. Judgment is God’s business. They are to move on.

## The 70 return (Verses 17–20)

[17] The 70 came back full of joy.
They said,
“Lord, even the demons obey us
when we use your name!”
[18] Jesus said to them,
“I saw Satan fall from heaven
like lightning.
[19] Listen!
I have given you authority to step on snakes and scorpions.
I have given you authority over all the power of the enemy.
Nothing at all will hurt you.
[20] But don’t be glad because the spirits obey you.
Be glad because your names are written in heaven.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: Jesus sees: The power of evil is broken. With Jesus, God’s Kingdom begins, and evil loses.
> Verse 19: Serpents (snakes) and scorpions are pictures of everything dangerous and evil (compare Psalm 91:13).
> Important: This verse is not a call to put yourself in danger on purpose. Some groups play with poisonous snakes. That is life-threatening and is not what is meant.
> Verse 20: More important than success and great deeds is this: God knows you and has written down your name. You belong to him.

## Jesus rejoices (Verses 21–24)

[21] At that time Jesus was full of joy in the Holy Spirit.
He said,
“I thank you, Father,
Lord of heaven and earth.
You have hidden these things
from people who are wise and clever.
And you have shown them to little children.
Yes, Father,
this is what pleased you.”
[22] Jesus turned to the disciples and said,
“My Father has given me everything.
No one knows who the Son is,
except the Father.
And no one knows who the Father is,
except the Son.
And the Son can show the Father
to anyone he chooses.”
[23] Then Jesus turned to the disciples.
He said to them alone,
“Blessed are the eyes
that see what you see.
[24] I tell you,
many prophets and kings wanted to see what you see.
But they did not see it.
They wanted to hear what you hear.
But they did not hear it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Only Luke says: Jesus rejoiced “in the Holy Spirit.” Here Father, Son and Holy Spirit are named together. Christians think of this when they speak of the Trinity.
> “Little children” means simple people. God does not show himself first to scholars, but to those who are open like children.
> Important: This is not criticism of education or of thinking. It is about pride and openness.
> Verse 22: Jesus knows God the way a son knows his father. Through him people can get to know God.
> Verse 24: The prophets of Israel hoped for this time. The disciples are living it.

## The Good Samaritan (Verses 25–37)

[25] An expert in the law stood up.
He wanted to test Jesus.
He asked,
“Teacher, what must I do
to receive eternal life?”
[26] Jesus said to him,
“What is written in the law?
What do you read there?”
[27] The man answered,
“You shall love the Lord your God
with all your heart,
with all your soul,
with all your strength,
and with all your mind.
And you shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
[28] Jesus said to him,
“Your answer is right.
Do this, and you will live.”
[29] But the man wanted to show that he was right.
So he asked Jesus,
“Who is my neighbor?”
[30] Jesus answered,
“A man was going down the road from Jerusalem to Jericho.
On the way robbers attacked him.
They took his clothes and beat him.
Then they went away
and left him lying there, almost dead.
[31] It happened that a priest was going down that road.
He saw the man.
But he went past on the other side of the road.
[32] Then a Levite came to that place too.
He saw the man.
But he also went past on the other side.
[33] Then a Samaritan came.
He was traveling on that road.
He came to the place where the man was.
When he saw him,
he felt very sorry for him.
[34] He went to the man.
He poured oil and wine on his wounds
and bandaged them.
Then he put the man on his own animal.
He brought him to an inn
and took care of him.
[35] The next day the Samaritan had to leave.
He took out two denarii
and gave them to the innkeeper.
He said,
‘Take care of him.
If you spend more than this,
I will pay you back when I return.’
[36] Which of these three men do you think
was a neighbor to the man
whom the robbers attacked?”
[37] The expert in the law said,
“The one who showed him mercy.”
Jesus said to him,
“Go and do the same.”

> **What does this mean?**
> An expert in the law was someone who studied and taught the law of Moses.
> Verse 27: The expert in the law joins two commandments from the Holy Scriptures of Israel: Deuteronomy 6:5 (loving God) and Leviticus 19:18 (loving your neighbor). Jesus agrees with him. This is a shared Jewish heritage.
> The road from Jerusalem to Jericho went about 3,300 feet (1,000 meters) downhill through the desert. It was dangerous and known for robbers.
> Priests and Levites served in the temple. Why do they pass by? Jesus does not say. Some interpreters think of fear of robbers. Others think of the rule not to make yourself unclean by touching a dead body. But in Judaism too it was taught: Saving a human life is more important than almost all other commandments.
> Important: The parable is not criticism of Judaism or of priests in general. Jesus and the expert in the law are both Jews. The point is that religion without mercy is empty. That applies to all religions, including Christians.
> The surprise: Of all people, a Samaritan helps. Back then Jews and Samaritans were enemies (compare Luke 9:52–53). The foreigner becomes the example.
> Back then oil and wine were used as medicine: Wine cleaned the wounds, oil soothed them.
> Two denarii were the pay for two days of work. That was enough for several days at the inn.
> Verse 36: Jesus turns the question around. Not: Who is my neighbor? But: To whom do I become a neighbor?
> For today: Every person in need is my neighbor, no matter where they come from. In an accident or emergency: call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK) and give first aid.

## Martha and Mary (Verses 38–42)

[38] Jesus and his disciples went on their way.
Jesus came to a village.
A woman named Martha welcomed him into her house.
[39] She had a sister named Mary.
Mary sat at the feet of Jesus
and listened to what he said.
[40] But Martha was busy
with all the work she had to do.
She came to Jesus and said,
“Lord, don’t you care
that my sister has left me to do all the work alone?
Tell her to help me!”
[41] Jesus answered her,
“Martha, Martha,
you are worried and upset
about many things.
[42] But only one thing is needed.
Mary has chosen the good part.
It will not be taken away from her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Martha and Mary also appear in the Gospel of John. There they live in Bethany, with their brother Lazarus (John 11:1).
> “Sitting at someone’s feet” was the position of a student with his teacher. Back then women were usually not allowed to learn from a teacher. Jesus clearly allows Mary to do so.
> Jesus does not criticize Martha’s hospitality. He sees her worry and lovingly calls her by name twice. Hospitality is good, as the Samaritan has just shown.
> Verse 42: Some old manuscripts say here: Only a few things are needed, or really only one. The WEB follows the shorter reading.
> The two stories belong together: The Samaritan shows how important doing is. Mary shows how important listening is. Both belong to faith.
> For today: Sometimes it is more important to become quiet and listen than to always be busy.
