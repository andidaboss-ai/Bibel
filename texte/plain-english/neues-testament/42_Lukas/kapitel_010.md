# Luke – Chapter 10
### Who is my neighbor?

---

## Jesus sends out seventy disciples (Verses 1–16)

[1] After this the Lord appointed seventy others.
He sent them two by two ahead of him
into every city and place
where he was about to come.
[2] He said to them,
“The harvest is indeed plentiful,
but the laborers are few.
So pray to the Lord of the harvest
that he may send out laborers into his harvest.
[3] Go your ways.
I send you out
as lambs among wolves.
[4] Carry no purse,
no bag,
and no sandals.
Greet no one on the way.
[5] Whatever house you enter,
first say,
‘Peace be to this house.’
[6] If a son of peace is there,
your peace will rest on him.
But if not,
it will return to you.
[7] Stay in that same house.
Eat and drink what they give you.
For the laborer is worthy of his wages.
Don’t go from house to house.
[8] Whatever city you enter,
if they welcome you,
eat what is set before you.
[9] Heal the sick who are there,
and tell them,
‘God’s Kingdom has come near to you.’
[10] But whatever city you enter
where they don’t welcome you,
go out into its streets
and say,
[11] ‘Even the dust from your city
that clings to us,
we wipe off against you.
But know this:
God’s Kingdom has come near to you.’
[12] I tell you,
it will be more bearable on that day for Sodom
than for that city.
[13] Woe to you, Chorazin!
Woe to you, Bethsaida!
If the mighty works had been done in Tyre and Sidon
which were done in you,
they would have repented long ago.
They would have sat in sackcloth and ashes.
[14] But it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon in the judgment
than for you.
[15] And you, Capernaum,
who are lifted up to heaven,
you will be brought down to Hades.
[16] Whoever listens to you
listens to me.
Whoever rejects you
rejects me.
And whoever rejects me
rejects him who sent me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Not only the twelve, but many more people are sent out. The WEB says “seventy.” Many old manuscripts say seventy-two. Both numbers remind us of the list of nations in Genesis 10: The message is to reach all nations.
> “Two by two”: No one is to travel alone. Two can help each other. And two witnesses were considered trustworthy.
> Verse 4: “Greet no one on the way”: Back then greetings were long and complicated. The messengers are not to let themselves be held up. It is not unfriendliness.
> Verse 6: “A son of peace”: someone who is open to God’s peace.
> Verse 7: “The laborer is worthy of his wages” means: Whoever works has earned his pay. This sentence is quoted again later in 1 Timothy 5:18.
> Verses 12–15: In the Bible, Sodom, Tyre and Sidon were cities known as godless. Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum were on the Sea of Galilee. Jesus had done a lot there. The “woe” is a serious lament, as with the prophets.
> “Sackcloth and ashes”: signs of mourning and being sorry. Sackcloth was rough cloth. Hades: the realm of the dead.
> Important: The messengers are not to punish. Judgment is God’s business. They are to move on.

## The seventy return (Verses 17–20)

[17] The seventy came back with joy
and said,
“Lord, even the demons obey us
in your name!”
[18] He said to them,
“I saw Satan
fall like lightning from heaven.
[19] I give you authority
to tread on serpents and scorpions,
and over all the power of the enemy.
Nothing will in any way hurt you.
[20] But don’t rejoice in this,
that the spirits obey you.
Rather rejoice
that your names are written in heaven.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: Jesus sees: The power of evil is broken. With Jesus, God’s Kingdom begins, and evil loses.
> Verse 19: Serpents (snakes) and scorpions are pictures of everything dangerous and evil (compare Psalm 91:13).
> Important: This verse is not a call to put yourself in danger on purpose. Some groups play with poisonous snakes. That is life-threatening and is not what is meant.
> Verse 20: More important than success and great deeds is this: God knows you and has written down your name. You belong to him.

## Jesus rejoices (Verses 21–24)

[21] In that same hour
Jesus rejoiced in the Holy Spirit
and said,
“I thank you, Father,
Lord of heaven and earth,
that you have hidden these things from the wise and understanding
and revealed them to little children.
Yes, Father,
for so it was well-pleasing in your sight.”
[22] He turned to the disciples
and said,
“All things have been handed over to me by my Father.
No one knows who the Son is
except the Father.
And no one knows who the Father is
except the Son,
and anyone to whom the Son wants to reveal him.”
[23] He turned to the disciples
and said to them privately,
“Blessed are the eyes
which see the things that you see.
[24] For I tell you,
many prophets and kings wanted to see
the things which you see,
and didn’t see them.
They wanted to hear
the things which you hear,
and didn’t hear them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Only Luke says: Jesus rejoiced “in the Holy Spirit.” Here Father, Son and Holy Spirit are named together. Christians think of this when they speak of the Trinity.
> “Little children” means simple people. God does not show himself first to scholars, but to those who are open like children.
> Important: This is not criticism of education or of thinking. It is about pride and openness.
> Verse 22: Jesus knows God the way a son knows his father. Through him people can get to know God.
> Verse 24: The prophets of Israel hoped for this time. The disciples are living it.

## The Good Samaritan (Verses 25–37)

[25] A lawyer stood up
and wanted to test Jesus.
He asked,
“Teacher, what shall I do
to inherit eternal life?”
[26] Jesus said to him,
“What is written in the law?
How do you read it?”
[27] He answered,
“You shall love the Lord your God
with all your heart,
with all your soul,
with all your strength,
and with all your mind;
and your neighbor as yourself.”
[28] Jesus said to him,
“You have answered correctly.
Do this,
and you will live.”
[29] But the lawyer wanted to justify himself.
So he asked Jesus,
“Who is my neighbor?”
[30] Jesus answered,
“A man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho.
On the way he fell among robbers.
They stripped him,
beat him,
and went away.
They left him half dead.
[31] By chance a priest was going down that way.
When he saw him,
he passed by on the other side.
[32] In the same way a Levite also came to the place.
He saw him
and passed by on the other side.
[33] But a Samaritan,
as he traveled,
came where he was.
When he saw him,
he was moved with compassion.
[34] He went to him
and bandaged his wounds,
pouring on oil and wine.
Then he set him on his own animal,
brought him to an inn,
and took care of him.
[35] The next day,
when he left,
he took out two denarii.
He gave them to the innkeeper
and said to him,
‘Take care of him.
Whatever you spend beyond that,
I will repay you
when I return.’
[36] Now which of these three do you think
was a neighbor
to the man who fell among the robbers?”
[37] He said,
“He who showed mercy on him.”
Then Jesus said to him,
“Go and do likewise.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A lawyer here is an expert in the law of Moses.
> Verse 27: The lawyer joins two commandments from the Holy Scriptures of Israel: Deuteronomy 6:5 (loving God) and Leviticus 19:18 (loving your neighbor). Jesus agrees with him. This is a shared Jewish heritage.
> The road from Jerusalem to Jericho went about 3,300 feet (1,000 meters) downhill through the desert. It was dangerous and known for robbers.
> Priests and Levites served in the temple. Why do they pass by? Jesus does not say. Some interpreters think of fear of robbers. Others think of the rule not to make yourself unclean by touching a dead body. But in Judaism too it was taught: Saving a human life is more important than almost all other commandments.
> Important: The parable is not criticism of Judaism or of priests in general. Jesus and the lawyer are both Jews. The point is that religion without mercy is empty. That applies to all religions, including Christians.
> The surprise: Of all people, a Samaritan helps. Back then Jews and Samaritans were enemies (compare Luke 9:52–53). The foreigner becomes the example.
> Back then oil and wine were used as medicine: Wine cleaned the wounds, oil soothed them.
> Two denarii were the pay for two days of work. That was enough for several days at the inn.
> Verse 36: Jesus turns the question around. Not: Who is my neighbor? But: To whom do I become a neighbor?
> For today: Every person in need is my neighbor, no matter where they come from. In an accident or emergency: call your local emergency number (911 in the US) and give first aid.

## Martha and Mary (Verses 38–42)

[38] As they went on their way,
Jesus came into a village.
A woman named Martha
welcomed him into her house.
[39] She had a sister
called Mary.
Mary sat at Jesus’ feet
and listened to his word.
[40] But Martha was distracted
with much serving.
She came up to Jesus
and said,
“Lord, don’t you care
that my sister has left me to serve alone?
Tell her
to help me.”
[41] Jesus answered her,
“Martha, Martha,
you are anxious and troubled
about many things.
[42] But one thing is needed.
Mary has chosen the good part,
which will not be taken away from her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Martha and Mary also appear in the Gospel of John. There they live in Bethany, with their brother Lazarus (John 11:1).
> “Sitting at someone’s feet” was the position of a student with his teacher. Back then women were usually not allowed to learn from a teacher. Jesus clearly allows Mary to do so.
> Jesus does not criticize Martha’s hospitality. He sees her worry and lovingly calls her by name twice. Hospitality is good, as the Samaritan has just shown.
> Verse 42: Some old manuscripts say here: Only a few things are needed, or really only one. The WEB follows the shorter reading.
> The two stories belong together: The Samaritan shows how important doing is. Mary shows how important listening is. Both belong to faith.
> For today: Sometimes it is more important to become quiet and listen than to always be busy.
