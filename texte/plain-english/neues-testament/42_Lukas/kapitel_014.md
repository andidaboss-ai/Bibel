# Luke – Chapter 14
### The great feast

---

## Jesus heals a sick man on the Sabbath (Verses 1–6)

[1] On a Sabbath Jesus went into the house
of one of the leading Pharisees
to eat a meal there.
The people were watching him closely.
[2] In front of him was a man
who had dropsy.
[3] Jesus asked the lawyers and the Pharisees,
“Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath?”
[4] But they were silent.
So he took the man,
healed him,
and let him go.
[5] Then he said to them,
“If your son or an ox fell into a well,
which of you wouldn’t immediately pull him out,
even on a Sabbath day?”
[6] They couldn’t answer him
about these things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus eats at a Pharisee’s house. He has connections with them and talks with them.
> “Dropsy” is an illness in which water collects in the body. The legs and belly swell up. It can happen with heart or kidney disease.
> Verse 5: Some manuscripts say donkey instead of son.
> Jewish law also allowed saving a life on the Sabbath. Jewish teachers discussed how far this permission went. Jesus says: Helping a person is always right.
> Important: This story is not criticism of the Sabbath. The Sabbath is a gift from God.

## The right place at the feast (Verses 7–14)

[7] Jesus noticed
how the guests chose the best seats.
So he told them a parable:
[8] “When you are invited by someone to a wedding feast,
don’t sit in the best seat.
Perhaps someone more honorable than you
has been invited by him.
[9] Then he who invited both of you would come
and tell you,
‘Make room for this person.’
Then you would have to take the lowest place,
with shame.
[10] But when you are invited,
go and sit in the lowest place.
Then, when he who invited you comes,
he may tell you,
‘Friend, move up higher.’
Then you will be honored
in front of all who sit at the table with you.
[11] For everyone who lifts himself up
will be humbled.
And whoever humbles himself
will be lifted up.”
[12] He also said to the one
who had invited him,
“When you give a lunch or a dinner,
don’t invite your friends,
or your brothers,
or your relatives,
or rich neighbors.
Otherwise they might invite you back,
and you would be paid back.
[13] But when you give a feast,
invite the poor, the maimed, the lame, and the blind.
[14] Then you will be blessed,
because they don’t have the means
to repay you.
For you will be repaid
at the resurrection of the righteous.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At feasts back then the seating order was very important. It showed how much honor someone had.
> Verses 8–10 remind us of Proverbs 25:6–7: It is better to be invited to come up higher than to be sent lower.
> Verse 11: God does not look at rank and honor. Whoever makes himself small, God makes great. Mary already sang about this in her song (Luke 1:52). Humbled: made low.
> Verses 12–14: Jesus turns the rules upside down. Normally you invite those who will invite you back. Jesus says: Invite those who cannot give anything back.
> The “maimed” were people with injuries or disabilities. Back then they were often shut out. Jesus says: They belong at the table, as honored guests.
> Important: People with disabilities are not just people to feel sorry for. They have the same dignity as everyone.
> For today: Whom do I invite? Who sits at our table, in the family, in the church, in society?

## The parable of the great supper (Verses 15–24)

[15] One of those
who were sitting at the table with him
heard this
and said to him,
“Blessed is he who will feast in God’s Kingdom!”
[16] Jesus said to him,
“A man gave a great supper
and invited many people.
[17] At supper time
he sent out his servant
to tell those who were invited,
‘Come, for everything is ready now.’
[18] But one after the other they all began
to make excuses.
The first said to him,
‘I have bought a field,
and I must go and see it.
Please have me excused.’
[19] Another said,
‘I have bought five yoke of oxen,
and I must go try them out.
Please have me excused.’
[20] Another said,
‘I have married a wife,
and so I can’t come.’
[21] The servant came back
and told his lord these things.
Then the master of the house was angry
and said to his servant,
‘Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city,
and bring in the poor, maimed,
blind, and lame.’
[22] The servant said,
‘Lord, it is done
as you commanded,
and there is still room.’
[23] The lord said to the servant,
‘Go out into the highways and hedges,
and compel them to come in,
so that my house may be filled.
[24] For I tell you,
none of those men who were invited
will taste of my supper.
For many are called,
but few are chosen.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then people were invited twice: first an invitation in advance, then a message when the meal was ready. Whoever first accepted and then cancelled insulted the host.
> The excuses sound polite, but they are weak. You look at a field before you buy it. You try out oxen before you buy them. A yoke of oxen is a pair of oxen that pull together.
> Verse 20: According to Deuteronomy 24:5, a newly married man was free from military service. But he could still have come to a feast.
> The supper is a picture of God’s Kingdom. God invites everyone. Whoever does not come shuts themselves out.
> Verse 21: The poor, people with disabilities and people on the margins are invited. Verse 23: Then also the people far outside, in the countryside. Many interpreters think of the non-Jews here.
> Important: Verse 23 (“compel them to come in”) was terribly misused in church history. People appealed to it to force others into the faith by violence, including Jews and people who thought differently. That was grave injustice. What is meant is: Invite people so warmly that they dare to come. Faith can never be forced.
> Important: The parable is also not a judgment on the Jewish people. It is a warning to everyone who does not take God’s invitation seriously, including Christians.
> Verse 24: The words “For many are called, but few are chosen” are not in most old manuscripts of Luke. The WEB has them here. They are also in Matthew 22:14.

## What it costs to follow Jesus (Verses 25–35)

[25] Large crowds were going with Jesus.
He turned around
and said to them,
[26] “If anyone comes to me
and doesn’t disregard his own father, mother,
wife, children,
brothers, and sisters,
yes, and his own life also,
he can’t be my disciple.
[27] Whoever doesn’t carry his own cross
and come after me
can’t be my disciple.
[28] For which of you, wanting to build a tower,
doesn’t first sit down
and count the cost?
He checks
whether he has enough to finish it.
[29] Otherwise he may lay a foundation
and not be able to finish.
Then everyone who sees it
begins to mock him.
[30] They say,
‘This man began to build
and wasn’t able to finish.’
[31] Or what king goes to war
against another king
without first sitting down to consider
whether he is able with ten thousand
to meet the one
who comes against him with twenty thousand?
[32] If not,
then while the other is still a long way off,
he sends an envoy
and asks for conditions of peace.
[33] So whoever of you
doesn’t renounce all that he has
can’t be my disciple.
[34] Salt is good.
But if the salt becomes flat and tasteless,
with what do you season it?
[35] It is fit neither for the soil
nor for the manure pile.
It is thrown out.
He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 26: The WEB says “disregard,” that is: not pay attention to, put second. In the Greek there is a word that is often translated “hate.” But in the language of the Bible it means: love less, put in second place (compare Genesis 29:30–31). Matthew says it this way: “Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me” (Matthew 10:37).
> Important: Jesus does not want anyone to hate their family or treat them without love. The commandment to honor your father and your mother still applies (Luke 18:20). Groups that force people to leave their families misuse these words. You can find advice, for example, from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.
> Important: Putting your own life second also does not mean despising or harming yourself. If you are thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verses 28–32: Two examples: building a tower, and a king before a war. Both say: Think carefully before you decide. Following Jesus is a decision for your whole life. An envoy is a messenger sent to negotiate.
> Verse 33: Renounce: give up. In Luke, freedom from possessions is very important. Not all disciples had to sell everything. But nothing should be more important than God.
> Verses 34–35: Back then salt was often mixed with other substances and could lose its taste. Disciples are to be like good salt: They give the world flavor.
