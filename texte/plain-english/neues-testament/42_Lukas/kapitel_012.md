# Luke – Chapter 12
### Don’t be afraid, and be ready

---

## No hypocrisy and no fear (Verses 1–12)

[1] Meanwhile many thousands of people had gathered.
There were so many
that they trampled on each other.
Then Jesus began
to speak first to his disciples:
“Beware of the yeast of the Pharisees,
which is hypocrisy.
[2] There is nothing covered up
that will not be revealed,
and nothing hidden
that will not be known.
[3] So whatever you have said in the darkness
will be heard in the light.
And what you have whispered in the ear in the inner rooms
will be proclaimed on the housetops.
[4] I tell you, my friends,
don’t be afraid of those
who kill the body
and after that can do nothing more.
[5] I will show you
whom you should fear:
Fear him
who, after he has killed,
has power to throw into Gehenna.
Yes, I tell you,
fear him.
[6] Aren’t five sparrows sold
for two assaria coins?
Yet not one of them is forgotten by God.
[7] But the very hairs of your head are all counted.
So don’t be afraid.
You are of more value than many sparrows.
[8] I tell you,
everyone who confesses me before people,
the Son of Man will also confess
before the angels of God.
[9] But whoever denies me before people
will be denied before God’s angels.
[10] Everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man
will be forgiven.
But those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit
will not be forgiven.
[11] When they bring you before the synagogues,
the rulers, and the authorities,
don’t be anxious
about how or what you will answer
or what you will say.
[12] For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that same hour
what you must say.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Yeast is a little bit of fermenting dough. It spreads through the whole dough. Hypocrisy can spread in the same way. Hypocrisy means: acting pious on the outside, but being different inside.
> Important: Jesus warns against hypocrisy, not against Pharisees as people. Hypocrisy exists in every community, including among Christians.
> Verse 5: “Gehenna” is the Valley of Hinnom near Jerusalem. In earlier times terrible sacrifices were made there (Jeremiah 7:31). Later the name became a picture of God’s judgment. The one to fear is God. In the Bible, “fearing God” means: taking God seriously and having deep respect for him.
> Verse 6: An assarion was a very small coin. Sparrows were the cheapest food for poor people. Even so, God forgets none of them.
> Verse 7: For today: You matter to God endlessly. He knows you through and through.
> Verse 10: Jesus says that “those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.” What this means is explained in different ways. Many interpreters say: What is meant is knowingly and permanently refusing God’s forgiveness. Whoever is afraid of having committed this sin has probably not committed it, for that very reason. This fear shows that God matters to them.
> Verses 11–12: The first Christians were put on trial. Jesus promises: You are not alone.

## The rich fool (Verses 13–21)

[13] Someone in the crowd said to him,
“Teacher, tell my brother
to divide the inheritance with me.”
[14] But Jesus said to him,
“Man, who made me a judge
or a referee over you?”
[15] He said to them,
“Beware!
Keep yourselves from greed.
For a man’s life doesn’t consist
of the abundance of the things which he possesses.”
[16] He told them a parable:
“The land of a rich man
produced a very good harvest.
[17] He thought to himself,
‘What will I do?
I don’t have room
to store my crops.’
[18] He said,
‘This is what I will do:
I will pull down my barns
and build bigger ones.
There I will store all my grain
and my goods.
[19] Then I will tell my soul,
“Soul, you have many goods
laid up for many years.
Take your ease,
eat, drink, and be merry.”’
[20] But God said to him,
‘You foolish one!
Tonight your soul is required of you.
Then whose will they be,
the things you have prepared?’
[21] So is he
who lays up treasure for himself
and is not rich toward God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: Back then teachers of the law often decided disputes about inheritance. Jesus does not want to be made into a judge. He sees a deeper problem: greed. A referee is someone who settles a dispute.
> The rich man talks only to himself, and he talks only about what is his: I, my, my. He does not think of God, nor of other people who are hungry.
> It is not wrong to put supplies aside. But possessions cannot make life secure. No one knows how long they will live.
> In the Bible a “fool” means: someone who lives as if there were no God (Psalm 14:1).
> Verse 21: Whoever shares and trusts God is “rich toward God.”
> For today: Questions to ask: What am I living for? What will I take with me when I die?

## Don’t worry (Verses 22–34)

[22] He said to his disciples,
“So I tell you,
don’t be anxious about your life,
what you will eat,
or about your body,
what you will wear.
[23] Life is more than food,
and the body is more than clothing.
[24] Consider the ravens:
They don’t sow,
they don’t reap,
they have no storeroom or barn.
And God feeds them.
How much more valuable are you than birds!
[25] Which of you
by being anxious
can add a cubit to his height?
[26] If then you aren’t able to do even the least things,
why are you anxious about the rest?
[27] Consider the lilies,
how they grow.
They don’t work,
and they don’t spin.
Yet I tell you,
even Solomon in all his glory
was not dressed like one of these.
[28] If this is how God clothes the grass in the field,
which is here today
and tomorrow is thrown into the oven,
how much more will he clothe you,
you of little faith!
[29] Don’t keep looking for
what you will eat
or what you will drink.
Don’t be anxious.
[30] For the nations of the world seek after all these things.
But your Father knows
that you need these things.
[31] But seek God’s Kingdom,
and all these things will be added to you.
[32] Don’t be afraid, little flock,
for it is your Father’s good pleasure
to give you the Kingdom.
[33] Sell what you have
and give gifts to the needy.
Make for yourselves purses
which don’t grow old:
a treasure in the heavens
that doesn’t fail.
No thief comes near it,
and no moth destroys it.
[34] For where your treasure is,
there will your heart be also.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ravens counted as unclean animals (Leviticus 11:15). Even so, God takes care of them too.
> Verse 25: A cubit was about 18 inches (half a meter). The Greek word can mean a person’s height or the length of their life.
> Verse 28: Dry grass was used as fuel for baking ovens.
> Verse 30: “The nations of the world” means people who do not know God.
> Verse 32: “Little flock”: The disciples are few. But God gives them his Kingdom.
> Important: Jesus does not say that you should not work or not plan ahead. He says: Don’t let worries rule you. Trust God.
> Important: People with anxiety disorders or depression are not lacking in faith. These are illnesses that can be treated. Doctors and therapists can help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> For today: Whoever relies on God can share more generously with others.

## Be ready (Verses 35–48)

[35] “Be dressed for work
and keep your lamps burning.
[36] Be like men
watching for their lord
when he returns from the wedding feast.
When he comes and knocks,
they will open to him immediately.
[37] Blessed are those servants
whom the lord will find watching
when he comes.
Most certainly I tell you,
he will dress himself for work,
make them recline at the table,
and will come and serve them.
[38] They will be blessed
if he comes in the second or third watch
and finds them so.
[39] But know this:
If the master of the house had known
at what hour the thief was coming,
he would have stayed awake
and not allowed
his house to be broken into.
[40] So you also be ready.
For the Son of Man is coming at an hour
when you don’t expect him.”
[41] Peter said to him,
“Lord, are you telling this parable to us,
or to everybody?”
[42] The Lord said,
“Who then is the faithful and wise steward,
whom his lord will set over his household,
to give them their portion of food at the right times?
[43] Blessed is that servant
whom his lord will find doing so
when he comes.
[44] Truly I tell you,
he will set him over all that he has.
[45] But if that servant says in his heart,
‘My lord is delaying his coming,’
and begins
to beat the menservants and the maidservants,
and to eat and drink and get drunk,
[46] then the lord of that servant will come
on a day when he isn’t expecting him
and at an hour that he doesn’t know.
He will cut him in two
and give him his place with the unfaithful.
[47] That servant
who knew his lord’s will,
but didn’t prepare
or do what he wanted,
will be beaten with many stripes.
[48] But he who didn’t know,
and did things worthy of stripes,
will be beaten with few stripes.
To whomever much is given,
of him will much be required.
And to whom much was entrusted,
of him more will be asked.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 35: People tied up their long robes with a belt so that they could work or run. This means: Be ready.
> Verse 37: A big surprise: The lord himself serves his servants! That is what Jesus did at the Last Supper (Luke 22:27).
> Verse 38: The night was divided into watches. The second and third watches were the late hours of the night.
> Verse 40: Christians believe that Jesus will come again. No one knows when.
> Steward: someone who manages a household for its owner.
> Verses 42–48: This applies especially to everyone who has responsibility, including in the church. They are to take care of others, not rule over them.
> Important: The parables use pictures from the world of that time, in which slaves were beaten. That was cruel injustice. The pictures show how serious God’s judgment is. They never permit violence against people. Stripes here means blows with a whip. Whoever experiences violence can find help. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247.
> Verses 47–48: God is just. Whoever knows more bears more responsibility.
> For today: “To whomever much is given, of him will much be required.” That applies to gifts and talents, money, power and knowledge.

## Jesus brings division (Verses 49–53)

[49] “I came to throw fire on the earth.
How I wish
it were already burning!
[50] But I have a baptism to be baptized with,
and how distressed I am
until it is accomplished!
[51] Do you think
that I have come
to give peace on the earth?
I tell you, no,
but rather division.
[52] For from now on
there will be five in one house divided,
three against two,
and two against three.
[53] They will be divided:
father against son,
and son against father;
mother against daughter,
and daughter against her mother;
mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law,
and daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 49: “Fire” is a picture of God’s judgment and also of the Holy Spirit (Luke 3:16; Acts 2:3).
> Verse 50: By “baptism” Jesus means his suffering and death. He will be completely “flooded” by it.
> Verses 51–53: Jesus does not want strife. But the decision for or against him can divide families. The first Christians experienced this. Verse 53 reminds us of Micah 7:6.
> Important: This is a description, not a command. No one should have to fight or leave their family in the name of faith. Groups that want to separate people from their families are dangerous. You can find advice, for example, from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.

## Reading the signs of the times (Verses 54–59)

[54] He also said to the crowds,
“When you see
a cloud rising in the west,
immediately you say,
‘A shower is coming.’
And so it happens.
[55] When a south wind blows,
you say,
‘There will be scorching heat.’
And it happens.
[56] You hypocrites!
You know how to interpret the appearance of the earth and the sky.
But how is it that you don’t interpret this time?
[57] Why don’t you judge for yourselves
what is right?
[58] When you are going with your opponent before the magistrate,
try hard on the way
to settle with him.
Otherwise he may drag you before the judge,
the judge hand you over to the officer,
and the officer throw you into prison.
[59] I tell you,
you will by no means get out of there
until you have paid the very last penny.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Israel the rain usually comes from the Mediterranean Sea in the west. The south wind comes from the desert and brings heat.
> Jesus says: You are clever about the weather. But you do not see that God is acting through me now.
> Magistrate: a judge or official of a court.
> Verses 58–59: Whoever is in a dispute should make peace in time, before it is too late. That also applies before God: Now is the time to turn back.
> Verse 59: The WEB says “penny.” What is meant is the lepton, the smallest coin.
> For today: Making peace is worth it. Whoever is in a dispute can also get help from a mediation or counseling service.
