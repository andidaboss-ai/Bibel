# Luke – Chapter 12
### Don’t be afraid, and be ready

---

## No hypocrisy and no fear (Verses 1–12)

[1] In the meantime, many thousands of people had come together.
There were so many
that they were stepping on each other.
Jesus began to speak first to his disciples.
“Watch out for the yeast of the Pharisees.
Their yeast is hypocrisy.
[2] Everything that is covered up will be uncovered.
Everything that is hidden will become known.
[3] So what you have said in the dark
will be heard in the light.
What you have whispered in a back room
will be shouted from the rooftops.
[4] I tell you, my friends,
don’t be afraid of people who kill the body.
After that they cannot do anything more.
[5] I will show you whom you should fear.
Fear the one who has power to throw you into Gehenna
after he has killed you.
Yes, I tell you, fear him!
[6] Five sparrows are sold for two small coins, called assaria.
But God does not forget a single one of them.
[7] God has even counted every hair on your head.
So don’t be afraid.
You are worth more than many sparrows.
[8] I tell you,
someone may say in front of people
that he belongs to me.
Then the Son of Man will also say in front of God’s angels
that this person belongs to him.
[9] But someone may say in front of people
that he does not know me.
Then he will be denied in front of God’s angels.
[10] Anyone who says something against the Son of Man
will be forgiven.
But those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit
will not be forgiven.
[11] People will bring you to the synagogues,
to the rulers and the authorities.
Then don’t worry about how you will defend yourselves
or what you will say.
[12] The Holy Spirit will teach you at that time
what you must say.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Yeast is a little bit of fermenting dough. It spreads through the whole dough. Hypocrisy can spread in the same way. Hypocrisy means: acting pious on the outside, but being different inside.
> Important: Jesus warns against hypocrisy, not against Pharisees as people. Hypocrisy exists in every community, including among Christians.
> Verse 5: “Gehenna” is the Valley of Hinnom near Jerusalem. In earlier times terrible sacrifices were made there (Jeremiah 7:31). Later the name became a picture of God’s judgment. The one to fear is God. In the Bible, “fearing God” means: taking God seriously and having deep respect for him.
> Verse 6: An assarion was a very small coin. Sparrows were the cheapest food for poor people. Even so, God forgets none of them.
> Verse 7: For today: You matter to God endlessly. He knows you through and through.
> Verse 10: Jesus says that “those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.” What this means is explained in different ways. Many interpreters say: What is meant is knowingly and permanently refusing God’s forgiveness. Whoever is afraid of having committed this sin has probably not committed it, for that very reason. This fear shows that God matters to them.
> Verses 11–12: The first Christians were put on trial. Jesus promises: You are not alone.

## The rich fool (Verses 13–21)

[13] A man in the crowd said to Jesus,
“Teacher, tell my brother
to share our father’s property with me.”
[14] But Jesus said to him,
“Man, who made me a judge or a referee between you two?”
[15] Then he said to the people,
“Be careful!
Guard yourselves against every kind of greed.
A person’s life does not depend
on how many things he owns,
even if he has more than enough.”
[16] Then Jesus told them a parable.
“A rich man had land
that grew a very big harvest.
[17] He thought to himself,
‘What shall I do?
I don’t have enough room to store my crops.’
[18] Then he said,
‘I know what I will do.
I will tear down my barns
and build bigger ones.
There I will store all my grain
and all my other things.
[19] Then I will say to myself,
“You have plenty of good things stored up for many years.
Take it easy!
Eat, drink, and enjoy yourself!”’
[20] But God said to him,
‘You fool!
Tonight your life will be taken from you.
Then who will get all the things you have stored up?’
[21] That is how it will be
for anyone who gathers riches for himself
but is not rich toward God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: Back then teachers of the law often decided disputes about inheritance. Jesus does not want to be made into a judge. He sees a deeper problem: greed. A referee is someone who settles a dispute.
> The rich man talks only to himself, and he talks only about what is his: I, my, my. He does not think of God, nor of other people who are hungry.
> It is not wrong to put supplies aside. But possessions cannot make life secure. No one knows how long they will live.
> In the Bible a “fool” means: someone who lives as if there were no God (Psalm 14:1).
> Verse 21: Whoever shares and trusts God is “rich toward God.”
> For today: Questions to ask: What am I living for? What will I take with me when I die?

## Don’t worry (Verses 22–34)

[22] Then Jesus said to his disciples,
“So I tell you:
Don’t worry about your life,
about what you will eat.
Don’t worry about your body,
about what you will wear.
[23] Life is more than food.
And the body is more than clothes.
[24] Consider the ravens.
They don’t plant seeds.
They don’t harvest.
They have no storeroom or barn.
And God feeds them.
You are worth much more than birds!
[25] Can any of you make yourself about 18 inches taller by worrying?
[26] You cannot do even such a small thing.
So why do you worry about the rest?
[27] Look at the lilies and how they grow.
They do not work hard.
They do not make cloth.
But I tell you,
even King Solomon in all his glory
was not dressed as beautifully as one of these flowers.
[28] God dresses the grass in the field like this.
Today the grass is here,
and tomorrow it is thrown into the oven.
So God will surely give you clothes too.
How little faith you have!
[29] Don’t keep thinking about what you will eat
or what you will drink.
Don’t worry about it.
[30] All the nations of the world run after these things.
But your Father knows that you need them.
[31] Instead, look for God’s Kingdom.
Then you will be given these things as well.
[32] Don’t be afraid, little flock.
Your Father is happy to give you the Kingdom.
[33] Sell what you own
and give the money to the poor.
Get yourselves money bags that do not wear out.
Get yourselves a treasure in heaven that never runs out.
No thief can come near it there.
No moth can destroy it there.
[34] For where your treasure is,
there your heart will be too.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ravens counted as unclean animals (Leviticus 11:15). Even so, God takes care of them too.
> Verse 25: Word for word it says: one cubit. A cubit was about 18 inches (half a meter). The Greek word can mean a person’s height or the length of their life.
> Verse 28: Dry grass was used as fuel for baking ovens.
> Verse 30: “The nations of the world” means people who do not know God.
> Verse 32: “Little flock”: The disciples are few. But God gives them his Kingdom.
> Important: Jesus does not say that you should not work or not plan ahead. He says: Don’t let worries rule you. Trust God.
> Important: People with anxiety disorders or depression are not lacking in faith. These are illnesses that can be treated. Doctors and therapists can help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> For today: Whoever relies on God can share more generously with others.

## Be ready (Verses 35–48)

[35] “Be dressed and ready for work.
Keep your lamps burning.
[36] Be like servants who are waiting for their master
to come back from a wedding feast.
When he comes and knocks,
they open the door for him at once.
[37] Blessed are those servants
whom the master finds awake when he comes.
I tell you for certain:
He will get dressed to serve.
He will have them sit down at the table.
And he will come and serve them.
[38] He may come in the second watch of the night,
or in the third.
Blessed are those servants
if he finds them ready.
[39] But understand this:
If the owner of the house had known
at what hour the thief was coming,
he would have stayed awake.
He would not have let anyone break into his house.
[40] You must be ready too.
The Son of Man will come at an hour
when you don’t expect him.”
[41] Peter said,
“Lord, are you telling this parable only to us,
or to everyone?”
[42] The Lord said,
“Who is the faithful and wise manager?
His master puts him in charge of his household.
He is to give the other servants their share of food
at the right time.
[43] Blessed is that servant
if his master finds him doing his work
when he comes back.
[44] I tell you the truth:
The master will put him in charge of everything he owns.
[45] But that servant may say to himself,
‘My master is taking a long time to come back.’
He may begin to beat the other servants,
the men and the women.
He may eat and drink and get drunk.
[46] Then the master of that servant will come
on a day when the servant does not expect him,
at an hour he does not know.
The master will cut him in two.
He will give him a place with the unfaithful.
[47] A servant who knows what his master wants
but does not get ready
and does not do what his master wants
will be beaten many times.
[48] But a servant who does not know what his master wants
and does something that deserves a beating
will be beaten only a few times.
If someone has been given much,
much will be expected from him.
If someone has been trusted with much,
even more will be asked of him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 35: People tied up their long robes with a belt so that they could work or run. This means: Be ready.
> Verse 37: A big surprise: The lord himself serves his servants! That is what Jesus did at the Last Supper (Luke 22:27).
> Verse 38: The night was divided into watches. The second and third watches were the late hours of the night.
> Verse 40: Christians believe that Jesus will come again. No one knows when.
> The manager (in older Bibles: steward) is someone who manages a household for its owner.
> Verses 42–48: This applies especially to everyone who has responsibility, including in the church. They are to take care of others, not rule over them.
> Important: The parables use pictures from the world of that time, in which slaves were beaten. That was cruel injustice. The pictures show how serious God’s judgment is. They never permit violence against people. The beatings here mean blows with a whip. Whoever experiences violence can find help. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247.
> Verses 47–48: God is just. Whoever knows more bears more responsibility.
> For today: “If someone has been given much, much will be expected from him.” That applies to gifts and talents, money, power and knowledge.

## Jesus brings division (Verses 49–53)

[49] “I came to throw fire on the earth.
How I wish it were already burning!
[50] But I have a baptism to go through.
How troubled I am until it is finished!
[51] Do you think I came to bring peace on the earth?
No, I tell you.
I came to bring division.
[52] From now on, five people in one family will be divided.
Three will be against two,
and two against three.
[53] They will be divided:
A father will be against his son,
and a son against his father.
A mother will be against her daughter,
and a daughter against her mother.
A mother-in-law will be against her daughter-in-law,
and a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 49: “Fire” is a picture of God’s judgment and also of the Holy Spirit (Luke 3:16; Acts 2:3).
> Verse 50: By “baptism” Jesus means his suffering and death. He will be completely “flooded” by it.
> Verses 51–53: Jesus does not want strife. But the decision for or against him can divide families. The first Christians experienced this. Verse 53 reminds us of Micah 7:6.
> Important: This is a description, not a command. No one should have to fight or leave their family in the name of faith. Groups that want to separate people from their families are dangerous. You can find advice, for example, from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.

## Reading the signs of the times (Verses 54–59)

[54] Jesus also said to the crowds,
“You see a cloud coming up in the west.
At once you say, ‘It is going to rain.’
And it does.
[55] You feel the south wind blowing.
Then you say, ‘It is going to be very hot.’
And it is.
[56] You hypocrites!
You know how to understand the signs of the earth and the sky.
Why don’t you understand the signs of this time?
[57] Why don’t you decide for yourselves what is right?
[58] Someone may take you to court.
You are on your way to the magistrate.
Then try hard to settle the matter with him on the way.
If you don’t, he may drag you to the judge.
The judge will hand you over to the officer.
And the officer will throw you into prison.
[59] I tell you,
you will not get out of there
until you have paid the very last penny.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Israel the rain usually comes from the Mediterranean Sea in the west. The south wind comes from the desert and brings heat.
> Jesus says: You are clever about the weather. But you do not see that God is acting through me now.
> Magistrate: a judge or official of a court.
> Verses 58–59: Whoever is in a dispute should make peace in time, before it is too late. That also applies before God: Now is the time to turn back.
> Verse 59: The WEB says “penny.” What is meant is the lepton, the smallest coin.
> For today: Making peace is worth it. Whoever is in a dispute can also get help from a mediation or counseling service.
