# Luke – Chapter 22
### The last meal and the long night

---

## Judas betrays Jesus (Verses 1–6)

[1] The Feast of Unleavened Bread was coming near.
It is also called the Passover.
[2] The chief priests and the scribes
were looking for a way to kill Jesus.
But they were afraid of the people.
[3] Then Satan entered into Judas,
who was called Iscariot.
Judas was one of the twelve.
[4] He went to the chief priests
and the captains of the temple guard.
He talked with them
about how he could hand Jesus over to them.
[5] They were glad
and agreed to give him money.
[6] Judas agreed.
He began to look for a good time
to hand Jesus over to them
when there was no crowd around.

> **What does this mean?**
> Passover (in Hebrew Pesach) is the great Jewish feast. It remembers how Israel was set free from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 12). The Feast of Unleavened Bread belongs to it. For a week people eat only bread without yeast. Many pilgrims came to Jerusalem.
> Verse 2: The temple leaders are afraid of the people. The people are on Jesus’ side.
> Important: Jesus’ death was not the guilt of “the Jews.” Jesus, Judas, Peter and all the disciples were Jews themselves. A few powerful people acted together with the Romans. The church teaches clearly: The death of Jesus may not be blamed on all the Jews of that time, nor on the Jews of today (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).
> Important: For centuries the name Judas was used to insult Jews as “traitors.” That is hatred of Jews and a grave injustice.
> Verse 3: “Satan entered into Judas”: Luke shows that an evil power is at work here. Even so, Judas remains responsible for his decision.

---

## The Passover meal is prepared (Verses 7–13)

[7] Then the day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread came.
On this day the Passover lamb had to be killed.
[8] Jesus sent Peter and John.
He said,
“Go and get the Passover meal ready for us,
so that we can eat it.”
[9] They asked him,
“Where do you want us to get it ready?”
[10] He said to them,
“Listen! When you go into the city,
a man carrying a jar of water will meet you.
Follow him into the house he goes into.
[11] Say to the owner of the house,
‘The Teacher asks you:
“Where is the guest room
where I can eat the Passover meal with my disciples?”’
[12] He will show you a large room upstairs.
It is already set up.
Get everything ready there.”
[13] Peter and John went.
They found everything just as Jesus had told them.
And they got the Passover meal ready.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover lambs were sacrificed in the temple and then eaten in the families, with bread without yeast, bitter herbs and wine.
> Verse 10: Back then carrying water was usually women’s work. A man with a jar of water stood out. He was a good sign to recognize.
> Jesus has prepared everything. He goes his way knowingly.

---

## The Last Supper (Verses 14–23)

[14] When the time came,
Jesus sat down at the table with the 12 apostles.
[15] He said to them,
“I have wanted very much
to eat this Passover meal with you
before I suffer.
[16] For I tell you,
I will not eat it again
until it comes true in God’s Kingdom.”
[17] Then Jesus took a cup
and thanked God for it.
He said,
“Take this and share it among yourselves.
[18] For I tell you,
from now on I will not drink wine again
until God’s Kingdom comes.”
[19] Then Jesus took bread
and thanked God for it.
He broke it and gave it to them.
He said,
“This is my body,
which is given for you.
Do this in memory of me.”
[20] In the same way, he took the cup after the meal.
He said,
“This cup is the new covenant in my blood,
which is poured out for you.
[21] But look!
The man who is going to hand me over
is here with me at the table.
[22] The Son of Man will go the way
that has been decided for him.
But how terrible it will be for the man
who hands him over!”
[23] Then the apostles began to ask each other
which of them would do this.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Luke there are two cups: one before the bread (verse 17) and one after the meal (verse 20). At the Jewish Passover meal people drink several cups of wine.
> Verses 19–20: These words are the origin of the Lord’s Supper, also called the Eucharist or Holy Communion. To this day Christians celebrate it “in memory of” Jesus. The words are similar to those in Paul (1 Corinthians 11:23–25).
> The churches understand the Lord’s Supper in different ways. Catholic and Orthodox Christians believe that bread and wine truly become the body and blood of Christ. Lutheran Christians believe that Christ is truly present in, with and under the bread and wine. Reformed and other Protestant Christians stress Christ’s spiritual presence and the remembering. What unites them all: Jesus gives himself.
> “New covenant” is a reminder of Jeremiah 31:31–34. This does not mean that God’s covenant with Israel has been canceled (Romans 11:29). Covenant: a binding agreement.
> In verses 19b–20, the words about the body given for you and the blood poured out for you are missing in a single old Greek manuscript and in some old translations. Almost all manuscripts have them.
> Verse 21: Judas is at the meal too. Jesus does not shut him out.

## Who is the greatest? (Verses 24–30)

[24] The apostles also began to argue.
They argued about which of them was the most important.
[25] Jesus said to them,
“The kings of the nations rule over their people with force.
And those who have power over the people
are called ‘benefactors.’
[26] But you must not be like that.
The most important one among you
should be like the youngest.
And the leader should be like a servant.
[27] Who is more important:
the person who sits at the table,
or the person who serves him?
Isn’t it the one who sits at the table?
But I am among you
like one who serves.
[28] You have stayed with me
in my hard times.
[29] So I give you a kingdom,
just as my Father gave a kingdom to me.
[30] You will eat and drink at my table
in my Kingdom.
And you will sit on thrones,
judging the 12 tribes of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Of all times, at the last meal the disciples argue about who is the most important.
> Verse 25: “Benefactors” (people who do good) was a title of honor that rulers liked to give themselves, even when they misused their power.
> Verses 26–27: With Jesus everything is turned around. Whoever leads should serve. Jesus himself is like a servant. In the Gospel of John he washes his disciples’ feet (John 13).
> Important: This applies to everyone who has responsibility in the church. Power in the church must never be misused.
> Verse 30: “Judging the 12 tribes of Israel” here probably means: leading, ruling, like the judges in the Old Testament. The 12 tribes stand for the whole people of Israel, whom God wants to gather.

## Jesus announces that Peter will deny him (Verses 31–34)

[31] The Lord said,
“Simon, Simon, listen!
Satan has asked to have all of you.
He wants to shake you like wheat in a sieve.
[32] But I have prayed for you
that your faith will not fail.
And when you have turned back,
make your brothers strong.”
[33] Peter said to Jesus,
“Lord, I am ready to go to prison with you.
I am even ready to die with you!”
[34] Jesus said,
“I tell you, Peter,
before the rooster crows today,
you will say three times
that you don’t know me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: When grain is sifted, it is shaken back and forth. In the same way the disciples will be hard tested in the coming night. “All of you” means all the disciples.
> Verse 32: Jesus prays for Peter. He knows that Peter will fail. But he does not give up on him. He even gives him a task for the time afterward.
> For today: Even someone who has failed can make a new start and be there for others.

## Two swords (Verses 35–38)

[35] Then Jesus said to the apostles,
“I sent you out without a money bag,
without a traveler’s bag, and without sandals.
Did you need anything?”
They said,
“No, nothing.”
[36] Jesus said to them,
“But now, if you have a money bag, take it.
Take a traveler’s bag too.
And if you have no sword,
sell your coat and buy one.
[37] For I tell you,
this Scripture must come true about me:
‘He was counted among the criminals.’
Yes, what is written about me is coming true.”
[38] The apostles said,
“Lord, look! Here are two swords.”
Jesus said to them,
“That is enough.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This passage is hard to understand. Jesus says: Now a hard time begins. The disciples will no longer be welcomed everywhere.
> Verse 37 quotes Isaiah 53:12. Jesus will be treated like a criminal. The WEB, our English source text, says “transgressors”: people who break the law.
> Verse 38: “That is enough” probably means: Enough of this! Once again the disciples take Jesus literally. Two swords would have been ridiculously few against an armed troop anyway.
> Important: Jesus is not calling for violence. A few hours later he forbids the use of the sword and heals the man who was wounded (verses 49–51). No one may appeal to this passage to justify violence in the name of faith.

## Jesus prays on the Mount of Olives (Verses 39–46)

[39] Jesus left the city
and went to the Mount of Olives, as he often did.
His disciples followed him.
[40] When he came to the place,
he said to them,
“Pray that you will not give in to temptation.”
[41] Then he went a short distance away from them,
about as far as a person can throw a stone.
He knelt down and prayed,
[42] “Father, if you are willing,
take this cup away from me.
But let your will be done, not mine.”
[43] Then an angel from heaven came to him
and gave him strength.
[44] Jesus was in great fear and pain.
He prayed even harder.
His sweat was like large drops of blood
falling to the ground.
[45] When he got up from praying,
he went back to the disciples.
He found them sleeping.
They were worn out because they were so sad.
[46] He said to them,
“Why are you sleeping?
Get up and pray
that you will not give in to temptation.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Matthew and Mark the garden on the Mount of Olives is called “Gethsemane.” Luke only names the Mount of Olives.
> Verse 42: The “cup” is a picture for the suffering that awaits Jesus. Jesus is afraid, like every human being. But he entrusts himself to God.
> Verses 43–44: These two verses are missing in many very old manuscripts. The WEB, our English source text, has them. They show how great Jesus’ distress was.
> Verse 44: Luke says “like” drops of blood. It is a comparison: The sweat fell in large, heavy drops. The WEB says he was “in agony”: in very great fear and pain.
> Verse 45: Only Luke says: The disciples were sleeping “because they were so sad.” He excuses them a little. Grief can make you tired.
> For today: Jesus was afraid too. Whoever is afraid may pray and may tell God everything. Whoever is in great distress can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Jesus is arrested (Verses 47–53)

[47] Jesus was still speaking
when a crowd came.
The man called Judas,
one of the twelve,
was leading them.
He came close to Jesus to kiss him.
[48] But Jesus said to him,
“Judas, are you handing over the Son of Man with a kiss?”
[49] The people around Jesus saw what was going to happen.
They said,
“Lord, should we fight with our swords?”
[50] And one of them struck the servant of the high priest
and cut off his right ear.
[51] But Jesus said,
“Let me at least do this.”
He touched the man’s ear
and healed him.
[52] Then Jesus spoke to the chief priests,
the captains of the temple guard,
and the elders who had come to arrest him.
“Have you come out with swords and clubs,
as if I were a robber?
[53] Every day I was with you in the temple.
But you did not lay a hand on me.
But this is your hour.
Now the power of darkness rules.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then a kiss was a common greeting between a student and his teacher. Judas misuses this sign of friendship.
> Verse 51: The WEB translates: “Let me at least do this.” Others translate it as an order to stop, enough of this. What is clear: Jesus stops the violence. Only Luke tells that Jesus heals the ear. Even in this hour he heals an opponent.
> John names the servant Malchus and the man with the sword Peter (John 18:10).
> Verse 53: They come at night, in secret, because they fear the people. “The power of darkness”: For a short time evil seems to win.
> For today: Jesus does not answer violence with violence, but with healing.

## Peter denies Jesus (Verses 54–62)

[54] They arrested Jesus and led him away.
They took him into the house of the high priest.
Peter followed far behind.
[55] People had made a fire in the middle of the courtyard.
They were sitting around it.
Peter sat down with them.
[56] A servant girl saw Peter sitting in the light of the fire.
She looked closely at him and said,
“This man was with Jesus too.”
[57] But Peter said it was not true.
He said,
“Woman, I don’t know him.”
[58] A little later someone else saw Peter.
He said,
“You are one of them too!”
But Peter said,
“Man, I am not!”
[59] About an hour later another man said firmly,
“This man was with Jesus too, for sure!
He is from Galilee!”
[60] But Peter said,
“Man, I don’t know what you are talking about!”
At that moment, while Peter was still speaking,
a rooster crowed.
[61] The Lord turned and looked at Peter.
Then Peter remembered what the Lord had said to him:
“Before the rooster crows,
you will say three times that you don’t know me.”
[62] Peter went outside
and cried bitterly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Peter had promised to go to death with Jesus (verse 33). Now he is afraid and says three times that he does not know Jesus.
> Verse 59: People from Galilee could be recognized by the way they spoke (compare Matthew 26:73).
> Verse 61: Only Luke tells: Jesus turns and looks at Peter. It is not a look of anger, but a look that brings Peter to his senses.
> Verse 62: Peter weeps. His regret is real. After the resurrection Jesus will forgive him (compare Luke 24:34; John 21:15–19).
> For today: Anyone can fail, even the strongest. But failure is not the end.

## Jesus is mocked and questioned (Verses 63–71)

[63] The men who were guarding Jesus
made fun of him and beat him.
[64] They covered his eyes.
They hit him in the face and said,
“Prophesy!
Tell us who hit you!”
[65] They said many other bad things to him
and insulted him.
[66] When morning came,
the elders of the people met together.
The chief priests and the scribes were there too.
They brought Jesus to their council.
They said,
[67] “If you are the Christ, tell us!”
Jesus said to them,
“If I tell you,
you will not believe me.
[68] And if I ask you a question,
you will not answer me
or let me go.
[69] But from now on,
the Son of Man will sit at the right side of the mighty God.”
[70] They all asked,
“So are you the Son of God?”
He said to them,
“You say it, because I am.”
[71] They said,
“Why do we need any more witnesses?
We ourselves have heard it from his own mouth!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 63–65: Jesus is tormented and mocked. He does not fight back.
> Important: Torturing and humiliating prisoners is always wrong, no matter who it is done to.
> Verse 66: The council (in Greek “Sanhedrin”) was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem. Under the Romans it had only limited power. Probably only the Romans were allowed to carry out a death sentence (compare John 18:31).
> Verse 69: Jesus is speaking of Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13. He says: God will honor me and raise me up to himself.
> Verse 70: “You say it, because I am”: Jesus openly confesses who he is.
> Important: Here individual members of the leadership of that time were acting. Not all council members agreed: Joseph of Arimathea had not consented (Luke 23:50–51). The Jewish people as a whole bear no guilt for Jesus’ death.
