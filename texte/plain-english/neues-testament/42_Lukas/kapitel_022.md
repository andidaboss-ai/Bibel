# Luke – Chapter 22
### The last meal and the long night

---

## Judas betrays Jesus (Verses 1–6)

[1] The feast of unleavened bread,
which is called the Passover,
was coming near.
[2] The chief priests and the scribes
were looking for a way
to put Jesus to death.
For they were afraid of the people.
[3] Then Satan entered into Judas,
who was also called Iscariot.
He was one of the twelve.
[4] He went away
and talked with the chief priests and the captains of the temple guard
about how he might hand Jesus over to them.
[5] They were glad
and agreed
to give him money.
[6] He agreed.
And he looked for an opportunity
to hand Jesus over to them
when no crowd was around.

> **What does this mean?**
> Passover (in Hebrew Pesach) is the great Jewish feast. It remembers how Israel was set free from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 12). The feast of unleavened bread belongs to it. Unleavened bread: bread made without yeast. Many pilgrims came to Jerusalem.
> Verse 2: The temple leaders are afraid of the people. The people are on Jesus’ side.
> Important: Jesus’ death was not the guilt of “the Jews.” Jesus, Judas, Peter and all the disciples were Jews themselves. A few powerful people acted together with the Romans. The church teaches clearly: The death of Jesus may not be blamed on all the Jews of that time, nor on the Jews of today (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).
> Important: For centuries the name Judas was used to insult Jews as “traitors.” That is hatred of Jews and a grave injustice.
> Verse 3: “Satan entered into Judas”: Luke shows that an evil power is at work here. Even so, Judas remains responsible for his decision.

---

## The Passover meal is prepared (Verses 7–13)

[7] Then the day of unleavened bread came,
on which the Passover lamb had to be sacrificed.
[8] Jesus sent Peter and John
and said,
“Go and prepare the Passover for us,
so that we may eat it.”
[9] They said to him,
“Where do you want us to prepare it?”
[10] He said to them,
“When you have entered into the city,
a man will meet you
carrying a pitcher of water.
Follow him into the house
which he enters.
[11] Tell the master of the house,
‘The Teacher says to you,
“Where is the guest room,
where I may eat the Passover with my disciples?”’
[12] He will show you a large upper room,
already furnished.
Make preparations there.”
[13] They went
and found things
just as Jesus had told them.
And they prepared the Passover.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover lambs were sacrificed in the temple and then eaten in the families, with unleavened bread, bitter herbs and wine.
> Verse 10: Back then carrying water was usually women’s work. A man with a pitcher of water stood out. He was a good sign to recognize.
> Jesus has prepared everything. He goes his way knowingly.

---

## The Last Supper (Verses 14–23)

[14] When the hour had come,
he sat down at the table with the twelve apostles.
[15] He said to them,
“I have earnestly desired
to eat this Passover with you
before I suffer.
[16] For I tell you,
I will no longer eat of it
until it is fulfilled in God’s Kingdom.”
[17] He took a cup,
gave thanks,
and said,
“Take this
and share it among yourselves.
[18] For I tell you,
from now on I will not drink again
from the fruit of the vine
until God’s Kingdom comes.”
[19] Then he took bread,
gave thanks,
broke it,
gave it to them,
and said,
“This is my body
which is given for you.
Do this in memory of me.”
[20] In the same way, after supper he took the cup
and said,
“This cup is the new covenant in my blood,
which is poured out for you.
[21] But look:
The hand of him who betrays me
is with me on the table.
[22] The Son of Man indeed goes the way
that has been determined.
But woe to that man
through whom he is betrayed!”
[23] Then they began
to ask each other
which of them it was who would do this.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Luke there are two cups: one before the bread (verse 17) and one after the meal (verse 20). At the Jewish Passover meal people drink several cups of wine.
> Verses 19–20: These words are the origin of the Lord’s Supper, also called the Eucharist or Holy Communion. To this day Christians celebrate it “in memory of” Jesus. The words are similar to those in Paul (1 Corinthians 11:23–25).
> The churches understand the Lord’s Supper in different ways. Catholic and Orthodox Christians believe that bread and wine truly become the body and blood of Christ. Lutheran Christians believe that Christ is truly present in, with and under the bread and wine. Reformed and other Protestant Christians stress Christ’s spiritual presence and the remembering. What unites them all: Jesus gives himself.
> “New covenant” is a reminder of Jeremiah 31:31–34. This does not mean that God’s covenant with Israel has been canceled (Romans 11:29). Covenant: a binding agreement.
> In verses 19b–20, the words about the body given for you and the blood poured out for you are missing in a single old Greek manuscript and in some old translations. Almost all manuscripts have them.
> Verse 21: Judas is at the meal too. Jesus does not shut him out.

## Who is the greatest? (Verses 24–30)

[24] A dispute also arose among the disciples
about which of them was considered to be greatest.
[25] Jesus said to them,
“The kings of the nations lord it over them.
And those who have authority over them are called ‘benefactors.’
[26] But not so with you.
Rather, the one who is greater among you,
let him become like the younger.
And the one who leads,
let him be like one who serves.
[27] For who is greater:
the one who sits at the table,
or the one who serves?
Isn’t it the one who sits at the table?
But I am among you
as one who serves.
[28] You are those
who have stayed with me
in my trials.
[29] So I give you a kingdom,
just as my Father gave one to me.
[30] You will eat and drink at my table
in my Kingdom.
And you will sit on thrones,
judging the twelve tribes of Israel.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Of all times, at the last meal the disciples argue about who is the most important.
> Verse 25: “Benefactors” (people who do good) was a title of honor that rulers liked to give themselves, even when they misused their power.
> Verses 26–27: With Jesus everything is turned around. Whoever leads should serve. Jesus himself is like a servant. In the Gospel of John he washes his disciples’ feet (John 13).
> Important: This applies to everyone who has responsibility in the church. Power in the church must never be misused.
> Verse 30: “Judging the twelve tribes of Israel” here probably means: leading, ruling, like the judges in the Old Testament. The twelve stand for the whole people of Israel, whom God wants to gather.

## Jesus announces that Peter will deny him (Verses 31–34)

[31] The Lord said,
“Simon, Simon,
Satan asked to have all of you,
so that he might sift you
like wheat.
[32] But I prayed for you,
that your faith wouldn’t fail.
And when you have turned back,
strengthen your brothers.”
[33] Peter said to him,
“Lord, I am ready to go with you
both to prison
and to death!”
[34] He said,
“I tell you, Peter,
the rooster will not crow today
until you have denied three times
that you know me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: When grain is sifted, it is shaken back and forth. In the same way the disciples will be hard tested in the coming night. “All of you” means all the disciples.
> Verse 32: Jesus prays for Peter. He knows that Peter will fail. But he does not give up on him. He even gives him a task for the time afterward.
> For today: Even someone who has failed can make a new start and be there for others.

## Two swords (Verses 35–38)

[35] Jesus said to them,
“When I sent you out without purse, bag,
and sandals,
did you lack anything?”
They said,
“Nothing.”
[36] Then he said to them,
“But now:
Whoever has a purse,
let him take it,
and likewise a bag.
And whoever has none,
let him sell his cloak
and buy a sword.
[37] For I tell you,
this which is written
must still be fulfilled in me:
‘He was counted with transgressors.’
For what is written about me
is now being fulfilled.”
[38] They said,
“Lord,
here are two swords.”
He said to them,
“That is enough.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This passage is hard to understand. Jesus says: Now a hard time begins. The disciples will no longer be welcomed everywhere.
> Verse 37 quotes Isaiah 53:12. Jesus will be treated like a criminal. Transgressors: people who break the law.
> Verse 38: “That is enough” probably means: Enough of this! Once again the disciples take Jesus literally. Two swords would have been ridiculously few against an armed troop anyway.
> Important: Jesus is not calling for violence. A few hours later he forbids the use of the sword and heals the man who was wounded (verses 49–51). No one may appeal to this passage to justify violence in the name of faith.

## Jesus prays on the Mount of Olives (Verses 39–46)

[39] Jesus came out
and went, as his custom was,
to the Mount of Olives.
His disciples also followed him.
[40] When he had come to the place,
he said to them,
“Pray
that you don’t enter into temptation.”
[41] He went away from them about a stone’s throw,
knelt down,
and prayed,
[42] “Father, if you are willing,
remove this cup from me.
Nevertheless, not my will,
but yours, be done.”
[43] An angel from heaven appeared to him
and gave him strength.
[44] He was in agony
and prayed more earnestly.
His sweat became like great drops of blood
falling down on the ground.
[45] He rose up from his prayer
and came to the disciples.
He found them sleeping
because of grief.
[46] He said to them,
“Why do you sleep?
Rise and pray
that you may not enter into temptation.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In Matthew and Mark the garden on the Mount of Olives is called “Gethsemane.” Luke only names the Mount of Olives.
> Verse 42: The “cup” is a picture for the suffering that awaits Jesus. Jesus is afraid, like every human being. But he entrusts himself to God.
> Verses 43–44: These two verses are missing in many very old manuscripts. The WEB has them. They show how great Jesus’ distress was.
> Verse 44: Luke says “like” drops of blood. It is a comparison: The sweat fell in large, heavy drops. Agony: very great fear and pain.
> Verse 45: Only Luke says: The disciples were sleeping “because of grief.” He excuses them a little. Grief can make you tired.
> For today: Jesus was afraid too. Whoever is afraid may pray and may tell God everything. Whoever is in great distress can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Jesus is arrested (Verses 47–53)

[47] While he was still speaking,
a crowd appeared.
The one who was called Judas,
one of the twelve,
was leading them.
He came near to Jesus
to kiss him.
[48] But Jesus said to him,
“Judas, do you betray the Son of Man with a kiss?”
[49] When those who were around Jesus saw
what was about to happen,
they said to him,
“Lord, shall we strike with the sword?”
[50] And one of them struck the servant of the high priest
and cut off his right ear.
[51] But Jesus answered,
“Let me at least do this.”
And he touched the ear
and healed him.
[52] Then Jesus said to the chief priests,
the captains of the temple guard,
and the elders
who had come against him,
“Have you come out as against a robber,
with swords and clubs?
[53] Every day I was with you in the temple.
You didn’t stretch out your hands against me.
But this is your hour,
and the power of darkness.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then a kiss was a common greeting between a student and his teacher. Judas misuses this sign of friendship.
> Verse 51: The WEB translates: “Let me at least do this.” Others translate it as an order to stop, enough of this. What is clear: Jesus stops the violence. Only Luke tells that Jesus heals the ear. Even in this hour he heals an opponent.
> John names the servant Malchus and the man with the sword Peter (John 18:10).
> Verse 53: They come at night, in secret, because they fear the people. “The power of darkness”: For a short time evil seems to win.
> For today: Jesus does not answer violence with violence, but with healing.

## Peter denies Jesus (Verses 54–62)

[54] They seized Jesus,
led him away,
and brought him into the high priest’s house.
Peter followed from a distance.
[55] They had lit a fire in the middle of the courtyard
and had sat down together.
Peter sat among them.
[56] A servant girl saw him sitting in the light of the fire.
She looked closely at him
and said,
“This man also was with him.”
[57] But he denied Jesus
and said,
“Woman, I don’t know him.”
[58] After a little while someone else saw him
and said,
“You also are one of them!”
But Peter answered,
“Man, I am not!”
[59] After about one hour
another man insisted,
“Truly this man also was with him,
for he is a Galilean!”
[60] But Peter said,
“Man, I don’t know
what you are talking about!”
Immediately, while he was still speaking,
a rooster crowed.
[61] The Lord turned
and looked at Peter.
Then Peter remembered the Lord’s word,
how he had said to him,
“Before the rooster crows
you will deny me three times.”
[62] And he went out
and wept bitterly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Peter had promised to go to death with Jesus (verse 33). Now he is afraid and says three times that he does not know Jesus.
> Verse 59: People from Galilee could be recognized by the way they spoke (compare Matthew 26:73).
> Verse 61: Only Luke tells: Jesus turns and looks at Peter. It is not a look of anger, but a look that brings Peter to his senses.
> Verse 62: Peter weeps. His regret is real. After the resurrection Jesus will forgive him (compare Luke 24:34; John 21:15–19).
> For today: Anyone can fail, even the strongest. But failure is not the end.

## Jesus is mocked and questioned (Verses 63–71)

[63] The men who were guarding Jesus
mocked him
and beat him.
[64] They blindfolded him,
struck him on the face,
and asked him,
“Prophesy!
Who is the one who struck you?”
[65] And they said many other things against him,
insulting him.
[66] As soon as it was day,
the assembly of the elders of the people gathered,
both chief priests and scribes.
They led him away into their council
and said,
[67] “If you are the Christ,
tell us.”
But he said to them,
“If I tell you,
you won’t believe.
[68] And if I ask you something,
you will not answer me
or let me go.
[69] From now on,
the Son of Man will be seated at the right hand of the power of God.”
[70] They all said,
“Are you then the Son of God?”
He said to them,
“You say it,
because I am.”
[71] They said,
“Why do we need any more witness?
For we ourselves have heard from his own mouth!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 63–65: Jesus is tormented and mocked. He does not fight back.
> Important: Torturing and humiliating prisoners is always wrong, no matter who it is done to.
> Verse 66: The council (in Greek “Sanhedrin”) was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem. Under the Romans it had only limited power. Probably only the Romans were allowed to carry out a death sentence (compare John 18:31).
> Verse 69: Jesus is speaking of Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13. He says: God will honor me and raise me up to himself.
> Verse 70: “You say it, because I am”: Jesus openly confesses who he is.
> Important: Here individual members of the leadership of that time were acting. Not all council members agreed: Joseph of Arimathea had not consented (Luke 23:50–51). The Jewish people as a whole bear no guilt for Jesus’ death.
