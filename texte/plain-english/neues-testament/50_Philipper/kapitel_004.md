# Philippians – Chapter 4
### Rejoice in the Lord always

---

## Stand firm and stay together (Verses 1–3)

[1] So,
my dear brothers,
whom I love and long for,
my joy and crown:
Stand firm in the Lord in this way,
my dear friends.
[2] I urge Euodia,
and I urge Syntyche:
Be of one mind in the Lord.
[3] Yes,
I ask you also,
true partner:
Help these women.
For they struggled with me for the Good News,
together with Clement
and my other fellow workers.
Their names are in the book of life.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Crown”: This means the victory wreath that a winner received at a competition. For Paul the church is like a winner’s prize.
> Verses 2–3: Euodia and Syntyche were two women who had an important role in the church. Paul calls them women who struggled with him for the Good News. Women helped lead and shape the first churches, as Lydia already did in Philippi (Acts 16).
> The two probably had a quarrel. Paul doesn’t take sides. He asks both of them in the same way and asks a third person for help. That is a good example of settling a dispute.
> We don’t know who the “true partner” is. The Greek word could also be a name: *Syzygus*.
> “Book of life”: A picture showing that God knows every person who belongs to him.

---

## Joy, prayer and peace (Verses 4–9)

[4] Rejoice in the Lord
always!
Again I will say:
“Rejoice!”
[5] Let your gentleness be known to all people.
The Lord is at hand.
[6] Don’t worry about anything.
But in everything bring your requests to God,
by prayer and petition
with thanksgiving.
[7] And the peace of God,
which is greater than all understanding,
will guard your hearts and your thoughts
in Christ Jesus.
[8] Finally,
brothers:
Whatever is true,
whatever is honorable,
whatever is just,
whatever is pure,
whatever is lovely,
whatever has a good reputation,
if there is any virtue
and anything worthy of praise:
think about these things.
Virtue means: a good quality in a person.
[9] What you learned
and received
and heard
and saw in me,
do that.
And the God of peace will be with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Paul writes this sentence in prison. Joy “in the Lord” doesn’t depend on everything going well. It comes from trusting God.
> Important: Christians don’t always have to be cheerful. Sadness, fear and lament also have a place before God. The Psalms are full of them.
> Verse 5: “The Lord is at hand” can mean: Jesus is coming again soon. It can also mean: God is near to us now.
> Verse 6: “Don’t worry about anything” is no reproach to people who have worries. It is an invitation: Bring your worries to God. If you suffer from strong fears or worries, you may also get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 7: God’s peace is greater than we can understand. It guards our hearts like a guard watches over a city.
> Verse 8: Paul lists values that many people outside the church also knew and valued. Christians may value what is good wherever they find it.

## Thanks for the church’s help (Verses 10–20)

[10] I rejoice greatly in the Lord
that now at last you could think of me again.
You did indeed think of me,
but you had no opportunity
to show it.
[11] I don’t say this
because I am in need.
For I have learned
to be content in whatever situation I am.
[12] I know how to live with little,
and I know how to live with plenty.
In any and all circumstances
I have learned the secret:
both to be filled and to be hungry,
both to have plenty and to be in need.
[13] I can do all things through Christ
who strengthens me.
[14] Still, you did well
to share in my trouble.
[15] You yourselves know,
you Philippians:
In the beginning of the Good News,
when I left Macedonia,
no church shared with me in giving and receiving
but you only.
[16] For even in Thessalonica you sent me something for my needs
more than once.
[17] It is not the gift I am looking for.
I am looking for the fruit
that is credited to your account.
[18] I have all things,
and more than enough.
I am well supplied,
since I received from Epaphroditus
what you sent.
It is like a sweet-smelling fragrance,
a sacrifice
that pleases God and that he gladly accepts.
[19] My God will supply every need of yours
according to his riches in glory
in Christ Jesus.
[20] Now to our God and Father
be the glory forever and ever!
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: Paul experienced hunger and poverty, but also good times. He learned to stay calm in both.
> Verse 13 is a very well-known verse. It doesn’t mean: With Christ I can reach every goal, win every competition or become rich. It means: With Christ I can endure every situation, even hunger and need (verse 12).
> Verses 15–16: The church in Philippi faithfully supported Paul from the beginning. In Corinth, on the other hand, Paul accepted no money (compare 2 Corinthians 11:9).
> Verse 18: The church’s gift is like a sacrifice to God. Whoever helps others honors God by doing so.
> Verse 19: A promise: God provides what you really need. It is no promise of wealth.

## Greetings (Verses 21–23)

[21] Greet every saint in Christ Jesus.
The brothers
who are with me
greet you.
[22] All the saints greet you,
especially those of Caesar’s household.
[23] The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ
be with you all.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: “Those of Caesar’s household”: This doesn’t mean the emperor’s family. It means slaves and officials in the emperor’s service. Even then there were Christians there. The Good News also reached people close to power.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Philippians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Joy in spite of prison:** Paul is in prison and doesn’t know whether he will survive. And yet he writes again and again about joy (chapters 1 to 4).
> **To live is Christ:** Paul holds on to life because others need him. And he is not afraid of death, because then he will be with Christ (chapter 1).
> **The song of Christ:** Jesus humbled himself to the point of death on the cross. God raised him to the highest place. That is the example for all Christians: to serve one another, not to rule (chapter 2).
> **Good friends:** Timothy and Epaphroditus show how to be there for one another (chapter 2).
> **Everything for Christ:** Paul was proud of his Jewish background. But Christ is even more valuable to him. He runs toward the goal like a runner (chapter 3).
> **Bringing worries to God:** prayer, thanks and God’s peace, which is greater than our understanding (chapter 4).
> **Being content:** With Christ you can get through good and hard times (chapter 4).
> **For today:** Joy is possible, even in hard times. And Christians should not only think of themselves, but also of others.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter to the Colossians. It says: Christ is Lord over all creation.
