# Philippians – Chapter 1
### To live is Christ

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Philippians

Philippi was a Roman city in the north of what is now Greece. There Paul founded the first Christian church in Europe (Acts 16). Among the first Christians there were the businesswoman Lydia and a jailer with his family.
Paul had an especially warm relationship with this church. The Christians in Philippi supported him again and again, also with money.
Paul writes the letter from prison. He doesn’t know whether he will be set free or executed. Where the prison was is not certain: perhaps in Rome, perhaps in Ephesus or in Caesarea.
And yet it is a letter full of joy. The words “joy” and “rejoice” appear very often.
In chapter 2 there is a famous song about Jesus, who humbled himself.
The letter to the Philippians has 4 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul and Timothy,
servants of Jesus Christ,
write to all the saints in Christ Jesus
who are at Philippi,
with the overseers and servants:
[2] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Timothy was a close coworker of Paul. He was there when the church in Philippi was founded.
> “Saints” are all Christians. They belong to God.
> “Overseers and servants”: These are leadership roles in the church. Later these words became the offices of *bishop* and *deacon*. At that time they were probably organized more simply.

---

## Thanks and prayer for the church (Verses 3–11)

[3] I thank my God
every time
I remember you.
[4] Always,
when I pray for you all,
I pray with joy.
[5] For you are my partners
in the Good News,
from the first day until now.
[6] And I am completely sure:
God began a good work in you.
He will also complete it
until the day of Jesus Christ.
[7] It is right
for me to think this way about all of you.
For I have you in my heart.
You all share with me in grace:
when I am in chains
and when I defend the Good News
and confirm it.
[8] For God is my witness
how I long for all of you
with the tender love of Christ Jesus.
[9] And so I pray
that your love may grow richer and richer
in knowledge
and in all discernment,
[10] so that you can test
what is truly excellent.
So may you be sincere
and without offense
until the day of Christ,
[11] filled with the fruit of righteousness
that comes through Jesus Christ,
to the glory and praise of God.

*discernment* = the ability to judge well what is right

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Already here we find the word “joy.” It runs through the whole letter.
> Verse 5: “Partners”: The church supported Paul with prayer and with money. It shared in his work (compare 4:15–16).
> Verse 6 is a word of comfort: God doesn’t give up on what he has begun. Even if we ourselves are not finished yet.
> Verses 9–10: Love is not blind. It needs understanding, so that you see what really helps.

---

## Prison helps the Good News (Verses 12–18)

[12] I want you to know,
brothers:
What has happened to me
has actually helped the Good News.
[13] For it has become clear to the whole palace guard
and to all the rest
that I am in chains because of Christ.
[14] And most of the brothers in the Lord
have gained confidence through my chains.
They are much bolder
to speak the word of God without fear.
[15] Some indeed preach Christ out of envy and strife,
but others out of good will.
[16] The former preach Christ insincerely,
out of selfish ambition.
They think
they are making my suffering in chains even harder.
[17] The others do it out of love.
They know:
I am appointed
to defend the Good News.
[18] What does it matter?
The main thing is
that Christ is proclaimed,
in every way,
whether in pretense or in truth.
I rejoice in this.
Yes,
and I will go on rejoicing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: “Palace guard”: These were soldiers who guarded Paul. They learned why he was a prisoner. So they heard about Jesus.
> Verses 15–17: Paul names no names. There were probably other preachers who competed with him.
> Verse 18: Paul is generous. It doesn’t matter to him who is right or becomes famous. What matters to him is that people hear about Jesus.
> For today: Among Christians, too, there is envy and competition. Paul shows another way: to rejoice when good things happen, even when others do them.

## Life or death (Verses 19–26)

[19] For I know:
This will turn out for my salvation,
through your prayers
and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ.
[20] This is my eager expectation and hope:
I will in no way be put to shame.
But with all boldness,
as always,
so now also,
Christ will be magnified in my body,
whether by my life
or by my death.
[21] For to me to live is Christ,
and to die is gain.
[22] But if I go on living in this body,
my work will bear fruit.
I don’t know
what I should choose.
[23] I am torn between the two.
I have the desire
to depart and be with Christ.
That would be far better.
[24] But it is more needful for your sake
that I remain in this body.
[25] And because I am convinced of this,
I know:
I will remain.
Yes,
I will remain with you all,
so that you make progress in the faith
and have joy.
[26] So you will have even more reason
to boast in Christ Jesus
because of me,
when I am with you again.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: “Salvation” can mean here: being set free from prison. It can also mean: being saved by God, no matter how the trial ends.
> Verse 21: A famous sentence. Paul means: My whole life belongs to Christ. And if I die, I lose nothing, because then I am with Christ.
> Verses 22–24: Paul is in prison and has to expect to be executed. He says honestly: To be with Christ would be good. But he chooses life, because others need him.
> Very important: These verses are no encouragement to end your own life. Paul chooses life (verses 24–25). If you long for death because life is too hard, you are not alone. Please talk to someone about it. Help is available around the clock. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. Children and young people in the UK can call Childline at 0800 1111. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Stand firm together (Verses 27–30)

[27] Only one thing:
Let your way of life be worthy of the Good News of Christ.
Whether I come and see you
or am absent:
I want to hear about you,
that you stand firm in one spirit,
that you strive together with one soul
for the faith of the Good News.
[28] Don’t be frightened in anything by your opponents.
For them this is a sign of destruction,
but for you a sign of salvation,
and that from God.
[29] For it has been granted to you
for the sake of Christ:
not only to believe in him,
but also to suffer for him.
[30] You have the same struggle
that you saw in me
and now hear
that I am in.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: The Greek word for “way of life” here literally means *to live as citizens*. Philippi was proud of its Roman citizenship. Paul says: Live as good citizens of God’s Kingdom.
> Verse 28: The church in Philippi had opponents in the city. The letter doesn’t say exactly who they were.
> Verses 29–30: Suffering is not something good in itself. But whoever belongs to Christ can get into trouble for it. Paul himself was beaten in Philippi and thrown into prison (Acts 16:22–24). The church saw this happen.
> For today: Today, too, Christians are persecuted for their faith in many countries. We are to pray for them and stand up for freedom of religion for all people.
