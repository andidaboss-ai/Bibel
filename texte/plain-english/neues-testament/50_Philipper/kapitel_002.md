# Philippians – Chapter 2
### The song of Christ

---

## Be one and think of others (Verses 1–4)

[1] So is there any encouragement in Christ among you?
Is there any comfort from love?
Is there any fellowship in the Spirit?
Is there any tenderness and compassion?
[2] Then make my joy full:
Be of one mind.
Have the same love.
Be of one heart
and have the same goal.
[3] Do nothing out of rivalry
or out of conceit.
But be humble.
Each one should count others better
than himself.
[4] Each of you should not only look to his own things,
but also to the things
of others and what they need.

*conceit* = thinking too highly of yourself

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Paul wishes that the church sticks together. That makes him glad, even though he is in prison.
> Verse 3: “Count others better” doesn’t mean making yourself small or letting yourself be used. It means: Others matter to me, not just I myself.
> Verse 4: A simple sentence for every day: Don’t only ask *What do I need?* but also *What does the other person need?*

---

## The song of Christ (Verses 5–11)

[5] Have the same attitude
that Christ Jesus also had:
[6] He existed in the form of God.
But he didn’t hold on to
equality with God
as a thing to be grasped.
[7] Rather, he emptied himself.
He took the form of a servant
and was made in the likeness of men.
[8] And when he lived as a human being,
he humbled himself.
He became obedient to the point of death,
yes,
the death of the cross.
[9] Therefore God also highly exalted him.
He gave him the name
which is above every name.
[10] So at the name of Jesus
every knee should bow:
in heaven,
on earth
and under the earth.
[11] And every tongue should confess:
Jesus Christ is Lord!
This happens to the glory of God the Father.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–11 are probably a song or poem of the first Christians. Paul quotes it or wrote it himself. It is one of the oldest texts about Jesus.
> The song has two parts: First Jesus goes the way down (verses 6–8): from God to human being, from human being to servant, from servant to death on the cross. Then God raises him up high (verses 9–11).
> Verse 6: Jesus did not use his power for himself. He did not cling to it like a prize grabbed in war.
> Verse 7: “He emptied himself”: He gave up his glory and became fully human. Christians have discussed a lot how exactly to understand this.
> Verse 8: Death on the cross was the cruelest and most shameful punishment of the Romans. No one could sink lower.
> Verses 10–11 take up Isaiah 45:23: *To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall take an oath.* There God speaks, the God of Israel. The song says: Whoever honors Jesus as Lord honors God the Father by doing so.
> Verse 5: The beginning is important: This song is to be our example. Christians are not to strive to get to the top, but to serve one another, just as Jesus did.

## Live out your faith (Verses 12–18)

[12] So then,
my beloved:
You have always obeyed,
not only
when I was with you,
but now much more
when I am absent.
Work out your own salvation
with fear and trembling.
[13] For it is God
who works in you.
He gives you the will
and the doing,
as it pleases him.
[14] Do all things
without complaining
and arguing,
[15] so that you may be blameless and pure,
children of God without fault,
in the middle of a crooked and perverse generation.
Among them you shine
as lights in the world.
[16] Hold up the word of life.
Then I can be proud on the day of Christ
that I didn’t run in vain
nor labor in vain.
[17] Yes,
and even if my life
is poured out like a drink offering
on the sacrifice and service of your faith:
I am glad and rejoice with you all.
[18] In the same way, you also should be glad
and rejoice with me.

*drink offering* = wine that was poured out at a sacrifice

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–13: These two verses belong together. We are to make an effort in our faith. But at the same time we know: God himself works in us. Salvation is not earned, but it is to become visible in our lives.
> “With fear and trembling” doesn’t mean: living in fear of God. It means: taking the matter seriously.
> Verse 14 recalls Israel in the wilderness, which often complained (compare Exodus 16). Verse 15 takes up words from Deuteronomy 32:5.
> Verse 15: “Lights in the world”: Christians are to show through their lives how good God is. This is no arrogance toward others.
> Verse 17: Paul is thinking that he might be executed. Still he speaks of joy.

## Timothy (Verses 19–24)

[19] I hope in the Lord Jesus
to send Timothy to you soon.
Then I too will be cheered up
when I learn
how you are doing.
[20] For I have no one else
who thinks like me
and who will truly care about you.
[21] For they all seek their own interests,
not the things of Jesus Christ.
[22] But you know
that Timothy has proved himself.
As a child serves a father,
so he served with me for the Good News.
[23] So I hope to send him at once,
as soon as I see
how it will go with me.
[24] But I trust in the Lord
that I myself will also come soon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–22: Timothy was like a son to Paul. He lives what the song of Christ says: He thinks of others, not of himself.
> Verse 21: Paul is disappointed in other coworkers. He doesn’t say whom he means.
> Verses 23–24: Paul is waiting for the verdict in his trial. He hopes to be set free.

## Epaphroditus (Verses 25–30)

[25] But I thought it necessary
to send Epaphroditus to you.
He is my brother,
my fellow worker
and fellow soldier.
He is your apostle
and has helped me in my need.
[26] For he longed for you all.
And he was very troubled
because you had heard
that he was sick.
[27] He was indeed sick nearly to death.
But God had mercy on him.
And not only on him,
but on me also,
so that I would not have sorrow on sorrow.
[28] That is why I am sending him all the sooner,
so that you may rejoice
when you see him again,
and that I may be less sorrowful.
[29] So receive him in the Lord with all joy.
And hold such people in honor.
[30] For because of the work of Christ
he came near to death.
He risked his life
to supply what was lacking in your service toward me,
because you could not be here yourselves.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: Epaphroditus came from Philippi. The church had sent him to Paul with a gift of money (compare 4:18). The WEB calls him “your apostle,” that is, the messenger sent by the church.
> Verses 26–27: Epaphroditus became seriously ill. Illness is not a punishment. Paul says openly how great his worry was. An apostle, too, feels sorrow and fear for friends.
> Verse 27: Paul did not simply heal Epaphroditus. He too depended on God’s mercy.
> Verse 30: “To supply what was lacking in your service toward me”: The church was far away. Epaphroditus was with Paul in its place. This is no reproach to the church.
