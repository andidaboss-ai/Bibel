# Philippians – Chapter 3
### Everything for Christ

---

> **Important note first**
> In this chapter Paul argues with other Christian teachers. They demanded that Christians from the nations be circumcised. Paul uses harsh words in doing so. These words are not directed against the Jewish people or the Jewish faith. Paul himself was a Jew and remained one all his life. These verses must never be used against Jews.

---

## Beware of false teachers (Verses 1–3)

[1] Finally,
my brothers:
Rejoice in the Lord!
To write the same things to you
is not tiresome for me.
And for you it is safe.
[2] Beware of the dogs.
Beware of the evil workers.
Beware of the false circumcision.
[3] For we are the circumcision:
We worship God in the Spirit.
We rejoice in Christ Jesus.
And we have no confidence in the flesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Finally”: Paul announces the end, but then writes two more chapters. That is why some interpreters think the letter was put together from several letters.
> Verse 2: “Dogs” was a crude insult at that time. Paul means traveling Christian preachers who demanded circumcision from the Christians from the nations. It is an argument among Christians, as in the letter to the Galatians.
> Verse 3: “We are the circumcision”: Paul means: Whoever serves God through the Spirit belongs to God’s people, even without circumcision. This does not mean that Israel is no longer God’s people (compare Romans 11:1–2, 29).
> “Flesh” here means: human advantages and achievements that people are proud of.

---

## What used to matter to me (Verses 4–11)

[4] And yet I myself could also have confidence in the flesh.
If anyone else thinks
he has confidence in the flesh,
I have even more:
[5] I was circumcised on the eighth day.
I come from the people of Israel,
from the tribe of Benjamin.
I am a Hebrew of Hebrews.
Concerning the law:
I was a Pharisee.
A Pharisee was a member of a Jewish group that took God’s law very seriously.
[6] Concerning zeal:
I persecuted the church.
Concerning the righteousness in the law:
I was found blameless.
[7] But what was gain to me,
I consider a loss for the sake of Christ.
[8] Yes,
most certainly:
I count all things to be a loss
compared with the surpassing worth
of knowing Christ Jesus,
my Lord.
For him I have lost all things.
And I count them nothing but refuse.
Refuse means: garbage, trash.
I do this
so that I may gain Christ
[9] and be found in him.
I don’t have a righteousness of my own
that comes from the law.
But I have the righteousness
that comes through faith in Christ:
the righteousness
that comes from God
by faith.
[10] I want to know him
and the power of his resurrection.
I want to share in his sufferings
and become like him in his death,
[11] so that by any means
I may reach the resurrection from the dead.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: Paul lists what he was proud of: his Jewish background, his faithfulness to the law, his zeal. All of this was good. He is not ashamed of it.
> Verse 6: “I was found blameless”: Paul had no bad conscience as a Jew. He kept the law faithfully. The law was no burden to him.
> Verses 7–8: Only when he came to know Christ did all this lose its weight for him. Not because it was bad, but because Christ is so much more valuable. Paul is speaking here about his own life’s path.
> Verse 8: The WEB says “refuse,” that is, garbage or trash. Paul uses a very strong word to show how much Christ surpasses everything else.
> Verse 9: Righteousness is a gift from God, not your own achievement. Here it says: “through faith in Christ.” The Greek text can also mean: *through the faithfulness of Christ*. Interpreters understand it in different ways.
> Verses 10–11: For Paul, knowing Christ means: living with him, suffering with him and rising with him.

## I run toward the goal (Verses 12–16)

[12] Not
that I have already reached it
or am already perfect.
But I press on
to take hold of it.
For I too was taken hold of by Christ Jesus.
[13] Brothers,
I don’t think
that I have already taken hold of it.
But one thing I do:
I forget
the things that are behind me
and stretch forward to the things
that are before me.
[14] I press on toward the goal
to win the prize:
the high calling of God
in Christ Jesus.
[15] Let all of us
who are mature
think this way.
And if you think otherwise about something,
God will also reveal that to you.
[16] But however far we have come:
Let’s live by the same rule.
Let’s be of the same mind.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul says honestly: I too am not finished yet. Being a Christian is a path, not a finished state.
> Verse 12: “I too was taken hold of by Christ Jesus”: It was not Paul who first looked for Christ. Christ found him first (compare Acts 9).
> Verses 13–14: A picture from sports: A runner doesn’t look back, but forward to the goal.
> For today: Forgetting what lies behind doesn’t mean that you have to push away suffering you have experienced. It means: The past, including your own mistakes, should not hold you captive. God gives a new beginning.
> Verse 15: Paul is patient: God himself will show the way to those who think differently.

## Our home is in heaven (Verses 17–21)

[17] Brothers,
together, follow my example.
And pay attention to those
who live this way,
just as you have us for an example.
[18] For many live differently.
I have often told you about them,
and now I tell you even weeping:
They live as enemies of the cross of Christ.
[19] Their end is destruction.
Their god is the belly.
They are proud of what
they should be ashamed of.
They only think about earthly things.
[20] For our citizenship is in heaven.
From there we also wait for a Savior,
the Lord Jesus Christ.
[21] He will change our weak and lowly body,
so that it becomes like his glorious body.
He does this by the power
with which he is able even to subject all things to himself.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Paul can say “Together, follow my example” because he himself follows Christ (compare 1 Corinthians 11:1).
> Verse 18: Paul weeps over these people. He doesn’t despise them; he is sad about them.
> Verse 19: “Their god is the belly”: They live only for their own wishes and pleasures. Whom exactly Paul means is disputed among interpreters.
> Verse 20: Philippi was a Roman colony. Many inhabitants were proud of their Roman citizenship. Paul says: Our most important citizenship is in heaven.
> For today: This doesn’t mean that Christians should not care about the world. Whoever is at home in heaven works on earth for justice and peace.
> Verse 21: “Our weak and lowly body” is our body that gets sick, grows old and dies. God promises a new, glorious body at the resurrection.
