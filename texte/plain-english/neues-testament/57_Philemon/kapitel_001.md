# Philemon – Chapter 1
### No longer as a slave, but as a brother

---

## Before we begin: The letter to Philemon

The letter to Philemon is the shortest letter of Paul. It has only one chapter. Almost all scholars agree that Paul wrote it himself.
Philemon was a well-off Christian, probably in Colossae. A church met in his house.
Onesimus was a slave of Philemon. He went away from him, perhaps he ran away. He came to Paul, who was in prison. There Onesimus became a Christian.
Now Paul sends him back to Philemon, with this letter. Paul asks Philemon to receive Onesimus no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother.
**Important note:** The letter does not openly demand the end of slavery. But it puts slavery in a new light: A slave is a brother. Later this message led many Christians to fight against slavery.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–3)

[1] Paul,
a prisoner of Christ Jesus,
and Timothy,
our brother,
write to Philemon,
our beloved fellow worker,
[2] to the beloved Apphia,
to Archippus,
our fellow soldier,
and to the church in your house:
[3] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul calls himself “a prisoner of Christ Jesus.” He is in prison, but he sees himself as someone who belongs to Christ.
> Verse 2: Apphia was probably Philemon’s wife. She is named specifically. Archippus is also mentioned in Colossians 4:17.
> “The church in your house”: The first Christians had no church buildings. They met in private homes.
> In verse 2 the WEB says “the beloved Apphia.” Some old manuscripts say *Apphia, the sister*.

---

## Thanks for Philemon’s love (Verses 4–7)

[4] I always thank my God
when I remember you in my prayers.
[5] For I hear of your love
and of the faith
that you have toward the Lord Jesus
and toward all the saints.
[6] I pray
that the fellowship
that comes from your faith
may become effective,
so that you know every good thing
that is ours in Christ Jesus.
[7] For we have much joy and comfort in your love.
For through you,
brother,
the hearts of the saints have been refreshed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–7: Philemon is known for his love for other Christians. Paul praises him honestly. Now Philemon is to show this love to Onesimus too.
> Verse 7: “Refreshed”: Philemon did others good, like a cool drink on a hot day.

---

## Paul appeals for Onesimus (Verses 8–22)

[8] So in Christ I could boldly command you
to do what is right.
[9] But for love’s sake I would rather appeal to you,
I,
Paul,
an old man
and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ.
[10] I appeal to you for my child Onesimus.
I became his father in my chains.
[11] He was once useless to you,
but now he is useful to you and to me.
[12] I am sending him back to you.
Receive him,
for he is my own heart.
[13] I would have liked to keep him with me,
so that he might serve me in your place
while I am in chains for the Good News.
[14] But I didn’t want to do anything without your consent.
For your good deed should not be forced,
but of your own free will.
[15] For perhaps he was separated from you for a while for this reason,
so that you would have him back forever,
[16] no longer as a slave,
but more than a slave:
a beloved brother.
He is that especially to me,
but how much more to you,
both as a person and in the Lord.
[17] So if you count me a partner,
receive him as you would receive me.
[18] But if he has wronged you
or owes you anything,
put that on my account.
[19] I,
Paul,
write this with my own hand:
I will repay it.
I won’t even mention
that you owe me your very self.
[20] Yes,
brother,
let me have joy from you in the Lord.
Refresh my heart in the Lord.
[21] I write to you
because I am confident
of your obedience.
I know
that you will do even beyond what I say.
[22] Also, prepare a guest room for me.
For I hope
that through your prayers I will be given back to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–9: Paul could command. But he appeals. Love does not force.
> Verse 10: Onesimus became a Christian in prison through Paul. That is why Paul calls him “my child.”
> Verse 11: A play on words: The name *Onesimus* means *useful* in Greek. Once he was “useless,” now he is truly “useful.”
> Verse 12: “My own heart”: Paul loves Onesimus very much. At that time a slave was considered property. Paul calls him his heart.
> Verse 16 is the most important verse: “no longer as a slave, but more than a slave: a beloved brother.” In Christ master and slave are brothers and sisters. That changed the relationship from the ground up.
> Verse 17: “Receive him as you would receive me”: Onesimus is to be treated like Paul, the apostle.
> Verses 18–19: Paul wants to pay any debts of Onesimus himself. This recalls how Christ stands up for us.
> Verse 21: “Even beyond what I say”: Many interpreters understand this as a hint that Philemon should set Onesimus free.
> For today: The letter shows how faith changes relationships. No human being may belong to another. Every person is a brother or a sister. Today there is still human trafficking and forced labor. Christians should stand up against it. If you know about trafficking or forced labor, you can get help. In the US, call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888. In the UK, call the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.

## Greetings (Verses 23–25)

[23] Epaphras,
my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus,
greets you,
[24] as do Mark,
Aristarchus,
Demas
and Luke,
my fellow workers.
[25] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ
be with your spirit.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–24: Almost the same names also appear in Colossians 4:10–14. That is why many interpreters think that the two letters were written at the same time and taken to Colossae together.
> According to an old tradition, an Onesimus later became bishop of Ephesus. We don’t know for sure whether it is the same man.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to Philemon?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A personal letter:** Paul writes from prison to his friend Philemon, to Apphia, to Archippus and to the house church.
> **Appealing instead of commanding:** Paul could command, but he appeals out of love. Philemon is to do good of his own free will.
> **A slave becomes a brother:** Onesimus has become a Christian. Philemon is to receive him no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother.
> **Paul stands up for Onesimus:** He wants to pay himself what Onesimus owes.
> **For today:** Faith changes how we see one another. Every person has the same dignity. No one may belong to another.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter to the Hebrews. It explains who Jesus is, with many pictures from the Scriptures of Israel.
