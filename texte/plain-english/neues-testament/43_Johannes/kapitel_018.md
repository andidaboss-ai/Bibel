# John – Chapter 18
### “My Kingdom is not of this world”

---

> **An important note first**
> In chapters 18 and 19 John tells about Jesus’ arrest, his sentencing and his death. In doing so he often writes “the Jews.” Here he almost always means the temple leaders in Jerusalem and their servants, not the Jewish people.
> For centuries these texts were misused to insult Jews as “Christ killers” and to persecute them. That is a grave guilt of Christians.
> Jesus, Peter, John and all the disciples were Jews. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. Today the churches teach clearly: The death of Jesus must not be blamed on all the Jews of that time nor on the Jews of today (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).

## Jesus is arrested (Verses 1–11)

[1] When Jesus had spoken these words,
he went out with his disciples
over the brook Kidron.
There was a garden there.
He and his disciples went into it.
[2] Judas,
who betrayed him,
also knew the place.
For Jesus often met there with his disciples.
[3] So Judas took a detachment of soldiers
and officers from the chief priests and the Pharisees
and came there
with lanterns, torches, and weapons.
[4] Jesus knew all the things
that were going to happen to him.
He went out
and said to them,
“Who are you looking for?”
[5] They answered him,
“Jesus of Nazareth.”
Jesus said to them,
“I am he.”
Judas also,
who betrayed him,
was standing with them.
[6] When he said to them,
“I am he,”
they went backward
and fell to the ground.
[7] So he asked them again,
“Who are you looking for?”
They said,
“Jesus of Nazareth.”
[8] Jesus answered,
“I told you
that I am he.
So if you are looking for me,
let these go their way.”
[9] This happened so that the word might be fulfilled
which he spoke,
“Of those whom you have given me,
I have lost none.”
[10] Simon Peter had a sword.
He drew it,
struck the high priest’s servant,
and cut off his right ear.
The servant’s name was Malchus.
[11] Then Jesus said to Peter,
“Put the sword into its sheath.
The cup which the Father has given me,
shall I not drink it?”

*detachment* = a group of soldiers
*sheath* = the cover for a sword

> **What does this mean?**
> The brook Kidron lies between Jerusalem and the Mount of Olives. In Matthew and Mark the garden is called Gethsemane.
> Verse 3: A “detachment of soldiers” were probably Roman soldiers. Temple guards came along too. The Romans and the temple leaders acted together.
> Verses 5–6: “I am he” is literally “I am,” like the name of God. The soldiers fall back. John shows: Jesus is not helpless. He gives himself up freely.
> Verse 8: Jesus protects his disciples.
> Verses 10–11: Only John names Peter and Malchus. Jesus stops the violence. He does not want to be defended with the sword.
> For today: Jesus’ way is the way of nonviolence.

## Jesus before Annas, and Peter denies him (Verses 12–27)

[12] So the detachment,
the commanding officer,
and the officers of the Jews
seized Jesus
and tied him up.
[13] They led him to Annas first.
He was the father-in-law of Caiaphas,
who was high priest that year.
[14] It was Caiaphas
who had advised the Jews
that it was better
that one man should die for the people.
[15] Simon Peter followed Jesus,
and so did another disciple.
That disciple was known to the high priest.
He went in with Jesus into the court of the high priest.
[16] But Peter was standing outside at the door.
So the other disciple,
who was known to the high priest,
went out,
spoke to the woman who kept the door,
and brought Peter in.
[17] Then the servant girl
who kept the door
said to Peter,
“Are you also one of this man’s disciples?”
He said,
“I am not.”
[18] The servants and the officers were standing there.
They had made a fire of coals,
because it was cold,
and were warming themselves.
Peter was with them,
standing and warming himself.
[19] The high priest asked Jesus
about his disciples
and about his teaching.
[20] Jesus answered him,
“I spoke openly to the world.
I always taught in synagogues
and in the temple,
where the Jews always meet.
I said nothing in secret.
[21] Why do you ask me?
Ask those
who have heard me
what I said to them.
They know
the things which I said.”
[22] When he had said this,
one of the officers
standing by
slapped Jesus with his hand,
saying,
“Do you answer the high priest like that?”
[23] Jesus answered him,
“If I have said something wrong,
show what was wrong.
But if I have spoken rightly,
why do you beat me?”
[24] Then Annas sent him, tied up,
to Caiaphas, the high priest.
[25] Simon Peter was standing
and warming himself.
So they said to him,
“You aren’t also one of his disciples, are you?”
He denied it and said,
“I am not.”
[26] One of the servants of the high priest,
a relative of the man
whose ear Peter had cut off,
said,
“Didn’t I see you in the garden with him?”
[27] Peter denied it again.
And immediately the rooster crowed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Annas had been high priest earlier and still had great influence. Caiaphas was his son-in-law.
> Verse 15: “The other disciple” is not named. Many think of “the disciple whom Jesus loved.”
> Verses 17 and 25: Peter says “I am not.” Jesus had said “I am he” (verse 5). John sets the two side by side.
> Verse 20: Jesus taught openly, in synagogue and temple, like a Jewish teacher.
> Verses 22–23: Jesus is beaten. He does not defend himself with violence, but he demands justice. That is dignity.
> Important: Violence against prisoners is always wrong.

## Jesus before Pilate (Verses 28–40)

[28] Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas
into the Praetorium.
It was early in the morning.
They themselves didn’t go into the Praetorium,
so that they would not become unclean,
but might eat the Passover.
[29] So Pilate went out to them
and said,
“What accusation do you bring against this man?”
[30] They answered him,
“If this man weren’t an evildoer,
we wouldn’t have handed him over to you.”
[31] So Pilate said to them,
“Take him yourselves,
and judge him according to your law.”
The Jews said to him,
“It is illegal for us to put anyone to death.”
[32] So the word of Jesus was to be fulfilled,
which he spoke
to show
by what kind of death he would die.
[33] Pilate went into the Praetorium again,
called Jesus,
and said to him,
“Are you the King of the Jews?”
[34] Jesus answered him,
“Do you say this by yourself,
or did others tell you about me?”
[35] Pilate answered,
“I’m not a Jew, am I?
Your own nation and the chief priests
handed you over to me.
What have you done?”
[36] Jesus answered,
“My Kingdom is not of this world.
If my Kingdom were of this world,
then my servants would fight,
so that I wouldn’t be handed over to the Jews.
But my Kingdom is not from here.”
[37] So Pilate said to him,
“Are you a king then?”
Jesus answered,
“You say that I am a king.
For this reason I have been born,
and for this reason I have come into the world:
to testify to the truth.
Everyone who is of the truth
listens to my voice.”
[38] Pilate said to him,
“What is truth?”
When he had said this,
he went out again to the Jews
and said to them,
“I find no basis for a charge against him.
[39] But you have a custom
that I should release someone to you at the Passover.
So do you want me
to release to you the King of the Jews?”
[40] Then they all shouted again,
“Not this man,
but Barabbas!”
Now Barabbas was a robber.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Praetorium was the official residence of the Roman governor in Jerusalem.
> Verse 28: Whoever entered the house of a non-Jew was considered unclean and could not take part in the Passover meal. In John, Jesus dies on the day when the Passover lambs were slaughtered.
> Verse 31: Under Roman rule the Jewish authorities were probably not allowed to carry out the death penalty. The death sentence was given by the Roman governor.
> Verse 35: “Your own nation”: This is how Pilate speaks. The temple leaders and their people are meant. Pilate wants to push the responsibility away from himself.
> Verse 36: “My Kingdom is not of this world”: Jesus is not a political rebel. His Kingdom does not come through violence. That is why his servants do not fight.
> Verse 38: “What is truth?” Pilate asks, but he doesn’t wait for an answer. The truth is standing in front of him.
> Verse 40: Barabbas is called a “robber.” The word could also mean a rebel against Rome.
> Important: Pilate had the power. He finds no guilt and still doesn’t set Jesus free. He bears responsibility. No people bears a collective guilt.
