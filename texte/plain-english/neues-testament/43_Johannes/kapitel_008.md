# John – Chapter 8
### The light of the world

---

## Jesus and the woman caught in adultery (Verses 1–11)

[1] But Jesus went to the Mount of Olives.
[2] Very early the next morning, he came back to the temple.
All the people came to him.
He sat down and taught them.
[3] Then the scribes and the Pharisees brought a woman to him.
She had been caught in adultery.
They made her stand in the middle of everyone.
[4] They said to Jesus,
“Teacher, we caught this woman while she was committing adultery.
[5] In our law, Moses told us to stone women like her.
So what do you say about her?”
[6] They said this to test him.
They wanted a reason to accuse him.
But Jesus bent down.
He wrote on the ground with his finger.
[7] They kept on asking him.
So he looked up and said to them,
“Is there anyone here who has never sinned?
That person may throw the first stone at her.”
[8] Then he bent down again.
He wrote on the ground with his finger.
[9] They heard what he said.
Their conscience told them that they were guilty too.
So they left, one by one.
The oldest ones left first, and then all the rest.
Only Jesus was left, with the woman still standing in the middle.
[10] Jesus stood up and looked at her.
He said to her,
“Woman, where are the people who accused you?
Didn’t anyone condemn you?”
[11] She said, “No one, Lord.”
Jesus said,
“I don’t condemn you either.
You may go.
From now on, stop sinning.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story (7:53–8:11) is missing in the oldest manuscripts of the Gospel of John. In some manuscripts it stands in another place, for example after Luke 21:38. Many scholars think: It is an old, genuine tradition about Jesus that was only later put here. The churches read it as part of the Bible.
> According to the law of Moses, in a case of adultery both were to die, the man and the woman (Leviticus 20:10; Deuteronomy 22:22). Here only the woman is brought. Where is the man? That shows the injustice.
> The question is a trap: If Jesus says to stone her, he goes against his own mercy, and only the Romans were allowed to carry out death sentences. If he says to let her go, he can be accused of disregarding the law.
> We do not know what Jesus wrote on the ground.
> Verse 7: “Is there anyone here who has never sinned? That person may throw the first stone at her.” No one is without sin. That is why no one has the right to judge others like this.
> Verse 11: Jesus does not condemn the woman. He does not pretend there was no wrong, but he gives her a new start.
> Important: This story is a strong word against violence toward women and against people taking justice into their own hands. In later Judaism, too, the death penalty was very strongly restricted.
> For today: Whoever points a finger at others should first look at themselves. Whoever experiences violence can find help: In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247.

## “I am the light of the world” (Verses 12–20)

[12] Jesus spoke to the people again.
He said,
“I am the light of the world.
Whoever follows me will not walk in the dark.
He will have the light that gives life.”
[13] So the Pharisees said to him,
“You are speaking as a witness for yourself.
So what you say does not count.”
[14] Jesus answered them,
“Even if I speak as a witness for myself, what I say is true.
I know where I came from, and I know where I am going.
But you don’t know where I came from or where I am going.
[15] You judge the way humans judge.
I don’t judge anyone.
[16] But even if I do judge, my judging is true.
I am not alone.
The Father who sent me is with me.
[17] Your own law says that when two people say the same thing, it counts as true.
[18] I am one witness for myself.
And the Father who sent me is a witness for me too.”
[19] Then they asked him, “Where is your Father?”
Jesus answered,
“You don’t know me, and you don’t know my Father.
If you knew me, you would know my Father too.”
[20] Jesus said these words while he was teaching in the temple, in the area where the offering boxes were.
But no one arrested him,
because his hour had not come yet.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the Feast of Booths, large lamps were lit in the temple that lit up all of Jerusalem. Here Jesus says: “I am the light of the world.” That is the second of the “I am” sayings.
> In the Bible “light” is a picture for God and for life (Psalm 27:1). Whoever follows Jesus finds the way even in the dark.
> Verse 17: The law requires two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). “Your law” sounds distant, but Jesus himself lived by this law. John is writing here from the point of view of a later time.
> Verse 20: The “treasury” was the area with the offering boxes in the Court of the Women.

## “Where I go, you can’t come” (Verses 21–30)

[21] Jesus spoke to them again.
He said,
“I am going away.
You will look for me.
But you will die in your sins.
You can’t come to the place where I am going.”
[22] So the Jews said,
“He says, ‘You can’t come to the place where I am going.’
Is he going to kill himself?”
[23] Jesus said to them,
“You come from below.
I come from above.
You belong to this world.
I don’t belong to this world.
[24] That is why I told you that you will die in your sins.
If you don’t believe that I am he, you will die in your sins.”
[25] So they asked him, “Who are you?”
Jesus said to them,
“I am the one I have been telling you about from the start.
[26] I have many things to say about you and to judge.
But the one who sent me is true.
And I tell the world what I have heard from him.”
[27] They didn’t understand that he was talking to them about the Father.
[28] So Jesus said to them,
“You will lift up the Son of Man.
Then you will know that I am he.
You will know that I do nothing on my own.
I say only what my Father taught me.
[29] The one who sent me is with me.
The Father has not left me alone,
because I always do what pleases him.”
[30] While Jesus was saying these things, many believed in him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24 and 28: “That I am he” literally means “that I am.” It is a reminder of God’s name (Exodus 3:14).
> Verse 28: “Lifted up” means the crucifixion. On the cross it becomes visible who Jesus is.
> Verse 22: In Judaism, taking one’s own life was seen as a grave sin. The question shows that the listeners do not understand.
> Important: Whoever is thinking about suicide is not alone. Help is available day and night: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 30: Many believe in Jesus. The conversation continues with people who at first believed him.

---

> **An important note before the next section**
> The following verses (8:31–59) contain some of the harshest words in the whole New Testament, especially verse 44: “Your father is the devil, and you belong to him.” For centuries this sentence was misused to slander Jews as children of the devil. That led to hatred, persecution and murder, all the way to the Holocaust. That is grave guilt on the part of Christians.
> You need to know: Here Jews are arguing with Jews. Jesus is a Jew. He is speaking to a certain group in a certain, very heated argument. He is not speaking about the Jewish people. In the same Gospel Jesus says that “salvation is from the Jews” (4:22).
> Sharp words were common in debates back then, also among Jewish groups themselves. They describe behavior (lying and wanting to kill), not descent.
> Today the churches teach clearly: The Jewish people are loved by God. God has never canceled his covenant with Israel (Romans 11:1–2,29). Hostility toward Jews is sin.

## The truth will make you free (Verses 31–47)

[31] Some of the Jews had started to believe him.
Jesus said to them,
“If you keep holding on to what I say, then you really are my disciples.
[32] You will know the truth.
And the truth will make you free.”
[33] They answered him,
“We are Abraham’s descendants.
We have never been anyone’s slaves.
So how can you say, ‘You will be made free’?”
[34] Jesus answered them,
“I tell you the truth:
Everyone who sins is a slave of sin.
[35] A slave does not stay in the family’s house forever.
A son stays forever.
[36] So if the Son makes you free, you will really be free.
[37] I know that you are Abraham’s descendants.
But you are trying to kill me,
because there is no room in you for what I say.
[38] I tell you what I have seen with my Father.
And you do what you have seen with your father.”
[39] They answered him, “Abraham is our father.”
Jesus said to them,
“If you were Abraham’s children, you would do what Abraham did.
[40] But now you are trying to kill me.
I am a man who has told you the truth that I heard from God.
Abraham did not do things like that.
[41] You are doing what your own father does.”
They said to him,
“We were not born from sexual sin.
We have one Father: God.”
[42] Jesus said to them,
“If God were your Father, you would love me.
I came from God, and now I am here.
I did not come on my own.
He sent me.
[43] Why don’t you understand what I am saying?
It is because you can’t stand to listen to my words.
[44] Your father is the devil, and you belong to him.
You want to do what your father wants.
He was a murderer from the beginning.
He does not hold on to the truth,
because there is no truth in him.
When he lies, he is doing what comes naturally to him.
He is a liar, and lies come from him.
He is the father of lies.
[45] But I tell the truth.
That is why you don’t believe me.
[46] Can any of you prove that I have sinned?
If I tell the truth, why don’t you believe me?
[47] Whoever belongs to God listens to God’s words.
You don’t listen,
because you don’t belong to God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 31–32: “The truth will make you free” is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible. What is meant is: Whoever trusts Jesus becomes free from the power of sin.
> Verse 33: Israel had often been oppressed (in Egypt, in Babylon, now under Rome). But the listeners mean inner freedom: As children of Abraham they are God’s free people.
> Verses 39–41: It is about the question: Who is a true child of Abraham and of God? Jesus says: That shows in how you act, not only in your descent. The prophets of Israel said this too.
> Verse 41: “Not born from sexual sin” is perhaps a hint at rumors about Jesus’ birth.
> Verse 44: Please read the note before this section. Here Jesus describes the behavior of people who want to kill him: Killing and lying come from evil. That applies to every person who acts like that, whatever their background. No people and no religion is “of the devil.”
> For today: Truth sets free. Lies and hatred make people unfree, Christians too.

## Before Abraham was, I AM (Verses 48–59)

[48] Then the Jews answered him,
“Aren’t we right when we say that you are a Samaritan and have a demon?”
[49] Jesus answered,
“I don’t have a demon.
I honor my Father.
But you dishonor me.
[50] I am not looking for honor for myself.
But there is someone who is looking for it.
He is the judge.
[51] I tell you the truth:
Whoever holds on to my word will never see death.”
[52] Then the Jews said to him,
“Now we know that you have a demon!
Abraham died.
The prophets died too.
And you say, ‘Whoever holds on to my word will never taste death.’
[53] Are you greater than our father Abraham?
He died.
The prophets died.
Who do you think you are?”
[54] Jesus answered,
“If I give myself honor, my honor means nothing.
My Father is the one who gives me honor.
You say that he is your God.
[55] But you have not known him.
I know him.
If I said, ‘I don’t know him,’ I would be a liar like you.
But I do know him, and I hold on to his word.
[56] Your father Abraham was full of joy at the thought of seeing my day.
He saw it and was glad.”
[57] So the Jews said to him,
“You are not even fifty years old!
And you have seen Abraham?”
[58] Jesus said to them,
“I tell you the truth:
Before Abraham was born, I AM.”
[59] Then they picked up stones to throw at him.
But Jesus hid himself.
He went out of the temple.
He walked right through the middle of them and went on his way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 48: Back then “Samaritan” was an insult for someone who strays from the true faith. Jesus is insulted here too. The argument is carried on with harsh words from both sides.
> Verse 56: Abraham believed God’s promise. Jesus says: Abraham looked forward with joy to what God would do through Jesus.
> Verse 58: “I AM” is God’s name from Exodus 3:14. Jesus says: I was there before Abraham, I belong to God. To the listeners that sounded like blasphemy. That is why they pick up stones (compare Leviticus 24:16). Blasphemy: speaking against God.
> Verse 59: The words about going through the middle of them are missing in the oldest manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Important: The opponents here are a certain group in the temple, not “the Jews” as a whole. Just a few verses earlier it says: “many believed in him” (verse 30). No Christian may use these verses against Jewish people.
