# John – Chapter 8
### The light of the world

---

## Jesus and the woman caught in adultery (Verses 1–11)

[1] But Jesus went to the Mount of Olives.
[2] Very early in the morning he came again into the temple.
All the people came to him.
He sat down
and taught them.
[3] Then the scribes and the Pharisees brought a woman
who had been caught in adultery.
They set her in the middle
[4] and said to Jesus,
“Teacher, we found this woman in adultery,
in the very act.
[5] Now in our law, Moses commanded us
to stone such women.
What then do you say about her?”
[6] They said this
to test him,
so that they might have something
to accuse him of.
But Jesus bent down
and wrote on the ground with his finger.
[7] When they kept on asking him,
he stood up
and said to them,
“He who is without sin among you,
let him throw the first stone at her.”
[8] Again he bent down
and wrote on the ground with his finger.
[9] When they heard it,
their conscience convicted them.
They went out one by one,
beginning from the oldest,
even to the last.
Jesus was left alone
with the woman, who was standing in the middle.
[10] Jesus stood up,
looked at her,
and said,
“Woman, where are your accusers?
Did no one condemn you?”
[11] She said,
“No one, Lord.”
Jesus said,
“Neither do I condemn you.
Go your way.
From now on, sin no more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This story (7:53–8:11) is missing in the oldest manuscripts of the Gospel of John. In some manuscripts it stands in another place, for example after Luke 21:38. Many scholars think: It is an old, genuine tradition about Jesus that was only later put here. The churches read it as part of the Bible.
> According to the law of Moses, in a case of adultery both were to die, the man and the woman (Leviticus 20:10; Deuteronomy 22:22). Here only the woman is brought. Where is the man? That shows the injustice.
> The question is a trap: If Jesus says to stone her, he goes against his own mercy, and only the Romans were allowed to carry out death sentences. If he says to let her go, he can be accused of disregarding the law.
> We do not know what Jesus wrote on the ground.
> Verse 7: “He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her.” No one is without sin. That is why no one has the right to judge others like this.
> Verse 11: Jesus does not condemn the woman. He does not pretend there was no wrong, but he gives her a new start.
> Important: This story is a strong word against violence toward women and against people taking justice into their own hands. In later Judaism, too, the death penalty was very strongly restricted.
> For today: Whoever points a finger at others should first look at themselves. Whoever experiences violence can find help: In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247.

## “I am the light of the world” (Verses 12–20)

[12] Again Jesus spoke to them,
saying,
“I am the light of the world.
He who follows me
will not walk in the darkness,
but will have the light of life.”
[13] So the Pharisees said to him,
“You testify about yourself.
Your testimony is not valid.”
[14] Jesus answered them,
“Even if I testify about myself,
my testimony is true.
For I know
where I came from,
and where I am going.
But you don’t know
where I came from,
or where I am going.
[15] You judge by outward appearance.
I judge no one.
[16] Even if I do judge,
my judgment is true.
For I am not alone,
but I am with the Father
who sent me.
[17] It’s also written in your law
that the testimony of two people is valid.
[18] I am one
who testifies about myself.
And the Father
who sent me
testifies about me.”
[19] So they asked him,
“Where is your Father?”
Jesus answered,
“You know neither me nor my Father.
If you knew me,
you would know my Father also.”
[20] Jesus spoke these words in the treasury,
as he taught in the temple.
Yet no one arrested him,
because his hour had not yet come.

> **What does this mean?**
> At the Feast of Booths, large lamps were lit in the temple that lit up all of Jerusalem. Here Jesus says: “I am the light of the world.” That is the second of the “I am” sayings.
> In the Bible “light” is a picture for God and for life (Psalm 27:1). Whoever follows Jesus finds the way even in the dark.
> Verse 17: The law requires two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). “Your law” sounds distant, but Jesus himself lived by this law. John is writing here from the point of view of a later time.
> Verse 20: The “treasury” was the area with the offering boxes in the Court of the Women.

## “Where I go, you can’t come” (Verses 21–30)

[21] Again Jesus said to them,
“I am going away.
You will look for me,
and you will die in your sins.
Where I go,
you can’t come.”
[22] So the Jews said,
“Will he kill himself,
because he says,
‘Where I am going, you can’t come’?”
[23] He said to them,
“You are from beneath.
I am from above.
You are of this world.
I am not of this world.
[24] That is why I said to you
that you will die in your sins.
For unless you believe
that I am he,
you will die in your sins.”
[25] So they asked him,
“Who are you?”
Jesus said to them,
“Just what I have been saying to you
from the beginning.
[26] I have many things to say about you
and to judge.
But he who sent me
is true.
And the things which I heard from him,
these I say to the world.”
[27] They didn’t understand
that he was speaking to them about the Father.
[28] So Jesus said to them,
“When you have lifted up the Son of Man,
then you will know
that I am he,
and that I do nothing by myself,
but say these things
as my Father taught me.
[29] He who sent me
is with me.
The Father hasn’t left me alone,
for I always do the things
that are pleasing to him.”
[30] As he said these things,
many believed in him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24 and 28: “That I am he” literally means “that I am.” It is a reminder of God’s name (Exodus 3:14).
> Verse 28: “Lifted up” means the crucifixion. On the cross it becomes visible who Jesus is.
> Verse 22: In Judaism, taking one’s own life was seen as a grave sin. The question shows that the listeners do not understand.
> Important: Whoever is thinking about suicide is not alone. Help is available day and night: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 30: Many believe in Jesus. The conversation continues with people who at first believed him.

---

> **An important note before the next section**
> The following verses (8:31–59) contain some of the harshest words in the whole New Testament, especially verse 44: “You are of your father the devil.” For centuries this sentence was misused to slander Jews as children of the devil. That led to hatred, persecution and murder, all the way to the Holocaust. That is grave guilt on the part of Christians.
> You need to know: Here Jews are arguing with Jews. Jesus is a Jew. He is speaking to a certain group in a certain, very heated argument. He is not speaking about the Jewish people. In the same Gospel Jesus says that “salvation is from the Jews” (4:22).
> Sharp words were common in debates back then, also among Jewish groups themselves. They describe behavior (lying and wanting to kill), not descent.
> Today the churches teach clearly: The Jewish people are loved by God. God has never canceled his covenant with Israel (Romans 11:1–2,29). Hostility toward Jews is sin.

## The truth will make you free (Verses 31–47)

[31] Then Jesus said to the Jews
who had believed him,
“If you remain in my word,
then you are truly my disciples.
[32] You will know the truth,
and the truth will make you free.”
[33] They answered him,
“We are Abraham’s offspring,
and have never been slaves to anyone.
How can you say,
‘You will be made free’?”
[34] Jesus answered them,
“Most certainly I tell you,
everyone who commits sin
is the slave of sin.
[35] A slave doesn’t live in the house forever.
A son remains forever.
[36] So if the Son makes you free,
you will be free indeed.
[37] I know
that you are Abraham’s offspring.
Yet you want to kill me,
because my word finds no place in you.
[38] I say the things
which I have seen with my Father.
And you do the things
which you have seen with your father.”
[39] They answered him,
“Our father is Abraham.”
Jesus said to them,
“If you were Abraham’s children,
you would do the works of Abraham.
[40] But now you want to kill me,
a man
who has told you the truth
which I heard from God.
Abraham didn’t do this.
[41] You do the works of your father.”
They said to him,
“We were not born of sexual immorality.
We have one Father, God.”
[42] Jesus said to them,
“If God were your father,
you would love me.
For I came out from God
and have come.
I haven’t come by myself,
but he sent me.
[43] Why don’t you understand
what I say?
Because you can’t hear my word.
[44] You are of your father the devil,
and you want to do
the desires of your father.
He was a murderer from the beginning
and doesn’t stand in the truth,
because there is no truth in him.
When he speaks a lie,
he speaks on his own.
For he is a liar,
and the father of lies.
[45] But because I tell the truth,
you don’t believe me.
[46] Which of you can prove me guilty of sin?
If I tell the truth,
why do you not believe me?
[47] He who is of God
hears the words of God.
For this reason you don’t hear,
because you are not of God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 31–32: “The truth will make you free” is one of the best-known sentences of the Bible. What is meant is: Whoever trusts Jesus becomes free from the power of sin.
> Verse 33: Israel had often been oppressed (in Egypt, in Babylon, now under Rome). But the listeners mean inner freedom: As children of Abraham they are God’s free people.
> Verses 39–41: It is about the question: Who is a true child of Abraham and of God? Jesus says: That shows in how you act, not only in your descent. The prophets of Israel said this too.
> Verse 41: “Not born of sexual immorality” is perhaps a hint at rumors about Jesus’ birth.
> Verse 44: Please read the note before this section. Here Jesus describes the behavior of people who want to kill him: Killing and lying come from evil. That applies to every person who acts like that, whatever their background. No people and no religion is “of the devil.”
> For today: Truth sets free. Lies and hatred make people unfree, Christians too.

## Before Abraham was, I AM (Verses 48–59)

[48] Then the Jews answered him,
“Aren’t we right
to say
that you are a Samaritan
and have a demon?”
[49] Jesus answered,
“I don’t have a demon,
but I honor my Father.
And you dishonor me.
[50] But I don’t seek my own glory.
There is one
who seeks it and judges.
[51] Most certainly I tell you,
if a person keeps my word,
he will never see death.”
[52] Then the Jews said to him,
“Now we know
that you have a demon.
Abraham died,
and so did the prophets.
And you say,
‘If a man keeps my word,
he will never taste of death.’
[53] Are you greater than our father Abraham,
who died?
The prophets died too.
Who do you make yourself out to be?”
[54] Jesus answered,
“If I glorify myself,
my glory is nothing.
It is my Father
who glorifies me,
of whom you say
that he is our God.
[55] You have not known him.
But I know him.
If I said,
‘I don’t know him,’
I would be like you, a liar.
But I know him
and keep his word.
[56] Your father Abraham rejoiced
to see my day.
He saw it
and was glad.”
[57] Then the Jews said to him,
“You are not yet fifty years old!
Have you seen Abraham?”
[58] Jesus said to them,
“Most certainly I tell you,
before Abraham came into existence,
I AM.”
[59] Then they picked up stones
to throw at him.
But Jesus hid himself
and went out of the temple,
going through the middle of them,
and so passed by.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 48: Back then “Samaritan” was an insult for someone who strays from the true faith. Jesus is insulted here too. The argument is carried on with harsh words from both sides.
> Verse 56: Abraham believed God’s promise. Jesus says: Abraham looked forward with joy to what God would do through Jesus.
> Verse 58: “I AM” is God’s name from Exodus 3:14. Jesus says: I was there before Abraham, I belong to God. To the listeners that sounded like blasphemy. That is why they pick up stones (compare Leviticus 24:16). Blasphemy: speaking against God.
> Verse 59: The words about going through the middle of them are missing in the oldest manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Important: The opponents here are a certain group in the temple, not “the Jews” as a whole. Just a few verses earlier it says: “many believed in him” (verse 30). No Christian may use these verses against Jewish people.
