# John – Chapter 12
### The grain of wheat

---

## Mary anoints Jesus in Bethany (Verses 1–11)

[1] Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany.
Lazarus lived there.
He had been dead, and Jesus had raised him from the dead.
[2] There they made a dinner for Jesus.
Martha served the food.
Lazarus was one of the people sitting at the table with him.
[3] Then Mary took a jar of very expensive perfume.
It was pure nard, about 12 ounces.
She poured it on Jesus’ feet and dried his feet with her hair.
The whole house was filled with the sweet smell of the perfume.
[4] One of Jesus’ disciples was Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon.
He was the one who would later betray Jesus.
He said,
[5] “Why wasn’t this perfume sold for three hundred denarii?
The money could have been given to poor people.”
[6] He did not say this because he cared about poor people.
He said it because he was a thief.
He kept the money box, and he used to steal from it.
[7] But Jesus said,
“Leave her alone.
She has kept this for the day of my burial.
[8] You will always have poor people with you.
But you will not always have me.”
[9] A large crowd of Jews found out that Jesus was there.
So they came.
They did not come only because of Jesus.
They also wanted to see Lazarus, whom Jesus had raised from the dead.
[10] So the chief priests made plans to kill Lazarus too.
[11] Because of him, many Jews were going away and believing in Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> The jar held a “pound” (in Greek “litra”). That was about 12 ounces (a third of a kilogram). Nard is a very expensive fragrant oil made from a plant that grows in the Himalayas.
> Three hundred denarii were about the wages for a whole year of work.
> Mary gives lavishly out of love. She honors Jesus before his death. The dead were anointed with oil. Mary does it in advance.
> Verse 6: John says Judas was a thief. Important: Later the name Judas was misused to insult all Jews as greedy and as traitors. That is a nasty anti-Jewish prejudice. All the disciples, and Mary, Martha and Lazarus too, were Jews.
> Verse 8: “You will always have poor people with you” is a reminder of Deuteronomy 15:11. There it goes on to say that you shall open your hand wide to your brother, to the needy and to the poor in your land. So Jesus is not saying that we may forget the poor. He is saying: Now is a special hour.
> Verses 9 and 11: Many Jews come to believe in Jesus.
> For today: Love may also be generous and lavish.

## Jesus enters Jerusalem (Verses 12–19)

[12] The next day, a large crowd had come to the feast.
They heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem.
[13] So they took palm branches and went out to meet him.
They shouted,
“Hosanna!
Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord, the King of Israel!”
[14] Jesus found a young donkey and sat on it.
This is what the Scriptures say:
[15] “Don’t be afraid, daughter of Zion.
Look, your King is coming.
He is sitting on a donkey’s colt.”
[16] At first his disciples did not understand these things.
But later, when Jesus had been glorified, they remembered:
These things had been written about him.
And people had done these things for him.
[17] Many people had been with Jesus when he called Lazarus out of the tomb and raised him from the dead.
They kept telling others about it.
[18] That is why the crowd went out to meet him.
They had heard that he had done this sign.
[19] So the Pharisees said to each other,
“You see, we are getting nowhere.
Look, the whole world is running after him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Palm Sunday, one week before Easter, remembers this event.
> Palm branches were a sign of victory and joy.
> Verse 13: “Hosanna” is Hebrew and means “Please help!” or “Please save!” It became a shout of joy. The shout comes from Psalm 118:25–26.
> Verse 15 quotes Zechariah 9:9. Jesus comes as a peaceful king on a donkey, not on a war horse.
> Verse 19: “The whole world is running after him”: The Jewish people cheer Jesus.

## The grain of wheat must die (Verses 20–36)

[20] Some Greeks were among the people who went up to worship at the feast.
[21] They came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee.
They asked him,
“Sir, we would like to see Jesus.”
[22] Philip went and told Andrew.
Then Andrew and Philip went and told Jesus.
[23] Jesus answered them,
“The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.
[24] I tell you the truth:
A grain of wheat must fall into the ground and die.
If it doesn’t, it stays just one single grain.
But if it dies, it produces a big harvest.
[25] Whoever loves his life will lose it.
Whoever hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life.
[26] Whoever wants to serve me must follow me.
Where I am, my servant will be there too.
My Father will honor anyone who serves me.
[27] “Now my soul is deeply upset.
What should I say?
Should I say, ‘Father, save me from this hour’?
But this is the very reason I came to this hour.
[28] Father, glorify your name!”
Then a voice came from the sky.
It said,
“I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”
[29] The crowd standing there heard it.
Some said that it had thundered.
Others said, “An angel has spoken to him.”
[30] Jesus answered,
“This voice did not come for my sake.
It came for your sake.
[31] Now is the time for this world to be judged.
Now the ruler of this world will be thrown out.
[32] And when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself.”
[33] He said this to show what kind of death he would die.
[34] The crowd answered him,
“We have heard from the law that the Christ will stay forever.
So how can you say, ‘The Son of Man must be lifted up’?
Who is this Son of Man?”
[35] Jesus said to them,
“The light will be with you for only a little while longer.
Walk while you have the light,
so that darkness doesn’t catch you.
Whoever walks in the dark doesn’t know where he is going.
[36] While you have the light, believe in the light.
Then you will become children of light.”
After Jesus said these things, he went away and hid from them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–21: “Greeks” are people from other nations who worshiped the God of Israel. They want to see Jesus. For Jesus that is a sign: Now the hour has come.
> Verse 24: A grain of wheat has to go into the earth so that a new plant can grow. That is how Jesus explains his death: From it comes new life for many.
> Verse 25: “Hating your life” is a strong picture. What is meant is: not clinging to yourself. Important: It does not mean that you should despise yourself or harm yourself. Whoever is thinking about self-harm or suicide can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 27: Jesus is afraid of what is coming. Here John tells what the other Gospels tell in the garden on the Mount of Olives.
> Verse 31: “The ruler of this world” is the power of evil. Through Jesus’ death it is defeated.
> Verse 32: “Lifted up” means the cross and at the same time being raised to God. Jesus wants to draw “all people” to himself.
> Verse 34: The crowd knows Bible passages that say the Messiah remains forever (for example Psalm 89:36, in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 89:37). That is why they do not understand why he should die. That is an honest question.

## Why many do not believe (Verses 37–43)

[37] Jesus had done so many signs in front of them.
But still they didn’t believe in him.
[38] This happened so that the words of the prophet Isaiah would come true.
Isaiah said:
“Lord, who has believed our message?
To whom has the Lord shown his power?”
[39] That is why they could not believe.
Isaiah also said:
[40] “He has made their eyes blind.
He has made their hearts hard.
So they cannot see with their eyes.
They cannot understand with their hearts.
They cannot turn back to me, so that I could heal them.”
[41] Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory.
He spoke about him.
[42] Yet even many of the leaders believed in Jesus.
But they did not say so openly because of the Pharisees.
They did not want to be put out of the synagogue.
[43] They loved praise from people more than praise from God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 38 quotes Isaiah 53:1. Verse 40 quotes Isaiah 6:10 freely.
> John asks: Why do not all believe in Jesus? He answers with words of the prophet Isaiah. Isaiah too experienced that many did not want to listen.
> Important: These verses were misused to claim that God had hardened the Jews forever. That is wrong. Paul writes: The hardening is only partial and only for a time. In the end all Israel will be saved (Romans 11:25–26). God has not rejected his people.
> Verse 42: Many leaders believe in Jesus. John shows once again: The Jewish people and their leaders were not all united against Jesus.
> Verse 43: Fear of what people think can paralyze faith. That applies to all people.

## Jesus’ last public words (Verses 44–50)

[44] Jesus called out loudly,
“Whoever believes in me does not believe in me.
He believes in the one who sent me.
[45] Whoever sees me sees the one who sent me.
[46] I have come into the world as a light.
Then whoever believes in me will not stay in the dark.
[47] If anyone hears my words but doesn’t believe, I don’t judge him.
I did not come to judge the world.
I came to save the world.
[48] There is a judge for whoever rejects me and doesn’t accept my words.
The word I have spoken will judge him on the last day.
[49] I have not spoken on my own.
The Father who sent me gave me a command.
He told me what to say and what to speak.
[50] I know that his command is eternal life.
So whatever I say, I say just as the Father told me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These are Jesus’ last public words in the Gospel of John. After this he speaks only with his disciples.
> Verses 44–45: Whoever meets Jesus meets God himself.
> Verse 47: Jesus came to save, not to condemn.
> Important: Judgment on people belongs to God alone. No person may presume to condemn others.
