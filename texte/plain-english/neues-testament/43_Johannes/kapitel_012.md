# John – Chapter 12
### The grain of wheat

---

## Mary anoints Jesus in Bethany (Verses 1–11)

[1] Six days before the Passover,
Jesus came to Bethany.
Lazarus lived there,
whom Jesus had raised from the dead.
[2] There they gave a dinner for him.
Martha served,
and Lazarus was one of those
who sat at the table with him.
[3] Then Mary took a pound of ointment
of pure nard, very precious.
She anointed Jesus’ feet
and wiped his feet with her hair.
The house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment.
[4] Then Judas Iscariot,
Simon’s son,
one of his disciples,
who would betray him,
said,
[5] “Why wasn’t this ointment sold for three hundred denarii
and the money given to the poor?”
[6] He said this, not
because he cared for the poor,
but because he was a thief.
He kept the money box
and used to steal what was put into it.
[7] But Jesus said,
“Leave her alone.
She has kept this for the day of my burial.
[8] For you always have the poor with you,
but you don’t always have me.”
[9] A large crowd of the Jews learned
that Jesus was there.
They came,
not only for Jesus’ sake,
but also
to see Lazarus,
whom he had raised from the dead.
[10] But the chief priests made plans
to put Lazarus to death also.
[11] For because of him many of the Jews went away
and believed in Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> A “pound” (in Greek “litra”) was about 12 ounces (a third of a kilogram). Nard is a very expensive fragrant oil made from a plant that grows in the Himalayas.
> Three hundred denarii were about the wages for a whole year of work.
> Mary gives lavishly out of love. She honors Jesus before his death. The dead were anointed with oil. Mary does it in advance.
> Verse 6: John says Judas was a thief. Important: Later the name Judas was misused to insult all Jews as greedy and as traitors. That is a nasty anti-Jewish prejudice. All the disciples, and Mary, Martha and Lazarus too, were Jews.
> Verse 8: “You always have the poor with you” is a reminder of Deuteronomy 15:11. There it goes on to say that you shall open your hand wide to your brother, to the needy and to the poor in your land. So Jesus is not saying that we may forget the poor. He is saying: Now is a special hour.
> Verses 9 and 11: Many Jews come to believe in Jesus.
> For today: Love may also be generous and lavish.

## Jesus enters Jerusalem (Verses 12–19)

[12] The next day
the great crowd
that had come to the feast
heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem.
[13] They took branches of palm trees,
went out to meet him,
and cried out,
“Hosanna!
Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord,
the King of Israel!”
[14] Jesus found a young donkey
and sat on it,
as it is written:
[15] “Don’t be afraid, daughter of Zion.
Your King comes,
sitting on a donkey’s colt.”
[16] His disciples didn’t understand these things at first.
But when Jesus was glorified,
they remembered
that these things were written about him,
and that they had done these things to him.
[17] The crowd
that had been with him
when he called Lazarus out of the tomb
and raised him from the dead
was telling about it.
[18] That is also why the crowd went to meet him,
because they had heard
that he had done this sign.
[19] So the Pharisees said to one another,
“See,
you are getting nowhere.
The world has gone after him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Palm Sunday, one week before Easter, remembers this event.
> Palm branches were a sign of victory and joy.
> Verse 13: “Hosanna” is Hebrew and means “Please help!” or “Please save!” It became a shout of joy. The shout comes from Psalm 118:25–26.
> Verse 15 quotes Zechariah 9:9. Jesus comes as a peaceful king on a donkey, not on a war horse.
> Verse 19: “The world has gone after him”: The Jewish people cheer Jesus.

## The grain of wheat must die (Verses 20–36)

[20] Among those
who went up to worship at the feast
there were also some Greeks.
[21] They came to Philip,
who was from Bethsaida in Galilee,
and asked him,
“Sir, we want to see Jesus.”
[22] Philip went
and told Andrew.
Andrew and Philip went together
and told Jesus.
[23] Jesus answered them,
“The time has come
for the Son of Man to be glorified.
[24] Most certainly I tell you,
unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies,
it remains by itself alone.
But if it dies,
it bears much fruit.
[25] He who loves his life
will lose it.
He who hates his life in this world
will keep it to eternal life.
[26] If anyone serves me,
let him follow me.
Where I am,
there my servant will also be.
If anyone serves me,
the Father will honor him.
[27] Now my soul is troubled.
What shall I say?
‘Father, save me from this time’?
But it is for this very reason that I came to this time.
[28] Father, glorify your name!”
Then a voice came out of the sky:
“I have both glorified it
and will glorify it again.”
[29] The crowd
that stood by and heard it
said,
“It thundered.”
Others said,
“An angel has spoken to him.”
[30] Jesus answered,
“This voice hasn’t come for my sake,
but for your sakes.
[31] Now is the judgment of this world.
Now the prince of this world will be thrown out.
[32] And I,
if I am lifted up from the earth,
will draw all people to myself.”
[33] He said this
to show
by what kind of death he would die.
[34] The crowd answered him,
“We have heard out of the law
that the Christ remains forever.
How can you say,
‘The Son of Man must be lifted up’?
Who is this Son of Man?”
[35] So Jesus said to them,
“The light is with you only a little while longer.
Walk while you have the light,
so that darkness doesn’t overtake you.
He who walks in the darkness
doesn’t know
where he is going.
[36] While you have the light,
believe in the light,
so that you may become children of light.”
Jesus said these things.
Then he went away
and hid himself from them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–21: “Greeks” are people from other nations who worshiped the God of Israel. They want to see Jesus. For Jesus that is a sign: Now the hour has come.
> Verse 24: A grain of wheat has to go into the earth so that a new plant can grow. That is how Jesus explains his death: From it comes new life for many.
> Verse 25: “Hating your life” is a strong picture. What is meant is: not clinging to yourself. Important: It does not mean that you should despise yourself or harm yourself. Whoever is thinking about self-harm or suicide can find help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 27: Jesus is afraid of what is coming. Here John tells what the other Gospels tell in the garden on the Mount of Olives.
> Verse 31: “The prince of this world” is the power of evil. Through Jesus’ death it is defeated.
> Verse 32: “Lifted up” means the cross and at the same time being raised to God. Jesus wants to draw “all people” to himself.
> Verse 34: The crowd knows Bible passages that say the Messiah remains forever (for example Psalm 89:36, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 89:37). That is why they do not understand why he should die. That is an honest question.

## Why many do not believe (Verses 37–43)

[37] Though Jesus had done so many signs before them,
they didn’t believe in him.
[38] This was so that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled,
which he spoke:
“Lord, who has believed our report?
To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”
[39] For this reason they couldn’t believe.
For Isaiah said again:
[40] “He has blinded their eyes
and he hardened their heart,
so that they would not see with their eyes,
and understand with their heart,
and turn,
and I would heal them.”
[41] Isaiah said these things
because he saw his glory
and spoke of him.
[42] Nevertheless, even many of the rulers believed in him.
But because of the Pharisees they didn’t confess it,
so that they wouldn’t be put out of the synagogue.
[43] For they loved people’s praise
more than God’s praise.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 38 quotes Isaiah 53:1. Verse 40 quotes Isaiah 6:10 freely.
> John asks: Why do not all believe in Jesus? He answers with words of the prophet Isaiah. Isaiah too experienced that many did not want to listen.
> Important: These verses were misused to claim that God had hardened the Jews forever. That is wrong. Paul writes: The hardening is only partial and only for a time. In the end all Israel will be saved (Romans 11:25–26). God has not rejected his people.
> Verse 42: Many leaders believe in Jesus. John shows once again: The Jewish people and their leaders were not all united against Jesus.
> Verse 43: Fear of what people think can paralyze faith. That applies to all people.

## Jesus’ last public words (Verses 44–50)

[44] Jesus cried out,
“Whoever believes in me
believes not in me,
but in him
who sent me.
[45] And he who sees me
sees him
who sent me.
[46] I have come as a light into the world,
so that whoever believes in me
may not remain in the darkness.
[47] If anyone listens to my sayings
and doesn’t believe,
I don’t judge him.
For I came not
to judge the world,
but to save the world.
[48] He who rejects me
and doesn’t receive my sayings
has one who judges him:
The word that I spoke
will judge him on the last day.
[49] For I didn’t speak from myself.
The Father who sent me
gave me a commandment,
what I should say
and what I should speak.
[50] And I know
that his commandment is eternal life.
So the things which I speak,
I speak
just as the Father has told me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> These are Jesus’ last public words in the Gospel of John. After this he speaks only with his disciples.
> Verses 44–45: Whoever meets Jesus meets God himself.
> Verse 47: Jesus came to save, not to condemn.
> Important: Judgment on people belongs to God alone. No person may presume to condemn others.
