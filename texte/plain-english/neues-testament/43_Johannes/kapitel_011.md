# John – Chapter 11
### “I am the resurrection and the life”

---

## Lazarus is sick (Verses 1–16)

[1] A man was sick.
His name was Lazarus,
and he was from Bethany,
the village of Mary and her sister Martha.
[2] This was the Mary
who had anointed the Lord with ointment
and wiped his feet with her hair.
Her brother Lazarus was sick.
[3] So the sisters sent a message to Jesus,
saying,
“Lord,
he for whom you have great affection
is sick.”
[4] When Jesus heard it,
he said,
“This sickness is not to death,
but for the glory of God,
so that God’s Son may be glorified by it.”
[5] Jesus loved Martha,
and her sister,
and Lazarus.
[6] When he heard
that Lazarus was sick,
he stayed two more days in the place
where he was.
[7] Then after this he said to the disciples,
“Let’s go into Judea again.”
[8] The disciples said to him,
“Rabbi, the Jews were just trying to stone you.
Are you going there again?”
[9] Jesus answered,
“Aren’t there twelve hours of daylight?
If a man walks in the day,
he doesn’t stumble,
because he sees the light of this world.
[10] But if a man walks in the night,
he stumbles,
because the light isn’t in him.”
[11] He said these things.
And after that he said to them,
“Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep.
But I am going
so that I may wake him out of sleep.”
[12] So the disciples said,
“Lord, if he has fallen asleep,
he will recover.”
[13] Now Jesus had spoken of his death.
But they thought
that he spoke of taking rest in sleep.
[14] So Jesus told them plainly,
“Lazarus is dead.
[15] I am glad for your sakes
that I was not there,
so that you may believe.
Nevertheless, let’s go to him.”
[16] Then Thomas,
who is called Didymus,
said to his fellow disciples,
“Let’s also go,
so that we may die with him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bethany was near Jerusalem. The sisters Martha and Mary and their brother Lazarus lived there. They were friends of Jesus.
> Verse 2: John already mentions the anointing here, which he only tells in chapter 12.
> Verse 3: “He for whom you have great affection”: The sisters know how close Jesus and Lazarus are.
> Verse 6: Jesus waits two days. That is hard to understand. John shows: Jesus acts according to God’s time, not according to what people expect.
> Important: If God seems not to help right away, that does not mean that he does not love us. Verse 5 says explicitly: Jesus loved them.
> Verse 8: “The Jews” here means the opponents in Jerusalem (compare 10:31). The disciples and Lazarus are Jews themselves.
> Verse 11: Christians sometimes speak of death as a sleep from which God wakes people up.
> Verse 16: Thomas (“Didymus” is Greek for “twin”) is brave. He wants to go with Jesus, even into danger.

## Jesus and Martha (Verses 17–27)

[17] When Jesus came,
he found that Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days.
[18] Bethany was near Jerusalem,
about fifteen stadia away.
[19] Many of the Jews had come to Martha and Mary
to comfort them about their brother.
[20] When Martha heard
that Jesus was coming,
she went and met him.
But Mary stayed in the house.
[21] Martha said to Jesus,
“Lord, if you had been here,
my brother wouldn’t have died.
[22] Even now I know
that whatever you ask of God,
God will give you.”
[23] Jesus said to her,
“Your brother will rise again.”
[24] Martha said to him,
“I know
that he will rise again
in the resurrection at the last day.”
[25] Jesus said to her,
“I am the resurrection and the life.
He who believes in me
will still live,
even if he dies.
[26] Whoever lives and believes in me
will never die.
Do you believe this?”
[27] She said to him,
“Yes, Lord.
I have come to believe
that you are the Christ,
God’s Son,
he who comes into the world.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Four days in the tomb: According to a Jewish idea that was written down later, the soul stays near the dead person for three days. After four days it was completely certain for everyone that Lazarus was dead.
> Verse 18: Fifteen stadia are almost 2 miles (3 kilometers).
> Verse 19: Many Jews come to comfort the sisters. Visiting people who are grieving was (and is) an important good deed in Judaism. Here “the Jews” is meant in a completely friendly way: They are friends and neighbors.
> Verse 21: Martha says honestly what she thinks, including her disappointment. You may tell God everything, even your complaints.
> Verse 24: Martha believes in the resurrection at the end of time. The Pharisees also believed this.
> Verse 25: “I am the resurrection and the life” is the fifth “I am” saying. These words are often read at funerals.
> Verse 27: Martha’s confession is one of the strongest in the whole Gospel. A woman speaks it.

## Jesus weeps (Verses 28–37)

[28] When she had said this,
she went away
and secretly called her sister Mary.
She said,
“The Teacher is here
and is calling you.”
[29] When Mary heard this,
she got up quickly
and went to him.
[30] Jesus had not yet come into the village.
He was still in the place
where Martha had met him.
[31] The Jews
who were with Mary in the house and were comforting her
saw that she got up quickly and went out.
They followed her,
for they thought,
“She is going to the tomb
to weep there.”
[32] When Mary came
to where Jesus was
and saw him,
she fell down at his feet
and said to him,
“Lord, if you had been here,
my brother wouldn’t have died.”
[33] When Jesus saw her weeping,
and the Jews weeping
who came with her,
he groaned in the spirit
and was troubled.
[34] He said,
“Where have you laid him?”
They said to him,
“Lord, come and see.”
[35] Jesus wept.
[36] Then the Jews said,
“See how much affection he had for him!”
[37] But some of them said,
“This man opened the eyes of the blind man.
Couldn’t he also have kept this man from dying?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 33: Jesus “groaned in the spirit and was troubled.” The Greek word describes deep inner agitation, perhaps also anger at the power of death.
> Verse 35: “Jesus wept” is the shortest verse of the Bible. Jesus weeps with those who are grieving, even though he knows that he will raise Lazarus. He takes their pain seriously.
> Verse 36: The Jews who are mourning with Mary see Jesus’ love. Here too “the Jews” is meant in a completely positive way.
> For today: Grief is not a weakness and not a lack of faith. Jesus wept too. Whoever is grieving may weep. Help is available from grief groups in churches and hospices. In a crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123.

## Jesus raises Lazarus (Verses 38–44)

[38] Jesus was again deeply moved within himself
and came to the tomb.
It was a cave,
and a stone lay against it.
[39] Jesus said,
“Take away the stone.”
Martha, the sister of the dead man,
said to him,
“Lord, by this time there is a stench,
for he has been dead four days.”
[40] Jesus said to her,
“Didn’t I tell you
that if you believed,
you would see God’s glory?”
[41] So they took away the stone.
Jesus lifted up his eyes
and said,
“Father, I thank you
that you listened to me.
[42] I know
that you always listen to me.
But because of the crowd
standing around
I said this,
so that they may believe
that you sent me.”
[43] When he had said this,
he cried with a loud voice,
“Lazarus, come out!”
[44] The man who was dead came out.
His hands and feet were bound with wrappings,
and his face was wrapped around with a cloth.
Jesus said to them,
“Free him,
and let him go.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then tombs were often caves in the rock, with a large stone in front.
> Verses 41–42: Jesus prays out loud so that people will understand: The power comes from God the Father.
> Verse 43: Jesus calls Lazarus by name, like the good shepherd calls his sheep (10:3).
> Verse 44: Lazarus comes back to life, but he will die again later. It is a sign of the resurrection that Jesus himself will experience and wants to give to everyone.
> “Free him”: The people are to help free Lazarus from the grave clothes.
> Many interpreters count this as the seventh and greatest sign in the Gospel of John.

## The decision to kill Jesus (Verses 45–57)

[45] Many of the Jews
who had come to Mary
and had seen
what Jesus did
believed in him.
[46] But some of them went away to the Pharisees
and told them
the things which Jesus had done.
[47] Then the chief priests and the Pharisees
gathered a council
and said,
“What are we doing?
This man does many signs.
[48] If we leave him alone like this,
everyone will believe in him.
Then the Romans will come
and take away both our place
and our nation.”
[49] But one of them,
Caiaphas,
who was high priest that year,
said to them,
“You know nothing at all.
[50] You don’t consider
that it is better for us
that one man should die for the people,
than that the whole nation perish.”
[51] He didn’t say this by himself.
But being high priest that year,
he prophesied
that Jesus would die for the nation,
[52] and not for the nation only,
but also
to gather together into one
the children of God who are scattered abroad.
[53] So from that day on
they made plans
to put him to death.
[54] Therefore Jesus no longer walked openly among the Jews.
He went away from there
into the country near the wilderness,
to a city called Ephraim.
He stayed there with his disciples.
[55] The Passover of the Jews was near.
Many went up from the country to Jerusalem before the Passover
to purify themselves.
[56] They were looking for Jesus
and said to one another
as they stood in the temple,
“What do you think?
Isn’t he coming to the feast at all?”
[57] For the chief priests and the Pharisees
had given orders:
If anyone knew where he was,
he should report it,
so that they might arrest him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: Many Jews believe in Jesus. John shows this again and again.
> Verse 48: “Our place” probably means the temple. The leaders are afraid: If a movement forms around Jesus, the Romans could step in and destroy everything. This fear was not without reason. In the year AD 70 the Romans really did destroy the temple.
> Verses 49–50: Caiaphas was high priest from about AD 18 to 36. He thinks politically: Better for one to die than many.
> Verses 51–52: John sees in this an unintended prophecy: Jesus really dies “for” everyone, for Israel and for the whole world.
> Important: This decision was made by a small group of powerful people in a politically dangerous situation, under Roman occupation. The Jewish people were not involved. And at the same time many people in the temple are looking for Jesus (verse 56). The church teaches clearly: The death of Jesus may not be blamed on the Jews (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).
> Verse 55: Before the Passover feast people had to purify themselves ritually.
