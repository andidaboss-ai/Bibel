# John – Chapter 11
### “I am the resurrection and the life”

---

## Lazarus is sick (Verses 1–16)

[1] A man named Lazarus was sick.
He was from Bethany, the village where Mary and her sister Martha lived.
[2] This Mary had poured fragrant oil on the Lord and dried his feet with her hair.
Her brother Lazarus was the one who was sick.
[3] So the sisters sent a message to Jesus.
They said,
“Lord, look, your dear friend is sick.”
[4] When Jesus heard this, he said,
“This sickness will not end in death.
It is for God’s glory.
Through it, God’s Son will be honored.”
[5] Jesus loved Martha, her sister, and Lazarus.
[6] He heard that Lazarus was sick.
But he stayed two more days in the place where he was.
[7] After that, he said to the disciples,
“Let’s go back to Judea.”
[8] The disciples said to him,
“Rabbi, the Jews there were trying to stone you just a short time ago.
And you want to go back there?”
[9] Jesus answered,
“Doesn’t the day have twelve hours of light?
Whoever walks during the day doesn’t trip,
because he sees the light of this world.
[10] But whoever walks at night trips,
because the light is not in him.”
[11] He said this.
Then he told them,
“Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep.
But I am going there to wake him up.”
[12] The disciples said,
“Lord, if he is sleeping, he will get better.”
[13] But Jesus had been talking about his death.
They thought he meant normal sleep.
[14] So Jesus told them plainly,
“Lazarus is dead.
[15] For your sake, I am glad that I was not there.
This will help you to believe.
But let’s go to him.”
[16] Thomas, who was also called Didymus, said to the other disciples,
“Let’s go too, so that we can die with Jesus.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Bethany was near Jerusalem. The sisters Martha and Mary and their brother Lazarus lived there. They were friends of Jesus.
> Verse 2: John already mentions the anointing here, which he only tells in chapter 12.
> Verse 3: “Your dear friend”: The sisters know how close Jesus and Lazarus are.
> Verse 6: Jesus waits two days. That is hard to understand. John shows: Jesus acts according to God’s time, not according to what people expect.
> Important: If God seems not to help right away, that does not mean that he does not love us. Verse 5 says explicitly: Jesus loved them.
> Verse 8: “The Jews” here means the opponents in Jerusalem (compare 10:31). The disciples and Lazarus are Jews themselves.
> Verse 11: Christians sometimes speak of death as a sleep from which God wakes people up.
> Verse 16: Thomas (“Didymus” is Greek for “twin”) is brave. He wants to go with Jesus, even into danger.

## Jesus and Martha (Verses 17–27)

[17] When Jesus arrived, he learned that Lazarus had already been in the tomb for four days.
[18] Bethany was near Jerusalem.
It was fifteen stadia away, almost 2 miles.
[19] Many Jews had come to Martha and Mary.
They wanted to comfort them because their brother had died.
[20] Martha heard that Jesus was coming.
She went out to meet him.
But Mary stayed at home.
[21] Martha said to Jesus,
“Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.
[22] But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask him for.”
[23] Jesus said to her,
“Your brother will rise again.”
[24] Martha said to him,
“I know that he will rise again on the last day, when the dead rise.”
[25] Jesus said to her,
“I am the resurrection and the life.
Whoever believes in me will live, even if he dies.
[26] And everyone who lives and believes in me will never die.
Do you believe this?”
[27] She said to him,
“Yes, Lord.
I believe that you are the Christ, God’s Son.
You are the one who was to come into the world.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Four days in the tomb: According to a Jewish idea that was written down later, the soul stays near the dead person for three days. After four days it was completely certain for everyone that Lazarus was dead.
> Verse 18: Fifteen stadia are almost 2 miles (3 kilometers).
> Verse 19: Many Jews come to comfort the sisters. Visiting people who are grieving was (and is) an important good deed in Judaism. Here “the Jews” is meant in a completely friendly way: They are friends and neighbors.
> Verse 21: Martha says honestly what she thinks, including her disappointment. You may tell God everything, even your complaints.
> Verse 24: Martha believes in the resurrection at the end of time. The Pharisees also believed this.
> Verse 25: “I am the resurrection and the life” is the fifth “I am” saying. These words are often read at funerals.
> Verse 27: Martha’s confession is one of the strongest in the whole Gospel. A woman speaks it.

## Jesus weeps (Verses 28–37)

[28] After Martha said this, she went back.
She called her sister Mary and said to her quietly,
“The Teacher is here.
He is asking for you.”
[29] When Mary heard this, she got up quickly and went to Jesus.
[30] Jesus had not yet come into the village.
He was still at the place where Martha had met him.
[31] The Jews who were in the house with Mary were comforting her.
They saw her get up quickly and go out.
So they followed her.
They thought, “She is going to the tomb to cry there.”
[32] Mary came to the place where Jesus was.
When she saw him, she fell down at his feet.
She said to him,
“Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.”
[33] Jesus saw her crying.
He also saw the Jews who had come with her crying.
He was deeply moved in his spirit and very upset.
[34] He asked, “Where have you put him?”
They said to him, “Lord, come and see.”
[35] Jesus cried.
[36] So the Jews said,
“See how much he loved him!”
[37] But some of them said,
“This man opened the eyes of the blind man.
Couldn’t he also have kept Lazarus from dying?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 33: Jesus “was deeply moved in his spirit and very upset.” The Greek word describes deep inner agitation, perhaps also anger at the power of death.
> Verse 35: “Jesus cried.” In many Bibles this is the shortest verse of the Bible. Jesus weeps with those who are grieving, even though he knows that he will raise Lazarus. He takes their pain seriously.
> Verse 36: The Jews who are mourning with Mary see Jesus’ love. Here too “the Jews” is meant in a completely positive way.
> For today: Grief is not a weakness and not a lack of faith. Jesus wept too. Whoever is grieving may weep. Help is available from grief groups in churches and hospices. In a crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123.

## Jesus raises Lazarus (Verses 38–44)

[38] Again Jesus was deeply moved inside.
He came to the tomb.
It was a cave, and a stone was lying in front of it.
[39] Jesus said, “Take the stone away.”
Martha, the sister of the dead man, said to him,
“Lord, by now there will be a bad smell.
He has been dead for four days.”
[40] Jesus said to her,
“Didn’t I tell you that you would see God’s glory if you believed?”
[41] So they took the stone away from the place where the dead man was lying.
Jesus looked up and said,
“Father, thank you for listening to me.
[42] I know that you always listen to me.
But I said this because of the crowd standing here.
I want them to believe that you sent me.”
[43] After he said this, he called out with a loud voice,
“Lazarus, come out!”
[44] The dead man came out.
His hands and feet were tied up with strips of cloth.
A cloth was wrapped around his face.
Jesus said to them,
“Free him, and let him go.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Back then tombs were often caves in the rock, with a large stone in front.
> Verses 41–42: Jesus prays out loud so that people will understand: The power comes from God the Father.
> Verse 43: Jesus calls Lazarus by name, like the good shepherd calls his sheep (10:3).
> Verse 44: Lazarus comes back to life, but he will die again later. It is a sign of the resurrection that Jesus himself will experience and wants to give to everyone.
> “Free him”: The people are to help free Lazarus from the grave clothes.
> Many interpreters count this as the seventh and greatest sign in the Gospel of John.

## The decision to kill Jesus (Verses 45–57)

[45] Many of the Jews who had come to visit Mary saw what Jesus did.
And they believed in him.
[46] But some of them went to the Pharisees.
They told them what Jesus had done.
[47] So the chief priests and the Pharisees called a meeting of the council.
They said,
“What are we going to do?
This man is doing many signs.
[48] If we let him go on like this, everyone will believe in him.
Then the Romans will come.
They will take away our place and our nation.”
[49] One of them was Caiaphas.
He was high priest that year.
He said to them,
“You don’t know anything at all.
[50] You don’t understand that it is better for you if one man dies for the people.
Then the whole nation will not be destroyed.”
[51] He did not say this on his own.
He was high priest that year.
So he spoke as a prophet:
Jesus would die for the nation.
[52] And he would not die only for the nation.
He would also gather God’s children who are scattered everywhere and make them one.
[53] So from that day on, they made plans to kill Jesus.
[54] That is why Jesus no longer moved around openly among the Jews.
He left and went to the countryside near the wilderness, to a town called Ephraim.
He stayed there with his disciples.
[55] It was almost time for the Passover of the Jews.
Many people went up from the countryside to Jerusalem before the Passover.
They went there to make themselves clean for the feast.
[56] They were looking for Jesus.
As they stood in the temple, they said to each other,
“What do you think?
Isn’t he coming to the feast at all?”
[57] The chief priests and the Pharisees had given an order:
Anyone who knew where Jesus was had to report it,
so that they could arrest him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: Many Jews believe in Jesus. John shows this again and again.
> Verse 48: “Our place” probably means the temple. The leaders are afraid: If a movement forms around Jesus, the Romans could step in and destroy everything. This fear was not without reason. In the year AD 70 the Romans really did destroy the temple.
> Verses 49–50: Caiaphas was high priest from about AD 18 to 36. He thinks politically: Better for one to die than many.
> Verses 51–52: John sees in this an unintended prophecy: Jesus really dies “for” everyone, for Israel and for the whole world.
> Important: This decision was made by a small group of powerful people in a politically dangerous situation, under Roman occupation. The Jewish people were not involved. And at the same time many people in the temple are looking for Jesus (verse 56). The church teaches clearly: The death of Jesus may not be blamed on the Jews (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).
> Verse 55: Before the Passover feast people had to purify themselves ritually.
