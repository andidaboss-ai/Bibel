# John – Chapter 15
### The vine and the branches

---

## “I am the true vine” (Verses 1–8)

[1] “I am the true vine,
and my Father is the farmer.
[2] Every branch in me
that doesn’t bear fruit,
he takes away.
And every branch
that bears fruit,
he prunes,
so that it may bear more fruit.
[3] You are already pruned clean
because of the word
which I have spoken to you.
[4] Remain in me,
and I in you.
A branch can’t bear fruit by itself
unless it remains in the vine.
So neither can you bear fruit,
unless you remain in me.
[5] I am the vine.
You are the branches.
He who remains in me
and I in him
bears much fruit.
For apart from me you can do nothing.
[6] If someone doesn’t remain in me,
he is thrown out like a branch
and withers.
People gather the branches,
throw them into the fire,
and they are burned.
[7] If you remain in me
and my words remain in you,
you will ask
whatever you desire,
and it will be done for you.
[8] In this my Father is glorified,
that you bear much fruit.
And so you will be my disciples.”

*prune* = cut back a plant so that it grows better

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am the true vine” is the seventh and last “I am” saying in John.
> In the Old Testament the vineyard or the vine is a picture for Israel (Isaiah 5:1–7; Psalm 80:8–16, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 80:9–17). Jesus takes up this picture.
> Important: “The true vine” does not mean that Israel is a “false” vine. Jesus himself is a Jew and belongs to Israel. He says: Whoever is connected with me has life.
> Verse 2: A farmer cuts back the branches so that they bear more grapes. That sometimes hurts, but it helps growth.
> Verse 5: “Apart from me you can do nothing”: As a branch gets its sap from the vine, so Christians get their strength from Jesus.
> Verse 6: This is a picture from growing grapes: Branches that had been cut off and dried out were burned.
> Important: In church history this verse was also used to justify burning “heretics.” That was a terrible injustice. The verse speaks in a picture about what happens without a connection to Jesus. It gives no one the right to do violence to people.
> For today: “Remain in me”: Faith is a living connection, not an achievement.

## “You are my friends” (Verses 9–17)

[9] “Even as the Father has loved me,
I also have loved you.
Remain in my love!
[10] If you keep my commandments,
you will remain in my love,
even as I have kept my Father’s commandments
and remain in his love.
[11] I have told you these things,
so that my joy may remain in you,
and that your joy may be made full.
[12] This is my commandment:
that you love one another,
even as I have loved you.
[13] Greater love has no one than this,
that someone lay down his life for his friends.
[14] You are my friends
if you do
whatever I command you.
[15] No longer do I call you servants,
for the servant doesn’t know
what his lord does.
But I have called you friends,
for everything that I heard from my Father,
I have made known to you.
[16] You didn’t choose me,
but I chose you
and appointed you,
so that you should go and bear fruit,
and that your fruit should remain.
Then the Father will give you
whatever you ask him in my name.
[17] I command these things to you:
that you love one another.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Jesus wants his disciples to have joy, a joy that remains.
> Verse 13: “Greater love has no one than this”: Jesus is speaking about his own death. He gives his life for those whom he loves.
> Important: This verse does not mean that you should throw your life away. It describes how great Jesus’ love is. If you are thinking about ending your life, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 15: Jesus calls his disciples “friends,” not servants. That is a great honor. Abraham was called God’s friend (Isaiah 41:8).
> Verse 16: God looks for people first. Faith begins with God’s call.

## The hatred of the world (Verses 18–27)

[18] “If the world hates you,
know
that it hated me before it hated you.
[19] If you were of the world,
the world would love its own.
But because you are not of the world,
since I chose you out of the world,
therefore the world hates you.
[20] Remember the word
that I said to you:
‘A servant is not greater than his lord.’
If they persecuted me,
they will also persecute you.
If they kept my word,
they will also keep yours.
[21] But they will do all these things to you
because of my name,
because they don’t know him
who sent me.
[22] If I had not come
and spoken to them,
they would not have had sin.
But now they have no excuse for their sin.
[23] He who hates me
hates my Father also.
[24] If I hadn’t done the works among them
which no one else did,
they wouldn’t have had sin.
But now they have seen them
and yet hated both me and my Father.
[25] But this happened
so that the word may be fulfilled
which was written in their law,
‘They hated me without a cause.’
[26] When the Counselor has come,
whom I will send to you from the Father,
the Spirit of truth,
who proceeds from the Father,
he will testify about me.
[27] And you will also testify,
because you have been with me from the beginning.”

*persecute* = hunt down and treat cruelly because of their faith

> **What does this mean?**
> In John, “the world” often means: everything that sets itself against God. It does not mean a certain group of people.
> Verses 18–21: The first Christians experienced rejection and persecution. Jesus prepares his disciples for it.
> Important: These verses do not give Christians the right to feel superior or to label others as “the world.” God loves the world (3:16).
> Verse 25 quotes Psalm 35:19 or Psalm 69:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 69:5). “Law” here means the whole Holy Scripture of Israel.
> Verse 26: The Holy Spirit is the Spirit “who proceeds from the Father.” The churches argued for a long time about where exactly the Spirit comes from: The Western churches confess in the Creed that the Spirit proceeds *from the Father and the Son*. The Orthodox churches only say *from the Father*. Today the churches talk about this in dialogue.
> For today: Christians are to tell about Jesus, kindly and honestly, even if they are rejected for it.
