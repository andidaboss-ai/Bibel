# John – Chapter 15
### The vine and the branches

---

## “I am the true vine” (Verses 1–8)

[1] “I am the true vine.
My Father is the farmer.
[2] He cuts off every branch in me that doesn’t bear fruit.
He prunes every branch that does bear fruit,
so that it will bear even more fruit.
[3] You are already pruned and clean because of the word I have spoken to you.
[4] Remain in me, and I will remain in you.
A branch can’t bear fruit by itself.
It has to remain in the vine.
In the same way, you can’t bear fruit unless you remain in me.
[5] I am the vine.
You are the branches.
If you remain in me and I remain in you, you will bear much fruit.
Without me you can do nothing.
[6] Anyone who doesn’t remain in me is thrown away like a branch.
He dries up.
People gather such branches.
They throw them into the fire, and the branches are burned.
[7] If you remain in me and my words remain in you,
you may ask for whatever you want.
And it will be done for you.
[8] “My Father is glorified when you bear much fruit.
In this way, you will be my disciples.

*prune* = cut back a plant so that it grows better

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am the true vine” is the seventh and last “I am” saying in John.
> In the Old Testament the vineyard or the vine is a picture for Israel (Isaiah 5:1–7; Psalm 80:8–16, in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 80:9–17). Jesus takes up this picture.
> Important: “The true vine” does not mean that Israel is a “false” vine. Jesus himself is a Jew and belongs to Israel. He says: Whoever is connected with me has life.
> Verse 2: A farmer cuts back the branches so that they bear more grapes. That sometimes hurts, but it helps growth.
> Verse 5: “Without me you can do nothing”: As a branch gets its sap from the vine, so Christians get their strength from Jesus.
> Verse 6: This is a picture from growing grapes: Branches that had been cut off and dried out were burned.
> Important: In church history this verse was also used to justify burning “heretics.” That was a terrible injustice. The verse speaks in a picture about what happens without a connection to Jesus. It gives no one the right to do violence to people.
> For today: “Remain in me”: Faith is a living connection, not an achievement.

## “You are my friends” (Verses 9–17)

[9] I have loved you just as the Father has loved me.
Remain in my love.
[10] If you do what I command, you will remain in my love.
In the same way, I have done what my Father commands, and I remain in his love.
[11] I have told you these things so that my joy will be in you,
and so that your joy will be complete.
[12] “This is my command:
Love each other in the same way that I have loved you.
[13] No one has a greater love than the one who gives his life for his friends.
[14] You are my friends if you do what I command you.
[15] I don’t call you servants anymore.
A servant doesn’t know what his master is doing.
But I have called you friends.
I have told you everything I heard from my Father.
[16] You did not choose me.
I chose you.
I gave you the task to go and bear fruit, fruit that lasts.
Then the Father will give you whatever you ask him for in my name.
[17] “This is what I command you:
Love each other.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Jesus wants his disciples to have joy, a joy that remains.
> Verse 13: “No one has a greater love”: Jesus is speaking about his own death. He gives his life for those whom he loves.
> Important: This verse does not mean that you should throw your life away. It describes how great Jesus’ love is. If you are thinking about ending your life, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 15: Jesus calls his disciples “friends,” not servants. That is a great honor. Abraham was called God’s friend (Isaiah 41:8).
> Verse 16: God looks for people first. Faith begins with God’s call.

## The hatred of the world (Verses 18–27)

[18] If the world hates you, remember that it hated me first.
[19] If you belonged to the world, the world would love you as its own.
But you don’t belong to the world.
I chose you out of the world.
That is why the world hates you.
[20] Remember what I told you:
‘A servant is not greater than his master.’
If people persecuted me, they will persecute you too.
If they held on to my word, they will hold on to yours too.
[21] But they will do all these things to you because of my name.
They don’t know the one who sent me.
[22] If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not be guilty of sin.
But now they have no excuse for their sin.
[23] Whoever hates me also hates my Father.
[24] I have done works among them that no one else has ever done.
If I had not done them, they would not be guilty of sin.
But now they have seen these works.
And they have hated both me and my Father.
[25] But this happened so that the words written in their law would come true:
‘They hated me for no reason.’
[26] “I will send you the Counselor from the Father.
He is the Spirit of truth, who comes from the Father.
When he comes, he will tell about me.
[27] And you will also tell about me,
because you have been with me from the beginning.”

*persecute* = hunt down and treat cruelly because of their faith

> **What does this mean?**
> In John, “the world” often means: everything that sets itself against God. It does not mean a certain group of people.
> Verses 18–21: The first Christians experienced rejection and persecution. Jesus prepares his disciples for it.
> Important: These verses do not give Christians the right to feel superior or to label others as “the world.” God loves the world (3:16).
> Verse 25 quotes Psalm 35:19 or Psalm 69:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 69:5). “Law” here means the whole Holy Scripture of Israel.
> Verse 26: The Holy Spirit is the Spirit “who comes from the Father.” The Creed says: He *proceeds* from the Father. The churches argued for a long time about where exactly the Spirit comes from: The Western churches confess in the Creed that the Spirit proceeds *from the Father and the Son*. The Orthodox churches only say *from the Father*. Today the churches talk about this in dialogue.
> For today: Christians are to tell about Jesus, kindly and honestly, even if they are rejected for it.
