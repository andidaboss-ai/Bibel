# John – Chapter 10
### The good shepherd

---

## The shepherd and the door (Verses 1–10)

[1] “Most certainly I tell you,
whoever doesn’t enter the sheepfold by the door,
but climbs in some other way,
is a thief and a robber.
[2] But whoever enters by the door
is the shepherd of the sheep.
[3] The gatekeeper opens the gate for him.
The sheep listen to his voice.
He calls his own sheep by name
and leads them out.
[4] When he has brought out all his own sheep,
he goes before them.
And the sheep follow him,
for they know his voice.
[5] They will by no means follow a stranger,
but will run away from him.
For they don’t know the voice of strangers.”
[6] Jesus told them this parable.
But they didn’t understand
what he was telling them.
[7] So Jesus said to them again,
“Most certainly I tell you,
I am the sheep’s door.
[8] All who came before me
are thieves and robbers.
But the sheep didn’t listen to them.
[9] I am the door.
If anyone enters in by me,
he will be saved.
He will go in and go out
and will find pasture.
[10] The thief only comes
to steal,
kill,
and destroy.
I came
so that they may have life,
and may have it abundantly.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The chapter follows on from the healing of the blind man (chapter 9). Jesus takes care of him like a good shepherd when others throw him out.
> Sheep knew the voice of their shepherd. Several flocks often spent the night together in one pen. In the morning each shepherd called his own sheep out.
> Verse 3: “He calls his own sheep by name.” God knows every person personally.
> Verses 7 and 9: “I am the door” is the third “I am” saying. Through Jesus people find the way to God.
> Verse 8: “All who came before me” does not mean Moses and the prophets. What is meant is false leaders who took advantage of people.
> Verse 10: “Have it abundantly”: God wants people to have a full life.
> For today: Today too there are “thieves” who take advantage of people, also in the name of religion. You can recognize a good shepherd by the fact that he serves life.

## “I am the good shepherd” (Verses 11–21)

[11] “I am the good shepherd.
The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.
[12] But the hired hand,
who is not a shepherd
and doesn’t own the sheep,
sees the wolf coming,
leaves the sheep,
and runs away.
And the wolf snatches the sheep
and scatters them.
[13] The hired hand runs away
because he is a hired hand
and doesn’t care for the sheep.
[14] I am the good shepherd.
I know my own,
and my own know me,
[15] even as the Father knows me,
and I know the Father.
And I lay down my life for the sheep.
[16] I have other sheep
which are not of this fold.
I must bring them also.
They will hear my voice.
And they will become one flock
with one shepherd.
[17] Therefore the Father loves me,
because I lay down my life,
so that I may take it again.
[18] No one takes it away from me,
but I lay it down by myself.
I have power to lay it down,
and I have power to take it again.
I received this commandment from my Father.”
[19] Because of these words a division arose again among the Jews.
[20] Many of them said,
“He has a demon and is insane!
Why do you listen to him?”
[21] Others said,
“These are not the sayings
of one possessed by a demon.
Can a demon open the eyes of the blind?”

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am the good shepherd” is the fourth “I am” saying. In the Old Testament God himself is the shepherd of his people (Psalm 23; Ezekiel 34). Kings and leaders were also called shepherds.
> Verses 11 and 15: Jesus gives his life for his own. This happens on the cross.
> Verse 16: “Other sheep” probably means the people from other nations who will come to Jesus. Important: This does not mean that the Jewish people are replaced. It is about belonging together, not about shutting people out.
> For today: The churches are different, but Jesus wants all who belong to him to be one. That is a call to unity among Christians.
> Verse 18: Jesus goes his way freely. No one can take his life from him unless he gives it himself.
> Verses 19–21: “The Jews” here are listeners who do not agree with each other. Many are open to Jesus.
> Important: Mental illnesses are not possession. “Insane” was used here as an insult. People with mental illnesses deserve respect and help.

## “I and the Father are one” (Verses 22–42)

[22] It was the Feast of the Dedication at Jerusalem.
[23] It was winter.
Jesus was walking in the temple, in Solomon’s porch.
[24] Then the Jews came around him
and said to him,
“How long will you keep us in suspense?
If you are the Christ,
tell us plainly.”
[25] Jesus answered them,
“I told you,
and you don’t believe.
The works
that I do in my Father’s name,
these testify about me.
[26] But you don’t believe,
because you are not of my sheep,
as I told you.
[27] My sheep hear my voice.
I know them,
and they follow me.
[28] I give eternal life to them.
They will never perish,
and no one will snatch them out of my hand.
[29] My Father,
who has given them to me,
is greater than all.
No one is able to snatch them out of my Father’s hand.
[30] I and the Father are one.”
[31] Then the Jews picked up stones again
to stone him.
[32] Jesus answered them,
“I have shown you many good works
from my Father.
For which of those works do you stone me?”
[33] The Jews answered him,
“We don’t stone you for a good work,
but for blasphemy,
because you, being a man,
make yourself God.”
[34] Jesus answered them,
“Isn’t it written in your law,
‘I said, you are gods’?
[35] If he called them gods,
to whom the word of God came
(and the Scripture can’t be broken),
[36] how can you say of him
whom the Father made holy
and sent into the world,
‘You blaspheme,’
because I said,
‘I am the Son of God’?
[37] If I don’t do the works of my Father,
don’t believe me.
[38] But if I do them,
believe the works,
even if you don’t believe me.
Then you will know and believe
that the Father is in me,
and I in the Father.”
[39] They tried again
to seize him.
But he got away from their hands.
[40] He went away again beyond the Jordan,
to the place
where John was baptizing at first.
And he stayed there.
[41] Many came to him
and said,
“John indeed did no sign.
But everything that John said about this man
is true.”
[42] And many believed in him there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The Feast of the Dedication is called “Hanukkah” in Hebrew. It remembers how the temple was dedicated again in 164 BC after it had been made unholy. To this day Jews celebrate it in winter by lighting lights.
> Verse 23: Solomon’s porch was a covered row of columns at the edge of the temple square.
> Verses 28–29: A great comfort: No one can snatch those who belong to Jesus out of God’s hand.
> Verse 30: “I and the Father are one.” Christians see in this a reason for their faith in the triune God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit are one God.
> Verses 31–33: To the opponents this sounds like blasphemy. According to the law, blasphemy was severely punished (Leviticus 24:16). Their concern for God’s honor was serious.
> Verse 34 quotes Psalm 82:6. There judges are called “gods,” because they judge on God’s behalf. Jesus argues like a Jewish teacher.
> Verse 35: “The Scripture can’t be broken”: Jesus holds the Holy Scriptures of Israel to be valid.
> Important: “The Jews” here means opponents in an argument in the temple. It is not a judgment on the Jewish people. At the end of the chapter “many believed in him” (verse 42).
