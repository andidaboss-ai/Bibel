# John – Chapter 10
### The good shepherd

---

## The shepherd and the door (Verses 1–10)

[1] “I tell you the truth:
Someone may not go into the sheep pen through the door.
He may climb in some other way.
That person is a thief and a robber.
[2] But the one who goes in through the door is the shepherd of the sheep.
[3] The gatekeeper opens the gate for him.
The sheep listen to his voice.
He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.
[4] When he has brought all his own sheep out, he walks ahead of them.
The sheep follow him, because they know his voice.
[5] They will never follow a stranger.
They will run away from him,
because they don’t know the voice of strangers.”
[6] Jesus told them this picture story.
But they didn’t understand what he was telling them.
[7] So Jesus spoke to them again.
He said,
“I tell you the truth:
I am the door for the sheep.
[8] All who came before me are thieves and robbers.
But the sheep didn’t listen to them.
[9] I am the door.
Anyone who comes in through me will be saved.
He will go in and out and find pasture.
[10] The thief comes only to steal, to kill, and to destroy.
I came so that they can have life, and have it in abundance.

> **What does this mean?**
> The chapter follows on from the healing of the blind man (chapter 9). Jesus takes care of him like a good shepherd when others throw him out.
> Sheep knew the voice of their shepherd. Several flocks often spent the night together in one pen. In the morning each shepherd called his own sheep out.
> Verse 3: “He calls his own sheep by name.” God knows every person personally.
> Verses 7 and 9: “I am the door” is the third “I am” saying. Through Jesus people find the way to God.
> Verse 8: “All who came before me” does not mean Moses and the prophets. What is meant is false leaders who took advantage of people.
> Verse 10: “Have it in abundance”: God wants people to have a full life.
> For today: Today too there are “thieves” who take advantage of people, also in the name of religion. You can recognize a good shepherd by the fact that he serves life.

## “I am the good shepherd” (Verses 11–21)

[11] “I am the good shepherd.
The good shepherd gives his life for the sheep.
[12] A hired worker is not the shepherd.
The sheep don’t belong to him.
So when he sees the wolf coming, he leaves the sheep and runs away.
The wolf grabs the sheep and scatters the flock.
[13] The hired worker runs away because he is only paid to do the work.
He doesn’t care about the sheep.
[14] I am the good shepherd.
I know my sheep, and my sheep know me,
[15] just as the Father knows me and I know the Father.
I give my life for the sheep.
[16] I have other sheep too.
They are not from this sheep pen.
I must bring them too.
They will listen to my voice.
Then there will be one flock and one shepherd.
[17] This is why the Father loves me:
I give my life, so that I can take it back again.
[18] No one takes my life from me.
I give it of my own free will.
I have the power to give it.
And I have the power to take it back again.
My Father gave me this command.”
[19] These words caused the Jews to be divided again.
[20] Many of them said,
“He has a demon.
He is out of his mind!
Why do you listen to him?”
[21] Others said,
“Someone who has a demon doesn’t talk like this.
A demon can’t open the eyes of blind people, can it?”

> **What does this mean?**
> “I am the good shepherd” is the fourth “I am” saying. In the Old Testament God himself is the shepherd of his people (Psalm 23; Ezekiel 34). Kings and leaders were also called shepherds.
> Verses 11 and 15: Jesus gives his life for his own. This happens on the cross.
> Verse 16: “Other sheep” probably means the people from other nations who will come to Jesus. Important: This does not mean that the Jewish people are replaced. It is about belonging together, not about shutting people out.
> For today: The churches are different, but Jesus wants all who belong to him to be one. That is a call to unity among Christians.
> Verse 18: Jesus goes his way freely. No one can take his life from him unless he gives it himself.
> Verses 19–21: “The Jews” here are listeners who do not agree with each other. Many are open to Jesus.
> Important: Mental illnesses are not possession. “Out of his mind” was used here as an insult. People with mental illnesses deserve respect and help.

## “I and the Father are one” (Verses 22–42)

[22] Then came the Feast of the Dedication in Jerusalem.
[23] It was winter.
Jesus was walking in the temple area, in Solomon’s porch.
[24] The Jews gathered around him.
They said to him,
“How long will you keep us guessing?
If you are the Christ, tell us clearly.”
[25] Jesus answered them,
“I told you, and you don’t believe.
I do works in my Father’s name.
These works speak for me.
[26] But you don’t believe,
because you are not my sheep.
I already told you that.
[27] My sheep listen to my voice.
I know them, and they follow me.
[28] I give them eternal life.
They will never be lost.
No one will tear them out of my hand.
[29] My Father has given them to me.
He is greater than all.
No one can tear them out of my Father’s hand.
[30] I and the Father are one.”
[31] Then the Jews picked up stones again to stone him.
[32] Jesus answered them,
“I have shown you many good works from my Father.
For which of these works are you stoning me?”
[33] The Jews answered him,
“We are not stoning you for a good work.
We are stoning you because you speak against God.
You are a human being, but you make yourself God.”
[34] Jesus answered them,
“Your law says, ‘I said, you are gods.’
Isn’t that so?
[35] God called those people gods, the people to whom his word came.
And the Scriptures can’t be broken.
[36] The Father set me apart as holy and sent me into the world.
So why do you say to me, ‘You are speaking against God,’
because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?
[37] If I don’t do the works of my Father, then don’t believe me.
[38] But if I do them, believe the works, even if you don’t believe me.
Then you will know and understand that the Father is in me,
and that I am in the Father.”
[39] Again they tried to grab him.
But he slipped out of their hands.
[40] Jesus went away again to the other side of the Jordan.
He went to the place where John had first baptized people.
He stayed there.
[41] Many people came to him.
They said,
“John did not do any sign.
But everything John said about this man is true.”
[42] Many believed in him there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The Feast of the Dedication is called “Hanukkah” in Hebrew. It remembers how the temple was dedicated again in 164 BC after it had been made unholy. To this day Jews celebrate it in winter by lighting lights.
> Verse 23: Solomon’s porch was a covered row of columns at the edge of the temple square.
> Verses 28–29: A great comfort: No one can tear those who belong to Jesus out of God’s hand.
> Verse 30: “I and the Father are one.” Christians see in this a reason for their faith in the triune God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit are one God.
> Verses 31–33: To the opponents this sounds like blasphemy, that is, speaking against God.
 According to the law, blasphemy was severely punished (Leviticus 24:16). Their concern for God’s honor was serious.
> Verse 34 quotes Psalm 82:6. There judges are called “gods,” because they judge on God’s behalf. Jesus argues like a Jewish teacher.
> Verse 35: “The Scriptures can’t be broken”: Jesus holds the Holy Scriptures of Israel to be valid.
> Important: “The Jews” here means opponents in an argument in the temple. It is not a judgment on the Jewish people. At the end of the chapter “many believed in him” (verse 42).
