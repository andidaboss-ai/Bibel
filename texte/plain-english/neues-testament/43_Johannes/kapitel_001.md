# John – Chapter 1
### The Word became human

---

## Before we begin: The Gospel of John

The Gospel of John is the fourth Gospel. It is different from the other three (Matthew, Mark, Luke). It tells fewer parables, but has long conversations and speeches. Here Jesus often says who he is: the bread of life, the light of the world, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life.
According to old church tradition, it was written by the apostle John, a son of Zebedee. The book itself names as its witness the disciple whom Jesus loved (21:20–24). Who exactly that was is debated among scholars. The Gospel was probably written around AD 90 to 100.
John tells of seven great “signs,” for example the wedding at Cana and the raising of Lazarus. He says himself why he writes: so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life (20:31).
**A very important note:** John often speaks of “the Jews,” even where he means opponents of Jesus. This language was terribly misused in history and has contributed to hatred and persecution. You need to know: Jesus, his mother, all the disciples and the author himself were Jews. In many places John uses “the Jews” to mean certain leaders in Jerusalem, not the whole people. The Gospel itself says that salvation comes from the Jews (4:22). We translate exactly what the text says. In the boxes we explain who is meant each time. No word of this book may be used against Jews.
The Gospel has 21 chapters.

## In the beginning was the Word (Verses 1–18)

[1] In the beginning was the Word.
And the Word was with God.
And the Word was God.
[2] The same was in the beginning with God.
[3] All things were made through him.
Without him, nothing was made
that has been made.
[4] In him was life.
And the life was the light of men.
[5] The light shines in the darkness.
And the darkness hasn’t overcome it.
[6] There came a man
sent from God.
His name was John.
[7] He came as a witness.
He was to tell about the light,
so that all might believe through him.
[8] He was not the light himself.
He was sent
to tell about the light.
[9] The true light,
which gives light to everyone,
was coming into the world.
[10] He was in the world,
and the world was made through him.
But the world didn’t recognize him.
[11] He came to his own,
and those who were his own didn’t receive him.
[12] But as many as received him,
to them he gave the right to become God’s children:
to those who believe in his name.
[13] They were born, not of blood,
nor of the will of the flesh,
nor of the will of man,
but of God.
[14] The Word became flesh
and lived among us.
We saw his glory,
the glory of the only born Son of the Father,
full of grace and truth.
[15] John testified about him.
He cried out,
“This was he
of whom I said,
‘He who comes after me
has surpassed me,
for he was before me.’”
[16] From his fullness we all received
grace upon grace.
[17] For the law was given through Moses.
Grace and truth became real through Jesus Christ.
[18] No one has ever seen God.
The only born Son,
who is close to the Father’s heart,
has made him known.

> **What does this mean?**
> These verses are called the “prologue,” the foreword. They are among the best-known texts of the Bible.
> “In the beginning” is a reminder of the first sentence of the Bible, where God creates the heavens and the earth (Genesis 1:1). God created the world through his word: God said, and it happened.
> “The Word” (in Greek “Logos”) is Jesus. John says: Jesus was already with God before creation. He is God. Everything came into being through him. That is why Christians believe: Jesus is true God and true man.
> Verses 6–8: John here is John the Baptizer, not the author of the Gospel.
> Verse 11: Some interpreters understand “his own” as the people of the world he created, others as his people Israel. Important: This is not a judgment on the Jewish people. Many Jews received Jesus, first of all his disciples. Verse 12 speaks right away of those who received him.
> Verse 14: “The Word became flesh” means: God became a human being, with a body, feelings and limits. Christians celebrate this at Christmas. “Lived among us” literally means “pitched his tent among us.” That is a reminder of the holy tent in which God lived among Israel in the wilderness (Exodus 40:34).
> Verse 17: The law through Moses is also a gift from God. John does not present it as bad. He says: In Jesus God’s grace comes once again, new and very close.
> Verse 18: The WEB says “The only born Son.” The oldest manuscripts have “the only God” or “the only born God.” The WEB says the Son is “in the bosom of the Father”: very close to him.
> For today: God is not far away. In Jesus he became human and knows our life from the inside.

## John the Baptizer gives an answer (Verses 19–28)

[19] This is John’s testimony:
The Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to him.
They were to ask him,
“Who are you?”
[20] He declared it openly
and didn’t deny it.
He declared,
“I am not the Christ.”
[21] They asked him,
“What then? Are you Elijah?”
He said,
“I am not.”
“Are you the prophet?”
He answered,
“No.”
[22] So they said to him,
“Who are you?
Give us an answer to take back
to those who sent us.
What do you say about yourself?”
[23] He said,
“I am the voice of one
crying in the wilderness,
‘Make straight the way of the Lord,’
as Isaiah the prophet said.”
[24] The ones who had been sent were from the Pharisees.
[25] They asked him,
“Why then do you baptize,
if you are not the Christ,
nor Elijah,
nor the prophet?”
[26] John answered them,
“I baptize in water.
But among you stands one
whom you don’t know.
[27] He is the one
who comes after me,
who is preferred before me.
I’m not worthy
to loosen the strap of his sandal.”
[28] These things happened in Bethany
beyond the Jordan,
where John was baptizing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: “The Jews” here means the religious leaders in Jerusalem. They send a group of messengers. The question of who John is was a serious and fair question for them.
> Priests and Levites served in the temple.
> Verse 21: Many expected the prophet Elijah to come back before the Messiah (Malachi 4:5, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 3:23). “The prophet” means the prophet like Moses whom God had promised (Deuteronomy 18:15).
> John says clearly who he is not. He does not want to be important. He points to someone else.
> Verse 23 quotes Isaiah 40:3.
> Verse 27: Loosening sandals was the work of a slave. John says: I am not even worthy to do him this service.
> Verse 28: This Bethany is by the Jordan, not the village near Jerusalem. Some manuscripts have “Bethabara.”

## The Lamb of God (Verses 29–34)

[29] The next day John saw Jesus coming to him
and said,
“Look, the Lamb of God,
who takes away the sin of the world!
[30] This is he
of whom I said,
‘After me comes a man
who is preferred before me,
for he was before me.’
[31] I didn’t know him.
But for this reason I came
baptizing in water,
so that he would be revealed to Israel.”
[32] John testified,
“I have seen
the Spirit coming down like a dove out of heaven,
and it remained on him.
[33] I didn’t recognize him.
But he who sent me
to baptize in water
said to me,
‘The one on whom you see the Spirit coming down
and remaining,
he is the one
who baptizes in the Holy Spirit.’
[34] I have seen it
and have testified
that this is the Son of God.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Lamb of God” is a reminder of the Passover lamb, which recalls the freeing from Egypt (Exodus 12). It is also a reminder of the suffering servant of God, who was led like a lamb (Isaiah 53:7).
> “Who takes away the sin of the world”: Christians believe that through his death Jesus carries the guilt of all people. In many worship services people still sing: *Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world.*
> John does not tell the baptism of Jesus directly. John the Baptizer only reports what he saw.
> Verse 34: Instead of Son of God, some old manuscripts have the Chosen One of God.

## The first disciples (Verses 35–42)

[35] The next day John was standing there again
with two of his disciples.
[36] He looked at Jesus as he walked by
and said,
“Look, the Lamb of God!”
[37] The two disciples heard him say this,
and they followed Jesus.
[38] Jesus turned,
saw them following,
and said to them,
“What are you looking for?”
They said to him,
“Rabbi (which means Teacher),
where are you staying?”
[39] He said to them,
“Come and see.”
They came
and saw where he was staying,
and they stayed with him that day.
It was about the tenth hour.
[40] One of the two
who heard John and followed Jesus
was Andrew,
Simon Peter’s brother.
[41] He first found his own brother, Simon,
and said to him,
“We have found the Messiah!”
(which means Christ).
[42] He brought him to Jesus.
Jesus looked at him
and said,
“You are Simon the son of Jonah.
You shall be called Cephas”
(which means Peter).

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 38: “What are you looking for?” are the first words of Jesus in the Gospel of John. This question is for all people.
> Verse 39: “Come and see.” Jesus invites. People are to get to know him for themselves. The tenth hour is about 4 in the afternoon.
> The second disciple is not named. Some interpreters think it was the disciple whom Jesus loved.
> “Messiah” (Hebrew) and “Christ” (Greek) both mean “the Anointed One.”
> Verse 42: “Cephas” (Aramaic) and “Peter” (Greek) both mean “rock” or “stone.” Instead of “son of Jonah” many old manuscripts have “son of John.”
> For today: Whoever has found Jesus tells others, like Andrew told his brother.

## Philip and Nathanael (Verses 43–51)

[43] The next day Jesus decided to go to Galilee.
He found Philip
and said to him,
“Follow me.”
[44] Philip was from Bethsaida,
the city of Andrew and Peter.
[45] Philip found Nathanael
and said to him,
“We have found him
of whom Moses wrote in the law,
and also the prophets:
Jesus of Nazareth,
the son of Joseph.”
[46] Nathanael said to him,
“Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?”
Philip said to him,
“Come and see.”
[47] Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him
and said about him,
“Here is an Israelite indeed,
in whom is no deceit!”
[48] Nathanael said to him,
“How do you know me?”
Jesus answered him,
“Before Philip called you,
when you were under the fig tree,
I saw you.”
[49] Nathanael answered him,
“Rabbi, you are the Son of God!
You are King of Israel!”
[50] Jesus answered him,
“Because I told you,
‘I saw you underneath the fig tree,’
do you believe?
You will see greater things than these!”
[51] And he said to him,
“Most certainly I tell you all,
from now on you will see heaven opened,
and the angels of God going up and coming down
on the Son of Man.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nathanael appears only in the Gospel of John. Some interpreters think he is the same person as Bartholomew in the other Gospels. That is not certain.
> Verse 46: Nazareth was a small, unimportant village. Nathanael is doubtful. Philip does not argue, but invites: “Come and see.”
> Verse 47: Jesus praises Nathanael as “an Israelite indeed,” honest and without deceit. That is great praise for a devout Jew.
> Verse 48: People liked to sit under the fig tree to read the Holy Scriptures and to pray.
> Verse 51 is a reminder of Jacob’s dream of the ladder to heaven (Genesis 28:12). Jesus is the connection between heaven and earth. The words “from now on” are missing in the oldest manuscripts.
> For today: Doubts and questions are allowed. Jesus takes doubters seriously too.
