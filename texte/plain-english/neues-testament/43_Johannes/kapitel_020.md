# John – Chapter 20
### Jesus is alive!

---

## The empty tomb (Verses 1–10)

[1] On the first day of the week,
Mary Magdalene went early to the tomb,
while it was still dark.
She saw
that the stone had been taken away from the tomb.
[2] So she ran
and came to Simon Peter
and to the other disciple
whom Jesus loved.
She said to them,
“They have taken away the Lord out of the tomb,
and we don’t know
where they have laid him!”
[3] So Peter and the other disciple went out,
and they went toward the tomb.
[4] They both ran together.
The other disciple outran Peter
and came to the tomb first.
[5] He stooped
and looked in.
He saw the linen cloths lying there.
But he didn’t go in.
[6] Then Simon Peter came,
following him,
and went into the tomb.
He saw the linen cloths lying there,
[7] and the cloth
that had been on Jesus’ head.
It was not lying with the linen cloths,
but rolled up in a place by itself.
[8] Then the other disciple also went in,
who had come to the tomb first.
He saw
and believed.
[9] For as yet they didn’t understand the Scripture,
that he must rise from the dead.
[10] So the disciples went away again to their own homes.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The first day of the week” is Sunday. That is why Christians hold their worship services on Sunday: It is the day of the resurrection.
> Mary Magdalene is the first at the tomb. She was a faithful disciple of Jesus (Luke 8:2). She was not a sinful woman or a prostitute, as people often claimed later. That is not written anywhere in the Bible.
> Verse 2: “We don’t know”: Perhaps other women were there too, as the other Gospels tell.
> Verses 5–7: The cloths are lying there neatly. That doesn’t look like grave robbery.
> Verse 8: The beloved disciple sees the empty tomb and believes.
> For today: Easter begins in the dark (verse 1). Even when it is dark in our lives, God can begin something new.

## Jesus meets Mary Magdalene (Verses 11–18)

[11] But Mary was standing outside at the tomb,
weeping.
As she wept,
she stooped
and looked into the tomb.
[12] She saw two angels in white sitting,
one at the head
and one at the feet,
where the body of Jesus had lain.
[13] They asked her,
“Woman, why are you weeping?”
She said to them,
“Because they have taken away my Lord,
and I don’t know
where they have laid him.”
[14] When she had said this,
she turned around
and saw Jesus standing there.
But she didn’t know
that it was Jesus.
[15] Jesus said to her,
“Woman, why are you weeping?
Who are you looking for?”
She thought
he was the gardener,
and said to him,
“Sir, if you have carried him away,
tell me
where you have laid him,
and I will take him away.”
[16] Jesus said to her,
“Mary.”
She turned
and said to him,
“Rabboni!”
which means,
“Teacher!”
[17] Jesus said to her,
“Don’t hold on to me,
for I haven’t yet ascended to my Father.
But go to my brothers
and tell them,
‘I am ascending to my Father
and your Father,
to my God
and your God.’”
[18] Mary Magdalene came
and told the disciples
that she had seen the Lord,
and that he had said these things to her.

*ascend* = go up

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: At first Mary doesn’t recognize Jesus. The risen Jesus is the same and yet changed.
> Verse 16: When Jesus calls her by name, she recognizes him. This reminds us of the good shepherd, who calls his own sheep by name (10:3).
> “Rabboni” is Aramaic and means “my teacher.”
> Verse 17: “Don’t hold on to me”: Mary is not to hold on to Jesus as before. Now a new kind of connection begins.
> “My brothers”: For the first time Jesus calls his disciples this. Through him God is their Father too.
> Verse 18: Mary Magdalene is the first witness of the resurrection. That is why the church calls her the apostle to the apostles. Women were the first messengers of Easter.

## Jesus comes to the disciples (Verses 19–23)

[19] It was evening on that day,
the first day of the week.
The disciples were gathered.
The doors were locked
for fear of the Jews.
Then Jesus came,
stood in the middle,
and said to them,
“Peace be to you.”
[20] When he had said this,
he showed them his hands
and his side.
The disciples were glad
when they saw the Lord.
[21] So Jesus said to them again,
“Peace be to you.
As the Father has sent me,
even so I send you.”
[22] When he had said this,
he breathed on them
and said to them,
“Receive the Holy Spirit!
[23] If you forgive anyone’s sins,
they have been forgiven them.
If you retain anyone’s sins,
they have been retained.”

*retain* = here: not forgive

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: “For fear of the Jews”: The disciples were Jews themselves. They were afraid of the leaders who had handed Jesus over.
> “Peace be to you” (in Hebrew “shalom”) is the greeting of the risen Jesus. He doesn’t blame the disciples, even though they had run away.
> Verse 20: The wounds show: The risen Jesus is really the one who was crucified.
> Verse 22: Jesus breathes on the disciples. This reminds us of creation: God breathed the breath of life into the human being (Genesis 2:7).
> Verse 23: The churches understand this verse in different ways. In the Catholic and Orthodox churches it is one basis for confession. Protestant churches stress: All Christians may speak forgiveness to one another.
> For today: Forgiveness sets people free.

## Jesus and Thomas (Verses 24–29)

[24] But Thomas,
one of the twelve,
called Didymus,
wasn’t with them
when Jesus came.
[25] So the other disciples said to him,
“We have seen the Lord!”
But he said to them,
“Unless I see in his hands
the print of the nails,
put my finger into the print of the nails,
and put my hand into his side,
I will not believe.”
[26] After eight days,
his disciples were inside again,
and Thomas was with them.
The doors were locked.
Jesus came,
stood in the middle,
and said,
“Peace be to you.”
[27] Then he said to Thomas,
“Reach your finger here,
and see my hands.
Reach your hand here,
and put it into my side.
Don’t be unbelieving,
but believing.”
[28] Thomas answered him,
“My Lord and my God!”
[29] Jesus said to him,
“Because you have seen me,
you have believed.
Blessed are those
who have not seen
and have believed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Didymus” is Greek and means “twin.”
> Verse 25: Thomas is often called doubting Thomas. But he only wants to experience for himself what the others have experienced. Doubt is allowed.
> Verse 27: Jesus doesn’t turn Thomas away. He comes to meet him. John doesn’t tell whether Thomas really touched Jesus.
> Verse 28: “My Lord and my God!” is the strongest confession in the Gospel of John. It links back to the beginning: “The Word was God” (1:1).
> Verse 29: This is true for all later Christians. They have not seen Jesus and can still believe.
> For today: Whoever doubts still belongs.

## Why this book was written (Verses 30–31)

[30] Jesus did many other signs in front of his disciples,
which are not written in this book.
[31] But these are written
so that you may believe
that Jesus is the Christ,
the Son of God,
and that believing
you may have life in his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here John says why he wrote his Gospel: The readers are to believe in Jesus and find life.
> He didn’t write down everything, but made a selection.
> Many scholars think: The Gospel originally ended here. Chapter 21 was probably added later, perhaps by students of John. But in all old manuscripts chapter 21 belongs to it.
