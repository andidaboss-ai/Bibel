# John – Chapter 20
### Jesus is alive!

---

## The empty tomb (Verses 1–10)

[1] It was the first day of the week.
Early in the morning, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb.
She saw that the stone had been taken away from the tomb.
[2] So she ran to Simon Peter and to the other disciple, the one Jesus loved.
She said to them, “They have taken the Lord out of the tomb!
And we don’t know where they have laid him!”
[3] So Peter and the other disciple set out for the tomb.
[4] The two of them ran together.
But the other disciple ran faster than Peter.
He came to the tomb first.
[5] He bent down and looked in.
He saw the strips of linen cloth lying there.
But he didn’t go in.
[6] Then Simon Peter came after him.
He went into the tomb.
He saw the strips of linen lying there.
[7] He also saw the cloth that had been on Jesus’ head.
It was not lying with the strips of linen.
It was rolled up in a place by itself.
[8] Then the other disciple went in too.
He was the one who had come to the tomb first.
He saw, and he believed.
[9] Until then they had not understood the Scripture.
It says that he must rise from the dead.
[10] Then the disciples went back to their homes.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The first day of the week” is Sunday. That is why Christians hold their worship services on Sunday: It is the day of the resurrection.
> Mary Magdalene is the first at the tomb. She was a faithful disciple of Jesus (Luke 8:2). She was not a sinful woman or a prostitute, as people often claimed later. That is not written anywhere in the Bible.
> Verse 2: “We don’t know”: Perhaps other women were there too, as the other Gospels tell.
> Verses 5–7: The cloths are lying there neatly. That doesn’t look like grave robbery.
> Verse 8: The beloved disciple sees the empty tomb and believes.
> For today: Easter begins in the dark (verse 1). Even when it is dark in our lives, God can begin something new.

## Jesus meets Mary Magdalene (Verses 11–18)

[11] But Mary stood outside at the tomb and cried.
As she cried, she bent down and looked into the tomb.
[12] She saw two angels in white.
They were sitting where the body of Jesus had been lying.
One sat at the head, and one sat at the feet.
[13] They asked her, “Woman, why are you crying?”
She said to them, “They have taken away my Lord.
And I don’t know where they have laid him.”
[14] After she said this, she turned around.
She saw Jesus standing there.
But she didn’t know that it was Jesus.
[15] Jesus said to her, “Woman, why are you crying?
Who are you looking for?”
She thought he was the gardener.
So she said to him, “Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him.
Then I will take him away.”
[16] Jesus said to her, “Mary.”
She turned and said to him, “Rabboni!”
That means “Teacher!”
[17] Jesus said to her, “Don’t hold on to me.
I have not yet ascended to my Father.
But go to my brothers and tell them:
‘I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.’”
[18] Mary Magdalene went to the disciples.
She told them that she had seen the Lord.
And she told them what he had said to her.

*ascend* = go up

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: At first Mary doesn’t recognize Jesus. The risen Jesus is the same and yet changed.
> Verse 16: When Jesus calls her by name, she recognizes him. This reminds us of the good shepherd, who calls his own sheep by name (10:3).
> “Rabboni” is Aramaic and means “my teacher.”
> Verse 17: “Don’t hold on to me”: Mary is not to hold on to Jesus as before. Now a new kind of connection begins.
> “My brothers”: For the first time Jesus calls his disciples this. Through him God is their Father too.
> Verse 18: Mary Magdalene is the first witness of the resurrection. That is why the church calls her the apostle to the apostles. Women were the first messengers of Easter.

## Jesus comes to the disciples (Verses 19–23)

[19] It was the evening of that same day, the first day of the week.
The disciples had gathered.
They had locked the doors, because they were afraid of the Jews.
Then Jesus came and stood among them.
He said to them, “Peace be to you.”
[20] After he said this, he showed them his hands and his side.
The disciples were glad when they saw the Lord.
[21] Jesus said to them again, “Peace be to you.
The Father has sent me.
In the same way I am sending you.”
[22] After he said this, he breathed on them.
He said to them, “Receive the Holy Spirit!
[23] If you forgive anyone’s sins, they are forgiven.
If you retain anyone’s sins, they are retained.”

*retain* = here: not forgive

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: “Afraid of the Jews”: The disciples were Jews themselves. They were afraid of the leaders who had handed Jesus over.
> “Peace be to you” (in Hebrew “shalom”) is the greeting of the risen Jesus. He doesn’t blame the disciples, even though they had run away.
> Verse 20: The wounds show: The risen Jesus is really the one who was crucified.
> Verse 22: Jesus breathes on the disciples. This reminds us of creation: God breathed the breath of life into the human being (Genesis 2:7).
> Verse 23: The churches understand this verse in different ways. In the Catholic and Orthodox churches it is one basis for confession. Protestant churches stress: All Christians may speak forgiveness to one another.
> For today: Forgiveness sets people free.

## Jesus and Thomas (Verses 24–29)

[24] Thomas, one of the Twelve, was not with them when Jesus came.
He was also called Didymus.
[25] So the other disciples told him, “We have seen the Lord!”
But he said to them, “First I must see the marks of the nails in his hands.
I must put my finger into the marks of the nails.
I must put my hand into his side.
If I don’t, I will not believe.”
[26] Eight days later the disciples were inside again.
This time Thomas was with them.
The doors were locked.
Jesus came and stood among them.
He said, “Peace be to you.”
[27] Then he said to Thomas, “Put your finger here, and look at my hands.
Reach out your hand, and put it into my side.
Don’t be an unbeliever. Be a believer.”
[28] Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!”
[29] Jesus said to him, “You have believed because you have seen me.
Blessed are those who have not seen and still have believed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Didymus” is Greek and means “twin.”
> Verse 25: Thomas is often called doubting Thomas. But he only wants to experience for himself what the others have experienced. Doubt is allowed.
> Verse 27: Jesus doesn’t turn Thomas away. He comes to meet him. John doesn’t tell whether Thomas really touched Jesus.
> Verse 28: “My Lord and my God!” is the strongest confession in the Gospel of John. It links back to the beginning: “The Word was God” (1:1).
> Verse 29: This is true for all later Christians. They have not seen Jesus and can still believe.
> For today: Whoever doubts still belongs.

## Why this book was written (Verses 30–31)

[30] Jesus did many other signs in front of his disciples.
They are not written in this book.
[31] But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God.
And if you believe, you may have life in his name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here John says why he wrote his Gospel: The readers are to believe in Jesus and find life.
> He didn’t write down everything, but made a selection.
> Many scholars think: The Gospel originally ended here. Chapter 21 was probably added later, perhaps by students of John. But in all old manuscripts chapter 21 belongs to it.
