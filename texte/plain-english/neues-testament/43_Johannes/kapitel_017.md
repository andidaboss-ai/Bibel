# John – Chapter 17
### Jesus prays for his own

---

## Jesus prays for himself (Verses 1–5)

[1] After Jesus had said these things, he looked up to heaven.
He said,
“Father, the time has come.
Glorify your Son,
so that your Son can glorify you.
[2] You gave him power over all flesh.
So he will give eternal life to everyone you have given him.
[3] And this is eternal life:
to know you, the only true God,
and to know Jesus Christ, whom you sent.
[4] I have glorified you on earth.
I have finished the work you gave me to do.
[5] So now, Father, glorify me with you.
Give me the glory that I had with you before the world began.

> **What does this mean?**
> John 17 is the longest prayer of Jesus in the Bible. It is often called the “High Priestly Prayer”: Jesus stands up for his own before God, like a priest.
> Verse 1: “The time” is the hour of his death and his glorification.
> Verse 2: “All flesh” is a biblical expression for all people.
> Verse 3: In John, “eternal life” is not only a life after death. It already begins now: knowing God and being connected with him.
> “The only true God”: Jesus confesses the one God of Israel. This connects Jews and Christians (Deuteronomy 6:4).

---

## Jesus prays for his disciples (Verses 6–19)

[6] “I have shown your name to the people you gave me out of the world.
They belonged to you, and you gave them to me.
They have held on to your word.
[7] Now they know that everything you have given me comes from you.
[8] I have given them the words you gave me.
They accepted them.
They know for sure that I came from you.
They have believed that you sent me.
[9] I am praying for them.
I don’t pray for the world.
I pray for the people you have given me,
because they belong to you.
[10] Everything that is mine is yours.
And everything that is yours is mine.
And I am glorified in them.
[11] I will not be in the world anymore.
But they are still in the world, and I am coming to you.
Holy Father, protect them through your name, the name you have given me.
Then they will be one, just as we are one.
[12] While I was with them in the world, I kept them safe in your name.
I protected the ones you gave me.
Not one of them is lost except the son of destruction.
This happened so that the Scriptures would come true.
[13] But now I am coming to you.
I am saying these things while I am still in the world,
so that my joy will fill them completely.
[14] I have given them your word.
The world has hated them,
because they don’t belong to the world,
just as I don’t belong to the world.
[15] I am not asking you to take them out of the world.
I am asking you to protect them from the evil one.
[16] They don’t belong to the world,
just as I don’t belong to the world.
[17] Sanctify them in your truth.
Your word is truth.
[18] You sent me into the world.
In the same way, I have sent them into the world.
[19] I sanctify myself for them,
so that they also will be sanctified in truth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sanctify means: make holy, set apart for God.
> Verse 9: “I don’t pray for the world”: Here Jesus prays especially for his disciples. That does not mean that he doesn’t care about the world. God loves the world (3:16), and the disciples are sent into the world (verse 18).
> Verse 11: “Then they will be one”: Jesus prays for the unity of his disciples.
> Verse 12: “The son of destruction” means Judas.
> Verse 15: Christians are not to flee from the world, but to live in the world, protected from evil.
> Verse 17: “Sanctify” means: to be set apart for God, to belong to God.
> For today: Jesus prays for his friends. We may also pray for one another.

## Jesus prays for all who will believe (Verses 20–26)

[20] “I am not praying only for these disciples.
I am also praying for the people who will believe in me because of their word.
[21] I pray that they will all be one.
Father, you are in me, and I am in you.
In the same way, may they also be one in us.
Then the world will believe that you sent me.
[22] I have given them the glory you gave me,
so that they will be one, just as we are one.
[23] I am in them, and you are in me.
So they will become completely one.
Then the world will know that you sent me.
It will know that you have loved them just as you have loved me.
[24] Father, you have given them to me.
I want them to be with me where I am.
I want them to see my glory, the glory you have given me.
You loved me before the world was made.
[25] Righteous Father, the world has not known you.
But I have known you.
And these people know that you sent me.
[26] I have made your name known to them, and I will keep making it known.
Then the love you have for me will be in them,
and I will be in them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: Jesus also prays for all later Christians, up to today.
> Verse 21: “That they will all be one” is the guiding verse of the ecumenical movement, the movement for the unity of Christians. The division of the churches into Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant and many others goes against this prayer. Today many churches work together and pray with one another.
> Unity does not mean that everyone has to be the same. It is about being connected in love.
> Verse 23: The world is to recognize from the unity and love of Christians that God loves it.
> Verse 26: The whole prayer ends with love: God’s love is to live in people.
> For today: Christians of different churches can pray together and do good together.
