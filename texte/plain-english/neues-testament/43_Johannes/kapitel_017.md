# John – Chapter 17
### Jesus prays for his own

---

## Jesus prays for himself (Verses 1–5)

[1] Jesus said these things.
Then he lifted up his eyes to heaven
and said,
“Father, the time has come.
Glorify your Son,
so that your Son may also glorify you.
[2] For you gave him authority over all flesh,
so that he will give eternal life
to all
whom you have given him.
[3] This is eternal life:
that they should know you,
the only true God,
and him whom you sent,
Jesus Christ.
[4] I glorified you on the earth.
I have finished the work
which you have given me to do.
[5] Now, Father,
glorify me with yourself
with the glory
which I had with you
before the world existed.”

> **What does this mean?**
> John 17 is the longest prayer of Jesus in the Bible. It is often called the “High Priestly Prayer”: Jesus stands up for his own before God, like a priest.
> Verse 1: “The time” is the hour of his death and his glorification.
> Verse 2: “All flesh” is a biblical expression for all people.
> Verse 3: In John, “eternal life” is not only a life after death. It already begins now: knowing God and being connected with him.
> “The only true God”: Jesus confesses the one God of Israel. This connects Jews and Christians (Deuteronomy 6:4).

## Jesus prays for his disciples (Verses 6–19)

[6] “I revealed your name to the people
whom you have given me out of the world.
They were yours,
and you have given them to me.
And they have kept your word.
[7] Now they have known
that all things
that you have given me
are from you.
[8] For the words
which you have given me
I have given to them.
They received them
and knew for sure
that I came from you.
And they have believed
that you sent me.
[9] I pray for them.
I don’t pray for the world,
but for those
whom you have given me,
for they are yours.
[10] All things that are mine
are yours,
and yours are mine.
And I am glorified in them.
[11] I am no more in the world,
but these are in the world,
and I am coming to you.
Holy Father,
keep them through your name
which you have given me,
so that they may be one,
even as we are.
[12] While I was with them in the world,
I kept them in your name.
I have guarded those
whom you have given me.
None of them is lost
except the son of destruction,
so that the Scripture might be fulfilled.
[13] But now I come to you.
And I say these things in the world,
so that they may have my joy made full in themselves.
[14] I have given them your word.
And the world hated them,
because they are not of the world,
even as I am not of the world.
[15] I don’t pray
that you would take them from the world,
but that you would keep them from the evil one.
[16] They are not of the world,
even as I am not of the world.
[17] Sanctify them in your truth.
Your word is truth.
[18] As you sent me into the world,
even so I have sent them into the world.
[19] For their sakes I sanctify myself,
so that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth.”

*sanctify* = make holy, set apart for God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “I don’t pray for the world”: Here Jesus prays especially for his disciples. That does not mean that he doesn’t care about the world. God loves the world (3:16), and the disciples are sent into the world (verse 18).
> Verse 11: “That they may be one”: Jesus prays for the unity of his disciples.
> Verse 12: “The son of destruction” means Judas.
> Verse 15: Christians are not to flee from the world, but to live in the world, protected from evil.
> Verse 17: “Sanctify” means: to be set apart for God, to belong to God.
> For today: Jesus prays for his friends. We may also pray for one another.

## Jesus prays for all who will believe (Verses 20–26)

[20] “I don’t pray for these only,
but also for all
who will believe in me through their word:
[21] that they may all be one.
Even as you, Father, are in me,
and I in you,
so they also may be one in us,
so that the world may believe
that you sent me.
[22] The glory
which you have given me,
I have given to them,
so that they may be one,
even as we are one:
[23] I in them,
and you in me,
so that they may be made perfectly one,
and the world may know
that you sent me
and loved them,
even as you loved me.
[24] Father, I desire
that they also whom you have given me
be with me
where I am.
They are to see my glory
which you have given me.
For you loved me
before the foundation of the world.
[25] Righteous Father,
the world hasn’t known you.
But I knew you,
and these knew
that you sent me.
[26] I made your name known to them,
and I will make it known,
so that the love
with which you loved me
may be in them,
and I in them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: Jesus also prays for all later Christians, up to today.
> Verse 21: “That they may all be one” is the guiding verse of the ecumenical movement, the movement for the unity of Christians. The division of the churches into Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant and many others goes against this prayer. Today many churches work together and pray with one another.
> Unity does not mean that everyone has to be the same. It is about being connected in love.
> Verse 23: The world is to recognize from the unity and love of Christians that God loves it.
> Verse 26: The whole prayer ends with love: God’s love is to live in people.
> For today: Christians of different churches can pray together and do good together.
