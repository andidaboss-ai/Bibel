# John – Chapter 21
### “Do you love me?”

---

## The great catch of fish (Verses 1–14)

[1] After these things,
Jesus showed himself again to the disciples
at the Sea of Tiberias.
It happened this way.
[2] Simon Peter,
Thomas called Didymus,
Nathanael of Cana in Galilee,
the sons of Zebedee,
and two others of his disciples
were together.
[3] Simon Peter said to them,
“I’m going fishing.”
They told him,
“We are also coming with you.”
They immediately went out
and got into the boat.
That night, they caught nothing.
[4] When morning had already come,
Jesus stood on the beach.
But the disciples didn’t know
that it was Jesus.
[5] Jesus said to them,
“Children, have you anything to eat?”
They answered him,
“No.”
[6] He said to them,
“Cast the net on the right side of the boat,
and you will find some.”
So they cast it.
And now they weren’t able to pull it in,
because there were so many fish.
[7] Then the disciple
whom Jesus loved
said to Peter,
“It’s the Lord!”
When Simon Peter heard
that it was the Lord,
he wrapped his coat around himself,
for he was naked,
and threw himself into the sea.
[8] The other disciples came in the little boat,
dragging the net full of fish.
For they were not far from the land,
only about two hundred cubits away.
[9] When they got out on the land,
they saw a fire of coals there,
with fish and bread laid on it.
[10] Jesus said to them,
“Bring some of the fish
which you have just caught.”
[11] Simon Peter went up
and pulled the net to land.
It was full of great fish,
one hundred fifty-three.
Even though there were so many,
the net wasn’t torn.
[12] Jesus said to them,
“Come and eat breakfast!”
None of the disciples dared
to ask him,
“Who are you?”
For they knew
that it was the Lord.
[13] Then Jesus came,
took the bread,
and gave it to them,
and the fish likewise.
[14] This was now the third time
that Jesus showed himself to his disciples
after he had risen from the dead.

*cubit* = an old measure of length, about 18 inches (45 centimeters)

> **What does this mean?**
> The Sea of Tiberias is the Sea of Galilee. The disciples are back at home and working as fishermen.
> Verse 3: Without Jesus they catch nothing. This reminds us of Luke 5:1–11.
> Verse 7: While working, Peter was probably wearing very little. Out of respect he puts on his coat before he goes to Jesus.
> Verse 8: Two hundred cubits are about 100 yards (90 meters).
> Verse 9: Again a fire of coals. At a fire of coals Peter had denied Jesus (18:18).
> Verse 11: It is not certain what the number 153 means. Some scholars think: That is how many kinds of fish people knew at that time. Then it would be a picture for all peoples. The net doesn’t tear: The church has room for everyone.
> Verse 13: Jesus shares bread and fish, as at the feeding of the 5,000 (6:11).
> For today: Jesus meets people in the middle of everyday life, at work and at meals.

## Jesus and Peter (Verses 15–19)

[15] When they had eaten their breakfast,
Jesus said to Simon Peter,
“Simon, son of Jonah,
do you love me more than these?”
He said to him,
“Yes, Lord;
you know
that I have affection for you.”
Jesus said to him,
“Feed my lambs.”
[16] He said to him again a second time,
“Simon, son of Jonah,
do you love me?”
He said to him,
“Yes, Lord;
you know
that I have affection for you.”
He said to him,
“Tend my sheep.”
[17] He said to him the third time,
“Simon, son of Jonah,
do you have affection for me?”
Peter was grieved,
because he asked him the third time,
“Do you have affection for me?”
He said to him,
“Lord, you know everything.
You know
that I have affection for you.”
Jesus said to him,
“Feed my sheep.
[18] Most certainly I tell you,
when you were young,
you dressed yourself
and walked
where you wanted to.
But when you are old,
you will stretch out your hands.
Another will dress you
and carry you
where you don’t want to go.”
[19] He said this
to show
by what kind of death Peter would glorify God.
When he had said this,
he said to him,
“Follow me.”

*tend* = take care of

> **What does this mean?**
> Peter had denied Jesus three times (18:17, 25–27). Now Jesus asks him three times whether he loves him. Three times Peter may say his love. In this way he is set back on his feet.
> In Greek there are two different words for “love” here. The WEB tells them apart: “love” and “have affection.” The third time, Jesus takes up Peter’s word. Some scholars see an important difference in this; others think the words mean almost the same.
> “Feed my sheep”: Peter is to take care of the church like a shepherd. The Catholic Church sees here a basis for the office of the pope. Other churches understand it as a task given to Peter and to all who have responsibility in the church.
> Verses 18–19: Jesus hints at Peter’s death. According to old tradition Peter died in Rome as a martyr, probably under the emperor Nero.
> For today: After failure a new beginning is possible. Jesus gives up on no one.

## Jesus and the beloved disciple (Verses 20–25)

[20] Then Peter turned around
and saw the disciple following
whom Jesus loved.
He was the one
who had also leaned on Jesus’ chest at the supper
and asked,
“Lord, who is going to betray you?”
[21] When Peter saw him,
he said to Jesus,
“Lord, what about this man?”
[22] Jesus said to him,
“If I desire
that he stay
until I come,
what is that to you?
You follow me.”
[23] So this saying went out among the brothers
that this disciple wouldn’t die.
Yet Jesus didn’t say to him
that he wouldn’t die,
but,
“If I desire
that he stay
until I come,
what is that to you?”
[24] This is the disciple
who testifies about these things
and wrote these things.
We know
that his witness is true.
[25] There are also many other things
which Jesus did.
If they were all written down one by one,
I suppose
that even the world itself wouldn’t have room
for the books
that would be written.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–22: Peter asks about the other disciple’s path. Jesus says: Everyone has his own path. Don’t compare yourself. Follow me.
> Verse 23: John corrects a misunderstanding. Jesus had not promised that the disciple would not die.
> Verse 24: “We know”: Here others are probably speaking, perhaps students of the beloved disciple. They testify that his report is true.
> Verse 25: A beautiful closing word: Jesus is greater than all the books in the world.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Gospel of John?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A different Gospel:** John tells his story differently from Matthew, Mark and Luke. He doesn’t begin with the birth, but with eternity: “In the beginning was the Word.” And: “The Word was God” (1:1). “The Word became flesh” (1:14). That means: The Word became a human being.
> **The signs:** John tells of great deeds of Jesus and calls them “signs”: water becomes wine in Cana, healings, the feeding of the 5,000, Jesus walks on the water, the man born blind sees, Lazarus is raised. Many scholars count seven such signs. The signs show who Jesus is.
> **The “I am” sayings:** “I am the bread of life,” “I am the light of the world,” “I am the door,” “I am the good shepherd,” “I am the resurrection and the life,” “I am the way, the truth, and the life,” “I am the true vine.”
> **God’s love:** “God loved the world very much” (3:16). Jesus washes his disciples’ feet and gives the new commandment: to love one another as he has loved us (13:34).
> **The Holy Spirit:** Jesus promises the Counselor, the Spirit of truth. He remains with the disciples.
> **Cross and Easter:** On the cross Jesus says: “It is finished!” Mary Magdalene is the first to meet the risen Jesus. Thomas confesses: “My Lord and my God!”
> **An important note:** John often writes “the Jews.” By this he mostly means the leaders in Jerusalem who rejected Jesus. Jesus, his mother, all the disciples and the first Christians were Jews. “Salvation is from the Jews” (4:22). These texts were often misused to hate Jews. That was a grave guilt. No text of this Gospel may be used against Jews.
> **For today:** John wrote this “so that you may believe” and so that believing you may have life (20:31). Whoever doubts like Thomas or fails like Peter is invited too.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the Acts of the Apostles. It was written by Luke and continues his Gospel. It tells how the Holy Spirit comes at Pentecost, and how the good news is carried from Jerusalem all the way to Rome, by Peter, Paul and many others.
