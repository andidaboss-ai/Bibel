# Ephesians – Chapter 1
### Blessed in Christ

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Ephesians

Ephesus was a large port city in what is now Turkey. A famous temple of the goddess Artemis stood there. Paul lived and worked in Ephesus for several years (Acts 19).
The letter calls itself a letter from Paul. Many scholars today think that it was written by a student of Paul, in Paul’s spirit. Others consider Paul himself to be the author. The letter was probably written in prison (3:1; 4:1). Some old manuscripts don’t have the words *at Ephesus* in verse 1. Perhaps the letter was meant for several churches.
The great theme is the one church: Jews and people from all nations have grown together into one people through Christ. Christ has overcome the separation between them.
The second part (chapters 4 to 6) is about life as a Christian in everyday life.
**An important note:** In chapters 5 and 6 there are rules for married couples, children, parents, slaves and masters. They reflect the order of society at that time. In the boxes we explain how Christians understand them today.
The letter to the Ephesians has 6 chapters.

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God,
writes to the saints who are at Ephesus,
the faithful in Christ Jesus.
[2] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Saints” are all Christians. They belong to God.

## Praise to God (Verses 3–14)

[3] Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.
He has blessed us in Christ
with every spiritual blessing
in the heavenly places.
[4] For he chose us in him
before the foundation of the world.
We were to be holy and without fault before him,
in love.
[5] He decided beforehand
that we would become his children
through Jesus Christ.
That is what he wanted,
because it gave him joy.
[6] This happened to the praise of his glorious grace.
He freely gave us this grace
in the Beloved.
[7] In him we have redemption through his blood,
the forgiveness of our wrongs,
according to the riches of his grace.
[8] He gave us this grace in rich measure,
with all wisdom and understanding.
[9] He made known to us the mystery of his will.
That is how he decided it in Christ,
because it pleased him.
[10] He wanted to carry it out
when the times are fulfilled:
All things are to be brought together in Christ,
the things in the heavens and the things on the earth,
in him.
[11] In him we have also received an inheritance.
We were chosen for it beforehand
according to the plan of him
who does all things according to the purpose of his will.
[12] We are to be to the praise of his glory,
we
who had hoped in Christ before.
[13] In him you also,
when you heard the word of the truth,
the Good News of your salvation,
and when you believed,
were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit.
[14] He is a pledge of our inheritance,
until God fully sets free his own possession,
to the praise of his glory.

*pledge* = something given as a guarantee that a promise will be kept

> **What does this mean?**
> In Greek, verses 3–14 are one single very long sentence, a great song of praise to God. We have divided it into short sentences.
> Verses 4–5: “Chose” and “decided beforehand”: God’s love for us was already there before we lived. Christians have thought much about these words and thought differently about them. Here they are mainly meant to comfort: God wanted us.
> Verse 10: God wants to bring together again in Christ everything that is broken and separated.
> Verses 12–13: “We who had hoped in Christ before” probably means the Christians of Jewish background. “You also” means the Christians from the nations. Both belong together.
> Verses 13–14: The Holy Spirit is like a seal and like a down payment: He shows that we belong to God, and he guarantees that God will keep his whole promise.

## Prayer for the church (Verses 15–23)

[15] That is why I too,
since I heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus
and of your love for all the saints,
[16] have not stopped
giving thanks for you.
I remember you in my prayers.
[17] I pray
that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ,
the Father of glory,
may give you a spirit of wisdom and revelation,
so that you know him.
[18] May he enlighten the eyes of your hearts,
so that you know
what the hope of his calling is,
how rich the glory of his inheritance among the saints is,
[19] and how surpassingly great his power is toward us
who believe.
This power works with the strength of his might.
[20] With this power he worked in Christ
when he raised him from the dead
and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places,
[21] far above all rule,
authority,
power
and dominion,
and above every name
that is named,
not only in this age,
but also in the age to come.
[22] He put all things under his feet.
And he gave him to the assembly as head over all things.
[23] The assembly is his body,
the fullness of him
who fills all in all.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: “The eyes of your hearts”: There are things you don’t see with your eyes, but only recognize with your heart.
> Verse 20: “At his right hand” is a picture: Christ has the highest honor and power (compare Psalm 110:1).
> Verse 21: Christ stands above all powers that make people afraid.
> Verse 22 takes up Psalm 8:6 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 8:7).
> Verses 22–23: Christ is the head, the church is his body. It cannot live without him.
