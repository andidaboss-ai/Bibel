# Ephesians – Chapter 5
### Living as children of light

---

## Live in love (Verses 1–7)

[1] So follow God’s example,
as his beloved children.
[2] Walk in love,
just as Christ also loved us.
He gave himself up for us,
as an offering and a sacrifice to God,
like a sweet-smelling fragrance.
[3] But sexual immorality,
any kind of uncleanness
or greed
should not even be mentioned among you.
That is fitting for saints.
[4] Also no shameless behavior,
no foolish talk
and no crude jokes.
They are not fitting.
Rather give thanks.
[5] For know this for sure:
No sexually immoral person,
no unclean person
and no greedy person
– greed is idolatry –
has an inheritance in the Kingdom of Christ and God.
[6] Let no one deceive you with empty words.
For because of these things the wrath of God comes
on the children of disobedience.
[7] So don’t take part with them.

*idolatry* = worshiping idols, false gods

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Christians are to imitate God, just as children imitate their parents. The most important example is the love of Jesus.
> Verse 5: “Greed is idolatry”: Whoever always wants more makes money and possessions into their god.
> Verse 4: “Crude jokes” means jokes that put others down or hurt them.

---

## Children of light (Verses 8–14)

[8] For you were once darkness.
But now you are light in the Lord.
Walk as children of light.
[9] For the fruit of the Spirit shows itself
in all goodness,
righteousness
and truth.
[10] Test
what pleases the Lord.
[11] Have no fellowship with the deeds of darkness,
which bear no fruit.
But rather expose them.
[12] For what these people do in secret,
it is a shame even to speak of it.
[13] But everything
that is exposed
is made visible by the light.
For everything
that is made visible
is light.
[14] That is why it says:
“Awake,
you who sleep,
and arise from the dead,
and Christ will shine on you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Not only: *You have light*, but: *You are light.* Christians are to make things bright through their lives.
> Verse 9: Here it says “the fruit of the Spirit,” as in the WEB. Some old manuscripts say *the fruit of the light*.
> Verses 11–13: Wrong should not be kept quiet, but exposed.
> For today: That is also true for wrongdoing and abuse in churches and families. Looking away only helps the abusers. If you experience abuse or know about it, you can get help. In the US, call the RAINN hotline at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, call NAPAC at 0808 801 0331. If a child is in danger, in the US call Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453; in the UK call the NSPCC at 0808 800 5000.
> Verse 14 is perhaps a song of the first Christians that was sung at baptism.

## Live wisely (Verses 15–21)

[15] So watch carefully
how you live:
not as unwise,
but as wise.
[16] Make good use of the time,
because the days are evil.
[17] So don’t be foolish,
but understand
what the will of the Lord is.
[18] Don’t get drunk with wine,
for that leads to a wild life.
But be filled with the Spirit.
[19] Speak to one another in psalms,
hymns
and spiritual songs.
Sing and make music to the Lord in your heart.
[20] Always give thanks to God the Father
for everything
in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[21] Subject yourselves to one another
in the fear of Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: “Make good use of the time”: Every day is a chance to do good.
> Verse 18: If you have problems with alcohol, you can find help at addiction counseling services. In the US, call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. In the UK, call Drinkline at 0300 123 1110. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 19: Singing and music have always been part of Christian worship.
> Verse 21 is very important: “Subject yourselves to one another.” This applies to all Christians toward each other. This verse is the heading for the following section about marriage.

---

## Husband and wife in marriage (Verses 22–33)

[22] Wives,
be subject to your own husbands,
as to the Lord.
[23] For the husband is the head of the wife,
just as Christ also is the head of the assembly.
He is the savior of the body.
[24] But as the assembly is subject to Christ,
so the wives are also to be subject to their husbands in everything.
[25] Husbands,
love your wives,
just as Christ also loved the assembly
and gave himself up for her.
[26] He wanted to make her holy
and cleansed her
by the washing of water with the word.
[27] He wanted to present the assembly to himself in glory,
without spot
or wrinkle
or any such thing,
but holy and without fault.
[28] In the same way husbands also ought to love their wives
as their own bodies.
He who loves his own wife
loves himself.
[29] For no one ever hated his own flesh,
but he nourishes and cherishes it,
just as the Lord also does the assembly.
[30] For we are members of his body,
of his flesh and bones.
[31] “For this cause a man will leave his father and mother
and will be joined to his wife.
Then the two will become one flesh.”
[32] This mystery is great.
But I speak about Christ and the assembly.
[33] Nevertheless each of you must also love his own wife
as himself.
And the wife should respect her husband.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 22–33 are called a “household code.” Such rules for living together in the family existed in many writings at that time. In the society of that time, the husband had authority over the wife.
> What is new and unusual: Everything stands under verse 21: “Subject yourselves to one another.” And much is asked of the husbands: They are to love their wives the way Christ loves the church. He gave his life for her. That is a love that serves, not one that rules.
> Verse 23: “Head” can mean *ruler over*, but also *source* or *the one who cares for* (compare verse 29). Interpreters understand it in different ways.
> Today many churches and Christians understand marriage as a partnership of two people with equal rights who love and respect each other. Other Christians hold on to a special responsibility of the husband. All stress: It is about love, not about power.
> Very important: These verses give no one the right to rule over their spouse, to oppress them or to use violence. Violence in marriage is a serious wrong and a crime. No one has to put up with it. You can get help. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. Men can also call the Men’s Advice Line at 0808 801 0327. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verse 31 quotes Genesis 2:24.
> Verse 32: Love in marriage is a picture of the love between Christ and his church.
