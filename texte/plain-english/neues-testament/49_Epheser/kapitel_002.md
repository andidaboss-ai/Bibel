# Ephesians – Chapter 2
### Saved by grace

---

## Made alive by God (Verses 1–10)

[1] You too were made alive
when you were dead in your wrongs and sins.
[2] You once lived in them,
according to the course of this world,
according to the will of the ruler
who rules over the power of the air.
That is the spirit
who now works in the children of disobedience.
[3] We all once lived among them too,
in the desires of our flesh.
We did
what the flesh and the mind wanted.
And we were by nature children of anger,
like the rest.
[4] But God is rich in mercy.
He loved us with his great love.
[5] Even when we were dead through our wrongs,
he made us alive together with Christ.
By grace you have been saved!
[6] He raised us up with him
and seated us with him in the heavenly places,
in Christ Jesus.
[7] In this way he wanted to show in the ages to come
how overflowing the riches of his grace are,
in his kindness toward us in Christ Jesus.
[8] For by grace you have been saved
through faith.
And that is not from yourselves.
It is the gift of God.
[9] It is not from works,
so that no one can boast.
[10] For we are his workmanship,
created in Christ Jesus
for good works.
God prepared the good works beforehand,
so that we would walk in them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Without God a person is as if dead, even while living. This is true for everyone, for Jews and for people from the nations (“we all,” verse 3).
> Verse 2: The ruler “who rules over the power of the air” is the power of evil, Satan. At that time people imagined that evil powers ruled in the air between heaven and earth.
> Verse 3: “Children of anger” is a Hebrew way of speaking. It means: people who would have deserved God’s judgment.
> Verses 8–9 are among the most important verses about grace: Salvation is a gift from God. No one can earn it.
> Verse 10: Good works are not the way to salvation, but its fruit. Whoever has received God’s gift does good.

## Christ breaks down the dividing wall (Verses 11–22)

[11] So remember:
You were once people from the nations
by birth.
You were called “uncircumcision”
by those
who are called “circumcision,”
with a circumcision in the body,
made by human hands.
[12] At that time you were without Christ.
You were shut out from the commonwealth of Israel.
You were strangers to the covenants of the promise.
You had no hope
and were without God in the world.
[13] But now in Christ Jesus
you
who once were far off
have been brought near by the blood of Christ.
[14] For he is our peace.
He made both one.
He broke down the dividing wall
that stood between them.
[15] In his body he did away with the hostility,
the law of commandments contained in ordinances.
In this way he wanted to create in himself one new man of the two
and make peace.
[16] And he wanted to reconcile both to God in one body
through the cross.
Through the cross he killed the hostility.
[17] He came and preached peace
to you
who were far off
and to those
who were near.
[18] For through him we both have access
in one Spirit to the Father.
[19] So you are no longer strangers and foreigners without rights,
but you are fellow citizens with the saints
and of the household of God.
[20] You are built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets.
And the cornerstone is Christ Jesus himself.
[21] In him the whole building is fitted together
and grows into a holy temple in the Lord.
[22] In him you also are built in
to become a home where God lives in the Spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Commonwealth means: a community of citizens who belong together.

> Verse 12: The letter reminds the Christians from the nations: Israel had God’s covenants and promises. You were once shut out from them. Israel is the starting point.
> Verse 13: “Far off” means the people from the nations. Through Christ they have been brought near, near to God and near to Israel.
> Verse 14: “The dividing wall”: In the temple in Jerusalem there was a wall. Non-Jews were not allowed to go further in. The letter says: In Christ this separation is overcome.
> Verse 15: “The law of commandments contained in ordinances” probably means mainly the rules that separated Jews and non-Jews from each other. Interpreters understand this verse in different ways.
> Important: The letter does not say that Israel is replaced. It says: The people from the nations are added to Israel. They become “fellow citizens” (verse 19). Jews and Christians from the nations are to live together in peace.
> Verse 17 takes up Isaiah 57:19: *Peace, peace, to him who is far off and to him who is near.*
> Verse 20: The cornerstone was the most important stone of a building. Everything was lined up by it.
> For today: Christ overcomes hostility between people and nations. Christians should tear down walls, not build new ones.
