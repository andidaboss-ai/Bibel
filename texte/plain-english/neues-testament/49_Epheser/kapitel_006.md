# Ephesians – Chapter 6
### Strong in the power of the Lord

---

## Children and parents (Verses 1–4)

[1] Children,
obey your parents in the Lord.
For this is right.
[2] “Honor your father and mother.”
That is the first commandment with a promise:
[3] “that it may be well with you,
and you may live long on the earth.”
[4] And you fathers,
don’t provoke your children to anger,
but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–3 quote the commandment from Exodus 20:12 and Deuteronomy 5:16.
> Verse 4: For that time this was new: Fathers also have duties. They must not provoke their children, treat them unfairly or discourage them.
> Very important: “Obey your parents in the Lord” does not mean that children have to do everything their parents demand. No child has to put up with violence, abuse or humiliation. In many countries children have a legal right to be raised without violence. Children and young people can get help. In the US, call Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111. Parents who need support can also call Childhelp, or in the UK the NSPCC at 0808 800 5000.

## Slaves and masters (Verses 5–9)

[5] Servants,
obey your earthly masters
with fear and trembling,
with a sincere heart,
as to Christ.
[6] Don’t do it only
when eyes are on you,
to please people,
but as servants of Christ,
doing the will of God from the heart.
[7] Serve with good will,
as if you were serving the Lord
and not people.
[8] For you know:
Whatever good someone does,
he will receive it back from the Lord,
whether he is a slave or free.
[9] And you masters,
treat them the same way.
Stop threatening.
For you know:
You and they have the same Master in heaven.
And there is no partiality with him.

*partiality* = treating some people better than others

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: The WEB says *servants* here. The Greek word means slaves. At the time of the letter there were very many slaves. Christians, too, were slaves or owned slaves.
> Verse 9: What was new: The masters are also addressed. They must not threaten. Before God masters and slaves are equal.
> Very important: Slavery is a grave injustice. These verses were later misused to justify slavery. That was wrong. Many Christians fought against slavery. Today there is still human trafficking and forced labor. Christians should stand up against it. If you know about trafficking or forced labor, you can get help. In the US, call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888. In the UK, call the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.
> For today: These verses also don’t mean that employees have to put up with everything. You may speak up about exploitation and injustice at work and fight against them.

## The armor of God (Verses 10–20)

[10] Finally:
Be strong in the Lord
and in the strength of his might.
[11] Put on the whole armor of God,
so that you can stand against the tricks of the devil.
[12] For our struggle is not against flesh and blood,
but against the rulers,
against the powers,
against the world’s rulers of the darkness of this age,
against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places.
[13] So put on the whole armor of God,
so that you can resist on the evil day
and,
having done everything,
stand firm.
[14] So stand firm:
Put on the belt of truth.
Put on the breastplate of righteousness.
[15] Put on as shoes the readiness
to proclaim the Good News of peace.
[16] Above all, take up the shield of faith.
With it you can put out all the flaming arrows of the evil one.
[17] Also take the helmet of salvation
and the sword of the Spirit.
That is the word of God.
[18] Pray at all times in the Spirit
with all prayers and requests.
Stay alert and keep at it,
and pray for all the saints.
[19] Pray for me too,
that the right words may be given to me
when I open my mouth,
so that I boldly make known the mystery of the Good News.
[20] For it I am an ambassador in chains.
Pray
that I may speak boldly about it,
as I ought to speak.

*armor* = protective clothing for soldiers
*breastplate* = a piece of armor that protects the chest

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Not against flesh and blood”: Christians do not fight against people. They fight against evil, against lies, hatred and injustice.
> Verses 13–17: The letter uses the picture of a Roman soldier. But all the “weapons” are spiritual: truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation and God’s word. Almost all the parts are for protection.
> Verses 14–17 recall Isaiah 11:5 and 59:17, where God himself is *armed* with righteousness and salvation.
> Very important: This text is no call to violence or war. In history it was sometimes misused that way. That was wrong. The “shoes” are the readiness to proclaim peace (verse 15).
> Verses 18–20: Prayer is the most important strength. The author is in prison (“in chains”) and asks for prayer.

## Greetings and blessing (Verses 21–24)

[21] So that you also may know
how I am and what I am doing,
Tychicus will tell you everything.
He is a beloved brother
and a faithful servant in the Lord.
[22] I have sent him to you for this very purpose,
so that you may know
how we are,
and so that he may comfort your hearts.
[23] Peace be to the brothers,
and love with faith,
from God the Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.
[24] Grace be with all
who love our Lord Jesus Christ
with undying love.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Tychicus was a coworker of Paul (compare Acts 20:4 and Colossians 4:7). He probably delivered the letter.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Ephesians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **God’s great plan:** God wants to bring together in Christ everything that is separated (chapter 1).
> **Saved by grace:** No one can earn salvation. It is God’s gift (chapter 2).
> **The dividing wall is broken down:** People from all nations belong to God’s people together with Israel. They are fellow heirs, not a replacement for Israel (chapters 2 and 3).
> **God’s love:** It is wider, longer, higher and deeper than we can understand (chapter 3).
> **One body:** One Lord, one faith, one baptism. All Christians belong together (chapter 4).
> **A new life:** Speaking the truth, forgiving, being kind, living as children of light (chapters 4 and 5).
> **The household code:** Rules for marriage, family and work from that time. What matters is: Everyone is to serve one another. No one may use violence (chapters 5 and 6).
> **The armor of God:** Christians do not fight against people, but against evil, with truth, faith and peace (chapter 6).
> **For today:** Christ is our peace. Christians are to tear down walls between people.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter to the Philippians. Paul writes it from prison, and yet it is a letter full of joy.
