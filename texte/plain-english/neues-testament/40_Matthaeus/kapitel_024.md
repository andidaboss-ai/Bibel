# Matthew – Chapter 24
### The speech about the end times

---

## Jesus announces the destruction of the temple (Verses 1–2)

[1] Jesus went out of the temple
and was on his way.
Then his disciples came to him
to show him the buildings of the temple.
[2] But he answered them,
“You see all these things, don’t you?
Most certainly I tell you,
not one stone here will be left on another.
Everything will be torn down.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time the temple in Jerusalem was one of the largest and most beautiful buildings in the world. King Herod the Great had it splendidly enlarged. The disciples are proud and excited.
> Jesus announces: The temple will be destroyed. That happened in the year AD 70. The Romans conquered Jerusalem and burned the temple.
> For the Jewish people this was a great catastrophe. To this day Jews remember it on the day of mourning called Tisha B’Av. The Western Wall (the “Wailing Wall”) is a remnant of the old wall around the Temple Mount.
> Jesus is not happy about the destruction. He lamented over Jerusalem (23:37).

## The beginning of the birth pains (Verses 3–14)

[3] As he was sitting on the Mount of Olives,
the disciples came to him privately and asked,
“Tell us: When will these things happen?
And what is the sign of your coming
and of the end of the age?”
[4] Jesus answered them,
“Be careful that no one leads you astray!
[5] For many will come in my name
and say, ‘I am the Christ.’
And they will lead many astray.
[6] You will hear of wars
and rumors of wars.
See that you are not frightened!
For all this must happen,
but it is not yet the end.
[7] For nation will rise against nation,
and kingdom against kingdom.
And there will be famines, plagues, and earthquakes
in various places.
[8] But all these things are only the beginning of the birth pains.
[9] Then they will hand you over to be oppressed
and will kill you.
You will be hated by all nations
because you belong to me.
[10] Then many will fall away from the faith.
They will betray one another
and hate one another.
[11] Many false prophets will appear
and lead many astray.
[12] And because wickedness will increase,
the love of many will grow cold.
[13] But whoever endures to the end
will be saved.
[14] And this Good News of the Kingdom
will be preached in the whole world
as a testimony to all nations.
And then the end will come.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples ask about two things: When will the temple be destroyed? And when will the end of the world come? Jesus answers in a way that blends the two together. That makes this speech hard to understand.
> The first and most important message: “Be careful that no one leads you astray!” Jesus warns against people who claim to know exactly when the end will come, or who present themselves as the savior.
> Verses 6–8: There have been wars, famines, plagues and earthquakes at all times. Jesus says: Don’t be frightened! This is not yet the end. “Birth pains” are the pains when a baby is born. The picture says: After the pain, something new comes.
> Verses 9–13: Whoever believes in Jesus may face disadvantages and persecution because of it. That is still the case in some countries today. Jesus encourages: Stand firm.
> Important for today: Again and again groups have claimed that the end would come on a certain date. They frightened people and took advantage of them. Jesus warns against exactly that. Whoever is in a group that puts pressure on people with fear of the end of the world may get help, for example from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.

## The great distress (Verses 15–28)

[15] “So when you see the ‘abomination of desolation’
standing in the holy place,
which the prophet Daniel spoke about
(let the reader understand!),
[16] then those who are in Judea
should flee to the mountains.
[17] Whoever is on the housetop
should not go down
to take anything out of his house.
[18] Whoever is in the field
should not go back
to get his clothes.
[19] But woe to pregnant women
and to mothers who are nursing a baby
in those days!
[20] Pray that you won’t have to flee in the winter
or on a Sabbath.
[21] For then there will be great suffering,
such as there has not been from the beginning of the world until now,
and never will be again.
[22] And if those days were not cut short,
no one would be saved.
But for the sake of the chosen ones
those days will be cut short.
[23] Then if anyone says to you,
‘Look, here is the Christ!’ or, ‘There he is!’
don’t believe it.
[24] For false christs
and false prophets will appear.
They will do great signs and wonders
to lead astray, if possible, even the chosen ones.
[25] I have told you ahead of time.
[26] So if they say to you,
‘Look, he is in the wilderness,’
don’t go out there.
Or, ‘Look, he is in the inner rooms,’
don’t believe it.
[27] For as the lightning flashes in the east
and is seen all the way to the west,
so will the coming of the Son of Man be.
[28] For wherever the dead body is,
that is where the vultures gather.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The “abomination of desolation” is an expression from the book of Daniel (Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). There it means a desecration of the temple. In the year 167 BC the Greek king Antiochus IV had a foreign altar set up in the temple. Jesus announces: Something like this will happen again. Many interpreters think of the events around the year AD 70.
> Abomination: something disgusting and shameful. Desolation: destruction, leaving a place empty.
> “Let the reader understand”: This is a note to the readers of the Gospel.
> Verses 16–20: When the time comes, people should flee at once, without losing time. Jesus thinks especially of pregnant women and mothers with small children, for whom fleeing is especially hard. In winter the roads are hard to travel. On the Sabbath devout Jews did not want to travel long distances.
> Verse 22: God does not forget people in distress. He puts a limit on suffering.
> Verses 23–27: Jesus says: When he comes again, no one will be able to miss it. It is like lightning across the whole sky. You don’t have to search for him or run after people who say he is here or there.
> Verse 28 is a proverb. It probably means: When the time comes, it will be as clear as seeing vultures over a dead animal. Instead of “vultures,” it can also be translated “eagles.”

## The coming of the Son of Man (Verses 29–31)

[29] “But immediately after the suffering of those days,
the sun will be darkened,
and the moon will not give its light.
The stars will fall from the sky,
and the powers of the heavens will be shaken.
[30] Then the sign of the Son of Man
will appear in the sky.
Then all the tribes of the earth will mourn.
And they will see the Son of Man
coming on the clouds of the sky
with power and great glory.
[31] He will send out his angels
with a loud trumpet call.
And they will gather his chosen ones
from the four winds,
from one end of the sky to the other.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29 takes up pictures from the prophets (Isaiah 13:10; 34:4; Joel 2:10). These pictures describe how God steps in and the whole world changes. It is picture language, not an exact description of what will happen in the sky.
> Verse 30 reminds us of Daniel 7:13–14: One like a son of man comes with the clouds of the sky and receives eternal rule from God. Jesus says: That is me.
> We are not told what the “sign of the Son of Man” is.
> Verse 31: In ancient Israel the trumpet (a horn) called the people together. At the end God gathers everyone who belongs to him from all over the world.
> For today: Christians believe that Jesus will come again. That is a hope, not a threat. In the end it is not evil that wins, but God.

## The parable of the fig tree (Verses 32–35)

[32] “Learn this parable from the fig tree:
When its branches become tender
and put out leaves,
you know that summer is near.
[33] In the same way,
when you see all these things,
you know that he is near,
right at the door.
[34] Most certainly I tell you,
this generation will not pass away
until all these things have happened.
[35] Heaven and earth will pass away,
but my words will not pass away.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Many trees in Israel, for example the olive tree, keep their leaves in winter. The fig tree loses them. When it gets new leaves, everyone knows: Summer will soon be here.
> Verse 34 is hard. “This generation” probably means the people who were alive at that time. Many interpreters say: This refers to the destruction of Jerusalem, which came about 40 years later. Others understand “generation” differently. There are different interpretations.
> Verse 35: Everything in the world passes away. But you can rely on Jesus’ words.

## No one knows the day or the hour (Verses 36–44)

[36] “But no one knows that day and that hour,
not even the angels of heaven,
but only my Father.
[37] As it was in the days of Noah,
so it will be when the Son of Man comes.
[38] For in the days before the flood
people were eating and drinking,
marrying and giving in marriage,
until the day Noah went into the ship.
[39] And they didn’t notice anything
until the flood came
and swept them all away.
That is how it will be when the Son of Man comes.
[40] Then two men will be in the field:
One will be taken,
and one will be left.
[41] Two women will be grinding grain at the mill:
One will be taken,
and one will be left.
[42] So keep watch!
For you don’t know
on what day your Lord is coming.
[43] But know this:
If the owner of the house had known
in which watch of the night the thief was coming,
he would have stayed awake
and would not have let his house be broken into.
[44] So you also must be ready!
For the Son of Man is coming at an hour
when you don’t expect him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 36 is very important: No one knows the day. Not the angels, not any human being, only God. That is why every calculation of a date for the end of the world is wrong.
> In verse 36 many old manuscripts also say that not even the Son knows it (compare Mark 13:32). In Mark these words are certainly there. The WEB does not have them here.
> Verses 37–39: The people in Noah’s time lived their everyday lives and did not reckon with God (Genesis 6–7). Eating, drinking and marrying are not bad. But you should not forget God while doing them.
> Verses 40–41: Life goes on quite normally, and suddenly the time has come. Some groups teach from this a “rapture,” in which believers suddenly disappear. That is one particular interpretation. Many churches do not share it.
> Verse 41: At that time grinding grain was women’s work. Jesus names men and women equally.
> Verses 42–44: “Keeping watch” does not mean being anxious. It means: living every day in such a way that you could meet God. Doing good, being honest, loving others.

## The faithful servant and the evil servant (Verses 45–51)

[45] “Who then is the faithful and wise servant?
His master has put him in charge of the people in his household,
to give them their food at the right time.
[46] Blessed is that servant
if his master comes
and finds him doing this work.
[47] Most certainly I tell you,
he will put him in charge of everything he owns.
[48] But if that evil servant says in his heart,
‘My master is taking a long time to come,’
[49] and begins to beat his fellow servants
and to eat and drink with the drunkards,
[50] then the master of that servant will come
on a day when he doesn’t expect it,
and at an hour he doesn’t know.
[51] He will cut him to pieces
and give him his place with the hypocrites.
There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A servant is given responsibility for others. The faithful servant takes good care of the people entrusted to him.
> The evil servant thinks: My master won’t come so soon anyway. He misuses his power, beats others and lives without caring about anyone.
> The message: Whoever has responsibility for others, whether in the family, at work or in the church, should use it well and faithfully. Violence and the abuse of power are against God’s will.
> Verse 51: “Cut him to pieces” is a harsh picture from the parable. It shows how seriously God takes the abuse of power. It is not an instruction to use violence. The word can also mean: punish severely, or cut off from himself.
> For today: Whoever is beaten or oppressed by someone who has power over them may get help: In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
