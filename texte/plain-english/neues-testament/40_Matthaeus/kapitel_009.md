# Matthew – Chapter 9
### Jesus forgives, calls and heals

---

## A paralyzed man is healed (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jesus got into a boat,
crossed over
and came into his own town.
[2] Some people brought to him a man who was paralyzed,
lying on a mat.
When Jesus saw their faith,
he said to the paralyzed man,
“Cheer up, son!
Your sins are forgiven.”
[3] Some of the scribes said to themselves,
“This man is blaspheming.”
[4] Jesus knew their thoughts and said,
“Why do you think evil in your hearts?
[5] Which is easier, to say,
‘Your sins are forgiven,’
or to say,
‘Get up and walk’?
[6] But so that you may know
that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins—”
then he said to the paralyzed man,
“Get up, take your mat,
and go home.”
[7] And he got up
and went home.
[8] When the crowds saw this,
they were amazed and praised God,
who had given such authority to people.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “His own town” is Capernaum, where Jesus lived (4:13).
> Verse 2: Friends bring the paralyzed man to Jesus. Jesus sees “their faith,” that is, the faith of the friends too. Sometimes others carry us when we cannot do it ourselves.
> First Jesus says: “Your sins are forgiven.” He is not saying that the illness was a punishment for sins. He shows: He wants to make the whole person well, body and soul.
> Verse 3: The scribes are outraged. According to Jewish faith, only God can forgive sins. When a human being says that, it is blasphemy, unless he really has God’s authority.
> To blaspheme: to speak against God or to dishonor God.
> Verses 5–7: Jesus shows his authority: The paralyzed man gets up and goes home.

## Jesus calls Matthew (Verses 9–13)

[9] As Jesus went on from there,
he saw a man called Matthew sitting at the tax office.
He said to him,
“Follow me!”
And he got up and followed him.
[10] As Jesus was sitting at the table in the house,
many tax collectors and sinners came
and sat at the table with Jesus and his disciples.
[11] When the Pharisees saw this,
they said to his disciples,
“Why does your teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?”
[12] When Jesus heard it, he said,
“Those who are healthy don’t need a doctor,
but those who are sick do.
[13] But go and learn what this means:
‘I desire mercy, and not sacrifice.’
For I came not to call the righteous,
but sinners to repentance.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Tax collectors collected taxes and fees for the rulers and often kept more than they were owed. People saw them as cheats and traitors. Of all people, Jesus calls someone like that. According to tradition, this Matthew is the author of this Gospel.
> Verses 10–11: Jesus eats with people who were shut out as “sinners.” At that time, eating together was a sign of fellowship and acceptance. For devout people this was offensive.
> Verse 12: “Those who are healthy don’t need a doctor, but those who are sick do.” Jesus is there for those who need him.
> Verse 13: Jesus says: “I desire mercy, and not sacrifice.” This is a quote from Hosea 6:6. Mercy is more important to God than religious rituals.
> For today: Jesus shuts no one out. No one is too “bad” for God.

## The question about fasting (Verses 14–17)

[14] Then John’s disciples came to him and said,
“Why do we and the Pharisees fast often,
but your disciples don’t fast?”
[15] Jesus said to them,
“Can the friends of the bridegroom mourn
as long as the bridegroom is with them?
But the days will come
when the bridegroom will be taken away from them,
and then they will fast.
[16] No one puts a piece of new, unshrunk cloth on an old garment.
For the patch would tear away from the garment,
and the hole would get worse.
[17] People also don’t put new wine into old wineskins.
Otherwise the skins burst,
the wine is spilled,
and the skins are ruined.
No, they put new wine into fresh wineskins,
and both are kept safe.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: Jesus compares his time to a wedding. At a wedding no one fasts; people celebrate. But he hints that he will be “taken away,” a pointer to his death. Then his disciples will fast too.
> Verses 16–17: Two pictures from everyday life. New cloth shrinks when it is washed and tears old cloth apart. New wine is still fermenting and expands. Old, stiff leather wineskins would burst. The new thing that comes with Jesus needs new forms.
> Wineskins: bags made of animal skin, used to hold wine.

## A girl comes back to life, a woman is healed (Verses 18–26)

[18] While he was saying these things to them,
a ruler came, knelt down in front of him and said,
“My daughter has just died.
But come and lay your hand on her,
and she will live.”
[19] Jesus got up and followed him,
and so did his disciples.
[20] A woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years
came up behind him
and touched the fringe of his garment.
[21] For she said to herself,
“If I just touch his garment,
I will be made well.”
[22] But Jesus turned around,
saw her and said,
“Cheer up, daughter!
Your faith has made you well.”
And the woman was well from that hour on.
[23] When Jesus came into the ruler’s house
and saw the flute players and the noisy crowd,
[24] he said to them,
“Go away! The girl isn’t dead,
but sleeping.”
They laughed at him.
[25] But when the crowd had been sent out,
he went in,
took the girl by the hand,
and the girl got up.
[26] And the news about this spread through all that land.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A “ruler,” probably the leader of a synagogue, comes to Jesus. Mark and Luke give his name: Jairus (Mark 5:22). His daughter has just died, but he trusts Jesus.
> Verse 20: The woman had suffered from bleeding for twelve years. According to the law, this made her unclean (Leviticus 15:25). She was probably shut out and lonely. That is why she only dares to come up secretly from behind. The “fringe” was a tassel on the corner of the garment that devout Jews wore (Numbers 15:38).
> Verse 22: Jesus lovingly calls her “daughter.” She does not have to be ashamed. He heals her and gives her back her dignity.
> “Your faith has made you well”: This does not mean that people who do not get well have too little faith. Illness is not a punishment for a lack of faith.
> Verse 23: At that time flute players and women who mourned aloud were part of every funeral.
> Verse 24: “Sleeping”: For Jesus, death is not the end, but like a sleep from which he can wake people up.
> Verse 25: Jesus takes the girl by the hand, and she gets up. He is not afraid to touch a dead person, even though according to the law that made a person unclean.

## Two blind men and a man who could not speak (Verses 27–34)

[27] As Jesus went on from there,
two blind men followed him.
They called out and said,
“Have mercy on us, son of David!”
[28] When he had come into the house,
the blind men came to him.
Jesus said to them,
“Do you believe that I am able to do this?”
They said to him,
“Yes, Lord.”
[29] Then he touched their eyes and said,
“Let it be done to you according to your faith.”
[30] And their eyes were opened.
Jesus strictly warned them,
“See that no one finds out about this!”
[31] But they went out
and spread the news about him in all that land.
[32] As they were going out,
people brought to him a man who could not speak,
who was possessed by a demon.
[33] When the demon had been cast out,
the man who could not speak began to talk.
And the crowds were amazed and said,
“Nothing like this has ever been seen in Israel!”
[34] But the Pharisees said,
“He casts out demons by the prince of the demons.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: “Son of David” is a title for the Messiah. The blind men recognize who Jesus is, even though they cannot see anything.
> Verse 30: Jesus does not want the news of the healing to spread. Perhaps he did not want to be celebrated as a miracle healer or to raise false political hopes.
> Verses 33–34: The people are thrilled. But some Pharisees claim that Jesus is working together with the devil. At that time the Pharisees had different attitudes toward Jesus; some were friendly toward him later (compare John 3:1–2; Acts 5:34–39).
> Note: Being unable to speak is a disability or an illness, not the result of something evil. People with disabilities deserve respect and an equal place in the community.

## The harvest is great (Verses 35–38)

[35] Jesus went through all the towns and villages.
He taught in their synagogues,
preached the Good News of the Kingdom,
and healed every disease and every sickness.
[36] When he saw the crowds,
he was moved with compassion for them,
because they were worn out and scattered
like sheep without a shepherd.
[37] Then he said to his disciples,
“The harvest is great,
but the workers are few.
[38] So pray to the Lord of the harvest
that he will send out workers into his harvest.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 36: Jesus sees the people and is moved with compassion for them. The Greek word means a deep feeling of sympathy that goes all the way into your gut. The people are like sheep without a shepherd (compare Zechariah 10:2; Ezekiel 34:5).
> Verses 37–38: A picture from farming: The field is ripe, but there are too few helpers for the harvest. Many people are waiting for help and for the good news. Jesus asks his disciples to pray that God will send helpers. In the next chapter he then sends out the twelve apostles.
