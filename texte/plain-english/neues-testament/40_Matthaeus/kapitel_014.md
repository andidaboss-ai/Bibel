# Matthew – Chapter 14
### Bread for thousands and a walk on the water

---

## The death of John the Baptizer (Verses 1–12)

[1] At that time Herod the tetrarch heard
what people were saying about Jesus.
[2] He said to his servants,
“This is John the Baptizer.
He has risen from the dead.
That is why these powers are at work in him.”
[3] For Herod had arrested John.
He had tied him up and put him in prison.
He did this because of Herodias,
the wife of his brother Philip.
[4] For John had said to him,
“It is not lawful for you to have her.”
[5] Herod wanted to kill him.
But he was afraid of the people,
because they thought John was a prophet.
[6] Then Herod’s birthday came.
The daughter of Herodias danced in front of the guests,
and she pleased Herod.
[7] So he promised her with an oath
to give her whatever she would ask for.
[8] Her mother had told her what to say.
So she said,
“Give me here on a platter
the head of John the Baptizer.”
[9] The king was sad.
But because of his oaths
and because of the guests who were sitting at the table with him,
he ordered that it be given to her.
[10] He sent men
and had John beheaded in the prison.
[11] His head was brought on a platter
and given to the girl.
And she brought it to her mother.
[12] John’s disciples came,
took the body and buried it.
Then they went and told Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> This Herod is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great (from Chapter 2). He ruled over Galilee and Perea. The WEB calls him “tetrarch,” which means “ruler over a fourth part,” that is, a regional ruler. Even so, in verse 9 he is called “king,” as people at that time often called him.
> Herodias had been married before to a half-brother of Herod. John said: This marriage is not allowed by God’s law (Leviticus 18:16). For this brave criticism of a powerful man he had to go to prison.
> The daughter’s name is not given here. The Jewish historian Josephus names a daughter of Herodias “Salome.” That is why she became known by this name.
> Herod made a careless oath. He does not want to look weak in front of his guests. That is why he has an innocent man killed. Keeping a wrong promise does not make a wrong thing right (compare 5:33–37).
> Important: The story is no proof that women are “seductive” or “evil.” The main guilt lies with the powerful man who orders the murder.
> John is an example for people who speak the truth against injustice, even when it is dangerous.
> Verse 12: John’s disciples go to Jesus. In their grief they look for him.

## Jesus feeds more than 5,000 people (Verses 13–21)

[13] When Jesus heard this,
he went away from there in a boat
to a lonely place to be alone.
When the people heard about it,
they followed him on foot from the towns.
[14] Jesus got out and saw a large crowd.
He had compassion on them
and healed their sick.
[15] When evening came,
his disciples came to him and said,
“This is a lonely place,
and it is already late.
Send the people away,
so that they can go into the villages
and buy themselves something to eat.”
[16] But Jesus said to them,
“They don’t need to go away.
You give them something to eat!”
[17] They said to him,
“We only have five loaves of bread and two fish here.”
[18] He said,
“Bring them here to me.”
[19] He told the people
to sit down on the grass.
He took the five loaves and the two fish,
looked up to heaven and said the blessing.
He broke the loaves
and gave them to the disciples.
And the disciples gave them to the people.
[20] They all ate and were full.
They picked up the pieces that were left over:
twelve baskets full.
[21] Those who had eaten
were about five thousand men,
besides women and children.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the terrible news, Jesus wants to be alone. He is grieving for John. Even so, he does not send the people away. He has compassion on them.
> The disciples think in practical terms: Send them away; they should take care of themselves. Jesus says: “You give them something to eat!” He makes his disciples take responsibility.
> What looks like little becomes enough for everyone in Jesus’ hands. In the end there is even a lot left over.
> Jesus says the blessing, as was usual at Jewish meals: You thank God for the bread. The words “took, looked up, blessed, broke, gave” remind Christians of the Lord’s Supper (26:26).
> Twelve baskets: The number twelve reminds us of the twelve tribes of Israel and the twelve disciples.
> Verse 21: According to the custom of that time, only the men were counted. But Matthew says clearly: Women and children were there too, and they were also filled. So there were many more people.
> The miracle reminds us of God, who fed Israel in the wilderness with manna (Exodus 16), and of the prophet Elisha (2 Kings 4:42–44).
> For today: Many people still go hungry today. Jesus’ words “You give them something to eat” still apply.

## Jesus walks on the water (Verses 22–33)

[22] Right after this, Jesus made the disciples
get into the boat
and go ahead of him to the other side.
Meanwhile he wanted to send the people home.
[23] After he had sent the people away,
he went up the mountain by himself to pray.
When evening came, he was there alone.
[24] By now the boat was in the middle of the lake.
It was tossed about by the waves,
for the wind was against them.
[25] In the fourth watch of the night,
Jesus came to them.
He was walking on the lake.
[26] When the disciples saw him walking on the lake,
they were terrified and said,
“It’s a ghost!”
And they cried out in fear.
[27] But at once Jesus spoke to them:
“Cheer up! It is I!
Don’t be afraid!”
[28] Peter answered him,
“Lord, if it is you,
command me to come to you on the water.”
[29] He said,
“Come!”
Peter got out of the boat
and walked on the water
to come to Jesus.
[30] But when he noticed how strong the wind was,
he became afraid.
He began to sink
and cried out,
“Lord, save me!”
[31] At once Jesus stretched out his hand,
took hold of him and said to him,
“You of little faith, why did you doubt?”
[32] When they had climbed into the boat,
the wind stopped.
[33] Those who were in the boat
came and worshiped him.
They said,
“You are truly the Son of God!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Jesus takes time alone with God in prayer. He too needs rest and prayer.
> Verse 25: The “fourth watch of the night” is the time between about 3 and 6 in the morning. So the disciples have been fighting against the wind all night.
> “It is I” literally means “I am.” This reminds us of the name by which God introduced himself to Moses (Exodus 3:14). In the Old Testament it is God who walks on the sea (Job 9:8).
> Peter is brave: He dares to step out of the boat. When he looks at the wind instead of at Jesus, he becomes afraid and sinks. But he calls for help, and Jesus holds on to him at once.
> “You of little faith” is not a verdict Jesus gives to put someone down. He says it while he is already holding on to Peter. Doubt and fear are part of faith. What matters is: Peter calls out to Jesus.
> Verse 33: For the first time the disciples confess together: Jesus is God’s Son.
> For today: When you are about to “go under” in life, you may call out: “Lord, save me!” Whoever is in a crisis may also get help from people, for example from a crisis line: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Healings in Gennesaret (Verses 34–36)

[34] When they had crossed over,
they came to the land of Gennesaret.
[35] The people there recognized him.
They sent messengers into all the surrounding region
and brought all the sick to him.
[36] They begged him
that they might just touch the fringe of his garment.
And all who touched it were made well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gennesaret is a fertile plain on the west side of the Sea of Galilee, south of Capernaum.
> The “fringe” probably means the tassels on the garment. Jewish men wore them because God had commanded it (Numbers 15:38–39). They remind people of God’s commandments. Jesus also wore them as a devout Jew. Compare the woman in 9:20.
> The people trust Jesus so much that even a small touch is enough.
> For today: Whoever is sick may pray and look for help from doctors. Both belong together. Someone who does not get well does not have less faith because of it.
