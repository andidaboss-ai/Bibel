# Matthew – Chapter 27
### Jesus is crucified

---

## Jesus is handed over to Pilate (Verses 1–2)

[1] When morning had come,
all the chief priests and the elders of the people
made plans against Jesus
to have him put to death.
[2] They tied him up,
led him away,
and handed him over to Pontius Pilate, the governor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea from about AD 26 to 36. He was the highest representative of the Roman emperor in the land. Only he could have a death sentence carried out (compare John 18:31).
> Other sources from this time also tell about Pilate, for example the Jewish historian Josephus and the Roman historian Tacitus. They describe him as a harsh and cruel ruler.

## The end of Judas (Verses 3–10)

[3] Judas, who had betrayed him, saw
that Jesus had been condemned.
Then he felt sorry for what he had done.
He brought the 30 pieces of silver
back to the chief priests and elders
[4] and said,
“I have sinned.
I have betrayed innocent blood.”
But they said,
“What is that to us?
That is your problem.”
[5] Then he threw the pieces of silver down in the sanctuary,
left,
and went away and hanged himself.
[6] The chief priests took the pieces of silver and said,
“It is not lawful
to put them into the treasury,
because it is the price of blood.”
[7] They talked it over
and with the money bought the potter’s field
as a place to bury strangers.
[8] That is why that field has been called
“The Field of Blood” to this day.
[9] Then what was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was fulfilled:
“They took the 30 pieces of silver,
the price of the one on whom a price had been set,
whom some of the people of Israel priced,
[10] and they gave them for the potter’s field,
as the Lord commanded me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Judas regrets what he has done. He says honestly: “I have sinned.” But the priests leave him alone. No one helps him.
> Judas takes his own life. That is very sad. Matthew tells it without passing judgment.
> Important: Whoever is thinking about suicide is not alone. There is help, even if you feel great guilt. Please reach out: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Christians believe: No guilt is so great that God cannot forgive it. Peter failed too, and he found a new beginning.
> Whoever has lost someone to suicide should know: Today the churches no longer condemn people who have taken their own lives. They are usually given a Christian funeral today. God is merciful.
> Verses 9–10: Matthew names the prophet Jeremiah. But the words about the 30 pieces of silver are mainly found in Zechariah (Zechariah 11:12–13). Jeremiah tells about a potter and about buying a field (Jeremiah 18:1–4; 19:1–2; 32:6–9). Matthew probably combines these passages and names the better-known prophet.
> The book of Acts tells about the death of Judas somewhat differently (Acts 1:18–19).

## Jesus before Pilate (Verses 11–26)

[11] Now Jesus stood before the governor.
The governor asked him,
“Are you the King of the Jews?”
Jesus said to him,
“So you say.”
[12] When the chief priests and elders accused him,
he answered nothing.
[13] Then Pilate said to him,
“Don’t you hear how many things they are saying against you?”
[14] But he didn’t answer him, not even one word,
so that the governor was greatly amazed.
[15] At the feast the governor was used to
releasing to the crowd one prisoner
whom they wanted.
[16] At that time they had a well-known prisoner
called Barabbas.
[17] So when they had gathered,
Pilate said to them,
“Whom do you want me to release to you?
Barabbas, or Jesus
who is called Christ?”
[18] For he knew
that they had handed him over out of envy.
[19] While he was sitting on the judgment seat,
his wife sent him a message:
“Have nothing to do with that righteous man!
For today I have suffered many things in a dream
because of him.”
[20] But the chief priests and the elders
persuaded the crowds
to ask for Barabbas
and to have Jesus killed.
[21] The governor asked them,
“Which of the two do you want me to release to you?”
They said,
“Barabbas!”
[22] Pilate said to them,
“Then what shall I do with Jesus
who is called Christ?”
They all said,
“Let him be crucified!”
[23] But the governor asked,
“Why? What evil has he done?”
But they shouted even louder,
“Let him be crucified!”
[24] Pilate saw
that he was getting nowhere,
but that a riot was starting.
So he took water,
washed his hands in front of the crowd, and said,
“I am innocent of the blood of this righteous person.
That is your problem.”
[25] All the people answered,
“May his blood be on us and on our children!”
[26] Then he released Barabbas to them.
But he had Jesus flogged
and handed him over to be crucified.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: For the Romans, “King of the Jews” was a political charge. Anyone who called himself king without the emperor’s permission was considered a rebel.
> Verses 16–17: Many old manuscripts give Barabbas his full name, Jesus Barabbas. “Barabbas” means “son of the father.” The WEB has only “Barabbas.”
> Verse 19: Pilate’s wife warns her husband. She calls Jesus a “righteous man.”
> Flogged: beaten hard with a whip.
> Verse 20: The crowd is persuaded by some leaders.
> Verse 24: Pilate washes his hands. From this comes the saying that someone washes their hands of something. But Pilate is not innocent. He had the power, and he gave the sentence. The Christian creed says that Jesus suffered under Pontius Pilate.

> **A very important note on verse 25**
> This verse is one of the most terribly misused verses in the Bible. For almost 2,000 years Christians used it to insult Jews as God-killers, to persecute them, to drive them from their homes and to kill them. This hatred of Jews helped prepare the way for the Holocaust, the murder of 6 million Jews.
> That is a terrible guilt of Christians.
> That is why it must be said clearly:
> What is meant is a crowd on one particular morning in Jerusalem that was persuaded by some leaders (verse 20). It was not “the Jewish people” of all times.
> No one can declare their children or grandchildren guilty by shouting something. The Bible says: Each person is responsible for their own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20).
> Jesus himself prayed for those who crucified him (Luke 23:34). Christians believe: Jesus’ blood brings forgiveness (26:28), not a curse.
> In 1965 the Catholic Church declared (*Nostra Aetate*): The Jews must not be presented as rejected or cursed by God. The Protestant churches in Germany and many other churches have also clearly confessed this.
> Whoever uses this verse against Jews acts against the will of Jesus.

## The soldiers mock Jesus (Verses 27–31)

[27] Then the governor’s soldiers
took Jesus into the Praetorium
and gathered the whole company of soldiers around him.
[28] They stripped him
and put a scarlet robe on him.
[29] They twisted together a crown of thorns
and put it on his head.
They put a reed in his right hand.
They knelt down in front of him,
mocked him, and said,
“Hail, King of the Jews!”
[30] They spat on him,
took the reed,
and hit him on the head with it.
[31] When they had mocked him,
they took the robe off him
and put his own clothes back on him.
Then they led him away
to crucify him.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “Praetorium” was the residence of the governor in Jerusalem.
> Scarlet: bright red.
> The Roman soldiers make fun of Jesus. They dress him up as a king: a red robe like a royal robe, a crown of thorns, a reed as a scepter.
> Jesus is humiliated, beaten and mocked. He suffers violence and torture.
> For today: Jesus stands by all people who are tortured, humiliated or abused. Whoever suffers violence is not abandoned by God.

## The crucifixion (Verses 32–44)

[32] As they were going out,
they met a man from Cyrene
named Simon.
They forced him
to go with them
and carry Jesus’ cross.
[33] They came to a place
called Golgotha,
which means “The place of a skull.”
[34] There they gave him sour wine to drink mixed with gall.
When he had tasted it,
he would not drink it.
[35] When they had crucified him,
they divided his clothes among them
by casting lots.
[36] Then they sat down
and kept watch over him there.
[37] Above his head they put up a sign
with the charge against him:
“THIS IS JESUS, THE KING OF THE JEWS.”
[38] Two robbers were crucified with him,
one on his right and one on his left.
[39] The people who passed by
insulted him.
They shook their heads
[40] and said,
“You who destroy the temple
and build it in three days,
save yourself!
If you are the Son of God,
come down from the cross!”
[41] In the same way the chief priests also mocked him,
together with the scribes, the Pharisees, and the elders.
They said,
[42] “He saved others,
but he can’t save himself.
If he is the King of Israel,
let him come down from the cross now,
and we will believe in him.
[43] He trusts in God.
Let God rescue him now,
if he wants him.
For he said,
‘I am the Son of God.’”
[44] The robbers who were crucified with him
insulted him in the same way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cyrene was a city in North Africa, in today’s Libya. Many Jews lived there. Simon had probably come to Jerusalem for the Passover.
> Golgotha (Aramaic) means “skull.” The Latin name is “Calvary.” At that time the place was outside the city wall.
> Crucifixion was the cruelest death penalty of the Romans. It was mainly meant for slaves and rebels. A person on a cross often died only after many hours.
> Verse 34: Jesus is given “sour wine to drink mixed with gall.” That is what the WEB says. Gall: a bitter liquid. Some manuscripts have wine instead of sour wine. This reminds us of Psalm 69:21 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 69:22).
> Casting lots: a way of deciding something by chance, a bit like rolling dice.
> Verse 35: Dividing the clothes reminds us of Psalm 22:18 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 22:19). Older Bible editions still have a quotation from this psalm in the text here. It is missing in the best manuscripts and also in the WEB.
> Verses 39–43: Jesus is mocked. This reminds us of Psalm 22:7–8 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 22:8–9).
> Verse 41: Again it is certain leaders, not the whole people.

## Jesus dies (Verses 45–56)

[45] From the sixth hour
there was darkness over all the land
until the ninth hour.
[46] About the ninth hour
Jesus cried out with a loud voice,
“Eli, Eli, lima sabachthani?”
That means,
“My God, my God,
why have you forsaken me?”
[47] Some of those who were standing there
heard it and said,
“This man is calling Elijah.”
[48] At once one of them ran,
took a sponge,
filled it with vinegar,
put it on a reed,
and gave him a drink.
[49] But the others said,
“Leave him alone!
Let’s see whether Elijah comes to save him.”
[50] Jesus cried out again with a loud voice
and gave up his spirit.
[51] And the curtain of the temple
was torn in two from top to bottom.
The earth shook,
and the rocks split apart.
[52] The tombs were opened,
and many bodies of the saints
who had died
were raised.
[53] After his resurrection they came out
of the tombs,
went into the holy city,
and appeared to many people.
[54] The centurion
and those who were with him guarding Jesus
saw the earthquake
and the things that happened.
They were terrified and said,
“Truly this was the Son of God!”
[55] Many women were there,
watching from a distance.
They had followed Jesus from Galilee
and had served him.
[56] Among them were Mary Magdalene,
Mary the mother of James and Joses,
and the mother of the sons of Zebedee.

> **What does this mean?**
> The sixth hour is about 12 noon, the ninth hour about 3 in the afternoon. Three hours of darkness in broad daylight: All of creation mourns.
> Verse 46: Jesus prays the first words of Psalm 22 in his native language, Aramaic (with Hebrew echoes). This psalm begins with a lament about being forsaken by God. But it ends with trust and praise of God.
> Forsaken: left completely alone, abandoned.
> Jesus really felt the deepest pain, including the feeling of being abandoned by God. That is why we, too, may lament to God like this. Whoever feels abandoned by God is not alone with this feeling. Jesus knows it.
> Verse 47: “Eli” sounds similar to “Elijah.” That is why some misunderstand him.
> Verse 51: The curtain in the temple separated the Most Holy Place from the rest of the temple. Christians understand its tearing like this: Through Jesus’ death the way to God is open for everyone.
> Verses 52–53 are told only by Matthew. These verses are hard to understand. Some see a picture in them: Jesus’ death and resurrection defeat death. This reminds us of Ezekiel 37:12–13.
> Verse 54: A centurion is a Roman officer in charge of about 100 soldiers. Of all people, a Roman officer, a non-Jew, confesses: This was the Son of God.
> Verses 55–56: The disciples have fled. But the women stay with Jesus to the very end. They are faithful disciples. Later they will be the first witnesses of the resurrection.

## Jesus is buried (Verses 57–61)

[57] When evening had come,
a rich man from Arimathaea came.
His name was Joseph,
and he too was a disciple of Jesus.
[58] He went to Pilate
and asked for Jesus’ body.
Then Pilate ordered
that the body be given to him.
[59] Joseph took the body
and wrapped it in a clean linen cloth.
[60] He laid it in his own new tomb,
which he had cut out in the rock.
Then he rolled a large stone against the entrance of the tomb
and went away.
[61] Mary Magdalene and the other Mary
were there,
sitting opposite the tomb.

> **What does this mean?**
> Arimathaea was a town in Judea. Joseph was a Jewish man, rich and respected. According to Mark 15:43 he was a member of the Council. He shows: Not all the leaders were against Jesus.
> According to Jewish law, someone who had been executed was to be buried on the same day (Deuteronomy 21:22–23). Joseph makes sure Jesus has a dignified burial.
> A tomb cut in the rock was like a small cave. A large round stone was rolled in front of the entrance.
> The women see where Jesus is buried. That is why on Easter morning they can go to the right tomb.

## The guard at the tomb (Verses 62–66)

[62] On the next day,
the day after the Preparation Day,
the chief priests and the Pharisees gathered
before Pilate
[63] and said,
“Sir, we remember
that this deceiver said
while he was still alive,
‘After three days I will rise again.’
[64] So give orders
that the tomb be made secure until the third day.
Otherwise his disciples may come at night,
steal him away,
and tell the people,
‘He is risen from the dead.’
Then the last deception would be worse than the first.”
[65] Pilate said to them,
“You have a guard.
Go and make it as secure as you can.”
[66] So they went
and made the tomb secure by sealing the stone
and setting the guard.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “Preparation Day” was the day before the Sabbath, when everything was prepared for the Sabbath. So the day after it is the Sabbath.
> The opponents want to prevent anyone from claiming that Jesus has risen. That is why they have the tomb sealed and guarded.
> Only Matthew tells this story. It shows: No seal and no guard can stop God. The next chapter tells about the resurrection.
> Verse 65: In the WEB Pilate says: “You have a guard.” This can also be understood as an order: Take a guard! Then they would have been Roman soldiers.
