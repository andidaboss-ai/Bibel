# Matthew – Chapter 6
### The Sermon on the Mount (Part 2): The Lord’s Prayer

---

## Doing good in secret (Verses 1–4)

[1] “Be careful that you don’t do your giving to the poor in front of people
in order to be seen by them.
Otherwise you have no reward from your Father in heaven.
[2] So when you do merciful deeds,
don’t sound a trumpet in front of you,
as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets,
so that people will praise them.
Most certainly I tell you,
they have already received their reward.
[3] But when you do merciful deeds,
don’t let your left hand know what your right hand is doing,
[4] so that your merciful deeds may be done in secret.
Then your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you openly.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Chapter 6 Jesus speaks about three important ways of living out faith in Judaism: giving to the poor (verses 1–4), praying (verses 5–15) and fasting (verses 16–18). Jesus does not reject them. He only says: Do them for God, not in order to be admired.
> “Hypocrite” was originally the Greek word for an actor. It means people who put their faith on show as if they were on a stage. That happens in every religion, among Christians too.
> Verse 3: “Don’t let your left hand know what your right hand is doing” means: Don’t make a big fuss about your good deeds, not even to yourself.

---

## Praying in secret (Verses 5–8)

[5] And when you pray,
you shall not be like the hypocrites.
For they love to stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners,
so that people will see them.
Most certainly I tell you,
they have already received their reward.
[6] But you, when you pray,
go into your room,
shut the door,
and pray to your Father who is in secret.
And your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you openly.
[7] When you pray, don’t keep repeating empty words as the Gentiles do.
For they think they will be heard because they use many words.
[8] So don’t be like them,
for your Father knows what you need before you ask him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Prayer is a personal conversation with God. You don’t need an audience for it. This does not forbid praying together in worship; Jesus himself also prayed with others.
> Verses 7–8: You don’t have to talk God into something with many words. He already knows what we need. Prayer is not a magic trick, but trust.

## The Lord’s Prayer (Verses 9–15)

[9] So pray like this:
‘Our Father in heaven,
may your name be kept holy.
[10] Let your Kingdom come.
Let your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.
[11] Give us today our daily bread.
[12] Forgive us our debts,
as we also forgive our debtors.
[13] Do not bring us into temptation,
but deliver us from the evil one.
For yours is the Kingdom, the power, and the glory forever.
Amen.’
[14] For if you forgive people their wrongdoings,
your heavenly Father will also forgive you.
[15] But if you don’t forgive people their wrongdoings,
your Father will not forgive your wrongdoings either.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Lord’s Prayer is the most important prayer of Christians. It is prayed in all churches around the world. Many of its requests have parallels in Jewish prayers of that time.
> “Our Father”: We may speak to God like a loving father, not like a distant ruler. “Our” shows: We do not pray alone, but as a community.
> The first three requests are about God: his name, his Kingdom, his will. The next requests are about us: bread, forgiveness, protection.
> Verse 11: “Our daily bread” means everything we need to live today.
> Verse 12: “Debts” is a picture for guilt before God and before people. Whoever receives forgiveness should also forgive others.
> Verse 13: “Do not bring us into temptation” means: Keep us from getting into situations in which we fail. God does not lead anyone into evil (James 1:13). That is why some churches pray this request in other words: God should keep us from falling into temptation.
> The ending “For yours is the Kingdom …” is not found in the oldest manuscripts of the Gospel of Matthew. It was added early on in worship. The WEB has it, so it is here. In Catholic services it is prayed in a slightly different way.
> Verses 14–15: Forgiveness is very important. But forgiving does not mean approving of wrong, or having to stay in contact with people who harm you. Forgiving can take time. Victims of violence may take their time and get help.

## Fasting in secret (Verses 16–18)

[16] And when you fast,
don’t be like the hypocrites with gloomy faces.
For they change the look of their faces
so that people will see that they are fasting.
Most certainly I tell you,
they have already received their reward.
[17] But you, when you fast,
put oil on your head and wash your face,
[18] so that people don’t see that you are fasting,
but your Father who is in secret.
And your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.

> **What does this mean?**
> To fast means to go without food or other things for a time in order to focus on God. Jesus says: Don’t make a show of it. Take care of yourself as on a normal day.
> Note: Fasting is not healthy for everyone, for example for people with certain illnesses or with eating disorders. Whoever suffers from an eating disorder should get help from a doctor or therapist. If you need someone to talk to, day or night: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Treasures in heaven (Verses 19–24)

[19] Don’t store up treasures for yourselves on the earth,
where moths and rust destroy them,
and where thieves break in and steal.
[20] Instead, store up treasures for yourselves in heaven,
where neither moths nor rust destroy them,
and where thieves don’t break in and steal.
[21] For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
[22] The eye is the lamp of the body.
So if your eye is healthy,
your whole body will be full of light.
[23] But if your eye is evil,
your whole body will be full of darkness.
So if the light in you is darkness,
how great that darkness is!
[24] No one can serve two masters.
Either he will hate the one and love the other,
or he will be devoted to one and despise the other.
You can’t serve both God and Mammon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–21: Everything we own on earth does not last. “Treasures in heaven” are what lasts before God: love, justice, mercy. “Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also”: What we hold on to shapes our life.
> Verses 22–23: A “healthy” eye here means a generous, honest way of looking at things. An “evil eye” was an expression for envy and greed.
> Verse 24: “Mammon” is an Aramaic word for money and possessions. Jesus says: Money can become an idol that rules our life. You cannot serve God and money at the same time.

## Don’t worry (Verses 25–34)

[25] So I tell you,
don’t worry about your life,
what you will eat or what you will drink,
or about your body, what you will wear.
Isn’t life more than food,
and the body more than clothing?
[26] Look at the birds of the sky:
They don’t sow, they don’t reap,
and they don’t gather into barns,
yet your heavenly Father feeds them.
Aren’t you worth much more than they are?
[27] Which of you by worrying
can add even one moment to your lifetime?
[28] And why do you worry about clothing?
Look at the lilies of the field, how they grow.
They don’t work, and they don’t spin.
[29] Yet I tell you
that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these.
[30] But if God dresses the grass of the field like this,
which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven,
won’t he much more dress you,
you of little faith?
[31] So don’t worry, saying,
‘What will we eat?’,
‘What will we drink?’
or, ‘What will we wear?’
[32] For the Gentiles run after all these things.
Your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things.
[33] But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness,
and all these things will be given to you as well.
[34] So don’t worry about tomorrow,
for tomorrow will worry about itself.
Each day has enough trouble of its own.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus is speaking to people who were often really poor and did not know whether they would have enough to eat tomorrow. He does not say that their worries are unimportant. He invites them to trust God.
> Verses 26 and 28: The birds and the flowers: God even takes care of them. How much more will he take care of you!
> Verse 27: Worrying does not make life longer. It only takes away strength.
> Verse 33: A key sentence: “Seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness …” When God comes first, many other things fall into place.
> Verse 34: Live today. Each day has its own challenges.
> Important: These words are an invitation to trust, not a reproach. Whoever suffers from fears or an anxiety disorder is not someone “of little faith.” Fears are often an illness, and it is good and right to get help from a doctor or therapist. If you need someone to talk to: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> For Christians, trusting God also includes helping others in need. In this way God’s care often becomes visible through people.
