# Matthew – Chapter 25
### What you did for one of the least

---

## The 10 young women (Verses 1–13)

[1] “Then the Kingdom of Heaven will be like 10 young women.
They took their lamps
and went out to meet the bridegroom.
[2] Five of them were foolish,
and five were wise.
[3] The foolish ones took their lamps,
but they took no oil with them.
[4] But the wise ones took oil in jars
along with their lamps.
[5] The bridegroom took a long time to come,
so they all became drowsy and fell asleep.
[6] But at midnight there was a cry:
‘The bridegroom is coming!
Come out to meet him!’
[7] Then all the young women got up
and got their lamps ready.
[8] The foolish ones said to the wise ones,
‘Give us some of your oil,
for our lamps are going out.’
[9] But the wise ones answered,
‘What if there isn’t enough for us and for you?
Go instead to those who sell oil
and buy some for yourselves.’
[10] While they were going away to buy oil,
the bridegroom came.
Those who were ready
went in with him to the wedding feast.
And the door was shut.
[11] Later the other young women also came and said,
‘Lord, Lord, open the door for us!’
[12] But he answered,
‘Most certainly I tell you,
I don’t know you.’
[13] So keep watch!
For you don’t know the day or the hour
when the Son of Man is coming.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At a wedding in those days, the bridegroom went to fetch the bride and brought her to his house. Young women from the town went with him carrying lamps or torches. The WEB says virgins, meaning young, unmarried women, bridesmaids.
> The bridegroom is a picture of Jesus, who will come again. He comes later than expected.
> All 10 fall asleep, even the wise ones. That is not criticized. The difference is: The wise ones were prepared.
> The “oil” is a picture. Many interpreters say: It stands for what you cannot lend, for example your own faith, good deeds or a living relationship with God. No one can give that to you.
> Verse 12: “I don’t know you” is a serious word (compare 7:23). It shows: You should not keep putting off God’s invitation.
> Important: This parable does not teach that you should not help others. It is about something every person has to do for themselves.

## The parable of the entrusted talents (Verses 14–30)

[14] “For it is like a man
who was going to travel to another country.
He called his servants
and entrusted his wealth to them.
[15] To one he gave five talents
(about 375 pounds of silver),
to another two
(about 150 pounds),
to another one
(about 75 pounds),
to each according to his own ability.
Then he went on his journey.
[16] The one who had received the five talents
went at once,
traded with them,
and gained five more talents.
[17] In the same way, the one with the two talents
gained two more.
[18] But the one who had received only one talent
went away,
dug a hole in the ground,
and hid his master’s money.
[19] After a long time the master of those servants came back
and settled accounts with them.
[20] The one who had received the five talents
came and brought five more talents.
He said,
‘Master, you gave me five talents.
Look, I have gained five more talents.’
[21] His master said to him,
‘Well done, good and faithful servant!
You have been faithful over a few things,
I will put you in charge of many things.
Come and share your master’s joy!’
[22] The one with the two talents also came and said,
‘Master, you gave me two talents.
Look, I have gained two more talents.’
[23] His master said to him,
‘Well done, good and faithful servant!
You have been faithful over a few things,
I will put you in charge of many things.
Come and share your master’s joy!’
[24] Then the one who had received one talent also came
and said,
‘Master, I knew that you are a hard man.
You reap where you didn’t sow,
and gather where you didn’t scatter seed.
[25] I was afraid.
So I went away
and hid your talent in the ground.
Here, you have what is yours.’
[26] But his master answered him,
‘You wicked and lazy servant!
You knew that I reap where I didn’t sow,
and gather where I didn’t scatter seed.
[27] Then you should have put my money in the bank.
Then when I came back
I would have received what is mine with interest.
[28] So take the talent away from him
and give it to the one who has the 10 talents.
[29] For to everyone who has, more will be given,
and he will have more than enough.
But from the one who doesn’t have,
even what he has will be taken away.
[30] And throw the useless servant
out into the outer darkness.
There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days a “talent” was not a gift or ability, but a large sum of money: about 75 pounds of silver, as much as the wages for many years of work. Because this parable is so well known, today we use the word “talent” for an ability.
> The master entrusts a lot to his servants, “to each according to his own ability.” No one gets too much. And the one with two talents is praised just like the one with five. What matters is not how much you have, but what you do with it.
> The third servant is afraid. He sees his master as hard and harsh. That is why he risks nothing. The parable shows: A false, fearful picture of God can paralyze you.
> The message: God has given every person gifts. We should use them boldly, for God and for others.
> Verse 29: Compare 13:12. Whoever uses their gifts receives even more. Whoever buries them loses them. This is not a rule for business or money.
> Important: The parable does not say that people with a disability, an illness or little strength are worth less. Every person has gifts, even if they don’t look like achievements.
> Verse 30: A harsh picture for the judgment (compare 8:12; 22:13).

## The final judgment (Verses 31–46)

[31] “But when the Son of Man comes in his glory,
and all the holy angels with him,
then he will sit on the throne of his glory.
[32] All the nations will be gathered before him.
And he will separate them from one another,
as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats.
[33] He will put the sheep on his right side,
but the goats on his left.
[34] Then the King will say to those on his right side,
‘Come, you who are blessed by my Father!
Inherit the Kingdom
that was prepared for you from the creation of the world.
[35] For I was hungry,
and you gave me food to eat.
I was thirsty,
and you gave me something to drink.
I was a stranger,
and you took me in.
[36] I was naked,
and you gave me clothes.
I was sick,
and you visited me.
I was in prison,
and you came to me.’
[37] Then the righteous will answer him,
‘Lord, when did we see you hungry
and feed you?
Or thirsty
and give you something to drink?
[38] When did we see you as a stranger
and take you in?
Or naked
and give you clothes?
[39] When did we see you sick or in prison
and come to you?’
[40] The King will answer them,
‘Most certainly I tell you,
whatever you did for one of the least of these my brothers and sisters,
you did for me.’
[41] Then he will also say to those on his left side,
‘Go away from me, you who are cursed,
into the eternal fire
that is prepared for the devil and his angels!
[42] For I was hungry,
and you didn’t give me food to eat.
I was thirsty,
and you gave me nothing to drink.
[43] I was a stranger,
and you didn’t take me in.
I was naked,
and you didn’t give me clothes.
I was sick and in prison,
and you didn’t visit me.’
[44] Then they will also answer,
‘Lord, when did we see you hungry or thirsty,
or a stranger or naked,
or sick or in prison,
and didn’t help you?’
[45] Then he will answer them,
‘Most certainly I tell you,
whatever you didn’t do for one of the least of these,
you didn’t do for me.’
[46] And these will go away into eternal punishment,
but the righteous into eternal life.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the last great speech of Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew. It shows a picture of the end of time: All nations stand before Jesus as King and Judge.
> In Israel sheep and goats often grazed together. In the evening the shepherd separated them. The picture says nothing bad about goats as animals.
> The standard at the judgment is surprising: People are not asked what they believed or knew, but how they treated people in need.
> The six deeds are: giving food to the hungry, giving drink to the thirsty, taking in strangers, clothing the naked, visiting the sick and visiting prisoners. In the church they are counted among the “works of mercy.” Later a seventh work was added: burying the dead. In Judaism, too, such deeds are considered very important (compare Isaiah 58:6–7).
> The amazing thing: Neither group knew that they had met Jesus. Jesus is hidden in the poor, the strangers, the sick and the prisoners.
> “My brothers and sisters” (the WEB says brothers; men and women are meant): Some interpreters think this means Jesus’ disciples. Most understand it this way: It means all people in need.
> Verses 41 and 46 are very serious words. Christians understand the “eternal fire” in different ways. What is certain: God cares how people treat each other. The fire is “prepared for the devil,” not for people. God wants all people to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4).
> For today: Whoever helps refugees, cares for the sick, gives food to homeless people or visits prisoners meets Jesus. These verses have moved many people to found hospitals, charities and food banks.
