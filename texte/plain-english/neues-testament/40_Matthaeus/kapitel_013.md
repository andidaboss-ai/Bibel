# Matthew – Chapter 13
### The parables of the Kingdom of Heaven

---

## The parable of the farmer who sows (Verses 1–9)

[1] On that day Jesus went out of the house
and sat down by the lake.
[2] Many people gathered around him.
So he got into a boat and sat down.
The whole crowd stood on the shore.
[3] He told them many things in parables.
He said,
“A farmer went out to sow.
[4] As he sowed, some seeds fell along the road.
The birds came and ate them up.
[5] Others fell on rocky ground,
where they didn’t have much soil.
They sprang up at once,
because the soil was not deep.
[6] When the sun had risen,
they were scorched.
Because they had no root,
they dried up.
[7] Others fell among thorns.
The thorns grew up and choked them.
[8] Others fell on good soil
and produced fruit:
some 100 times as much,
some 60,
and some 30.
[9] Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear!”

> **What does this mean?**
> A parable is a short story from everyday life. It shows something about God and his Kingdom. You have to think about it in order to understand it.
> Jesus sits in the boat; the people stand on the shore. That way many can hear him well.
> At that time people sowed by throwing the seeds over the field by hand. Some of it also fell on the road, on stones or among thorns.
> The amazing thing is the ending: In spite of all the losses, there is a great harvest. A harvest of 100 times as much was a huge amount.
> Jesus himself explains the parable further below (verses 18–23).

## Why Jesus speaks in parables (Verses 10–17)

[10] The disciples came and asked him,
“Why do you speak to them in parables?”
[11] He answered them,
“You have been given the gift
of knowing the secrets of the Kingdom of Heaven.
But they have not been given it.
[12] For whoever has will be given more,
and he will have more than enough.
But whoever does not have,
even what he has will be taken away from him.
[13] That is why I speak to them in parables:
because seeing, they don’t see,
and hearing, they don’t hear,
and they don’t understand.
[14] In them the prophecy of Isaiah comes true,
which says,
‘You will keep on hearing
but will not understand at all.
You will keep on seeing
but will not perceive at all.
[15] For the heart of this people has grown hard.
Their ears hardly hear,
and they have closed their eyes.
Otherwise they might see with their eyes,
hear with their ears,
understand with their heart,
and turn back,
and I would heal them.’
[16] But blessed are your eyes, because they see,
and your ears, because they hear.
[17] For most certainly I tell you,
many prophets and righteous people wanted to see what you see,
and didn’t see it.
They wanted to hear what you hear,
and didn’t hear it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Secrets of the Kingdom of Heaven”: This means how God’s Kingdom comes. People do not understand that on their own. God gives this understanding as a gift.
> Verse 12: Whoever opens up to Jesus’ words understands more and more. Whoever closes himself off loses even the little he had. This is not a rule about money and possessions.
> Verses 14–15 quote Isaiah 6:9–10. Already the prophet Isaiah had to experience that many did not accept his message. Jesus says: It is the same now.
> Important: This does not apply to “the Jews” as a whole. Jesus and all his disciples were Jews themselves. It is about all people who close their hearts, then as now, in the church too.
> At the end there is hope: “I would heal them.” God wants to heal.
> Verses 16–17: The disciples are allowed to experience what many before them had waited for.

## Jesus explains the parable of the farmer (Verses 18–23)

[18] “So listen to what the parable of the farmer means.
[19] When anyone hears the word of the Kingdom
and doesn’t understand it,
the evil one comes
and snatches away what was sown in his heart.
This is what was sown along the road.
[20] What was sown on rocky ground
is the person who hears the word
and at once receives it with joy.
[21] But he has no root in himself.
He lasts only for a while.
When trouble or persecution comes because of the word,
he falls away at once.
[22] What was sown among the thorns
is the person who hears the word.
But the worries of this life
and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word,
and he produces no fruit.
[23] What was sown on good soil
is the person who hears the word and understands it.
He most certainly bears fruit
and produces a crop:
some 100 times as much,
some 60,
and some 30.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The seed is God’s word. The different kinds of soil are different people, or also different times in a person’s life.
> The road: The word does not get through at all.
> The rocky ground: Great enthusiasm, but no depth. When things get hard, people give up.
> The thorns: Worries and the wish for riches leave no room for God.
> The good soil: The word is heard, understood, and changes a person’s life.
> For today: The parable does not want us to judge other people. It asks each person: How do I listen? What chokes the good in me? And it gives courage: In spite of everything, God’s word bears fruit.
> Someone who has many worries is not a bad person because of it. Worries are part of life. It is right to look for help.

## The weeds among the wheat (Verses 24–30)

[24] He told them another parable.
He said,
“The Kingdom of Heaven is like a man
who sowed good seed in his field.
[25] But while people were sleeping,
his enemy came,
sowed darnel weeds among the wheat,
and went away.
[26] When the plants grew and formed grain,
the weeds appeared too.
[27] The servants of the owner came to him and said,
‘Sir, didn’t you sow good seed in your field?
Where did the weeds come from?’
[28] He said to them,
‘An enemy has done this.’
The servants asked him,
‘Do you want us to go and pull them up?’
[29] But he said,
‘No. If you pull up the weeds,
you might pull up the wheat with them.
[30] Let both grow together until the harvest.
At harvest time I will tell the harvest workers,
“First gather up the weeds
and tie them in bundles to burn them.
But gather the wheat into my barn.”’”

> **What does this mean?**
> The WEB says “darnel weeds.” Darnel is a weed. When it is young, it looks almost exactly like wheat. Only when the grain grows can you see the difference.
> The servants want to clean up at once. The owner says: No! Wait! Otherwise you will do damage.
> For today: People should not decide for themselves who is a “weed” and who is “wheat.” We cannot see into people’s hearts. Judgment belongs to God alone.
> This parable is a strong warning against everyone who wants to persecute, shut out or kill others in the name of God. Sadly, Christians have often forgotten this in history.
> Jesus explains the parable himself in verses 36–43.

## The mustard seed and the yeast (Verses 31–35)

[31] He told them another parable.
He said,
“The Kingdom of Heaven is like a mustard seed.
A man took it and sowed it in his field.
[32] It is the smallest of all seeds.
But when it has grown,
it is bigger than the garden plants
and becomes a tree,
so that the birds of the sky come
and live in its branches.”
[33] He told them another parable:
“The Kingdom of Heaven is like yeast.
A woman took it
and mixed it into about 30 pounds of flour,
until it was all leavened.”
[34] Jesus told the crowd all these things in parables.
He did not speak to them without a parable.
[35] This happened so that what the prophet had said would come true:
“I will open my mouth in parables.
I will speak about things
that have been hidden since the creation of the world.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The mustard seed was known as the smallest seed people knew in daily life. A large bush grows from it, up to about 10 feet tall. Jesus calls it a tree.
> The message: God’s Kingdom begins very small, with Jesus and a few disciples. But it becomes great. Many find room in it, like the birds in the branches. This reminds us of pictures in the prophets (for example Ezekiel 17:23).
> The yeast: A small amount changes the whole dough. The Bible says “three measures” of flour. That is a lot, about 30 pounds, enough bread for many people. That is how God’s Kingdom works: quietly, invisibly, but it changes everything.
> Leavened: made to rise by yeast.
> What is beautiful: Here a woman at her everyday work is the picture for what God does.
> Verse 35 quotes Psalm 78:2. There the psalm is linked to Asaph. Matthew calls him a “prophet.” Some old manuscripts name the prophet Isaiah here. The WEB does not have that.

## Jesus explains the parable of the weeds (Verses 36–43)

[36] Then Jesus sent the crowd away
and went into the house.
His disciples came to him and said,
“Explain to us the parable of the weeds in the field.”
[37] He answered them,
“The one who sows the good seed is the Son of Man.
[38] The field is the world.
The good seeds are the children of the Kingdom.
The weeds are the children of the evil one.
[39] The enemy who sowed them is the devil.
The harvest is the end of the age.
The harvest workers are angels.
[40] As the weeds are gathered up and burned in the fire,
so it will be at the end of this age.
[41] The Son of Man will send out his angels.
They will gather out of his Kingdom everything
that causes people to stumble,
and all who do evil.
[42] They will throw them into the furnace of fire.
There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.
[43] Then the righteous will shine like the sun
in the Kingdom of their Father.
Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus explains the picture: God sows good things in the world. But there is evil too. Both grow side by side until the end.
> Important: It is not the servants (people) who sort things out, but God’s angels, and only at the end. Until then, patience is needed.
> “Furnace of fire” and “weeping and grinding of teeth” are strong pictures for judgment (compare 8:12). They show: Evil and injustice have no future. Christians understand in different ways what exactly that will be like. Many see picture language in it, not an exact description.
> Verse 43 reminds us of Daniel 12:3: The righteous will shine. That is the goal: light, joy, fellowship with God.
> For today: These words are not meant to frighten, but to take seriously how important our actions are. And they comfort everyone who suffers injustice: God does not look away.

## The treasure, the pearl and the net (Verses 44–50)

[44] “Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a treasure
hidden in a field.
A man found it and hid it again.
In his joy he goes
and sells everything he has
and buys that field.
[45] Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a merchant
who is looking for fine pearls.
[46] When he had found one very valuable pearl,
he went and sold everything he had
and bought it.
[47] Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a large net
that was thrown into the sea
and caught fish of every kind.
[48] When it was full,
the fishermen pulled it up on the beach.
They sat down
and gathered the good fish into containers.
But the bad ones they threw away.
[49] That is how it will be at the end of the world.
The angels will come
and separate the wicked from among the righteous.
[50] They will throw them into the furnace of fire.
There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The treasure and the pearl: Two men find something unbelievably valuable. They give everything for it, and they do it with joy! One finds the treasure by chance; the other searches for a long time. Both happen: Some people find God suddenly, others after a long search.
> At that time people often hid their money in the ground, especially in times of war. According to the understanding of that time, whoever bought the field also owned the treasure.
> The message: God’s Kingdom is the most valuable thing there is. Whoever discovers it is glad to give up other things for it.
> The net: At first it catches “fish of every kind.” The sorting happens at the end, and it is done by God’s angels. As with the weeds: We humans do not do the sorting.
> “Bad” fish were, for example, fish that were not allowed to be eaten according to the Jewish food laws (Leviticus 11:9–12), or fish that could not be eaten.

## New and old (Verses 51–52)

[51] Jesus asked them,
“Have you understood all these things?”
They answered him,
“Yes, Lord.”
[52] He said to them,
“So every scribe
who has become a disciple in the Kingdom of Heaven
is like the owner of a house
who brings new and old things out of his treasure.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A scribe knew the Holy Scriptures very well, that is, what Christians today call the Old Testament. Jesus says: Whoever knows the old and understands the new from Jesus has a great treasure.
> The new does not replace the old. Both belong together. That is also one reason why the Christian Bible keeps the Old Testament.
> Some interpreters think that Matthew is also describing himself here: a Jewish scholar who follows Jesus.

## Jesus in his hometown (Verses 53–58)

[53] When Jesus had finished these parables,
he went away from there.
[54] He came to his hometown
and taught the people in their synagogue.
They were amazed and said,
“Where did this man get this wisdom
and these mighty works?
[55] Isn’t this the carpenter’s son?
Isn’t his mother called Mary,
and his brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas?
[56] Aren’t all his sisters here with us?
Where did this man get all these things?”
[57] And they took offense at him.
But Jesus said to them,
“A prophet is not without honor
except in his own hometown
and in his own house.”
[58] And he did not do many mighty works there
because of their unbelief.

> **What does this mean?**
> His hometown is Nazareth. There everyone knows him as the son of a craftsman. The Greek word for “carpenter” can also mean a builder in general, who works with wood or stone.
> The people think: We know him! That is why they cannot believe that God is working through him.
> About the “brothers” and “sisters” of Jesus, see the boxes on 1:25 and 12:46–50: Protestant Christians usually understand them as real brothers and sisters, Catholic and Orthodox Christians as close relatives. Instead of “Joses,” some manuscripts have “Joseph.”
> Verse 58: Jesus does not force anyone. Where people do not trust him, he does few miracles.
> Important: This does not mean that sick people today are to blame if they do not get well. Illness is not a sign of too little faith.
> For today: Sometimes it is hard to see the good in people you have known for a long time.
