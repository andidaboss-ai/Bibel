# Matthew – Chapter 3
### John the Baptizer and the baptism of Jesus

---

## John calls people to repent (Verses 1–6)

[1] In those days John the Baptizer came
and preached in the wilderness of Judea.
[2] He said,
“Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is near!”
[3] For he is the one the prophet Isaiah spoke about when he said,
“The voice of one crying in the wilderness:
Make the way of the Lord ready!
Make his paths straight!”
[4] John himself wore clothing made of camel’s hair
and a leather belt around his waist.
His food was locusts and wild honey.
[5] Then people went out to him from Jerusalem, from all of Judea,
and from all the region around the Jordan.
[6] They were baptized by him in the Jordan
and confessed their sins.

> **What does this mean?**
> John the Baptizer is the messenger whom Malachi had announced (Malachi 3:1; 4:5). He was a relative of Jesus (Luke 1:36).
> Verse 2: His message: “Repent!” The Greek word means: Change your thinking, change your life, turn to God. “The Kingdom of Heaven” is the Kingdom of God. Matthew says “Heaven” instead of “God,” like many Jews at that time, out of deep respect for the name of God.
> Verse 3: Matthew quotes Isaiah 40:3. John prepares the way for the one who comes after him.
> Verse 4: His clothing reminds us of the prophet Elijah (2 Kings 1:8). Locusts were a common food in the wilderness and were allowed by the law (Leviticus 11:22).
> Locusts: large grasshoppers.
> Verse 6: Baptism in the Jordan was a sign: I want to make a new start; God should wash me clean. The people openly confessed their guilt while doing it.

---

## Produce fruit that shows you have repented! (Verses 7–12)

[7] But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to be baptized,
he said to them,
“You offspring of vipers!
Who warned you to flee from the coming anger of God?
[8] So produce fruit that shows you have repented!
[9] And don’t think you can say to yourselves,
‘We have Abraham as our father.’
For I tell you that God is able to raise up children for Abraham from these stones.
[10] The ax is already lying at the root of the trees.
Every tree that doesn’t produce good fruit
is cut down and thrown into the fire.
[11] I baptize you with water for repentance.
But the one who comes after me is mightier than I am.
I am not worthy to carry his sandals.
He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.
[12] His winnowing fork is in his hand,
and he will thoroughly clean his threshing floor.
He will gather his wheat into the barn,
but he will burn up the chaff with fire that can never be put out.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Pharisees and Sadducees were two important religious groups in the Judaism of that time. The Pharisees thought it was very important to keep the law exactly in everyday life. The Sadducees often belonged to the priests at the temple. John criticizes them harshly, the way the prophets in earlier times criticized the powerful. This is an argument within Judaism. It is not a statement about “the Jews” or about Judaism as a whole. John, Jesus and the disciples were Jews themselves.
> Offspring of vipers: children of poisonous snakes. A very harsh insult.
> Verses 8–9: Repentance has to show itself in life, in good deeds. It is not enough to rely on the right ancestry (“We have Abraham as our father”). The same is true for Christians: It is not enough to be baptized or to belong to a church.
> Verses 10 and 12: Pictures from farming. A tree without fruit is cut down. When threshing, people throw the grain into the air with a fork: The heavy kernels fall down, the light chaff blows away and is burned. In the same way God will one day separate what lasts from what is worthless.
> Chaff: the dry, useless husks around the grain.
> Verse 11: John is humble: He does not even think he is worthy to carry the sandals of the one who is coming, a job for slaves. John baptizes with water; the one who is coming will baptize with the Holy Spirit.

---

## Jesus is baptized (Verses 13–17)

[13] Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan to John
to be baptized by him.
[14] But John tried to stop him and said,
“I need to be baptized by you,
and you come to me?”
[15] But Jesus answered him,
“Allow it now,
for this is the fitting way for us
to fulfill all righteousness.”
Then John allowed it.
[16] When Jesus had been baptized,
he came up out of the water at once.
The heavens were opened to him,
and he saw the Spirit of God coming down like a dove
and coming on him.
[17] And a voice out of the heavens said,
“This is my beloved Son,
with whom I am well pleased.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: Jesus comes to be baptized. John is amazed: Really, Jesus should baptize him!
> Verse 15: Jesus answers: In this way we fulfill “all righteousness,” that is, what God wants. Jesus stands beside the people who come to be baptized. He joins the line, even though, according to Christian faith, he himself had no need to repent.
> Verse 16: Heaven opens, and the Spirit of God comes on Jesus like a dove. Since then the dove has been a sign of the Holy Spirit and also of peace.
> Verse 17: God’s voice says: “This is my beloved Son.” The words remind us of Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1. Christians see the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit together in this scene.
> For today: Before Jesus has done anything at all, God already says: I love you. Many Christians understand their own baptism in a similar way: God’s love comes first, not later as a reward.
