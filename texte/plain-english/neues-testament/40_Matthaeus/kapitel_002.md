# Matthew – Chapter 2
### The wise men from the east

---

## The wise men come to Jerusalem (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Judea,
in the days of King Herod.
Then wise men from the east came to Jerusalem
[2] and said,
“Where is the newborn King of the Jews?
For we saw his star in the east
and have come to worship him.”
[3] When King Herod heard this,
he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.
[4] He gathered all the chief priests and scribes of the people
and asked them where the Christ would be born.
[5] They said to him,
“In Bethlehem in Judea,
for this is what the prophet wrote:
[6] ‘You Bethlehem, land of Judah,
are in no way least among the princes of Judah.
For out of you will come a ruler
who will shepherd my people Israel.’”
[7] Then Herod secretly called the wise men
and learned from them exactly when the star had appeared.
[8] He sent them to Bethlehem and said,
“Go and search carefully for the young child.
When you have found him, bring me word,
so that I can also come and worship him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Herod the Great was king over Judea by the favor of Rome. He died in the year 4 BC. So Jesus was born a little before the year “0.” (Our calendar was only fixed in the 6th century, and at that time people miscalculated by a few years.)
> The “wise men” (in Greek “magoi”) were probably star readers and scholars from Persia or Babylonia. The Bible does not say that there were three of them or that they were kings. Later tradition concluded that from the three gifts.
> Important: The first people who worship Jesus are people from foreign nations. This fits with the women from foreign peoples in the family tree (Chapter 1). Jesus came for all nations.
> Verse 2: We do not know exactly what the “star” was. Some think of a special position of the planets (Jupiter and Saturn in the year 7 BC), of a comet, or of a miraculous sign.
> Verse 3: Herod is troubled. He was known for having even members of his own family killed because he was afraid of losing his power. A new “King of the Jews” was a threat to him.
> Verses 5–6: The scribes know the answer from the prophet Micah (Micah 5:2; in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 5:1). They know where the Messiah is to be born, but they do not set out themselves.
> Verse 8: Herod is lying. He does not want to worship the child, but to kill him.

## The wise men find Jesus (Verses 9–12)

[9] After they had heard the king, they went on their way.
And the star that they had seen in the east
went ahead of them,
until it came and stopped over the place where the young child was.
[10] When they saw the star,
they rejoiced with very great joy.
[11] They went into the house
and saw the young child with Mary, his mother.
They fell down and worshiped him.
They opened their treasures
and offered him gifts:
gold, frankincense, and myrrh.
[12] In a dream they were warned
not to go back to Herod.
So they went back to their own country another way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The joy of the wise men is enormous, “very great joy.”
> Verse 11: It is striking: They come into a “house,” not into a stable. The wise men probably came some time after the birth, perhaps months later (compare verse 16: “two years old and under”). Only Luke mentions the manger (Luke 2:7).
> Early on, the three gifts were understood like this:
> Gold for a king.
> Frankincense for God, because frankincense was burned in worship.
> Myrrh, a precious resin that was also used for anointing the dead, as a pointer to Jesus’ suffering and death.
> Frankincense: a sweet-smelling resin that gives off fragrant smoke when burned.
> Christians remember this on January 6 (Epiphany, also called Three Kings’ Day). In Germany, on this day “star singers” go from house to house and collect donations for children in need.
> Verse 12: God protects the child and warns the wise men.

## The escape to Egypt (Verses 13–15)

[13] When they had left,
an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said,
“Get up, take the young child and his mother,
and flee to Egypt.
Stay there until I tell you.
For Herod will look for the young child to kill him.”
[14] So he got up,
took the young child and his mother by night,
and went to Egypt.
[15] He stayed there until the death of Herod,
so that what the Lord had spoken through the prophet would come true:
“Out of Egypt I called my son.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus becomes a refugee as a baby. His family has to flee in the middle of the night from a violent ruler and look for safety in a foreign land. At that time Egypt was a common place of refuge for Jews; a large Jewish community lived in the city of Alexandria.
> For today: Many people in the world have to flee from violence and war, like Jesus and his family. Jesus knows what they go through.
> Verse 15: Matthew quotes Hosea 11:1. There “my son” means the people of Israel, whom God set free from Egypt. Matthew shows: Jesus lives through the history of Israel once more. Like Israel, he comes out of Egypt.
> Joseph obeys God at once, “by night.” He protects his family.

## The killing of the children in Bethlehem (Verses 16–18)

[16] When Herod saw that the wise men had tricked him,
he was very angry.
He sent soldiers and had all the boys killed
who were in Bethlehem and in all the surrounding area,
from two years old and under,
according to the exact time he had learned from the wise men.
[17] Then what the prophet Jeremiah had said came true:
[18] “A voice was heard in Ramah,
lamentation, weeping and great mourning.
Rachel is weeping for her children
and will not be comforted,
because they are no more.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A terrible crime: Herod has all the little boys in Bethlehem killed. Bethlehem was a small place; scholars estimate that there were perhaps 10 to 30 children. Other sources do not report it, but it fits with what we know about Herod’s cruelty.
> Churches remember this on December 28 (the Feast of the Holy Innocents).
> Verse 18: Matthew quotes Jeremiah 31:15. Rachel was Jacob’s favorite wife and the “mother” of the people. According to tradition, her grave was near Bethlehem. She weeps for her children. The Bible does not hide the suffering. It lets the pain and the lament stand.
> For today: Even today children suffer from violence, war and the abuse of power. God is on their side. If you are a child or teenager and you experience violence: In the US, call or text Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).

## The return to Nazareth (Verses 19–23)

[19] When Herod had died,
an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt
[20] and said,
“Get up, take the young child and his mother,
and go to the land of Israel.
For those who wanted to kill the young child are dead.”
[21] So he got up,
took the young child and his mother,
and came into the land of Israel.
[22] But when he heard
that Archelaus was ruling over Judea in the place of his father Herod,
he was afraid to go there.
After he was warned in a dream,
he went away into the region of Galilee
[23] and came and lived in a town called Nazareth,
so that what the prophets had said would come true:
He will be called a Nazarene.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–21: After Herod’s death the family can return.
> Verse 22: Archelaus, a son of Herod, was just as cruel as his father. The Romans removed him after about ten years. That is why Joseph moves to Galilee, in the north, where another son of Herod ruled.
> Verse 23: Nazareth was a small, unimportant village. It is not mentioned in the Old Testament. “He will be called a Nazarene” is not found word for word anywhere in the prophets. Matthew also says “the prophets” (plural). Perhaps he is thinking of a play on words with the Hebrew word “netzer,” which means “branch” (Isaiah 11:1): Jesus is the branch from the root of Jesse. Others think of the “Nazirites,” people who were set apart for God in a special way (Judges 13:5).
> So Jesus grows up in a small village, far from the capital city. He will be called “Jesus of Nazareth.”
