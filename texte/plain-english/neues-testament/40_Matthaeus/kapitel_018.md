# Matthew – Chapter 18
### How Jesus’ disciples should live together

---

## Who is the greatest? (Verses 1–5)

[1] At that time the disciples came to Jesus and asked,
“Who then is the greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven?”
[2] Jesus called a little child to himself
and stood the child in the middle of them.
[3] He said,
“Most certainly I tell you,
unless you turn around
and become like little children,
you will never enter the Kingdom of Heaven.
[4] Whoever makes himself as humble as this little child
is the greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven.
[5] And whoever welcomes one such little child
in my name
welcomes me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples want to know who is most important. Jesus puts a child in the middle.
> At that time children had no rights and no power. They had nothing to show for themselves and depended on others.
> Jesus says: That is how you should be before God. Not great and proud, but ready to receive gifts. It is not about being “childish,” but about trust and humility.
> Verse 5: Whoever takes care of children takes care of Jesus himself. Children have a special place with God.

---

## A warning against leading others into sin (Verses 6–9)

[6] “But whoever causes one of these little ones
who believe in me
to stumble,
it would be better for him
if a huge millstone were hung around his neck
and he were sunk in the depths of the sea.
[7] Woe to the world because of the things that cause people to stumble!
Such things have to come.
But woe to the person
through whom they come!
[8] If your hand or your foot causes you to stumble,
cut it off and throw it away.
It is better for you
to enter into life disabled or lame
than to have two hands or two feet
and be thrown into the eternal fire.
[9] And if your eye causes you to stumble,
pull it out and throw it away.
It is better for you
to enter into life with one eye
than to have two eyes
and be thrown into the fire of Gehenna.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “The little ones” are children, but also all weak and simple people who believe in Jesus.
> Verse 6: Jesus uses very harsh words for everyone who harms such people or turns them away from faith. A millstone was a very heavy stone. This is a picture, not an instruction to use violence.
> Millstone: a large, round stone used to grind grain into flour.
> This saying is especially important today: Children were abused in churches too, and abusers were protected. Jesus clearly takes the side of children. Whoever has experienced abuse or knows about it can find help: In the US, call Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453 or RAINN at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, children and young people can call Childline at 0800 1111. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 8–9 are picture language, just as in 5:29–30. Jesus wants to say: Take evil very seriously and separate yourself from everything that leads you to evil. He does not mean that anyone should really hurt themselves.
> If you have thoughts of hurting yourself, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> “Gehenna” was a valley near Jerusalem. It became a picture for God’s judgment (see the box on 5:22).

## The lost sheep (Verses 10–14)

[10] “Make sure that you don’t look down on one of these little ones!
For I tell you
that in heaven their angels
always see the face of my Father in heaven.
[11] For the Son of Man came
to save what was lost.
[12] What do you think?
If a man has one hundred sheep,
and one of them wanders away,
doesn’t he leave the ninety-nine,
go into the mountains,
and look for the one that wandered away?
[13] And if he finds it,
most certainly I tell you,
he is happier about that one
than about the ninety-nine
that did not wander away.
[14] In the same way, it is not the will of your Father in heaven
that one of these little ones should be lost.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: Jesus says that the little ones have “angels” with God. The idea of the “guardian angel” came from this. The message is: God keeps a special eye on the little ones. No one may look down on them.
> Verse 11 is missing in many old manuscripts. Something similar is found in Luke 19:10. The WEB has it.
> The parable of the lost sheep: Every single person counts for God. He gives up on no one. He looks for them until he finds them.
> In the Old Testament God himself is the good shepherd who looks for his lost sheep (Ezekiel 34:11–16; Psalm 23).
> For today: If you feel lost, you can know: God is looking for you. And the church should care for people who are on the edge.

## If someone sins against you (Verses 15–20)

[15] “If your brother sins against you,
go
and show him his fault,
just between you and him alone.
If he listens to you,
you have won back your brother.
[16] But if he doesn’t listen,
take one or two others with you.
For every matter is to be settled by the testimony
of two or three witnesses.
[17] If he refuses to listen to them,
tell it to the assembly.
If he refuses to listen to the assembly too,
let him be to you
like a Gentile or a tax collector.
[18] Most certainly I tell you,
whatever you bind on earth
will be bound in heaven.
And whatever you release on earth
will be released in heaven.
[19] Again I assure you,
if two of you on earth
agree about anything
they ask for,
it will be done for them by my Father in heaven.
[20] For where two or three
are gathered together in my name,
there I am in the middle of them.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Brother” here means every person in the church, men and women.
> Jesus describes a way to deal with conflict and guilt: first talk privately, without putting the other person to shame in front of others. Then with witnesses. Then before the church. The goal is always: to win the other person back.
> Verse 16 refers to Deuteronomy 19:15.
> Verse 17: “Gentiles” (people who did not belong to God’s people) and tax collectors were seen as outsiders. But Jesus himself went to tax collectors and foreigners with love (9:9–13; 15:21–28). So this does not mean: hate them, but: invite them anew.
> Important: This way does not apply to violence, abuse or crimes. Victims do not have to go to the abuser alone. They may get help at once and report it to the police. That is not against Jesus’ will.
> Verse 18: What was said to Peter in 16:19 applies here to all the disciples together.
> Verse 20: A great promise: Even two or three people who meet in Jesus’ name are not alone. Jesus is with them. Jewish teachers said something similar: Where two study the Torah together, God’s presence is with them.

## How often should I forgive? (Verses 21–22)

[21] Then Peter came to him and asked,
“Lord, how often should I forgive my brother
when he sins against me?
Up to seven times?”
[22] Jesus said to him,
“I don’t tell you up to seven times,
but up to seventy times seven.

> **What does this mean?**
> Peter thinks seven times is already very generous. Jesus says: Seventy times seven! That means: Never stop forgiving. You should not keep count.
> The Greek words can also mean “seventy-seven times.” Many Bibles, including many German Bibles, translate it that way. The WEB says “seventy times seven.” The meaning is the same: without limit.
> This reminds us of Genesis 4:24. There Lamech wanted to take revenge seventy-seven times. Jesus turns this around: not unlimited revenge, but unlimited forgiveness.
> Important: Forgiving does not mean approving of wrong. Nor does it mean that you have to stay with a person who hurts or hits you again and again. You may protect yourself and set boundaries. Forgiveness often takes time.

## The servant who would not forgive (Verses 23–35)

[23] So the Kingdom of Heaven is like a king
who wanted to settle accounts with his servants.
[24] When he began to settle accounts,
someone was brought to him
who owed him ten thousand talents.
[25] Because he couldn’t pay,
his master ordered
that he be sold,
with his wife, his children and everything he had,
and that the debt be paid.
[26] So the servant fell down,
knelt before him and said,
‘Master, have patience with me,
and I will pay you back everything!’
[27] The master of that servant was moved with compassion.
He let him go
and forgave him the debt.
[28] But that servant went out
and found one of his fellow servants
who owed him one hundred denarii.
He grabbed him,
took him by the throat
and said,
‘Pay what you owe!’
[29] So his fellow servant fell down in front of him
and begged him,
‘Have patience with me,
and I will pay you back!’
[30] But he refused.
He went and had him thrown into prison
until he paid back what he owed.
[31] When his fellow servants saw what had happened,
they were very sad.
They went to their master
and told him everything that had happened.
[32] Then his master called him in
and said to him,
‘You wicked servant!
I forgave you all that debt
because you begged me.
[33] Shouldn’t you also have had mercy on your fellow servant,
just as I had mercy on you?’
[34] His master was angry
and handed him over to the torturers
until he paid back everything he owed.
[35] That is how my heavenly Father will also treat you
if each of you does not forgive your brother
from your heart.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Ten thousand talents is an unimaginably large sum. One talent was as much as the wages for many years of work. No one could ever pay back this debt.
> One hundred denarii, on the other hand, is the wages for about one hundred days of work. That is a lot of money, but tiny by comparison.
> The king forgives the huge debt completely. But the servant has learned nothing from it. He is harsh with his fellow servant because of a small sum.
> The message: God has forgiven us an endless amount. Whoever has experienced that should forgive others too. This reminds us of the Lord’s Prayer: “Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors” (6:12, 14–15).
> Verse 25: At that time, in many countries people could be sold together with their families because of debts. Jesus tells this because his listeners knew it. He is not saying that it is good. Slavery is wrong.
> Verses 34–35 are harsh words. They show how seriously God means it. It is a picture from the parable, not an exact description of what God does. God is gracious, but grace should be passed on.
> “From your heart”: Forgiveness should be real. Whoever has been badly hurt often needs a long time for it. God knows that.
