# Matthew – Chapter 12
### The Lord of the Sabbath

---

## Heads of grain on the Sabbath (Verses 1–8)

[1] At that time Jesus went through the grain fields on the Sabbath.
His disciples were hungry
and began to pick heads of grain and eat them.
[2] When the Pharisees saw this,
they said to him,
“Look, your disciples are doing what is not allowed on the Sabbath.”
[3] But he said to them,
“Haven’t you read what David did
when he and those who were with him were hungry?
[4] How he went into God’s house
and ate the bread of the Presence,
which neither he nor those with him were allowed to eat,
but only the priests?
[5] Or haven’t you read in the law
that on the Sabbath the priests in the temple break the Sabbath
and yet are not guilty?
[6] But I tell you
that someone greater than the temple is here.
[7] But if you had understood what this means,
‘I desire mercy, and not sacrifice,’
you would not have condemned those who are not guilty.
[8] For the Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Sabbath is the seventh day of the week, a holy day of rest for God (Exodus 20:8–11). It is a great gift: a day on which the poor, servants, and animals may also rest. In Judaism it is still celebrated today.
> Verses 1–2: Picking heads of grain at the edge of a field was allowed (Deuteronomy 23:25). But some teachers counted picking grain on the Sabbath as “harvest work,” which was forbidden on the Sabbath. Jewish teachers had different opinions about this.
> Verses 3–4: Jesus reminds them of King David, who in a time of need ate the holy bread from the sanctuary (1 Samuel 21:1–6).
> Bread of the Presence: 12 loaves of bread that were laid out before God in the sanctuary.
> Verse 5: The priests also work in the temple on the Sabbath, and that is right.
> Verse 7: Again Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6: Mercy is more important to God than rules. Hungry people are more important.
> Verse 8: Jesus claims great authority: He is “Lord of the Sabbath.”

## A healing on the Sabbath (Verses 9–14)

[9] He went on from there
and came into their synagogue.
[10] There was a man there who had a withered hand.
They asked Jesus,
so that they could accuse him,
“Is it allowed to heal on the Sabbath?”
[11] He said to them,
“Which of you who has just one sheep
will not grab it and lift it out
if it falls into a pit on the Sabbath?
[12] How much more valuable is a person than a sheep!
So it is allowed to do good on the Sabbath.”
[13] Then he said to the man,
“Stretch out your hand!”
He stretched it out,
and it was made healthy again, just like the other one.
[14] But the Pharisees went out
and made plans against him,
how they could kill him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–12: Jesus says: If you rescue a sheep from a pit on the Sabbath, how much more may you help a human being! Even at that time Jewish law allowed people to save a life on the Sabbath. Jesus says: It is always allowed to do good.
> Withered: shrunken and unable to move.
> Verse 14: Some Pharisees decide to kill Jesus. That is a sad turning point. Important: These were individual opponents, not “the Jews” as a whole. Many ordinary people from the Jewish people followed Jesus with enthusiasm (verse 15).

## God’s beloved servant (Verses 15–21)

[15] When Jesus found out about this,
he went away from there.
Large crowds followed him,
and he healed them all.
[16] And he commanded them
not to make him known.
[17] This happened so that what the prophet Isaiah had said would come true:
[18] “Here is my servant whom I have chosen,
my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased.
I will put my Spirit on him,
and he will proclaim justice to the nations.
[19] He will not quarrel or shout,
and no one will hear his voice in the streets.
[20] He will not break a bruised reed,
and he will not put out a smoldering wick,
until he leads justice to victory.
[21] And in his name the nations will hope.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Matthew quotes Isaiah 42:1–4. Jesus is the “servant of God” on whom God’s Spirit rests (compare 3:16–17).
> Verse 19: He is not a loud, power-hungry man who gets his way by force.
> Verse 20: A beautiful picture: A bruised reed is almost broken; a smoldering wick has almost gone out. Jesus does not throw such people away. He treats the weak and the wounded gently.
> Verse 21: The nations, that is, the non-Jews, will also hope in him.
> For today: If you feel like a bruised reed, weak and at the end of your strength: God has not given up on you.

## Jesus and Beelzebul (Verses 22–37)

[22] Then people brought to him a man possessed by a demon,
who was blind and could not speak.
And he healed him,
so that the man could speak and see.
[23] All the crowds were amazed and said,
“Can this be the son of David?”
[24] But when the Pharisees heard this,
they said,
“This man casts out demons only by Beelzebul,
the prince of the demons.”
[25] Jesus knew their thoughts and said to them,
“Every kingdom divided against itself is ruined,
and every town or house divided against itself will not stand.
[26] If Satan casts out Satan,
he is divided against himself.
How then will his kingdom stand?
[27] And if I cast out demons by Beelzebul,
by whom do your own people cast them out?
So they will be your judges.
[28] But if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God,
then God’s Kingdom has come upon you.
[29] Or how can anyone go into the strong man’s house
and steal his goods
unless he first ties up the strong man?
Only then will he rob his house.
[30] “Whoever is not with me is against me,
and whoever doesn’t gather with me scatters.
[31] So I tell you,
every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven people,
but blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven people.
[32] Whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man
will be forgiven.
But whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit
will not be forgiven,
either in this age or in the age to come.
[33] “Either make the tree good, and its fruit will be good,
or make the tree rotten, and its fruit will be rotten.
For the tree is known by its fruit.
[34] You offspring of vipers!
How can you speak good things when you are evil?
For the mouth speaks what the heart is full of.
[35] The good person brings good things out of his good treasure,
and the evil person brings evil things out of his evil treasure.
[36] But I tell you
that people will have to give an account on the day of judgment
for every careless word they speak.
[37] For by your words you will be declared right,
and by your words you will be condemned.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24–26: Some Pharisees claim that Jesus works with the devil. Jesus shows how senseless that is: The devil would not fight against himself.
> Verse 27: Other Jewish healers also cast out demons at that time. The opponents are using a double standard.
> Verses 28–29: If Jesus drives out evil by God’s Spirit, then God’s Kingdom is already here. Jesus is stronger than “the strong man,” that is, evil.
> Verses 31–32: “Blasphemy against the Spirit” is a serious saying. It probably means a person who knowingly and constantly calls God’s good work devilish. In this way they close themselves off completely from God’s forgiveness.
> Important: Many people are afraid that they have committed this sin. Pastoral counselors say: Whoever worries about it and looks for forgiveness has precisely not committed it. God forgives everyone who comes to him. If such fears torment you, talk with a pastor or call a crisis line: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 34–37: “The mouth speaks what the heart is full of.” Our words show what is inside us. Words carry weight.

## The sign of Jonah (Verses 38–45)

[38] Then some of the scribes and Pharisees said to him,
“Teacher, we want to see a sign from you.”
[39] But he answered them,
“An evil and adulterous generation asks for a sign,
but no sign will be given to it
except the sign of the prophet Jonah.
[40] For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish,
so the Son of Man will be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.
[41] The people of Nineveh will stand up at the judgment with this generation
and will condemn it,
for they repented at the preaching of Jonah.
And someone greater than Jonah is here.
[42] The Queen of the South will rise up at the judgment with this generation
and will condemn it,
for she came from the ends of the earth
to hear the wisdom of Solomon.
And someone greater than Solomon is here.
[43] “When an unclean spirit has gone out of a person,
it wanders through dry places, looking for rest, and doesn’t find it.
[44] Then it says,
‘I will go back to my house that I came out of.’
And when it comes, it finds the house empty, swept, and put in order.
[45] Then it goes and takes along seven other spirits
more evil than itself.
They go in and live there.
And in the end that person is worse off than before.
That is how it will also be with this evil generation.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 39: “Adulterous” is a picture from the prophets for unfaithfulness to God (compare Hosea). It is not about adultery in the literal sense.
> Verse 40: The “sign of Jonah”: As Jonah was in the fish for three days, so Jesus will be in the grave for three days and then rise again (compare Jonah 1:17; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 2:1). “Three days and three nights” was a way of speaking; in Jewish counting, a day that had only begun also counted.
> Verses 41–42: The pagan people of Nineveh (book of Jonah) and the Queen of Sheba (1 Kings 10) listened to God’s messengers. With Jesus, even more is here.
> Verses 43–45: A picture: It is not enough just to throw evil out. If the “house” stays empty, evil comes back. You have to fill your life with good things.

## The true family of Jesus (Verses 46–50)

[46] While he was still speaking to the crowds,
his mother and his brothers stood outside
and wanted to speak with him.
[47] Someone said to him,
“Look, your mother and your brothers are standing outside
and want to speak with you.”
[48] But he answered the man who had told him,
“Who is my mother,
and who are my brothers?”
[49] He stretched out his hand toward his disciples and said,
“Here are my mother and my brothers!
[50] For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven
is my brother and sister and mother.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus does not reject his family. He says: There is a new, larger family, the family of God. Everyone who does God’s will belongs to it. He also names “sister” on purpose: Women naturally belong to it.
> About the “brothers of Jesus,” see the box on 1:25: Protestant Christians usually understand them as real brothers, Catholic and Orthodox Christians as close relatives.
> Verse 47 is missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB, our English source text, has it.
> For today: People who have no family, or who were hurt by their family, can find a home in God’s family.
