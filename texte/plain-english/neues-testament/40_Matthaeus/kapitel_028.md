# Matthew – Chapter 28
### Jesus has risen

---

## The empty tomb (Verses 1–10)

[1] After the Sabbath,
as the first day of the week was dawning,
Mary Magdalene and the other Mary came
to see the tomb.
[2] There was a great earthquake.
For an angel of the Lord came down from the sky,
came to the tomb,
rolled away the stone from the entrance,
and sat on it.
[3] He looked like lightning,
and his clothes were white as snow.
[4] The guards shook with fear of him
and became like dead men.
[5] The angel said to the women,
“Don’t be afraid!
I know that you are looking for Jesus,
who was crucified.
[6] He is not here.
For he has risen,
just as he said.
Come and see the place
where the Lord was lying.
[7] Go quickly
and tell his disciples,
‘He has risen from the dead.
He is going ahead of you into Galilee.
There you will see him.’
Now I have told you.”
[8] They left the tomb quickly,
with fear and great joy,
and ran to tell his disciples.
[9] As they were on their way
to tell his disciples,
Jesus met them and said,
“Rejoice!”
They came to him,
took hold of his feet,
and worshiped him.
[10] Then Jesus said to them,
“Don’t be afraid!
Go and tell my brothers
that they should go to Galilee.
There they will see me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In the Jewish way of counting, the “first day of the week” is Sunday. Because Jesus rose on this day, Christians celebrate Sunday as a special day. Easter is the most important feast of Christians.
> The women are the first witnesses of the resurrection. In those days the testimony of women often counted for little in court. But God chooses them of all people. They are given the task of bringing the good news to the men. That is why some call Mary Magdalene the “apostle to the apostles.”
> The angel does not open the tomb so that Jesus can come out, but so that the women can see: The tomb is empty.
> The guards who were supposed to guard Jesus “became like dead men.” But the dead man is alive.
> “Don’t be afraid!” say the angel and Jesus. The resurrection is a message against fear.
> Verse 8: “With fear and great joy”: Both are there. Something too great to grasp can also be frightening.
> Verse 10: Jesus calls the disciples “my brothers,” even though they left him. He forgives them.
> The Bible does not describe exactly how the resurrection happened. No one saw the moment itself. The Gospels tell of the empty tomb and of meetings with the risen Jesus. The four Gospels tell the events of Easter morning somewhat differently.

## The guards’ report (Verses 11–15)

[11] While the women were on their way,
some of the guards came into the city
and told the chief priests everything
that had happened.
[12] The chief priests met with the elders
and made a plan.
Then they gave a large amount of silver to the soldiers
[13] and said,
“Say,
‘His disciples came at night
and stole him away
while we were sleeping.’
[14] And if the governor hears about this,
we will persuade him
and make sure you don’t get into trouble.”
[15] So they took the money
and did as they were told.
And this story spread among the Jews
and is still told today.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Matthew tells this story. It answers a rumor that existed in Matthew’s time: that the disciples had stolen the body.
> It is a small group of leaders who pay money for a lie. Just as Judas got money for the betrayal (26:15), now the soldiers get money for their silence.
> Verse 15: “Among the Jews” here means people in Matthew’s time who did not believe in Jesus. Matthew and the first Christians were mostly Jews themselves. The sentence is not a judgment on the Jewish people.
> For today: The truth cannot be bought. Lies and bribes cannot stop what God does.

## The great commission (Verses 16–20)

[16] But the eleven disciples went to Galilee,
to the mountain
where Jesus had told them to go.
[17] When they saw him,
they bowed down to him.
But some doubted.
[18] Jesus came to them and spoke to them.
He said,
“All authority has been given to me
in heaven and on earth.
[19] So go
and make disciples of all nations.
Baptize them in the name of the Father
and of the Son
and of the Holy Spirit.
[20] And teach them
to obey everything
that I have commanded you.
And remember:
I am with you always,
even to the end of the age.”
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Commission: an important task that someone is given.
> There are only eleven disciples now, because Judas is dead.
> Verse 17: “But some doubted”: Even when the disciples see the risen Jesus, some doubt. Jesus sends them out anyway. Doubts are part of faith. Even someone who doubts belongs.
> Verse 18: Jesus has received all authority from God. This reminds us of Daniel 7:14.
> Verse 19: At the beginning the disciples were sent only to Israel (10:5–6). Now they are to go to all nations. The good news is for all people.
> “In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit”: These words are still spoken at every Christian baptism today. From them Christians later developed the teaching of the Trinity, the one God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
> Important: “Make disciples” does not mean bringing people to faith by force or violence. In history Christians baptized people by force, including many Jews. That was a grave injustice and against the will of Jesus. Faith can only be freely given and freely accepted.
> Verse 20: Jesus’ last word is a promise: “I am with you always.” At the beginning of the Gospel, Jesus was called “Immanuel,” which means “God with us” (1:23). At the end he promises exactly that. So the circle closes.
> “Amen” means “So it is” or “So be it.” Some old manuscripts do not have this Amen at the end. The WEB has it.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Gospel of Matthew?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Jesus, the son of David and of Abraham:** Matthew begins with the family tree (Chapter 1). He shows: Jesus belongs completely to the history of Israel. He is the promised Messiah. Again and again Matthew says that something happened so that what the prophets had said would be fulfilled.
> **God with us:** Jesus is called “Immanuel,” “God with us” (1:23). At the end he promises: “I am with you always” (28:20).
> **Five great speeches:** Matthew collected Jesus’ words in five great speeches: the Sermon on the Mount (Chapters 5–7), the sending speech (Chapter 10), the parables of the Kingdom of Heaven (Chapter 13), the speech about life in the community (Chapter 18) and the speech about the end times (Chapters 24–25).
> **The Sermon on the Mount:** The Beatitudes, love for enemies, the Lord’s Prayer and the Golden Rule: “Whatever you want people to do to you, you shall also do to them” (7:12).
> **The Kingdom of Heaven:** In parables Jesus tells about God’s Kingdom: like a mustard seed, like a treasure in a field, like a precious pearl (Chapter 13).
> **The greatest commandment:** Loving God with all your heart and loving your neighbor as yourself (22:37–40).
> **Jesus with the little ones:** He heals the sick, eats with tax collectors and sinners, blesses children and praises the faith of a foreign woman (15:28). At the final judgment he says: “Whatever you did for one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you did for me” (25:40).
> **Suffering, death and resurrection:** Jesus goes the way to Jerusalem. He is betrayed, condemned and crucified. But God raises him from the dead. The women are the first witnesses.
> **A serious note:** In history some passages in the Gospel of Matthew were misused against Jews. Jesus, his mother, his disciples and Matthew himself were Jews. The conflict in the Gospel was a conflict within the Jewish people. God stays faithful to his people Israel.
> **For today:** Matthew invites us to follow Jesus: by listening to his words and by doing them. “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter into the Kingdom of Heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father in heaven” (7:21).
> **What comes next?** Next comes the Gospel of Mark. It is the shortest of the four Gospels and probably the oldest. Mark tells his story in a lively and fast way. We already know many of the stories from Matthew, but Mark often tells them with other details of his own.
