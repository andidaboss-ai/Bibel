# Matthew – Chapter 7
### The Sermon on the Mount (Part 3): The house on the rock

---

## Don’t judge (Verses 1–6)

[1] “Don’t judge,
so that you won’t be judged.
[2] For with the judgment you use to judge others,
you will be judged,
and with the measure you use to measure,
it will be measured to you.
[3] Why do you see the speck in your brother’s eye,
but don’t notice the beam in your own eye?
[4] Or how can you say to your brother,
‘Let me take the speck out of your eye,’
when there is a beam in your own eye?
[5] You hypocrite!
First take the beam out of your own eye,
and then you can see clearly
to take the speck out of your brother’s eye.
[6] “Don’t give what is holy to the dogs,
and don’t throw your pearls to the pigs,
or they may trample them under their feet,
and turn and tear you to pieces.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: “Don’t judge” means: Don’t condemn other people harshly and without love. Whoever condemns others strictly will be judged by the same standard. It does not mean that you may no longer call wrong what it is.
> Verses 3–5: A funny, exaggerated picture: Someone has a whole wooden beam in his eye and wants to pull a tiny speck out of someone else’s eye. We often see the faults of others more quickly than our own. First look honestly at yourself; then you can really help others.
> Verse 6: A verse that is hard to understand. It probably means: Be wise with the most precious thing you have. Don’t force it on people who mock it and attack you. The pictures “dogs” and “pigs” are not names for certain groups of people or nations, and no one may use them that way.

## Ask, and it will be given to you (Verses 7–12)

[7] “Ask, and it will be given to you.
Seek, and you will find.
Knock, and the door will be opened to you.
[8] For everyone who asks receives.
Whoever seeks finds.
And to whoever knocks, the door will be opened.
[9] Or who among you
will give his son a stone
if he asks him for bread?
[10] Or if he asks for a fish,
who will give him a snake?
[11] If you, then, though you are evil,
know how to give good gifts to your children,
how much more will your Father in heaven give good things
to those who ask him!
[12] So whatever you want people to do to you,
you shall also do to them.
For this is the law and the prophets.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–8: An invitation to pray: Ask, seek, knock! God is listening.
> Verses 9–11: No good father gives his child a stone instead of bread. If even people who are not perfect give good things to their children, how much more will God do so.
> This does not mean that God fulfills every wish exactly the way we express it. Many people experience that prayers are answered differently, or not in the way they wish. Jesus promises: God gives “good things,” that is, what is really good for us. Whoever does not receive what they asked for is not to blame and did not have “too little faith.”
> Verse 12: The “Golden Rule”: Treat others the way you yourself want to be treated. Similar sentences exist in many religions and cultures. The famous Jewish teacher Hillel, who lived shortly before Jesus, is said to have said: What is hateful to you, do not do to your neighbor. That is the whole Torah. Jesus puts the rule in a positive way: Actively do good!

## The narrow gate (Verses 13–14)

[13] “Enter through the narrow gate.
For the gate is wide and the road is broad
that leads to destruction,
and there are many who go in by it.
[14] How narrow is the gate
and how hard the road that leads to life,
and there are few who find it!

> **What does this mean?**
> A picture of two ways: a broad, comfortable road on which many walk, and a narrow road that leads to life. This reminds us of the two ways in Psalm 1 and Deuteronomy 30:15–20.
> The way of Jesus is not always the comfortable way that the crowd takes. It takes decision and courage.
> Here Jesus does not give an exact number of how many people will be saved in the end. It is a call to look for the right way yourself, not a reason to judge others (compare verse 1).

## You will know them by their fruits (Verses 15–23)

[15] “Beware of false prophets,
who come to you in sheep’s clothing,
but inside they are hungry wolves.
[16] By their fruits you will know them.
Do people gather grapes from thorns
or figs from thistles?
[17] In the same way, every good tree produces good fruit,
but the rotten tree produces bad fruit.
[18] A good tree can’t produce bad fruit,
and a rotten tree can’t produce good fruit.
[19] Every tree that doesn’t produce good fruit
is cut down and thrown into the fire.
[20] So by their fruits you will know them.
[21] “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’
will enter into the Kingdom of Heaven,
but the one who does the will of my Father in heaven.
[22] Many will say to me on that day,
‘Lord, Lord, didn’t we prophesy in your name,
cast out demons in your name,
and do many mighty works in your name?’
[23] Then I will tell them,
‘I never knew you.
Go away from me, you who do evil!’

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–20: From the outside, false prophets look harmless and devout like sheep, but inside they want to harm others. How can you recognize them? By their “fruits,” that is, by how they live and what their actions bring about.
> For today: When religious leaders take advantage of people, put them under pressure, squeeze money out of them, or abuse them, those are bad fruits, no matter how devoutly they talk. Whoever experiences pressure, manipulation, or abuse in a religious group may get help, for example from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673.
> Verses 21–23: Devout words and even spectacular deeds are not enough. What matters is whether someone does God’s will: justice, mercy, and love.

## The house on the rock (Verses 24–29)

[24] “So everyone who hears these words of mine and does them
is like a wise man
who built his house on the rock.
[25] The rain came down,
the floods came,
and the winds blew and beat against that house,
but it did not fall,
for it was built on the rock.
[26] And everyone who hears these words of mine and doesn’t do them
will be like a foolish man
who built his house on the sand.
[27] The rain came down,
the floods came,
and the winds blew and beat against that house,
and it fell,
and its fall was great.”
[28] When Jesus had finished saying these things,
the crowds were amazed at his teaching.
[29] For he taught them like someone who has authority,
and not like the scribes.

> **What does this mean?**
> The parable of the two houses ends the Sermon on the Mount. Two men build a house. From the outside both houses may look the same. You only notice the difference when the storm comes.
> The rock is: hearing Jesus’ words and acting on them. Whoever lives like this has a firm foundation, even in the storms of life such as illness, loss, or crises.
> Verses 28–29: The people are amazed. The scribes usually explained the Scriptures by pointing to earlier teachers. Jesus speaks with his own authority: “But I tell you.” This does not put down the Jewish scribes. Many of them were serious and devout teachers, and Jesus himself was called “Rabbi” (teacher).

## Looking back: What have we read in the Sermon on the Mount (Chapters 5–7)?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The Beatitudes:** God turns to the poor, to those who mourn, to the gentle, to those who show mercy, and to those who make peace (5:3–12).
> **Salt and light:** “You are the salt of the earth. … You are the light of the world” (5:13–14).
> **God’s commandment from the heart:** Not only not killing, but making peace. Not only not committing adultery, but being faithful in your heart. No revenge, but love for your enemies (5:21–48).
> **True faith:** Doing good, praying, and fasting, not for show, but for God (6:1–18).
> **The Lord’s Prayer:** The prayer that Jesus taught his disciples (6:9–13).
> **Trust instead of worry:** “Seek first God’s Kingdom” (6:33).
> **The Golden Rule:** “Whatever you want people to do to you, you shall also do to them” (7:12).
> **Hearing and doing:** Whoever hears Jesus’ words and does them builds his house on the rock (7:24).
> **For today:** The Sermon on the Mount has moved people all over the world, even beyond Christianity. Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr., for example, were inspired by it to resist without violence.
