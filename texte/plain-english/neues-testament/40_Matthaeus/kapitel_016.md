# Matthew – Chapter 16
### “You are the Christ”

---

## The demand for a sign (Verses 1–4)

[1] The Pharisees and Sadducees came.
They wanted to test him
and asked him
to show them a sign from heaven.
[2] But he answered them,
“When it is evening, you say,
‘It will be fair weather,
for the sky is red.’
[3] And in the morning you say,
‘Today there will be bad weather,
for the sky is red and threatening.’
You hypocrites!
You know how to read the look of the sky,
but you can’t read the signs of the times!
[4] An evil and adulterous generation
asks for a sign.
But no sign will be given to it
except the sign of the prophet Jonah.”
And he left them and went away.

> **What does this mean?**
> Pharisees and Sadducees were two Jewish groups with very different views. The Sadducees belonged mainly to the leading priestly families at the temple. Here both appear together.
> They ask for a “sign from heaven”: proof that Jesus comes from God. Jesus refuses. Faith cannot be forced.
> Verses 2–3: Jesus uses a weather rule from everyday life. The people can read the sky, but they cannot recognize what God is doing right now. The “signs of the times” are Jesus’ words and deeds.
> Verses 2b–3 (from “When it is evening” to “the signs of the times”) are missing in some important old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verse 4: “Adulterous” is a picture from the prophets. It means: unfaithful to God. The “sign of Jonah” was already explained in 12:39–41: Jonah was in the fish for three days. Christians see in this a pointer to Jesus’ death and resurrection.
> Important: This is not a judgment on the Jewish people. It is about those who want to test Jesus here.

## The yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees (Verses 5–12)

[5] The disciples came to the other side of the lake.
They had forgotten to bring bread.
[6] Jesus said to them,
“Watch out and beware
of the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees!”
[7] They discussed this among themselves and said,
“We didn’t bring any bread.”
[8] Jesus noticed it and said,
“You of little faith,
why are you discussing among yourselves
that you have no bread?
[9] Don’t you understand yet?
Don’t you remember the 5 loaves for the 5,000,
and how many baskets you picked up?
[10] And the 7 loaves for the 4,000,
and how many baskets you picked up?
[11] How is it that you don’t understand
that I was not talking to you about bread?
Beware of the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees!”
[12] Then they understood:
He had not told them
to beware of the yeast in bread,
but of the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here yeast is a picture for something that spreads without being noticed and goes through everything. In 13:33 it was a good picture for God’s Kingdom. Here it is a warning.
> The disciples are only thinking about the bread they forgot. Jesus reminds them: You have seen that I can provide bread! Don’t worry about it.
> Verse 12: He means a certain “teaching.” Jesus probably means the attitude of demanding signs and rejecting him (verses 1–4). He does not mean that everything these teachers say is wrong. In 23:2–3 Jesus even says: Do what they teach.
> Important: Judaism is not “yeast.” Jesus and his disciples were Jews. In history Christians have often misused passages like this to speak badly of Jews. That is wrong.

## Peter confesses: You are the Christ (Verses 13–20)

[13] Jesus came into the region of Caesarea Philippi.
There he asked his disciples,
“Who do people say that I, the Son of Man, am?”
[14] They said,
“Some say John the Baptizer.
Others say Elijah.
And still others say Jeremiah or one of the prophets.”
[15] He said to them,
“But who do you say that I am?”
[16] Simon Peter answered,
“You are the Christ,
the Son of the living God.”
[17] Jesus answered him,
“Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah.
For flesh and blood has not revealed this to you,
but my Father in heaven.
[18] And I tell you
that you are Peter,
and on this rock I will build my church.
And the gates of Hades
will not overpower it.
[19] I will give you the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven.
Whatever you bind on earth
will have been bound in heaven.
And whatever you release on earth
will have been released in heaven.”
[20] Then he commanded the disciples
to tell no one
that he was Jesus the Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Caesarea Philippi is a town in the north, at the foot of Mount Hermon, near the springs of the Jordan.
> “Christ” is Greek and means the same as the Hebrew “Messiah”: “the anointed one.” That is the king God promised.
> Verse 17: “Flesh and blood” means: no human being. Peter did not figure this out himself. God showed it to him. “Bar Jonah” means “son of Jonah.”
> Verse 18: There is a play on words here. “Peter” comes from the Greek word for “rock” or “stone.” The WEB, our English source text, says “assembly,” that is, a gathering or congregation. Here we say “church,” as many Bibles do.
> “The gates of Hades” (the realm of the dead): Not even death will defeat the church.
> Christians understand verses 18–19 in different ways. Catholic Christians see here the task given to Peter and his successors, the popes. Protestant Christians often see the “rock” in Peter’s faith or confession, or in Christ himself. Orthodox Christians see Peter as the first among the apostles. All agree: Jesus builds his church, not a single human being.
> Verse 19: “Binding and releasing” was a way of speaking used by Jewish teachers. It means, for example: forbidding or allowing something, or holding on to guilt or forgiving it. In 18:18 all the disciples receive this task.
> Verse 20: Jesus does not want false hopes to arise. Many thought the Messiah would be a war hero. Jesus goes a different way, as the next verses show.

## Jesus announces his suffering (Verses 21–23)

[21] From that time on, Jesus began to show his disciples
that he must go to Jerusalem
and suffer many things from the elders,
the chief priests and the scribes.
He must be killed
and be raised up on the third day.
[22] Peter took him aside
and began to scold him.
He said,
“Far be it from you, Lord!
This must never happen to you!”
[23] But he turned around
and said to Peter,
“Get behind me, Satan!
You are a stumbling block to me.
For you are not thinking about the things of God,
but about the things of people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> For the first time Jesus speaks openly about his death and his resurrection. The word “must” shows: This is God’s way.
> The opponents Jesus names are certain leaders in Jerusalem, not the Jewish people. According to the report of the Gospels, in the end it was the Roman governor Pilate who had Jesus crucified. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. For centuries Christians wrongly insulted Jews as “Christ killers” and persecuted them. That is a grave guilt of the church.
> Peter has just said: You are the Christ. But he cannot imagine that the Christ must suffer.
> In Hebrew, “Satan” means “opponent” or “accuser.” Here Peter is like the tempter in the wilderness (4:1–11): He wants to turn Jesus away from the way of suffering. “Behind me” can also mean: Get back behind me, as my disciple.
> A moment ago Peter was the “rock.” Now he is a “stumbling block.” Even so, he remains Jesus’ disciple.
> Stumbling block: something that makes someone trip and fall.
> For today: People who believe can also be wrong. Jesus does not give up on them because of it.

## What it means to follow Jesus (Verses 24–28)

[24] Then Jesus said to his disciples,
“If anyone wants to follow me,
let him deny himself,
take up his cross,
and follow me.
[25] For whoever wants to save his life
will lose it.
But whoever loses his life for my sake
will find it.
[26] For what will it profit a person
if he gains the whole world
but loses his life?
Or what can a person give
in exchange for his life?
[27] For the Son of Man will come
in the glory of his Father
with his angels.
And then he will repay everyone
according to his deeds.
[28] Most certainly I tell you,
some of those who are standing here
will not taste death
until they see the Son of Man
coming in his Kingdom.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “To deny yourself” means: no longer putting yourself at the center, but Jesus. “To take up your cross”: Whoever follows Jesus must be ready to bear disadvantages or suffering too.
> Important: This does not mean that you simply have to put up with violence, abuse or injustice. Whoever experiences violence may and should get help. That is not against Jesus’ will. Help is available, for example: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. For domestic violence: US: 1-800-799-7233. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verses 25–26: The Greek word for “life” can also mean “soul.” A life only for possessions and success is lost in the end. A life for God and for others is never wasted.
> Verse 27: Jesus will come again as judge. This reminds us of Psalm 62:12 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 62:13).
> Verse 28 is hard to understand. Some interpreters think it is about the transfiguration, which is told right after this (17:1–8). Others think of the resurrection or of Pentecost. There are different interpretations.
