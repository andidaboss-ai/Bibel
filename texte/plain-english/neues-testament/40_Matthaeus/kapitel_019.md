# Matthew – Chapter 19
### Marriage, children and riches

---

## The question about divorce (Verses 1–12)

[1] When Jesus had finished saying these things,
he left Galilee
and came into the region of Judea on the other side of the Jordan.
[2] Large crowds followed him,
and he healed them there.
[3] Pharisees came to him.
They wanted to test him and asked,
“Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife
for any reason?”
[4] He answered,
“Haven’t you read
that he who made them at the beginning
made them male and female?
[5] And he said,
‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother
and shall be joined to his wife,
and the two shall become one flesh.’
[6] So they are no longer two,
but one flesh.
So what God has joined together,
let no one separate.”
[7] They asked him,
“Why then did Moses command us
to give her a certificate of divorce
and divorce her?”
[8] He said to them,
“Because your hearts were so hard,
Moses allowed you
to divorce your wives.
But it was not like that from the beginning.
[9] I tell you
that whoever divorces his wife,
except for sexual unfaithfulness,
and marries another woman,
commits adultery.
And whoever marries a divorced woman
commits adultery.”
[10] His disciples said to him,
“If that is how it is between a man and his wife,
it is better not to marry.”
[11] But he said to them,
“Not everyone can accept this saying,
but only those to whom it is given.
[12] For there are eunuchs
who were born that way.
And there are eunuchs
who were made that way by people.
And there are eunuchs
who made themselves that way
for the sake of the Kingdom of Heaven.
Whoever is able to accept this, let him accept it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> At that time there was an argument among Jewish teachers: For what reasons may a man divorce his wife? Some said: only for serious unfaithfulness. Others said: for almost any reason. The question “for any reason” refers to this argument.
> At that time in Israel it was usually only the man who could get a divorce. After a divorce, women were often poor and without protection. With his answer, Jesus mainly protects women from being divorced for no good reason.
> Verses 4–5 quote Genesis 1:27 and 2:24. Jesus goes back to the beginning: God wants marriage to last.
> Verse 7: Moses allowed divorce and set rules for it (Deuteronomy 24:1–4). The certificate of divorce protected the woman: She could marry again.
> Verse 9: Compare 5:31–32. Christians understand this saying in different ways. The Catholic Church holds that marriage cannot be dissolved. Protestant and Orthodox churches allow divorce and a new marriage under certain circumstances.
> Very important: No one has to stay in a marriage in which he or she is hit, threatened or abused. Violence harms everything marriage is meant to be. Help is available: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. For domestic violence: US: 1-800-799-7233. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> People who are divorced are not “worse” Christians. Many have suffered a lot. God loves them.
> Verse 12: Eunuchs were men who could not father children, often because they had been mutilated. Jesus speaks about them with respect. “For the sake of the Kingdom of Heaven” probably means people who choose to stay unmarried in order to be there fully for God. It is not a call to hurt yourself.
> Jesus says: Not everyone can live like this. Marriage and staying single are both good ways.

## Jesus blesses the children (Verses 13–15)

[13] Then people brought little children to him,
so that he would lay his hands on them and pray.
But the disciples scolded them.
[14] But Jesus said,
“Let the little children come,
and don’t stop them from coming to me!
For the Kingdom of Heaven belongs to people like these.”
[15] And he laid his hands on them
and went on from there.

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples think children are a bother. Jesus sees it completely differently. He takes time for them and blesses them.
> Compare 18:1–5: Children have a special place with God.
> These verses are often read at baptisms and blessings of children.
> For today: Children should be welcome in the church, not just put up with.

## The rich young man (Verses 16–22)

[16] Someone came to him and said,
“Good teacher, what good thing shall I do
to have eternal life?”
[17] He said to him,
“Why do you call me good?
No one is good but one, that is, God.
But if you want to enter into life,
keep the commandments.”
[18] He asked him,
“Which ones?”
Jesus said,
“‘You shall not murder.’
‘You shall not commit adultery.’
‘You shall not steal.’
‘You shall not give false testimony.’
[19] ‘Honor your father and your mother.’
And, ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’”
[20] The young man said to him,
“I have kept all these things since I was young.
What do I still lack?”
[21] Jesus said to him,
“If you want to be perfect,
go,
sell what you have,
and give to the poor.
Then you will have treasure in heaven.
And come, follow me!”
[22] When the young man heard this,
he went away sad.
For he had great possessions.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: In the WEB the man asks: “Good teacher, what good thing shall I do,” and Jesus answers: “Why do you call me good?” Many old manuscripts have a slightly different form here: The man only says Teacher, and Jesus asks why he is asking him about what is good. The meaning is similar: Only God is truly good.
> Jesus names commandments from the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:12–16) and the commandment to love your neighbor (Leviticus 19:18). These are the commandments about life with other people.
> The young man is serious and devout. But he senses: Something is still missing.
> Jesus invites him to follow him. But his wealth holds him back. He goes away sad. Matthew does not tell what became of him later.
> “Perfect” here means: whole, undivided for God (compare 5:48).
> Christians understand verse 21 in different ways. Some, for example monks and nuns, really give up everything they own. Most Christians understand it this way: Nothing may be more important to us than God. Whoever has a lot should share generously with the poor.

## Riches and God’s Kingdom (Verses 23–30)

[23] Jesus said to his disciples,
“Most certainly I tell you,
it is hard for a rich person to enter the Kingdom of Heaven.
[24] Again I tell you,
it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle
than for a rich person to enter God’s Kingdom.”
[25] When the disciples heard this,
they were very shocked and said,
“Then who can be saved?”
[26] Jesus looked at them and said,
“With people this is impossible,
but with God all things are possible.”
[27] Then Peter answered,
“Look, we have left everything
and followed you.
What then will we have?”
[28] Jesus said to them,
“Most certainly I tell you,
you who have followed me,
in the renewal of the world,
when the Son of Man sits on the throne of his glory,
you will also sit on 12 thrones,
judging the 12 tribes of Israel.
[29] And everyone who has left houses,
or brothers or sisters,
or father or mother,
or wife or children,
or fields,
for my name’s sake,
will receive 100 times as much
and will inherit eternal life.
[30] But many who are first will be last,
and the last will be first.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: The camel was the largest animal people knew in Israel. The eye of a needle is the smallest hole. Jesus exaggerates on purpose so that people will remember it: This is impossible. Some explain that there was a small city gate called “the Needle’s Eye.” But there is no reliable evidence for this.
> The disciples are shocked. At that time many thought: Wealth is a sign of God’s blessing. If even the rich have a hard time, then who can be saved?
> Verse 26: The answer is a comfort: No one can save himself, whether poor or rich. God can. Salvation is a gift.
> Verse 28: “Renewal” (in the WEB: “regeneration”) means the new world that God will create at the end. The 12 disciples match the 12 tribes of Israel. The word “judging” here probably also means “leading” and “ruling,” as with the judges in the Old Testament.
> Verse 29: Whoever gives something up for Jesus finds a new, large family in the church. That does not mean you should abandon your family. In 15:4–6 Jesus stressed how important it is to care for your parents.
> Verse 30: With God, different standards apply. Whoever counts for little here below can be right at the front with God. The next chapter tells a parable about this.
