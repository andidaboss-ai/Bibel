# Matthew – Chapter 20
### The last will be first

---

## The workers in the vineyard (Verses 1–16)

[1] “For the Kingdom of Heaven is like the owner of a house.
He went out early in the morning
to hire workers for his vineyard.
[2] He agreed with the workers
on one denarius for the day
and sent them into his vineyard.
[3] About the third hour he went out again
and saw others standing in the marketplace
who had no work.
[4] He said to them,
‘You go into the vineyard too.
I will give you whatever is right.’
So they went.
[5] About the sixth and the ninth hour
he went out again
and did the same.
[6] About the eleventh hour he went out
and found still others standing around with no work.
He said to them,
‘Why are you standing here all day doing nothing?’
[7] They said to him,
‘Because no one has hired us.’
He said to them,
‘You go into the vineyard too,
and you will receive whatever is right.’
[8] When evening came,
the owner of the vineyard said to his manager,
‘Call the workers and pay them their wages.
Begin with the last and end with the first.’
[9] Those who had started at about the eleventh hour came.
Each of them received a denarius.
[10] When the first ones came,
they thought they would receive more.
But each of them also received a denarius.
[11] When they received it,
they grumbled against the owner of the house.
[12] They said,
‘These last ones have worked only one hour,
and you have made them equal to us,
who have carried the burden of the day and the scorching heat!’
[13] But he answered one of them,
‘Friend, I am doing you no wrong.
Didn’t you agree with me on one denarius?
[14] Take what belongs to you and go!
I want to give this last one just as much as you.
[15] Isn’t it lawful for me to do what I want with what belongs to me?
Or is your eye evil because I am good?’
[16] So the last will be first,
and the first will be last.
For many are called,
but few are chosen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> A denarius was the usual wage for a day of work. A family could live on it for one day.
> At that time the hours were counted from sunrise, at about 6 in the morning. So the third hour is about 9 in the morning, the sixth about 12 noon, the ninth about 3 in the afternoon, and the eleventh about 5 in the afternoon. The last ones worked only one more hour.
> Day laborers stood in the marketplace in the morning and waited for work. Whoever found none had no money for food in the evening. The last ones were not lazy. No one had hired them.
> The owner gives everyone what they need to live. The first ones receive exactly what was agreed. No wrong is done to them. But they are annoyed because the owner is generous to others.
> “Is your eye evil” is an old way of speaking about envy.
> The message: God’s goodness is not like wages that you earn. It is a gift. Whoever finds God late is just as welcome to him as someone who has always been there.
> For today: We should be glad when God does good to others, instead of being envious. And the parable also shows: People without work are not worth less.
> Verse 16: The sentence “For many are called, but few are chosen” is missing here in many old manuscripts. It is certainly found in 22:14. The WEB has it here too.

## Jesus announces his suffering a third time (Verses 17–19)

[17] As Jesus was going up to Jerusalem,
he took the 12 disciples aside.
On the way he said to them,
[18] “We are going up to Jerusalem.
There the Son of Man
will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes.
They will condemn him to death
[19] and hand him over to the Gentiles.
They will mock him, whip him and crucify him.
And on the third day he will be raised up.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the third and most exact announcement (compare 16:21 and 17:22–23). For the first time Jesus says: He will be crucified.
> “The Gentiles” here are the Romans. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. According to John 18:31, the Jewish leaders at that time were not allowed to have anyone executed themselves. That was decided by the Romans.
> Important: Jesus names certain leaders, not the Jewish people. Blaming “the Jews” for Jesus’ death is wrong and has led to terrible injustice in history.
> Again the announcement ends with hope: He will be raised up.

## The request of the mother of James and John (Verses 20–28)

[20] Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came to him
with her sons.
She knelt down in front of him
and wanted to ask him for something.
[21] He said to her,
“What do you want?”
She said to him,
“Command that these two sons of mine may sit
next to you in your Kingdom,
one on your right and one on your left.”
[22] But Jesus answered,
“You don’t know what you are asking.
Are you able to drink the cup
that I am about to drink,
and be baptized with the baptism
that I am baptized with?”
They said to him,
“We are able.”
[23] He said to them,
“You will indeed drink my cup,
and be baptized with the baptism
that I am baptized with.
But the places on my right and on my left
are not mine to give.
They belong to those
for whom my Father has prepared them.”
[24] When the other 10 heard this,
they were angry with the two brothers.
[25] But Jesus called them to himself and said,
“You know
that the rulers of the nations lord it over them,
and their great ones use their power over them.
[26] It shall not be so among you!
But whoever wants to become great among you
shall be your servant.
[27] And whoever wants to be first among you
shall be your slave,
[28] just as the Son of Man did not come
to be served,
but to serve
and to give his life
as a ransom for many.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The sons of Zebedee are James and John (4:21). Their mother asks for the places of honor for them. The sons are standing there and obviously want this too.
> The “cup” is a picture from the Old Testament for a hard fate that God sends (for example Isaiah 51:17). Jesus means his suffering. The “baptism” is also a picture for suffering here: like going under deep water.
> The words about baptism (in verses 22 and 23) are missing in many old manuscripts. Something similar is found in Mark 10:38–39. The WEB has them.
> Verse 23: James was later actually killed because of his faith (Acts 12:2).
> Verse 25: The rulers “lord it over” the nations. That means: Powerful people rule from above and take advantage of their power.
> Verses 26–28: With Jesus it is the other way around. Great is the one who serves others. Jesus himself is the example.
> “Ransom”: At that time people paid money to buy prisoners or slaves free. Jesus gives his life to set people free. In Hebrew ways of speaking, “for many” means: for the great crowd, for all. This reminds us of Isaiah 53:11–12.
> For today: In the church and everywhere, no one should misuse their power. Whoever leads should serve.

## Two blind men near Jericho (Verses 29–34)

[29] As they were leaving Jericho,
a large crowd followed him.
[30] Two blind men were sitting by the road.
When they heard that Jesus was passing by,
they cried out,
“Lord, have mercy on us, you son of David!”
[31] The crowd scolded them
and told them to be quiet.
But they cried out even louder,
“Lord, have mercy on us, you son of David!”
[32] Jesus stood still,
called them
and asked,
“What do you want me to do for you?”
[33] They said to him,
“Lord, that our eyes may be opened.”
[34] Jesus was moved with compassion for them
and touched their eyes.
At once they could see again,
and they followed him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jericho lies in the Jordan Valley. From there the road goes up to Jerusalem.
> The crowd wants to silence the blind men. They are a bother. But they will not be stopped.
> Jesus stands still. He asks: “What do you want me to do for you?” He takes them seriously and lets them say for themselves what they need. That is an attitude of respect.
> Just before this the disciples had asked for places of honor (verse 21). The blind men ask for mercy. Jesus helps them, and they follow him.
> In Mark 10:46–52 and Luke 18:35–43 we are told about one blind man (in Mark his name is Bartimaeus). Matthew tells about two blind men. The Gospels tell some stories a little differently.
> Important: Blindness is not a punishment. People with disabilities should not be silenced, but heard.
