# Matthew – Chapter 26
### The Last Supper and the night of the betrayal

---

## The plan against Jesus (Verses 1–5)

[1] When Jesus had finished all these speeches,
he said to his disciples,
[2] “You know that in two days it will be the Passover.
Then the Son of Man will be handed over
to be crucified.”
[3] Then the chief priests, the scribes,
and the elders of the people gathered
in the courtyard of the high priest,
who was called Caiaphas.
[4] They made plans together
about how they could arrest Jesus by a trick
and kill him.
[5] But they said,
“Not during the feast,
so that there won’t be a riot among the people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover (in Hebrew “Pesach”) is one of the most important Jewish feasts. It recalls how God freed the people of Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 12). To this day Jews celebrate it every year in the spring.
> Caiaphas was high priest from about AD 18 to 36. He was the highest priest at the temple and worked closely with the Romans.
> Important: Here a small group of leaders makes plans against Jesus. The people are more on Jesus’ side. That is why the leaders are afraid of a riot. It is wrong to blame “the Jews” for the death of Jesus.

## Jesus is anointed in Bethany (Verses 6–13)

[6] When Jesus was in Bethany,
in the house of Simon the leper,
[7] a woman came to him.
She had an alabaster jar
of very expensive ointment.
She poured it on his head
as he was sitting at the table.
[8] When his disciples saw this,
they were angry and said,
“Why this waste?
[9] This ointment could have been sold for a lot of money,
and the money could have been given to the poor.”
[10] Jesus knew this and said to them,
“Why are you troubling the woman?
She has done a good thing for me.
[11] For you always have the poor with you,
but you don’t always have me.
[12] When she poured this ointment on my body,
she did it to prepare me for my burial.
[13] Most certainly I tell you,
wherever this Good News is preached
in the whole world,
what this woman has done will also be told
in memory of her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Simon “the leper” was perhaps a man whom Jesus had healed. Matthew does not explain this.
> Alabaster is a light-colored, soft stone. Precious jars for ointment were made from it.
> Ointment: a fragrant oil that was rubbed or poured on the body.
> The woman anoints Jesus like a king. In the Old Testament kings were anointed with oil. “Christ” means “the Anointed One.”
> The disciples think it is a waste. Jesus defends the woman. She has understood what the disciples do not yet understand: Jesus will soon die.
> Verse 11: Jesus is not saying that the poor are unimportant. He is quoting Deuteronomy 15:11. There it goes on to say: That is why you shall open your hand to the poor. Jesus means: You can always help the poor, and you should.
> Verse 13: Jesus promises that this woman will be remembered everywhere. Matthew does not give her name. In John 12:3 it is Mary of Bethany.

## Judas wants to betray Jesus (Verses 14–16)

[14] Then one of the 12 disciples,
who was called Judas Iscariot,
went to the chief priests
[15] and said,
“What are you willing to give me
if I hand him over to you?”
They weighed out 30 pieces of silver for him.
[16] From then on he looked for a good opportunity
to betray him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Matthew does not say exactly why Judas betrayed Jesus. Here it is about money. Other reasons have been suggested, but they are not certain.
> 30 pieces of silver remind us of Zechariah 11:12. There it is the wage with which the shepherd is fobbed off. According to Exodus 21:32 it was the price of a slave.
> Very important: In many languages the name “Judas” sounds like the word for “Jew.” For centuries Christians used Judas as a picture for “the Jews.” That was wrong and caused great injustice. All 12 disciples were Jews, Peter too, John too. And Jesus himself was a Jew.

## The last Passover meal (Verses 17–25)

[17] On the first day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread,
the disciples came to Jesus and asked him,
“Where do you want us to prepare the Passover meal for you?”
[18] He said,
“Go into the city to a certain man
and tell him,
‘The Teacher says,
“My time is near.
I will celebrate the Passover at your house with my disciples.”’”
[19] The disciples did as Jesus had told them
and prepared the Passover meal.
[20] When evening came,
he was reclining at the table with the 12 disciples.
[21] While they were eating, he said,
“Most certainly I tell you,
one of you will betray me.”
[22] They became very sad,
and each one began to ask him,
“It isn’t me, is it, Lord?”
[23] He answered,
“The one who dipped his hand into the dish with me
will betray me.
[24] The Son of Man is going his way,
as it is written about him.
But how terrible it will be for that man
through whom the Son of Man is betrayed!
It would be better for that man
if he had not been born.”
[25] Then Judas, who betrayed him, answered,
“It isn’t me, is it, Rabbi?”
He said to him,
“You said it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Feast of Unleavened Bread is part of the Passover. For one week people eat bread without yeast (matzah), in memory of the hurried departure from Egypt.
> Unleavened: made without yeast, so the bread stays flat.
> At festive meals in those days people reclined on cushions around a low table.
> Jesus celebrates the Passover meal with his disciples, as devout Jews do. There is an order with prayers, bread, wine and songs.
> Verse 22: All the disciples ask: “It isn’t me, is it, Lord?” Each one knows that he can be weak.
> Verse 23: Everyone dipped their bread into the same dish. Eating together was a sign of friendship and trust. That makes the betrayal all the worse.
> Verse 25: Judas calls Jesus “Rabbi” (teacher), not “Lord” like the others.

## The Lord’s Supper (Verses 26–30)

[26] While they were eating,
Jesus took bread,
gave thanks to God for it,
and broke it.
He gave it to the disciples and said,
“Take, eat;
this is my body.”
[27] Then he took the cup,
gave thanks,
gave it to them, and said,
“All of you drink from it,
[28] for this is my blood of the new covenant,
which is poured out for many
for the forgiveness of sins.
[29] But I tell you,
from now on I will not drink of this fruit of the vine
until that day
when I drink it new with you
in my Father’s Kingdom.”
[30] When they had sung a hymn,
they went out to the Mount of Olives.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins what Christians still celebrate today: the Lord’s Supper, also called the Eucharist or Holy Communion. All Christian churches remember these words.
> Jesus takes bread and wine from the Passover meal and gives them a new meaning: His body and his blood are given for people.
> Christians understand this in different ways. Catholic and Orthodox Christians believe that the bread and wine really become the body and blood of Christ. Lutheran Christians believe that Christ is present in, with and under the bread and wine. Reformed and many other Protestant Christians understand it more as a sign and a remembrance, in which Christ is spiritually present.
> Covenant: a binding agreement, a promise of faithfulness between God and people.
> Verse 28: “My blood of the new covenant” reminds us of Exodus 24:8. There God made a covenant with Israel at Mount Sinai. The new covenant reminds us of Jeremiah 31:31–34. The word “new” is missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has it.
> Important: The new covenant does not cancel God’s covenant with Israel. God stays faithful to his people (Romans 11:29).
> In Hebrew ways of speaking, “for many” means: for the great multitude, for all (compare 20:28).
> Verse 30: At the Passover meal people still sing psalms today, the “Hallel psalms” (Psalms 113–118). This hymn probably means them.

## Jesus predicts that Peter will deny him (Verses 31–35)

[31] Then Jesus said to them,
“Tonight all of you will fall away because of me.
For it is written,
‘I will strike the shepherd,
and the sheep of the flock will be scattered.’
[32] But after I am raised up,
I will go ahead of you into Galilee.”
[33] But Peter answered him,
“Even if all fall away because of you,
I will never fall away.”
[34] Jesus said to him,
“Most certainly I tell you,
tonight,
before the rooster crows,
you will deny me three times.”
[35] Peter said to him,
“Even if I must die with you,
I will not deny you.”
All the other disciples said the same.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31 quotes Zechariah 13:7.
> Deny: to say that you don’t know someone or don’t belong to him.
> Jesus knows: His friends will leave him alone. But he already gives them a promise now: After the resurrection we will meet again in Galilee. He does not give up on them.
> Peter means it honestly. But he overestimates himself.

## Jesus prays in Gethsemane (Verses 36–46)

[36] Then Jesus came with them to a place
called Gethsemane.
He said to the disciples,
“Sit here,
while I go over there and pray.”
[37] He took Peter and the two sons of Zebedee with him.
And he began to be sad
and deeply troubled.
[38] Then Jesus said to them,
“My soul is so sad that I could die.
Stay here and keep watch with me!”
[39] He went a little farther,
fell with his face to the ground, and prayed,
“My Father,
if it is possible,
let this cup pass away from me.
But not what I want,
but what you want.”
[40] He came back to the disciples
and found them sleeping.
He said to Peter,
“Couldn’t you keep watch with me for one hour?
[41] Watch and pray,
so that you don’t fall into temptation!
The spirit indeed is willing,
but the flesh is weak.”
[42] Again he went away,
a second time,
and prayed,
“My Father,
if this cup can’t pass away from me
unless I drink it,
your will be done.”
[43] He came again
and found them sleeping,
for their eyes were heavy.
[44] He left them,
went away again,
and prayed a third time,
saying the same words.
[45] Then he came to his disciples and said to them,
“Are you still sleeping and resting?
The hour has come,
and the Son of Man is betrayed into the hands of sinners.
[46] Get up, let’s go!
The one who betrays me is here.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gethsemane means “oil press.” It was a garden with olive trees on the Mount of Olives.
> Here we see Jesus as completely human. He is very afraid and deeply sad. He asks his friends to stay with him.
> The “cup” is a picture of the heavy suffering that lies ahead of him (compare 20:22).
> Jesus prays honestly: “If it is possible, let this cup pass away from me.” But he entrusts himself to God: “Your will be done” – as in the Lord’s Prayer (6:10).
> For today: Jesus, too, was afraid. Fear and despair are not signs of weak faith. You may tell God everything. Whoever has great fear or dark thoughts should not stay alone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 41: The disciples want to stay awake, but they can’t manage it. Jesus is disappointed, but he understands their weakness.

## Jesus is arrested (Verses 47–56)

[47] While he was still speaking,
Judas, one of the 12 disciples, came.
With him came a large crowd with swords and clubs,
sent by the chief priests and the elders of the people.
[48] The betrayer had given them a sign:
“The one I kiss is the man.
Arrest him!”
[49] At once he went to Jesus and said,
“Greetings, Rabbi!”
And he kissed him.
[50] Jesus said to him,
“Friend, why are you here?”
Then they came,
laid hands on Jesus,
and arrested him.
[51] And one of those who were with Jesus
stretched out his hand,
drew his sword,
struck the servant of the high priest,
and cut off his ear.
[52] Then Jesus said to him,
“Put your sword back into its place!
For all those who take the sword
will die by the sword.
[53] Or do you think
that I couldn’t ask my Father,
and he would at once send me more than 12 legions of angels?
[54] But then how would the Scriptures be fulfilled
that say it must happen this way?”
[55] At that hour Jesus said to the crowd,
“Have you come out with swords and clubs to arrest me,
as if I were a robber?
Every day I sat in the temple teaching,
and you didn’t arrest me.
[56] But all this has happened
so that the Scriptures of the prophets would be fulfilled.”
Then all the disciples left him and fled.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days a kiss was a normal greeting among friends and from students to their teacher. Judas misuses this sign of love.
> Even so, Jesus calls Judas “Friend.”
> Verse 51: John 18:10 tells us that it was Peter.
> Verse 52 is a very important word: Jesus forbids violence, even to defend himself. “All those who take the sword will die by the sword.” Violence brings new violence. No one may use violence in the name of Jesus.
> Verse 53: A Roman legion had about 5,000 to 6,000 soldiers. Jesus could defend himself, but he does not. He goes this way of his own free will.
> Verse 56: All the disciples flee. Jesus is completely alone.

## Jesus before the Council (Verses 57–68)

[57] Those who had arrested Jesus
led him to Caiaphas the high priest.
The scribes and the elders had gathered there.
[58] But Peter followed him at a distance
as far as the courtyard of the high priest.
He went in
and sat down with the guards
to see how it would end.
[59] The chief priests, the elders, and the whole council
were looking for false testimony against Jesus,
so that they could put him to death.
[60] But they found none,
even though many false witnesses came forward.
At last two false witnesses came forward
[61] and said,
“This man said,
‘I am able to destroy the temple of God
and to build it again in three days.’”
[62] Then the high priest stood up and said to him,
“Have you no answer?
What is this that these men are saying against you?”
[63] But Jesus stayed silent.
Then the high priest said to him,
“I command you by the living God:
Tell us whether you are the Christ, the Son of God!”
[64] Jesus said to him,
“You have said so.
But I tell you,
from now on you will see the Son of Man
sitting at the right hand of Power
and coming on the clouds of the sky.”
[65] Then the high priest tore his clothes and said,
“He has spoken blasphemy!
Why do we need any more witnesses?
Look, now you have heard his blasphemy.
[66] What do you think?”
They answered,
“He deserves to die!”
[67] Then they spat in his face
and beat him with their fists.
Others slapped him
[68] and said,
“Prophesy to us, you Christ!
Who hit you?”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Council (in Greek “Synedrion,” in Hebrew “Sanhedrin”) was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem. It was made up of priests, elders and scribes.
> Matthew tells us that the trial was not fair: They looked for false witnesses. According to the Law of Moses, two witnesses who agree are needed (Deuteronomy 19:15).
> Verse 63: Jesus stays silent. That reminds us of Isaiah 53:7: Like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, he did not open his mouth.
> Verse 64: Jesus combines Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13. “Power” is a respectful way of speaking about God. Out of reverence, Jews often avoid saying the name of God.
> Blasphemy: speaking against God or insulting God.
> Verse 65: Tearing one’s clothes was a sign of great horror and grief.
> Very important: Here a particular group of leaders acts on a particular night. The Jewish people as a whole were not involved. In 1965 the Catholic Church declared in the council document *Nostra Aetate*: The suffering of Jesus may not be blamed on all the Jews living then, nor on the Jews of today. Protestant churches have also said this clearly.
> Verses 67–68: Jesus is mocked and beaten. He does not fight back.

## Peter denies Jesus (Verses 69–75)

[69] Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard.
A servant girl came to him and said,
“You were also with Jesus, the Galilean!”
[70] But he denied it in front of everyone and said,
“I don’t know what you are talking about.”
[71] When he had gone out onto the porch,
someone else saw him
and said to the people who were there,
“This man also was with Jesus of Nazareth.”
[72] Again he denied it
with an oath:
“I don’t know the man!”
[73] A little while later the people standing there came
and said to Peter,
“Surely you are also one of them,
for the way you speak gives you away.”
[74] Then he began to curse and to swear,
“I don’t know the man!”
And at once the rooster crowed.
[75] Then Peter remembered the word
that Jesus had said to him:
“Before the rooster crows,
you will deny me three times.”
And he went out and wept bitterly.

> **What does this mean?**
> While inside Jesus bravely tells the truth, outside Peter says three times: I don’t know him.
> Verse 71: The WEB says someone else. In the Greek text it is another servant girl.
> Verse 73: Peter came from Galilee. The people there spoke with a different sound than the people in Jerusalem, just as there are accents today.
> Verse 74: Peter begins “to curse and to swear.” That means: He insists by every means that he has nothing to do with Jesus.
> Verse 75: Peter weeps bitterly. He sees his failure.
> For today: Peter, the rock (16:18), has fallen deeply. But the story goes on. Jesus did not give up on him (compare 26:32; 28:7; John 21:15–19). Even someone who has failed can make a new start. Whoever feels very bad because of their own guilt may talk with someone about it. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
