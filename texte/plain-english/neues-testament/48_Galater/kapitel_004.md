# Galatians – Chapter 4
### Children of God, not slaves

---

## God sent his Son (Verses 1–7)

[1] But I say:
As long as the heir is a child,
he is no different from a slave,
though he is lord of all.
[2] He is under guardians and managers
until the day
set by the father.
[3] So we also,
when we were children,
were held as slaves under the elemental powers of the world.
[4] But when the fullness of the time came,
God sent his Son,
born to a woman,
born under the law,
[5] so that he might redeem those
who were under the law,
so that we might be adopted as children.
[6] And because you are children,
God sent the Spirit of his Son into your hearts.
He cries out:
“Abba, Father!”
[7] So you are no longer a slave,
but a son.
And if you are a son,
then you are also an heir of God through Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Elemental powers means: powers that people felt dependent on.
> Adopted means: taken into a family as a child.
> Verses 1–2: A child who will one day receive a large inheritance has no say yet while still a child. That is how people were before Jesus came.
> Verse 3: “Elemental powers of the world”: This probably means powers that people felt dependent on, for example stars, gods or fixed religious rules. Interpreters understand the word in different ways.
> Verse 4: “Born to a woman, born under the law”: Jesus was a real human being and a Jew who lived according to the Torah.
> Verse 6: “Abba” is Aramaic and means *Father*, the way a child lovingly speaks to their father (compare Romans 8:15).
> Verse 7: The WEB says “son.” This means all Christians, women and men. In chapter 3:28 Paul said: There is neither male nor female.

---

## Don’t go back into slavery (Verses 8–11)

[8] But at that time,
when you didn’t know God,
you were slaves to gods
that by nature are not gods.
[9] But now you know God,
or rather, God knows you.
How can you turn back again to the weak and miserable elemental powers?
Do you want to be their slaves all over again?
[10] You observe days, months, seasons and years.
[11] I am afraid for you,
that I may have worked hard for you in vain.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: The Galatians used to worship the gods of their peoples.
> Verse 10: The Galatians thought they had to keep certain feast days and times in order to belong to God. Paul says: That doesn’t make you more righteous before God.
> Important: Paul is not speaking badly about the Jewish feasts here. For Jews, the Sabbath and the feasts are a gift from God. Paul is only against Christians from the nations seeing such rules as a condition for salvation.

## Paul’s worry for the Galatians (Verses 12–20)

[12] I beg you, brothers:
Become as I am,
for I also have become as you are.
You did me no wrong.
[13] But you know
that I preached the Good News to you the first time
because I was weak in body.
[14] My weakness was a test for you.
But you didn’t despise me
or reject me.
Instead you welcomed me
like an angel of God,
even like Christ Jesus.
[15] What has happened to your joy?
For I testify to you:
If it had been possible,
you would have torn out your eyes
and given them to me.
[16] So have I become your enemy
by telling you the truth?
[17] They are eager for you,
but not for a good purpose.
They want to separate you from me,
so that you will be eager for them.
[18] But it is always good
to be eager for a good cause,
and not only
when I am with you.
[19] My little children!
I am again in the pains of childbirth for you
until Christ is formed in you.
[20] I wish
I could be with you now
and change my tone.
For I am at a loss about you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–15: Paul came to Galatia because of an illness. Even so, the Galatians welcomed him warmly. Verse 15 makes some people think that Paul had an eye disease.
> For today: People with illness or disability deserve respect and welcome, just as the Galatians welcomed Paul.
> Verse 19: Paul compares himself to a mother in the pains of childbirth. He worries about the Galatians as a mother worries about her child.

## The picture of Hagar and Sarah (Verses 21–31)

[21] Tell me,
you
who want to be under the law:
Don’t you listen to
what the law says?
[22] For it is written
that Abraham had two sons:
one by the servant woman
and one by the free woman.
[23] The son of the servant woman was born in the natural way,
but the son of the free woman was born through the promise.
[24] These things are used as a picture.
For the two women stand for two covenants.
One covenant is from Mount Sinai
and bears children into slavery.
That is Hagar.
[25] For Hagar stands for Mount Sinai in Arabia.
She corresponds to the present Jerusalem,
for she is in slavery with her children.
[26] But the Jerusalem above is free.
She is the mother of us all.
[27] For it is written:
“Rejoice,
you barren woman
who doesn’t bear children.
Break out and shout,
you
who don’t have labor pains.
For the lonely woman has more children
than the one
who has a husband.”
[28] Now you, brothers,
like Isaac, are children of the promise.
[29] But just as back then the one
who was born in the natural way
persecuted the one
who was born by the Spirit,
so it is now too.
[30] But what does the Scripture say?
“Throw out the servant woman and her son,
for the son of the servant woman will not inherit with the son of the free woman.”
[31] So, brothers,
we are not children of the servant woman,
but of the free woman.

> **What does this mean?**
> Barren means: not able to have children.
> Verses 21–31: Paul interprets the story of Hagar and Sarah (Genesis 16 and 21) as a picture. This is an unusual interpretation. Paul uses it in his argument with the Christian teachers in Galatia, who demanded circumcision from Christians from the nations.
> Very important: This text is not a judgment on the Jewish people or on Judaism. It must not be read as if Jews were *children of the servant woman* and Christians *children of the free woman*. This interpretation has caused much harm in church history. Paul says clearly in the letter to the Romans: God has not rejected his people (Romans 11:1–2).
> It is also important: In the Bible God himself cares for Hagar and her son Ishmael. He hears their crying, rescues them and blesses Ishmael (Genesis 16:7–13; 21:17–20). Hagar is the first person in the Bible who gives God a name.
> Verse 27 quotes Isaiah 54:1. Verse 30 quotes Genesis 21:10.
> Verse 30: This sentence is no call to shut people out or to drive them away.
