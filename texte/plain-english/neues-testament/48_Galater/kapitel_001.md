# Galatians – Chapter 1
### There is only one Good News

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Galatians

Galatia was a region and a Roman province in what is now Turkey. Paul had founded churches there. Most Christians there did not come from Judaism, but from other nations.
After Paul, other Christian teachers came to Galatia. They said: Whoever wants to belong to Christ must also be circumcised and keep the Jewish law. Paul disagrees passionately. He says: People from all nations belong to God through faith in Christ, without first having to become Jews.
The letter was probably written between AD 50 and 55. It is one of Paul’s oldest letters.
**A very important note:** In the letter to the Galatians, Christians argue with Christians. The question is what people from other nations must do when they become Christians. The letter is not an attack on Judaism. Paul was a Jew himself and remained one. Sadly, this letter has often been used in history to speak badly of Judaism, as if Jewish faith consisted only of rules. That is false and unjust. For Jews the Torah is still a gift from God and a way of life today.
The letter to the Galatians has 6 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–5)

[1] Paul,
an apostle,
not sent by people
or through a human being,
but through Jesus Christ
and God the Father,
who raised him from the dead,
[2] and all the brothers
who are with me,
write to the churches of Galatia:
[3] Grace to you and peace
from God the Father
and our Lord Jesus Christ.
[4] He gave himself for our sins,
to rescue us out of this present evil age,
according to the will of our God and Father.
[5] To him be the glory forever and ever.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Right at the beginning Paul stresses: My task doesn’t come from people, but directly from God. Some in Galatia doubted that.
> Verse 4: Jesus gave his life for us. He rescues us out of a world in which evil still has power.

---

## No other Good News (Verses 6–10)

[6] I am amazed
that you are so quickly turning away
from him
who called you in the grace of Christ,
to a different “good news.”
[7] But there isn’t another “good news.”
There are only some
who confuse you
and want to twist the Good News of Christ.
[8] But even if we
or an angel from heaven
should preach to you any “good news”
other than the one
that we preached to you,
let him be cursed.
[9] As we have said before,
so I now say again:
If anyone preaches to you any “good news”
other than the one
that you received,
let him be cursed.
[10] Am I now seeking the approval of people,
or of God?
Or am I trying to please people?
If I were still trying to please people,
I wouldn’t be a servant of Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paul writes this letter without the thanksgiving that usually comes at the beginning. He is upset and worried.
> Verses 6–7: For Paul the Good News is clear: God accepts people by grace. Whoever adds extra conditions changes this message.
> Verses 8–9: “Cursed” is a very harsh word. It is a fixed formula that leaves the judgment to God. It is no permission to persecute people or to harm them.

---

## How Paul was called (Verses 11–24)

[11] Brothers,
I make known to you:
The Good News
that I preached
does not come from people.
[12] For I didn’t receive it from a human being,
and no one taught it to me.
But it came to me through a revelation of Jesus Christ.
[13] For you have heard
how I used to live in the Jewish religion.
I persecuted the church of God beyond measure
and tried to destroy it.
[14] In the Jewish religion I was ahead of many of my own age among my people.
I was extremely zealous
for the traditions of my ancestors.
[15] But God had set me apart from my mother’s womb
and called me through his grace.
When it pleased him
[16] to reveal his Son in me,
so that I might preach him among the Gentiles,
I didn’t immediately ask any human being for advice.
[17] Nor did I go up to Jerusalem
to those
who were apostles before me.
Instead I went away into Arabia
and then returned to Damascus.
[18] Only after three years did I go up to Jerusalem
to get to know Peter.
I stayed with him 15 days.
[19] I saw none of the other apostles,
except James,
the Lord’s brother.
[20] About the things I write to you,
I say before God:
I am not lying.
[21] Then I came to the regions of Syria and Cilicia.
[22] The churches of Judea
that belong to Christ
still didn’t know me personally.
[23] They only heard:
“He who once persecuted us
now preaches the faith
that he once tried to destroy.”
[24] And they praised God because of me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Revelation means: something God shows.
> Verses 13–14: Paul was a very zealous Jew. He persecuted the Christians because he thought he had to defend God’s honor.
> Verses 15–16: Paul describes his calling with words that recall the calling of the prophets (for example Jeremiah 1:5 and Isaiah 49:1). He doesn’t see himself as someone who changed his religion, but as someone whom God gave the task of telling the nations about Jesus.
> Verse 17: “Arabia” probably means the area east and south of Damascus, the kingdom of the Nabataeans.
> Verses 18–19: Paul met Peter and James, the brother of Jesus. The churches understand “brother” in different ways (compare 1 Corinthians 9:5).
> Verses 23–24: The churches praised God because the persecutor became a preacher. God can change people.
