# Galatians – Chapter 5
### Called to freedom

---

## Stay in freedom (Verses 1–12)

[1] So stand firm in the freedom
for which Christ has set us free.
And don’t let yourselves be tied again to a yoke of slavery.
[2] Listen,
I, Paul,
tell you:
If you let yourselves be circumcised,
Christ will be of no use to you.
[3] Yes, I testify again to every man
who lets himself be circumcised:
He is obliged
to keep the whole law.
[4] You are cut off from Christ,
you
who want to be made righteous by the law.
You have fallen away from grace.
[5] For through the Spirit,
by faith,
we wait for the righteousness
that we hope for.
[6] For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision
nor uncircumcision counts for anything,
but faith working through love.
[7] You were running well!
Who got in your way,
so that you don’t obey the truth?
[8] This persuasion doesn’t come from him
who calls you.
[9] A little yeast works through the whole lump of dough.
[10] I am confident in the Lord
that you will not think any other way.
But whoever is troubling you
will bear his judgment,
whoever he is.
[11] But as for me, brothers:
If I still preached circumcision,
why am I still persecuted?
Then the offense of the cross would be removed.
[12] I wish
that those
who are troubling you
would cut themselves off completely!

> **What does this mean?**
> Yoke means: a wooden bar put on animals so they can pull loads; here a picture for a heavy burden.
> Verse 1: Christ has set us free. Christians should not make themselves dependent again on rules by which they want to earn salvation.
> Verses 2–4: Here Paul is speaking to Christians from the nations in Galatia. They thought they also had to be circumcised in order to truly belong to God. Paul says: Whoever does that no longer relies on Christ.
> Important: These verses say nothing against the circumcision of Jewish boys. For the Jewish people circumcision is still a sign of the covenant with God today (Genesis 17). Paul himself was circumcised. He also had Timothy circumcised (Acts 16:3).
> Verse 6: A key sentence: What matters is “faith working through love.”
> Verse 12: Here Paul is very angry and uses a crude, biting play on words about circumcision. It shows his anger, but it is no call to harm anyone.

---

## Freedom that serves in love (Verses 13–15)

[13] For you, brothers,
were called to freedom.
Only don’t use your freedom as an excuse for the flesh,
but through love serve one another.
[14] For the whole law is fulfilled in one word,
in this:
“You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
[15] But if you bite and devour one another,
be careful
that you don’t destroy one another.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Freedom doesn’t mean that you can do whatever you want without caring about others. True freedom serves others out of love.
> Verse 14 quotes Leviticus 19:18. The commandment to love your neighbor is in the Torah. Paul says: The whole law is summed up in it.
> Verse 15: Quarrels in the church can destroy everything.

---

## Spirit and flesh (Verses 16–26)

[16] But I say:
Walk by the Spirit,
and you won’t carry out the desires of the flesh.
[17] For the flesh desires what is against the Spirit,
and the Spirit what is against the flesh.
The two are opposed to each other,
so that you don’t simply do
what you want.
[18] But if you are led by the Spirit,
you are not under the law.
[19] The deeds of the flesh are obvious.
They are:
adultery,
sexual immorality,
uncleanness,
wild lust,
[20] idolatry,
sorcery,
hatred,
strife,
jealousy,
outbursts of anger,
rivalries,
divisions,
factions,
[21] envy,
murders,
drunkenness,
wild parties
and things like these.
I warn you,
as I warned you before:
Those who do such things
will not inherit God’s Kingdom.
[22] But the fruit of the Spirit is
love,
joy,
peace,
patience,
kindness,
goodness,
faith,
[23] gentleness
and self-control.
Against such things there is no law.
[24] Those who belong to Christ
have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.
[25] If we live by the Spirit,
let’s also walk by the Spirit.
[26] Let’s not become conceited,
provoking one another
and envying one another.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sorcery means: magic used to harm or control others.
> Verses 16–17: “Flesh” here doesn’t mean the body, but a life that only looks at itself. “Spirit” means a life led by God’s Spirit.
> Verses 19–21: Paul lists what destroys community. Many points concern how people live together: hatred, strife, envy, divisions.
> Verse 20: “Sorcery” here probably means magical practices used to harm others or to gain power over them.
> Verses 22–23: “The fruit of the Spirit”: These qualities grow in a person when God’s Spirit works in them, like fruit on a tree. You don’t have to *make* them by your own strength.
> Verse 22: The WEB says “faith.” The Greek word can mean *faith* or *faithfulness*. Many interpreters think that in this list it mainly means faithfulness.
> Verse 24: “Crucified the flesh” is a picture. It does not mean that you should hurt yourself. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
