# Galatians – Chapter 3
### Children of Abraham through faith

---

## How did you receive the Spirit? (Verses 1–5)

[1] Foolish Galatians!
Who has bewitched you,
so that you no longer obey the truth?
Jesus Christ was clearly portrayed before your eyes
as crucified.
[2] I just want to learn this from you:
Did you receive the Spirit
by the works of the law,
or by hearing with faith?
[3] Are you so foolish?
You began in the Spirit.
Are you now going to finish in the flesh?
[4] Did you suffer so many things in vain?
If it really was in vain!
[5] God gives you the Spirit
and does miracles among you.
Does he do it because of the works of the law,
or because of hearing with faith?

> **What does this mean?**
> Bewitched means: put under a magic spell.
> Verse 1: “Bewitched” is a strong picture. Paul is shocked at how quickly the Galatians let themselves be persuaded otherwise.
> Verses 2–5: The Galatians received the Holy Spirit when they heard the Good News and believed, not because they had kept commandments. Why should they need something else now?
> Verse 3: “Finish in the flesh” here means: by your own human strength, for example through circumcision.

---

## Abraham believed God (Verses 6–14)

[6] It was the same with Abraham:
“He believed God,
and it was counted to him as righteousness.”
[7] So understand this:
Those who live by faith
are the children of Abraham.
[8] The Scripture foresaw
that God would make the Gentiles righteous by faith.
So it announced the Good News to Abraham beforehand:
“In you all the nations will be blessed.”
[9] So those
who live by faith
are blessed together with Abraham, the man of faith.
[10] For all
who rely on the works of the law
are under a curse.
For it is written:
“Cursed is everyone
who doesn’t continue in all things
that are written in the book of the law,
to do them.”
[11] Now it is clear
that no one is made righteous before God by the law.
For:
“The righteous will live by faith.”
[12] The law is not based on faith,
but:
“The man who does them
will live by them.”
[13] Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law
by becoming a curse for us.
For it is written:
“Cursed is everyone
who hangs on a tree.”
[14] So the blessing of Abraham was to come to the Gentiles
through Christ Jesus,
so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith.

> **What does this mean?**
> Redeem means: to buy free.
> Verse 6 quotes Genesis 15:6. Abraham trusted God long before there was the law at Sinai.
> Verse 8 quotes Genesis 12:3 (compare 18:18). Abraham was already told: Through him all nations are to be blessed.
> Verse 10 quotes Deuteronomy 27:26. Verse 11 quotes Habakkuk 2:4. Verse 12 quotes Leviticus 18:5. Verse 13 quotes Deuteronomy 21:23.
> Verses 10–13 are difficult verses. Paul wants to tell the Galatians: If you rely on the law instead of on Christ, you have to keep everything perfectly. Christ has opened this way for us.
> Important: Paul does not say that the Jewish people are under a curse. He is speaking to Christians from the nations who thought they also had to bind themselves to the law. These verses were often used against Jews. That was wrong.
> Verse 13: On the cross Jesus was treated like someone cursed. But in exactly this way he brought the blessing to all people.

---

## The law and the promise (Verses 15–25)

[15] Brothers,
I speak in human terms:
Even if it is only a human agreement,
no one can cancel it or add to it
once it has been confirmed.
[16] Now the promises were spoken to Abraham
and to his offspring.
It doesn’t say,
“To descendants,”
as if many were meant,
but as if one is meant:
“To your offspring.”
That is Christ.
[17] Now I mean this:
God confirmed a covenant beforehand in Christ.
The law,
which came 430 years later,
cannot cancel this covenant,
so that the promise would be made useless.
[18] For if the inheritance came from the law,
it would no longer come from the promise.
But God gave it to Abraham by a promise.
[19] Then why is there the law?
It was added because of wrongdoing,
until the offspring should come
to whom the promise was made.
It was put in place through angels,
by the hand of a go-between.
[20] But a go-between doesn’t stand for one side only.
But God is one.
[21] Is the law then against the promises of God?
Certainly not!
For if a law had been given
that could make alive,
then righteousness would really come from the law.
[22] But the Scripture locked up all things under sin,
so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ
might be given to those
who believe.
[23] But before faith came,
we were kept in custody under the law,
locked up
until faith would be revealed.
[24] So the law has become our tutor
to bring us to Christ,
so that we might be made righteous by faith.
[25] But now that faith has come,
we are no longer under a tutor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Go-between means: someone who stands between two sides, a mediator.
> Verse 16: Paul interprets the word “offspring” in the singular (for example Genesis 12:7) as referring to Christ. This is a way of interpreting that Jewish teachers also used at that time.
> Verse 17: The promise to Abraham came first. The law came much later, at Sinai. It doesn’t cancel the earlier promise.
> Verse 21: “Certainly not!” The law is not against God’s promises. Paul does not say that the law is bad.
> Verse 24: At that time the “tutor” was often a slave who went along with children and looked after them until they were grown up. Paul says: In this way the law had an important task.

## All are one in Christ (Verses 26–29)

[26] For you are all children of God
through faith in Christ Jesus.
[27] For all of you
who were baptized into Christ
have put on Christ.
[28] There is neither Jew nor Greek,
there is neither slave nor free,
there is neither male nor female.
For you are all one in Christ Jesus.
[29] If you belong to Christ,
then you are Abraham’s offspring
and heirs according to the promise.

> **What does this mean?**
> Heirs means: people who receive an inheritance.
> Verse 27: “Put on Christ” is a picture: In baptism a person is completely joined to Christ, like putting on new clothes.
> Verse 28 is one of the most important verses of the Bible about the equality of people. In Christ, background, social position and sex do not count as a ranking. All have the same dignity.
> That doesn’t mean that differences disappear: Jews remain Jews, women remain women. But no difference makes one person more valuable before God than another.
> For today: This verse encourages Christians to stand up against racism, exploitation and discrimination against women.
> Verse 29: Christians from the nations also belong to Abraham’s family through Christ.
