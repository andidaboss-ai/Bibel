# Galatians – Chapter 6
### Carry one another’s burdens

---

## Helping one another (Verses 1–10)

[1] Brothers,
if someone is caught in some wrong,
you
who are spiritual
should gently set that person right again.
And watch yourself,
so that you aren’t tempted too.
[2] Bear one another’s burdens.
In this way you fulfill the law of Christ.
[3] For if anyone thinks
that he is something
when he is nothing,
he deceives himself.
[4] But each one should examine his own work.
Then he will have a reason to boast in himself,
and not in comparison with someone else.
[5] For each one will carry his own load.
[6] Whoever is taught the word
should share all good things
with the one
who teaches him.
[7] Don’t be deceived:
God is not mocked.
For whatever a person sows,
that he will also reap.
[8] He who sows to his own flesh
will reap ruin from the flesh.
But he who sows to the Spirit
will reap eternal life from the Spirit.
[9] Let’s not grow weary
of doing good.
For if we don’t give up,
we will reap at the right time.
[10] So then,
as long as we have the opportunity,
let’s do good to all people,
and especially to those
who belong to the family of faith.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Whoever makes a mistake should not be condemned, but gently brought back onto the right path.
> Verse 2: “Bear one another’s burdens”: Christians help one another in hard times. That is “the law of Christ”: the commandment of love.
> Verses 2 and 5: This is no contradiction. With heavy burdens we help one another (verse 2). For your own life and actions, everyone is responsible themselves (verse 5).
> Verses 7–8: A picture from farming: What you sow, you reap.
> Verse 10: Christians should do good to all people, not only to other Christians.

---

## Paul’s ending in his own hand (Verses 11–18)

[11] Look
what large letters I use as I write to you with my own hand!
[12] Those
who want to make a good impression outwardly
are forcing you
to be circumcised.
They only do it
so that they won’t be persecuted for the cross of Christ.
[13] For even those
who are circumcised
don’t keep the law themselves.
But they want
you to be circumcised,
so that they can boast about your body.
[14] But far be it from me to boast
except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ.
Through it the world has been crucified to me,
and I to the world.
[15] For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision counts for anything,
nor uncircumcision,
but a new creation.
[16] As many
as walk by this rule,
peace and mercy be on them,
and on God’s Israel.
[17] From now on, let no one cause me any trouble.
For I bear the marks of the Lord Jesus on my body.
[18] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Paul had dictated the letter. He writes the ending himself, with large letters. Perhaps because of poor eyesight (compare 4:15), perhaps to stress his words.
> Verses 12–13: Here Paul means the Christian teachers who pressed the Galatians to be circumcised, not the Jewish people in general.
> Verse 15: “A new creation”: What counts is that God makes people new.
> Verse 16: Interpreters understand “God’s Israel” in different ways. Some think of Jews who believe in Jesus. Others think of the whole Jewish people, for whom Paul wishes peace and mercy. Still others think of the church made up of Jews and non-Jews.
> Important: This verse must not be understood as if the church had replaced Israel. The church claimed this for a long time, and it brought great suffering to the Jewish people. Today the major churches teach: God’s covenant with Israel remains valid (compare Romans 11:29).
> Verse 17: “The marks of the Lord Jesus” are probably the scars Paul had from beatings and persecution (compare 2 Corinthians 11:23–25).

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Galatians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A passionate letter:** Paul writes in great distress to the churches in Galatia. Christian teachers wanted to make circumcision a duty for Christians from the nations.
> **Good News for everyone:** God accepts people from all nations, through faith in Christ, by grace (chapters 1 to 3).
> **Abraham:** Abraham trusted God. All who trust God belong to his family (chapter 3).
> **All are one:** In Christ all are of equal worth, whether Jew or Greek, slave or free, man or woman (3:28).
> **Freedom:** Christ has set us free. This freedom shows itself in serving one another in love (chapter 5).
> **The fruit of the Spirit:** love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith (or: faithfulness), gentleness and self-control (5:22–23).
> **Carrying one another:** Christians help one another in hard times (6:2).
> **A very important note:** The letter to the Galatians is an argument among Christians. It is not an attack on Judaism. Paul remained a Jew. For Jews the Torah is still a gift from God today.
> **For today:** No one has to earn God’s love. And no one may shut others out because they are different.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter to the Ephesians. It is about the one church made up of Jews and people from all nations, about God’s grace and about life as Christians in everyday life.
