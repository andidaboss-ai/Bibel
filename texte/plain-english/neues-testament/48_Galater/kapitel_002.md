# Galatians – Chapter 2
### Agreement in Jerusalem, conflict in Antioch

---

## The meeting in Jerusalem (Verses 1–10)

[1] Then,
after fourteen years,
I went up again to Jerusalem
with Barnabas.
I also took Titus with me.
[2] I went up
because God had shown me to.
I laid before them the Good News
that I preach among the Gentiles.
But I did it privately,
before those who were respected.
For I didn’t want
to be running in vain
or to have run in vain.
[3] But not even Titus,
who was with me,
was forced
to be circumcised,
even though he was a Greek.
[4] This happened because of the false brothers
who had been secretly brought in.
They slipped in
to spy on our freedom
which we have in Christ Jesus.
They wanted to make us slaves.
[5] We did not give in to them for a moment
or submit to them,
so that the truth of the Good News would remain with you.
[6] But from those
who were thought to be important
(whatever they were before
makes no difference to me,
for God doesn’t show favoritism),
from these respected people I received no new requirements.
[7] On the contrary:
They saw
that I had been entrusted with the Good News for the uncircumcised,
just as Peter had been with the Good News for the circumcised.
[8] For he who worked through Peter
to make him an apostle to the circumcised
also worked through me
for the Gentiles.
[9] When they recognized the grace
that was given to me,
James, Cephas and John,
who were seen as pillars,
gave Barnabas and me the right hand of fellowship.
We were to go to the Gentiles,
and they to the circumcised.
[10] They only asked us
to remember the poor.
And that is the very thing I was eager to do.

*circumcised* = here: Jews
*uncircumcised* = here: people who are not Jews

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–10: Paul tells about an important meeting in Jerusalem (compare Acts 15). The leaders of the church in Jerusalem, James, Peter (Cephas) and John, agreed: People from the nations don’t have to be circumcised to be Christians.
> Verse 3: Titus was a Greek, so not a Jew. He was not forced to be circumcised.
> Verses 7–9: The tasks were divided: Peter went mainly to the Jews, Paul mainly to the other nations. Both preached the same Good News.
> Verse 9: To give “the right hand of fellowship” was a sign of a firm agreement.
> Verse 10: Caring for the poor unites all Christians.

## Paul opposes Peter (Verses 11–14)

[11] But when Peter came to Antioch,
I opposed him to his face.
For he was clearly in the wrong.
[12] For before some people came from James,
he ate with the Gentiles.
But when they came,
he drew back and kept himself apart,
because he was afraid of those
who belonged to the circumcision group.
[13] And the rest of the Jews joined him in his hypocrisy,
so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy.
[14] But when I saw
that they were not walking straight according to the truth of the Good News,
I said to Peter in front of them all:
“You are a Jew,
yet you live like the Gentiles
and not like the Jews.
How then can you force the Gentiles
to live like the Jews?

*hypocrisy* = pretending to be different from how you really are

> **What does this mean?**
> Antioch was a large city in Syria. There was a church there with Christians of Jewish background and Christians from other nations. They ate together.
> Verse 12: When visitors came from Jerusalem, Peter suddenly stopped eating with the Christians from the nations. In this way he divided the church.
> Verse 13: “The rest of the Jews” here means the other Christians of Jewish background in Antioch, not the Jewish people in general.
> For Paul, eating together was very important: It shows that everyone belongs to the church equally.
> For today: Church leaders can make mistakes too. It is right to disagree openly and honestly when people are being shut out.

## Made right through faith (Verses 15–21)

[15] We are Jews by birth
and not Gentile sinners.
[16] But we know:
A person is not made righteous by the works of the law,
but through faith in Jesus Christ.
So we too have believed in Christ Jesus,
so that we might be made righteous by faith in Christ
and not by the works of the law.
For by the works of the law
no one will be made righteous.
[17] But if,
while we seek to be made righteous in Christ,
we ourselves are found to be sinners,
is Christ then a servant of sin?
Certainly not!
[18] For if I build up again
what I tore down,
I show that I myself am a lawbreaker.
[19] For through the law I died to the law,
so that I might live for God.
[20] I have been crucified with Christ.
It is no longer I who live,
but Christ lives in me.
And the life
that I now live in the body,
I live by faith in the Son of God,
who loved me
and gave himself for me.
[21] I don’t reject the grace of God.
For if righteousness could come through the law,
then Christ died for nothing!”

*righteous* = right with God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: “Gentile sinners” was a common way among Jews at that time to speak of people who didn’t know God’s commandments. Paul quotes it in order to show right away: Before God everyone needs grace.
> Verse 16: This is a key sentence of Paul: You are not made righteous before God by your own achievements, but by trusting Jesus Christ. The Greek words for *faith in Jesus Christ* can also be translated as *the faithfulness of Jesus Christ*. Both belong together.
> “The works of the law” here means mainly commandments like circumcision and food laws, which set Jews apart from other nations. Paul says: These differences must not divide people in the church.
> Important: Paul is not speaking badly about the Torah here. He is asking what people from the nations must do to belong to God.
> Verse 20: “Christ lives in me”: Faith joins a person so closely to Jesus that their whole life is shaped by him.
> Verse 21: It is not quite clear where Paul’s speech to Peter ends. The WEB lets the quotation run to verse 21.
