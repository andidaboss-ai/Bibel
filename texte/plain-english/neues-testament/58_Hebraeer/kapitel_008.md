# Hebrews – Chapter 8
### The new covenant

---

## Jesus serves in the true sanctuary (Verses 1–6)

[1] The most important point of what we are saying
is this:
We have such a high priest.
He sat down at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens.
[2] He serves in the sanctuary,
in the true tent,
which the Lord set up,
not a human being.
[3] For every high priest is appointed
to offer both gifts and sacrifices.
That is why this high priest also must have something
to offer.
[4] If he were on earth,
he would not be a priest at all.
For there are priests
who offer the gifts according to the law.
[5] They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things.
This is what God told Moses
when he was about to build the tent.
For God said,
“See
that you make everything according to the pattern
that was shown to you on the mountain.”
[6] But now Jesus has received a much better ministry,
just as he is also the mediator of a better covenant.
This covenant is founded on better promises.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “The true tent”: During the journey through the wilderness Israel had a holy tent, the tabernacle. There God met his people. The letter says: The tent on earth was a copy of the heavenly sanctuary.
> Verse 5 quotes Exodus 25:40.
> Verse 6: “A better covenant”: For the letter to the Hebrews Jesus is the mediator of the new covenant that God promised through the prophet Jeremiah.

---

## God promises a new covenant (Verses 7–13)

[7] For if the first covenant had been without fault,
no one would have looked for a place for a second.
[8] For God finds fault with them and says,
“Look,
the days are coming,”
says the Lord,
“when I will make a new covenant
with the house of Israel
and with the house of Judah,
[9] not like the covenant
that I made with their fathers
on the day
when I took them by the hand
to lead them out of the land of Egypt.
For they didn’t continue in my covenant,
and I no longer cared for them,”
says the Lord.
[10] “For this is the covenant
that I will make with the house of Israel after those days,”
says the Lord:
“I will put my laws into their mind,
and I will also write them on their heart.
I will be their God,
and they will be my people.
[11] Then no one will teach his fellow citizen
and no one his brother
and say,
‘Know the Lord!’
For all will know me,
from the least to the greatest.
[12] For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness.
And I will remember their sins and lawless deeds
no more.”
[13] When he says,
“a new covenant,”
he has declared the first one old.
But what is becoming old and growing aged
is near to disappearing.

> **Important note**
> In history this passage was often understood as if God had canceled the covenant with the Jewish people and put the church in Israel’s place. This teaching is called “replacement theology.” Today the major churches have clearly rejected it. God has not canceled his covenant with Israel (Romans 11:1–2, 29).

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 8–12 quote Jeremiah 31:31–34. This promise is addressed specifically to “the house of Israel and the house of Judah.” God promises his own people renewal and forgiveness.
> Verse 9: Here the letter to the Hebrews quotes the old Greek translation. That is why it says “I no longer cared for them.” The Hebrew text of Jeremiah says instead: *although I was a husband to them*. God’s faithfulness to his people is especially clear in it.
> Verse 10: The heart of the new covenant: God’s teaching is not only on stone tablets, but in the heart. All people are to know God personally.
> Verse 12: God forgives and doesn’t remember the sins any more.
> Verse 13: Here the author speaks above all about the sacrifices in the temple. For Christians Jesus is the fulfillment of this service. Jewish people live faithfully in their covenant with God to this day. Christians are to meet them with respect and honor.
