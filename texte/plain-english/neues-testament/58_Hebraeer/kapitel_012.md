# Hebrews – Chapter 12
### Look to Jesus

---

## Run with endurance (Verses 1–3)

[1] So since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses,
let’s also lay aside everything
that weighs us down,
and the sin
that so easily entangles us.
And let’s run with endurance the race
that is set before us.
[2] Let’s look to Jesus,
the author and perfecter of faith.
For the joy
that was set before him
he endured the cross
and despised its shame.
He sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.
[3] Think of him
who endured such opposition from sinners against himself,
so that you don’t grow weary
and lose heart.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: We are “surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses.” These are the people of faith from Chapter 11. They are like spectators in a stadium who cheer us on.
> A picture from sports: Whoever wants to run a long race lays aside everything that is heavy.
> Verse 2: “Look to Jesus”: He has already gone the way and knows the goal.
> Verse 3: When we grow weary, we may think of Jesus. He also endured a lot.

---

## God trains his children (Verses 4–13)

[4] In your struggle against sin
you have not yet resisted to the point of blood.
[5] And you have forgotten the encouragement
that speaks to you as to children:
“My son,
don’t take the Lord’s discipline lightly.
And don’t lose heart
when he corrects you.
[6] For whom the Lord loves,
he disciplines.
And he punishes every son
whom he receives.”
[7] Endure,
for it serves your training.
God deals with you as with his children.
For what child is there whom his father doesn’t discipline?
[8] But if you are without discipline,
which all have received,
then you are illegitimate
and not true children.
[9] Besides, we had our earthly fathers
who disciplined us,
and we respected them.
Shouldn’t we much more be subject to the Father of spirits
and live?
[10] For they disciplined us for a few days,
as seemed good to them.
But he disciplines us for our good,
so that we may share in his holiness.
[11] All discipline seems for the moment to be no joy,
but sorrow.
But later it gives those
who have been trained by it
the peaceful fruit of righteousness.
[12] So lift up the tired hands
and the weak knees again.
[13] And make straight paths for your feet,
so that what is lame is not put out of joint,
but rather healed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6 quote Proverbs 3:11–12.
> Verses 5–11: The author compares hard experiences in life with the training by a father. At that time strictness in raising children was normal.
> Very important: These verses give no one the right to hit, humiliate or mistreat children. Children have a right to be raised without violence. Help for children and parents: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453.
> Important: Not every suffering is a punishment or training from God. Sickness, misfortune or violence are not a punishment from God. The letter wants to give persecuted Christians courage: Hard times too can make us more mature, and God is with us in them.
> Verses 12–13 take up Isaiah 35:3 and Proverbs 4:26: Help one another to get strength again.

## Live in peace and holiness (Verses 14–17)

[14] Pursue peace with all people
and holiness.
Without it no one will see the Lord.
[15] Make sure
that no one misses the grace of God,
that no bitter root grows up
and causes trouble,
and that many are not made unclean by it.
[16] Make sure
that no one is sexually immoral
or godless like Esau,
who sold his birthright for one meal.
[17] For you know:
When he later wanted to inherit the blessing,
he was rejected.
He found no chance for a change of mind,
though he looked for it eagerly with tears.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Peace with all people”: not only with Christians, but with everyone.
> Verse 15: “A bitter root”: Bitterness and resentment can grow like weeds and poison a whole community.
> Verses 16–17: The story of Esau is in Genesis 25 and 27. Esau sold his right as firstborn son for a bowl of lentils. Later he regretted it, but he could no longer undo it.
> Important: Later Esau made peace with his brother Jacob (Genesis 33). God blessed Esau too.

## You have come to Mount Zion (Verses 18–29)

[18] For you have not come to a mountain
that could be touched
and that burned with fire,
to blackness,
darkness
and storm,
[19] to the sound of a trumpet
and to a voice
speaking words.
Those who heard it
begged
that not one more word should be spoken to them.
[20] For they could not stand
what was commanded:
“If even an animal touches the mountain,
it shall be stoned.”
[21] And the sight was so terrible
that Moses said,
“I am terrified and trembling.”
[22] But you have come to Mount Zion,
to the city of the living God,
the heavenly Jerusalem,
to countless angels,
[23] to the festal gathering
and the church of the firstborn
whose names are written in heaven,
to God,
the Judge of all,
to the spirits of righteous people made perfect,
[24] to Jesus,
the mediator of a new covenant,
and to the blood of sprinkling
that speaks better than the blood of Abel.
[25] See
that you don’t refuse him
who speaks.
For they didn’t escape
when they refused him
who warned on the earth.
How much less will we escape
if we turn away from him
who warns from heaven!
[26] His voice shook the earth then.
But now he has promised,
“Yet once more I will shake not only the earth,
but also the heavens.”
[27] This “yet once more” shows
that what is shaken
is taken away,
because it is created,
so that what can’t be shaken
remains.
[28] So since we receive a Kingdom
that can’t be shaken,
let’s be thankful.
So let’s serve God
in a way that pleases him,
with reverence and awe.
[29] For our God is a consuming fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 18–21: This recalls Mount Sinai, where God gave his people Israel the Ten Commandments (Exodus 19–20). The people were frightened by God’s greatness.
> Verse 22: Mount Zion and Jerusalem are pictures from the Scriptures of Israel for the place where God lives with his people. The letter speaks of the “heavenly Jerusalem”: God’s presence, where all people of faith come together.
> Important: Both Sinai and Zion belong to God’s story with Israel. The letter does not set Judaism and Christianity against each other, but two pictures of God’s nearness: reverence and joy.
> Verse 24: The blood of Abel cried out for revenge (Genesis 4:10). The blood of Jesus speaks of forgiveness and reconciliation.
> Verse 26 quotes Haggai 2:6.
> Verse 28: We receive a Kingdom “that can’t be shaken.” Many things in the world are shaky. But God’s Kingdom stays firm.
> Verse 29 takes up Deuteronomy 4:24. God is holy and mighty. The picture of fire shows his cleansing power.
