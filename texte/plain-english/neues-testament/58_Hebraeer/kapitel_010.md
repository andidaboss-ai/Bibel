# Hebrews – Chapter 10
### One sacrifice forever

---

## The one sacrifice of Jesus (Verses 1–18)

[1] For the law has only a shadow of the good things to come,
not the real form of the things themselves.
That is why it can never make perfect those
who come to God,
with the same sacrifices
that are offered again and again year after year.
[2] Otherwise wouldn’t people have stopped
offering them?
For those
who serve God
would have been cleansed once for all
and would no longer have had any awareness of sins.
[3] But in those sacrifices sins are remembered every year.
[4] For it is impossible
for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.
[5] That is why he says,
when he comes into the world,
“You didn’t want sacrifice and offering.
But you prepared a body for me.
[6] You had no pleasure in whole burnt offerings and sin offerings.
[7] Then I said,
‘Look,
I have come
– in the scroll of the book it is written of me –
to do your will,
O God.’”
[8] First he says,
“You didn’t want sacrifices and offerings,
whole burnt offerings and sin offerings,
and you had no pleasure in them”
– which are offered according to the law.
[9] Then he says,
“Look,
I have come
to do your will.”
He takes away the first
to set up the second.
[10] By this will we have been made holy
through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ
once for all.
[11] Every priest stands day by day
serving
and often offering the same sacrifices.
But they can never take away sins.
[12] But he offered one sacrifice for sins
forever
and sat down at the right hand of God.
[13] Since then he is waiting
until his enemies are made the footstool of his feet.
[14] For by one offering
he has made perfect forever those
who are being made holy.
[15] The Holy Spirit also testifies to us.
For first he says,
[16] “This is the covenant
that I will make with them after those days,”
says the Lord:
“I will put my laws on their heart,
and I will write them on their mind.”
[17] Then he says,
“I will remember their sins and their unrighteous deeds
no more.”
[18] But where there is forgiveness of these,
there is no more offering for sin.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “A shadow”: A shadow shows that something is there, but it is not the thing itself. For the author the sacrifices point to Christ.
> Verses 5–7 quote Psalm 40:6–8 (in some Bibles 40:7–9), in the form of the old Greek translation. Already the prophets and psalms of Israel said: God doesn’t only want sacrifices, but a heart that does his will (compare also Hosea 6:6 and Psalm 51).
> Verses 12–13 take up Psalm 110:1. Verses 16–17 take up Jeremiah 31:33–34.
> Verses 14 and 18: This is the good news: Jesus has given forgiveness once for all. No one has to buy forgiveness with their own sacrifices.

## Come to God and stick together (Verses 19–25)

[19] So,
brothers,
through the blood of Jesus we have the freedom
to enter into the Holy Place.
[20] He opened for us a new and living way,
through the curtain,
that is,
through his body.
[21] And we have a great priest over the house of God.
[22] So let’s come to God with a true heart
and in full assurance of faith.
Our hearts are sprinkled and free from an evil conscience,
and our body is washed with pure water.
[23] Let’s hold on to the confession of our hope
without wavering.
For he
who promised
is faithful.
[24] And let’s pay attention to one another,
to spur one another on to love and good works.
[25] Let’s not stop meeting together,
as some are in the habit of doing.
But let’s encourage one another,
and all the more
as you see the Day coming nearer.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–20: In the past only the high priest was allowed to go behind the curtain into the Most Holy Place, once a year. Now all who believe in Jesus may come near to God.
> Verse 22: “Washed with pure water”: This probably means baptism.
> Verses 24–25: Faith needs community. Christians are to meet, encourage one another and spur one another on to good.

## A serious warning (Verses 26–31)

[26] For if we sin on purpose
after we have come to know the truth,
there is no longer any sacrifice for sins,
[27] but only a terrible waiting for judgment
and a raging fire
that will consume the enemies.
[28] Whoever disregards the law of Moses
must die without mercy
if two or three witnesses confirm it.
[29] How much worse punishment will he deserve
who has trampled the Son of God under foot,
who has counted the blood of the covenant,
by which he was made holy,
an unholy thing,
and who has insulted the Spirit of grace?
[30] For we know him
who said,
“Vengeance belongs to me.
I will repay,”
says the Lord.
Again,
“The Lord will judge his people.”
[31] It is a terrible thing
to fall into the hands of the living God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 26–31: As in Chapter 6 this is about people who reject Jesus very deliberately and with contempt (“trampled under foot,” “insulted”). It is not about people who are weak, doubt or regret mistakes.
> Very important: Whoever is afraid of not being forgiven any more may know: God accepts everyone who comes to him. The worry itself shows that God matters to you. If such thoughts torment you, talk to a pastor or a counselor. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 30: “Vengeance belongs to me.” This is a quotation from Deuteronomy 32:35–36. It also means: People are not to take revenge themselves (compare Romans 12:19).

## Don’t throw away your confidence (Verses 32–39)

[32] But remember the earlier days.
After you had been enlightened,
you endured a hard struggle with sufferings.
[33] Partly you were publicly insulted and oppressed.
Partly you stood by those
who were treated this way.
[34] For you had compassion on the prisoners.
And you accepted with joy
that your possessions were taken from you.
For you knew
that you have a better and lasting possession in heaven.
[35] So don’t throw away your confidence.
It has a great reward.
[36] For you need endurance
so that you do the will of God
and receive what was promised.
[37] “For in a very little while,
he who comes
will come,
and will not wait.
[38] But my righteous one will live by faith.
If he shrinks back,
my soul has no pleasure in him.”
[39] But we are not of those
who shrink back and are lost,
but of those
who have faith and save their soul.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 32–34: The church had already experienced persecution. Some were insulted, others lost their possessions. And they visited prisoners. The author reminds them of their courage.
> Verses 37–38 quote Habakkuk 2:3–4 in the form of the old Greek translation. Paul also quotes these words: “But the righteous shall live by faith” (Romans 1:17).
> Verse 39: An encouraging ending: You belong to those who trust.
