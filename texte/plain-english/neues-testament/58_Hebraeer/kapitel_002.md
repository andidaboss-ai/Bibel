# Hebrews – Chapter 2
### Jesus has become our brother

---

## Pay attention to the great salvation (Verses 1–4)

[1] So we must pay even closer attention
to the things that we have heard,
so that we don’t drift away.
[2] For even the word
that was spoken through angels
was valid.
And every wrongdoing and every disobedience
received a just punishment.
[3] How will we escape then
if we neglect so great a salvation?
It was first announced by the Lord.
And those
who heard him
confirmed it to us.
[4] God himself also gave his testimony to it,
by signs and wonders,
by various mighty works
and by gifts of the Holy Spirit,
as he wanted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Drift away” is a picture from sailing: like a boat that slowly drifts away from the shore if it is not tied up. In the same way you can slowly drift away from faith without noticing.
> Verse 2: “The word that was spoken through angels”: According to Jewish tradition angels took part when the Law was given at Sinai (compare Acts 7:53).
> Verse 3: The author already belongs to the second generation. He did not hear Jesus himself, but heard from those who heard him.

---

## Jesus became a human being (Verses 5–9)

[5] For God didn’t put the world to come,
of which we speak,
under the angels.
[6] But someone has testified somewhere,
“What is man,
that you think of him?
Or the son of man,
that you care for him?
[7] You made him a little lower than the angels.
You crowned him with glory and honor.
[8] You have put all things under his feet.”
When God put all things under him,
he left nothing
that is not under him.
But now we don’t see yet
that all things are under him.
[9] But we see Jesus,
who was made a little lower than the angels.
Because of his suffering of death
he is crowned with glory and honor.
For by the grace of God he was to taste death for everyone.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–8 quote Psalm 8:4–6 (in some Bibles 8:5–7). The psalm is amazed at how important human beings are to God.
> Verses 8–9: The author reads the psalm with Jesus in view: He became a human being, “a little lower than the angels.” Through his suffering and death he was raised up.
> Verse 8: “Now we don’t see yet”: To be honest: The world is not yet the way God wants it. There is still suffering and injustice. But we already see Jesus.
> Verse 9: “For everyone”: Jesus died for all people.

---

## Jesus is our brother (Verses 10–18)

[10] For it was fitting for God,
for whom all things exist
and through whom all things exist:
He wanted to bring many children to glory.
That is why he made the author of their salvation
perfect through sufferings.
[11] For he
who makes holy
and those
who are made holy
all come from one.
That is why he is not ashamed
to call them brothers.
[12] He says,
“I will declare your name to my brothers.
In the middle of the assembly I will sing your praise.”
[13] Again,
“I will put my trust in him.”
Again,
“Look,
here am I and the children
whom God has given me.”
[14] The children share in flesh and blood.
That is why he himself also shared in the same way.
So through death he wanted to bring to nothing him
who had the power of death,
that is, the devil.
[15] And he wanted to free all
who through the fear of death
were slaves all their lives.
[16] For he doesn’t help angels,
but he helps the offspring of Abraham.
[17] That is why he had to be made like his brothers in all things,
so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest before God,
to make atonement for the sins of the people.
[18] For he himself has suffered
and has been tempted.
That is why he is able to help those
who are tempted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: “Perfect through sufferings”: Jesus went all the way through human suffering. So he fully became a Savior who understands us.
> Verse 11: Jesus calls us “brothers” (and sisters). He is not ashamed of us.
> Verse 12 quotes Psalm 22:22 (in some Bibles 22:23). Verse 13 quotes Isaiah 8:17–18.
> Verses 14–15: Many people are afraid of death. Jesus died himself and defeated death. He wants to set us free from this fear.
> Verse 16: “The offspring of Abraham”: This means Israel and all who believe like Abraham (compare Galatians 3:7).
> Verse 17: The “high priest” was the most important priest in the temple in Jerusalem. Once a year, on the Day of Atonement, he brought the sacrifice for the whole people (Leviticus 16). The letter to the Hebrews uses this picture for Jesus. It writes a lot about it in the next chapters.
> Verse 18: Jesus knows temptation and suffering from his own experience. That is why he understands us when things are hard for us.
