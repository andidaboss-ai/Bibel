# Hebrews – Chapter 5
### Jesus, the high priest who feels with us

---

## What a high priest is (Verses 1–4)

[1] For every high priest is taken from among people.
He is appointed for people,
in things that concern God.
He is to offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.
[2] The high priest can deal gently with those
who are ignorant and go astray.
For he himself is also surrounded by weakness.
[3] That is why he must offer sacrifices for sins,
for the people
and also for himself.
[4] And nobody takes this honor for himself.
But he is called by God,
just as Aaron was.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The high priest stood between God and the people. He offered sacrifices for the people in the temple.
> Verse 2: Because the high priest is himself a human being with weaknesses, he can have understanding for others.
> Verse 4: Aaron, the brother of Moses, was the first high priest of Israel (Exodus 28).

---

## Jesus is called by God (Verses 5–10)

[5] So also Christ didn’t take for himself the honor
of becoming high priest.
But God said to him,
“You are my Son.
Today I have become your father.”
[6] And in another place he says,
“You are a priest forever,
after the order of Melchizedek.”
[7] When Jesus lived on earth as a human being,
he offered up prayers and petitions to God
with loud crying and tears.
God was able to save him from death.
And God heard him
because of his reverence for God.
[8] Although he was a Son,
he learned obedience
by the things
that he suffered.
[9] And having been made perfect,
he became the author of eternal salvation
for all
who obey him.
[10] God named him a high priest
after the order of Melchizedek.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 quotes Psalm 2:7. Verse 6 quotes Psalm 110:4.
> Verse 6: Melchizedek was a king and priest at the time of Abraham. He blessed Abraham (Genesis 14:18–20). The letter to the Hebrews explains more about him in Chapter 7.
> Verse 7: Jesus prayed, wept and cried out. This recalls his prayer in the garden of Gethsemane before his death (Mark 14:32–36). Jesus knows fear and despair.
> “God heard him”: God did not keep Jesus from death, but saved him out of death, through the resurrection.
> Important: We too may pray to God with tears and loud complaints. Jesus did it too.
> Verse 8: Suffering is not good. But Jesus stayed faithful through his suffering.

---

## Grow in faith (Verses 11–14)

[11] We have much to say about him,
and it is hard to explain,
because you have become slow to hear.
[12] For by this time you should already be teachers.
But you need someone again
to teach you the first principles of God’s words.
You need milk again
and not solid food.
[13] For whoever still lives on milk
has no experience in the word of righteousness.
For he is still a baby.
[14] But solid food is for grown-ups.
By practice they have trained their senses
to tell the difference between good and evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–14: A picture: Babies drink milk, grown-ups eat solid food. Faith is to grow in the same way. At the beginning you learn the basics. Later you can understand more deeply.
> The author is a little disappointed: The church has not made progress. But he doesn’t give up on them. In the next chapter he encourages them.
> For today: Faith is a journey. You may have questions and keep on learning. No one has to be ashamed if they are still at the beginning.
