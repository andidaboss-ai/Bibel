# Hebrews – Chapter 13
### Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever

---

## How Christians are to live (Verses 1–6)

[1] Let love among brothers and sisters continue.
[2] Don’t forget to show hospitality.
For by doing so some have welcomed angels
without knowing it.
[3] Remember the prisoners,
as if you were in prison with them.
And remember those
who are mistreated,
for you also live in a body that can be hurt.
[4] Let marriage be held in honor among all,
and let the marriage bed stay pure.
For God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.
[5] Be free from the love of money.
Be content with what you have.
For he himself has said,
“I will never leave you
and never forsake you.”
[6] So we can boldly say,
“The Lord is my helper.
I will not fear.
What can a human being do to me?”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Abraham and Sarah welcomed three strangers who turned out to be messengers of God (Genesis 18). Hospitality is an important commandment in the whole Bible.
> For today: This also applies to strangers, refugees and people in need.
> Verse 3: Christians are to feel with prisoners and with people who are tormented, as if it were happening to them.
> Verse 4: Marriage is to be honored. Faithfulness is important.
> Verse 5 takes up Deuteronomy 31:6 and Joshua 1:5. Verse 6 quotes Psalm 118:6.

---

## Jesus stays the same (Verses 7–16)

[7] Remember your leaders,
who spoke the word of God to you.
Look at the outcome of their lives
and imitate their faith.
[8] Jesus Christ is the same
yesterday, today,
and forever.
[9] Don’t be carried away by various and strange teachings.
For it is good
that the heart is made firm by grace,
not by foods.
Those
who were occupied with them
didn’t benefit from them.
[10] We have an altar
from which those have no right to eat
who serve the tent.
[11] For the bodies of the animals
whose blood is brought by the high priest into the Holy Place as a sin offering
are burned outside the camp.
[12] That is why Jesus also suffered outside the gate,
to make the people holy through his own blood.
[13] So let’s go out to him,
outside the camp,
and bear his disgrace.
[14] For we don’t have here a lasting city,
but we seek the one to come.
[15] Through him let’s always offer to God a sacrifice of praise,
that is, the fruit of lips
that confess his name.
[16] But don’t forget
to do good and to share with others.
For God is pleased with such sacrifices.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: A famous sentence: Jesus doesn’t change. You can rely on him, then, today and tomorrow.
> Verses 9–10: The author warns against teachings about certain foods. By the “altar” he probably means Christ himself.
> Important: Verse 10 is not a judgment on Jewish people. The letter speaks in pictures of the worship in the holy tent to explain what Christ means for Christians.
> Verse 12: Jesus was crucified outside the city wall of Jerusalem, at a place for criminals and outcasts.
> Verse 13: “So let’s go out to him, outside the camp”: Christians are not just to stay in their comfortable world, but to go to the outcasts, like Jesus.
> Verses 15–16: The sacrifices that please God are praise, thanks, good deeds and sharing.

## Leaders, prayer and blessing (Verses 17–21)

[17] Obey your leaders
and submit to them.
For they watch over your souls,
as those who must give account.
So they can do it with joy
and not with groaning.
For that would not be good for you.
[18] Pray for us.
For we are persuaded
that we have a good conscience.
We want to live honestly in all things.
[19] I especially ask you
to do this,
so that I may be given back to you sooner.
[20] The God of peace
brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep,
our Lord Jesus,
through the blood of an eternal covenant.
[21] May he make you complete in every good work,
so that you do his will.
May he work in you
what pleases him,
through Jesus Christ.
To him be the glory forever and ever.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: Church leaders have a great responsibility. They must give account before God.
> Very important: This verse gives no one the right to misuse power in the church. No leader may control people, put them under pressure or exploit them. Whoever experiences spiritual abuse may resist and seek help. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 20–21 are a blessing that is spoken in many worship services to this day. Jesus is the “great shepherd” who cares for his sheep.

## Greetings (Verses 22–25)

[22] But I urge you,
brothers:
Accept this word of encouragement.
For I have written to you only briefly.
[23] You should know:
Our brother Timothy has been set free.
If he comes soon,
I will visit you with him.
[24] Greet all your leaders and all the saints.
Those from Italy greet you.
[25] Grace be with you all.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: “Only briefly”: For the author the long letter is more a short sermon. He calls it a “word of encouragement.”
> Verse 23: Timothy had been in prison and is now free.
> Verse 24: “Those from Italy”: Perhaps the letter was written in Italy, or Christians from Italy send greetings.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Hebrews?

> **What does this mean?**
> **God has spoken through his Son:** Jesus is the radiance of God’s glory, greater than the angels (chapters 1 to 2).
> **Jesus is our brother:** He became a human being, suffered and understands our weaknesses (chapters 2 and 4).
> **Jesus, the high priest:** With pictures from the temple the letter explains what Jesus means for Christians: one sacrifice once for all, forgiveness forever (chapters 5 to 10).
> **The new covenant:** The promise from Jeremiah 31. God has not canceled his covenant with Israel. The teaching that the church has replaced Israel is wrong (chapter 8).
> **The cloud of witnesses:** The great people of faith of Israel are examples for everyone (chapter 11).
> **Run with endurance:** Look to Jesus and don’t give up (chapter 12).
> **Love in everyday life:** Hospitality, compassion for prisoners, faithfulness, contentment, sharing (chapter 13).
> **For today:** Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever. You can rely on him.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter of James. It says very practically: Faith shows itself in deeds.
