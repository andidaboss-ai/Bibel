# Hebrews – Chapter 11
### People who trusted God

---

## What faith is (Verses 1–3)

[1] Faith is assurance of things
hoped for,
proof of things
not seen.
[2] For by this the elders received God’s approval.
[3] By faith we understand
that the universe was made by the word of God.
So what is seen was not made out of things that are visible.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Faith means: trusting God, even when you don’t see everything yet. Faith is like a firm hope that already gives support now.
> Verse 2: “The elders” are the women and men of Israel who are told about in this chapter. They are examples of faith, for Jews and for Christians.
> Verse 3: Recalls Genesis 1: God made the world through his word.

---

## The first people of faith (Verses 4–7)

[4] By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than Cain.
Through it he received the testimony
that he was righteous.
God himself testified to it with regard to his gifts.
And through faith he still speaks,
though he is dead.
[5] By faith Enoch was taken away,
so that he wouldn’t see death.
He was not found,
because God had taken him away.
For before he was taken away,
he received the testimony
that he had pleased God.
[6] Without faith it is impossible
to please God.
For whoever comes to God
must believe
that he exists
and that he rewards those
who seek him.
[7] By faith Noah built a ship
to save his family.
God had warned him about things
not yet seen.
And Noah had reverence for God.
Through his faith he condemned the world
and became heir of the righteousness
that comes from faith.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: The story of Cain and Abel is in Genesis 4.
> Verse 5: Genesis 5:24 tells about Enoch: He walked with God, and then he was no longer there.
> Verse 6: Faith means: trusting that God is there and that it is worth seeking him.
> Verse 7: The story of Noah is in Genesis 6–9.

---

## Abraham and Sarah (Verses 8–19)

[8] By faith Abraham obeyed
when he was called,
and went out to a place
that he was to receive as an inheritance.
He went out
without knowing
where he was going.
[9] By faith he lived as a foreigner in the land of promise,
as in a foreign land.
He lived in tents
with Isaac and Jacob,
who were heirs with him of the same promise.
[10] For he was waiting for the city
that has firm foundations.
Its builder and maker is God.
[11] By faith even Sarah herself received power
to become pregnant.
She bore a child
although she was already too old.
For she considered him faithful
who had promised.
[12] That is why from one man,
who was as good as dead,
there came as many descendants
as the stars of the sky
and as the sand on the seashore,
which can’t be counted.
[13] These all died in faith.
They didn’t receive the promises,
but they saw them from far away and welcomed them.
And they confessed
that they were strangers and foreigners on the earth.
[14] Whoever says such things
makes it clear
that he is looking for a homeland.
[15] If they had been thinking of the land
that they had left,
they would have had enough time
to return there.
[16] But now they long for a better homeland,
that is, a heavenly one.
That is why God is not ashamed
to be called their God.
For he has prepared a city for them.
[17] By faith Abraham offered up Isaac
when he was put to the test.
Yes,
he
who had gladly received the promises
was ready to offer up his only son.
[18] To him it had been said,
“Your offspring will be counted through Isaac.”
[19] For he thought:
God is able to raise up even from the dead.
That is why he also received him back from the dead,
figuratively speaking.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Abraham is the father of faith for Jews, Christians and Muslims. He set out without knowing the goal (Genesis 12).
> Verse 11: Sarah is specifically named as a woman of faith. She had her son Isaac in old age (Genesis 21).
> Verse 13: “Strangers and foreigners on the earth”: People of faith know that their final home is with God.
> Verses 17–19: The story is in Genesis 22. It is very hard to understand. God prevented the sacrifice. For Jewish and Christian interpreters it shows, among other things: God does not want human sacrifices.
> Very important: This story gives no one the right to harm a child. Children must always be protected.

## Isaac, Jacob, Joseph and Moses (Verses 20–31)

[20] By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau,
even concerning the things
that were to come.
[21] By faith Jacob,
when he was dying,
blessed each of the sons of Joseph.
And he worshiped,
leaning on the top of his staff.
[22] By faith Joseph at the end of his life
spoke of the departure of the people of Israel
and gave instructions about his bones.
[23] By faith Moses, when he was born,
was hidden for three months by his parents.
For they saw
that he was a beautiful child.
And they were not afraid of the king’s command.
[24] By faith Moses,
when he had grown up,
refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter.
[25] He chose rather to suffer wrong with God’s people
than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a short time.
[26] He considered the disgrace of Christ greater riches
than the treasures of Egypt.
For he looked to the reward.
[27] By faith he left Egypt,
not fearing the anger of the king.
For he endured,
as seeing him who is invisible.
[28] By faith he kept the Passover
and the sprinkling of the blood,
so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them.
[29] By faith they passed through the Red Sea
as on dry land.
When the Egyptians tried to do the same,
they were swallowed up.
[30] By faith the walls of Jericho fell down,
after people had gone around them for seven days.
[31] By faith Rahab,
the prostitute,
didn’t die with those who were disobedient,
because she had received the spies in peace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Moses’ parents bravely resisted Pharaoh’s command to kill all the boys (Exodus 1–2). The midwives and Moses’ sister also helped.
> Verse 26: “The disgrace of Christ”: The author already sees Moses’ suffering for his people in the light of Christ.
> Verse 28: The Passover feast recalls the freeing from Egypt (Exodus 12). Jews celebrate it to this day as a great feast of freedom.
> Verses 30–31: The story of Jericho and Rahab is in Joshua 2 and 6. Rahab was a woman from another people. She is named as an example of faith. God’s people are open to people from outside.
> Important: The war stories from that time give no one the right to use violence in God’s name today.

## Many more witnesses (Verses 32–40)

[32] What more should I say?
The time would fail me
if I told
of Gideon,
Barak,
Samson,
Jephthah,
David,
Samuel
and the prophets.
[33] Through faith they conquered kingdoms,
did what is right,
obtained promises,
stopped the mouths of lions,
[34] quenched the power of fire,
escaped the edge of the sword,
from weakness were made strong,
grew mighty in war
and put foreign armies to flight.
[35] Women received their dead back by resurrection.
Others were tortured
and did not accept their release,
so that they might obtain a better resurrection.
[36] Others were mocked and whipped,
yes,
even put in chains and thrown into prison.
[37] They were stoned.
They were sawn apart.
They were tempted.
They were killed with the sword.
They went around in sheepskins and goatskins,
poor,
oppressed,
mistreated.
[38] The world was not worthy of them.
They wandered in deserts and mountains,
in caves and holes in the earth.
[39] These all received a good testimony through their faith.
And yet they didn’t receive the promise.
[40] For God had planned something better for us,
so that they would not be made perfect without us.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 32: These people are in the books of Judges and Samuel. They were not perfect people. Samson and Jephthah also did terrible things. Jephthah’s vow cost his daughter her life (Judges 11). That was a great injustice. The letter doesn’t praise everything they did, but their trust in God.
> Verses 33–34: Here it says: “stopped the mouths of lions” and “quenched the power of fire.” The first recalls Daniel in the lions’ den (Daniel 6). The second recalls the three men in the fiery furnace (Daniel 3).
> Verse 35: Women received their dead back, for example through the prophets Elijah and Elisha (1 Kings 17, 2 Kings 4).
> Verses 35–38: Many faithful people of Israel were persecuted, tortured and killed, for example in the time of the Maccabees. Today too people are persecuted because of their faith.
> Verse 40: The faithful people of Israel and the Christians belong together. Together they wait for God to make all things complete.
