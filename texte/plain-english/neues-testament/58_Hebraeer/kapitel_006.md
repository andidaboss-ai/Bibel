# Hebrews – Chapter 6
### Hope is an anchor

---

## Growing further in faith (Verses 1–3)

[1] So let’s leave behind the first teaching about Christ
and press on toward perfection.
Let’s not lay the foundation again:
turning away from dead works,
faith toward God,
[2] the teaching about baptisms,
about laying on of hands,
about the resurrection of the dead
and about eternal judgment.
[3] And we will do this
if God permits.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Here are the basics of faith, as the first Christians learned them: turning back, faith in God, baptism, laying on of hands, resurrection and judgment.
> These basics are important. But faith is to keep growing, like a house that is built on a foundation.

---

## A serious warning (Verses 4–8)

[4] For it is impossible
to bring people back to repentance
who were once enlightened,
who have tasted the heavenly gift,
who have received a share in the Holy Spirit,
[5] who have tasted the good word of God
and the powers of the age to come,
[6] and who then have fallen away.
For they crucify the Son of God for themselves again
and put him to open shame.
[7] For land
that drinks the rain
that often falls on it,
and that produces good plants
for those
who farm it,
receives blessing from God.
[8] But if it bears thorns and thistles,
it is rejected
and near being cursed.
In the end it is burned.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 4–6 are among the most difficult verses of the Bible. They speak of people who very deliberately and publicly rejected and mocked Christ after they knew him well.
> Christians understand these verses in different ways. Many stress: This is about a deliberate, final turning away from Christ, not about doubt, weakness or sins that people regret.
> Very important: Whoever is afraid of being abandoned by God or lost is, in fact, not lost. This worry shows that God matters to them. God accepts everyone who turns back to him (compare Luke 15, the parable of the lost son).
> If such thoughts torment you very much, talk to a pastor or a counselor. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verses 7–8: A picture from farming: Good land brings good fruit. The author wants to wake the church up, not frighten it.

## God doesn’t forget your love (Verses 9–12)

[9] But,
beloved,
we are persuaded of better things for you,
things that belong to salvation,
even though we speak like this.
[10] For God is not unjust.
He doesn’t forget your work
and the love
that you showed toward his name.
For you served the saints
and still serve them.
[11] We want
each one of you to show the same eagerness,
so that the hope is fully fulfilled,
to the end.
[12] Don’t become lazy,
but follow the example of those
who through faith and patience
inherited the promises.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Right after the serious warning comes encouragement: About you I am confident.
> Verse 10: God sees and doesn’t forget what we do for others out of love. No good deed is in vain.

---

## God’s sure promise (Verses 13–20)

[13] For when God made a promise to Abraham,
he swore by himself,
because he could swear by no one greater.
[14] He said,
“I will surely bless you,
and I will surely multiply you.”
[15] And so Abraham waited patiently
and received the promise.
[16] For people swear by someone greater.
And in every dispute the oath is the final confirmation.
[17] In the same way God wanted to show the heirs of the promise
even more clearly
that his decision cannot change.
That is why he guaranteed it with an oath.
[18] Through these two things that cannot change,
in which it is impossible for God to lie,
we are to have strong encouragement.
We have fled for refuge
to take hold of the hope
set before us.
[19] We have this hope like an anchor of the soul,
sure and steadfast.
It reaches behind the curtain,
[20] where Jesus went in for us
as the one who goes ahead of us.
He has become a high priest forever
after the order of Melchizedek.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: God gave Abraham a promise and confirmed it with an oath (Genesis 22:16–17).
> Verse 18: “Two things that cannot change”: God’s promise and God’s oath. You can rely completely on both.
> Verse 19: A beautiful picture: Hope is like an anchor. An anchor holds a ship firm, even when storm and waves come. In the same way hope holds us firm when life is stormy.
> “Behind the curtain”: In the temple a curtain separated off the Most Holy Place. Only the high priest was allowed to go in, once a year. Jesus “went in for us,” into God’s presence.
