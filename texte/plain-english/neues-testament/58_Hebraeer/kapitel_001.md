# Hebrews – Chapter 1
### God has spoken through his Son

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Hebrews

We don’t know who wrote the letter to the Hebrews. In the past people thought of Paul. Today almost all scholars think that it comes from someone else. Some have thought of Apollos, Barnabas or Priscilla. Already in the early church people said: Only God knows who wrote it.
The letter is more a long sermon than a letter. It is written to Christians who have become tired and are in danger of giving up their faith.
The author knows the Scriptures of Israel very well. He quotes them often and uses their pictures to explain who Jesus is: the Son of God, greater than the angels, the true high priest.
**An important note:** The letter to the Hebrews often compares the “old” and the “new” covenant. In history it was sometimes read as if God had rejected the Jewish people. That is wrong. Paul writes clearly: God has not rejected his people (Romans 11:1–2, 29). We explain this in the boxes.
The letter to the Hebrews has 13 chapters.

---

## God speaks through his Son (Verses 1–4)

[1] Long ago God spoke to our ancestors
at many times and in many ways,
through the prophets.
[2] Now at the end of these days
he has spoken to us through his Son.
He made him heir of all things.
Through him he also made the worlds.
[3] The Son is the radiance of his glory
and the exact image of his being.
He holds up all things by his powerful word.
He himself made us clean from our sins.
After that he sat down
at the right hand of the Majesty on high.
[4] He has become as much greater than the angels
as the name
that he has inherited
is greater than their name.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: God has always spoken: through the prophets of Israel. That was God truly speaking. It remains valuable.
> Verse 2: Now God speaks through his Son Jesus. He is God’s most important word to people.
> Verse 3: “The radiance of his glory”: Just as the rays of the sun belong to the sun, the Son belongs to God. Whoever sees Jesus sees what God is like.
> “At the right hand”: the place of honor next to the king. The picture comes from Psalm 110:1.

## The Son is greater than the angels (Verses 5–14)

[5] For to which of the angels did God ever say,
“You are my Son.
Today I have become your father”?
And again,
“I will be to him a Father,
and he will be to me a Son.”
[6] And when he brings the firstborn into the world again,
he says,
“Let all the angels of God worship him.”
[7] Of the angels he says,
“He makes his angels winds,
and his servants a flame of fire.”
[8] But of the Son he says,
“Your throne,
O God,
is forever and ever.
The scepter of righteousness
is the scepter of your Kingdom.
[9] You have loved righteousness
and hated wrongdoing.
That is why God,
your God,
has anointed you with the oil of gladness
above your companions.”
[10] And,
“You,
Lord,
in the beginning laid the foundation of the earth.
The heavens are the works of your hands.
[11] They will perish,
but you remain.
They will all grow old like a garment.
[12] You will roll them up like a cloak,
and they will be changed.
But you are the same.
Your years will not fail.”
[13] And to which of the angels has he ever said,
“Sit at my right hand,
until I make your enemies
the footstool of your feet”?
[14] Aren’t they all serving spirits,
sent out
to serve those
who will inherit salvation?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–13: The author quotes seven passages from the Scriptures of Israel. He reads them as words about Jesus:
> Verse 5: Psalm 2:7 and 2 Samuel 7:14. At first these are words about the king from David’s family.
> Verse 6: The quotation follows the Greek translation of Deuteronomy 32:43. It also recalls Psalm 97:7.
> Verse 7: Psalm 104:4.
> Verses 8–9: Psalm 45:6–7 (in some Bibles 45:7–8). At first a song for the king’s wedding. A scepter is the staff of a king. Righteousness means: living right before God.
> Verses 10–12: Psalm 102:25–27 (in some Bibles 102:26–28). There God the Creator is meant. The letter applies this to the Son.
> Verse 13: Psalm 110:1.
> Important: These psalms first had their own meaning for Israel. The Jewish people read them in their own way to this day. The letter to the Hebrews reads them as a Christian in the light of Jesus.
> Verse 14: Angels are “serving spirits.” They serve God and help people. At that time some people honored angels very much. The letter says: Jesus is greater.
