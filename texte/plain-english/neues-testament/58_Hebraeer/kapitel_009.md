# Hebrews – Chapter 9
### Christ, the true high priest

---

## The sanctuary on earth (Verses 1–10)

[1] The first covenant also had rules for worship
and a sanctuary on earth.
[2] For a tent was set up.
In the first part were the lampstand,
the table
and the show bread.
This part is called the Holy Place.
[3] Behind the second curtain
was the part of the tent
that is called the Most Holy Place.
[4] There was a golden altar of incense
and the ark of the covenant,
covered all around with gold.
In it was a golden jar holding the manna,
Aaron’s rod
that had budded,
and the tablets of the covenant.
[5] Above the ark were the cherubim of glory.
They overshadowed the mercy seat.
We can’t speak about these things in detail now.
[6] This is how everything was set up.
The priests always go into the first part of the tent
and carry out the services there.
[7] But into the second part only the high priest goes,
once a year,
and not without blood.
He offers it for himself
and for the errors of the people.
[8] By this the Holy Spirit shows:
The way into the Holy Place was not yet revealed
while the first tent was still standing.
[9] That is a picture for the present time.
At that time gifts and sacrifices were offered
that could not make the one
who serves God
perfect in his conscience.
[10] They were only outward rules
about food,
drink
and various washings,
set until the time of renewal.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2–5: Here the holy tent is described that Israel built in the wilderness (Exodus 25–26). It had two rooms: the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place. In the Most Holy Place stood the ark of the covenant with the tablets of the Ten Commandments. The show bread was holy bread that lay on the table before God. Cherubim are heavenly beings with wings.
> “Mercy seat”: That was the lid of the ark of the covenant. On the Day of Atonement blood was sprinkled there.
> Verse 7: On the Day of Atonement (in Hebrew *Yom Kippur*) the high priest went into the Most Holy Place (Leviticus 16). Yom Kippur is the highest Jewish holy day to this day. Since the destruction of the temple in AD 70, Jews keep it without sacrifices, with fasting, prayer and turning back to God.
> Verses 9–10: The author sees the sacrifices as a picture and a preparation for Christ.

## Christ brought redemption once for all (Verses 11–15)

[11] But Christ has come
as high priest of the good things to come.
He went through the greater and more perfect tent,
not made with hands,
that is,
not belonging to this creation.
[12] Not with the blood of goats and calves,
but with his own blood
he entered into the Holy Place once for all.
And he obtained eternal redemption.
[13] For if the blood of goats and bulls
and the ashes of a young cow
sprinkled on those who are unclean
make them holy,
so that their body becomes clean,
[14] how much more will the blood of Christ
cleanse your conscience from dead works
so that you serve the living God!
Through the eternal Spirit he offered himself without defect to God.
[15] That is why he is the mediator of a new covenant.
Through his death redemption was to come
from the wrongdoings under the first covenant,
so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “With his own blood”: Jesus did not sacrifice an animal, but gave himself.
> Verse 13: The “ashes of a young cow” were used for cleansing (Numbers 19).
> Verse 14: Jesus cleanses the conscience. Whoever carries guilt may start anew.

---

## Covenant and will (Verses 16–22)

[16] For where there is a will,
the death of the one
who made it
must be shown.
[17] For a will only takes effect after death.
It is never in force
while the one
who made it is alive.
[18] That is why even the first covenant was not put into effect without blood.
[19] For after Moses had told the whole people every commandment according to the law,
he took the blood of the calves and the goats,
with water,
scarlet wool
and hyssop.
And he sprinkled the book itself
and all the people.
[20] As he did so he said,
“This is the blood of the covenant
which God has commanded you.”
[21] In the same way he sprinkled the tent
and all the vessels of the service with the blood.
[22] According to the law almost everything is cleansed with blood.
And without the shedding of blood
there is no forgiveness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: The Greek word *diatheke* can mean “covenant,” but also “will.” The author plays with both meanings: A will only takes effect after death. In the same way the new covenant came into effect through the death of Jesus.
> Verses 19–20: This recalls Exodus 24:6–8. There Moses confirmed the covenant at Sinai with blood. Hyssop is a small bushy plant. It was used for sprinkling.
> Verse 22: At that time blood was seen as a sign of life (Leviticus 17:11). The author explains the death of Jesus in this way.
> Important: At all times God also forgave those who honestly regretted and turned back to him. The psalms and prophets bear witness to this (for example Psalm 51). God’s mercy is greater than any rule.

## Christ appears for us before God (Verses 23–28)

[23] So the copies of the heavenly things
had to be cleansed in this way.
But the heavenly things themselves had to be cleansed
with better sacrifices than these.
[24] For Christ hasn’t entered into a holy place
made with hands,
which is only a copy of the true one.
But he entered into heaven itself,
now to appear in the presence of God for us.
[25] Nor did he need to offer himself often,
as the high priest enters into the Holy Place every year
with blood not his own.
[26] Otherwise he would have had to suffer often since the creation of the world.
But now,
at the end of the ages,
he has appeared once
to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.
[27] And just as it is appointed for people
to die once,
and after this comes judgment,
[28] so Christ also was offered once
to bear the sins of many.
He will appear a second time,
not because of sin,
but to save those
who are eagerly waiting for him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Jesus is with God and pleads for us there.
> Verses 26 and 28: “Once”: The sacrifice of Jesus does not have to be repeated. It counts forever.
> Verse 28: When Jesus comes again, he doesn’t come to punish, but to save. That is a message of hope.
