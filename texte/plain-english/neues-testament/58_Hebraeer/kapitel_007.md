# Hebrews – Chapter 7
### A priest like Melchizedek

---

> **Important note first**
> In this chapter the letter to the Hebrews compares the priesthood in the temple with Jesus. It uses harsh words about the law (verses 18–19). These words are not contempt for the Jewish faith. For Jews the Torah, God’s law, is a precious gift to this day. Paul also says: “So the law indeed is holy” (Romans 7:12). The letter wants to show how great Jesus is for Christians, not to put Israel down.

---

## Who Melchizedek was (Verses 1–10)

[1] This Melchizedek was king of Salem
and priest of God Most High.
He met Abraham
as he came back from defeating the kings,
and blessed him.
[2] Abraham gave him a tenth of everything.
His name means first “king of righteousness.”
And then he is also “king of Salem,”
which means “king of peace.”
[3] He is without father,
without mother,
without family tree.
He has neither beginning of days
nor end of life.
So he is made like the Son of God
and remains a priest forever.
[4] Now think
how great this man was:
Even Abraham,
the patriarch,
gave him a tenth of the best of the plunder.
[5] The descendants of Levi
who receive the priest’s office
have a commandment in the law
to take a tenth from the people,
that is, from their brothers,
although these also come from Abraham.
[6] But Melchizedek,
whose family does not come from them,
took a tenth from Abraham.
And he blessed him
who had the promises.
[7] Without any doubt the lesser
is blessed by the greater.
[8] Here people who die receive tithes.
But there one receives them
of whom it is testified
that he lives.
[9] We can even say:
Through Abraham also Levi,
who receives tithes,
has paid tithes.
[10] For he was still in the body of his ancestor
when Melchizedek met him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: The story is in Genesis 14:17–20. Melchizedek was king of Salem, probably the later Jerusalem. He brought Abraham bread and wine and blessed him.
> Verse 3: The Bible tells nothing about Melchizedek’s parents, his birth or his death. The letter takes this as a picture: He is like a priest “forever,” similar to the Son of God.
> Verse 5: The priests in Israel came from the tribe of Levi. They received a tenth of the harvest to live on. Such a tenth is called a tithe.
> Verses 9–10: A thought game: Levi descended from Abraham. When Abraham gave Melchizedek a tenth, Levi was, so to speak, already “there.”

## A new priest (Verses 11–19)

[11] Now if perfection had come
through the priesthood of the Levites
(for on that basis the people received the law),
why was it still necessary
for another priest to come
after the order of Melchizedek
and not after the order of Aaron?
[12] For when the priesthood changes,
the law must change too.
[13] For he
of whom these things are said
belongs to another tribe.
No one from that tribe has ever served at the altar.
[14] For it is clear
that our Lord came from Judah.
Moses said nothing about priesthood concerning that tribe.
[15] This becomes even clearer
when another priest comes
who is like Melchizedek.
[16] He has become a priest,
not by the law of a human rule,
but by the power of an endless life.
[17] For it is testified,
“You are a priest forever,
after the order of Melchizedek.”
[18] For an earlier commandment is set aside
because of its weakness and uselessness
[19] (for the law made nothing perfect),
and a better hope is brought in.
Through it we draw near to God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: Jesus came from the tribe of Judah, from the family of King David. According to the law only descendants of Levi could be priests. The letter explains: Jesus is a priest in another, new way.
> Verse 17 quotes Psalm 110:4.
> Verses 18–19: These words are harsh. The author means: The sacrifices in the temple could not connect people with God completely and forever. For him only Jesus did that.
> Important: This is the view of Christian faith. It is not a judgment on Jewish people. God has not canceled his covenant with Israel (compare Romans 11:29). Jews and Christians read the same holy Scriptures of Israel in different ways. Both have reason to meet one another with respect.

## Jesus, the eternal high priest (Verses 20–28)

[20] And this did not happen without an oath.
[21] For those became priests without an oath.
But he became a priest with an oath by him
who says to him,
“The Lord swore
and will not change his mind,
‘You are a priest forever,
after the order of Melchizedek.’”
[22] So Jesus has become the guarantee of a better covenant.
[23] And those became priests in great numbers,
because death kept them
from continuing in office.
[24] But he has a priesthood that never changes,
because he lives forever.
[25] That is why he is also able to save completely all
who come to God through him.
For he lives forever
to plead for them.
[26] For such a high priest was fitting for us:
holy,
without guilt,
pure,
separated from sinners
and higher than the heavens.
[27] He doesn’t need, like those high priests,
to offer up sacrifices every day,
first for his own sins
and then for the sins of the people.
For he did this once for all
when he offered up himself.
[28] For the law appoints people as high priests
who have weaknesses.
But the word of the oath,
which came after the law,
appoints a Son
who is made perfect forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21 quotes Psalm 110:4 again.
> Verse 22: “A better covenant”: The letter speaks about it in detail in Chapter 8.
> Verse 25: A great comfort: Jesus lives and always pleads for us before God, like a lawyer for his friends.
> Verse 27: “Once for all”: Jesus gave himself. This does not have to be repeated.
