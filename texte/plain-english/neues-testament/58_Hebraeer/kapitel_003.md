# Hebrews – Chapter 3
### Today, if you hear his voice

---

## Jesus and Moses (Verses 1–6)

[1] So,
holy brothers,
you who share in the heavenly calling:
Look at the Apostle and High Priest
whom we confess:
look at Jesus.
[2] He was faithful to him
who appointed him,
just as Moses also was faithful in all God’s house.
[3] For Jesus is worthy of more glory than Moses,
just as he
who builds a house
has more honor than the house itself.
[4] For every house is built by someone.
But he
who built all things
is God.
[5] Moses was faithful in all God’s house
as a servant,
as a witness of the things
that were to be spoken later.
[6] But Christ is faithful as a Son over God’s house.
We are his house,
if we hold on to our confidence
and to the hope
that we are proud of
firm to the end.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 2 and 5: Moses is greatly honored. He was “faithful in all God’s house.” That is what Numbers 12:7 says. Moses remains a great figure of faith for Jews and Christians.
> Verses 3–6: The letter compares Moses to a faithful servant in the house and Jesus to the son of the house. This does not put Moses down. The letter wants to say how great Jesus is.
> Verse 6: “We are his house”: The church is like a family and a house where God lives.

---

## Don’t harden your hearts (Verses 7–19)

[7] So,
as the Holy Spirit says,
“Today,
if you hear his voice,
[8] don’t harden your hearts
as in the rebellion,
on the day of testing in the wilderness.
[9] There your ancestors put me to the test
and tried me.
And they saw my works for 40 years.
[10] That is why I was displeased with that generation.
And I said,
‘They always go astray in their heart.
They didn’t know my ways.’
[11] That is why I swore in my anger,
‘They will not enter into my rest.’”
[12] Be careful,
brothers,
that none of you has an evil,
unbelieving heart
and turns away from the living God.
[13] But encourage one another every day,
as long as it is called “today,”
so that none of you is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.
[14] For we have come to share in Christ,
if we hold on to our first confidence
firm to the end.
[15] As it is said,
“Today,
if you hear his voice,
don’t harden your hearts
as in the rebellion.”
[16] For who rebelled
when they heard?
Wasn’t it all
who came out of Egypt led by Moses?
[17] With whom was God displeased for 40 years?
Wasn’t it with those
who sinned
and whose bodies fell in the wilderness?
[18] To whom did he swear
that they would not enter into his rest?
Wasn’t it to those
who were disobedient?
[19] So we see:
They were not able to enter in because of unbelief.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–11 quote Psalm 95:7–11. This psalm is prayed in Jewish worship to this day. It is a warning of Israel to itself: Listen to God and trust him.
> The story behind it is in Exodus 17 and Numbers 14: The people of Israel went through the wilderness after leaving Egypt. They doubted God again and again. That is why this generation had to stay in the wilderness for 40 years.
> Important: The letter to the Hebrews uses this story as a warning for Christians. It does not say: Israel is bad. It says: The same can happen to us Christians if we don’t trust God. The Bible tells honestly about the weaknesses of God’s people, so that everyone can learn from them.
> Verse 13: “Encourage one another every day”: Faith needs community. We are to help one another not to become tired.
> “Today”: Every day is a new chance to listen to God.
> “My rest”: God’s rest at first meant the promised land. In the next chapter the letter understands it more deeply: as rest and peace with God.
