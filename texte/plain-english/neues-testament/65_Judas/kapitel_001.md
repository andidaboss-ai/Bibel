# Jude – Chapter 1
### Contend for the faith

---

## Before we begin: The letter of Jude

This short letter comes from Jude. He calls himself “brother of James.” This probably means James, the brother of Jesus. Then Jude would also be a brother of Jesus (compare Mark 6:3).
Important: This Jude is **not** Judas Iscariot, who betrayed Jesus. Judas or Jude was a very common name at that time. It comes from Judah, one of the sons of Jacob.
The letter warns against people who have crept into the church. They probably said: Because God is gracious, we may do what we want.
The letter uses many examples from the Hebrew Bible. It also quotes old Jewish books that are not in the Bible, for example the Book of Enoch.
The second letter of Peter (chapter 2) is very similar to this letter.
The letter of Jude has only 1 chapter.

---

> **Important note first**
> This letter uses very harsh words against false teachers. That is how people wrote about disputes at that time. The harsh words are no permission to insult people today who believe differently. Judgment on people belongs to God alone.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Jude,
a servant of Jesus Christ
and brother of James,
writes to those who are called,
made holy by God the Father
and kept for Jesus Christ.
[2] May mercy, peace and love
be given to you in abundance.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Kept for Jesus Christ”: God watches over those who believe. The word “keep” appears often in this letter, at the beginning and at the end (verse 24).

---

## Contend for the faith (Verses 3–4)

[3] Beloved,
I was very eager to write to you
about our common salvation.
But now I must write to you
and urge you:
Contend for the faith
which was once for all delivered to the saints.
[4] For some people have secretly crept in.
Long ago it was written about them
that they would be condemned.
They are ungodly people.
They turn the grace of our God
into an excuse for wild living.
And they deny our only Master,
God, and Lord,
Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “Contend” here doesn’t mean using violence. It means: standing up for the faith with all your strength and remaining with the truth.
> Verse 4: The false teachers probably said: God forgives anyway, so everything is allowed. The letter says: That is a misuse of grace.
> At the end of verse 4 it says: “our only Master, God, and Lord, Jesus Christ.” Some manuscripts don’t have the word “God” there.

---

## Examples from the past (Verses 5–7)

[5] I want to remind you,
though you already know all this:
The Lord saved a people out of the land of Egypt.
But afterward he destroyed those
who didn’t believe.
[6] And the angels
who didn’t keep their first place,
but left their own home,
he keeps in everlasting chains in darkness
for the judgment of the great day.
[7] It is the same with Sodom and Gomorrah
and the cities around them.
In the same way as these, they gave themselves over to sexual immorality
and went after strange flesh.
They are a warning example
and suffer the punishment of eternal fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 recalls the time in the wilderness after leaving Egypt (Numbers 14). Some manuscripts say “Jesus” instead of “the Lord.”
> Verse 6 recalls old Jewish stories about angels who set themselves against God (compare Genesis 6:1–4).
> Verse 7: “Strange flesh”: Many scholars think that this means: The men of Sodom wanted to attack the angels who were Lot’s guests (Genesis 19). Like the angels in verse 6 they disregarded the boundary between heaven and earth.
> The Hebrew Bible also names as the guilt of Sodom: pride, too much of everything, and that they did not help the poor (Ezekiel 16:49). The story of Sodom is above all about violence against strangers.

---

## What the false teachers are like (Verses 8–13)

[8] In the same way these people also,
in their dreaming,
defile the body,
despise all authority
and speak evil of heavenly powers.
[9] Even Michael, the archangel,
did not dare
to bring a slanderous judgment
when he argued with the devil
and disputed about the body of Moses.
He only said,
“May the Lord rebuke you!”
[10] But these people speak evil of whatever
they don’t know.
And what they understand by nature,
like animals without reason,
in these things they are destroyed.
[11] Woe to them!
For they have gone in the way of Cain.
For money they have run into the error of Balaam.
And they have perished in Korah’s rebellion.
[12] They are like hidden rocks under water
at your love feasts,
when they feast and eat with you.
They are shepherds
who without fear only feed themselves.
They are like clouds without water,
driven by the winds.
They are like trees in autumn without fruit,
twice dead
and pulled up by the roots.
[13] They are like wild waves of the sea,
foaming out their own shame.
They are like wandering stars
that roam without a goal.
The blackest darkness
is kept for them forever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: This story about the archangel Michael and the body of Moses is not in the Bible. It comes from an old Jewish book. The message: Not even an archangel judges the devil himself. He leaves the judgment to God.
> Verse 11 names three examples from the Hebrew Bible: Cain, who killed his brother (Genesis 4). Balaam, who let himself be hired for money (Numbers 22). Korah, who rebelled against Moses and Aaron (Numbers 16).
> Verse 12: “Love feasts” were the shared meals of the church. “Hidden rocks” are dangerous because you can’t see them. The false teachers seem harmless, but they do damage.
> Verses 12–13: Many pictures from nature: They promise something, as clouds promise rain, but they give nothing.

---

## The words of Enoch (Verses 14–16)

[14] Enoch,
the seventh from Adam,
also prophesied about these people.
He said,
“Look,
the Lord came with ten thousands of his holy ones,
[15] to execute judgment on all.
He will convict all the ungodly
of all their ungodly works
which they have done in an ungodly way,
and of all the hard things
which ungodly sinners have spoken against him.”
[16] These people are always grumbling and complaining.
They live by their desires.
Their mouth speaks proud words.
And they flatter people
to gain advantage.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “The seventh from Adam”: You count Adam, Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch. Enoch appears in Genesis 5:18–24. He lived so closely with God that God took him to himself.
> Verses 14–15: This quotation is not in the Bible. It comes from the Book of Enoch, an old Jewish writing. Jude knew and valued this book. In the Ethiopian Orthodox Church the Book of Enoch even belongs to the Bible. In the other churches it does not belong to it.
> Verse 16: “And they flatter people to gain advantage”: They treat people according to how useful they are to them.

---

## Keep yourselves in God’s love (Verses 17–23)

[17] But you,
beloved,
remember the words
which were spoken before by the apostles
of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[18] They said to you,
“In the last time there will be mockers,
who live by their own ungodly desires.”
[19] These are the ones
who cause divisions.
They live only by their natural instincts
and don’t have the Spirit.
[20] But you,
beloved,
keep building yourselves up
on your most holy faith
and pray in the Holy Spirit.
[21] Keep yourselves in the love of God
and wait for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ
that leads to eternal life.
[22] On some have compassion,
making a distinction.
[23] Save some,
snatching them out of the fire,
with fear.
Hate even the clothing
stained by the body.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 20–21: What Christians are to do: grow in faith, pray and remain in God’s love.
> Verses 22–23: Very important: Christians are not simply to write others off. They are to have compassion and try to save people. You are to hate evil, but not people.
> Verses 22–23 have come down in different forms in the old manuscripts. Some have two groups of people, others three.

---

## Praise to God (Verses 24–25)

[24] God is able to keep you
from stumbling.
He is able to present you without fault
before his glory,
with great joy.
[25] To him,
the only God,
our Savior,
who alone is wise,
belong glory and majesty,
power and authority,
now and forever.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> After all the harsh words the letter ends with wonderful praise. The most important thing: We don’t have to hold on to ourselves. God holds on to us and keeps us.
> These verses are spoken as a blessing at the end of many worship services.
> In many manuscripts verse 25 says more: God is praised through Jesus Christ our Lord. And glory belongs to him already before all time.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter of Jude?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Contend for the faith:** Remain with the message that you received (verse 3).
> **Grace is no excuse:** Whoever uses God’s grace as a permission for everything misuses it (verse 4).
> **Examples from the Hebrew Bible:** God sees evil and judges it (verses 5–16).
> **Keep yourselves in God’s love:** Pray, grow in faith and have compassion on others (verses 20–23).
> **God keeps you:** He holds you firm until you reach the goal (verses 24–25).
> **For today:** Hate evil, but try to save people.
> **What comes next?** Last comes the Revelation to John. It is the last book of the Bible. In pictures it speaks of God’s victory over evil and of new heavens and a new earth.
