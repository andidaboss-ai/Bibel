# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 1
### The God of all comfort

---

## Before we begin: The second letter to the Corinthians

Paul writes once more to the church in Corinth. After the first letter there had been new difficulties there. Some people in the church were against Paul. They doubted whether he was really an apostle.
Paul probably visited the church in between. This visit was painful. Afterwards he wrote a letter *with many tears* (2:4). Meanwhile he has received good news: The church has been reconciled again (chapter 7).
The letter was probably written around AD 55 or 56 in Macedonia. Some scholars think that several letters of Paul were put together here.
In this letter Paul shares many personal things: about fear and suffering, about comfort and hope. He shows: God’s power shows itself precisely in human weakness.
The second letter to the Corinthians has 13 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God,
and Timothy our brother,
write to the assembly of God which is at Corinth,
with all the saints in the whole of Achaia.
[2] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Achaia was the Roman province in the south of Greece. Corinth was its capital.
> Timothy was a close co-worker of Paul.

---

## God comforts us (Verses 3–11)

[3] Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ,
the Father of mercies
and God of all comfort.
[4] He comforts us in all our trouble,
so that we may be able to comfort those
who are in any trouble,
with the comfort
with which we ourselves are comforted by God.
[5] For as the sufferings of Christ
overflow to us,
so our comfort also overflows through Christ.
[6] But if we are in trouble,
it is for your comfort and salvation.
If we are comforted,
it is for your comfort.
This comfort helps you
to endure patiently the same sufferings
which we also suffer.
[7] Our hope for you is firm.
For we know:
As you share in the sufferings,
so you also share in the comfort.
[8] For we don’t want you to be uninformed, brothers,
about the trouble
which happened to us in the province of Asia.
We were weighed down beyond measure,
far beyond our strength.
We even despaired of life.
[9] Yes,
we had the sentence of death within ourselves.
That was so that we should not trust in ourselves,
but in God
who raises the dead.
[10] He rescued us from so great a danger of death,
and he does rescue us.
We have set our hope on him
that he will also rescue us again.
[11] You also help with this
by praying for us.
So many people will give thanks for us,
for the gift
given to us through the prayers of many.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 3–4: God is the “God of all comfort.” Whoever has experienced comfort can comfort others.
> Verses 8–9: Paul tells quite openly: He despaired and thought he would die. We don’t know exactly what happened in the province of Asia (in what is now Turkey).
> For today: People with strong faith can despair too. That is no shame. If you don’t know how to go on, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 11: Praying for one another connects people.

## Paul explains his travel plans (Verses 12–24)

[12] For this is what we boast about:
the testimony of our conscience.
We have lived in the world,
and especially toward you,
in holiness and sincerity
that come from God,
not in human wisdom,
but in the grace of God.
[13] For we write nothing else to you
than what you read
and also understand.
And I hope
you will understand fully to the end,
[14] as you have already understood us in part:
that we are your reason to boast,
as you also are ours,
in the day of our Lord Jesus.
[15] In this confidence I wanted to come to you first,
so that you might have a double joy.
[16] I wanted to pass through your city into Macedonia
and come back to you from Macedonia.
Then you would send me on my journey to Judea.
[17] When I planned this,
was I fickle?
Or do I plan in a human way,
so that with me it is “Yes, yes”
and at the same time “No, no”?
[18] But as God is faithful:
Our word to you was not “Yes and no.”
[19] For the Son of God,
Jesus Christ,
whom we preached among you,
I, Silvanus and Timothy,
was not “Yes and no.”
But in him is “Yes.”
[20] For however many promises of God there are,
in him is the “Yes.”
That is why through him we also say the “Amen,”
to the glory of God.
[21] It is God
who makes us firm in Christ together with you.
And he has anointed us.
[22] He also sealed us
and gave us the Spirit as a down payment in our hearts.
[23] But I call on God as my witness,
on my life:
It was to spare you
that I didn’t come to Corinth.
[24] We don’t want to rule over your faith,
but we are fellow workers with you for your joy.
For you stand firm in faith.

*fickle* = changing your mind all the time, unreliable
*anointed* = chosen by God and given a task

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–17: Paul had changed his travel plans. Some in Corinth accused him of being unreliable. Paul explains: I wanted to spare you (verse 23).
> Verse 20: The “Yes” means Jesus. All of God’s promises are fulfilled in Jesus. That is why Christians say “Amen.” It means: *So it is*.
> Verse 22: The WEB says “down payment.” The Holy Spirit is like a first installment. He guarantees that God will keep his whole promise.
> Verse 24: “We don’t want to rule over your faith.” Leaders in the church should not control people, but serve their joy.
