# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 13
### Last warning and blessing

---

## Examine yourselves (Verses 1–10)

[1] This is the third time I am coming to you.
“At the mouth of two or three witnesses
every matter shall be established.”
[2] I warned you before,
and I warn you again now.
As I said when I was with you the second time,
so now, while I am away, I write it
to those
who sinned before
and to all the rest:
If I come again,
I will not spare anyone.
[3] For you are looking for proof
that Christ speaks through me.
He is not weak toward you,
but powerful among you.
[4] For he was crucified in weakness,
yet he lives through the power of God.
We also are weak in him.
But we will live with him
through the power of God
toward you.
[5] Examine yourselves,
whether you are in the faith.
Test yourselves.
Or don’t you realize about yourselves
that Jesus Christ is in you?
Unless, of course,
you fail the test.
[6] But I hope
you will realize
that we have not failed the test.
[7] Now I pray to God
that you do nothing evil.
Not so that we may appear approved,
but so that you may do what is good,
even if we seem to have failed.
[8] For we can do nothing against the truth,
but only for the truth.
[9] For we rejoice
when we are weak
and you are strong.
And we also pray for this:
that you may become complete.
[10] So I write these things while I am away,
so that I don’t have to be harsh
when I am with you.
I want to use the authority
which the Lord gave me
for building up
and not for tearing down.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 quotes Deuteronomy 19:15. In the law of Israel you needed at least two or three witnesses to find someone guilty. A good protection against false accusations.
> Verse 4: Jesus was crucified in weakness, but he lives through God’s power. It is the same with Paul: weak, but carried by God.
> Verse 5: The Corinthians wanted to examine Paul. Paul says: Rather examine yourselves.
> Verse 10: Once more Paul stresses: Authority is there for building up, not for destroying (compare 10:8).

---

## Greeting and blessing (Verses 11–14)

[11] Finally, brothers:
Rejoice!
Let yourselves be set right.
Let yourselves be comforted.
Be of one mind.
Live in peace.
And the God of love and peace will be with you.
[12] Greet one another with a holy kiss.
[13] All the saints greet you.
[14] The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ,
God’s love,
and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit
be with you all.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: After all the hard words, Paul ends with joy, comfort and peace.
> Verse 14: This blessing names Jesus Christ, God (the Father) and the Holy Spirit together. It is still spoken in many worship services today.
> About verse numbers: The WEB divides the end into verses 12, 13 and 14. Many German Bibles have only 13 verses here. There verses 12–13 are one verse, and the blessing is verse 13.

## Looking back: What have we read in the second letter to the Corinthians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A very personal letter:** Paul speaks openly about fear, despair, suffering and comfort. He shows his feelings for the church.
> **The God of all comfort:** God comforts us in every trouble, so that we can comfort others (chapter 1).
> **Reconciliation:** After quarrels and hurt, the church makes peace with Paul. God himself has reconciled the world to himself through Christ. Christians are ambassadors of reconciliation (chapters 2, 5 and 7).
> **A treasure in clay jars:** People are weak and fragile. But God’s power works through them (chapter 4).
> **Generosity:** The collection for the poor Christians in Jerusalem shows: Christians share with one another, freely and with joy (chapters 8 and 9).
> **Strong in weakness:** Paul doesn’t boast about his successes, but about his weaknesses. God says to him: My grace is enough for you (chapters 10 to 13).
> **An important note:** In chapter 3 Paul speaks about the old and the new covenant. These words were often used against Jews. That was wrong. Paul himself is proud to be a Jew (11:22).
> **For today:** No one has to be strong or perfect to be loved and used by God.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Paul’s letter to the churches in Galatia. In it Paul fights passionately for the freedom of Christians. A famous thought from it: In Christ all are one, whether Jew or Greek, slave or free, man or woman (Galatians 3:28).
