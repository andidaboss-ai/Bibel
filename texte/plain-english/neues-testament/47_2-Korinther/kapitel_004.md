# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 4
### A treasure in clay jars

---

## The light of the Good News (Verses 1–6)

[1] So we have this ministry
because God has shown us mercy.
And we don’t lose heart.
[2] We have turned away from everything
that people hide out of shame.
We don’t act with trickery,
and we don’t twist the word of God.
Instead we make the truth plain.
In this way we recommend ourselves to every person’s conscience
in the sight of God.
[3] Even if our Good News is veiled,
it is veiled among those
who are dying.
[4] Among them the god of this world
has blinded the minds of the unbelieving.
So the light of the Good News
of the glory of Christ,
who is the image of God,
cannot dawn on them.
[5] For we don’t preach ourselves,
but Christ Jesus as Lord.
And we present ourselves as your servants
for Jesus’ sake.
[6] For God said:
“Light will shine out of darkness.”
He has also shone in our hearts,
so that the knowledge of the glory of God
shines out in the face of Jesus Christ.

*ministry* = service for God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Paul works openly and honestly. He has nothing to hide.
> Verse 4: “The god of this world” means Satan, the power of evil. Paul says: When people don’t understand the Good News, it is not God’s fault.
> Verse 5: Paul doesn’t want to make himself great. He is a servant.
> Verse 6 recalls the beginning of the Bible: *Let there be light* (Genesis 1:3). Just as God created light back then, he creates light in people’s hearts.

---

## Strong in weakness (Verses 7–15)

[7] But we have this treasure in clay jars.
So it becomes clear
that the surpassing power comes from God
and not from us.
[8] We are pressed on every side,
yet not crushed.
We are puzzled,
yet not in despair.
[9] We are hunted,
yet not abandoned.
We are struck down,
yet not destroyed.
[10] We always carry in our body the dying of Jesus,
so that the life of Jesus
may also be seen in our body.
[11] For we who live
are always handed over to death for Jesus’ sake,
so that the life of Jesus
may also be seen in our mortal body.
[12] So death works in us,
but life in you.
[13] But we have the same spirit of faith,
as it is written:
“I believed,
and therefore I spoke.”
We also believe,
and therefore we also speak.
[14] For we know:
He who raised the Lord Jesus
will also raise us through Jesus
and present us together with you.
[15] For all things are for your sake.
So grace will spread through more and more people
and cause even more thanks to the glory of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: “Clay jars” were simple, cheap pots that break easily. Paul says: We human beings are weak and fragile. But in us is a precious treasure: the Good News of Jesus.
> Verses 8–9: Paul describes honestly how hard his life is. But each time he says: “yet not.” God doesn’t let him fall.
> Verse 13 quotes Psalm 116:10 (in the Greek Bible 115:1).
> For today: No one has to be strong to be used by God. Especially in our weakness God’s power can be seen.

---

## What cannot be seen is eternal (Verses 16–18)

[16] So we don’t lose heart.
Even though our outer person is wasting away,
our inner person
is renewed day by day.
[17] For our light trouble,
which lasts only for a moment,
is producing for us an eternal weight of glory
that far surpasses everything.
[18] For we don’t look at the things
which are seen,
but at the things
which are not seen.
For the things which are seen
are temporary.
But the things which are not seen
are eternal.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: “Our outer person” is the body, which gets older and weaker. “Our inner person” is life with God, which becomes new every day.
> Verse 17: Paul calls his trouble “light.” That doesn’t mean that suffering isn’t bad. Paul suffered greatly (chapter 11). But compared with what God will give, it seems small to him.
> Important: No one has to play down their suffering. Whoever suffers may lament and look for help.
> Verse 18: The most important things in life often cannot be seen: love, hope, God himself.
