# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 5
### Made new and reconciled

---

## A house in heaven (Verses 1–10)

[1] For we know:
If our earthly house, this tent, is taken down,
we have a building from God,
a house
not made with hands,
eternal, in the heavens.
[2] For in this tent we groan
and long
to be clothed with our dwelling from heaven.
[3] When we are clothed with it,
we will not be found naked.
[4] For we
who are in this tent
groan and are burdened.
We don’t want to be unclothed,
but to be clothed,
so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life.
[5] God has prepared us for this very thing.
He has also given us the Spirit as a down payment.
[6] So we are always confident.
We know:
While we are at home in the body,
we are away from the Lord.
[7] For we walk by faith,
not by sight.
[8] We are confident
and would rather be away from the body
and at home with the Lord.
[9] So we also make it our aim
to please him,
whether we are at home or away.
[10] For we must all be revealed before the judgment seat of Christ.
Then each one will receive
what he has done in the body,
whether good or bad.

*groan* = to sigh deeply because something is hard

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul compares our body with a tent: It is only for a time. Paul was a tentmaker himself. After death God gives a solid house, a new life with him.
> Verses 2–4: “To be clothed”: Paul doesn’t hope simply to get rid of the body, but that God will give us a new life that overcomes death.
> Verse 7: “We walk by faith, not by sight.” We don’t see God now. But we trust him.
> Verse 8: Paul longs to be with Jesus. That does not mean that anyone should end their life. At the same time Paul wants to go on living and to serve others (compare Philippians 1:21–25). If you are thinking about suicide, get help right away. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verse 10: Every person is responsible to God for their life.

## The love of Christ drives us (Verses 11–15)

[11] Because we know
what the fear of the Lord means,
we try
to persuade people.
But we are known to God.
And I hope
that we are also known in your consciences.
[12] We are not recommending ourselves to you again.
Instead we are giving you a reason
to be proud of us.
Then you can answer those
who boast about outward appearance
and not about
what is in the heart.
[13] For if we are out of our minds,
it is for God.
And if we are of sober mind,
it is for you.
[14] For the love of Christ drives us.
For we have come to this conclusion:
One died for all,
so all died.
[15] He died for all,
so that those
who live
should no longer live for themselves,
but for him
who died and rose again for them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul’s opponents boasted about outward things, for example great speeches and letters of recommendation. Paul says: What counts is the heart.
> Verse 13: “Out of our minds” perhaps means special spiritual experiences. Paul says: They are a matter between me and God. For the church what counts is that I speak clearly and understandably.
> Verse 14: “The love of Christ drives us”: That is the driving force behind everything Paul does.
> Verse 15: Whoever belongs to Christ no longer lives only for themselves.

## A new creation (Verses 16–21)

[16] So from now on we know no one
according to the flesh.
Even though we once knew Christ according to the flesh,
we don’t know him that way any more.
[17] So:
If anyone is in Christ,
he is a new creation.
The old things have passed away.
Look,
all things have become new.
[18] But all things are from God.
He reconciled us to himself through Jesus Christ.
And he gave us the ministry of reconciliation.
[19] That is:
God was in Christ
reconciling the world to himself.
He doesn’t count people’s wrongs against them.
And he has entrusted us with the word of reconciliation.
[20] So we are ambassadors on behalf of Christ.
God himself is pleading through us.
We beg you on behalf of Christ:
Be reconciled to God!
[21] God made him
who knew no sin
to be sin for us,
so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.

*reconcile* = to make peace again after a quarrel
*ambassador* = someone who speaks for a king or a country

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: The WEB says “according to the flesh.” That means: by human standards. Paul once judged Jesus only from the outside, as a crucified man. Now he sees him with the eyes of faith.
> Verse 17: “A new creation”: Through Christ a person can start completely new. The past no longer has to decide who they are.
> Verses 18–20: Reconciliation is a key word in this chapter. It is not God who has to be reconciled. God himself takes the first step and reconciles the world to himself.
> Verse 20: Christians are “ambassadors” of reconciliation. They are to make peace, with God and among people.
> Verse 21: Jesus was without sin. He took our guilt on himself, so that we can be righteous before God. Later Martin Luther called this a *joyful exchange*.
> For today: Reconciliation is also possible between people and between nations. Christians are called to work for it.
