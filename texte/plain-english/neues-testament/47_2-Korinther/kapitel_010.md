# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 10
### Paul defends his ministry

---

> **Important note first**
> From chapter 10 on, the tone of the letter changes sharply. Paul becomes harsh and defends himself against opponents in Corinth. That is why some scholars think that chapters 10 to 13 were originally a separate letter, perhaps the letter written *with many tears* (2:4).
> The opponents were Christian traveling preachers who praised themselves. Later Paul mockingly calls them the *very best apostles* (11:5).

---

## Our weapons are not human (Verses 1–6)

[1] I, Paul, myself,
beg you
by the humility and gentleness of Christ.
I am lowly when I am face to face with you,
but bold toward you when I am away.
[2] Yes, I beg you:
Don’t force me,
when I am with you,
to act with the boldness
with which I intend to stand up against some people.
They think
that we live in a merely human way.
[3] For though we live as human beings,
we don’t fight in a merely human way.
[4] For the weapons of our fight are not human,
but mighty before God
for tearing down strongholds.
[5] We tear down arguments
and every proud thing
that rises up against the knowledge of God.
And we take every thought captive
to obey Christ.
[6] And we are ready
to punish all disobedience
once your obedience is complete.

*stronghold* = a fortress, a strong building that is hard to capture

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The opponents said about Paul: He writes bold letters, but when he is here, he is weak.
> Verses 3–5: Paul speaks of a fight. But that is a picture. His “weapons” are words, truth and prayer. It is about wrong ideas, not about violence against people.
> Important: These verses give no one the right to use violence in the name of faith.
> Verse 6: “Punish” here means: As an apostle Paul wants to act against wrong developments in the church, with words and warnings.

## Letters and presence (Verses 7–11)

[7] Do you only look at
what is in front of your face?
If anyone is convinced
that he belongs to Christ,
let him think again:
As he belongs to Christ,
so we also belong to Christ.
[8] For even if I boast a little too much
about our authority,
which the Lord gave us
to build you up
and not to tear you down,
I will not be ashamed.
[9] I don’t want to seem
as if I wanted to frighten you with my letters.
[10] For some say:
“His letters are weighty and strong.
But when he is here in person,
he is weak,
and his speech is worth nothing.”
[11] Whoever talks like that
should think about this:
What we say in our letters when we are away,
we will also do
when we are present.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Authority in the church is there to build up, not to tear down. That is still true today for everyone who has responsibility in the church.
> Verse 10: Paul was clearly not an impressive speaker. Perhaps he was also ill or looked weak. His opponents made fun of him for it.
> For today: A person’s worth doesn’t depend on how they come across or how they look.

## Boast in the Lord (Verses 12–18)

[12] For we don’t dare
to compare ourselves with some of those
who recommend themselves.
They measure themselves by themselves
and compare themselves with themselves.
So they are without understanding.
[13] But we will not boast beyond proper limits,
but only within the limits
that God has given us.
And these limits reach even to you.
[14] For we are not going too far,
as if we had not reached you.
For we really did come all the way to you
with the Good News of Christ.
[15] We don’t boast beyond proper limits
about the work of others.
But we hope:
As your faith grows,
our area of work will become much larger through you.
[16] Then we can preach the Good News in the regions beyond you too,
without boasting about
what someone else has already done.
[17] But:
“He who boasts,
let him boast in the Lord.”
[18] For it isn’t the one
who recommends himself
who is approved,
but the one
whom the Lord recommends.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: The opponents only compared themselves with themselves and thought they were wonderful.
> Verses 13–16: Paul founded the church in Corinth. But the opponents came later and wanted to take credit for someone else’s work.
> Verse 17 takes up Jeremiah 9:24 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 9:23). Paul already used these words in 1 Corinthians 1:31.
> Verse 18: It is not praising yourself that counts, but what God says about a person.
