# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 11
### What Paul suffered

---

## Worry for the church (Verses 1–6)

[1] I wish
that you would put up with a little foolishness from me.
But you are already putting up with me.
[2] For I am jealous for you
with a jealousy
that comes from God.
For I promised you in marriage to one husband,
so that I might present you as a pure virgin to Christ.
[3] But I am afraid
that somehow your minds might be led astray
and you might lose your simple devotion to Christ,
as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning.
[4] For if someone comes
and preaches another Jesus
whom we didn’t preach,
or if you receive a different spirit
which you didn’t receive,
or a different “good news”
which you didn’t accept,
you put up with that well enough.
[5] For I think
that I am not at all behind the “very best apostles.”
[6] Even if I am unskilled in speech,
I am not unskilled in knowledge.
We have shown you this in every way and in all things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Paul compares the church to a bride and Christ to the bridegroom. Paul is like the father who leads the bride to the wedding. This picture for God and his people already appears in the prophets (for example Hosea 2).
> Verse 3 recalls Genesis 3. Paul names Eve as an example of how one can be deceived. This is no statement that women are more easily deceived. Here Paul is warning the whole church.
> Verse 4: The opponents preached a different message. Paul is worried that the church will let itself be led astray.
> Verse 5: Paul says “very best apostles” mockingly about his opponents.

## Paul preached without charge (Verses 7–15)

[7] Or did I commit a sin
by humbling myself
so that you might be lifted up?
Because I preached God’s Good News to you free of charge?
[8] I robbed other churches
by taking wages from them
so that I could serve you.
[9] And when I was with you and in need,
I wasn’t a burden to anyone.
For the brothers
who came from Macedonia
supplied what I needed.
In everything I kept myself
from being a burden to you,
and I will continue to do so.
[10] As surely as the truth of Christ is in me:
No one will take this boasting from me
in the regions of Achaia.
[11] Why?
Because I don’t love you?
God knows I do.
[12] But what I do,
I will continue to do.
That way I take the opportunity away from those
who are looking for an opportunity
to be seen as equal to us
in what they boast about.
[13] For such people are false apostles,
deceitful workers.
They disguise themselves as Christ’s apostles.
[14] And no wonder.
For even Satan disguises himself
as an angel of light.
[15] So it is no great thing
if his servants also disguise themselves
as servants of righteousness.
Their end will match their deeds.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–9: Paul took no money from the Corinthians. He lived from his own work and from gifts from other churches. His opponents said this showed that he was not a real apostle.
> Verse 8: “Robbed” is a strong exaggeration. Paul means: Other churches paid for me.
> Verses 13–15: Paul warns against people who look pious but deceive others.
> For today: Today too there are people who pretend to be pious leaders and who use, manipulate or abuse others. If you experience this, you may get help, for example from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse. If you have experienced sexual abuse: In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## I speak like a fool (Verses 16–21)

[16] I say again:
Let no one think I am a fool.
But if you do,
then accept me as a fool,
so that I too may boast a little.
[17] What I am saying now
I don’t say as the Lord would,
but as in foolishness,
in this confident boasting.
[18] Since many boast in a human way,
I will boast too.
[19] For you gladly put up with fools,
since you are so wise.
[20] For you put up with it
if someone makes you slaves,
if someone exploits you,
if someone takes advantage of you,
if someone puts himself above you,
if someone strikes you in the face.
[21] To my shame I say:
We were too weak for that!
But in whatever way anyone else is bold,
I speak in foolishness:
I am bold too.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paul doesn’t like to praise himself. But because the opponents boast, he does it too, and he himself calls it “foolishness.” That is irony.
> Verse 20: The opponents treated the church badly: They exploited them and humiliated them. And the church put up with it.
> Important: No one has to let themselves be exploited, humiliated or hit in a church. That is wrong.

## The sufferings of the apostle (Verses 22–33)

[22] Are they Hebrews?
So am I.
Are they Israelites?
So am I.
Are they the offspring of Abraham?
So am I.
[23] Are they servants of Christ?
(I speak as if I were out of my mind.)
I am more so:
with far more hard work,
far more often in prisons,
with beatings beyond measure,
often in danger of death.
[24] Five times I received 40 lashes minus one
from the Jews.
[25] Three times I was beaten with rods.
Once I was stoned.
Three times I was shipwrecked.
I spent a night and a day in the open sea.
[26] I have often been on journeys,
in dangers from rivers,
in dangers from robbers,
in dangers from my own people,
in dangers from the Gentiles,
in dangers in the city,
in dangers in the wilderness,
in dangers at sea,
in dangers among false brothers.
[27] I have known hard work and toil,
often sleepless nights,
hunger and thirst,
often fasting,
cold and not enough clothing.
[28] And besides all that,
there is what presses on me every day:
my worry for all the churches.
[29] Who is weak,
and I am not weak?
Who is made to stumble,
and I don’t burn inside?
[30] If I must boast,
I will boast about my weakness.
[31] The God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ,
who is blessed forever,
knows
that I am not lying.
[32] In Damascus the governor under King Aretas
had the city of the Damascenes guarded
in order to arrest me.
[33] But I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall.
So I escaped his hands.

*lashes* = blows with a whip

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: Paul stresses: He is a Jew, an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham. He is proud of it.
> Verse 24: The “40 lashes minus one” were a punishment of the synagogue courts (compare Deuteronomy 25:3). The fact that Paul accepted this punishment shows: He still saw himself as a member of the Jewish community. This verse is not a judgment on the Jewish people.
> Verse 25: “Beaten with rods” was a Roman punishment (compare Acts 16:22–23). Acts 14:19 tells about the stoning.
> Verse 26: Paul was in danger from his own people and from people of other nations, and even from “false brothers,” that is, Christians.
> Verses 29–30: Paul doesn’t boast about his successes, but about his weakness. Especially in that, God’s power shows itself.
> Verses 32–33: Acts 9:23–25 also tells about this escape.
