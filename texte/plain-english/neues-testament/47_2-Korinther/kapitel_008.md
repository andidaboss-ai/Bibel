# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 8
### Giving generously

---

## The example of the churches in Macedonia (Verses 1–7)

[1] Brothers,
we want to tell you about the grace of God
which has been given to the churches of Macedonia.
[2] They were severely tested by great trouble.
But their overflowing joy
and their deep poverty
have overflowed into rich generosity.
[3] For I testify:
They gave
as much as they were able,
yes, even beyond their ability,
and they did it of their own free will.
[4] They begged us very earnestly
to let them take part in this grace
and in the fellowship of serving the saints.
[5] They did more than we had expected.
First they gave themselves to the Lord,
and then to us,
through the will of God.
[6] So we urged Titus:
As he had made a beginning before,
so he should also complete this grace among you.
[7] You are rich in everything:
in faith,
in speech,
in knowledge,
in all earnestness
and in your love for us.
So see that you are also rich in this grace.

> **What does this mean?**
> Generosity means: being glad to give.
> Paul was collecting money for the poor Christians in Jerusalem (compare 1 Corinthians 16:1–4 and Romans 15:25–27).
> Verses 2–3: The churches in Macedonia, for example in Philippi and Thessalonica, were poor themselves. Even so, they gave with joy.
> Paul calls giving “grace.” Generosity is a gift from God.

---

## How Jesus became poor (Verses 8–15)

[8] I don’t say this as a command.
But through the eagerness of others I want to test
whether your love is also genuine.
[9] For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ:
He was rich,
yet for your sake he became poor,
so that through his poverty you might become rich.
[10] I give you my advice in this.
It is good for you.
For a year ago you were the first to start,
not only to do something,
but also to want to do it.
[11] But now finish the work as well.
As you were ready
to want it,
so you should also complete it,
as far as you are able.
[12] For if the readiness is there,
the gift is welcome
according to what a person has,
not according to
what he doesn’t have.
[13] It is not about others having it easy
while you are in need.
[14] It is about fairness.
Right now your plenty supplies what they lack,
so that one day their plenty may supply what you lack.
That way there is fairness.
[15] As it is written:
“He who gathered much
had nothing left over.
And he who gathered little
had no lack.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Paul doesn’t command. Giving should be voluntary.
> Verse 9: An important sentence about Jesus: With God he was “rich.” But he became a human being, lived poor and died on the cross. In this way he made us rich.
> Verse 12: Everyone should give what they can, not more. God looks at the readiness, not at the size of the amount.
> Verses 13–14: It is not about givers becoming poor themselves, but about a fair balance between rich and poor.
> Verse 15 quotes Exodus 16:18. When God provided manna in the wilderness, everyone had enough.
> For today: Christians work for fairness in the world, for example through donations, fair trade and help for people in need.

## Titus and his companions (Verses 16–24)

[16] But thanks be to God,
who puts the same eagerness for you into the heart of Titus.
[17] For he accepted our request.
But because he himself is very eager,
he is coming to you of his own accord.
[18] We are sending with him the brother
whom all the churches praise for his work for the Good News.
[19] And not only that:
The churches also appointed him
to travel with us
to bring this gift.
We do this service for the glory of the Lord
and to show our readiness.
[20] In this way we avoid
anyone blaming us
about this large sum
that we are managing.
[21] For we pay attention to what is honorable,
not only in the sight of the Lord,
but also in the sight of people.
[22] We are sending with them another brother of ours.
We have often tested him in many things,
and he has always been eager.
Now he is even more eager,
because he has great confidence in you.
[23] As for Titus:
He is my partner and fellow worker for you.
And as for our brothers:
They are the apostles of the churches,
the glory of Christ.
[24] So show them before the churches
the proof of your love
and that we are right to be proud of you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 18 and 22: Paul doesn’t name these brothers. We don’t know who they were.
> Verses 20–21: Paul deals with the money very carefully. Several people travel with the gift. So no one can accuse him of dishonesty.
> For today: Whoever manages donations should do so openly and honestly.
> Verse 23: The WEB says “apostles.” Here this means messengers who were sent by the churches.
