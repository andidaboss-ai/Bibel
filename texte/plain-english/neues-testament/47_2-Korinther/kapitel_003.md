# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 3
### Serving the new covenant

---

## You are our letter of recommendation (Verses 1–6)

[1] Are we beginning again
to recommend ourselves?
Or do we need,
as some do,
letters of recommendation to you or from you?
[2] You yourselves are our letter,
written in our hearts.
All people know it and read it.
[3] It is clear:
You are a letter of Christ,
delivered by us.
It is written not with ink,
but with the Spirit of the living God.
It is written not on tablets of stone,
but on tablets
that are hearts of flesh.
[4] We have such confidence through Christ toward God.
[5] Not
that we are able by ourselves
to claim anything as coming from us.
But our ability comes from God.
[6] He also made us able
to be servants of a new covenant,
not of the letter
but of the Spirit.
For the letter kills,
but the Spirit gives life.

*letter of recommendation* = a letter that says someone can be trusted

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Other preachers came to Corinth with letters of recommendation. Paul says: I don’t need any. The church itself is my letter of recommendation.
> Verse 3: Here Paul takes up words of the prophets. In Jeremiah 31:31–33 God promises a new covenant in which he writes his law on people’s hearts. In Ezekiel 36:26 God promises a heart of flesh instead of a heart of stone. These promises were first given to Israel.
> Verse 6: “The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life”: Paul doesn’t mean that God’s commandments are bad (compare Romans 7:12). He means: Written rules alone cannot change a person. Only God’s Spirit can do that.
> Important: This verse has often been wrongly used against Judaism, as if Jews only clung to a *dead letter*. That is unjust. In Judaism too, faith lives from God’s Spirit and from love for God’s teaching.

## Greater glory (Verses 7–11)

[7] The service of death,
engraved in letters on stones,
already came with glory.
The people of Israel could not look at Moses’ face,
because of the glory of his face,
which was passing away.
[8] Won’t the service of the Spirit
have much more glory?
[9] For if the service of condemnation has glory,
then the service of righteousness
is even richer in glory.
[10] For what was made glorious
has in this respect no glory at all,
compared with the glory
that surpasses everything.
[11] For if what passes away
came with glory,
then what remains
is much more glorious.

*condemnation* = being judged guilty

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Paul recalls Exodus 34:29–35. When Moses came down from Mount Sinai, his face shone because he had talked with God.
> Verses 7–11: Paul compares: The gift of the Torah at Sinai was already full of glory. Paul says this clearly. But in Christ he sees an even greater glory.
> “The service of death” and “the service of condemnation” are hard words. Paul means: The commandment shows people their guilt, but it alone cannot save them. He does not say that the Torah is worthless.

---

## The veil is taken away (Verses 12–18)

[12] Because we have such a hope,
we speak with great boldness.
[13] We are not like Moses,
who put a veil over his face,
so that the people of Israel wouldn’t look at the end
of what was passing away.
[14] But their minds were hardened.
For until this very day the same veil remains
when the old covenant is read.
For only in Christ is it taken away.
[15] Yes, to this day a veil lies on their heart
when Moses is read.
[16] But whenever someone turns to the Lord,
the veil is taken away.
[17] Now the Lord is the Spirit.
And where the Spirit of the Lord is,
there is freedom.
[18] But we all, with unveiled face,
see the glory of the Lord as in a mirror.
And we are changed into the same image,
from glory to glory,
as it comes from the Lord,
the Spirit.

*veil* = a cloth that covers the face

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Paul interprets the story from Exodus 34:33–35 in his own way.
> Verses 14–15: Paul describes why many of his own people do not recognize Jesus as the Christ. He speaks as a Jew about his own people, with pain, not with contempt.
> Very important: In church history these verses were often used against Jews. In the Middle Ages, the “Synagogue” was shown as a woman with her eyes blindfolded. Such pictures encouraged contempt and hatred. That was wrong. Paul himself says in the letter to the Romans: God has not rejected his people (Romans 11:1–2), and his gifts and his calling can never be taken back (Romans 11:29).
> Verse 17: “Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.” God’s Spirit sets people free.
> Verse 18: Whoever looks at Jesus becomes more like him step by step.
