# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 6
### Now is the time of grace

---

## Now is the day of salvation (Verses 1–10)

[1] As God’s fellow workers we also beg you:
Don’t receive the grace of God in vain.
[2] For he says:
“At an acceptable time I listened to you.
In a day of salvation I helped you.”
Look,
now is the acceptable time.
Look,
now is the day of salvation.
[3] We give no cause for stumbling in anything,
so that our service may not be blamed.
[4] But in everything we show ourselves to be servants of God:
in great endurance,
in troubles,
in hardships,
in distress,
[5] in beatings,
in prisons,
in riots,
in hard work,
in sleepless nights,
in fasting,
[6] in purity,
in knowledge,
in patience,
in kindness,
in the Holy Spirit,
in sincere love,
[7] in the word of truth,
in the power of God,
with the armor of righteousness
on the right hand and on the left,
[8] in honor and dishonor,
in bad reputation and good reputation,
regarded as deceivers and yet true,
[9] as unknown and yet well known,
as dying,
and look:
we live,
as punished and yet not killed,
[10] as sorrowful
yet always rejoicing,
as poor
yet making many rich,
as having nothing
and yet possessing all things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Salvation means: being saved, rescued by God.
> Verse 2 quotes Isaiah 49:8. Paul says: The time the prophet spoke about has come now. Now is the time to accept God’s grace.
> Verses 4–10: Paul describes his life as an apostle: full of hardship, but carried by God. He shows opposites: What looks like defeat from the outside is in truth riches.
> Verse 7: “The armor of righteousness” is a picture. It means truth and righteousness, not real weapons.
> Verse 10: “As sorrowful yet always rejoicing”: Christians may be sad. But beneath the sadness there is a deep joy that comes from God.

---

## Open your hearts (Verses 11–13)

[11] Our mouth has spoken openly to you, Corinthians.
Our heart has opened wide.
[12] There is no lack of room for you in us.
But there is a lack of room in your own hearts.
[13] I speak to you as to my children:
In return, open your hearts wide too.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paul is asking for the church’s love. He has opened his heart to them. He asks: Open your hearts to me too.

---

## You are a temple of God (Verses 14–18)

[14] Don’t be unequally yoked with unbelievers.
For what do righteousness and lawlessness have in common?
Or what does light have in common with darkness?
[15] What agreement does Christ have with Belial?
Or what share does a believer have with an unbeliever?
[16] What agreement does a temple of God have with idols?
For you are a temple of the living God.
As God said:
“I will live in them
and walk among them.
I will be their God,
and they will be my people.”
[17] So:
“‘Come out from among them,
and be separate,’
says the Lord.
‘Touch no unclean thing,
and I will receive you.
[18] I will be a Father to you.
You will be my sons and daughters,’
says the Lord Almighty.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Yoke means: a wooden bar that joins two animals so they can pull together.
> Verse 14: “Unequally yoked” is a picture from farming: You didn’t put two very different animals under the same yoke (compare Deuteronomy 22:10). Paul means: Christians should not get so involved with idol worship that their faith suffers.
> Important: In 1 Corinthians 7:12–14 Paul clearly said that Christians should stay in a marriage with a partner who is not a Christian. So this verse is not a command to separate from people of other faiths. Nor is it permission to despise people of other faiths.
> Verse 15: “Belial” is a Hebrew name for Satan, the power of evil.
> Verses 16–18 combine several words from the Bible of Israel: Leviticus 26:11–12, Ezekiel 37:27, Isaiah 52:11 and 2 Samuel 7:14.
> Verse 18: Here God clearly also names “daughters.” Women and men are equally children of God.
