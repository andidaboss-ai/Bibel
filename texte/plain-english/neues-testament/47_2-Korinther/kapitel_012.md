# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 12
### Strong in weakness

---

## Paul is caught up into heaven (Verses 1–6)

[1] It doesn’t do me any good
to boast.
But I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord.
[2] I know a man in Christ.
He was caught up into the third heaven 14 years ago.
Whether he was in the body,
I don’t know.
Or whether he was out of the body,
I don’t know.
God knows.
[3] And I know this man.
Whether he was in the body
or outside of the body,
I don’t know.
God knows.
[4] He was caught up into Paradise.
There he heard words
that cannot be spoken.
No human being is allowed to say them.
[5] On behalf of such a man I will boast.
But on my own behalf I will not boast,
except about my weaknesses.
[6] For if I wanted to boast,
I would not be a fool.
For I would be speaking the truth.
But I hold back.
No one should think more of me
than what he sees in me
or hears from me.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paradise means: here: the place where God is.
> Verses 2–4: Paul is speaking about himself, but in the third person, as if it were about someone else. That way he doesn’t show off.
> “The third heaven” and “Paradise” mean the place where God is. Paul had a special experience of being close to God. He himself cannot say exactly what happened.
> Verse 6: Paul doesn’t want to be admired because of special experiences, but only for what people see in him and hear from him.

---

## “My grace is sufficient for you” (Verses 7–10)

[7] So that I would not become too proud
because of these surpassingly great revelations,
a thorn in the flesh was given to me:
a messenger of Satan
to torment me,
so that I would not become too proud.
[8] I begged the Lord three times about this,
that it might leave me.
[9] But he said to me:
“My grace is sufficient for you,
for my power is made perfect in weakness.”
So I will most gladly boast about my weaknesses,
so that the power of Christ may rest on me.
[10] So I take pleasure in weaknesses,
in insults,
in hardships,
in persecutions
and in distress,
for Christ’s sake.
For when I am weak,
then I am strong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: We don’t know what the “thorn in the flesh” was. Some think of an illness, for example of the eyes, or headaches. Others think of persecution or of his opponents.
> Important: Illness or disability are not a punishment from God and not a sign of too little faith. Paul prayed three times for healing and was not healed. Even so, he was an apostle and loved by God.
> Verse 9: This is one of the best-known words of the Bible. God doesn’t take away every hardship. But he says: My grace is enough. Especially in weakness God’s power can work.
> Verse 10: “When I am weak, then I am strong”: Paul doesn’t have to be strong. He trusts in the power of Christ.
> Important: This text doesn’t mean that you should look for suffering or abuse or put up with it. If you experience violence, you may protect yourself and get help. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Paul’s worry for the church (Verses 11–21)

[11] I have become foolish in boasting.
You forced me to.
For really you should have recommended me.
For I am in no way less than the “very best apostles,”
even though I am nothing.
[12] The signs of an apostle
were done among you with all patience,
with signs,
wonders
and mighty works.
[13] For in what way were you treated worse
than the other churches?
Only in this:
that I myself was not a burden to you.
Forgive me this wrong!
[14] Look,
this is the third time I am ready
to come to you.
And I will not be a burden to you.
For I am not looking for your possessions,
but for you.
For the children ought not to save up for the parents,
but the parents for the children.
[15] I will most gladly spend everything for you
and give myself completely for you.
If I love you more,
am I loved less?
[16] All right,
I myself was not a burden to you.
But you might say:
I was crafty
and caught you by trickery.
[17] Did I take advantage of you through any of those
whom I sent to you?
[18] I urged Titus to go,
and I sent the brother with him.
Did Titus take advantage of you?
Didn’t we live in the same spirit?
Didn’t we walk in the same steps?
[19] Do you think again
that we are defending ourselves to you?
In the sight of God we speak in Christ.
But everything,
beloved,
is done
to build you up.
[20] For I am afraid
that when I come I might not find you
the way I want,
and that you might not find me
the way you want.
I am afraid
that there may be strife,
jealousy,
outbursts of anger,
factions,
slander,
gossip,
pride
and disorder.
[21] I am afraid
that when I come again my God will humble me before you
and that I will have to mourn over many
who sinned earlier
and have not turned away
from the uncleanness,
sexual immorality
and lust
that they have practiced.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul also did miracles in Corinth. But he doesn’t boast about them.
> Verse 13: “Forgive me this wrong!” is irony: Paul took no money from them.
> Verse 14: “The third time”: Paul had already been in Corinth twice. He is planning a third visit.
> Verses 14–15: Paul loves the church as a father loves his children. He doesn’t want to take anything from them, but to give to them.
> Verses 20–21: Paul is worried that the problems in Corinth have not yet been solved.
