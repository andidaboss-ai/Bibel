# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 7
### Joy about the reconciliation

---

## Open your hearts to us (Verses 1–4)

[1] Since we have these promises, beloved,
let’s cleanse ourselves
from everything
that makes body and spirit unclean.
And let’s make our holiness complete
in the fear of God.
[2] Open your hearts to us.
We wronged no one.
We corrupted no one.
We took advantage of no one.
[3] I don’t say this
to condemn you.
For I have said before:
You are in our hearts
to die together
and live together.
[4] I speak to you very openly.
I am very proud of you.
I am filled with comfort.
In all our trouble
I overflow with joy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “These promises” are God’s promises from 6:16–18: God wants to live with his people and be their Father.
> Verse 2: Paul stresses: He has harmed no one and taken advantage of no one. His opponents had probably accused him of this.
> Verse 3: “You are in our hearts to die together and live together”: Paul feels very deeply connected with the church.

---

## Titus brings good news (Verses 5–16)

[5] For even when we came into Macedonia,
our body had no rest.
We were troubled on every side:
fighting outside,
fear inside.
[6] But God,
who comforts the downcast,
comforted us
by the coming of Titus.
[7] And not only by his coming,
but also by the comfort
he had received from you.
He told us
about your longing,
about your sorrow
and about your zeal for me.
So I rejoiced even more.
[8] For even if I made you sad with my letter,
I don’t regret it,
even though I did regret it before.
For I see
that this letter made you sad,
though only for a short time.
[9] But now I rejoice.
Not
that you were made sad,
but
that your sadness led you to repentance.
For you were made sad in a godly way.
So you suffered no harm from us in anything.
[10] For godly sorrow
produces repentance
that leads to salvation
and brings no regret.
But the sorrow of the world produces death.
[11] For look
what this godly sorrow
has produced in you:
what earnest care,
yes, what defense,
what indignation,
what fear,
what longing,
what zeal,
what readiness
to set things right!
In everything you have shown
that you are innocent in this matter.
[12] So I wrote to you,
but not because of the one
who did the wrong,
nor because of the one
who suffered the wrong.
But your earnest care for us
was to become clear among you in the sight of God.
[13] That is why we have been comforted.
And on top of our comfort came a much greater joy:
the joy of Titus.
For his spirit has been refreshed by all of you.
[14] For I had boasted to him about you,
and I was not disappointed.
But just as everything we told you was true,
so our boasting about you to Titus also proved true.
[15] And his love for you is even greater
when he remembers your obedience,
and how you welcomed him with fear and trembling.
[16] I rejoice
that I can be confident about you in everything.

> **What does this mean?**
> Repentance means: turning away from wrong and turning back to God.
> Indignation means: anger about something unjust.
> Verses 5–7: Here the story from 2:12–13 continues. Paul was full of fear. Then Titus came with good news from Corinth: The church has made peace with Paul.
> Verse 6: “God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus.” God often comforts through other people, here through Titus.
> Verses 8–10: Paul distinguishes two kinds of sadness: a sadness that leads to repentance and a new beginning, and a sadness that only pulls you down.
> For today: If you cannot get free of sadness, you may be suffering from depression. Depression is an illness, not a weakness of faith. Help is available from doctors and therapists. In a crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 12: There was one person who did wrong and one who suffered wrong (compare 2:5–11).
> Verse 15: “With fear and trembling” here means: with great respect and seriousness.
