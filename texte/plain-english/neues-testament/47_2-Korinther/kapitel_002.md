# 2 Corinthians – Chapter 2
### Forgiveness and the aroma of Christ

---

## A letter written with tears (Verses 1–4)

[1] But I decided for myself
that I would not come to you again in sorrow.
[2] For if I make you sad,
then who will make me glad
except the one
whom I have made sad?
[3] And I wrote this very thing to you for that reason.
For when I came,
I didn’t want to be made sad by those
who should make me rejoice.
I am confident about all of you
that my joy is the joy of all of you.
[4] For out of much trouble
and anguish of heart
I wrote to you with many tears.
I didn’t want to make you sad,
but I wanted you to know
how great my love for you is.

> **What does this mean?**
> Anguish means: great pain and worry.
> Paul had visited the church once, and it was a sad visit. Afterwards he wrote a letter “with many tears.” This letter has probably not survived. Some scholars think that parts of it are found in 2 Corinthians 10 to 13.
> Verse 4: Paul writes strictly, but out of love. He doesn’t want to destroy the relationship, but to heal it.

---

## Forgive him (Verses 5–11)

[5] But if anyone has caused sorrow,
he has not caused sorrow to me,
but in part to all of you.
I don’t want to say it too strongly.
[6] For such a person the punishment
which the majority of you gave him
is enough.
[7] So now you should rather forgive him
and comfort him,
so that he isn’t swallowed up by too much sorrow.
[8] So I beg you:
Show him your love.
[9] For this is also why I wrote:
I wanted to test you,
whether you are obedient in all things.
[10] Whomever you forgive anything,
I forgive too.
For if I have forgiven anything,
I have forgiven it for your sake,
in the presence of Christ,
[11] so that Satan doesn’t take advantage of us.
For we know his schemes well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–8: Someone in Corinth had hurt Paul badly. The church punished him. Now Paul says: That is enough. Forgive him, comfort him and show him your love.
> Verse 7: Paul doesn’t want the person to break under his sorrow. Punishment should never lead to despair.
> Verse 11: Satan wants quarrels and an unwillingness to make peace. Forgiveness spoils his plans.
> For today: Forgiveness is important. But forgiveness doesn’t mean that victims of violence have to live with the offender again. Safety comes first.

## The aroma of Christ (Verses 12–17)

[12] When I came to Troas
to preach the Good News of Christ,
and a door was opened to me in the Lord,
[13] I still had no rest in my spirit,
because I didn’t find my brother Titus.
So I said goodbye to them
and went on to Macedonia.
[14] But thanks be to God!
In Christ he always leads us in a victory parade
and through us makes known in every place
the sweet aroma of his knowledge.
[15] For we are a sweet aroma of Christ to God
among those
who are being saved
and among those
who are perishing.
[16] To the one we are a stench from death to death,
to the other a sweet aroma from life to life.
Who is fit for these things?
[17] For we are not like so many
who sell the word of God for profit.
But we speak in Christ
with sincerity,
as from God
and in the sight of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Aroma means: a pleasant smell.
> Verses 12–13: Troas was a port city in what is now Turkey. Paul waited there for Titus, who was to bring news from Corinth. Because Titus didn’t come, Paul was very restless. The story continues in chapter 7:5–7.
> Verse 14: A “victory parade” was a Roman triumphal procession. Incense was often burned during it. Paul uses this picture: God leads him along, and everywhere a pleasant smell spreads: the message of Jesus.
> Verse 16: The Good News has different effects: Some accept it, others reject it.
> Verse 17: Paul doesn’t preach for money. In those days there were traveling preachers who did business with religion.
