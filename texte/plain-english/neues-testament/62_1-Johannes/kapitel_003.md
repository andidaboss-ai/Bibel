# 1 John – Chapter 3
### We are God’s children

---

## Children of God (Verses 1–3)

[1] See
how great the love is
that the Father has given us:
We should be called children of God!
That is why the world doesn’t know us,
because it didn’t know him.
[2] Dear friends,
now we are children of God.
It is not yet revealed
what we will be.
But we know:
When he appears,
we will be like him.
For we will see him
just as he is.
[3] Everyone who has this hope set on him
purifies himself,
just as he is pure.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A wonderful sentence: God calls people his children. You don’t have to earn this. It is a gift of love.
> Verse 2: “Already now” and “not yet”: We are already God’s children. But we don’t know yet what it will be like when it is complete. We will see God as he is.

---

## Whoever belongs to God does what is right (Verses 4–10)

[4] Everyone who sins
also acts against the law.
Sin is lawlessness.
[5] You know
that he was revealed
to take away our sins.
And in him is no sin.
[6] Whoever remains in him
doesn’t sin.
Whoever sins
hasn’t seen him
and doesn’t know him.
[7] Little children,
don’t let anyone lead you the wrong way.
Whoever does what is right
is righteous,
just as he is righteous.
[8] Whoever sins
is of the devil.
For the devil has been sinning from the beginning.
This is why the Son of God was revealed:
to destroy the works of the devil.
[9] Whoever is born of God
doesn’t commit sin.
For God’s seed remains in him.
And he can’t sin,
because he is born of God.
[10] This is how it becomes clear
who the children of God are
and who the children of the devil are:
Whoever doesn’t do what is right
is not of God.
And neither is he
who doesn’t love his brother.

> **Important note**
> Verses 6 and 9 sound as if Christians never make mistakes any more. But in chapter 1:8 the same letter says: Whoever says that he has no sin deceives himself. Both belong together.
> What is probably meant: Whoever belongs to God no longer lives in evil constantly and on purpose. He makes mistakes, but he doesn’t stay in them. He comes back to God again and again.
> No one has to despair because they still make mistakes. God forgives (1:9).

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Righteous means: living right before God.
> Verse 8: Jesus came to defeat evil.
> Verse 9: “God’s seed” is a picture: God has put something of his life into people. It grows and changes them.
> Verse 10: The measure is simple: righteousness and love. That is how you recognize who belongs to God, not by words that only sound religious.

## Love one another (Verses 11–18)

[11] For this is the message
that you heard from the beginning:
We should love one another.
[12] We should not be like Cain.
He belonged to the evil one
and killed his brother.
And why did he kill him?
Because his deeds were evil,
but his brother’s deeds were righteous.
[13] Don’t be surprised,
my brothers,
if the world hates you.
[14] We know
that we have passed out of death into life,
because we love the brothers.
Whoever doesn’t love his brother
remains in death.
[15] Whoever hates his brother
is a murderer.
And you know:
No murderer has eternal life
remaining in him.
[16] By this we have known love:
He laid down his life for us.
And we also ought to lay down our lives
for the brothers.
[17] But if anyone has enough to live on
and sees
that his brother is in need,
and then closes his heart against him,
how can the love of God remain in him?
[18] My little children,
let’s not love only with words
or only with the tongue,
but in deed and in truth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 recalls Cain and Abel (Genesis 4). Cain was jealous and killed his brother.
> Verse 15: A hard saying: Hatred is the beginning of murder. Jesus says something similar in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:21–22).
> Verse 16: Jesus laid down his life. For us this usually means: using our time, our strength, our money for others.
> Verses 17–18: Love is very practical. Whoever has something and sees that someone is hungry or in need is to help. Nice words alone are not love.

> **Important note**
> “Laying down your life” doesn’t mean that you have to endure violence or being exploited. Whoever experiences violence may protect themselves and get help. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK). For domestic violence: US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## God is greater than our heart (Verses 19–24)

[19] By this we know
that we are of the truth.
And so we can reassure our heart before him.
[20] For if our heart accuses us:
God is greater than our heart,
and he knows all things.
[21] Dear friends,
if our heart doesn’t accuse us,
we have boldness toward God.
[22] And whatever we ask,
we receive from him.
For we keep his commandments
and do the things that please him.
[23] And this is his commandment:
We should believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ
and love one another,
just as he commanded.
[24] Whoever keeps his commandments
remains in God,
and God remains in him.
And by this we know
that he remains in us:
by the Spirit
whom he gave us.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20 is a great comfort for all who blame themselves harshly: God is greater than our heart. He knows us completely and is gracious anyway. Our bad conscience doesn’t have the last word.
> Whoever feels guilty and worthless all the time can talk about it. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 22: This doesn’t mean that God grants every wish. Whoever is connected with God asks for what God also wants.
> Verse 23: The whole commandment in one sentence: believing in Jesus and loving one another.
