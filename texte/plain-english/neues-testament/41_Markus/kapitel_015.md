# Mark – Chapter 15
### Jesus is crucified

---

## Jesus before Pilate (Verses 1–15)

[1] Immediately, early in the morning,
the chief priests held a meeting
with the elders, the scribes,
and the whole council.
They tied Jesus up,
led him away,
and handed him over to Pilate.
[2] Pilate asked him,
“Are you the King of the Jews?”
He answered,
“So you say.”
[3] The chief priests accused him of many things.
[4] Pilate asked him again,
“Have you no answer?
See how many things they are saying against you!”
[5] But Jesus made no further answer,
so that Pilate was amazed.
[6] At the feast he used to release one prisoner to them,
whomever they asked for.
[7] There was a man called Barabbas.
He was in prison with his fellow rebels,
men who had committed murder in the uprising.
[8] The crowd, crying aloud,
began to ask him
to do as he always did for them.
[9] Pilate answered them,
“Do you want me to release to you the King of the Jews?”
[10] For he saw
that the chief priests had handed him over out of envy.
[11] But the chief priests stirred up the crowd
to have him release Barabbas to them instead.
[12] Pilate asked them again,
“Then what should I do with the one
you call the King of the Jews?”
[13] They shouted again,
“Crucify him!”
[14] Pilate said to them,
“Why, what evil has he done?”
But they shouted all the louder,
“Crucify him!”
[15] Pilate wanted to please the crowd.
So he released Barabbas to them.
But he had Jesus flogged
and handed him over to be crucified.

> **What does this mean?**
> Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea from about AD 26 to 36. Only he could have a death sentence carried out. Other sources from this time describe him as a harsh and cruel ruler.
> For the Romans, “King of the Jews” was a political charge: Anyone who called himself king without the emperor’s permission was considered a rebel.
> Verse 8: The WEB says “crying aloud.” Instead, many old manuscripts say that the crowd came up.
> Verse 11: The crowd is stirred up by some leaders. It is a particular crowd on a particular morning.
> Flogged: beaten hard with a whip.
> Verse 15: Pilate knows that Jesus is innocent. Even so, he condemns him in order to please the crowd. He bears the responsibility for the sentence. The Christian creed says that Jesus suffered under Pontius Pilate.
> Very important: For centuries Christians held “the Jews” responsible for the death of Jesus. That is wrong and led to persecution and murder, all the way to the Holocaust. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. In 1965 the Catholic Church in *Nostra Aetate*, and the Protestant churches too, clearly declared: The death of Jesus may not be blamed on all the Jews of that time, nor on the Jews of today.

## The soldiers mock Jesus (Verses 16–20)

[16] The soldiers led him away into the courtyard,
that is, the Praetorium, the governor’s headquarters.
They called together the whole company of soldiers.
[17] They dressed him in purple.
They twisted together a crown of thorns
and put it on him.
[18] Then they began to salute him,
“Hail, King of the Jews!”
[19] They hit him on the head with a reed,
spat on him,
knelt down,
and bowed before him to mock him.
[20] When they had mocked him,
they took the purple cloak off him
and put his own clothes back on him.
Then they led him out
to crucify him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Purple was the color of kings. The Roman soldiers dress Jesus up as a king to make fun of him.
> Jesus is humiliated, beaten and mocked. He suffers torture.
> For today: Jesus stands by all people who are humiliated, tortured or abused.

## The crucifixion (Verses 21–32)

[21] A man was passing by,
coming in from the country:
Simon of Cyrene,
the father of Alexander and Rufus.
They forced him
to go with them and carry Jesus’ cross.
[22] They brought Jesus to the place
called Golgotha,
which means “The place of a skull.”
[23] They offered him wine mixed with myrrh to drink.
But he didn’t take it.
[24] They crucified him
and divided his clothes among them.
They cast lots for them
to decide what each one should take.
[25] It was the third hour
when they crucified him.
[26] Above him was written the charge against him:
“THE KING OF THE JEWS.”
[27] With him they crucified two robbers,
one on his right and one on his left.
[28] So the Scripture was fulfilled which says,
“He was counted with transgressors.”
[29] Those who passed by
insulted him.
They shook their heads and said,
“Ha! You who destroy the temple
and build it in three days,
[30] save yourself,
and come down from the cross!”
[31] In the same way the chief priests also mocked him
among themselves, together with the scribes, and said,
“He saved others.
He can’t save himself.
[32] Let the Christ, the King of Israel,
come down from the cross now,
so that we may see and believe him.”
Those who were crucified with him
also insulted him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Cyrene was a city in North Africa. Many Jews lived there. Only Mark names Simon’s sons, Alexander and Rufus. The first readers probably knew them. A Rufus is also mentioned in Romans 16:13. Whether it is the same man, no one knows.
> Golgotha (Aramaic) means “skull.” At that time the place was outside the city wall.
> Verse 23: Wine mixed with myrrh was perhaps meant to dull the pain. Jesus refuses it.
> Casting lots: a way of deciding something by chance, a bit like rolling dice.
> Verse 24 reminds us of Psalm 22:18 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 22:19).
> Verse 25: The third hour is about 9 in the morning.
> Verse 28 is missing in the best old manuscripts. It quotes Isaiah 53:12. The WEB has it. Transgressors: people who break the law, criminals.
> Crucifixion was the cruelest death penalty of the Romans, mainly for slaves and rebels.
> Verse 31: Again it is certain leaders who mock him, not the whole people.

## Jesus dies (Verses 33–41)

[33] When the sixth hour had come,
there was darkness over the whole land
until the ninth hour.
[34] At the ninth hour
Jesus cried out with a loud voice,
“Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani?”
which means,
“My God, my God,
why have you forsaken me?”
[35] Some of those standing there
heard it and said,
“Look, he is calling Elijah.”
[36] Someone ran,
filled a sponge with vinegar,
put it on a reed,
gave it to him to drink,
and said,
“Leave him alone!
Let’s see
whether Elijah comes to take him down.”
[37] Jesus cried out with a loud voice
and gave up the spirit.
[38] The curtain of the temple was torn in two
from top to bottom.
[39] The centurion was standing there opposite him.
When he saw
that he cried out like this and breathed his last,
he said,
“Truly this man was the Son of God!”
[40] There were also women
watching from a distance.
Among them were Mary Magdalene,
Mary the mother of James the less and of Joses,
and Salome.
[41] When he was in Galilee,
they had followed him and served him.
And there were many other women
who had come up with him to Jerusalem.

> **What does this mean?**
> The sixth hour is about 12 noon, the ninth about 3 in the afternoon. Three hours of darkness in broad daylight.
> Verse 34: “Eloi, Eloi …” is Aramaic, Jesus’ native language. It is the beginning of Psalm 22 (Psalm 22:1; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 22:2). This psalm begins with a lament, but ends with trust and praise of God.
> Forsaken: left completely alone, abandoned.
> Jesus feels the deepest pain, including the feeling of being abandoned by God. That is why we, too, may lament to God like this. Whoever feels abandoned by God is not alone. Jesus knows this feeling.
> Verse 38: The curtain separated the Most Holy Place in the temple. Christians understand its tearing like this: Through Jesus’ death the way to God is open for everyone.
> Verse 39: A centurion is a Roman officer in charge of about 100 soldiers. This verse is the high point of the Gospel of Mark. The whole time the question was: Who is Jesus? Now a Roman officer, a non-Jew, says it at the cross: “Truly this man was the Son of God!” At the beginning these words stood in the title (1:1) and at the baptism (1:11).
> Verses 40–41: The disciples have fled. But the women stay. They are faithful disciples who have followed Jesus for a long time and served him.

## Jesus is buried (Verses 42–47)

[42] It was already evening.
It was the Preparation Day,
that is, the day before the Sabbath.
[43] So Joseph of Arimathaea came.
He was a respected member of the council.
He too was waiting for God’s Kingdom.
He boldly went to Pilate
and asked for Jesus’ body.
[44] Pilate was surprised to hear
that he was already dead.
He called the centurion
and asked him
whether he had been dead long.
[45] When he found out from the centurion,
he gave the body to Joseph.
[46] Joseph bought a linen cloth,
took Jesus down from the cross,
and wrapped him in the linen cloth.
He laid him in a tomb
that had been cut out of a rock.
Then he rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb.
[47] Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses
saw where he was laid.

> **What does this mean?**
> The “Preparation Day” was Friday, when everything was prepared for the Sabbath. The Sabbath begins on Friday evening at sunset. That is why the burial had to happen quickly.
> According to Jewish law, someone who had been executed was to be buried on the same day (Deuteronomy 21:22–23).
> Joseph of Arimathaea was a Jewish member of the Council. He shows: Not all the leaders were against Jesus. He is brave, because it was dangerous to stand up for someone who had been crucified.
> Verse 44: Pilate is surprised, because people who were crucified often died only after days. The centurion confirms: Jesus is really dead.
> Verse 47: The women see where Jesus is buried. That is why on Easter morning they find the right tomb.
