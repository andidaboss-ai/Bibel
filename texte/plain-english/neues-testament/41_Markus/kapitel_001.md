# Mark – Chapter 1
### The beginning of the Good News

---

## Before we begin: The Gospel of Mark

The Gospel of Mark is the shortest of the four Gospels. Most scholars think it is the oldest. It was probably written around AD 70, perhaps shortly before or shortly after the destruction of the temple in Jerusalem.
According to an old church tradition, it was written by John Mark. He was a companion of Peter and Paul (Acts 12:12; 1 Peter 5:13). Peter is said to have told him many things. We do not know this for certain. The book itself does not name an author.
Mark tells his story in a lively and fast way. One of his favorite words is “immediately.” He reports fewer speeches than Matthew, but more of the things Jesus did. He often tells small details that the others leave out.
A big theme in Mark is the question: Who is Jesus? For a long time the disciples don’t understand. Jesus often does not want people to call him the Christ yet. Only at the cross does a Roman centurion say that this man was truly the Son of God (15:39).
Many stories from Mark are also in Matthew and Luke, often with almost the same words. That is why scholars think: Matthew and Luke knew and used the Gospel of Mark.
An important note: In Mark, too, there are arguments between Jesus and other Jewish teachers. These were arguments within Judaism. Jesus and all his disciples were Jews. These texts must never be used to look down on Jews.
The Gospel has 16 chapters.

## John the Baptizer (Verses 1–8)

[1] The beginning of the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.
[2] As it is written in the prophets,
“I send my messenger ahead of you.
He will prepare your way before you.
[3] The voice of one crying in the wilderness:
‘Make ready the way of the Lord!
Make his paths straight!’”
[4] So John came.
He baptized in the wilderness
and preached a baptism of repentance
for the forgiveness of sins.
[5] All the country of Judea
and all the people of Jerusalem went out to him.
They were baptized by him in the Jordan River
and confessed their sins.
[6] John wore clothes made of camel’s hair
and a leather belt around his waist.
He ate locusts and wild honey.
[7] He preached,
“After me comes one who is mightier than I.
I am not worthy
to stoop down
and untie the strap of his sandals.
[8] I baptized you in water.
But he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Mark begins without a birth story. He starts right away with Jesus as an adult. The first sentence is like a title over the whole book.
> The words “the Son of God” in verse 1 are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verses 2–3: The quotation combines Malachi 3:1 and Isaiah 40:3. Many old manuscripts say “Isaiah the prophet” instead of “the prophets.” Here the WEB follows other manuscripts.
> Repentance: turning around, changing your life and turning to God again. Baptism in the Jordan was a sign of this.
> His clothing reminds us of the prophet Elijah (2 Kings 1:8). According to the Law of Moses, locusts were allowed as food (Leviticus 11:22). Locusts: large grasshoppers.
> Verse 7: Taking off someone’s sandals was the work of slaves. John says: I am not even worthy to serve the one who is coming in this way.

## Jesus is baptized and tempted (Verses 9–13)

[9] In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee
and was baptized by John in the Jordan.
[10] Immediately, as he came up out of the water,
he saw the heavens opening
and the Spirit coming down on him like a dove.
[11] And a voice came out of the sky:
“You are my Son. I love you.
I am very pleased with you.”
[12] Immediately the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness.
[13] He was in the wilderness 40 days
and was tempted by Satan.
He was with the wild animals,
and the angels were serving him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Nazareth was a small, unimportant village in Galilee, in the north of the country.
> In Mark, Jesus himself sees the open heavens, and the voice speaks directly to him: “You are my Son. I love you.” This reminds us of Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1.
> The dove is a picture of the Holy Spirit, of God’s power and peace.
> Verses 12–13: Mark tells about the temptation very briefly (in more detail in Matthew 4:1–11). The 40 days remind us of Israel’s 40 years in the wilderness and of Moses and Elijah.
> “With the wild animals”: Only Mark tells this. Some interpreters see it as a picture of peace with creation, as in paradise (compare Isaiah 11:6–9).

## Jesus begins to preach and calls disciples (Verses 14–20)

[14] After John had been arrested,
Jesus came into Galilee.
He preached the Good News of God’s Kingdom
[15] and said,
“The time is fulfilled,
and God’s Kingdom is near!
Repent, and believe in the Good News!”
[16] As he was walking along the Sea of Galilee,
he saw Simon and Andrew, Simon’s brother.
They were casting a net into the sea,
for they were fishermen.
[17] Jesus said to them,
“Come, follow me!
I will make you into fishers for people.”
[18] Immediately they left their nets
and followed him.
[19] When he had gone on a little farther,
he saw James the son of Zebedee
and his brother John.
They were also in the boat, mending the nets.
[20] Immediately he called them.
They left their father Zebedee
in the boat with the hired workers
and followed him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 sums up Jesus’ whole message: God’s Kingdom is near. God is now beginning to set up his good rule. So: Turn around and trust in it!
> In Greek, “the Good News” is “euangelion.” That is where the word “Gospel” comes from.
> Jesus calls ordinary fishermen. They leave everything and follow him at once.
> “Fishers for people”: A play on words. From now on they are to win people for God’s Kingdom.
> Verse 20: Only Mark mentions the hired workers. So Zebedee is not left alone. His family apparently had a small fishing business.

## Jesus in the synagogue of Capernaum (Verses 21–28)

[21] They went into Capernaum.
Immediately on the Sabbath he went into the synagogue
and taught.
[22] The people were amazed at his teaching.
For he taught them as one who has authority,
and not like the scribes.
[23] Immediately there was a man in their synagogue
with an unclean spirit.
He cried out,
[24] “Ha! What do we have to do with you,
Jesus, you Nazarene?
Have you come to destroy us?
I know who you are:
the Holy One of God!”
[25] Jesus rebuked him and said,
“Be quiet, and come out of him!”
[26] The unclean spirit shook the man violently,
cried out with a loud voice,
and came out of him.
[27] They were all amazed.
They asked one another,
“What is this?
What is this new teaching?
With authority he commands even the unclean spirits,
and they obey him!”
[28] And immediately the news about him spread
through the whole region of Galilee and the area around it.

> **What does this mean?**
> Capernaum was a town on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus lived there for a while. Today you can see the remains of a later synagogue there. It may stand on the foundations of the synagogue from Jesus’ time.
> On the Sabbath people met in the synagogue to pray and to hear the Holy Scriptures. Like every devout Jew, Jesus goes there and teaches.
> Verse 22: Jesus does not only teach what other teachers have said. He speaks with his own, divine authority. This is not a judgment on all scribes.
> “Unclean spirit”: In those days people explained some illnesses or suffering of the soul this way. Important: Mental illnesses are not a sin and not a punishment. Whoever is mentally ill needs understanding and help, for example from doctors and therapists.
> Verses 24–25: The spirit recognizes Jesus. But Jesus orders it to be silent. In Mark, Jesus often does not want people to say too early who he is.

## Jesus heals many people (Verses 29–39)

[29] Immediately, when they had come out of the synagogue,
they went into the house of Simon and Andrew,
together with James and John.
[30] Simon’s mother-in-law was lying in bed with a fever.
Immediately they told Jesus about her.
[31] He went to her,
took her by the hand,
and raised her up.
The fever left her immediately,
and she served them.
[32] In the evening, when the sun had set,
they brought to him all who were sick
and those who were possessed by demons.
[33] The whole town was gathered at the door.
[34] He healed many who were sick with various diseases
and cast out many demons.
He didn’t allow the demons to speak,
because they knew him.
[35] Early in the morning, while it was still dark,
he got up and went out
to a deserted place.
There he prayed.
[36] Simon and those who were with him
searched for him.
[37] They found him and said to him,
“Everyone is looking for you!”
[38] He said to them,
“Let’s go somewhere else,
into the next towns,
so that I may preach there too.
For this is why I came.”
[39] And he went into their synagogues throughout all Galilee,
preaching and casting out demons.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 30: Simon (Peter) was married. His mother-in-law lived in the house with them.
> Verse 31: Jesus takes the sick woman by the hand. He touches her without hesitating. After that, “she served them.” The Greek word for serving is the same word that is used for the service of the angels (verse 13) and for Jesus’ own service (10:45). So she becomes one of the first to serve Jesus.
> Verse 32: The Sabbath ends at sunset. That is why people only bring their sick in the evening. On the Sabbath they did not want to carry loads.
> Verse 35: Jesus needs time alone with God. In spite of all the work, he takes time to pray.
> Verse 38: Jesus does not let himself be held back, even though everyone wants him. He knows what he came for.
> Important: If sick people do not get well today, it is not their fault. Illness is not a sign of too little faith.

## Jesus heals a leper (Verses 40–45)

[40] A leper came to him.
He knelt down in front of him,
begged him, and said,
“If you want to,
you can make me clean.”
[41] Being moved with compassion,
he stretched out his hand,
touched him,
and said to him,
“I want to. Be made clean.”
[42] When he had said this,
the leprosy left him immediately,
and he was made clean.
[43] Jesus strictly warned him
and sent him away at once.
[44] He said to him,
“See that you say nothing to anybody.
But go,
show yourself to the priest,
and offer for your cleansing the things
that Moses commanded.
That will be a testimony to them.”
[45] But the man went out
and began to tell it everywhere
and to spread the news.
So Jesus could no longer openly go into a town.
He stayed outside in deserted places.
And people came to him from everywhere.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days “leprosy” was a name for various skin diseases. Whoever had it was considered unclean. They had to live apart from other people (Leviticus 13:45–46). That meant great loneliness.
> Jesus touches the sick man. No one else did that. He not only gives him back his health, but also his place in the community.
> Verse 41: Instead of “moved with compassion,” some old manuscripts say that Jesus became angry. Perhaps Jesus was angry about the illness and the suffering. The WEB has compassion.
> Verse 44: Jesus sends the man to the priest, as the Law of Moses requires (Leviticus 14). Jesus respects the law. The priest was to confirm that the man was healthy. Then he could return to the community.
> For today: People are still excluded today because of illnesses. Jesus shows: No one should be left alone.
