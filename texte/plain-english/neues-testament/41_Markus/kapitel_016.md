# Mark – Chapter 16
### He has risen!

---

## The empty tomb (Verses 1–8)

[1] When the Sabbath was over,
Mary Magdalene,
Mary the mother of James,
and Salome
bought spices,
so that they could go and anoint Jesus.
[2] Very early on the first day of the week
they came to the tomb,
when the sun had risen.
[3] They were saying to one another,
“Who will roll away the stone from the entrance of the tomb for us?”
[4] For it was very big.
When they looked up,
they saw
that the stone had already been rolled back.
[5] They went into the tomb
and saw a young man
sitting on the right side.
He was dressed in a white robe.
And they were very alarmed.
[6] He said to them,
“Don’t be alarmed!
You are looking for Jesus, the Nazarene,
who was crucified.
He has risen!
He is not here.
See the place
where they laid him!
[7] But go,
tell his disciples and Peter,
‘He is going ahead of you into Galilee.
There you will see him,
as he told you.’”
[8] They went out
and fled from the tomb.
For trembling and astonishment had taken hold of them.
And they said nothing to anyone,
for they were afraid.

> **What does this mean?**
> The women who were at the cross (15:40) and had seen where Jesus was buried (15:47) now come to the tomb. With spices and oils they want to pay him their last respects.
> The “first day of the week” is Sunday. That is why Christians celebrate Sunday. Easter is the most important feast of Christians.
> The women are the first to hear: “He has risen!”
> Verse 7: “And Peter”: Only Mark mentions Peter separately here. Peter of all people, who denied Jesus three times, is to know: Jesus wants to see him again. He has not been written off.
> Verse 8: The women flee, full of fear, and say nothing to anyone. That is a surprising ending.
> In the oldest and most important manuscripts the Gospel of Mark ends here with verse 8. Many scholars think: This is how Mark originally ended. The open ending invites the readers: Now it is your turn to pass on the good news. Others think the original ending has been lost.
> Because the ending is so sudden, later copyists added endings. There is a short ending and a long ending. The WEB has the long ending (verses 9–20). It follows here, as in the WEB.

---

## The long ending: Jesus appears (Verses 9–14)

[9] When Jesus had risen early on the first day of the week,
he appeared first to Mary Magdalene.
He had cast seven demons out of her.
[10] She went
and told those who had been with him,
as they were mourning and weeping.
[11] When they heard
that he was alive
and that she had seen him,
they did not believe it.
[12] After this he appeared in another form
to two of them
as they were walking on their way into the country.
[13] They went
and told it to the others.
But they didn’t believe them either.
[14] Later he appeared to the eleven themselves
as they were sitting at the table.
He rebuked them for their unbelief
and hardness of heart,
because they hadn’t believed those
who had seen him after he had risen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–20 are missing in the oldest complete manuscripts, for example the Codex Sinaiticus and the Codex Vaticanus from the 4th century. They were probably added in the 2nd century. Even so, the church accepted them as part of the Bible. Many Bibles print them in brackets or with a note.
> This ending sums up appearances that are also told in other Gospels: the meeting with Mary Magdalene (John 20:11–18), the two disciples on the way (Luke 24:13–35) and the appearance to the eleven (Luke 24:36–43).
> Verse 9: Mary Magdalene is the first witness of the risen Jesus. “Seven demons” probably means a severe illness or great suffering (compare Luke 8:2). It does not mean that she was a bad woman. The old idea that Mary Magdalene was a prostitute is not in the Bible.
> The disciples do not believe at first. Doubt is human. The first disciples too had to find their way to faith.

## The long ending: The commission (Verses 15–18)

[15] He said to them,
“Go into all the world
and preach the Good News to the whole creation.
[16] Whoever believes and is baptized
will be saved.
But whoever does not believe
will be condemned.
[17] These signs will go with those
who believe:
In my name they will cast out demons.
They will speak with new languages.
[18] They will pick up snakes.
And if they drink anything deadly,
it will in no way hurt them.
They will lay hands on the sick,
and they will get well.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: The good news is meant for all people in the whole world.
> Verse 16: A serious word. Christians understand it in different ways. Many stress: God alone judges people. God is merciful and wants all people to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4). No one should use this verse to frighten or condemn other people.
> “New languages” reminds us of Pentecost (Acts 2:4). Acts 28:3–6 tells how Paul was bitten by a snake and was not harmed.
> Very important: Verse 18 is not a call to test God! Some groups have handled poisonous snakes or drunk poison to prove their faith. People have died doing this. In the wilderness Jesus himself said: “Do not test the Lord your God” (Matthew 4:7). That means: You should not put God to the test. Please never do anything like this. In case of poisoning, call your local emergency number (911 in the US) at once, or Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the US. In the UK, call 999 or NHS 111.
> Whoever is sick may pray and should go to the doctor. Both belong together. Whoever does not get well has not believed too little.

## The long ending: Jesus is taken up into heaven (Verses 19–20)

[19] After the Lord had spoken to them,
he was taken up into heaven
and sat down at the right hand of God.
[20] They went out
and preached everywhere.
The Lord worked with them
and confirmed the word
by the signs that went with it.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Jesus is taken up to God. “At the right hand of God” is a picture for the place of honor. It reminds us of Psalm 110:1. Christians celebrate this on Ascension Day, 40 days after Easter (compare Acts 1:9–11).
> Verse 20: The disciples are not alone. The Lord works with them.
> After verse 8 some manuscripts have a short ending. It reports in two sentences: The women told Peter and the others everything. After that, Jesus sent out through the disciples the message of eternal salvation into the whole world, from east to west. The WEB does not have this short ending in the text.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Gospel of Mark?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Fast and lively:** Mark tells his story briefly and in a lively way. His favorite word is “immediately.” He does not begin with the birth, but right away with John the Baptizer and the baptism of Jesus (Chapter 1).
> **Who is Jesus?** That is the big question of the whole book. The people are amazed, the opponents argue, the disciples do not understand for a long time. In the middle of the book Peter confesses: “You are the Christ” (8:29). And at the cross a Roman officer says: “Truly this man was the Son of God!” (15:39).
> **Jesus acts:** Mark tells about many healings. Jesus touches lepers, raises up the sick, gives people back their dignity. He is moved by people’s need, for example by the father who cries out: “I believe. Help my unbelief!” (9:24).
> **The way of serving:** Three times Jesus announces his suffering. And he says: “The Son of Man also came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many” (10:45).
> **Honest disciples:** The disciples often don’t understand, argue about who is the greatest, sleep in Gethsemane and flee. Peter denies Jesus. Mark tells this honestly. And yet Jesus does not give up on them (16:7).
> **Faithful women:** The women stay at the cross, see the tomb and are the first to hear the message: “He has risen!”
> **An important note:** The debates in Mark were arguments within Judaism. Jesus and all the disciples were Jews. A Jewish scribe hears from Jesus: “You are not far from God’s Kingdom” (12:34). These texts must never be used against Jews.
> **For today:** The open ending in verse 8 asks us: Will we pass on what we have heard?
> **What comes next?** Next comes the Gospel of Luke. It is the longest of the four Gospels. Luke tells especially about how Jesus turns to the poor, to women, to the sick and to outcasts. In Luke we find the Christmas story with the manger in Bethlehem and famous parables like the Good Samaritan and the lost son.
