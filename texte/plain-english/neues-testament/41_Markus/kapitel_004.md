# Mark – Chapter 4
### Parables and a storm on the lake

---

## The parable of the farmer who sows (Verses 1–9)

[1] Again Jesus began to teach by the seaside.
A very large crowd gathered around him.
So he got into a boat on the sea
and sat down.
The whole crowd was on the shore, on the land.
[2] He taught them many things in parables.
In his teaching he said to them,
[3] “Listen!
A farmer went out to sow.
[4] As he was sowing,
some seed fell by the road.
The birds came and ate it up.
[5] Other seed fell on rocky ground,
where there was little soil.
It sprang up immediately,
because the soil was not deep.
[6] When the sun came up,
it was scorched.
And because it had no root,
it withered away.
[7] Other seed fell among the thorns.
The thorns grew up and choked it,
and it produced no fruit.
[8] Other seed fell on good ground.
It came up, grew, and produced fruit:
some 30 times as much,
some 60 times,
and some 100 times as much.”
[9] And he said,
“Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear!”

> **What does this mean?**
> This parable is also in Matthew 13:1–9. Mark tells it in a similar way.
> In those days people sowed by throwing the seeds across the field by hand. Some seed was lost. But in the end there is still a great harvest.
> In Mark the harvest grows: 30, 60, 100. God’s word produces more and more fruit.
> Jesus himself explains the parable in verses 14–20.

---

## Why Jesus speaks in parables (Verses 10–12)

[10] When he was alone,
those who were around him,
together with the Twelve,
asked him about the parables.
[11] He said to them,
“To you is given the mystery of God’s Kingdom.
But to those who are outside, everything comes in parables,
[12] so that
‘seeing they may see and not perceive,
and hearing they may hear and not understand,
so that they won’t turn back
and have their sins forgiven.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12 quotes Isaiah 6:9–10. This sentence sounds very harsh, as if Jesus did not want people to understand and turn back.
> Interpreters understand it like this: Jesus describes what actually happens. Many hear his words but close themselves off. It was already like this with the prophet Isaiah. Parables open up only to those who get involved with them.
> Mystery: here, a secret of God that he reveals.
> Important: The whole Gospel shows that Jesus invites all people to repent (1:15). God wants to forgive. This verse is not a judgment on “the Jews.” It is about everyone who does not want to listen, Christians too.
> “Those who are outside” are not outside forever. Whoever comes to Jesus and asks becomes one of “those who were around him.”

## Jesus explains the parable (Verses 13–20)

[13] He said to them,
“Don’t you understand this parable?
Then how will you understand all the parables?
[14] The farmer sows the word.
[15] The ones by the road
are those where the word is sown.
When they have heard it,
immediately Satan comes
and takes away the word
that was sown in them.
[16] In the same way, the ones sown on rocky ground
are those who hear the word
and immediately receive it with joy.
[17] But they have no root in themselves.
They last only a short time.
When trouble or persecution comes
because of the word,
they immediately fall away.
[18] Others are the ones sown among the thorns.
These are those who have heard the word.
[19] But the worries of this age,
the deceitfulness of riches,
and the desire for other things
come in and choke the word.
And it produces no fruit.
[20] The ones sown on the good ground
are those who hear the word,
accept it,
and bear fruit:
some 30 times as much,
some 60 times,
and some 100 times.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The seed is God’s word. The different kinds of ground are different ways in which people respond to it.
> Verse 13: In Mark the disciples often don’t understand. Even so, Jesus is patient with them.
> The parable asks each of us: How do I listen? What is stopping good things from growing in me?
> Important: Worries are part of life. Whoever has many worries is not a bad person. It is about not letting worries and greed choke everything else.

## Light and measure (Verses 21–25)

[21] He said to them,
“Is a lamp brought in
to be put under a basket
or under a bed?
Isn’t it put on a stand?
[22] For nothing is hidden
except to be made known.
And nothing is secret
except to come to light.
[23] If anyone has ears to hear,
let him hear!”
[24] He said to them,
“Pay attention to what you hear!
With whatever measure you measure,
it will be measured to you.
And more will be given to you who hear.
[25] For whoever has,
more will be given to him.
And whoever doesn’t have,
even what he has will be taken away from him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days a lamp was a small oil lamp made of clay. People put it up high so that it lit the whole room.
> God’s Kingdom is still hidden now. But it will come to light. What Jesus teaches now in a small way will later reach everyone.
> Verses 24–25: Whoever is open to God’s word understands more and more. Whoever closes themselves off loses even the little they have. This is not a rule about money or possessions.

## The seed that grows by itself (Verses 26–29)

[26] He said,
“God’s Kingdom is like this:
A man scatters seed on the ground.
[27] He sleeps and gets up,
night and day.
And the seed sprouts and grows,
though he doesn’t know how.
[28] The earth produces fruit by itself:
first the stalk,
then the head of grain,
then the full grain in the head.
[29] But when the fruit is ripe,
immediately he puts in the sickle,
because the harvest has come.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Mark tells this parable.
> The man sows. Then all he can do is wait. The growing happens by itself, without his being able to make it happen or fully understand it.
> That is how it is with God’s Kingdom: God makes it grow. We don’t have to do everything ourselves.
> For today: A comforting parable for everyone who worries about others or works a lot. You may also sleep. God keeps on working.
> Sickle: a curved knife for cutting grain.
> Verse 29 reminds us of Joel 3:13 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 4:13): The harvest is a picture of the end of time.

## The mustard seed (Verses 30–34)

[30] He said,
“What shall we compare God’s Kingdom to?
With what parable shall we describe it?
[31] It is like a mustard seed.
When it is sown in the ground,
it is smaller than all the seeds on the earth.
[32] But when it is sown,
it grows up
and becomes bigger than all the garden plants.
It puts out large branches,
so that the birds of the sky
can live in its shade.”
[33] With many parables like these
he spoke the word to them,
as they were able to hear it.
[34] He didn’t speak to them without a parable.
But when he was alone with his own disciples,
he explained everything to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> The mustard seed was proverbially the smallest seed people knew in everyday life. It grows into a large bush, often over 10 feet high (compare Matthew 13:31–32).
> God’s Kingdom begins very small, with Jesus and a few disciples. But it becomes large, and many find shelter in it, like the birds in the shade.
> Verse 33: Jesus speaks to them “as they were able to hear it.” He thinks of his listeners. That is also the idea behind plain language: God’s word should be understandable for everyone.

## Jesus calms the storm (Verses 35–41)

[35] On that day, when evening had come,
he said to them,
“Let’s go over to the other side.”
[36] They left the crowd
and took him with them in the boat,
even as he was.
Other small boats were also with him.
[37] A big windstorm came up.
The waves beat into the boat,
so that it was already filling with water.
[38] He himself was in the back of the boat,
asleep on the cushion.
They woke him up and said to him,
“Teacher, don’t you care
that we are dying?”
[39] He woke up,
rebuked the wind,
and said to the sea,
“Peace! Be still!”
Then the wind stopped,
and there was a great calm.
[40] He said to them,
“Why are you so afraid?
How is it that you have no faith?”
[41] They were very afraid
and said to one another,
“Who then is this,
that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

> **What does this mean?**
> On the Sea of Galilee strong storms can come up suddenly, because wind comes rushing down from the mountains.
> Only Mark tells small details: the cushion, the other boats, Jesus “even as he was.”
> Jesus sleeps peacefully in the storm. He trusts God completely.
> Verse 38: The disciples ask: “Teacher, don’t you care?” Many people in need ask God this question too. You may ask it.
> Verse 39: Jesus speaks to the storm as if to a living being. In the Old Testament it is God who calms the raging sea (Psalm 107:28–30).
> Verse 40: Many old manuscripts have a slightly different question here. Then Jesus asks whether they still have no faith. The WEB has: “How is it that you have no faith?”
> Verse 41: The disciples ask: Who is this? That is the big question in the Gospel of Mark.
> For today: Whoever is in one of life’s storms may call out to Jesus. Being afraid is not a sign of bad faith. And you may also get help from other people.
