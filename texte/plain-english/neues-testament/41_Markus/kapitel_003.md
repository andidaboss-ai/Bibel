# Mark – Chapter 3
### The Twelve and the new family

---

## Healing on the Sabbath (Verses 1–6)

[1] Jesus went into the synagogue again.
There was a man there
whose hand was withered.
[2] They watched Jesus closely
to see whether he would heal him on the Sabbath.
For they wanted to accuse him.
[3] He said to the man with the withered hand,
“Stand up.”
[4] And he said to them,
“Is it lawful on the Sabbath
to do good or to do harm?
To save a life or to kill?”
But they were silent.
[5] Then he looked around at them with anger.
He was grieved
that their hearts were so hard.
And he said to the man,
“Stretch out your hand.”
He stretched it out,
and his hand was made as healthy as the other.
[6] The Pharisees went out
and immediately made plans with the Herodians
about how they could destroy him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Withered: shriveled and unable to move, for example because of an illness.
> Verse 3: The WEB says only “Stand up.” The Greek word probably also means: Come into the middle! Jesus puts the man in the middle in front of everyone.
> Jesus asks: What is right on the Sabbath, to do good or to do harm? Jewish teachers also said: To save a life you may break the Sabbath. But was this man’s life in danger? People could argue about that. Jesus says: Doing good is always right.
> Verse 5: Only Mark tells us that Jesus was angry and sad. Jesus had real feelings.
> Verse 6: The Herodians were supporters of King Herod. The Pharisees and the Herodians were actually opponents. Here they join together against Jesus. So early in the Gospel it is already clear that Jesus’ way leads to suffering.
> Important: Not all Pharisees were against Jesus. Some later even became Christians (Acts 15:5). And people with disabilities are not worth less. Jesus puts the man in the middle.

## Many people come to Jesus (Verses 7–12)

[7] Jesus withdrew to the sea with his disciples.
A great crowd from Galilee followed him.
From Judea too,
[8] from Jerusalem, from Idumaea,
from beyond the Jordan,
and from the area around Tyre and Sidon,
a great crowd came to him.
They had heard about all the great things he was doing.
[9] He told his disciples
to keep a little boat ready for him,
so that the crowd would not crush him.
[10] For he had healed many.
So all who had diseases pushed toward him
to touch him.
[11] And whenever the unclean spirits saw him,
they fell down in front of him and cried,
“You are the Son of God!”
[12] He sternly warned them
not to make him known.

> **What does this mean?**
> The people come from everywhere: from the south (Judea, Idumaea), from the east (beyond the Jordan) and from the north (Tyre and Sidon, in today’s Lebanon). Non-Jews are among them too.
> Verses 11–12: Again Jesus does not want the spirits to say who he is. In Mark this is called the “Messianic secret.” Jesus does not want to be celebrated as a miracle worker or a political hero. Who he really is becomes clear only at the cross and at the resurrection.

## Jesus chooses the 12 apostles (Verses 13–19)

[13] Jesus went up onto the mountain.
He called to himself those whom he wanted,
and they came to him.
[14] He appointed 12.
They were to be with him,
and he would send them out to preach
[15] and to have authority to heal sicknesses
and to cast out demons.
[16] They were:
Simon, to whom he gave the name Peter;
[17] James the son of Zebedee,
and John, the brother of James,
whom he called Boanerges,
which means “Sons of Thunder”;
[18] Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas,
James the son of Alphaeus,
Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot,
[19] and Judas Iscariot,
who later betrayed him.
Then he went into a house.

> **What does this mean?**
> There are 12 apostles, like the 12 tribes of Israel. Jesus gathers a renewed people of God.
> Verse 14: The first thing is: “to be with him.” Only after that come preaching and healing.
> “Peter” means “rock.” “Sons of Thunder”: Perhaps James and John had a fiery temper (compare Luke 9:54).
> “Simon the Zealot”: The Zealots were zealous Jews, some of whom wanted to fight against the Romans. Matthew, on the other hand, was a tax collector who worked with the Romans. Jesus calls both. In his community very different people come together.
> In verse 14 some old manuscripts also say that he called them apostles. The WEB does not have this.

## Is Jesus out of his mind or in league with evil? (Verses 20–30)

[20] Again a crowd gathered,
so that they could not even eat.
[21] When his friends heard about it,
they went out to take hold of him by force.
For they said,
“He is insane.”
[22] The scribes who had come down from Jerusalem
said,
“He has Beelzebul,”
and,
“By the prince of the demons
he casts out the demons.”
[23] So he called them to himself
and spoke to them in parables:
“How can Satan cast out Satan?
[24] If a kingdom is divided against itself,
that kingdom cannot stand.
[25] If a house is divided against itself,
that house cannot stand.
[26] So if Satan has risen up against himself
and is divided,
he can’t stand,
but his end has come.
[27] No one can go into the house of the strong man
and steal his things
unless he first ties up the strong man.
Only then can he rob his house.
[28] Most certainly I tell you,
people will be forgiven all their sins,
including all the blasphemies they speak.
[29] But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit
never has forgiveness,
but is subject to eternal condemnation.”
[30] He said this because they were saying,
“He has an unclean spirit.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: The WEB says “his friends.” The Greek word means “his own people,” probably his family (compare verse 31). They are worried and think he is confused. Even his own family does not understand Jesus.
> Important: “Insane” was an accusation. Mental illnesses are nothing to be ashamed of. People with mental illnesses deserve respect and help.
> “Beelzebul” is a name for the prince of the demons, Satan.
> Verses 23–27: Jesus shows how senseless the accusation is. Satan would not fight against himself! Jesus is the stronger one who defeats the “strong man” (Satan).
> Verses 28–29: God’s forgiveness is huge: All sins can be forgiven. “Whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit …”: Here this means deliberately calling God’s good work devilish, and so closing yourself off completely against God.
> Condemnation: being judged guilty and punished.
> Very important: Many people are afraid that they have committed this unforgivable sin. Interpreters say: Whoever worries about it and seeks God has precisely not committed it. Whoever cannot get rid of such tormenting thoughts may talk about them with a pastor or counselor. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Jesus’ true family (Verses 31–35)

[31] Then his mother and his brothers came.
They stood outside,
sent someone to him,
and called for him.
[32] Many people were sitting around him.
They said to him,
“Your mother, your brothers, and your sisters
are outside looking for you.”
[33] He answered them,
“Who are my mother
and my brothers?”
[34] He looked around at the people
who were sitting around him
and said,
“Here are my mother and my brothers!
[35] For whoever does the will of God
is my brother, my sister, and mother.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus does not reject his family. He shows: There is a new, large family of God. All who do God’s will belong to it, men and women.
> Verse 32: The words “and your sisters” are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> About the “brothers” of Jesus: Protestant Christians usually understand them as his brothers and sisters by birth, Catholic and Orthodox Christians as close relatives.
> Later Jesus’ family belonged to the first church: Mary and Jesus’ brothers were with the disciples in Jerusalem (Acts 1:14). His brother James became a leader of the church.
> For today: Whoever has no family or has been hurt by their own family can find a home in the community of believers.
