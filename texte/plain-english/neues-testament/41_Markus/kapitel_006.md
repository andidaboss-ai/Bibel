# Mark – Chapter 6
### Sent out, fed, and not alone in the storm

---

## Jesus in his hometown (Verses 1–6)

[1] Jesus went away from there
and came into his hometown.
His disciples followed him.
[2] When the Sabbath came,
he began to teach in the synagogue.
Many who heard him were amazed and said,
“Where did this man get these things?”
and,
“What is this wisdom that has been given to him?
And such mighty works happen by his hands!
[3] Isn’t this the carpenter,
the son of Mary,
the brother of James, Joses, Judah, and Simon?
Aren’t his sisters here with us?”
And they were offended at him.
[4] Jesus said to them,
“A prophet is not without honor,
except in his own hometown,
among his own relatives,
and in his own house.”
[5] He could do no mighty work there.
He only laid his hands on a few sick people
and healed them.
[6] And he was amazed at their unbelief.
He went around the villages and taught.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus’ hometown is Nazareth (1:9). The people have known him since he was a child. That is why they cannot believe that God is working through him.
> Verse 3: In Mark, Jesus himself is called “the carpenter.” The Greek word means a craftsman who builds with wood or stone. Some old manuscripts say instead the son of the carpenter (as in Matthew 13:55).
> It is unusual that he is called “the son of Mary” and not named after his father. Perhaps Joseph was no longer alive.
> About Jesus’ “brothers” and “sisters”: Protestant Christians usually understand them as his brothers and sisters by birth, Catholic and Orthodox Christians as close relatives.
> Verse 5: “He could do no mighty work there”: Jesus forces his help on no one. Where there is no trust, little happens. Even so, he heals a few sick people.
> Important: This does not mean that sick people today are to blame if they don’t get well.

## Jesus sends out the twelve (Verses 7–13)

[7] He called the twelve to himself
and began to send them out two by two.
He gave them authority over the unclean spirits.
[8] He commanded them
to take nothing for the journey
except a walking staff:
no bread, no bag,
no money in their belts.
[9] They were to wear sandals,
but not put on two tunics.
[10] He said to them,
“Wherever you go into a house,
stay there
until you leave that place.
[11] And wherever people will not welcome you
or listen to you,
leave that place
and shake off the dust under your feet
as a testimony against them.
Most certainly I tell you,
on the day of judgment it will be more bearable for Sodom and Gomorrah
than for that city!”
[12] They went out
and preached that people should repent.
[13] They cast out many demons,
anointed many sick people with oil,
and healed them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus sends the disciples two by two. No one should travel alone.
> They are to take almost nothing with them. In this way they show: We trust in God and in people’s hospitality.
> Tunic: a long shirt that was worn under the outer clothing.
> Verse 11: Shaking the dust off your feet was a sign: We have nothing more to do with you. It is not violence and not revenge.
> The sentence about Sodom and Gomorrah is missing in many old manuscripts. A similar sentence is in Matthew 10:15. The WEB has it.
> Verse 13: In those days anointing the sick with oil was also a remedy. In many churches there is still an anointing of the sick today (compare James 5:14).

## The death of John the Baptizer (Verses 14–29)

[14] King Herod heard about this,
for Jesus’ name had become known.
He said,
“John the Baptizer has risen from the dead.
That is why these powers are at work in him.”
[15] But others said,
“He is Elijah.”
Still others said,
“He is a prophet,
like one of the prophets.”
[16] But when Herod heard this,
he said,
“This is John,
whom I beheaded.
He has risen from the dead.”
[17] For Herod himself had sent men to arrest John
and had him tied up in prison.
He did this because of Herodias,
the wife of his brother Philip.
For he had married her.
[18] John had said to Herod,
“It is not lawful for you
to have your brother’s wife.”
[19] Herodias held it against him
and wanted to kill him.
But she couldn’t.
[20] For Herod was afraid of John.
He knew that he was a righteous and holy man,
and he kept him safe.
When he heard him,
he did many things,
and he liked to listen to him.
[21] Then a convenient day came:
On his birthday Herod gave a dinner
for his nobles, the high officers,
and the leading men of Galilee.
[22] The daughter of Herodias came in
and danced.
She pleased Herod and his guests.
The king said to the young lady,
“Ask me for whatever you want,
and I will give it to you.”
[23] He swore to her,
“Whatever you ask of me,
I will give you,
up to half of my kingdom.”
[24] She went out
and said to her mother,
“What shall I ask for?”
Her mother said,
“The head of John the Baptizer.”
[25] Immediately she hurried in to the king
and asked,
“I want you to give me right now
the head of John the Baptizer on a platter.”
[26] The king was very sorry.
But because of his oaths
and because of his dinner guests,
he didn’t want to refuse her.
[27] Immediately the king sent a soldier of his guard
and ordered him to bring John’s head.
He went
and beheaded him in the prison.
[28] He brought his head on a platter
and gave it to the young lady.
And the young lady gave it to her mother.
[29] When John’s disciples heard about it,
they came,
took his body,
and laid it in a tomb.

> **What does this mean?**
> This Herod is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. He ruled over Galilee. He was not really a king, but a regional ruler. But people called him “King.”
> John had criticized Herod because he had married his brother’s wife. That was forbidden by the Law of Moses (Leviticus 18:16). He was put in prison for this brave criticism of a powerful man.
> Beheaded: killed by having the head cut off.
> Verse 20: Only Mark tells us that Herod liked to listen to John and protected him. Herod was torn between two sides.
> Verse 22: Some old manuscripts say here his daughter Herodias. The WEB has “the daughter of Herodias.” The historian Josephus calls a daughter of Herodias Salome.
> Herod makes a careless promise in front of his guests. So as not to look weak in front of them, he has an innocent man killed. The responsibility lies above all with him as ruler.
> Important: This story is not proof that women are seductive or evil. Here powerful people used a girl for their own purposes.
> Verse 29: John’s disciples bury him with dignity.

## Jesus feeds 5,000 people (Verses 30–44)

[30] The apostles gathered around Jesus again.
They told him everything
that they had done and taught.
[31] He said to them,
“Come away to a deserted place,
where you can be by yourselves,
and rest a while.”
For so many people were coming and going
that they didn’t even have time to eat.
[32] They went away in the boat
to a deserted place
to be by themselves.
[33] But many people saw them leaving
and recognized him.
They ran there on foot from all the towns
and arrived ahead of them.
[34] When Jesus got out of the boat,
he saw a large crowd.
He had compassion on them,
because they were like sheep without a shepherd.
And he began to teach them many things.
[35] When it was already late in the day,
his disciples came to him and said,
“This place is deserted,
and it is already late.
[36] Send them away,
so that they can go into the farms and villages around here
and buy themselves bread.
For they have nothing to eat.”
[37] But he answered them,
“You give them something to eat!”
They asked him,
“Shall we go
and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread
and give them something to eat?”
[38] He said to them,
“How many loaves do you have?
Go and see!”
When they had found out,
they said,
“Five, and two fish.”
[39] He told them
to have everyone sit down in groups
on the green grass.
[40] They sat down in groups,
by hundreds and by fifties.
[41] He took the five loaves and the two fish,
looked up to heaven,
blessed,
broke the loaves,
and gave them to his disciples
to set before the people.
He also divided the two fish among them all.
[42] They all ate and were filled.
[43] They picked up twelve baskets full of broken pieces
and also of the fish.
[44] Those who ate the loaves
were 5,000 men.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: Jesus takes care of his disciples. After a lot of work they should rest. Breaks and rest are good and important.
> Verse 34: “Like sheep without a shepherd”: A picture from the Old Testament (Numbers 27:17; Ezekiel 34:5). Jesus is the good shepherd who takes care of his people.
> Verse 37: A denarius was about a day’s wage for a worker. So two hundred denarii were about the wages for two hundred days of work. The disciples think it is impossible.
> Verse 39: Only Mark mentions the “green grass.” That reminds us of Psalm 23:2, where God lets us lie down in green pastures.
> The words took, looked up, blessed, broke and gave remind Christians of the Lord’s Supper (14:22).
> Verse 44: In those days often only the men were counted. Matthew adds that women and children were there too (Matthew 14:21).
> For today: What looks like little becomes enough for everyone in Jesus’ hands. And Jesus still says today: “You give them something to eat!”

## Jesus walks on the water (Verses 45–52)

[45] Immediately after that he made his disciples
get into the boat
and go ahead of him to the other side, to Bethsaida.
Meanwhile he sent the crowd away.
[46] After he had said goodbye to them,
he went up the mountain to pray.
[47] When evening had come,
the boat was in the middle of the sea,
and he was alone on the land.
[48] He saw that they were struggling with the rowing,
for the wind was against them.
About the fourth watch of the night
he came to them,
walking on the sea.
He would have passed by them.
[49] But when they saw him walking on the sea,
they thought it was a ghost
and cried out.
[50] For they all saw him
and were terrified.
But he immediately spoke with them and said,
“Cheer up! It is I!
Don’t be afraid!”
[51] He got into the boat with them,
and the wind stopped.
They were completely amazed.
[52] For they hadn’t understood about the loaves,
but their hearts were hardened.

> **What does this mean?**
> Bethsaida was a town on the north shore of the lake.
> The “fourth watch of the night” is between about 3 and 6 in the morning. The disciples have been fighting against the wind all night.
> Verse 48: Jesus sees his disciples’ distress from far away and comes to them.
> “He would have passed by them”: This reminds us of God, who passed by Moses and Elijah to show himself to them (Exodus 33:22; 1 Kings 19:11). It does not mean that Jesus wanted to leave them alone.
> “It is I” literally means: I am. That is how God names himself in the Old Testament (Exodus 3:14).
> Verse 52: The disciples do not yet understand who Jesus is. In Mark it takes them a long time. That gives courage: Even someone who doesn’t understand right away may stay with Jesus.

## Many sick people are healed (Verses 53–56)

[53] When they had crossed over,
they came to land at Gennesaret
and tied up the boat there.
[54] When they got out of the boat,
the people immediately recognized him.
[55] They ran around that whole region
and began to carry the sick on their mats
to wherever they heard he was.
[56] And wherever he went,
into villages, into towns, or into the country,
they laid the sick in the marketplaces.
They begged him
that they might just touch the fringe of his garment.
And all who touched him
were made well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Gennesaret is a fertile plain on the west shore of the lake. Actually they wanted to go to Bethsaida (verse 45). Perhaps the wind drove them off course.
> The “fringe” probably means the tassels on the garment that devout Jews wore according to God’s commandment (Numbers 15:38–39).
> The people carry their sick to Jesus. They help one another.
> Important: Whoever is sick may pray and go to the doctor. Both belong together. Whoever does not get well is not less faithful.
