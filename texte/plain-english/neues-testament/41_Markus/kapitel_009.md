# Mark – Chapter 9
### “I believe. Help my unbelief!”

---

## The transfiguration on the mountain (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jesus said to them,
“I tell you the truth:
Some of the people standing here
will not die
before they see
God’s Kingdom come with power.”
[2] Six days later Jesus took Peter, James and John with him.
He led them up a high mountain.
They were alone there.
There Jesus was changed in front of them.
He looked different.
[3] His clothes began to shine.
They became very white, like snow.
No one on earth who washes clothes could make them that white.
[4] Elijah and Moses appeared to them.
They were talking with Jesus.
[5] Peter said to Jesus,
“Rabbi, it is good for us to be here.
Let’s make three tents:
one for you, one for Moses and one for Elijah.”
Rabbi means: teacher.
[6] Peter did not know what to say.
They were all very afraid.
[7] Then a cloud came and covered them with its shadow.
A voice came out of the cloud:
“This is my Son. I love him.
Listen to him.”
[8] Suddenly they looked around.
They did not see anyone with them anymore,
only Jesus.

> **What does this mean?**
> Transfiguration: Jesus’ appearance is changed, and his divine glory shines out.
> Verse 1 is hard to understand. Many interpreters relate it to the transfiguration, which is told right after it. Others think of Easter or Pentecost.
> For a moment three disciples see Jesus’ divine glory. Only Mark mentions the “launderer”: No human being can make anything that white. Launderer: someone who washes and bleaches clothes.
> Moses stands for the Law, Elijah for the prophets. Both are among the great figures of Israel. Jesus stands in their midst.
> Verse 6: Mark is honest: Peter speaks out of fear and embarrassment.
> The voice says the same as at the baptism (1:11) and adds: “Listen to him” (compare Deuteronomy 18:15).
> Told in more detail in Matthew 17:1–8.

## The question about Elijah (Verses 9–13)

[9] They were coming down from the mountain.
On the way Jesus gave them an order:
They must not tell anyone what they had seen
until the Son of Man had risen from the dead.
[10] They kept these words to themselves.
But they asked each other
what the “rising from the dead” meant.
[11] They asked Jesus,
“Why do the scribes say
that Elijah must come first?”
[12] He said to them,
“It is true: Elijah comes first.
He sets everything right again.
But what is written about the Son of Man?
It is written that he must suffer many things.
People will look down on him.
[13] But I tell you:
Elijah has already come.
And people have done to him whatever they wanted,
just as it is written about him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples don’t understand what the “rising from the dead” is supposed to mean for Jesus.
> The scribes referred to Malachi: Before the day of the LORD, Elijah will come (Malachi 4:5–6; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 3:23–24).
> Jesus says: Elijah has already come. He means John the Baptizer, who was killed (6:14–29). Jesus too will suffer, as it is written, for example in Isaiah 53 and Psalm 22.

## A father asks for help for his sick son (Verses 14–29)

[14] Jesus and the three disciples came back to the other disciples.
There they saw a large crowd around them.
Some scribes were arguing with them.
[15] As soon as the people saw Jesus,
they were all very surprised.
They ran to him and greeted him.
[16] Jesus asked the scribes,
“What are you arguing with them about?”
[17] A man from the crowd answered,
“Teacher, I brought my son to you.
He has a spirit in him that makes him unable to speak.
[18] Wherever the spirit takes hold of him,
it throws him to the ground.
Then foam comes out of his mouth.
He grinds his teeth.
And his body becomes stiff.
I asked your disciples
to drive the spirit out.
But they could not do it.”
[19] Jesus answered,
“You people of today do not believe!
How long do I still have to be with you?
How long do I have to put up with you?
Bring the boy to me!”
[20] They brought the boy to Jesus.
When the spirit saw Jesus,
it shook the boy hard at once.
The boy fell on the ground.
He rolled around,
and foam came out of his mouth.
[21] Jesus asked the father,
“How long has this been happening to him?”
The father said,
“Since he was a small child.
[22] The spirit has often thrown him into the fire
and into the water.
It wants to kill him.
But if you can do anything,
please feel sorry for us and help us!”
[23] Jesus said to him,
“If you can believe:
Everything is possible for the one who believes.”
[24] At once the father of the boy cried out in tears,
“I believe.
Help my unbelief!”
That means: Help me where I still do not believe.
[25] Jesus saw that a crowd was running together.
So he spoke sharply to the unclean spirit.
He said to it,
“You spirit that makes him unable to speak and hear,
I command you:
Come out of him,
and never go into him again!”
[26] The spirit screamed.
It shook the boy hard,
and it came out.
The boy lay there as if he were dead.
Most of the people said,
“He is dead.”
[27] But Jesus took him by the hand
and helped him up.
And the boy stood up.
[28] Jesus went into the house.
When his disciples were alone with him, they asked him,
“Why could we not drive the spirit out?”
[29] He said to them,
“This kind can only be driven out
by prayer and fasting.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The seizures described here (falling down, foaming at the mouth, grinding the teeth, becoming stiff) are similar to epilepsy. In those days people explained such things as the work of a spirit.
> Very important: Epilepsy is an illness of the brain. It has nothing to do with demons or sin. People with epilepsy need medical help and understanding, not an exorcism. No one may use this story to keep medicine or treatment from sick people.
> Verses 21–22: Jesus asks about the history of the illness. He listens to the father. The father has suffered for years and feared for his child.
> Verse 24: “I believe. Help my unbelief!” That is one of the most honest sentences in the Bible. Faith and doubt are often there at the same time. Jesus accepts this small, doubting faith and helps.
> Important: This does not mean that someone does not get well because they believe too little. Illness is not a sign of too little faith.
> Verse 27: Jesus takes the boy by the hand and raises him up, like the daughter of Jairus (5:41).
> Verse 29: The words “and fasting” are missing in important old manuscripts. The WEB has them.

## Jesus announces his suffering again (Verses 30–32)

[30] They left that place
and traveled through Galilee.
Jesus did not want anyone to know about it.
[31] He was teaching his disciples.
He said to them,
“The Son of Man is being handed over to people.
They will kill him.
But after he has been killed,
he will rise again on the third day.”
[32] But the disciples did not understand what he meant.
And they were afraid to ask him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the second announcement of his suffering (the first was in 8:31).
> The disciples don’t understand it. And they don’t dare to ask. Perhaps they also did not want to accept it.
> For today: You may ask questions, also about hard things in the faith.

## Who is the greatest? (Verses 33–37)

[33] They came to Capernaum.
When Jesus was in the house,
he asked them,
“What were you arguing about on the way?”
[34] But they did not say anything.
On the way they had argued with each other
about who was the greatest.
[35] Jesus sat down.
He called the 12 disciples
and said to them,
“If anyone wants to be first,
he must be the last of all
and the servant of all.”
[36] He took a little child
and stood him in the middle of them.
He took the child in his arms
and said to them,
[37] “Whoever welcomes one little child like this in my name
welcomes me.
And whoever welcomes me
does not welcome only me.
He welcomes the one who sent me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus has just spoken about his suffering. And the disciples argue about who is the most important! They are ashamed and silent.
> Jesus sits down, like a teacher, and explains: With me, whoever serves others is great.
> Only Mark tells us that Jesus takes the child in his arms. In those days children had no rights and counted for little. Jesus puts them in the middle.
> For today: Children are important in the church. Whoever protects children and cares for them does it for Jesus and for God himself.

## Whoever is not against us is for us (Verses 38–41)

[38] John said to Jesus,
“Teacher, we saw someone
driving out demons in your name.
He is not one of us.
We told him to stop,
because he is not one of us.”
[39] But Jesus said,
“Do not stop him!
No one who does a miracle in my name
can soon after that say bad things about me.
[40] Whoever is not against us
is on our side.
[41] I tell you the truth:
Someone may give you a cup of water to drink
in my name,
because you belong to Christ.
That person will surely not lose his reward.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples want to decide who may belong to Jesus. Jesus says: Don’t be so narrow! Whoever does good in my name is not an enemy.
> For today: Christians of different churches should not shut each other out. God also works outside your own group.
> Verse 41: Even a small kindness, a cup of water, is precious to God.

## A warning against leading others astray (Verses 42–50)

[42] “Someone may cause one of these little ones
who believe in me
to fall away from God.
It would be better for him
to have a millstone tied around his neck
and to be thrown into the sea.
[43] If your hand causes you to stumble,
cut it off.
It is better for you
to go into life with only one hand
than to have two hands and go into Gehenna,
into the fire that never goes out,
[44] ‘where their worm does not die,
and the fire does not go out.’
[45] If your foot causes you to stumble,
cut it off.
It is better for you
to go into life with only one foot
than to have two feet and be thrown into Gehenna,
into the fire that never goes out,
[46] ‘where their worm does not die,
and the fire does not go out.’
[47] If your eye causes you to stumble,
throw it away.
It is better for you
to go into God’s Kingdom with one eye
than to have two eyes and be thrown into the fire of Gehenna,
[48] ‘where their worm does not die,
and the fire does not go out.’
[49] Everyone will be salted with fire,
and every sacrifice will be seasoned with salt.
[50] Salt is good.
But if salt loses its salty taste,
how can you make it salty again?
Have salt in yourselves.
And live in peace with each other.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 42: Jesus clearly takes the side of the weak and of children. Whoever harms them does great wrong. Whoever has experienced abuse or knows about it can find help. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673. Children and young people in the UK can call Childline at 0800 1111.
> Millstone: a large, heavy stone for grinding grain.
> Verses 43–47 are picture language. Jesus wants to say: Take evil very seriously. Separate yourself from everything that leads you to evil. He does not mean that anyone should really hurt themselves.
> Whoever is thinking about hurting themselves, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> “Gehenna” was a valley near Jerusalem. It became a picture for God’s judgment.
> Verses 44 and 46 are missing in the best old manuscripts. They repeat verse 48. The WEB has them. The words come from Isaiah 66:24.
> Verse 49 is hard to understand. Salt cleans and preserves. Perhaps it means: Whoever follows Jesus is tested and purified by hard things. The second part (“every sacrifice will be seasoned with salt”) is missing in some manuscripts. It reminds us of Leviticus 2:13.
> Verse 50: At the end comes peace. The disciples who were just arguing about who is the greatest (verse 34) are to be at peace with one another.
