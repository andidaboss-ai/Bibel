# Mark – Chapter 9
### “I believe. Help my unbelief!”

---

## The transfiguration on the mountain (Verses 1–8)

[1] Jesus said to them,
“Most certainly I tell you,
there are some standing here
who will not taste death
until they see
God’s Kingdom come with power.”
[2] After six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John with him
and brought them up onto a high mountain, by themselves.
There he was changed in front of them.
[3] His clothes became shining and very white, like snow,
whiter than any launderer on earth can make them.
[4] Elijah and Moses appeared to them,
and they were talking with Jesus.
[5] Peter said to Jesus,
“Rabbi, it is good for us to be here.
Let’s make three tents:
one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.”
[6] For he didn’t know what to say,
because they were very afraid.
[7] Then a cloud came
and overshadowed them.
And a voice came out of the cloud:
“This is my beloved Son.
Listen to him.”
[8] Suddenly, when they looked around,
they saw no one with them any more
except Jesus only.

> **What does this mean?**
> Transfiguration: Jesus’ appearance is changed, and his divine glory shines out.
> Verse 1 is hard to understand. Many interpreters relate it to the transfiguration, which is told right after it. Others think of Easter or Pentecost.
> For a moment three disciples see Jesus’ divine glory. Only Mark mentions the “launderer”: No human being can make anything that white. Launderer: someone who washes and bleaches clothes.
> Moses stands for the Law, Elijah for the prophets. Both are among the great figures of Israel. Jesus stands in their midst.
> Verse 6: Mark is honest: Peter speaks out of fear and embarrassment.
> The voice says the same as at the baptism (1:11) and adds: “Listen to him” (compare Deuteronomy 18:15).
> Told in more detail in Matthew 17:1–8.

## The question about Elijah (Verses 9–13)

[9] As they were coming down from the mountain,
he commanded them
to tell no one what they had seen
until the Son of Man had risen from the dead.
[10] They kept this saying to themselves.
But they asked one another
what the “rising from the dead” meant.
[11] They asked him,
“Why do the scribes say
that Elijah must come first?”
[12] He said to them,
“Elijah does come first
and restores all things.
And how is it written about the Son of Man?
That he must suffer many things
and be despised.
[13] But I tell you,
Elijah has come,
and they have done to him whatever they wanted,
just as it is written about him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples don’t understand what the “rising from the dead” is supposed to mean for Jesus.
> The scribes referred to Malachi: Before the day of the LORD, Elijah will come (Malachi 4:5–6; in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 3:23–24).
> Jesus says: Elijah has already come. He means John the Baptizer, who was killed (6:14–29). Jesus too will suffer, as it is written, for example in Isaiah 53 and Psalm 22.

## A father asks for help for his sick son (Verses 14–29)

[14] When they came to the disciples,
they saw a large crowd around them
and scribes arguing with them.
[15] Immediately, when the crowd saw Jesus,
they were all very surprised.
They ran to him and greeted him.
[16] He asked the scribes,
“What are you arguing with them about?”
[17] One of the crowd answered,
“Teacher, I brought my son to you.
He has a spirit that makes him mute.
[18] Wherever it seizes him,
it throws him down.
Then he foams at the mouth,
grinds his teeth,
and becomes stiff.
I asked your disciples
to cast it out,
but they couldn’t.”
[19] He answered him,
“Unbelieving generation!
How long shall I be with you?
How long shall I put up with you?
Bring him to me!”
[20] They brought the boy to him.
When the spirit saw Jesus,
immediately it shook the boy violently.
He fell on the ground,
rolled around,
and foamed at the mouth.
[21] Jesus asked the father,
“How long has this been happening to him?”
He said,
“Since he was a child.
[22] It has often thrown him into the fire
and into the water
to destroy him.
But if you can do anything,
have compassion on us and help us!”
[23] Jesus said to him,
“If you can believe:
all things are possible to him who believes.”
[24] Immediately the father of the child cried out with tears,
“I believe.
Help my unbelief!”
[25] When Jesus saw
that a crowd was running together,
he rebuked the unclean spirit and said to it,
“You mute and deaf spirit,
I command you:
come out of him,
and never enter him again!”
[26] The spirit cried out,
shook the boy violently,
and came out.
The boy lay there like one who was dead,
so that most of them said,
“He is dead.”
[27] But Jesus took him by the hand
and raised him up.
And he got up.
[28] When Jesus had come into the house,
his disciples asked him privately,
“Why couldn’t we cast it out?”
[29] He said to them,
“This kind can come out by nothing
but by prayer and fasting.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The seizures described here (falling down, foaming at the mouth, grinding the teeth, becoming stiff) are similar to epilepsy. In those days people explained such things as the work of a spirit.
> Very important: Epilepsy is an illness of the brain. It has nothing to do with demons or sin. People with epilepsy need medical help and understanding, not an exorcism. No one may use this story to keep medicine or treatment from sick people.
> Verses 21–22: Jesus asks about the history of the illness. He listens to the father. The father has suffered for years and feared for his child.
> Verse 24: “I believe. Help my unbelief!” That is one of the most honest sentences in the Bible. Faith and doubt are often there at the same time. Jesus accepts this small, doubting faith and helps.
> Important: This does not mean that someone does not get well because they believe too little. Illness is not a sign of too little faith.
> Verse 27: Jesus takes the boy by the hand and raises him up, like the daughter of Jairus (5:41).
> Verse 29: The words “and fasting” are missing in important old manuscripts. The WEB has them.

## Jesus announces his suffering again (Verses 30–32)

[30] They went away from there
and passed through Galilee.
He didn’t want anyone to know it.
[31] For he was teaching his disciples
and said to them,
“The Son of Man is being handed over into the hands of men.
They will kill him.
And when he has been killed,
on the third day he will rise again.”
[32] But they didn’t understand the saying
and were afraid to ask him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the second announcement of his suffering (the first was in 8:31).
> The disciples don’t understand it. And they don’t dare to ask. Perhaps they also did not want to accept it.
> For today: You may ask questions, also about hard things in the faith.

## Who is the greatest? (Verses 33–37)

[33] They came to Capernaum.
When he was in the house,
he asked them,
“What were you arguing about with one another on the way?”
[34] But they were silent.
For on the way they had argued with one another
about who was the greatest.
[35] He sat down,
called the twelve,
and said to them,
“If anyone wants to be first,
he must be last of all
and servant of all.”
[36] He took a little child
and set him in the middle of them.
He took him in his arms
and said to them,
[37] “Whoever welcomes one such little child in my name
welcomes me.
And whoever welcomes me
welcomes not only me,
but the one who sent me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus has just spoken about his suffering. And the disciples argue about who is the most important! They are ashamed and silent.
> Jesus sits down, like a teacher, and explains: With me, whoever serves others is great.
> Only Mark tells us that Jesus takes the child in his arms. In those days children had no rights and counted for little. Jesus puts them in the middle.
> For today: Children are important in the church. Whoever protects children and cares for them does it for Jesus and for God himself.

## Whoever is not against us is for us (Verses 38–41)

[38] John said to him,
“Teacher, we saw someone
casting out demons in your name.
He doesn’t follow us.
We told him to stop,
because he doesn’t follow us.”
[39] But Jesus said,
“Don’t stop him!
For no one who does a mighty work in my name
can soon afterward speak evil of me.
[40] For whoever is not against us
is on our side.
[41] For most certainly I tell you,
whoever gives you a cup of water to drink
in my name
because you belong to Christ
will in no way lose his reward.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples want to decide who may belong to Jesus. Jesus says: Don’t be so narrow! Whoever does good in my name is not an enemy.
> For today: Christians of different churches should not shut each other out. God also works outside your own group.
> Verse 41: Even a small kindness, a cup of water, is precious to God.

## A warning against leading others astray (Verses 42–50)

[42] “Whoever causes one of these little ones
who believe in me
to stumble,
it would be better for him
to have a millstone hung around his neck
and be thrown into the sea.
[43] If your hand causes you to stumble,
cut it off.
It is better for you
to enter into life maimed
than to have two hands and go into Gehenna,
into the fire that never goes out,
[44] ‘where their worm doesn’t die,
and the fire is not quenched.’
[45] If your foot causes you to stumble,
cut it off.
It is better for you
to enter into life lame
than to have two feet and be thrown into Gehenna,
into the fire that never goes out,
[46] ‘where their worm doesn’t die,
and the fire is not quenched.’
[47] If your eye causes you to stumble,
throw it out.
It is better for you
to enter into God’s Kingdom with one eye
than to have two eyes and be thrown into the Gehenna of fire,
[48] ‘where their worm doesn’t die,
and the fire is not quenched.’
[49] For everyone will be salted with fire,
and every sacrifice will be seasoned with salt.
[50] Salt is good.
But if the salt has lost its saltiness,
how will you season it?
Have salt in yourselves,
and be at peace with one another.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 42: Jesus clearly takes the side of the weak and of children. Whoever harms them does great wrong. Whoever has experienced abuse or knows about it can find help: In the US, call RAINN at 1-800-656-4673. Children and young people: In the US, call or text Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111.
> Millstone: a large, heavy stone for grinding grain.
> Verses 43–47 are picture language. Jesus wants to say: Take evil very seriously. Separate yourself from everything that leads you to evil. He does not mean that anyone should really hurt themselves.
> Whoever is thinking about hurting themselves, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> “Gehenna” was a valley near Jerusalem. It became a picture for God’s judgment.
> Verses 44 and 46 are missing in the best old manuscripts. They repeat verse 48. The WEB has them. The words come from Isaiah 66:24.
> Verse 49 is hard to understand. Salt cleans and preserves. Perhaps it means: Whoever follows Jesus is tested and purified by hard things. The second part (“every sacrifice will be seasoned with salt”) is missing in some manuscripts. It reminds us of Leviticus 2:13.
> Verse 50: At the end comes peace. The disciples who were just arguing about who is the greatest (verse 34) are to be at peace with one another.
