# Mark – Chapter 10
### The way to Jerusalem

---

## The question about divorce (Verses 1–12)

[1] Jesus got up and left that place.
He went into the area of Judea
and to the other side of the Jordan.
Again crowds came together around him.
And he taught them again, as he usually did.
[2] Some Pharisees came to him.
They wanted to test him.
They asked him,
“Is a man allowed by the law
to divorce his wife?”
[3] He answered,
“What did Moses command you?”
[4] They said,
“Moses allowed a man to write a divorce paper
and send his wife away.”
[5] But Jesus said to them,
“Moses wrote this commandment for you
because your hearts are so hard.
[6] But at the beginning, when God created the world,
he made people male and female.
[7] That is why a man will leave his father and mother
and be joined to his wife.
[8] And the two will become one body.
So they are no longer two,
but one body.
[9] God has joined them together.
So no one should separate them.”
[10] In the house the disciples asked Jesus about this again.
[11] He said to them,
“If a man divorces his wife
and marries another woman,
he commits adultery against her.
[12] And if a woman divorces her husband
and marries another man,
she commits adultery.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Moses allowed divorce and gave rules for it (Deuteronomy 24:1–4). In those days in Israel usually only the man could get a divorce. Afterward women were often poor and without protection.
> Jesus goes back to the beginning (Genesis 1:27 and 2:24): God wants marriage to last. In this way he protects above all the women against being divorced at will.
> Verse 11: Adultery means: being unfaithful in marriage. “Against her”: Jesus sees the woman as someone who can be wronged. That was not taken for granted in those days.
> Verse 12: Under Roman law women could also get a divorce. Mark is probably also writing for readers who lived under Roman law.
> Christians understand this saying in different ways. The Catholic Church holds that marriage cannot be dissolved. Protestant and Orthodox churches allow divorce and a new marriage under certain circumstances. Matthew gives an exception (Matthew 19:9).
> Very important: No one has to stay in a marriage in which they are beaten, threatened or abused. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Divorced people are not worse Christians. God loves them.

## Jesus blesses the children (Verses 13–16)

[13] People were bringing little children to Jesus.
They wanted him to touch them.
But the disciples scolded the people who brought them.
[14] Jesus saw this.
He was moved with indignation.
That means: He was angry.
He said to the disciples,
“Let the little children come to me!
Do not stop them!
God’s Kingdom belongs to people like them.
[15] I tell you the truth:
Whoever does not receive God’s Kingdom like a little child
will never get into it.”
[16] Then he took the children in his arms.
He put his hands on them
and blessed them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Mark tells us that Jesus was “moved with indignation,” that is, angry with his disciples. Children are important to him.
> Verse 15: Children can accept gifts. They don’t have to earn anything. That is how adults too should receive God’s Kingdom: as a gift.
> Jesus takes the children in his arms. That is tender and loving.
> For today: Children should be welcome in the church. And they have a right to be protected from violence.

## The rich man (Verses 17–22)

[17] Jesus was setting out on his way.
Then a man ran up to him.
He knelt down in front of Jesus
and asked him,
“Good Teacher,
what must I do
to receive eternal life?”
[18] Jesus said to him,
“Why do you call me good?
No one is good except one: God.
[19] You know the commandments:
‘Do not murder.’
‘Do not commit adultery.’
‘Do not steal.’
‘Do not tell lies about others in court.’
‘Do not defraud.’
‘Honor your father and mother.’”
[20] The man said to him,
“Teacher, I have kept all these commandments since I was young.”
[21] Jesus looked at him and loved him.
He said to him,
“You are missing one thing.
Go and sell everything you have.
Give the money to the poor.
Then you will have treasure in heaven.
And come, follow me,
taking up the cross.”
[22] When the man heard this, his face fell.
He went away sad.
He was very rich.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus names commandments from the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:12–16). “Do not defraud” (10:19) is only in Mark. It reminds us of Deuteronomy 24:14: You must not hold back a worker’s wages. Defraud: to cheat someone out of what belongs to them.
> Verse 21: Only Mark tells us: “Jesus looked at him and loved him.” Jesus does not challenge him out of strictness, but out of love.
> The words “taking up the cross” are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> The man is sincere and devout. But his possessions hold him tight.
> Christians understand verse 21 in different ways. Some really give up all their possessions, for example monks and nuns. Most understand it like this: Nothing may be more important to us than God. Whoever has a lot should share with the poor.

## Riches and God’s Kingdom (Verses 23–31)

[23] Jesus looked around.
He said to his disciples,
“It is so hard for people
who have riches
to get into God’s Kingdom!”
[24] The disciples were amazed at his words.
But Jesus said to them again,
“Children, it is so hard for those
who trust in riches
to get into God’s Kingdom!
[25] It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle
than for a rich man to get into God’s Kingdom.”
[26] The disciples were even more amazed.
They said to him,
“Then who can be saved?”
[27] Jesus looked at them and said,
“For people it is impossible,
but not for God.
For God, everything is possible.”
[28] Peter began to say to him,
“Look, we have left everything
and followed you.”
[29] Jesus said,
“I tell you the truth:
Some have left their house
or brothers or sisters
or father or mother
or wife or children
or land
because of me and because of the Good News.
[30] Each one of them will receive 100 times as much
now in this time:
houses, brothers, sisters, mothers, children and land,
together with persecutions.
And in the world to come they will receive eternal life.
[31] But many who are first will be last,
and the last will be first.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Jesus tenderly calls his disciples “Children.” The words “who trust in riches” are missing in some old manuscripts.
> Verse 25: The camel was the largest animal people knew, the eye of a needle the smallest hole. Jesus exaggerates on purpose. The explanation that there was a small city gate called the Needle’s Eye has no evidence behind it.
> Verse 27: A great comfort: No one can save themselves. But God can.
> Verse 30: Whoever follows Jesus finds a new, large family in the church. Notice: Fathers are not named a second time here. Some interpreters say: In the new family there is only one Father, God. And: Only Mark mentions “with persecutions.” Persecutions means: being treated badly because of your faith. Following Jesus is not always easy.
> Important: This does not mean that you should abandon your family (compare 7:10–13).

## Jesus announces his suffering for the third time (Verses 32–34)

[32] They were on the road going up to Jerusalem.
Jesus was walking ahead of them.
The disciples were amazed.
And the people who followed were afraid.
Again Jesus took the Twelve aside.
He began to tell them
what was going to happen to him:
[33] “Look, we are going up to Jerusalem.
There the Son of Man
will be handed over to the chief priests and the scribes.
They will sentence him to death.
They will hand him over to the Gentiles.
[34] The Gentiles will make fun of him.
They will spit on him.
They will whip him
and kill him.
But on the third day he will rise again.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus walks ahead, bravely, even though he knows what is waiting for him. The disciples are afraid.
> This is the third and most detailed announcement (compare 8:31 and 9:31).
> “The Gentiles” here are the Romans. Gentiles: people who are not Jews. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment.
> Important: Jesus names certain leaders, not the Jewish people. Blaming “the Jews” for the death of Jesus is wrong and has caused great injustice.

## Whoever serves is great (Verses 35–45)

[35] Then James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to Jesus.
They said,
“Teacher, we want you
to do for us
whatever we ask.”
[36] He said to them,
“What do you want me to do for you?”
[37] They said to him,
“When you are in your glory,
let us sit next to you,
one at your right side and one at your left side.”
[38] But Jesus said to them,
“You do not know what you are asking.
Can you drink the cup
that I drink?
Can you be baptized with the baptism
that I am baptized with?”
[39] They said to him,
“We can.”
Jesus said to them,
“You will drink the cup that I drink.
And you will be baptized with the baptism
that I am baptized with.
[40] But I cannot decide who sits at my right side or at my left side.
Those places are for the people
for whom they have been prepared.”
[41] The other 10 disciples heard about it.
They became angry with James and John.
[42] Jesus called them together
and said to them,
“You know:
Those who are seen as rulers over the nations
act like lords over them.
And their great men use their power over them.
[43] But it must not be like that among you!
Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant.
[44] And whoever wants to be first among you
must be the slave of all.
[45] Even the Son of Man did not come
to be served.
He came to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.”
A ransom is a price paid to set someone free.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Mark, James and John ask for themselves (in Matthew 20:20 it is their mother).
> The “cup” and the “baptism” are pictures for the suffering that Jesus expects.
> Verse 39: James was later killed because of his faith (Acts 12:2).
> Verses 42–44: In the world the powerful often rule from above. With Jesus it is the other way around: Whoever leads should serve.
> Verse 45 is one of the most important sentences in the Gospel of Mark. Ransom: In those days people paid money to buy prisoners or slaves free. Jesus gives his life to set people free. This reminds us of Isaiah 53:10–12.
> For today: In the family, at work, in politics and in the church, no one should misuse their power.

## Blind Bartimaeus (Verses 46–52)

[46] They came to Jericho.
Then Jesus left Jericho with his disciples and a large crowd.
A blind beggar was sitting by the road.
His name was Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus.
[47] He heard
that it was Jesus from Nazareth.
Then he began to shout,
“Jesus, son of David,
have mercy on me!”
[48] Many people told him sharply
to be quiet.
But he shouted even louder,
“Son of David, have mercy on me!”
[49] Jesus stopped
and said,
“Call him.”
So they called the blind man.
They said to him,
“Cheer up! Get up.
He is calling you!”
[50] The blind man threw off his cloak.
He jumped up
and came to Jesus.
[51] Jesus asked him,
“What do you want me to do for you?”
The blind man said to him,
“Rabboni, I want to see again.”
[52] Jesus said to him,
“Go.
Your faith has made you well.”
At once the man could see again.
And he followed Jesus on the way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jericho is in the Jordan Valley. From there the road goes up to Jerusalem.
> Bartimaeus is the only healed person whom Mark names. “Bar” means “son”: Bartimaeus means “son of Timaeus.”
> He calls Jesus “son of David,” that is, the promised King. He sees more than many who can see.
> The crowd wants to silence him. But he will not be stopped.
> Verse 50: For a beggar, the cloak was often his only possession. He throws it away. Very different from the rich man (verse 22).
> Verse 51: Jesus asks the same question as he asked the sons of Zebedee (verse 36): “What do you want me to do for you?” Bartimaeus does not ask for honor, but for help.
> “Rabboni” means “my teacher.” It is an especially warm way of speaking to someone.
> “On the way”: He follows Jesus to Jerusalem. He becomes a disciple.
> Important: People with disabilities should not be silenced. Jesus asks what they themselves want. That is an attitude of respect.
