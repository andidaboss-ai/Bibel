# Mark – Chapter 8
### “You are the Christ”

---

## Jesus feeds 4,000 people (Verses 1–10)

[1] In those days there was a very large crowd.
They had nothing to eat.
So Jesus called his disciples to himself
and said to them,
[2] “I have compassion on the people.
They have been with me for three days now
and have nothing to eat.
[3] If I send them home hungry,
they will faint on the way.
For some of them have come a long way.”
[4] His disciples answered him,
“From where could anyone get enough bread
to feed these people here in a deserted place?”
[5] He asked them,
“How many loaves do you have?”
They said,
“Seven.”
[6] He told the crowd
to sit down on the ground.
He took the seven loaves,
gave thanks,
broke them,
and gave them to his disciples to serve.
And they served them to the crowd.
[7] They also had a few small fish.
He blessed them
and said to serve these too.
[8] They ate and were filled.
They picked up the broken pieces that were left over:
seven baskets.
[9] About 4,000 people
had eaten.
Then he sent them away.
[10] Immediately he got into the boat with his disciples
and came into the region of Dalmanutha.

> **What does this mean?**
> This story is similar to the feeding of the 5,000 (6:30–44). Mark tells both. This one probably happens in the region of Decapolis (7:31), where many non-Jews lived. Some interpreters see in it: Jesus also cares for the nations.
> Verse 3: “Some of them have come a long way”: Jesus thinks of the people who have a long way home. He sees the concrete need.
> Again the disciples ask: “From where?” They have already seen Jesus feed thousands, and still they forget it.
> Dalmanutha: No one knows exactly where this place was. Some manuscripts have other names.
> For today: Jesus has compassion on hungry people. Many people are still hungry today. Christians should help.

## The demand for a sign (Verses 11–13)

[11] The Pharisees came out
and began to argue with him.
They asked him for a sign from heaven,
to test him.
[12] He sighed deeply in his spirit and said,
“Why does this generation look for a sign?
Most certainly I tell you,
no sign will be given to this generation.”
[13] He left them,
got into the boat again,
and went to the other side.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus has just fed thousands. Even so they still want proof. Jesus sighs deeply. He is sad, not angry.
> Faith cannot be forced by proof. Whoever does not want to see sees nothing, even with great signs.
> Important: This is not a judgment on all Pharisees or on the Jewish people.

## The yeast of the Pharisees and of Herod (Verses 14–21)

[14] The disciples had forgotten to take bread.
They had only one loaf with them in the boat.
[15] Jesus warned them and said,
“Watch out!
Beware of the yeast of the Pharisees
and the yeast of Herod!”
[16] They talked with one another and said,
“He says this because we have no bread.”
[17] Jesus noticed it and said to them,
“Why are you talking about
having no bread?
Don’t you see yet,
or understand?
Is your heart still hardened?
[18] You have eyes, don’t you see?
You have ears, don’t you hear?
Don’t you remember?
[19] When I broke the five loaves for the 5,000,
how many baskets full of broken pieces did you pick up?”
They told him,
“12.”
[20] “And when I broke the seven loaves for the 4,000,
how many baskets full of broken pieces did you pick up?”
They told him,
“Seven.”
[21] He asked them,
“Don’t you understand yet?”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Yeast” is a picture for something that spreads without being noticed. Jesus warns against an attitude that demands signs and closes itself off from him, and against the greed for power of a ruler like Herod.
> The disciples think only about their missing bread. Jesus reminds them: I have fed thousands twice! You don’t need to worry.
> Verse 18 reminds us of Jeremiah 5:21 and Ezekiel 12:2.
> The “one loaf” in the boat: Some interpreters see in it a hint at Jesus himself. He is with them. They need nothing more.
> In Mark the disciples don’t understand for a long time. This is told honestly and gives courage: Even someone who is slow to understand stays with Jesus.

## Jesus heals a blind man in Bethsaida (Verses 22–26)

[22] They came to Bethsaida.
People brought a blind man to him
and begged him to touch him.
[23] He took the blind man by the hand
and led him out of the village.
He spat on his eyes,
laid his hands on him,
and asked him,
“Do you see anything?”
[24] He looked up and said,
“I see people.
But I see them like trees
walking around.”
[25] Then Jesus laid his hands on his eyes again.
The man looked hard,
and he was healed
and saw everything clearly.
[26] Jesus sent him home
and said,
“Don’t go into the village,
and don’t tell anyone in the village!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Only Mark tells about this healing. It is special: It happens in two steps. First the man sees only blurry shapes, then completely clearly.
> Jesus takes the man by the hand and leads him himself. He is very close to him.
> Many interpreters see this story as a picture of the disciples: They already see something, but not yet clearly. Right after this Peter recognizes who Jesus is, but he does not yet understand what that means (verses 29–33).
> Verse 26: Some manuscripts have only “Don’t go into the village.”
> Important: Blindness is not a punishment. People who stay blind are also loved by God.

## Peter confesses: You are the Christ (Verses 27–30)

[27] Jesus went away with his disciples
into the villages near Caesarea Philippi.
On the way he asked his disciples,
“Who do people say that I am?”
[28] They told him,
“Some say John the Baptizer.
Others say Elijah.
Others, one of the prophets.”
[29] He asked them,
“But who do you say that I am?”
Peter answered,
“You are the Christ.”
[30] He ordered them
to tell no one about him.

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the center of the Gospel of Mark. The big question was always: Who is Jesus? Now Peter says it for the first time: “You are the Christ,” the Messiah, the King promised by God.
> Caesarea Philippi was in the far north, near the springs of the Jordan.
> Verse 30: Jesus does not want them to tell others. Many expected a Messiah who would fight with violence against the Romans. Jesus goes a different way, as he explains right away.
> In Matthew this scene is told in more detail (Matthew 16:13–20).

## Jesus announces his suffering (Verses 31–33)

[31] And he began to teach them
that the Son of Man must suffer many things.
He would be rejected by the elders, the chief priests,
and the scribes.
He would be killed
and after three days rise again.
[32] He said this quite openly.
Then Peter took him aside
and began to rebuke him.
[33] But Jesus turned around,
looked at his disciples,
and rebuked Peter.
He said,
“Get behind me, Satan!
For you have in mind not the things of God,
but the things of men.”

> **What does this mean?**
> For the first time Jesus says openly: I will suffer, die and rise again. The word “must” shows: This is God’s way.
> The opponents whom Jesus names are certain leaders, not the Jewish people. Crucifixion was a Roman punishment. It is wrong, and it has caused much suffering, to blame “the Jews” for the death of Jesus.
> Rebuke: to tell someone off sharply.
> Peter cannot imagine that the Christ should suffer.
> “Satan” means “opponent.” Peter wants to turn Jesus away from God’s way, like the tempter in the wilderness. “Get behind me” can also mean: Take your place behind me again, as my disciple. Jesus does not give up on Peter.

## What it means to follow Jesus (Verses 34–38)

[34] He called the crowd to himself together with his disciples
and said to them,
“Whoever wants to come after me
must deny himself,
take up his cross,
and follow me.
[35] For whoever wants to save his life
will lose it.
But whoever loses his life
for my sake and for the sake of the Good News
will save it.
[36] For what good is it for a person
to gain the whole world
and lose his life?
[37] For what can a person give
in exchange for his life?
[38] For whoever is ashamed of me and of my words
in this adulterous and sinful generation,
the Son of Man will also be ashamed of him
when he comes in his Father’s glory
with the holy angels.”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Deny himself”: no longer putting yourself at the center, but God and others. “Take up his cross”: being ready to bear disadvantages for your faith too.
> Very important: This does not mean that you simply have to put up with violence, abuse or injustice. Whoever experiences violence may and should get help: In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verses 35–37: The Greek word for “life” can also mean “soul.” A life lived only for possessions and success is lost in the end.
> Verse 38: “Adulterous” is a picture used by the prophets for being unfaithful to God. Jesus encourages: Stand by me, even when it is hard.
