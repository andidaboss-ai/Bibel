# Mark – Chapter 13
### Keep watch!

---

## Jesus announces the destruction of the temple (Verses 1–2)

[1] As Jesus was going out of the temple,
one of his disciples said to him,
“Teacher, look
what huge stones and what buildings!”
[2] Jesus said to him,
“Do you see these great buildings?
Not one stone here will be left on another.
Everything will be torn down.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple in Jerusalem was magnificent. King Herod the Great had it enlarged with enormous blocks of stone.
> Jesus announces: It will be destroyed. That happened in the year AD 70 through the Romans.
> For the Jewish people this was a great catastrophe. To this day Jews remember it on the day of mourning called Tisha B’Av. Jesus is not happy about the destruction.

---

## The beginning of the birth pains (Verses 3–13)

[3] As he was sitting on the Mount of Olives,
opposite the temple,
Peter, James, John, and Andrew asked him privately,
[4] “Tell us:
When will these things happen?
And what is the sign
that all these things are about to be fulfilled?”
[5] Jesus began to answer them,
“Be careful that no one leads you astray!
[6] For many will come in my name
and say, ‘I am he!’
And they will lead many astray.
[7] When you hear of wars
and rumors of wars,
don’t be troubled!
For these things must happen,
but the end is not yet.
[8] For nation will rise against nation,
and kingdom against kingdom.
There will be earthquakes in various places.
There will be famines and troubles.
These things are the beginning of birth pains.
[9] But watch out for yourselves!
They will hand you over to councils.
You will be beaten in synagogues.
Because of me you will stand before rulers and kings,
as a testimony to them.
[10] The Good News must first be preached to all the nations.
[11] When they lead you away and hand you over,
don’t worry beforehand
about what you will say.
But say whatever is given you in that hour.
For it is not you who speak,
but the Holy Spirit.
[12] Brother will hand over brother to death,
and the father his child.
Children will rise up against parents
and have them put to death.
[13] You will be hated by everyone
because you belong to me.
But whoever endures to the end
will be saved.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples ask: When will the temple be destroyed? Jesus answers in detail. His answer is about the destruction of the temple and about the end of time at the same time. That makes this speech hard to understand.
> The first and most important message: “Be careful that no one leads you astray!” Jesus warns against false saviors and against people who claim to know exactly when the end will come.
> Verses 7–8: There have been wars, earthquakes and famines at all times. Jesus says: Don’t be troubled! “Birth pains” are the pains when a baby is born. After the pain, something new comes.
> Verse 9: The first Christians were mostly Jews. That is why some of them were punished in synagogues (compare 2 Corinthians 11:24). That was a conflict within Judaism. It is not a judgment on the Jewish people.
> Verse 11: A comfort for everyone who stands before a court because of their faith: God’s Spirit helps them.
> Important for today: Again and again groups have calculated a date for the end of the world and frightened people. Jesus warns against exactly that. Whoever is in a group that puts pressure on people with fear of the end of the world may get help, for example from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.

## The great distress (Verses 14–23)

[14] “But when you see the ‘abomination of desolation,’
spoken of by Daniel the prophet,
standing where it ought not
(let the reader understand!),
then those who are in Judea
should flee to the mountains.
[15] Whoever is on the housetop
should not go down
or go into the house
to take anything out of his house.
[16] Whoever is in the field
should not go back
to get his cloak.
[17] But woe to pregnant women
and to mothers who are nursing babies
in those days!
[18] Pray that it won’t happen in the winter.
[19] For in those days there will be suffering
such as there has never been
from the beginning of the creation which God created
until now,
and never will be again.
[20] If the Lord had not shortened those days,
no one would be saved.
But for the sake of the chosen ones,
whom he picked out,
he has shortened the days.
[21] Then if anyone says to you,
‘Look, here is the Christ!’
or, ‘Look, there!’
don’t believe it.
[22] For false christs
and false prophets will appear.
They will show signs and wonders
to lead astray, if possible, even the chosen ones.
[23] But you, watch out!
I have told you everything ahead of time.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: The “abomination of desolation” is an expression from the book of Daniel (Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). There it means a desecration of the temple by a foreign king. Jesus announces: Something like this will happen again. Many interpreters think of the time around the year AD 70.
> “Let the reader understand”: A note to the readers of the Gospel.
> Verses 15–18: When the time comes, people should flee at once. Jesus thinks especially of pregnant women and mothers with small children, for whom fleeing is especially hard.
> Verse 20: God puts a limit on suffering. He does not forget the people in distress.
> Verses 21–23: Again Jesus warns against false saviors. You should not run after anyone who says: “Look, here is the Christ!”

## The coming of the Son of Man (Verses 24–27)

[24] “But in those days,
after that suffering,
the sun will be darkened,
and the moon will not give its light.
[25] The stars will be falling from the sky,
and the powers in the heavens will be shaken.
[26] Then they will see the Son of Man
coming in clouds
with great power and glory.
[27] Then he will send out his angels
and gather together his chosen ones
from the four winds,
from the ends of the earth to the ends of the sky.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24–25 take up pictures from the prophets (Isaiah 13:10; 34:4). They describe how God steps in and everything changes. It is picture language.
> Verse 26 reminds us of Daniel 7:13–14: One like a son of man comes with the clouds of the sky.
> Verse 27: At the end God gathers everyone who belongs to him from all over the world.
> Christians believe that Jesus will come again. That is a hope, not a threat. In the end God wins, not evil.

## The parable of the fig tree (Verses 28–32)

[28] “Learn this parable from the fig tree:
When its branches become tender
and put out leaves,
you know that summer is near.
[29] In the same way,
when you see these things happening,
you know
that it is near, right at the door.
[30] Most certainly I tell you,
this generation will not pass away
until all these things happen.
[31] Heaven and earth will pass away,
but my words will not pass away.
[32] But no one knows that day or that hour,
not even the angels in heaven,
nor the Son,
but only the Father.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The fig tree loses its leaves in winter. When it gets new ones, everyone knows: Summer will soon be here.
> Verse 30 is hard to understand. Many interpreters relate it to the destruction of Jerusalem about 40 years later. There are different interpretations.
> Verse 32 is very important: No one knows the day, not even the Son, only God the Father. That is why every calculation of a date for the end of the world is wrong.

## Keep watch! (Verses 33–37)

[33] “Watch out, stay alert, and pray!
For you don’t know
when the time will come.
[34] It is like a man
who went on a trip to another country.
He left his house
and put his servants in charge,
each one with his work.
And he commanded the doorkeeper
to keep watch.
[35] So keep watch!
For you don’t know
when the lord of the house is coming:
in the evening, or at midnight,
or when the rooster crows, or in the morning.
[36] He must not find you sleeping
when he comes suddenly.
[37] What I tell you,
I tell everyone:
Keep watch!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 33: The words “and pray” are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Each servant has his task. In the same way, every person has received a task from God.
> The four times (evening, midnight, rooster crow, morning) were the four watches of the night used by the Romans. Some interpreters notice: Jesus’ passion in Mark 14–15 happens at exactly these times: the Last Supper in the evening, Gethsemane in the night, Peter’s denial when the rooster crows, the handing over to Pilate in the morning. And in Gethsemane the disciples sleep.
> “Keeping watch” does not mean being anxious. It means: living every day in such a way that you could meet God. Doing good, being honest, loving others.
> Verse 37: This is not only for the disciples back then, but for everyone, including us today.
