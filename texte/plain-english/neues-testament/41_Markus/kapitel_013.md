# Mark – Chapter 13
### Keep watch!

---

## Jesus announces the destruction of the temple (Verses 1–2)

[1] Jesus was going out of the temple.
One of his disciples said to him,
“Teacher, look at these stones!
Look at these buildings!”
[2] Jesus said to him,
“Do you see these great buildings?
Not one stone here will be left on top of another.
Every stone will be thrown down.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The temple in Jerusalem was magnificent. King Herod the Great had it enlarged with enormous blocks of stone.
> Jesus announces: It will be destroyed. That happened in the year AD 70 through the Romans.
> For the Jewish people this was a great catastrophe. To this day Jews remember it on the day of mourning called Tisha B’Av. Jesus is not happy about the destruction.

---

## The beginning of the birth pains (Verses 3–13)

[3] Later Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives,
across from the temple.
Peter, James, John, and Andrew came to him alone.
They asked him,
[4] “Tell us, when will these things happen?
What sign will show
that all these things are about to come true?”
[5] Jesus began to answer them.
“Be careful that no one fools you.
[6] Many will come and use my name.
They will say, ‘I am he!’
And they will fool many people.
[7] You will hear about wars.
You will hear news of wars far away.
Don’t be afraid.
These things must happen.
But that is not yet the end.
[8] One nation will fight against another nation.
One kingdom will fight against another kingdom.
There will be earthquakes in many places.
There will be times of hunger and times of trouble.
These things are only the beginning of the birth pains.
[9] But watch out for yourselves.
People will hand you over to the courts.
You will be beaten in the synagogues.
You will stand in front of rulers and kings because of me.
In this way you will tell them about me.
[10] First the Good News must be told to all the nations.
[11] People will arrest you and hand you over.
Then don’t worry ahead of time about what you will say.
Don’t plan it.
Just say what God gives you to say at that time.
For it is not you who will speak.
The Holy Spirit will speak.
[12] A brother will hand over his brother to be killed.
A father will hand over his child.
Children will turn against their parents
and have them put to death.
[13] Everyone will hate you
because you belong to me.
But whoever stays faithful to the end
will be saved.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The disciples ask: When will the temple be destroyed? Jesus answers in detail. His answer is about the destruction of the temple and about the end of time at the same time. That makes this speech hard to understand.
> The first and most important message: “Be careful that no one fools you.” Jesus warns against false saviors and against people who claim to know exactly when the end will come.
> Verses 7–8: There have been wars, earthquakes and famines at all times. Jesus says: Don’t be afraid. “Birth pains” are the pains when a baby is born. After the pain, something new comes.
> Verse 9: The first Christians were mostly Jews. That is why some of them were punished in synagogues (compare 2 Corinthians 11:24). That was a conflict within Judaism. It is not a judgment on the Jewish people.
> Verse 11: A comfort for everyone who stands before a court because of their faith: God’s Spirit helps them.
> Important for today: Again and again groups have calculated a date for the end of the world and frightened people. Jesus warns against exactly that. Whoever is in a group that puts pressure on people with fear of the end of the world may get help, for example from counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.

## The great distress (Verses 14–23)

[14] “You will see the ‘abomination of desolation’
standing where it does not belong.
Daniel the prophet spoke about it.”
(The reader should understand what this means.)
“Then the people in Judea must run away to the mountains.
[15] Someone on the roof of a house must not go down.
He must not go into his house to take anything out.
[16] Someone in the field must not go back to get his coat.
[17] How terrible it will be in those days for women who are pregnant!
How terrible for mothers who are nursing babies!
[18] Pray that you will not have to run away in the winter.
[19] In those days there will be great suffering.
There has never been suffering like it,
from the time God created the world until now.
And there never will be again.
[20] The Lord has made those days shorter.
If he had not done that, no one would be saved.
But he made the days shorter for the sake of his chosen people,
the ones he picked out.
[21] At that time someone may say to you,
‘Look, here is the Christ!’
or, ‘Look, there he is!’
Don’t believe it.
[22] False christs and false prophets will come.
They will do signs and wonders.
They want to fool even God’s chosen people, if that were possible.
[23] So watch out!
I have told you everything ahead of time.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: The “abomination of desolation” is an expression from the book of Daniel (Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). There it means a desecration of the temple by a foreign king. Jesus announces: Something like this will happen again. Many interpreters think of the time around the year AD 70.
> “The reader should understand what this means”: A note to the readers of the Gospel.
> Verses 15–18: When the time comes, people should flee at once. Jesus thinks especially of pregnant women and mothers with small children, for whom fleeing is especially hard.
> Verse 20: God puts a limit on suffering. He does not forget the people in distress.
> Verses 21–23: Again Jesus warns against false saviors. You should not run after anyone who says: “Look, here is the Christ!”

## The coming of the Son of Man (Verses 24–27)

[24] “In those days, after that time of suffering,
the sun will become dark.
The moon will not give its light.
[25] The stars will fall from the sky.
The powers in the heavens will be shaken.
[26] Then people will see the Son of Man coming in the clouds
with great power and glory.
[27] He will send out his angels.
They will gather his chosen people from all four directions,
from the ends of the earth to the ends of the sky.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24–25 take up pictures from the prophets (Isaiah 13:10; 34:4). They describe how God steps in and everything changes. It is picture language.
> Verse 26 reminds us of Daniel 7:13–14: One like a son of man comes with the clouds of the sky.
> Verse 27: At the end God gathers everyone who belongs to him from all over the world.
> Christians believe that Jesus will come again. That is a hope, not a threat. In the end God wins, not evil.

## The parable of the fig tree (Verses 28–32)

[28] “Learn this lesson from the fig tree.
When its branches become soft and green
and its leaves come out,
you know that summer is near.
[29] It is the same with you.
When you see these things happening,
you know that the time is near.
It is right at the door.
[30] I tell you for certain:
This generation will not pass away
before all these things happen.
[31] Heaven and earth will pass away.
But my words will never pass away.
[32] But no one knows that day or that hour.
Not even the angels in heaven know it.
Not even the Son knows it.
Only the Father knows it.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The fig tree loses its leaves in winter. When it gets new ones, everyone knows: Summer will soon be here.
> Verse 30 is hard to understand. Many interpreters relate it to the destruction of Jerusalem about 40 years later. There are different interpretations.
> Verse 32 is very important: No one knows the day, not even the Son, only God the Father. That is why every calculation of a date for the end of the world is wrong.

## Keep watch! (Verses 33–37)

[33] “Watch out! Stay awake! Pray!
For you don’t know when that time will come.
[34] It is like a man who went on a trip to another country.
He left his house and put his servants in charge.
He gave each one his own work.
And he told the doorkeeper to keep watch.
[35] So keep watch!
For you don’t know when the master of the house will come.
It may be in the evening, or at midnight,
or when the rooster crows, or in the morning.
[36] He may come suddenly.
Then he must not find you sleeping.
[37] What I tell you, I tell everyone:
Keep watch!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 33: The words “and pray” are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB, our English source text, has them.
> Each servant has his task. In the same way, every person has received a task from God.
> The four times (evening, midnight, rooster crow, morning) were the four watches of the night used by the Romans. Some interpreters notice: Jesus’ passion in Mark 14–15 happens at exactly these times: the Last Supper in the evening, Gethsemane in the night, Peter’s denial when the rooster crows, the handing over to Pilate in the morning. And in Gethsemane the disciples sleep.
> “Keeping watch” does not mean being anxious. It means: living every day in such a way that you could meet God. Doing good, being honest, loving others.
> Verse 37: This is not only for the disciples back then, but for everyone, including us today.
