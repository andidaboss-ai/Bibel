# Mark – Chapter 5
### Jesus brings healing and life

---

## The man from the tombs (Verses 1–20)

[1] They came to the other side of the sea,
into the country of the Gadarenes.
[2] When Jesus got out of the boat,
immediately a man came out of the tombs to meet him.
He had an unclean spirit.
[3] He lived in the tombs.
Nobody could tie him up any more,
not even with chains.
[4] For he had often been tied up with shackles and chains.
But he had torn the chains apart
and broken the shackles in pieces.
Nobody was strong enough to tame him.
[5] Always, night and day,
he was crying out in the tombs and in the mountains
and cutting himself with stones.
[6] When he saw Jesus from far away,
he ran
and bowed down in front of him.
[7] He cried out with a loud voice,
“What have I to do with you,
Jesus, you Son of the Most High God?
I beg you by God,
don’t torment me!”
[8] For Jesus had said to him,
“Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!”
[9] Jesus asked him,
“What is your name?”
He said to him,
“My name is Legion,
for we are many.”
[10] And he begged Jesus very much
not to send them away out of the country.
[11] On the mountainside a large herd of pigs was feeding.
[12] All the demons begged him,
“Send us into the pigs,
so that we may go into them!”
[13] At once Jesus gave them permission.
The unclean spirits came out
and went into the pigs.
The herd, about 2,000 animals,
rushed down the steep bank into the sea
and drowned in the sea.
[14] Those who took care of the pigs fled
and told it in the town and in the country.
The people came
to see what had happened.
[15] They came to Jesus
and saw the man who had been possessed by demons,
the one who had had the legion.
He was sitting there, clothed,
and in his right mind.
And they were afraid.
[16] Those who had seen it
told them
what had happened to the man possessed by demons,
and about the pigs.
[17] Then they began to beg Jesus
to leave their region.
[18] As Jesus was getting into the boat,
the man who had been possessed by demons begged him
that he might be with him.
[19] But Jesus didn’t allow it.
Instead he said to him,
“Go to your house, to your friends,
and tell them
what great things the Lord has done for you
and how he had mercy on you.”
[20] So he went away
and began to proclaim in Decapolis
what great things Jesus had done for him.
And everyone was amazed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Jesus is now on the east shore of the Sea of Galilee, in an area where mostly non-Jews lived. That is why there are pigs there. For Jews pigs were considered unclean (Leviticus 11:7).
> Verse 1: The manuscripts name the place differently: Gerasenes, Gadarenes or Gergesenes. The WEB has “Gadarenes.” Some Bibles have Gerasenes.
> The man is in a terrible situation: He lives alone among the graves, is chained up, cries out day and night and hurts himself. People in those days saw this as the work of demons.
> Today we would probably think of a severe mental illness. Important: People with mental illnesses are not possessed by demons. They need help from doctors and therapists, and they need people who don’t shut them out.
> Whoever hurts themselves or thinks about it is not alone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. Children and young people: In the US, call or text Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. In the UK, call Childline at 0800 1111. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> “Legion” was a Roman troop of about 5,000 to 6,000 soldiers. Some interpreters also see in it a hint at the Roman occupation, which tormented the land.
> At the end the man sits there calmly, “clothed, and in his right mind.” Jesus has given him back his life and his dignity.
> The people are afraid and ask Jesus to leave. Perhaps because of the loss of the pigs, perhaps out of fear of such great power.
> Verses 19–20: Jesus sends the man back to his own people. He becomes Jesus’ first messenger among non-Jews. Decapolis means “Ten Cities.” They were cities with Greek culture east of the Jordan.

## A father asks for help (Verses 21–24)

[21] Jesus crossed back over in the boat to the other side.
A large crowd gathered around him,
and he was by the sea.
[22] Then one of the rulers of the synagogue came,
named Jairus.
When he saw Jesus,
he fell at his feet
[23] and begged him very much,
“My little daughter is dying.
Please come and lay your hands on her,
so that she may be made healthy and live!”
[24] Jesus went with him.
A large crowd followed him
and pressed against him from all sides.

> **What does this mean?**
> A ruler of the synagogue was a respected man. He took care of the worship services in the synagogue.
> Jairus is a Jewish leader who trusts Jesus and asks for help. Not all leaders were against Jesus.
> A father in great worry about his child: He throws all his dignity aside and falls at Jesus’ feet.

## The woman who touches his clothes (Verses 25–34)

[25] There was a woman
who had suffered from bleeding for 12 years.
[26] She had suffered many things from many doctors
and had spent everything she had.
But she was no better.
Instead she had grown worse.
[27] She had heard about Jesus.
She came up behind him in the crowd
and touched his clothes.
[28] For she said,
“If I just touch his clothes,
I will be made well.”
[29] Immediately the bleeding stopped,
and she felt in her body
that she was healed of her illness.
[30] Immediately Jesus noticed
that power had gone out from him.
He turned around in the crowd
and asked,
“Who touched my clothes?”
[31] His disciples said to him,
“You see the crowd pressing against you,
and you ask, ‘Who touched me?’”
[32] He looked around
to see the woman who had done this.
[33] But the woman was afraid and trembling.
She knew what had happened to her.
She came, fell down in front of him,
and told him the whole truth.
[34] He said to her,
“Daughter, your faith has made you well.
Go in peace,
and be healed of your disease.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The woman had suffered from bleeding for 12 years. According to the Law of Moses a woman with bleeding was considered unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27). She was not allowed to take part in worship, and whoever touched her also became unclean. So she was not only sick, but also shut out and had become poor.
> Important: Monthly bleeding and women’s illnesses are nothing to be ashamed of. They are not a sign of sin.
> Verse 26: In those days the doctors could not help her. That is no reason not to go to the doctor today. Doctors and medicine are a gift.
> She does not dare to come to Jesus openly. Secretly she touches his clothes.
> Jesus does not want her to slip away secretly. In front of everyone he calls her “Daughter.” He takes her into the community and speaks peace to her. Not only her body is healed, but also her dignity.
> “Your faith has made you well”: The Greek word means both “saved” and “healed.”
> Important: Whoever does not get well is not to blame for it. Illness is not a sign of too little faith.

## Jesus raises the daughter of Jairus (Verses 35–43)

[35] While he was still speaking,
people came from the house of the synagogue ruler
and said,
“Your daughter is dead.
Why bother the Teacher any more?”
[36] When Jesus heard what was said,
he immediately said to the ruler of the synagogue,
“Don’t be afraid,
only believe.”
[37] He allowed no one to go with him
except Peter, James,
and John the brother of James.
[38] They came to the house of the synagogue ruler.
He saw the uproar,
the weeping and loud wailing.
[39] He went in and said to them,
“Why are you making an uproar and weeping?
The child is not dead,
but is asleep.”
[40] They laughed at him.
But he put them all out.
He took the father and the mother of the child
and those who were with him
and went in where the child was lying.
[41] He took the child by the hand
and said to her,
“Talitha cumi!”
That means,
“Girl, I tell you, get up!”
[42] Immediately the girl got up
and walked around.
For she was 12 years old.
And they were completely amazed.
[43] He strictly ordered them
that no one should know about this.
And he told them
to give her something to eat.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mark weaves two stories into each other: While Jesus is helping the woman, the girl dies. Both stories have the number 12: The woman was sick for 12 years, the girl is 12 years old.
> Verse 36: “Don’t be afraid, only believe.” Jesus gives courage to the desperate father when everything seems lost.
> Verse 38: When someone died, there was loud mourning. Often paid mourning women and flute players came too.
> Verse 39: “Is asleep”: For Jesus death is not the end. He can wake people from death as from sleep.
> Verse 41: “Talitha cumi” is Aramaic, Jesus’ native language. Here Mark keeps Jesus’ own words.
> Verse 43: Jesus thinks of something very practical: The girl needs something to eat. He cares for the whole person.
> For today: For parents who have lost a child, this story may be hard to read. Christians hope that God also holds their children who have died in his hand. Whoever is grieving for a child can find support, for example, from The Compassionate Friends (compassionatefriends.org in the US, tcf.org.uk in the UK). In the US you can also call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123.
