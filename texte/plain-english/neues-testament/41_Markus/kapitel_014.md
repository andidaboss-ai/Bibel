# Mark – Chapter 14
### The Lord’s Supper, Gethsemane, and the night of the arrest

---

## The plan against Jesus (Verses 1–2)

[1] It was now two days before the Passover
and the Feast of Unleavened Bread.
The chief priests and the scribes
were looking for a way
to arrest Jesus by a trick and kill him.
[2] For they said,
“Not during the feast,
so that there won’t be a riot among the people.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Passover (Pesach) recalls how Israel was freed from Egypt (Exodus 12). For a week people eat unleavened bread (matzah), bread made without yeast.
> A small group of leaders makes plans against Jesus. The people are more on his side, so the leaders are afraid of a riot.
> Important: It is wrong to blame “the Jews” for the death of Jesus.

---

## A woman anoints Jesus (Verses 3–9)

[3] Jesus was in Bethany,
in the house of Simon the leper.
As he was sitting at the table,
a woman came.
She had an alabaster jar
of ointment of pure nard, very costly.
She broke the jar
and poured the ointment over his head.
[4] But some of the people there were angry and said to one another,
“Why has this ointment been wasted?
[5] It could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii,
and the money given to the poor.”
And they grumbled at her.
[6] But Jesus said,
“Leave her alone!
Why are you troubling her?
She has done a good thing for me.
[7] For you always have the poor with you,
and whenever you want to,
you can do them good.
But you will not always have me.
[8] She has done what she could.
She has anointed my body ahead of time for the burial.
[9] Most certainly I tell you,
wherever this Good News is preached
throughout the whole world,
what this woman has done will also be told
in memory of her.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Nard oil was made from a plant from the faraway Himalayas. It was very expensive. Three hundred denarii were about the wages for a whole year of work.
> The woman anoints Jesus like a king. “Christ” means “the Anointed One.” She has understood what the disciples do not yet understand: Jesus will soon die.
> Verse 7: Jesus is not saying that the poor are unimportant. He reminds us of Deuteronomy 15:11: Because there will always be poor people, you shall help them. “Whenever you want to, you can do them good” (14:7) is only in Mark.
> Verse 9: Jesus promises that this woman will be remembered everywhere. In Mark her name is not given.

---

## Judas wants to betray Jesus (Verses 10–11)

[10] Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve,
went to the chief priests
to hand Jesus over to them.
[11] When they heard it,
they were glad
and promised to give him money.
And he looked for a good opportunity
to hand him over.

> **What does this mean?**
> Mark does not say why Judas betrays Jesus.
> Very important: In many languages the name “Judas” sounds like the word for “Jew.” For centuries Christians used Judas as a picture for “the Jews.” That was wrong and caused great injustice. All twelve disciples were Jews, and so was Jesus himself.

---

## The Passover meal is prepared (Verses 12–16)

[12] On the first day of Unleavened Bread,
when the Passover lamb was sacrificed,
his disciples asked him,
“Where do you want us to go
and prepare the Passover meal for you?”
[13] He sent two of his disciples
and said to them,
“Go into the city.
There a man carrying a jar of water
will meet you.
Follow him.
[14] And wherever he goes in,
say to the owner of the house,
‘The Teacher says,
“Where is the guest room,
where I may eat the Passover with my disciples?”’
[15] He himself will show you a large upper room,
furnished and ready.
Get things ready for us there.”
[16] The disciples went out
and came into the city.
They found everything just as he had told them,
and they prepared the Passover meal.

> **What does this mean?**
> On the afternoon before the feast the Passover lambs were sacrificed in the temple. In the evening people ate them with their family or with friends.
> A man carrying a jar of water stood out, because usually women carried the water.
> Some interpreters think Jesus had prepared this secretly, so that Judas could not betray the place ahead of time. That is a guess.

## The last meal (Verses 17–21)

[17] When it was evening,
he came with the twelve.
[18] As they were sitting at the table and eating,
Jesus said,
“Most certainly I tell you,
one of you will betray me,
one who is eating with me.”
[19] They became sad
and began to ask him one after another,
“Surely not I?”
And another said,
“Surely not I?”
[20] He answered them,
“It is one of the twelve,
the one who dips into the dish with me.
[21] For the Son of Man is going his way,
as it is written about him.
But woe to that man
through whom the Son of Man is betrayed!
It would be better for that man
if he had not been born.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Eating together was a sign of friendship and trust. That makes the betrayal all the more painful. This reminds us of Psalm 41:9 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 41:10). There someone complains that even his close friend, who ate his bread with him, has lifted his heel against him. That means: He has turned against me.
> All the disciples ask: “Surely not I?” Each one knows that he can be weak.
> Verse 19: The words “And another said” are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.

---

## The Lord’s Supper (Verses 22–26)

[22] As they were eating,
Jesus took bread,
blessed it,
broke it,
gave it to them, and said,
“Take, eat.
This is my body.”
[23] Then he took the cup,
gave thanks,
and gave it to them.
And they all drank from it.
[24] He said to them,
“This is my blood of the new covenant,
which is poured out for many.
[25] Most certainly I tell you,
I will no longer drink of the fruit of the vine
until that day
when I drink it new in God’s Kingdom.”
[26] When they had sung a hymn,
they went out to the Mount of Olives.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here begins what Christians still celebrate today: the Lord’s Supper, also called the Eucharist or Holy Communion.
> Jesus takes bread and wine from the Passover meal and gives them a new meaning: His body and his blood are given for people.
> Christians understand this in different ways. Catholic and Orthodox Christians believe that the bread and wine really become the body and blood of Christ. Lutheran Christians believe that Christ is present in, with and under the bread and wine. Reformed and many other Protestant Christians understand it more as a sign and a remembrance, in which Christ is spiritually present.
> Covenant: a binding agreement, a promise of faithfulness between God and people.
> Verse 24: “My blood of the new covenant” reminds us of Exodus 24:8. The word “new” is missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has it. The new covenant reminds us of Jeremiah 31:31–34.
> Important: The new covenant does not cancel God’s covenant with Israel. God stays faithful to his people (Romans 11:29).
> Verse 26: At the Passover meal people sing psalms, the “Hallel psalms” (Psalms 113–118).

## Jesus predicts that Peter will deny him (Verses 27–31)

[27] Jesus said to them,
“Tonight all of you will fall away because of me.
For it is written,
‘I will strike the shepherd,
and the sheep will be scattered.’
[28] But after I am raised up,
I will go ahead of you into Galilee.”
[29] But Peter said to him,
“Even if all fall away,
I will not!”
[30] Jesus said to him,
“Most certainly I tell you,
today, this very night,
before the rooster crows twice,
you will deny me three times.”
[31] But he said all the more strongly,
“If I must die with you,
I will not deny you.”
They all said the same thing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27 quotes Zechariah 13:7.
> Jesus knows that his friends will leave him alone. But already now he promises: After the resurrection we will see each other again.
> Only Mark says: “before the rooster crows twice.”

## Jesus prays in Gethsemane (Verses 32–42)

[32] They came to a place
called Gethsemane.
He said to his disciples,
“Sit here
while I pray.”
[33] He took Peter, James, and John with him.
And he began to be deeply troubled and distressed.
[34] He said to them,
“My soul is so sad that I could die.
Stay here and keep watch.”
[35] He went a little farther,
fell on the ground,
and prayed
that, if it were possible,
the hour might pass away from him.
[36] He said,
“Abba, Father,
all things are possible to you.
Please take this cup away from me.
But not what I want,
but what you want.”
[37] He came back
and found them sleeping.
He said to Peter,
“Simon, are you sleeping?
Couldn’t you keep watch for one hour?
[38] Watch and pray,
so that you don’t fall into temptation.
The spirit indeed is willing,
but the flesh is weak.”
[39] Again he went away
and prayed, saying the same words.
[40] When he came back,
he found them sleeping again,
for their eyes were very heavy.
And they didn’t know what to answer him.
[41] He came the third time
and said to them,
“Are you still sleeping and resting?
It is enough.
The hour has come.
The Son of Man is betrayed into the hands of sinners.
[42] Get up! Let’s go.
The one who betrays me is here.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Gethsemane means “oil press.” It was a garden with olive trees on the Mount of Olives.
> Jesus is deeply shaken and very afraid. He throws himself on the ground. Here we see him as completely human.
> “Abba” is Aramaic and means “Father.” It is a trusting way of speaking, the way a child speaks to his father. Here only Mark keeps this word.
> Jesus prays honestly: “Please take this cup away from me.” But he entrusts himself to God: “Not what I want, but what you want.”
> For today: Jesus, too, was afraid. Fear and despair are not signs of weak faith. Whoever has great fear or dark thoughts should not stay alone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> The disciples sleep. Jesus is disappointed, but he understands: “The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.”

## Jesus is arrested (Verses 43–52)

[43] Immediately, while he was still speaking,
Judas, one of the twelve, came.
With him came a crowd with swords and clubs,
sent by the chief priests,
the scribes, and the elders.
[44] The betrayer had given them a sign:
“The one I kiss is the man.
Arrest him,
and lead him away under guard!”
[45] When he came,
he went straight to Jesus and said,
“Rabbi! Rabbi!”
And he kissed him.
[46] Then they laid their hands on him
and arrested him.
[47] But one of those who were standing by
drew his sword,
struck the servant of the high priest,
and cut off his ear.
[48] Jesus said to them,
“Have you come out with swords and clubs to arrest me,
as if I were a robber?
[49] Every day I was with you in the temple teaching,
and you didn’t arrest me.
But this has happened so that the Scriptures might be fulfilled.”
[50] They all left him and fled.
[51] A young man was following him.
He was wearing only a linen cloth around his naked body.
The young men grabbed him.
[52] But he left the linen cloth behind
and ran away from them naked.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days a kiss was a normal greeting, also from students to their teacher. Judas misuses this sign of friendship.
> Verse 47: Mark does not say who drew the sword. In John 18:10 it is Peter. In Matthew, Jesus says about it: “All those who take the sword will die by the sword” (Matthew 26:52). Jesus does not want violence.
> Verse 50: All the disciples flee. Jesus is completely alone.
> Verses 51–52 are told only by Mark. No one knows who this young man was. Some think it was Mark himself. But that is not certain. The scene shows: Everyone abandons Jesus, so quickly that one even leaves his clothing behind.

## Jesus before the Council (Verses 53–65)

[53] They led Jesus away to the high priest.
All the chief priests, the elders, and the scribes
came together with him.
[54] Peter had followed him at a distance
right into the courtyard of the high priest.
He was sitting with the guards
and warming himself at the fire.
[55] The chief priests and the whole council
were looking for testimony against Jesus,
so that they could put him to death.
But they found none.
[56] For many gave false testimony against him,
but their testimony did not agree.
[57] Some stood up
and gave false testimony against him:
[58] “We heard him say,
‘I will destroy this temple
that is made with hands,
and in three days I will build another
made without hands.’”
[59] But even so their testimony did not agree.
[60] Then the high priest stood up in the middle
and asked Jesus,
“Have you no answer?
What is this that these men are saying against you?”
[61] But he stayed silent
and answered nothing.
Again the high priest asked him,
“Are you the Christ,
the Son of the Blessed?”
[62] Jesus said,
“I am.
And you will see the Son of Man
sitting at the right hand of Power
and coming with the clouds of the sky.”
[63] Then the high priest tore his clothes and said,
“Why do we need any more witnesses?
[64] You have heard the blasphemy!
What do you think?”
And they all condemned him
as deserving death.
[65] Some began
to spit on him,
to cover his face,
to beat him with their fists,
and to say to him,
“Prophesy!”
And the guards slapped him in the face.

> **What does this mean?**
> The Council was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem.
> Mark tells us that the trial was not fair. The witnesses contradict each other. According to the Law of Moses, two witnesses must agree (Deuteronomy 19:15).
> Verse 61: “The Blessed” is a respectful way of speaking about God. Out of reverence, devout Jews avoid saying the name of God.
> Verse 62: In Mark, Jesus answers very clearly: “I am.” For the first time he says in public who he is. He combines Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13.
> Blasphemy: speaking against God or insulting God.
> Very important: Here a particular group of leaders acts on a particular night. The Jewish people as a whole were not involved. In 1965 the Catholic Church declared in the document *Nostra Aetate*: The suffering of Jesus may not be blamed on all the Jews living then, nor on the Jews of today. Protestant churches have also said this clearly.
> Verse 65: Jesus is mocked and beaten. He does not fight back.

## Peter denies Jesus (Verses 66–72)

[66] Peter was below in the courtyard.
One of the servant girls of the high priest came.
[67] She saw Peter warming himself,
looked at him, and said,
“You were also with the Nazarene, Jesus!”
[68] But he denied it and said,
“I neither know nor understand what you are saying.”
He went out onto the porch,
and the rooster crowed.
[69] The servant girl saw him
and began again to say to those who were standing there,
“This man is one of them.”
[70] But he denied it again.
A little while later those standing there said to Peter again,
“You really are one of them.
For you are a Galilean,
and your speech shows it.”
[71] But he began
to curse and to swear,
“I don’t know this man
you are talking about!”
[72] Then the rooster crowed the second time.
Peter remembered the words
that Jesus had said to him:
“Before the rooster crows twice,
you will deny me three times.”
And when he thought about that, he wept.

> **What does this mean?**
> While inside Jesus bravely tells the truth, outside Peter says three times: I don’t know him.
> Verse 68: The words “and the rooster crowed” are missing in some old manuscripts. In verse 70 the words “and your speech shows it” are also missing in some manuscripts. The WEB has both.
> Peter came from Galilee. People could tell that from the way he spoke, just as people recognize accents today.
> Verse 72: Peter weeps. He sees his failure.
> According to an old tradition, Peter was an important witness for the Gospel of Mark. If so, he did not keep quiet about this embarrassing story about himself.
> For today: Even someone who has failed can make a new start. Jesus did not give up on Peter (16:7). Whoever feels very bad because of their own guilt may talk with someone. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
