# Mark – Chapter 2
### New wine into fresh wineskins

---

## Four friends and a paralyzed man (Verses 1–12)

[1] After some days Jesus came back to Capernaum.
People heard that he was at home.
[2] Immediately many people gathered,
so that there was no more room,
not even around the door.
And he spoke the word to them.
[3] Then four people came,
carrying a paralyzed man to him.
[4] Because of the crowd
they could not get near him.
So they removed the roof
right above the place where he was.
They broke an opening through it
and let down the mat
that the paralyzed man was lying on.
[5] When Jesus saw their faith,
he said to the paralyzed man,
“Son, your sins are forgiven you.”
[6] But some of the scribes were sitting there.
They were thinking in their hearts,
[7] “Why does this man speak like that?
He is speaking blasphemy!
Who can forgive sins but God alone?”
[8] Immediately Jesus knew in his spirit
what they were thinking.
He said to them,
“Why are you thinking these things in your hearts?
[9] Which is easier:
to say to the paralyzed man,
‘Your sins are forgiven,’
or to say,
‘Get up, take up your mat, and walk’?
[10] But I want you to know
that the Son of Man has authority
on earth to forgive sins.”
And he said to the paralyzed man,
[11] “I tell you,
get up, take up your mat,
and go to your house!”
[12] He got up,
immediately took up the mat,
and went out in front of them all.
They were all amazed,
praised God, and said,
“We have never seen anything like this!”

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days houses had flat roofs made of wooden beams, branches and clay. You could climb up by a staircase on the outside of the house.
> The four friends don’t give up. They find a way, even if it is unusual. Jesus sees “their faith,” the faith of the friends. The trust of other people can help someone too.
> Paralyzed: unable to move parts of the body, for example the legs.
> Verse 5: Jesus speaks first about forgiveness. That does not mean the paralysis was a punishment for sins. Illness and disability are not a punishment from God (compare John 9:1–3). Jesus shows: God wants to make the whole person well, body and soul.
> Verse 7: The scribes are right that only God can forgive sins. That is why what Jesus does is so amazing. He acts in God’s place.
> Blasphemy: speaking against God or insulting God.
> “Son of Man”: Jesus often calls himself this. The term reminds us of Daniel 7:13–14.

## Jesus calls Levi and eats with tax collectors (Verses 13–17)

[13] Jesus went out again by the seaside.
The whole crowd came to him,
and he taught them.
[14] As he was passing by,
he saw Levi the son of Alphaeus
sitting at the tax office.
He said to him,
“Follow me!”
And he got up and followed him.
[15] Jesus was reclining at the table in Levi’s house.
Many tax collectors and sinners
sat down with Jesus and his disciples.
For there were many of them,
and they followed him.
[16] When the scribes and the Pharisees saw
that he was eating with the sinners and tax collectors,
they said to his disciples,
“Why does he eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?”
[17] When Jesus heard it,
he said to them,
“Those who are healthy don’t need a doctor,
but those who are sick do.
I didn’t come to call the righteous,
but sinners to repentance.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Levi is a tax collector. In the Gospel of Matthew he is called Matthew (Matthew 9:9).
> Tax collectors collected customs and taxes for the rulers. Many demanded more than they were allowed. That is why they were unpopular.
> In those days eating together meant: We belong together. Jesus eats with people whom others avoid. He shows: God shuts no one out.
> Verse 16: The WEB says “the scribes and the Pharisees.” Many old manuscripts say the scribes of the Pharisees, meaning scribes who belonged to the Pharisees. The Pharisees wanted to keep God’s commandments very carefully in everyday life, including when eating. Their question is meant seriously, not simply in a mean way.
> Verse 17: The words “to repentance” are missing in many old manuscripts. They are in Luke 5:32. The WEB has them.
> For today: The church should be a place for everyone, especially for those who feel shut out.

## The question about fasting (Verses 18–22)

[18] John’s disciples and the Pharisees were fasting.
People came and asked Jesus,
“Why do John’s disciples
and the disciples of the Pharisees fast,
but your disciples don’t fast?”
[19] Jesus said to them,
“Can the groomsmen fast
while the bridegroom is with them?
As long as they have the bridegroom with them,
they can’t fast.
[20] But the days will come
when the bridegroom will be taken away from them.
Then, in that day, they will fast.
[21] No one sews a piece of new, unshrunk cloth
on an old garment.
Otherwise the new patch shrinks,
tears away from the old cloth,
and the hole becomes worse.
[22] And no one puts new wine into old wineskins.
Otherwise the wine will burst the skins.
The wine pours out,
and the skins are ruined.
Instead, new wine is put into fresh wineskins.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Fasting means: going without food for a time, in order to turn completely toward God. Devout Jews often fasted on certain days.
> Jesus compares his time to a wedding. At a wedding people celebrate and don’t fast. Jesus is the “bridegroom.” As long as he is there, there is joy.
> Groomsmen: the friends of the bridegroom at a wedding.
> Verse 20: Jesus hints at his death. Then there will be times of fasting again. Many Christians still fast today, for example in the time before Easter.
> Verses 21–22: Wineskins were made of animal skin. New wine is still fermenting and expands. Old skins are brittle and burst. Jesus’ message is so new and alive that it needs new forms.
> Important: This is not a judgment that Judaism is old and broken. Jesus himself lived as a Jew. It is about being open to the new things God is doing.

## Grain on the Sabbath (Verses 23–28)

[23] On a Sabbath day Jesus was going through the grain fields.
As they went along, his disciples began
to pick the heads of grain.
[24] The Pharisees said to him,
“Look!
Why are they doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”
[25] He said to them,
“Did you never read what David did
when he was in need and hungry,
he and those who were with him?
[26] How he went into God’s house
at the time of Abiathar the high priest,
and ate the show bread,
which only the priests are allowed to eat,
and also gave some to those who were with him?”
[27] He said to them,
“The Sabbath was made for man,
not man for the Sabbath.
[28] So the Son of Man is lord
even of the Sabbath.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The Sabbath is the holy day of rest (Exodus 20:8–11). It was allowed to pick heads of grain at the edge of someone else’s field (Deuteronomy 23:25; in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 23:26). But some teachers saw picking grain on the Sabbath as harvest work. People had different opinions about this.
> Verses 25–26: The story of David is in 1 Samuel 21:1–6 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 21:2–7). There, however, the priest is called Ahimelech. Abiathar was his son and later became high priest. There are different explanations for this difference. Perhaps “at the time of Abiathar” simply means the time when Abiathar lived.
> Show bread: twelve holy loaves of bread that were laid out before God in the house of God.
> Verse 27 is told only by Mark. A similar sentence is also found in an old Jewish explanation of the Scriptures. It says, in effect: The Sabbath is there for you, not you for the Sabbath. So Jesus says something that other Jewish teachers also believed.
> The Sabbath is God’s gift to people, so that they can rest and breathe again. It should not be a burden that harms people.
> For today: People still need days of rest today. Sunday or another day off protects us from doing nothing but work.
