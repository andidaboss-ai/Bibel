# Colossians – Chapter 1
### Christ, the image of the invisible God

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Colossians

Colossae was a small town in what is now Turkey, in the valley of the Lycus River. The cities of Laodicea and Hierapolis were nearby.
Paul did not found the church in Colossae himself. His coworker Epaphras preached the Good News there (1:7).
The letter calls itself a letter from Paul and Timothy. It was written in prison (4:18). Many scholars today think that a student of Paul wrote it, in Paul’s spirit. Others consider Paul himself to be the author.
In the church there were teachers who thought special rules, festival days and the worship of angels were necessary. The letter says against this: In Christ you already have everything. He is Lord over all creation.
In chapter 1 there is a famous song about Christ. The letter is closely related to the letter to the Ephesians.
**An important note:** In chapters 3 and 4 there are rules for married couples, children, parents, slaves and masters. They reflect the order of society at that time. In the boxes we explain how Christians understand them today.
The letter to the Colossians has 4 chapters.

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God,
and Timothy,
our brother,
[2] write to the saints and faithful brothers in Christ
at Colossae:
Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Saints” are all Christians. They belong to God.
> Timothy was a close coworker of Paul.

---

## Thanks for the church’s faith (Verses 3–8)

[3] We give thanks to God,
the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ,
and always pray for you.
[4] For we have heard of your faith in Christ Jesus
and of your love for all the saints.
[5] You believe and love because of the hope
that is laid up for you in the heavens.
You heard of it before,
in the word of the truth,
the Good News.
[6] It has come to you.
In all the world it is bearing fruit and growing,
just as it does among you,
since the day
you heard the grace of God
and knew it in truth.
[7] That is how you learned it from Epaphras,
our beloved fellow servant.
He is a faithful servant of Christ for you.
[8] He also told us about your love in the Spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 4–5: Faith, love and hope belong together (compare 1 Corinthians 13:13).
> Verse 6: The Good News is like a plant. It grows and bears fruit, everywhere in the world.
> Verse 7: Epaphras came from Colossae (4:12). He founded the church. Paul knows the church only through him.

---

## Prayer for the church (Verses 9–14)

[9] For this reason we also have not stopped
praying for you
since the day
we heard about it.
We ask God
that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will,
in all wisdom and understanding
that the Spirit gives.
[10] In this way you are to live
in a way worthy of the Lord
and please him in all things.
You are to bear fruit in every good work
and grow in the knowledge of God.
[11] You are to be strengthened with all power,
according to the might of his glory,
so that you have endurance and patience,
with joy.
[12] Give thanks to the Father.
He has made us fit
to share in the inheritance of the saints in light.
[13] He has rescued us from the power of darkness
and brought us into the Kingdom of the Son he loves.
[14] In him we have redemption:
the forgiveness of sins.

*redemption* = being set free, like a prisoner who is bought free

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: Knowledge is not an end in itself. Whoever knows God’s will is to live by it.
> Verse 11: Patience and endurance don’t come from your own strength. God gives the strength for them.
> Verse 13: A picture of a change of ruler: Darkness once had power over us. Now we belong to the Kingdom of Jesus.
> Verse 14: “Redemption” means: being bought free, the way a prisoner is bought free.

## The song of Christ (Verses 15–20)

[15] He is the image of the invisible God,
the firstborn of all creation.
[16] For by him all things were created,
in the heavens and on the earth,
visible things and invisible things:
whether thrones or dominions
or principalities or powers.
All things have been created through him and for him.
[17] He is before all things.
And in him all things hold together.
[18] He is the head of the body,
the assembly.
He is the beginning,
the firstborn from the dead,
so that in all things he might be first.
[19] For all the fullness was pleased
to live in him,
[20] and through him to reconcile all things to himself,
whether things on the earth
or things in the heavens.
He made peace
through the blood of his cross.

*principalities* = rulers, powers
*reconcile* = to make peace again between people who were apart

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 15–20 are probably a song of the first Christians. It has two parts: Christ and creation (verses 15–17), and Christ and reconciliation (verses 18–20).
> Verse 15: “The image of the invisible God”: No one can see God. But in Jesus you can see what God is like.
> “The firstborn of all creation” doesn’t mean that Christ himself was created. At that time the firstborn had the highest rank and the right of the heir. Christ stands above everything that is created.
> Verse 16: “Thrones or dominions or principalities or powers”: This means invisible powers that people at that time were afraid of. The letter says: They too were created by Christ. No one needs to fear them.
> Verse 17: Christ holds the world together.
> Verse 20: God wants to reconcile not only people, but “all things,” the whole creation. For today: That is why caring for creation is also part of the Christian faith.

---

## You are reconciled (Verses 21–23)

[21] You too were once far from God.
In your minds you were his enemies,
because you did evil deeds.
[22] But now he has reconciled you
in the body of his flesh through death.
In this way he wants to present you holy,
without defect
and blameless before him.
[23] But for this you must continue in the faith,
grounded and steadfast.
Don’t let yourselves be moved away from the hope of the Good News
that you heard.
It was proclaimed in all creation under heaven.
And I,
Paul,
was made its servant.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–22: It was not God who had to be reconciled. God himself reconciled people to himself. He took the first step.
> Verse 22: “In the body of his flesh”: Jesus was a real human being with a real body. The letter stresses this against teachers who looked down on the physical.
> Verse 23: “In all creation” is an exaggeration to show: The Good News is meant for all people.

---

## Paul serves the church (Verses 24–29)

[24] Now I rejoice in my sufferings for you.
What is still lacking of the afflictions of Christ,
I fill up in my own body
for the sake of his body,
the assembly.
[25] I was made its servant.
God gave me this task for you:
I am to proclaim the word of God fully,
[26] the mystery
that has been hidden for ages and generations.
But now it has been revealed to his saints.
[27] God wanted to show them
how rich and glorious this mystery is among the nations:
Christ in you,
the hope of glory.
[28] We proclaim him.
We admonish every person
and teach every person
in all wisdom,
so that we may present every person
perfect in Christ Jesus.
[29] For this I work hard and struggle,
with his power
that works mightily in me.

*admonish* = to warn and advise someone in a serious way

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24 is hard to understand. It doesn’t mean that the suffering of Jesus on the cross was not enough. Interpreters usually understand it like this: Christ suffers along with his church. When Paul suffers for the church, he shares in this suffering.
> Important: Suffering is not something good in itself. No one should seek suffering or expect others to suffer.
> Verse 27: The “mystery” is: Christ is also with the people from the nations, not only with Israel. He is “in you.”
> Verse 28: Three times it says “every person” here. The Good News is for everyone, not just for an elite with special knowledge.
