# Colossians – Chapter 2
### In Christ you have everything

---

## Paul struggles for the churches (Verses 1–5)

[1] I want
you to know
how greatly I struggle for you,
for you and for those at Laodicea
and for all
who have never seen me in person.
[2] I struggle
so that their hearts may be encouraged,
that they may be knit together in love
and gain all the riches of full understanding.
In this way they are to know the mystery of God,
both of the Father and of Christ.
[3] In him all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.
[4] I say this
so that no one may deceive you with persuasive-sounding speech.
[5] For even though I am absent in the flesh,
I am with you in the spirit.
I rejoice
when I see
how good your order is
and how firm your faith in Christ is.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Laodicea was a neighboring city of Colossae. Paul did not know the Christians there in person. Still he struggles for them in prayer.
> Verse 3: Some teachers claimed they had special, secret knowledge. The letter says: You find all wisdom in Christ. You don’t need secret knowledge.
> Verse 4: A warning that is also true today: What sounds good doesn’t have to be true.

---

## Stay rooted in Christ (Verses 6–15)

[6] You received Christ Jesus as Lord.
So now live in him,
[7] rooted and built up in him.
Stay firm in the faith,
just as you were taught,
and overflow with thanksgiving.
[8] Be careful
that no one robs you
through his philosophy and empty deceit.
They are based on human tradition
and on the elemental spirits of the world,
and not on Christ.
[9] For in him all the fullness of the Deity lives in bodily form.
Deity means: God’s nature, being God.
[10] And in him you too are made full.
He is the head over all rulers and powers.
[11] In him you were also circumcised
with a circumcision
not made with hands.
You put off the body of the sins of the flesh,
in the circumcision of Christ.
[12] You were buried with him in baptism.
In it you were also raised with him,
through faith in the working of God,
who raised him from the dead.
[13] You were dead through your wrongs
and because you were uncircumcised.
But God made you alive together with him.
He forgave us all our wrongs.
[14] He wiped out the record of debt
that stood against us with its rules.
He took it away
and nailed it to the cross.
A record of debt is a signed list of what someone owes.
[15] He disarmed the rulers and powers
and made a public show of them.
On the cross he triumphed over them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Like a tree with deep roots and like a house on a firm foundation: This is how firm faith in Christ should be.
> Verse 8: “Philosophy” here doesn’t mean thinking in itself. It means certain teachings that lead away from Christ. “Elemental spirits of the world”: At that time many believed that the stars and the forces of nature decide people’s lives.
> Verse 9: One of the most important sentences about Jesus: In him God is fully present, in a human body.
> Verses 11–12: The Christians from the nations were not circumcised. The letter says: Through baptism you belong completely to Christ. You don’t have to be circumcised in addition.
> Verse 14: A picture: God has crossed out the list of our guilt, the “record of debt,” and nailed it to the cross.
> Verse 15: A picture from Rome: After a victory the general led the defeated enemies through the city in a victory parade. On the cross Christ defeated the evil powers.

## Don’t let rules be forced on you (Verses 16–23)

[16] So let no one judge you
about eating or drinking,
or about a feast day,
a new moon
or a Sabbath day.
[17] These are a shadow of the things
that are to come.
But the reality belongs to Christ.
[18] Let no one rob you of your prize
who delights in self-abasement
and the worshiping of angels.
Self-abasement means: making yourself small and low in a showy way.
Such a person relies on things
that he has not seen.
He is puffed up for no reason
by his human mind.
[19] He doesn’t hold firmly to the Head.
From him the whole body is supplied
and held together by joints and ligaments.
So it grows
the way God makes it grow.
[20] You died with Christ
and were set free from the elemental spirits of the world.
Why then do you let rules be made for you,
as though you were still living in the world?
[21] “Don’t handle!
Don’t taste!
Don’t touch!”
[22] All these things perish
when they are used.
These are commandments and teachings of people.
[23] These rules do look wise:
with self-made worship,
with humility
and with severity to the body.
But they are of no value
against the desires of the flesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: This is about teachers in Colossae who wanted to force strict rules on the Christians from the nations, together with the worship of angels and fasting. It was a mix of different religions.
> Very important: This verse is no criticism of the Jewish Sabbath or of Jewish festivals. For Jews they are a gift from God. Many Jewish Christians also continued to keep them. The letter only says: No one may judge Christians from the nations because they don’t keep these days.
> Verse 17: A picture: A shadow shows that something is there. The reality itself is Christ.
> Verse 18: “The worshiping of angels”: Some thought you had to worship angels or gain special insight through visions. The letter says: Christ is enough.
> Verses 21–23: Strict bans and harshness against your own body don’t make a person better.
> For today: No person and no group may frighten others with ever new rules. Where groups control people with strict rules and put them under pressure, be careful. Counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse can help.
