# Colossians – Chapter 4
### Pray and speak kindly

---

## A word to the masters (Verse 1)

[1] Masters,
give your servants
what is just and fair.
For you know:
You also have a Master in heaven.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1 still belongs to the household code from chapter 3. The masters are reminded: You are not the highest power. You must answer to God.
> The WEB says *servants* here. Slaves are meant. For today: Fair wages and fair working conditions are God’s command. Exploitation is wrong.

---

## Pray and live wisely (Verses 2–6)

[2] Continue steadfastly in prayer.
Stay alert in it with thanksgiving.
[3] Pray for us too,
that God may open to us a door for the word,
so that we can speak the mystery of Christ.
Because of it I am also in chains.
[4] Pray
that I may make it as clear
as I ought to speak.
[5] Walk in wisdom toward those
who are outside the church.
Make good use of the time.
[6] Let your speech always be kind,
seasoned with salt.
Then you will know
how you ought to answer each one.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “Open to us a door”: A picture for a good opportunity. Paul is in prison. He doesn’t first ask for freedom, but for opportunities to tell people about Christ.
> Verse 5: Christians are to behave wisely, fairly and kindly toward people outside the church.
> Verse 6: “Seasoned with salt”: Words should not be bland, but have a good taste. They should be kind, interesting and helpful.
> For today: This is also true for conversations about faith. Not pushy, but kind and respectful.

## News and greetings (Verses 7–18)

[7] Tychicus will tell you everything
about how I am doing.
He is a beloved brother,
a faithful servant
and fellow servant in the Lord.
[8] I am sending him to you for this very purpose,
so that he may know
how you are doing
and may comfort your hearts.
[9] He comes together with Onesimus,
the faithful and beloved brother.
He is one of you.
The two of them will tell you everything
that is going on here.
[10] Aristarchus,
my fellow prisoner,
greets you,
and Mark,
the cousin of Barnabas.
You have already received instructions about him:
“If he comes to you,
receive him.”
[11] And Jesus,
who is called Justus,
greets you.
Of those who are of the circumcision, these are my only fellow workers for God’s Kingdom.
They have been a comfort to me.
[12] Epaphras greets you.
He is one of you,
a servant of Christ Jesus.
He is always struggling for you in his prayers,
so that you may stand mature and complete
in all the will of God.
[13] For I testify about him:
He works very hard for you
and for those in Laodicea
and for those in Hierapolis.
[14] Luke,
the beloved physician,
and Demas greet you.
[15] Greet the brothers in Laodicea,
and Nymphas
and the assembly in his house.
[16] When this letter has been read among you,
make sure
that it is also read in the assembly of the Laodiceans.
And you also read the letter from Laodicea.
[17] And tell Archippus:
“Take heed to the ministry
that you have received in the Lord,
and fulfill it.”
[18] I,
Paul,
write this greeting with my own hand.
Remember my chains.
Grace be with you.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Physician means: doctor.
> Verse 9: Onesimus was a slave from Colossae. The letter to Philemon tells about him. Here it says: Tychicus comes “together with Onesimus, the faithful and beloved brother.” In the church he is a brother of equal worth.
> Verse 10: Mark had once parted from Paul (Acts 15:37–39). Now they are reconciled again. Many connect him with the Gospel of Mark.
> Verse 11: “Jesus” was a common Jewish name at that time. Paul is glad about Jewish coworkers who stand by him.
> Verse 14: Luke was a doctor. Tradition sees him as the author of the Gospel of Luke and of Acts.
> Verse 15: Here it says “Nymphas and the assembly in his house,” as in the WEB. Some old manuscripts say *Nympha* and *in her house*. Then it was a woman who led a house church.
> Verse 16: Letters were read aloud in worship and passed on to other churches. The “letter from Laodicea” has not survived. Some interpreters think it could mean the letter to the Ephesians.
> Verse 18: Paul usually dictated his letters. He wrote the closing greeting himself, as a sign that the letter was genuine.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Colossians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Christ above all:** Christ is the image of the invisible God. All things were created through him. In him all things hold together (chapter 1).
> **Reconciliation for all things:** Through Christ God wants to reconcile the whole creation (chapter 1).
> **In Christ you have everything:** Christians need no secret knowledge, no worship of angels and no strict special rules. No one may judge them for this (chapter 2).
> **The new life:** Putting away the old and putting on compassion, kindness, patience and above all love (chapter 3).
> **All are equally valuable:** In Christ background and social rank don’t count (chapter 3).
> **The household code:** Rules from that time for family and work. What matters is: Everyone stands under Christ. No one may use violence (chapters 3 and 4).
> **Prayer and kind words:** Pray steadfastly and speak kindly with all people (chapter 4).
> **For today:** Christ is greater than all the powers we are afraid of. That is why Christians can live freely and thankfully.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the first letter to the Thessalonians. It is probably the oldest letter of Paul that we know.
