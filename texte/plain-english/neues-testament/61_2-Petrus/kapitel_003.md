# 2 Peter – Chapter 3
### God is patient – Jesus is coming again

---

## Remember (Verses 1–2)

[1] Beloved,
this is now the second letter
that I have written to you.
In both letters I want to stir up your sincere mind
by reminding you.
[2] You are to remember the words
that were spoken before by the holy prophets,
and the commandment
that you received from us,
the apostles of the Lord and Savior.

> **What does this mean?**
> “The second letter”: This probably means the first letter of Peter.
> The letter puts the prophets and the apostles side by side. Both together are the foundation of faith.

---

## “Where is he?” (Verses 3–7)

[3] Know this above all:
In the last days mockers will come.
They live by their own desires.
[4] And they say,
“Where is the promise
of his coming?
Since the fathers died,
all things continue
as they were from the beginning of the creation.”
[5] For they deliberately forget:
Long ago there were heavens
and an earth.
It was formed by the word of God
out of water and in the middle of water.
[6] And by water the world of that time
was flooded and destroyed.
[7] But the heavens and the earth that exist now
are kept for fire by the same word.
They are reserved for the day of judgment,
when ungodly people are destroyed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: The first Christians hoped that Jesus would come back very soon. When the first generation died, some asked mockingly: “Where is he?”
> Verses 5–6: The letter answers: It is not true that everything always stays the same. God created the world (Genesis 1), and there was the great flood (Genesis 6–9).
> Verse 7: The “fire” is a picture for God’s judgment. The Bible uses such pictures to say: Evil will not remain forever.

---

## God is patient (Verses 8–10)

[8] But don’t forget this one thing,
beloved:
With the Lord one day is like a thousand years,
and a thousand years like one day.
[9] The Lord is not slow about his promise,
as some think he is slow.
But he is patient with you.
For he doesn’t want
anyone to be lost.
He wants
all to turn back.
[10] But the day of the Lord will come
like a thief in the night.
On that day the heavens
will pass away with a great noise.
The elements will be dissolved in the heat.
And the earth and the works that are in it
will be burned up.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8 recalls Psalm 90:4: For God a thousand years are like one day. God measures time differently from us.
> Verse 9 is one of the most beautiful sentences in this letter: That Jesus has not come back yet is no sign of weakness. It is patience. God wants **all** people to be saved. He waits for everyone.
> Verse 10: “Like a thief in the night”: No one knows when the time will come (compare 1 Thessalonians 5:2). That is why no one should calculate a date.
> At the end of verse 10 it says: “And the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up.” Some old manuscripts say something else there: The earth and everything that people have done on it will be found. That means: It will be uncovered before God.

> **Important note**
> These verses are no permission not to take care of the earth. God has entrusted creation to people (Genesis 2:15). Christians are to treat it well. The pictures of fire are also not an exact description of how the world will end. They say: God has the last word.

---

## How should we live? (Verses 11–13)

[11] If all these things will pass away like this,
how much should you then
live holy and godly lives!
[12] You are waiting for the coming of the day of God
and longing for it.
On that day the heavens will burn and pass away,
and the elements will melt in the heat.
[13] But according to his promise we are waiting
for new heavens and a new earth.
Righteousness lives there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Longing for it”: You can’t force God’s coming. But you can look forward to it and already live now in a way that fits God’s new world.
> Verse 13: The goal is not destruction, but something new: new heavens and a new earth. This promise is already found in the prophet Isaiah (Isaiah 65:17 and 66:22). At the end of the Bible it comes again (Revelation 21).
> “Righteousness lives there”: In God’s new world there is no more injustice.

---

## Last words (Verses 14–18)

[14] So,
beloved,
since you are waiting for all these things,
make every effort
to be found by him in peace,
without spot and without blame.
[15] Regard the patience of our Lord as salvation.
Our beloved brother Paul also wrote this to you,
with the wisdom
given to him.
[16] He speaks of these things
in all his letters.
In them some things are hard to understand.
Ignorant people
who are not standing firm
twist them,
as they also do with the other Scriptures.
In this way they bring destruction on themselves.
[17] But you,
beloved,
already know this beforehand.
So be careful
that you are not carried away by the error of the wicked
and lose your firm footing.
[18] But grow in the grace
and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.
To him be the glory,
both now and forever.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: God’s patience is a chance. Every day is a gift to turn back.
> Verses 15–16: Here Paul is called “our beloved brother.” Peter and Paul once had a dispute (Galatians 2:11–14). Here they stand together.
> Verse 16: An honest sentence: In the Bible too some things are hard to understand. That is why you shouldn’t simply twist the Bible the way you need it at the moment. It helps to read it together with others.
> Verse 16 also shows: Paul’s letters were already collected at that time and read like “Scriptures.”
> Verse 18: The letter ends as it began: with growing in faith (compare 1:5–8).

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the second letter of Peter?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Growing in faith:** From faith up to love, step by step (chapter 1).
> **Eyewitnesses:** The apostles did not tell fables. They saw the glory of Jesus (chapter 1).
> **God’s word like a lamp:** It shines in the dark until the day comes (chapter 1).
> **A warning against false teachers:** They are greedy and promise freedom, but they make people unfree (chapter 2).
> **God is patient:** He wants all people to turn back and no one to be lost (chapter 3).
> **Hope:** God creates new heavens and a new earth, where righteousness lives (chapter 3).
> **For today:** Read the Bible together and honestly. Some things are hard to understand, and that is okay.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the first letter of John. Its big theme is love: God is love.
