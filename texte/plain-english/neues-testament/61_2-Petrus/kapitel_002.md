# 2 Peter – Chapter 2
### A warning against false teachers

---

> **Important note first**
> This chapter uses very harsh words against false teachers. That is how people wrote about disputes at that time. The harsh words are no permission to insult people today who believe differently. Judgment on people belongs to God alone.
> Many parts of this chapter are very similar to the letter of Jude.

---

## False teachers will come (Verses 1–3)

[1] But there were also false prophets among the people.
In the same way there will be false teachers among you.
They will secretly bring in destructive false teachings.
They even deny the Master
who bought them.
So they bring swift destruction on themselves.
[2] Many will follow their immoral ways.
Because of them the way of the truth will be spoken against.
[3] Out of greed they will exploit you
with deceptive words.
The judgment on them has long been ready
and doesn’t wait.
And their destruction doesn’t sleep.

> **What does this mean?**
> “False prophets among the people”: This means the people of Israel. The Hebrew Bible also warns against false prophets.
> “The Master who bought them”: Jesus has set people free. But the false teachers live as if he had nothing to say.
> “Out of greed”: One mark of false teachers is that they want to make money with faith. That is a good warning sign today too.

---

## God knows how to rescue and to judge (Verses 4–10a)

[4] For God didn’t spare even angels
when they sinned.
He cast them down to Tartarus
and handed them over to pits of darkness,
where they are kept for judgment.
[5] He also didn’t spare the ancient world.
But he kept Noah safe,
a preacher of righteousness,
with seven others,
when he brought a flood on the world of the ungodly.
[6] And he turned the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah
into ashes
and condemned them to destruction.
So he made them an example
for those who would live ungodly lives.
[7] And he rescued righteous Lot,
who suffered greatly from the wild living
of wicked people.
[8] For this righteous man lived among them.
And day after day
his righteous soul was tormented
as he saw and heard their lawless deeds.
[9] So the Lord knows
how to rescue the godly from temptation
and how to keep the unrighteous
under punishment until the day of judgment,
[10] especially those
who follow the desires of the body
and defile themselves
and who despise all authority.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Tartarus” is a Greek word. It means a deep, dark place of punishment. Here the letter recalls old Jewish stories about angels who set themselves against God (compare Genesis 6:1–4).
> Verses 5–8 recall the flood with Noah (Genesis 6–9) and Sodom and Lot (Genesis 19). The message: God sees evil. But he also doesn’t forget those who hold on to him.
> Verse 9 is a comfort: Whoever trusts God is not forgotten, even when a lot of evil happens around them.

---

## How the false teachers live (Verses 10b–16)

They are bold and self-willed.
They are not afraid
to speak evil of the glorious ones.
[11] Yet not even angels,
who are much stronger and more powerful,
bring a slanderous judgment against them before the Lord.
[12] But these people
are like animals without reason,
born by nature
to be caught and killed.
They speak evil of things
that they don’t understand.
That is why in their destruction
they will surely be destroyed themselves.
[13] They receive the wages of their wrongdoing.
For them it is a pleasure
to party in broad daylight.
They are stains and blemishes.
They enjoy their deceptions
while they feast and eat with you.
[14] Their eyes are full of adultery.
They can’t stop sinning.
They entice people
who are not yet standing firm.
Their heart is trained in greed.
They are children of a curse!
[15] They have left the right way
and gone astray.
They have followed the way of Balaam,
the son of Beor.
He loved the wages of wrongdoing.
[16] But he was rebuked for his disobedience:
A mute donkey spoke with a human voice
and stopped the madness of the prophet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: “The glorious ones” are probably powers of angels. The letter says: Even the angels don’t speak without respect. But the false teachers mock everything.
> Verse 13: “While they feast and eat with you”: This means the shared meals of the church. The false teachers sit at the table too and take advantage of the fellowship.
> Verses 15–16 recall Balaam and his talking donkey (Numbers 22). Balaam let himself be hired for money. Of all things, an animal saw more than the prophet.

---

## Empty promises (Verses 17–22)

[17] These people are springs without water
and clouds driven by a storm.
The blackest darkness
is kept for them forever.
[18] For they speak big, boastful words
that are empty.
With the desires of the body
and with wild living
they entice people
who are just escaping from those
who live in error.
[19] They promise them freedom.
But they themselves are slaves of corruption.
For a person is a slave of whatever
has overcome him.
[20] For they had already escaped the filth of the world
because they came to know the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.
But if they get entangled in it again
and are overcome,
then in the end things are worse for them
than at the beginning.
[21] For it would have been better for them
not to know the way of righteousness at all
than to know it
and then to turn away from the holy commandment
that was given to them.
[22] With them it has happened
as the true proverb says,
“The dog turns to his own vomit again,”
and,
“The sow that has washed
goes back to rolling in the mud.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 17: “Springs without water”: They promise a lot, but they give nothing that satisfies life.
> Verse 19: An important sentence: Whoever may do everything is not yet free. Whatever rules a person makes them unfree, for example an addiction.
> Verses 20–22: These verses are a serious warning to the false teachers. They were not written to frighten people who doubt or have become weak. Whoever worries whether they are “too far away” shows by this very worry that God matters to them. God waits for everyone who comes back (compare Luke 15).
> Verse 22: The first proverb is also in Proverbs 26:11.
