# 2 Peter – Chapter 1
### Grow in faith

---

## Before we begin: The second letter of Peter

The letter names itself a letter of Simon Peter. It is written like a farewell letter: Peter knows that he will die soon (1:14).
Today many scholars think that the letter was written much later by a student in Peter’s name. At that time this was a well-known way to pass on the legacy of a teacher. Others consider Peter himself the author.
The letter warns against false teachers. Some said: Jesus is not coming back at all. The letter answers: God keeps his promises. He is only patient because he wants all people to turn back.
Chapter 2 is very similar to the letter of Jude.
The second letter of Peter has 3 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Simon Peter,
a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ,
writes to those
who have received the same precious faith as we have,
through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ:
[2] May grace and peace be given to you in abundance
through the knowledge of God
and of Jesus our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The letter goes to all “who have received the same precious faith as we have.” The readers did not see Jesus themselves. But their faith is just as precious as the faith of the apostles.

---

## A ladder of faith (Verses 3–11)

[3] His divine power has given us all things
that we need for life and godliness.
This happens through the knowledge of him
who called us by his own glory and goodness.
[4] Through these he has given us precious and very great promises.
Through them you are to share in the divine nature,
having escaped from the corruption
that is in the world through desire.
[5] For this very reason, make every effort
and add to your faith moral excellence,
to moral excellence knowledge,
[6] to knowledge self-control,
to self-control endurance,
to endurance godliness,
[7] to godliness love for brothers and sisters,
and to love for brothers and sisters love.
[8] If you have all these things
and they grow in you,
you will not be idle
or without fruit
in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[9] But whoever doesn’t have them
is blind
and sees only what is near.
He has forgotten
that he was cleansed from his old sins.
[10] So,
brothers,
be all the more eager
to make your calling and election sure.
For if you do these things,
you will never stumble.
[11] For in this way entrance
into the eternal Kingdom
of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ
will be richly given to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Share in the divine nature”: A great saying. Christians don’t become equal to God, but God gives them fellowship with himself. In the Orthodox Church this thought is especially important.
> Verses 5–7: A kind of ladder: From faith it goes step by step up to love. Love is at the very top.
> Verse 9: “Sees only what is near”: Whoever only thinks about today loses sight of the goal.

## Peter recalls what he experienced (Verses 12–21)

[12] That is why I will always remind you of these things,
though you know them
and are firmly established in the truth
that you have.
[13] I think it right
to keep you awake by reminding you,
as long as I am in this tent.
[14] For I know
that I will soon put off my tent,
as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.
[15] But I will make every effort
so that after my departure
you can always remember these things.
[16] For we did not follow cleverly invented fables
when we made known to you the power and coming
of our Lord Jesus Christ.
But we saw his majesty with our own eyes.
[17] For he received honor and glory
from God the Father
when the voice came to him from the Majestic Glory,
“This is my Son. I love him.
I am very pleased with him.”
[18] We heard this voice come from heaven
when we were with him on the holy mountain.
[19] And we have the word of prophecy made more sure.
You do well
to pay attention to it
as to a lamp
shining in a dark place,
until the day dawns
and the morning star rises in your hearts.
[20] For know this above all:
No prophecy of Scripture
comes from someone’s own interpretation.
[21] For no prophecy ever
came by the will of a human being.
But people spoke from God,
moved by the Holy Spirit.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–14: “Tent” is a picture for the body. Peter knows that he will die soon. According to an old tradition Peter was crucified in Rome.
> Verses 16–18 recall the transfiguration of Jesus on the mountain (Matthew 17:1–8). Peter, James and John saw Jesus in God’s glory.
> Verse 19: God’s word is like a lamp in the dark. “The morning star” is a picture for Christ.
> Verses 20–21: The prophets did not tell their own ideas. God’s Spirit moved them.
