# 1 Peter – Chapter 2
### Living stones

---

## You are living stones (Verses 1–10)

[1] So put away all wickedness,
all deceit,
all hypocrisy,
all envy
and all evil talk.
[2] Be like newborn babies
and long for the pure spiritual milk,
so that by it you may grow,
[3] if you have indeed tasted
that the Lord is kind.
[4] Come to him,
the living stone.
People rejected him,
but God chose him,
and he is precious.
[5] You also, as living stones,
let yourselves be built up into a spiritual house,
into a holy priesthood.
So you are to offer up spiritual sacrifices
that please God
through Jesus Christ.
[6] For it is contained in Scripture,
“Look,
I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone,
chosen and precious.
Whoever believes in him
will not be disappointed.”
[7] So for you
who believe
he is precious.
But for those
who are disobedient
it is true:
“The stone
which the builders rejected
has become the chief cornerstone,”
[8] and,
“a stone
that people stumble over,
and a rock
that makes them fall.”
They stumble at the word
because they are disobedient.
They were also appointed to this.
[9] But you are a chosen race,
a royal priesthood,
a holy nation,
a people
who belong to God.
You are to proclaim the great deeds of him
who called you out of darkness
into his marvelous light.
[10] In the past you were not a people,
but now you are God’s people.
In the past you had not received mercy,
but now you have received mercy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Just as a baby longs for milk, Christians are to long for God’s word.
> Verses 4–5: A beautiful picture: Every Christian is like a living stone. Together they form a house where God lives.
> Verses 5 and 9: “Priesthood”: All Christians may come near to God directly and pray for others. Protestant churches speak of the “priesthood of all believers.”
> Verse 6 quotes Isaiah 28:16. Verse 7 quotes Psalm 118:22. Verse 8 quotes Isaiah 8:14.
> Verse 9: God first said these words to Israel at Sinai (Exodus 19:5–6). They are true for Israel to this day.
> Important: The letter doesn’t say that the Christians replace Israel. It says: The people from the nations who “were not a people” in the past now also belong to God’s people (verse 10, compare Hosea 2:23 – in some Bibles 2:25). God has not canceled his covenant with Israel (Romans 11:29).

## Live good lives among people (Verses 11–17)

[11] Beloved,
I urge you as foreigners and strangers:
Keep away from the desires of the body,
which fight against the soul.
[12] Live a good life among the nations.
Then those
who now speak against you as evildoers
will see your good deeds
and glorify God on the day of his coming.
[13] For the Lord’s sake be subject
to every human order:
to the king as the highest,
[14] and to the governors
whom he sends
to punish those
who do evil
and to praise those
who do good.
[15] For this is God’s will:
By doing good
you are to put to silence the ignorance of foolish people.
[16] Live as free people.
But don’t use your freedom as a cover for evil,
but live as servants of God.
[17] Honor all people.
Love the brothers and sisters.
Fear God.
Honor the king.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: At that time Christians were slandered. The letter says: Answer with good deeds.
> Verses 13–14: Christians are to respect the order of the state. At that time the “king” was the Roman emperor.
> Important: That doesn’t mean that you have to go along with every injustice of the state. If the state commands injustice, you must obey God rather than people (Acts 5:29).
> Verse 17: A short, strong sentence: Honor all people. Every person has dignity.

## A word to the slaves (Verses 18–25)

[18] Servants,
be subject to your masters with all respect,
not only to the good and gentle ones,
but also to the wicked ones.
[19] For it is a sign of grace
when someone endures pain
and suffers unjustly
because he takes his conscience before God seriously.
[20] For what glory is it
if you are beaten
because you have sinned
and you endure it patiently?
But if you do good
and suffer for it and endure it patiently,
that is pleasing to God.
[21] For you were called to this.
For Christ also suffered for you
and left you an example,
so that you should follow in his steps.
[22] He did no sin,
“and no deceit was found in his mouth.”
[23] When he was insulted,
he didn’t insult back.
When he suffered,
he didn’t threaten,
but handed everything over to him
who judges justly.
[24] He himself carried our sins
in his body on the tree,
so that we might die to sins
and live for righteousness.
By his wounds you were healed.
[25] For you were like sheep
that had gone astray.
But now you have returned
to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

> **Important note**
> Verses 18–20 are addressed to slaves who were beaten by their masters. Later these verses were misused to justify slavery, violence and oppression. That was wrong and is a grave injustice.

> **What does this mean?**
> In verse 18 the WEB says “servants.” Slaves are meant. At that time they could hardly defend themselves or run away. The letter wants to give them dignity and comfort: God sees your unjust suffering. Christ also suffered innocently.
> Very important: These verses are not a call to endure violence or injustice when you can protect yourself. No one has to accept beatings, abuse or exploitation, neither at work nor in the family. Whoever experiences violence may and should seek help. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK). In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. US: 1-800-799-7233.
> Verses 22–25 take up Isaiah 53: “By his wounds we are healed.” The suffering servant of God is applied to Jesus here.
> Verse 25: Jesus is like a good shepherd who brings lost sheep back.
