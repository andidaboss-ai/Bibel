# 1 Peter – Chapter 3
### Ready to tell about our hope

---

## A word to wives and husbands (Verses 1–7)

[1] In the same way, you wives, be subject to your own husbands.
If some of them don’t obey the word,
they are to be won by the behavior of their wives
without a word,
[2] when they see your pure life in reverence.
[3] Your beauty is not to be outward,
with braided hair,
gold jewelry
or fine clothing.
[4] But your beauty is to be the hidden person of the heart,
with the beauty that doesn’t perish,
of a gentle and quiet spirit.
That is very precious in God’s sight.
[5] For this is how the holy women of the past also made themselves beautiful,
who put their hope in God.
They were subject to their own husbands.
[6] So Sarah obeyed Abraham
and called him “lord.”
You are now her children
if you do good
and are not put in fear by any terror.
[7] In the same way, you husbands, live with your wives with understanding.
Give honor to the woman as to the weaker vessel.
For they are heirs with you of the grace of life.
So your prayers will not be hindered.

> **Important note**
> Verses 1–7 reflect the family order of that time. At that time the husband legally had power over his wife. In history these verses were often used to keep women down. That was wrong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This is above all about women who had become Christians while their husbands still believed in other gods. That was dangerous for them. The letter advises: Live so well that your husband is won by your example.
> Verses 3–4: The letter doesn’t forbid jewelry. It says: A good heart is more important than outward beauty.
> Verse 6: The women are to be “not put in fear by any terror.” They are not to have to be afraid.
> Verse 7: New and important for that time: Husbands are to honor their wives. And women are “heirs with you of the grace of life,” that is, of equal worth before God. “Weaker vessel” at that time probably meant less physical strength and a weaker legal position, not a lower worth.
> Today many Christians understand marriage as a partnership of two people with equal rights. Others hold on to a special responsibility of the husband. All stress: It is about love, not about power.
> Very important: These verses give no one the right to rule over their spouse or to use violence. Violence in marriage is a grave injustice and a crime. No one has to endure it. If you experience domestic violence, get help. For domestic violence, US: 1-800-799-7233. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

## Bless instead of insulting (Verses 8–12)

[8] Finally:
Be all of one mind,
compassionate,
love one another as brothers and sisters,
be tenderhearted
and kind.
[9] Don’t pay back evil for evil
or insult for insult.
But bless,
for you were called to this,
so that you may inherit a blessing.
[10] For,
“Whoever wants to love life
and see good days,
let him keep his tongue from evil
and his lips
from speaking deceit.
[11] Let him turn away from evil
and do good.
Let him seek peace
and pursue it.
[12] For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous,
and his ears are open to their prayer.
But the face of the Lord is against those
who do evil.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Not hitting back, but blessing: Jesus taught this too (compare Luke 6:28).
> Verses 10–12 quote Psalm 34:12–16 (in some Bibles 34:13–17).
> Verse 11: “Let him seek peace and pursue it”: Peace doesn’t come by itself. You have to work for it actively.

## Ready to give an answer (Verses 13–17)

[13] Who can harm you
if you are eager to do good?
[14] But even if you have to suffer for righteousness’ sake,
you are blessed.
“Don’t fear what they fear,
and don’t be troubled.”
[15] But keep God,
the Lord,
holy in your hearts.
Always be ready
to give an answer to everyone
who asks you the reason for the hope
that is in you.
Do it with humility and reverence
[16] and with a good conscience.
Then those will be put to shame
who speak against you as evildoers
and run down your good life in Christ.
[17] For it is better,
if it is God’s will,
to suffer for doing good
than for doing evil.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14 takes up Isaiah 8:12.
> Verse 15 is a well-known verse: Christians are to be able to explain why they hope. But not pushy or arrogant, but “with humility and reverence,” that is, with respect for others.
> Verse 17: Suffering is not good. But if you have to suffer, then better because you have done good.

## Christ suffered and lives (Verses 18–22)

[18] For Christ also suffered for sins once,
the righteous for the unrighteous,
to bring you to God.
He was put to death in the flesh,
but made alive in the Spirit.
[19] In the Spirit he also went
and preached to the spirits in prison.
[20] They had been disobedient in the past,
when God waited patiently in the days of Noah,
while the ship was being built.
In it only a few,
that is, eight people,
were saved through water.
[21] This is a picture of baptism,
which now saves you.
It is not washing dirt off the body,
but the answer of a good conscience toward God,
through the resurrection of Jesus Christ.
[22] He has gone into heaven
and is at the right hand of God.
Angels,
authorities
and powers are subject to him.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: “The righteous for the unrighteous”: Jesus, who was without guilt, died for everyone.
> Verses 19–20 are very hard to understand. Many Christians see in them a hint that after his death Christ also went to the dead. That is why many churches confess in the Creed that Jesus went to the realm of the dead after his death. This shows: No place is so far away that Christ could not go there. Other interpreters understand the verses differently.
> Verses 20–21: Noah’s ark is a picture of baptism. Just as Noah was saved through the water, a new life begins in baptism.
> Verse 22: Christ is Lord over all powers. No one has to be afraid of them.
