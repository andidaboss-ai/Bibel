# 1 Peter – Chapter 1
### A living hope

---

## Before we begin: The first letter of Peter

The letter names itself a letter of Peter, the apostle. Peter was one of the first disciples of Jesus. At the end of the letter Silvanus is named, who helped with the writing (5:12). Many scholars think that the letter was written later in the spirit of Peter. Others consider Peter himself the author.
The letter is written to Christians in several regions of today’s Turkey. They were despised and treated with hostility because of their faith. The letter calls them “foreigners” in this world.
The big theme is hope in suffering: Christ suffered and rose again. That is why the Christians also have a living hope.
**Important note:** In chapters 2 and 3 there are rules for slaves and for wives. They reflect the order of that time. In the boxes we explain how Christians understand them today.
The first letter of Peter has 5 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Peter,
an apostle of Jesus Christ,
writes to the chosen ones
who live as foreigners in the Dispersion,
in Pontus,
Galatia,
Cappadocia,
Asia
and Bithynia.
[2] You are chosen according to the foreknowledge of God the Father,
through the making holy by the Spirit,
so that you obey Jesus Christ
and are sprinkled with his blood.
May grace and peace be given to you in abundance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The regions named were in the north and west of today’s Turkey.
> “Foreigners in the Dispersion”: The word “Dispersion” (in Greek *Diaspora*) first described Jews who lived outside the land of Israel. The letter uses it for Christians: They feel like strangers in their surroundings, because they live differently.
> Verse 2: God the Father, the Spirit and Jesus Christ appear in this verse. Later Christians speak of the Trinity of God.

## A living hope (Verses 3–12)

[3] Blessed be God,
the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.
In his great mercy
he has given us new birth
to a living hope,
through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,
[4] to an inheritance that can’t perish,
that is pure
and that doesn’t fade away.
It is kept ready in heaven for you.
[5] By the power of God you are guarded through faith
for a salvation
that is ready
to be revealed in the last time.
[6] You greatly rejoice in this,
even though now for a little while,
if it must be,
you are grieved by various trials.
[7] So your faith is to prove genuine.
It is more precious than gold,
which perishes,
even though it is tested by fire.
It is to lead to praise,
glory and honor
when Jesus Christ is revealed.
[8] You have not seen him,
and yet you love him.
You don’t see him now,
but you believe in him.
And you rejoice with a joy
that can’t be put into words
and that is full of glory.
[9] So you reach the goal of your faith:
the salvation of your souls.
[10] The prophets searched and inquired about this salvation.
They spoke of the grace
that was meant for you.
[11] They searched
to whom or to what time the Spirit of Christ in them pointed
when he testified beforehand about the sufferings of Christ
and the glories that would follow.
[12] It was revealed to them
that they were serving not themselves,
but you, with the things
that have now been announced to you.
Those who brought you the Good News announced them to you
by the Holy Spirit
sent from heaven.
Even angels long to look into these things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “A living hope”: Because Jesus lives, our hope is alive too. It is not just a wish, but has a firm foundation.
> Verses 6–7: Just as gold is purified in fire, faith can become stronger in hard times. Still, suffering is not something good. And “for a little while”: It comes to an end.
> Verse 8: The Christians never saw Jesus and love him anyway. That is true for all Christians to this day.
> Verses 10–12: The prophets of Israel announced God’s grace. Christians read their words in the light of Jesus.

## Live holy lives (Verses 13–25)

[13] So prepare your minds for action.
Be sober.
Set your hope fully on the grace
that will be brought to you
when Jesus Christ is revealed.
[14] Be obedient children.
Don’t shape your lives any more by your former desires
that you had
when you were still ignorant.
[15] But just as he
who called you
is holy,
so you also are to be holy in all your way of life.
[16] For it is written,
“You shall be holy,
for I am holy.”
[17] You call on him as Father,
who judges each one according to his work
without partiality.
So live in reverence
as long as you live here as foreigners.
[18] For you know:
You were redeemed from the empty way of life
handed down from your fathers.
This did not happen with things that perish,
like silver or gold,
[19] but with the precious blood of Christ,
as of a lamb
without defect and without spot.
[20] He was chosen before the creation of the world.
But at the end of the times he was revealed
for your sake.
[21] Through him you believe in God,
who raised him from the dead
and gave him glory.
So your faith and hope are in God.
[22] You have purified your souls
by obeying the truth through the Spirit,
for sincere love among brothers and sisters.
So love one another from the heart and with all your strength.
[23] For you have been born again,
not of seed that perishes,
but of seed that doesn’t perish,
through the word of God,
which lives and remains forever.
[24] For,
“All people are like grass,
and all their glory is like the flower in the grass.
The grass withers,
and the flower falls.
[25] But the Lord’s word remains forever.”
This is the word of Good News
that was announced to you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: “You shall be holy, for I am holy.” This is a quotation from Leviticus 11:44–45 and 19:2. This word was first spoken to Israel. The letter invites the Christians from the nations to live this way too.
> Verse 18: “The empty way of life handed down from your fathers”: Most of those who received the letter had worshiped other gods before. This former life is meant, not the Jewish faith.
> Verse 19: “As of a lamb without defect and without spot”: A picture from the Passover feast (Exodus 12) and from Isaiah 53.
> Verse 22: Love is to come “from the heart,” not just from duty.
> Verses 24–25 quote Isaiah 40:6–8. People and their fame pass away, but God’s word remains.
