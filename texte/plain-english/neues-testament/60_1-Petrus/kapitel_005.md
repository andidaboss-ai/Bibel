# 1 Peter – Chapter 5
### Give your worries to God

---

## A word to the leaders and to everyone (Verses 1–7)

[1] I urge the elders among you,
as a fellow elder
and a witness of the sufferings of Christ.
I also share in the glory
that will be revealed.
[2] Shepherd the flock of God
that is among you.
Take care of it,
not because you are forced,
but willingly,
not to gain money dishonestly,
but gladly from the heart.
[3] Don’t lord it over those
entrusted to you,
but be examples to the flock.
[4] Then when the chief Shepherd appears,
you will receive the crown of glory that doesn’t fade away.
[5] In the same way, you younger ones:
Be subject to the elder.
Yes,
all of you are to clothe yourselves with humility
and be subject to one another.
For,
“God resists the proud,
but gives grace to the humble.”
[6] So humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God,
so that he may lift you up at the right time.
[7] Cast all your worries on him,
because he cares for you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Peter calls himself a “fellow elder.” He doesn’t put himself above the other leaders, but next to them.
> Verses 2–3: A picture from the life of shepherds: Leaders are to take care of the church like a good shepherd takes care of his sheep, not because they are forced, not for money and not to rule.
> Very important: “Don’t lord it over”: Whoever has responsibility in the church may not misuse their power. If you experience spiritual or sexual abuse, you can find help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In the US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453, RAINN 1-800-656-4673.
> Verse 4: “The chief Shepherd” is Jesus.
> Verse 5: “All of you are to clothe yourselves with humility and be subject to one another”: This applies to everyone, toward each other. Then the verse quotes Proverbs 3:34.
> Verse 7: A great comfort: We may tell God all our worries and leave them with him. He cares for us. Whoever has heavy worries may also get help from people. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Be watchful (Verses 8–11)

[8] Be clear-minded and self-controlled.
Be watchful.
Your adversary,
the devil,
walks around like a roaring lion,
looking for
whom he may devour.
[9] Resist him,
firm in your faith.
And know:
Your brothers and sisters in the whole world
have to endure the same sufferings.
[10] But the God of all grace
has called you in Christ Jesus to his eternal glory.
After you have suffered a little while,
may he himself make you perfect,
establish,
strengthen
and settle you on firm ground.
[11] To him belong the glory and the power
forever and ever.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: A picture for the power of evil: like a dangerous lion. That is why Christians are to be watchful.
> Verse 9: You are not alone. Christians all over the world experience similar things.
> Verse 10: A strong promise: The suffering lasts only “a little while.” God will strengthen you and set you on firm ground.

## Last greetings (Verses 12–14)

[12] Through Silvanus,
whom I consider a faithful brother,
I have written to you briefly.
I wanted to encourage you and to testify
that this is the true grace of God
in which you stand.
[13] She who is in Babylon,
chosen together with you,
greets you,
and so does Mark,
my son.
[14] Greet one another with a kiss of love.
Peace be to all of you
who are in Christ Jesus.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Silvanus (also called Silas) helped with writing or delivering the letter.
> Verse 13: “She who is in Babylon”: This probably means the church. “Babylon” is probably a code name for Rome here, the capital of the Roman Empire.
> “Mark, my son”: not his own son, but a close fellow worker. According to an old tradition Mark wrote down Peter’s memories in the Gospel of Mark.
> Verse 14: The “kiss of love” was a sign of greeting among Christians.

## Looking back: What have we read in the first letter of Peter?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A living hope:** Through the resurrection of Jesus, Christians have a hope that lasts (Chapter 1).
> **Living holy lives:** “You shall be holy, for I am holy.” This word was first spoken to Israel (Chapter 1).
> **Living stones:** All Christians together build the house of God. The Christians from the nations are added to God’s people; they don’t replace Israel (Chapter 2).
> **Living good lives among people:** Answering slander with good deeds. Honor all people (Chapter 2).
> **Difficult rules:** The rules for slaves and wives reflect the time back then. No one has to endure violence (Chapters 2 and 3).
> **Ready to give an answer:** Christians are to tell about their hope with respect (Chapter 3).
> **Serving one another:** With love and with the gifts that God has given (Chapter 4).
> **Suffering as a Christian:** Whoever suffers because of their faith is not alone (Chapters 4 and 5).
> **For today:** Cast all your worries on God, because he cares for you.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the second letter of Peter. It warns against false teachers and speaks about the coming again of Jesus.
