# Romans – Chapter 16
### Greetings to many friends

---

## Phoebe (Verses 1–2)

[1] I commend to you Phoebe,
our sister.
She is a servant of the assembly
that is at Cenchreae.
[2] Receive her in the Lord
in a way worthy of the saints.
Help her in whatever matter
she may need from you.
For she herself has been a helper of many,
and of me too.

*commend* = recommend, speak well of someone

> **What does this mean?**
> Cenchreae was the port of the city of Corinth. Paul probably wrote this letter from there.
> Phoebe probably carried the letter to Rome.
> Verse 1: The WEB says “servant.” The Greek word is *diakonos*. It can simply mean *servant*, or it can name an office: *deacon*. Many Bibles and scholars today translate *deacon*.
> Verse 2: The word for “helper” can also mean *protector* or *patron*. Phoebe was probably a respected woman who supported Paul and others.

## Greetings to the church in Rome (Verses 3–16)

[3] Greet Prisca and Aquila,
my fellow workers in Christ Jesus.
[4] They risked their own necks for my life.
Not only I give thanks to them,
but also all the assemblies of the Gentiles.
[5] Greet also the assembly
that meets in their house.
Greet Epaenetus,
my beloved friend.
He is the first fruits of Achaia to Christ.
[6] Greet Mary,
who worked hard for us.
[7] Greet Andronicus and Junia,
my relatives
and my fellow prisoners.
They are notable among the apostles.
They were also in Christ before me.
[8] Greet Amplias,
my beloved in the Lord.
[9] Greet Urbanus,
our fellow worker in Christ,
and Stachys,
my beloved.
[10] Greet Apelles,
who is approved in Christ.
Greet those of the household of Aristobulus.
[11] Greet Herodion,
my relative.
Greet those of the household of Narcissus
who are in the Lord.
[12] Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa,
who work hard in the Lord.
Greet Persis, the beloved,
who worked very hard in the Lord.
[13] Greet Rufus,
the chosen in the Lord,
and his mother,
who has been a mother to me too.
[14] Greet Asyncritus,
Phlegon,
Hermes,
Patrobas,
Hermas,
and the brothers
who are with them.
[15] Greet Philologus and Julia,
Nereus and his sister,
and Olympas,
and all the saints
who are with them.
[16] Greet one another with a holy kiss.
The assemblies of Christ greet you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: We know Prisca (also called Priscilla) and her husband Aquila from Acts (chapter 18). Prisca is often named first. She was probably especially important in the church.
> Verse 5: At that time there were no church buildings. Christians met in people’s homes.
> Verse 5: The WEB says “Achaia” here, a region in Greece. Many old manuscripts have *Asia* instead, a province in today’s Turkey. This edition follows the WEB.
> Verse 7: Junia is a woman’s name. Paul says that she and Andronicus are “notable among the apostles.” Many scholars understand this to mean: Junia was an apostle herself. Others understand: The apostles thought highly of them. In some later Bibles Junia was turned into a man (*Junias*). Today most experts agree that Junia was a woman.
> Verse 13: Rufus is perhaps the son of Simon of Cyrene, who carried the cross of Jesus (Mark 15:21).
> Paul greets many women and men, slaves and free people, Jews and non-Jews. Many of the names were typical names of slaves or freed slaves. In the church they all belonged together.
> Verse 16: The “holy kiss” was a sign of fellowship. Today many Christians give each other a sign of peace in worship, for example a handshake.

## A last warning (Verses 17–20)

[17] Now I beg you, brothers:
Look out for those
who cause divisions
and make others stumble,
against the teaching
which you learned.
Turn away from them.
[18] For such people don’t serve our Lord Jesus Christ,
but their own belly.
With smooth and flattering speech
they deceive the hearts of the innocent.
[19] For your obedience has become known to all.
So I rejoice over you.
But I want you
to be wise
in what is good,
and innocent
in what is evil.
[20] And the God of peace
will quickly crush Satan under your feet.
The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: Paul warns against people who sow conflict and only look for their own advantage. Whoever uses beautiful words to exploit others is not serving Christ.
> For today: Today too there are groups that use beautiful words to make people dependent. If you feel pressured in a group, you can find help at counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse. In a crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Verse 20 recalls Genesis 3:15: In the end evil will be defeated. God is a God of peace.

## Greetings from his co-workers (Verses 21–24)

[21] Timothy,
my fellow worker,
greets you,
and so do Lucius,
Jason
and Sosipater,
my relatives.
[22] I, Tertius,
who write the letter,
greet you in the Lord.
[23] Gaius greets you.
He is my host
and the host of the whole assembly.
Erastus,
the treasurer of the city,
greets you,
and so does Quartus, the brother.
[24] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all!
Amen.

*treasurer* = the official who takes care of a city’s money

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: Paul didn’t write the letter himself, but dictated it. Tertius was the writer. Here he sends his own greeting.
> Verse 23: Erastus was an important official in Corinth. An old inscription with the name Erastus has been found in Corinth. Whether it is the same man is not certain.
> Verse 24 is missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has it.
> About the numbering: Many Bibles have verses 25–27 here, a praise of God. The WEB placed these verses at the end of chapter 14 (Romans 14:24–26). You will find them there in this edition.

## Looking back: What have we read in the letter to the Romans?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A letter to Rome:** Paul writes to a church that he doesn’t know yet. He introduces himself and his message.
> **Everyone needs God’s grace:** Jews and people from the nations, all have sinned. No one can make themselves righteous (chapters 1 to 3).
> **Made right through faith:** God makes people righteous, by grace, through faith in Jesus Christ. Abraham is the great example of faith (chapters 3 to 5).
> **A new life:** Through baptism Christians belong to Christ. Sin is no longer to rule. The Holy Spirit makes people children of God. Nothing can separate us from God’s love (chapters 6 to 8).
> **God remains faithful to Israel:** God has not rejected his people. His gifts and his calling are irrevocable. Christians are like branches grafted into the olive tree of Israel. They must not consider themselves better than Jews (chapters 9 to 11).
> **Living from love:** Christians are to accept one another, be considerate, overcome evil with good and live at peace with all people (chapters 12 to 15).
> **A colorful church:** At the end Paul greets many women and men, among them Phoebe, Prisca and Junia (chapter 16).
> **In history:** The letter to the Romans has strongly shaped the church, for example Augustine, Martin Luther and Karl Barth. In 1999 Catholic and Lutheran Christians declared together: At heart we understand justification by grace in the same way.
> **For today:** God’s love is for all people. We don’t have to earn it.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Paul’s first letter to the church in Corinth. There were many problems there: conflict, divisions and questions about worship. This letter also contains the famous chapter about love (chapter 13).
