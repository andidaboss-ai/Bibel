# Romans – Chapter 15
### Accept one another

---

## Carrying one another (Verses 1–13)

[1] Now we
who are strong
ought to bear the weaknesses of the weak,
and not to please ourselves.
[2] Let each one of us please his neighbor
for what is good,
to build him up.
[3] For even Christ didn’t please himself.
But it is
as it is written:
“The reproaches of those
who reproached you
fell on me.”
[4] For whatever things
were written before
were written
for our learning.
Through perseverance
and through the encouragement of the Scriptures
we are to have hope.
[5] Now may the God of perseverance and of encouragement
grant you
to be of the same mind with one another
according to Christ Jesus,
[6] so that together
and with one voice
you may glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[7] So accept one another,
even as Christ also accepted you,
to the glory of God.
[8] For I say:
Christ has become a servant of the circumcised
for the sake of the truth of God.
He was to confirm the promises
given to the fathers.
[9] And the Gentiles were to glorify God
for his mercy.
As it is written,
“Therefore I will give praise to you among the Gentiles
and sing to your name.”
[10] Again he says,
“Rejoice, you Gentiles,
with his people.”
[11] Again,
“Praise the Lord, all you Gentiles!
Let all the peoples praise him.”
[12] Again, Isaiah says,
“There will be the root of Jesse,
he who arises
to rule over the Gentiles;
in him the Gentiles will hope.”
[13] Now may the God of hope
fill you with all joy and peace
in believing,
so that you may overflow with hope
in the power of the Holy Spirit.

*reproaches* = insults, blame

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Whoever is strong should be considerate of the weaker. This continues chapter 14.
> Verse 3 quotes Psalm 69:9 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 69:10).
> Verse 4: The Scriptures of Israel, the Old Testament, are written for Christians too. They give comfort and hope.
> Verse 7: “Accept one another, even as Christ also accepted you.” A guiding word for every church.
> Verse 8: Jesus was a Jew and came first to his people. In this way God confirmed his promises to Israel.
> Verses 9–12: With four quotations Paul shows: The Bible of Israel already announces that the other nations will also praise God, together with Israel. Verse 9 quotes Psalm 18:49 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 18:50). Verse 10 quotes Deuteronomy 32:43. Verse 11 quotes Psalm 117:1. Verse 12 quotes Isaiah 11:10. Jesse is the father of King David.
> Verse 13 is a beautiful blessing: May God, who gives hope, fill us with joy and peace.

## Paul and his task (Verses 14–21)

[14] I myself am convinced about you,
my brothers:
You yourselves are full of goodness,
filled with all knowledge,
and able
to warn one another.
[15] But in some places I have written to you
quite boldly,
to remind you.
I do this because of the grace
that God has given me:
[16] I am to be a servant of Christ Jesus
to the Gentiles.
Like a priest I serve the Good News of God,
so that the Gentiles may become an offering acceptable to God,
made holy by the Holy Spirit.
[17] So I can boast in Christ Jesus
in things that concern God.
[18] For I will not dare
to speak of anything
except what Christ has worked through me
to bring the Gentiles to obedience,
by word and deed,
[19] in the power of signs and wonders,
in the power of God’s Spirit.
So from Jerusalem
and around as far as Illyricum
I have fully preached the Good News of Christ.
[20] In this my aim has been
to preach the Good News
where Christ was not yet named.
For I didn’t want to build on a foundation
that someone else had laid.
[21] But it is
as it is written:
“They will see,
to whom no news of him came.
They who haven’t heard
will understand.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 14–15: Paul praises the Christians in Rome. He doesn’t want to lecture them, but to remind them.
> Verse 16: Paul describes his service with pictures from the temple. The people from the nations who come to faith are like a gift to God.
> Verse 19: Illyricum is a region on the coast of the Adriatic Sea, in today’s Croatia, Bosnia and Albania. So Paul had already traveled far.
> Verse 21 quotes Isaiah 52:15.

## Travel plans: Jerusalem, Rome, Spain (Verses 22–33)

[22] That is also why I was often prevented
from coming to you.
[23] But now I no longer have any place in these regions.
And for many years I have longed
to come to you.
[24] When I travel to Spain,
I will come to you.
For I hope
to see you on my journey
and to be helped by you on my way there,
after I have first enjoyed your company for a while.
[25] But now I am going to Jerusalem,
to serve the saints.
[26] For the churches in Macedonia and Achaia
have been glad to make a collection
for the poor among the saints in Jerusalem.
[27] Yes, they were glad to do it,
and they also owe it to them.
For the Gentiles have received a share
in their spiritual things.
So they also owe it to them
to serve them with material things.
[28] When I have done this
and have safely handed this fruit over to them,
I will go on by way of you to Spain.
[29] I know
that when I come to you,
I will come in the fullness of the blessing
of the Good News of Christ.
[30] Now I beg you, brothers,
by our Lord Jesus Christ
and by the love of the Spirit:
Strive together with me
in your prayers to God for me,
[31] that I may be rescued from those
who are disobedient in Judea,
and that my service for Jerusalem
may be welcome to the saints,
[32] so that I may come to you with joy,
if God wills,
and find rest together with you.
[33] Now the God of peace be with you all.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 24 and 28: Paul wanted to travel as far as Spain, at that time the western end of the known world. We don’t know for sure whether he ever got there.
> Verses 25–27: The collection for the poor Christians in Jerusalem was very important to Paul. The Christians from the nations owe their faith to the church in Jerusalem and to Israel. That is why they are glad to help.
> Verse 31: By “those who are disobedient in Judea,” Paul means certain opponents who were hostile to him. It doesn’t mean the Jews in general. What happened later in Jerusalem is told in Acts (chapters 21 to 26). Paul was arrested there and finally came to Rome as a prisoner (Acts 28).
> For today: Christians help one another across borders. And they pray for one another.
