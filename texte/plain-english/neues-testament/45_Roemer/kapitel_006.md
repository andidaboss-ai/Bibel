# Romans – Chapter 6
### A new life with Christ

---

## Died and alive with Christ (Verses 1–14)

[1] What shall we say then?
Shall we continue in sin,
so that grace may increase?
[2] May it never be!
We died to sin.
How could we live in it any longer?
[3] Or don’t you know:
All of us
who were baptized into Christ Jesus
were baptized into his death.
[4] So we were buried with him
through baptism into death.
And just as Christ was raised from the dead
through the glory of the Father,
so we also might walk in newness of life.
[5] For if we have become united with him
in the likeness of his death,
we will also be part of his resurrection.
[6] We know this:
Our old self was crucified with him,
so that the body of sin might be done away with,
and we would no longer be slaves to sin.
[7] For whoever has died
has been freed from sin.
[8] But if we died with Christ,
we believe
that we will also live with him.
[9] For we know:
Christ has been raised from the dead
and dies no more.
Death no longer has dominion over him!
[10] For the death that he died,
he died to sin one time, once for all.
But the life that he lives,
he lives to God.
[11] So consider yourselves also:
You are dead to sin,
but alive to God
in Christ Jesus our Lord.
[12] So don’t let sin reign
in your mortal body,
so that you obey its lusts.
[13] Don’t offer the parts of your body to sin
as tools of unrighteousness.
But offer yourselves to God
as people
who are alive from the dead,
and the parts of your body to God as tools of righteousness.
[14] For sin will not have dominion over you.
For you are not under law,
but under grace.

*dominion* = power, rule
*mortal* = able to die

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: A possible objection: If God’s grace is so great, can we simply go on sinning? Paul says: May it never be!
> Verses 3–4: Baptism is like dying and rising again with Christ. In the early days people were completely immersed: under the water (as if buried) and out again (as if risen). After that a new life begins.
> Verse 6: “Our old self” means the old life that was ruled by sin.
> Verse 9: “Death no longer has dominion over him”: That is the Easter message.
> Verse 13: “The parts of your body” means the whole body: hands, feet, mouth. With everything we are, we can do good.
> Verse 14: “Not under law, but under grace”: Christians don’t live out of fear of punishment, but out of God’s gift.

---

## Free for God (Verses 15–23)

[15] What then?
Shall we sin
because we are not under law
but under grace?
May it never be!
[16] Don’t you know:
When you offer yourselves as servants to someone
and obey them,
you are the servants of the one you obey.
Either of sin,
which leads to death,
or of obedience,
which leads to righteousness.
[17] But thanks be to God!
You were slaves of sin.
But you became obedient from the heart
to the form of teaching
to which you were handed over.
[18] You were set free from sin
and became slaves of righteousness.
[19] I speak in human terms
because of the weakness of your flesh:
In the past you offered the parts of your body as servants
to uncleanness and to wickedness,
so that there was more and more wickedness.
So now offer the parts of your body as servants
to righteousness,
so that you become holy.
[20] For when you were servants of sin,
you were free from righteousness.
[21] What fruit did you have at that time?
Today you are ashamed of these things.
For their end is death.
[22] But now you have been set free from sin
and have become servants of God.
Your fruit is
that you become holy.
And the result is eternal life.
[23] For the wages of sin is death,
but the free gift of God is eternal life
in Christ Jesus our Lord.

*wages* = pay for work

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–18: Paul uses a picture from his time: Slaves belonged to their master. He says: Every person serves something. The only question is: what? Whoever serves God becomes free.
> Important: With the picture of slavery, Paul is not describing a good order. Slavery is a grave injustice. The picture only shows how strong the power of sin is.
> Verse 19: “I speak in human terms”: Paul himself says that he uses a human picture so that people understand him.
> Verse 23 is one of the best-known verses in the Bible: Sin pays with death. God gives life. Life is not a wage, but a gift.
