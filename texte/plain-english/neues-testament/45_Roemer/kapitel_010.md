# Romans – Chapter 10
### The word is near you

---

## Paul prays for Israel (Verses 1–4)

[1] Brothers,
with all my heart I wish that the people of Israel may be saved.
That is my prayer to God for them.
[2] I can tell you this about them:
They are eager for God.
But they do not really understand.
[3] They do not know God’s righteousness.
Instead, they try to set up their own righteousness.
So they did not put themselves under God’s righteousness.
Righteousness means: being right with God.
[4] Christ is the fulfillment of the law.
Through him, everyone who believes is made right with God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul prays for his people. He loves Israel.
> Verse 2: Paul praises how eager his people are for God. He knows this eagerness from his own experience.
> Verse 4: The WEB, our English source text, says “fulfillment.” The Greek word *telos* can mean *goal*, *fulfillment* or *end*. Many scholars today say: Paul means that the law finds its goal in Christ. It does not mean that the law is worthless (compare 3:31 and 7:12).

---

## The word of faith (Verses 5–13)

[5] Moses writes about the righteousness that comes from the law.
He says,
“The one who does these things will live by them.”
[6] But the righteousness that comes from faith says this:
“Do not say in your heart,
‘Who will go up into heaven?’ ”
That means: to bring Christ down.
[7] “Or do not say,
‘Who will go down into the abyss?’ ”
That means: to bring Christ up from the dead.
The abyss is a very deep place. Here it means the place of the dead.
[8] So what does it say?
“The word is near you.
It is in your mouth and in your heart.”
This is the word about faith.
We preach this word:
[9] Say with your mouth that Jesus is Lord.
Believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead.
If you do that, you will be saved.
[10] With the heart a person believes.
This makes them right with God.
With the mouth a person says openly what they believe.
This leads to salvation.
Salvation means: being rescued by God.
[11] The Scripture says,
“Whoever believes in him will not be disappointed.”
[12] There is no difference between Jew and Greek.
The same Lord is Lord of all.
He gives richly to all who call on him.
[13] For,
“Whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 quotes Leviticus 18:5.
> Verses 6–8 take up words from Deuteronomy 30:12–14. There Moses says: God’s command is not far away, but very near, in your mouth and in your heart. Paul applies this to the word about Jesus.
> Verse 9: This is one of the oldest Christian confessions of faith: Jesus is Lord.
> Verse 11 quotes Isaiah 28:16. Verse 13 quotes Joel 2:32 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 3:5).
> Verse 12: God is the Lord of all people. With him there is no difference.

---

## How will they believe if they don’t hear? (Verses 14–21)

[14] But how can people call on someone they have not believed in?
How can they believe in someone they have not heard about?
How can they hear without someone who preaches?
[15] And how can people preach unless someone sends them?
It is written:
“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring the Good News of peace!
They bring the happy news of good things.”
[16] But not all of them listened to the happy news.
Isaiah says,
“Lord, who has believed what we told them?”
[17] So faith comes from hearing.
And hearing comes through the word of God.
[18] But I ask:
Did they not hear?
Yes, they certainly did.
“Their sound went out into all the earth.
Their words went to the ends of the world.”
[19] But I ask:
Did Israel not understand?
First, Moses says,
“I will make you jealous of a people that is not a nation.
I will make you angry with a nation that has no understanding.”
[20] Isaiah is very bold.
He says,
“People who did not look for me found me.
I showed myself to people who did not ask for me.”
[21] But about Israel he says,
“All day long I stretched out my hands
to a people who do not obey and who argue against me.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 quotes Isaiah 52:7. Verse 16 quotes Isaiah 53:1.
> Verse 17: “So faith comes from hearing”: That is why it is important that people tell others about God.
> Verse 18 quotes Psalm 19:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 19:5). Verse 19 quotes Deuteronomy 32:21. Verses 20–21 quote Isaiah 65:1–2.
> Verse 21: God stretches out his hands to Israel “all day long.” This is not a picture of rejection, but of love: God does not give up on his people. He waits with open arms.
> Important: These hard words of the prophets come from the Bible of Israel itself. The prophets spoke like this to their own people. They give Christians no right to look down on Jews. In the next chapter Paul says clearly: God has not rejected his people (11:1–2).
