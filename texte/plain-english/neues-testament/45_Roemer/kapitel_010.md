# Romans – Chapter 10
### The word is near you

---

## Paul prays for Israel (Verses 1–4)

[1] Brothers,
my heart’s desire and my prayer to God for Israel
is that they may be saved.
[2] For I testify about them:
They have a zeal for God,
but not according to knowledge.
[3] For they don’t know God’s righteousness
and try
to set up their own righteousness.
So they didn’t submit to the righteousness of God.
[4] For Christ is the fulfillment of the law
for righteousness to everyone
who believes.

*zeal* = eager devotion

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul prays for his people. He loves Israel.
> Verse 2: Paul praises his people’s zeal for God. He knows this zeal from his own experience.
> Verse 4: The WEB says “fulfillment.” The Greek word *telos* can mean *goal*, *fulfillment* or *end*. Many scholars today say: Paul means that the law finds its goal in Christ. It does not mean that the law is worthless (compare 3:31 and 7:12).

---

## The word of faith (Verses 5–13)

[5] For Moses writes about the righteousness of the law,
“The one who does them
will live by them.”
[6] But the righteousness which is of faith says this,
“Don’t say in your heart,
‘Who will ascend into heaven?’ ”
that is,
to bring Christ down;
[7] “or,
‘Who will descend into the abyss?’ ”
that is,
to bring Christ up from the dead.
[8] But what does it say?
“The word is near you,
in your mouth and in your heart.”
That is the word of faith
which we preach:
[9] If you confess with your mouth
that Jesus is Lord,
and believe in your heart
that God raised him from the dead,
you will be saved.
[10] For with the heart one believes,
and this leads to righteousness.
And with the mouth one confesses,
and this leads to salvation.
[11] For the Scripture says,
“Whoever believes in him
will not be disappointed.”
[12] For there is no distinction
between Jew and Greek.
For the same Lord is Lord of all.
He is rich to all
who call on him.
[13] For,
“Whoever will call on the name of the Lord
will be saved.”

*abyss* = a very deep place; here: the place of the dead

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5 quotes Leviticus 18:5.
> Verses 6–8 take up words from Deuteronomy 30:12–14. There Moses says: God’s command is not far away, but very near, in your mouth and in your heart. Paul applies this to the word about Jesus.
> Verse 9: This is one of the oldest Christian confessions of faith: Jesus is Lord.
> Verse 11 quotes Isaiah 28:16. Verse 13 quotes Joel 2:32 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 3:5).
> Verse 12: God is the Lord of all people. With him there is no distinction.

---

## How will they believe if they don’t hear? (Verses 14–21)

[14] But how will they call on him
in whom they have not believed?
How will they believe in him
whom they have not heard?
How will they hear
without someone
who preaches?
[15] And how will they preach
unless they are sent?
As it is written:
“How beautiful are the feet of those
who preach the Good News of peace,
who bring glad tidings of good things!”
[16] But they didn’t all listen to the glad news.
For Isaiah says,
“Lord,
who has believed our report?”
[17] So faith comes by hearing,
and hearing by the word of God.
[18] But I say,
didn’t they hear?
Yes, most certainly,
“Their sound went out into all the earth,
their words to the ends of the world.”
[19] But I ask,
didn’t Israel know?
First Moses says,
“I will provoke you to jealousy
with that which is no nation.
I will make you angry
with a nation void of understanding.”
[20] Isaiah is very bold and says,
“I was found by those
who didn’t seek me.
I was revealed to those
who didn’t ask for me.”
[21] But about Israel he says,
“All day long I stretched out my hands
to a disobedient and contrary people.”

*glad tidings* = good news

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15 quotes Isaiah 52:7. Verse 16 quotes Isaiah 53:1.
> Verse 17: “So faith comes by hearing”: That is why it is important that people tell others about God.
> Verse 18 quotes Psalm 19:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 19:5). Verse 19 quotes Deuteronomy 32:21. Verses 20–21 quote Isaiah 65:1–2.
> Verse 21: God stretches out his hands to Israel “all day long.” This is not a picture of rejection, but of love: God does not give up on his people. He waits with open arms.
> Important: These hard words of the prophets come from the Bible of Israel itself. The prophets spoke like this to their own people. They give Christians no right to look down on Jews. In the next chapter Paul says clearly: God has not rejected his people (11:1–2).
