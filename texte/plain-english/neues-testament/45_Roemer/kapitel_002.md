# Romans – Chapter 2
### God judges all people fairly

---

## Whoever judges others (Verses 1–11)

[1] So you are without excuse, O man,
whoever you are
who judge.
For in the things you judge another,
you condemn yourself.
For you
who judge
do the same things.
[2] We know
that the judgment of God
against those who do such things
is according to truth.
[3] O man,
you judge those
who do such things,
and you do the same yourself.
Do you think
that you will escape the judgment of God?
[4] Or do you despise the riches of his goodness,
his patience
and his forbearance?
Don’t you know
that the goodness of God leads you to repentance?
[5] But you have a hard heart
that doesn’t want to repent.
So you are storing up wrath for yourself
for the day of wrath,
when the righteous judgment of God will be revealed.
[6] He “will pay back to everyone according to their works”:
[7] To those
who patiently keep doing good
and seek glory, honor and incorruptibility,
he gives eternal life.
[8] But to those
who are self-seeking
and don’t obey the truth,
but obey unrighteousness,
there will be wrath and anger,
[9] oppression and anguish
on every human being
who does evil,
to the Jew first,
and also to the Greek.
[10] But glory, honor and peace go to everyone
who does good,
to the Jew first,
and also to the Greek.
[11] For there is no partiality with God.

*forbearance* = holding back punishment, patience
*partiality* = treating some people better than others

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul turns around. He has just described the sins of the world. Now he says: Whoever points a finger at others now is meant himself. No one is better than the others.
> Verse 4: A beautiful sentence: The goodness of God leads to repentance. God doesn’t threaten first; he invites.
> Verse 6 quotes Psalm 62:12 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 62:13) and Proverbs 24:12.
> Verses 9–10: “To the Jew first, and also to the Greek”: Israel has a special place with God. But God measures everyone with the same measure.
> Verse 11: “No partiality”: God does not favor anyone because of their background, wealth or reputation.

## The law in the heart (Verses 12–16)

[12] All
who have sinned without the law
will also perish without the law.
And all
who have sinned under the law
will be judged by the law.
[13] For it isn’t those
who hear the law
who are righteous before God.
But those
who do the law
will be declared righteous.
[14] For when Gentiles
who don’t have the law
do by nature
the things the law requires,
they are a law to themselves,
even though they don’t have the law.
[15] In this way they show:
The work of the law
is written in their hearts.
Their conscience testifies to it,
and their thoughts accuse them
or else excuse them.
[16] This will be seen on the day
when God will judge the secrets of people
through Jesus Christ,
as my Good News says.

*conscience* = the inner voice that tells us what is right and wrong

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “The law” is the Torah, which God gave to Israel. The other nations didn’t know it.
> Verses 14–15: People who don’t know the Bible also have a conscience. They often know what is good and evil. God has written it in their hearts.
> For today: The conscience is a gift from God. Many people of other religions or of no religion do good.

## What makes someone a Jew (Verses 17–29)

[17] But you call yourself a Jew.
You rely on the law
and boast in God.
[18] You know his will
and can tell
what is excellent,
because you have been taught by the law.
[19] You are sure
that you are a guide of the blind,
a light to those
who are in darkness,
[20] a corrector of the foolish,
a teacher of babies.
For in the law you have the form of knowledge and of the truth.
[21] So you teach others.
Don’t you teach yourself?
You preach
that people shouldn’t steal.
Do you steal?
[22] You say
that people shouldn’t commit adultery.
Do you commit adultery?
You hate idols.
Do you rob temples?
[23] You boast in the law.
Do you dishonor God
by disobeying the law?
[24] For “the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you,”
just as it is written.
[25] Circumcision is indeed useful
if you do the law.
But if you break the law,
your circumcision has become uncircumcision.
[26] So if the uncircumcised
keep the commands of the law,
won’t their uncircumcision
be counted as circumcision?
[27] And won’t those
who are physically uncircumcised
but fulfill the law
judge you?
For you have the letter of the law and circumcision,
and yet you break the law.
[28] For he is not a Jew
who is one only outwardly.
And circumcision is not only what is outward in the flesh.
[29] But he is a Jew
who is one inwardly.
And circumcision is that of the heart,
in the spirit,
not in the letter.
His praise does not come from people,
but from God.

*circumcision* = cutting away the foreskin; the sign of God’s covenant with Israel

> **What does this mean?**
> Important: Paul is a Jew himself. Here he writes like a prophet who warns his own people. He speaks to an imagined partner in a conversation, as teachers did at that time. This is not about the Jews as a whole.
> Verses 17–20: Paul lists the real gifts of Israel: the law, the knowledge of God’s will, the task of being a light for the nations. These are great gifts from God.
> Verses 21–23: Paul warns against hypocrisy: teaching others but acting differently yourself. This warning is for all people, and especially for Christians.
> Verse 24 quotes Isaiah 52:5.
> Verse 29: “Circumcision is that of the heart”: This is a picture from the Bible of Israel itself (Deuteronomy 10:16; Jeremiah 4:4). God wants the heart, not only outward signs.
> Important: These verses were later misused to claim that Jews were no longer *real* Jews. Paul did not mean that. In chapter 3 he says right away that the advantage of the Jews is great (3:1–2). And in chapter 11: God has not rejected his people.
