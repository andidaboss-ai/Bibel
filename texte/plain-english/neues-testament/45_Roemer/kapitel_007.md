# Romans – Chapter 7
### The inner struggle

---

## Free from the law for Christ (Verses 1–6)

[1] Or don’t you know, brothers,
for I speak to people
who know the law,
that the law has power over a person
for as long as he lives?
[2] A married woman is bound by law
to her husband
while he lives.
But if the husband dies,
she is released from the law
that binds her to the husband.
[3] So if,
while her husband lives,
she is joined to another man,
she would be called an adulteress.
But if the husband dies,
she is free from the law.
Then she is no adulteress,
though she is joined to another man.
[4] So you also, my brothers,
were made dead to the law
through the body of Christ.
So you can be joined to another,
to him
who was raised from the dead,
so that we might bear fruit for God.
[5] For when we were in the flesh,
the sinful passions,
which were stirred up through the law,
worked in the parts of our body.
They brought fruit for death.
[6] But now we have been released from the law.
We have died to that
in which we were held.
So we serve in the new way of the spirit,
and not in the old way of the letter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Adulteress means: a married woman who is unfaithful to her husband.
> Verses 1–3: Paul uses a picture from the marriage law of his time: When one spouse dies, the other is free. Here he is not giving a teaching about marriage and divorce, but a picture.
> Verse 4: In the same way, the Christians have become free through the death of Christ, to belong to Christ.
> Verse 5: “In the flesh” means: in the old life, ruled by human weakness.
> Verse 6: “The new way of the spirit”: Christians live from the Holy Spirit, not only by written rules.

## Is the law bad? (Verses 7–13)

[7] What shall we say then?
Is the law sin?
Of course not!
But I wouldn’t have known sin
except through the law.
For I wouldn’t have known coveting
unless the law had said,
“You shall not covet.”
[8] But sin used the commandment as an opportunity
and produced in me all kinds of coveting.
For apart from the law, sin is dead.
[9] I was once alive apart from the law.
But when the commandment came,
sin came to life,
and I died.
[10] The commandment
that was meant for life
brought me death.
[11] For sin used the commandment as an opportunity,
deceived me,
and through it killed me.
[12] So the law indeed is holy,
and the commandment holy, righteous, and good.
[13] Did then that which is good become death to me?
Of course not!
But sin produced death in me
through that which is good,
so that it might be shown to be sin.
So through the commandment sin was to become
extremely sinful.

> **What does this mean?**
> Covet means: want something that belongs to someone else.
> Verse 7 quotes the tenth commandment (Exodus 20:17; Deuteronomy 5:21).
> Verses 7–11: When people hear that something is forbidden, they sometimes want it even more. In this way sin even misuses the good commandment.
> Verse 12 is very important: “The law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy, righteous, and good.” Paul doesn’t speak badly about the Torah. The problem is not the law, but the power of sin.
> Important: Christians have often claimed that the Jewish law is a burden or bad. That contradicts Paul. For Jews, the Torah is still a gift from God today.

## The good I want, I don’t do (Verses 14–25)

[14] For we know:
The law is spiritual.
But I am fleshly,
sold under sin.
[15] For I don’t understand
what I am doing.
For I don’t do
what I want to do.
But what I hate,
that I do.
[16] But if I do
what I don’t want,
I agree with the law
that it is good.
[17] So now it is no more I that do it,
but sin
which lives in me.
[18] For I know:
In me,
that is, in my flesh,
lives no good thing.
The wanting is there,
but I don’t find
how to do what is good.
[19] For the good
which I want,
I don’t do.
But the evil
which I don’t want,
that I do.
[20] But if I do
what I don’t want,
it is no more I that do it,
but sin
which lives in me.
[21] So I find this law in me:
When I want to do good,
evil is there.
[22] For in my inner self
I delight in God’s law.
[23] But I see a different law in the parts of my body.
It wars against the law of my mind
and makes me a prisoner
under the law of sin
which is in the parts of my body.
[24] What a wretched man I am!
Who will rescue me
out of the body of this death?
[25] I thank God
through Jesus Christ, our Lord!
So then with the mind I myself serve God’s law,
but with the flesh, sin’s law.

> **What does this mean?**
> Wretched means: very unhappy, miserable.
> Verses 15–19: Many people know this: You want to do something good and don’t manage it. You want to stop doing something bad and do it anyway. Paul describes this inner conflict very honestly.
> Scholars think differently about who is speaking as “I” here: Paul himself as a Christian, Paul before his encounter with Jesus, or human beings in general. All three readings are found in the churches.
> Verse 22: Paul delights in God’s law. The law is good.
> Verses 24–25: The way out doesn’t come from our own strength, but from God, through Jesus Christ.
