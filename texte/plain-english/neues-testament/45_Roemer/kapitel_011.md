# Romans – Chapter 11
### God has not rejected his people

---

> **An important note first**
> This chapter is the high point of chapters 9 to 11. Here Paul says very clearly: God has not rejected his people Israel (verses 1–2). He does not take back his gifts and his calling (verse 29). Christians are like branches that were grafted into an olive tree that was not their own. They must not boast over the original branches (verse 18).
> This chapter is very important for the relationship between Christians and Jews today. The churches refer to it when they say: God’s covenant with Israel still holds.

---

## A remnant remains (Verses 1–10)

[1] So I ask:
Did God reject his people?
Certainly not!
I myself am an Israelite.
I am a descendant of Abraham, from the tribe of Benjamin.
[2] God did not reject his people.
He knew them from the beginning.
Do you not know what the Scripture says about Elijah?
Elijah complains to God against Israel.
He says,
[3] “Lord, they have killed your prophets.
They have torn down your altars.
I am the only one left,
and they are trying to kill me.”
[4] But how does God answer him?
“I have kept 7,000 men for myself.
They have not bowed down to Baal.”
[5] It is the same today.
There is a remnant, that is, a small group that is left.
God chose them because of his grace.
Grace means: God’s kindness that we do not earn.
[6] If it is by grace, then it is no longer because of what people do.
Otherwise grace would no longer be grace.
But if it is because of what people do, it is no longer grace.
Otherwise work would no longer be work.
[7] What does this mean?
Israel did not get what it was looking for.
But the chosen ones got it.
The rest were hardened.
That means: their hearts became hard.
[8] It is written,
“God gave them a spirit of deep sleep.
He gave them eyes that do not see
and ears that do not hear,
to this very day.”
[9] David says,
“Let their table become a snare,
a trap,
something to stumble over,
and a punishment for them.
[10] Let their eyes be darkened, so that they cannot see.
Keep their backs bent forever.”
A snare is a trap.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Paul himself is a Jew. For that reason alone God cannot have rejected his people.
> Verse 3 quotes 1 Kings 19:10 and 19:14. Verse 4 quotes 1 Kings 19:18. Elijah thought he was all alone. But God had kept many people faithful.
> Verse 5: “A remnant”: Even in Paul’s time there were many Jews who believed in Jesus. The first Christians were all Jews.
> Verse 8 combines Deuteronomy 29:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 29:3) and Isaiah 29:10. Verses 9–10 quote Psalm 69:22–23 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 69:23–24).
> Verses 7–10: “Hardened” is a hard word. But Paul says right after: This hardening is only partial and only for a time (verses 11 and 25). It is not a final verdict.
> Important: In the past these verses were often used against Jews. That was wrong. Here Paul speaks as a Jew about his own people, and his goal is the salvation of all Israel (verse 26).

---

## Stumbled, but not fallen (Verses 11–16)

[11] So I ask:
Did they stumble so that they would fall for good?
Certainly not!
But because they fell, salvation has come to the Gentiles.
Gentiles are people who are not Jews.
This happened to make Israel jealous.
[12] Their fall has made the world rich.
Their loss has made the Gentiles rich.
So how much more will their fullness bring!
[13] Now I am speaking to you who are Gentiles.
I am an apostle to the Gentiles.
So I honor my work.
[14] Perhaps I can make my own people jealous in this way,
and save some of them.
[15] Their rejection means that the world is made friends with God again.
So what will their acceptance be?
It will be life from the dead!
[16] If the first fruit is holy, then the whole lump of dough is holy too.
If the root is holy, then the branches are holy too.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Israel did not fall in order to stay down. God has a good plan.
> Verses 12 and 15: Paul expects something great for Israel: “fullness” and “life from the dead.” His hope for his people is huge.
> Verse 15: “Rejection” here means a time in which many do not believe in Jesus. It is not God’s last word. Right after, Paul speaks of their “acceptance.”
> Verse 16: “The first fruit”: In the law of Israel, the first piece of the bread dough was given to God (Numbers 15:17–21). “The root” probably means the fathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Because they are holy, the whole people is dedicated to God.

---

## The olive tree (Verses 17–24)

[17] Some of the branches were broken off.
You were a wild olive branch.
But you were grafted in among the other branches.
To graft means: to join a branch from one tree onto another tree, so that it grows there.
Now you share with them in the root and in the rich sap of the olive tree.
[18] So do not boast that you are better than the branches.
If you do boast, remember this:
It is not you who support the root, but the root supports you.
[19] Then you will say,
“Branches were broken off, so that I could be grafted in.”
[20] That is true.
They were broken off because they did not believe.
And you stand because you believe.
Do not be proud.
Be afraid instead.
[21] God did not spare the natural branches.
So he will not spare you either.
[22] So look at God’s goodness and his strictness.
He is strict with those who fell.
But he is good to you,
if you stay in his goodness.
If you do not, you will be cut off too.
[23] And they will be grafted in again,
if they do not stay in their unbelief.
God is able to graft them in again.
[24] You were cut out of an olive tree that is wild by nature.
And against nature you were grafted into a good olive tree.
So how much more will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree!

> **What does this mean?**
> The olive tree is a picture of God’s people. The root and the tree belong to Israel. The Christians from the other nations are like wild branches that were grafted in. They live from the sap of this root.
> Verse 18: “It is not you who support the root, but the root supports you.” The Christian faith is rooted in Judaism. Christians owe their faith in the one God, the Holy Scriptures and Jesus himself to Israel.
> Verses 18–21: Paul expressly warns the Christians from the nations against arrogance toward Jews. This warning was forgotten far too often in church history. Contempt, hatred and violence against Jews came from this. That was a grave guilt.
> Verses 23–24: God can and will graft the “natural branches” in again. The tree is “their own olive tree.”

---

## All Israel will be saved (Verses 25–32)

[25] Brothers, I want you to know this mystery.
A mystery is something hidden that God now makes known.
I tell you this so that you do not think you are wise.
Part of Israel has become hard.
This will last until the full number of Gentiles has come in.
[26] And so all Israel will be saved.
It is written,
“The Deliverer will come out of Zion.
He will turn Jacob away from ungodliness.
[27] This is my covenant with them,
when I take away their sins.”
A covenant is a binding agreement.
[28] When it comes to the Good News, they are enemies for your sake.
But when it comes to God’s choosing, they are loved,
because of their fathers.
[29] God’s gifts and his calling can never be taken back.
[30] In the past, you did not obey God.
But now you have received mercy, because they did not obey.
[31] In the same way, they have now not obeyed.
So through the mercy shown to you, they may also receive mercy.
[32] God has shut up all people in disobedience,
so that he can have mercy on all.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: The hardening affects only part of Israel, and it lasts only “until” a certain time. It is not forever.
> Verse 26: “And so all Israel will be saved.” Paul does not say exactly how this happens. Scholars understand it differently. What is clear: God keeps his promise to Israel.
> Verses 26–27 combine Isaiah 59:20–21 and Isaiah 27:9.
> Verse 28: “Enemies” here means: opponents of the Christian message. But in the same sentence Paul says: They are “loved” by God. God’s love has the last word.
> Verse 29 is one of the most important verses for the relationship between Christians and Jews: “God’s gifts and his calling can never be taken back.” God never takes back his choosing of Israel. The Catholic Church also refers to this in the declaration *Nostra Aetate* (1965). Many Protestant churches have confessed this too.
> Verse 32: All people, Jews and Gentiles, depend on God’s mercy. And God wants to have mercy on all.

---

## Praise of God’s wisdom (Verses 33–36)

[33] Oh, how deep are the riches of God’s wisdom and knowledge!
No one can search out his judgments.
No one can trace his ways.
[34] “Who has known the mind of the Lord?
Or who has given him advice?”
[35] “Or who has first given something to him,
so that God must pay it back?”
[36] Everything comes from him.
Everything exists through him.
Everything is for him.
To him be the glory forever!
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the hard questions in chapters 9 to 11, Paul doesn’t end with a teaching but with a song of praise. He is amazed at God. God’s ways are greater than people can understand.
> Verse 34 quotes Isaiah 40:13. Verse 35 takes up Job 41:11 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 41:3).
> Verse 36: Everything comes from God, everything exists through him, and everything is heading toward him.
> For today: Where we don’t understand God’s ways, we may still trust and praise God.
