# Romans – Chapter 3
### Made right by grace, through faith

---

## God remains faithful (Verses 1–8)

[1] Then what advantage does the Jew have?
Or what is the benefit of circumcision?
[2] Much,
in every way!
First of all:
They were entrusted with the words of God.
[3] What if
some were without faith?
Will their lack of faith cancel the faithfulness of God?
[4] May it never be!
Let God be found true,
even if every person is a liar.
As it is written,
“that you might be justified in your words,
and might prevail when you come into judgment.”
[5] But if our unrighteousness
shows the righteousness of God even more clearly,
what will we say?
Is God unrighteous
when he brings wrath?
I speak like people do.
[6] May it never be!
For then how will God judge the world?
[7] For if through my lie
the truth of God becomes even greater,
to his glory,
why am I still judged as a sinner?
[8] And why not say,
“Let’s do evil,
that good may come”?
Some people slander us
and claim
that we say this.
Those who say so are justly condemned.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–2: Paul says clearly: The advantage of the Jews is great. God entrusted his word, the Holy Scriptures, to them.
> Verses 3–4: Even when people are unfaithful, God remains faithful. God’s faithfulness to Israel does not end. This is very important for the relationship between Christians and Jews.
> Verse 4 quotes Psalm 51:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 51:6).
> Verses 5–8: Paul goes through objections, as in a debate. He rejects the idea that you may do evil so that God appears all the more gracious.

---

## All people need God’s grace (Verses 9–20)

[9] What then?
Are we better than they?
No, in no way.
For we have already shown:
Jews and Greeks
are all under the power of sin.
[10] As it is written,
“There is no one righteous;
no, not one.
[11] There is no one
who understands.
There is no one
who seeks after God.
[12] They have all turned away.
They have together become useless.
There is no one
who does good,
no, not so much as one.”
[13] “Their throat is an open tomb.
With their tongues they have used deceit.”
“The poison of vipers is under their lips.”
[14] “Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.”
[15] “Their feet are swift to shed blood.
[16] Destruction and misery are in their ways.
[17] The way of peace, they haven’t known.”
[18] “There is no fear of God before their eyes.”
[19] Now we know:
Whatever the law says,
it says to those
who are under the law.
So every mouth is to be closed,
and all the world is to be brought under the judgment of God.
[20] For by the works of the law
no one will be justified in his sight.
For through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

*justified* = declared righteous by God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “We” here probably means the Jews, to whom Paul himself belongs. He says: We are not better, but not worse either. All people, Jews and non-Jews, need God’s grace.
> Verses 10–18 are a collection of quotations from the Bible of Israel: Psalm 14:1–3 (and Psalm 53), Psalm 5:9 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 5:10), Psalm 140:3 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 140:4), Psalm 10:7, Isaiah 59:7–8 and Psalm 36:1 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 36:2).
> “The works of the law” (verse 20) means: doing what the law requires. Paul says: No one can make themselves righteous before God by their own achievement. Verse 20 recalls Psalm 143:2.
> Verse 20: The law is good. It shows what is right and wrong. But it cannot set people free from sin.

---

## Made right by grace (Verses 21–31)

[21] But now the righteousness of God has been revealed
apart from the law.
The law and the prophets testify to it.
[22] It is the righteousness of God
through faith in Jesus Christ,
to all and on all
who believe.
For there is no distinction.
[23] For all have sinned,
and fall short of the glory of God.
[24] They are justified freely
by his grace
through the redemption
that is in Christ Jesus.
[25] God put him forward as an atoning sacrifice,
through faith in his blood.
In this way God wanted to show his righteousness.
For in his patience
he had passed over the sins done before.
[26] So now, at this present time, he shows his righteousness:
He himself is just,
and he makes righteous the one
who has faith in Jesus.
[27] Where then is the boasting?
It is excluded.
By what kind of law?
By the law of works?
No,
but by a law of faith.
[28] For we hold
that a person is justified by faith
apart from the works of the law.
[29] Or is God the God of Jews only?
Isn’t he the God of Gentiles also?
Yes,
of Gentiles also.
[30] For there is one God.
He will justify the circumcised by faith
and the uncircumcised through faith.
[31] Do we then cancel the law through faith?
May it never be!
No, we establish the law.

*redemption* = being set free, being bought free
*atoning sacrifice* = an offering that makes peace between God and people

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 21–26 are a heart of the letter to the Romans. The message is: God makes people righteous. Not because they deserve it, but as a gift, by grace, through Jesus Christ.
> Verse 23: “All have sinned”: No one is without guilt. But no one is shut out from God’s grace either.
> Verse 25: “Atoning sacrifice”: This recalls the Day of Atonement in the temple (Leviticus 16). Christians believe: Through the death of Jesus God gives reconciliation. How exactly to understand this is something the churches still think about today.
> Verse 28: In his German translation, Martin Luther added the word *alone* here. This word is not in the Greek text and not in the WEB either. There was a long argument about it. In the *Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification* (1999), the Catholic Church and the Lutheran World Federation stated together: We are made righteous by grace alone, in faith, not because of our merits.
> Verses 29–30: God is the one God of all people, of the Jews and of the other nations.
> Verse 31: Paul says clearly: Faith does not cancel the law, but establishes it. God’s law remains good and holy.
