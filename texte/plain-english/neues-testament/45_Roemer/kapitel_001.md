# Romans – Chapter 1
### The Good News is the power of God

---

## Before we begin: The letter to the Romans

The letter to the Romans is a letter from the apostle Paul to the Christians in Rome. Paul probably wrote it around AD 56 or 57, probably in Corinth. He had never been to Rome, but he wanted to go there soon (compare Acts 19:21 and 28).
The church in Rome was made up of Christians of Jewish background and Christians who came from other nations. Paul explains to them in detail what the Good News of Jesus means: God accepts all people, Jews and non-Jews, not because of what they achieve, but by grace, through faith.
The letter to the Romans is one of the most important texts of Christianity. It deeply shaped people like Augustine and Martin Luther. The churches argued for a long time about some passages. Today they agree on many things, for example in the *Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification* of 1999.
**Important note:** In chapters 9 to 11 Paul writes in detail about Israel. He says: God has not rejected his people. His gifts and his calling cannot be taken back. Today these chapters are the basis for the churches recognizing God’s lasting covenant with the Jewish people.
Paul sometimes writes difficult and long sentences. We divide them into short sentences, but we leave nothing out.
The letter to the Romans has 16 chapters.

## Greeting to the church in Rome (Verses 1–7)

[1] Paul,
a servant of Jesus Christ,
called to be an apostle,
set apart for the Good News of God.
[2] God promised this Good News beforehand
through his prophets in the holy Scriptures.
[3] It is about his Son.
He was born of the offspring of David according to the flesh.
[4] Through the Spirit of holiness
he was declared to be the Son of God with power,
by the resurrection from the dead:
Jesus Christ our Lord.
[5] Through him we received grace and apostleship,
to bring about the obedience of faith
among all the nations
for the sake of his name.
[6] You are also among them.
You too are called
to belong to Jesus Christ.
[7] To all who are in Rome,
who are loved by God
and called
to be saints:
Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Apostle means: someone sent out by Jesus with his message.
> Saints here means: all who belong to God.
> In those days a letter began with the name of the sender and a greeting. Paul turns this into a small confession of faith.
> Verse 1: “Servant” means: Paul belongs completely to Jesus and serves him.
> Verse 2: The Good News is not something completely new. God already promised it in the Bible of Israel.
> Verse 3: The WEB, our English source text, says “according to the flesh.” That means: as a human being. Jesus was a descendant of King David, so he was a Jew.
> Verse 7: “Saints” are all Christians. Holy means: belonging to God.
> “Grace” and “peace”: Grace recalls the Greek greeting, peace (shalom) the Hebrew one. Paul joins the two.

## Paul wants to come to Rome (Verses 8–15)

[8] First, I thank my God
through Jesus Christ for all of you.
For your faith is spoken of throughout the whole world.
[9] God is my witness.
I serve him in my spirit
in the Good News of his Son.
He knows
how I always remember you in my prayers
without ceasing.
[10] I ask
that now at last I may succeed
in coming to you by the will of God.
[11] For I long
to see you.
I want to share some spiritual gift with you,
so that you may be made strong.
[12] That is,
I want to be encouraged together with you,
each of us by the other’s faith,
both yours and mine.
[13] Brothers,
I want you to know:
I often planned
to come to you.
But so far I have been prevented.
I wanted to have some fruit among you also,
as among the rest of the Gentiles.
[14] I am in debt
both to Greeks and to foreigners,
both to the wise and to the foolish.
[15] So as much as I can,
I am eager
to preach the Good News to you also who are in Rome.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul doesn’t only want to give, but also to receive. They strengthen each other’s faith.
> Verse 14: “To Greeks and to foreigners”: This means all people, educated or not, from all cultures. Paul feels he owes something to all of them.

## The power of the Good News (Verses 16–17)

[16] For I am not ashamed of the Good News of Christ.
For it is the power of God
for salvation
for everyone who believes,
for the Jew first,
and also for the Greek.
[17] For in it God’s righteousness is revealed,
from faith to faith.
As it is written,
“But the righteous shall live by faith.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Righteousness means: being right with God.
> Verses 16–17 are like a heading over the whole letter.
> “For the Jew first”: For Paul, Israel has a special place. The Good News comes from Israel and is meant first for Israel.
> Verse 17 quotes Habakkuk 2:4. “God’s righteousness” means: God makes people righteous. He gives them a good relationship with himself. This happens through faith, that is, through trusting God.
> This verse was especially important for Martin Luther. It became the heart of the Reformation.

## All people need God (Verses 18–32)

[18] For the anger of God is revealed from heaven
against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of people
who hold down the truth by unrighteousness.
[19] For what can be known about God
is plain to them.
God has shown it to them.
[20] For since the creation of the world
his invisible qualities
are clearly seen in the things he has made:
his everlasting power and divinity.
So they are without excuse.
[21] For they knew God,
but they didn’t glorify him as God
and didn’t give thanks.
Instead their thinking became empty,
and their senseless heart was darkened.
[22] They claimed
to be wise,
and became fools.
[23] They traded the glory of the God who never decays
for images
that look like human beings who decay,
and like birds,
four-footed animals,
and creeping things.
[24] So God gave them up
to the lusts of their hearts,
to uncleanness,
so that their bodies were dishonored among themselves.
[25] They exchanged the truth of God for a lie.
They worshiped and served the creature
rather than the Creator.
He is blessed forever.
Amen.
[26] For this reason God gave them up
to shameful passions.
For their women changed the natural function
into that which is against nature.
[27] Likewise the men also
left the natural function of the woman
and burned in their lust toward one another.
Men did with men
what is inappropriate.
And they received in themselves
the penalty
their error deserved.
[28] Because they refused
to have God in their knowledge,
God gave them up to a corrupt mind,
so that they do
what is not fitting.
[29] They are filled with all unrighteousness,
sexual immorality,
wickedness,
greed
and malice.
They are full of envy,
murder,
strife,
deceit
and evil habits.
They are secret slanderers,
[30] backbiters,
hateful to God,
insolent,
arrogant,
boastful,
inventors of evil things,
disobedient to parents,
[31] without understanding,
unfaithful to promises,
without natural affection,
unforgiving
and unmerciful.
[32] They know what God has ruled:
Those who do such things
are worthy of death.
Yet they not only do the same themselves,
but also approve of others
who do them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Slanderer means: someone who tells lies about others.
> Verses 18–32: Paul describes what the world looks like when people don’t honor God: They make their own gods, and much injustice follows from that. “The anger of God” means: God takes evil seriously. He lets people feel the results of what they do (“God gave them up”).
> Verse 20: You can recognize God in creation, in nature and its greatness.
> Verses 29–31: The long list shows: It is about many kinds of wrong, including very everyday ones like envy, strife, gossip and arrogance. No one can leave themselves out.
> Important: Right at the beginning of the next chapter Paul says that whoever judges others has no excuse, whoever they are (2:1). Whoever points at others is also meant. Paul wants to show: All people need God’s grace.
> Verses 26–27: These verses speak about same-sex sexuality. Today the churches understand them in different ways. Some churches see them as a lasting command against same-sex relationships. Other churches say: Paul had mainly the practices of his time in mind, for example exploitation and cult practices, not loving, faithful partnerships as we know them today. Many churches are seriously wrestling with this question.
> Very important: These verses give no one the right to despise, insult or persecute people or to use violence against them. Every person has the same dignity. If you experience exclusion or violence because of your sexual orientation, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988; young LGBTQ people can also contact The Trevor Project. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verse 32: “Worthy of death” describes God’s judgment on sin. It is not a call for people to kill or punish others.
