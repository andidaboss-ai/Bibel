# Romans – Chapter 5
### Peace with God

---

## Peace and hope (Verses 1–11)

[1] So we have been justified by faith.
That is why we have peace with God
through our Lord Jesus Christ.
[2] Through him we also have access by faith
into this grace
in which we stand.
And we rejoice
in hope of the glory of God.
[3] Not only this:
We also rejoice in our sufferings.
For we know:
Suffering produces perseverance,
[4] perseverance produces proven character,
and proven character produces hope.
[5] And hope doesn’t disappoint us.
For God’s love has been poured into our hearts
through the Holy Spirit
who was given to us.
[6] For while we were still weak,
at the right time
Christ died for the ungodly.
[7] One will hardly die for a righteous person.
Perhaps someone would even dare
to die for a good person.
[8] But God shows his own love toward us in this:
While we were still sinners,
Christ died for us.
[9] Much more then,
now that we have been justified by his blood,
we will be saved from God’s wrath through him.
[10] For while we were enemies,
we were reconciled to God
through the death of his Son.
Much more,
now that we are reconciled,
we will be saved by his life.
[11] Not only so:
We also rejoice in God
through our Lord Jesus Christ.
Through him we have now received the reconciliation.

*perseverance* = keeping going in hard times
*reconciled* = made friends again after being apart

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Peace with God”: The relationship with God is whole again. We no longer need to be afraid of God.
> Verses 3–5: Suffering is not good. But Paul says: Even in hard times something can grow: perseverance, proven character, hope. This doesn’t mean that you should look for suffering. Whoever suffers may lament and get help.
> Verse 5: God’s love is like water that flows into our hearts.
> Verse 8 is one of the most beautiful sentences in the Bible: God doesn’t love us only once we are good. He loved us already while we were still sinners.
> Verse 10: “Enemies”: People stood against God. But God has given reconciliation.
> For today: If you are going through deep suffering, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Adam and Christ (Verses 12–21)

[12] Through one man sin entered into the world,
and death through sin.
So death passed to all people,
because all sinned.
[13] For sin was in the world even before the law.
But sin is not charged
when there is no law.
[14] Still, death reigned from Adam until Moses,
even over those
whose sins weren’t like Adam’s disobedience.
Adam is a foreshadowing of him
who was to come.
[15] But the free gift isn’t like the trespass.
For if by the trespass of the one
the many died,
much more did the grace of God
and the gift by the grace of the one man,
Jesus Christ,
overflow to the many.
[16] The gift is not
like what came
through the one who sinned.
For the judgment followed one trespass and brought condemnation.
But the free gift followed many trespasses
and brought justification.
[17] For if by the trespass of the one
death reigned
through the one,
so much more will those
who receive the abundance of grace
and of the gift of righteousness
reign in life
through the one,
Jesus Christ.
[18] So then, as through one trespass
all people were condemned,
even so through one act of righteousness
all people were justified to life.
[19] For as through the one man’s disobedience
many were made sinners,
even so through the obedience of the one
many will be made righteous.
[20] The law came in
so that the trespass might increase.
But where sin abounded,
grace abounded more exceedingly.
[21] As sin reigned in death,
even so grace might reign
through righteousness
to eternal life
through Jesus Christ our Lord.

*foreshadowing* = a picture or sign of something that comes later
*condemnation* = being judged guilty

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul recalls Adam, the first human being (Genesis 3). With him sin entered the world. From this verse Christians later developed the teaching of *original sin*. The churches understand it in different ways. It means: All people are born into a world in which there is guilt and harm.
> Verse 14: Adam is a “foreshadowing” of Christ: As Adam stands for all people, so does Christ. But Christ brings life instead of death.
> Verses 15–19: Paul compares again and again: what came through Adam, and what comes through Christ. Grace is always greater.
> Verse 20: “Where sin abounded, grace abounded more exceedingly.” A word of comfort: No guilt is so great that God’s grace cannot be greater.
> Important: The law is not bad. It makes sin visible.
