# Romans – Chapter 5
### Peace with God

---

## Peace and hope (Verses 1–11)

[1] God has made us righteous through faith.
So now we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.
[2] Through Jesus we can come into God’s grace by faith.
We now stand in this grace.
And we rejoice because we hope to share in the glory of God.
[3] But that is not all.
We also rejoice in our sufferings.
For we know that suffering produces perseverance.
[4] Perseverance produces proven character.
And proven character produces hope.
[5] And this hope will not let us down.
For God has poured his love into our hearts.
He did this through the Holy Spirit, whom he has given to us.
[6] We were still weak.
But at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.
[7] It is rare for anyone to die even for a righteous person.
Maybe someone would dare to die for a good person.
[8] But God shows how much he loves us:
Christ died for us while we were still sinners.
[9] Now we have been made righteous by his blood.
So we can be even more sure that he will save us from God’s anger.
[10] We were God’s enemies.
But we were made friends with God again through the death of his Son.
Now we are his friends again.
So we can be even more sure that the life of his Son will save us.
[11] But that is not all.
We also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ.
Through him we have now been reconciled with God.

*perseverance* = keeping going in hard times
*reconciled* = made friends again after being apart

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Peace with God”: The relationship with God is whole again. We no longer need to be afraid of God.
> Verses 3–5: Suffering is not good. But Paul says: Even in hard times something can grow: perseverance, proven character, hope. This doesn’t mean that you should look for suffering. Whoever suffers may lament and get help.
> Verse 5: God’s love is like water that flows into our hearts.
> Verse 8 is one of the most beautiful sentences in the Bible: God doesn’t love us only once we are good. He loved us already while we were still sinners.
> Verse 10: “Enemies”: People stood against God. But God has given reconciliation.
> For today: If you are going through deep suffering, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Adam and Christ (Verses 12–21)

[12] Sin came into the world through one man.
And through sin came death.
So death came to all people, because all have sinned.
[13] Even before the law was given, sin was in the world.
But where there is no law, sin is not counted.
[14] Still, death ruled from the time of Adam to the time of Moses.
It even ruled over people whose sins were not like Adam’s disobedience.
Adam is a foreshadowing of the one who was to come.
[15] But the free gift is not like Adam’s sin.
Through the sin of one man, many people died.
But God’s grace is much greater.
The gift of grace came through one man, Jesus Christ.
And this gift has overflowed to many people.
[16] The gift is not like what came through the one man who sinned.
The judgment followed one sin, and it brought condemnation.
But the free gift followed many sins, and it made people righteous.
[17] Through the sin of one man, death ruled because of that one man.
But think of those who receive God’s overflowing grace and the gift of righteousness.
They will rule in life through the one man, Jesus Christ.
That is so much greater!
[18] So one sin brought condemnation to all people.
In the same way one righteous act makes all people righteous and gives them life.
[19] Through the disobedience of one man, many people became sinners.
In the same way, through the obedience of one man, many people will be made righteous.
[20] The law came in, so that sin would grow.
But where sin grew, grace grew even more.
[21] Sin ruled and brought death.
In the same way grace now rules through righteousness.
It brings eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

*foreshadowing* = a picture or sign of something that comes later
*condemnation* = being judged guilty

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Paul recalls Adam, the first human being (Genesis 3). With him sin entered the world. From this verse Christians later developed the teaching of *original sin*. The churches understand it in different ways. It means: All people are born into a world in which there is guilt and harm.
> Verse 14: Adam is a “foreshadowing” of Christ: As Adam stands for all people, so does Christ. But Christ brings life instead of death.
> Verses 15–19: Paul compares again and again: what came through Adam, and what comes through Christ. Grace is always greater.
> Verse 20: “Where sin grew, grace grew even more.” A word of comfort: No guilt is so great that God’s grace cannot be greater.
> Important: The law is not bad. It makes sin visible.
