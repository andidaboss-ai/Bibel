# Romans – Chapter 14
### The strong and the weak

---

## Don’t judge one another (Verses 1–12)

[1] Accept the one
who is weak in faith,
but not to argue about opinions.
[2] One person has faith
to eat all things.
But the one who is weak eats only vegetables.
[3] Don’t let the one who eats everything
despise the one
who doesn’t.
And don’t let the one who doesn’t eat everything
judge the one
who does.
For God has accepted him.
[4] Who are you
to judge another person’s servant?
Whether he stands or falls
is up to his own master.
Yes, he will be made to stand.
For God has the power
to make him stand.
[5] One person considers one day more important than others.
Another considers every day alike.
Let each person be fully sure in his own mind.
[6] Whoever observes the day
observes it for the Lord.
And whoever doesn’t observe the day
doesn’t observe it for the Lord.
Whoever eats
eats for the Lord,
for he gives God thanks.
And whoever doesn’t eat
doesn’t eat for the Lord,
and he too gives God thanks.
[7] For none of us lives to himself,
and none dies to himself.
[8] For if we live,
we live to the Lord.
Or if we die,
we die to the Lord.
If therefore we live or die,
we are the Lord’s.
[9] For this is why Christ died,
rose, and lived again:
so that he might be Lord of both the dead and the living.
[10] But you,
why do you judge your brother?
Or you,
why do you despise your brother?
For we will all stand before the judgment seat of Christ.
[11] For it is written,
“ ‘As I live,’
says the Lord,
‘to me every knee will bow.
Every tongue will confess to God.’ ”
[12] So then each one of us
will give account of himself to God.

*despise* = look down on someone

> **What does this mean?**
> There was conflict in the church in Rome. Some Christians didn’t eat meat, for example because they worried it might have been offered to idols or not prepared according to the Jewish food laws. Others ate everything. Some kept certain feast days, others didn’t.
> “Weak” and “strong”: Paul calls the careful ones “weak in faith.” This is not an insult. He asks all sides to respect one another.
> Verse 3: “God has accepted him.” Whoever God accepts, we should accept too.
> Verses 7–8: “If therefore we live or die, we are the Lord’s.” These words are often read at funerals. They comfort.
> Verse 11 takes up Isaiah 45:23 and combines it with words from Isaiah 49:18.
> For today: Christians have different opinions on many questions, for example about food, about Sunday or about customs. That is no reason to look down on each other.

## Be considerate of one another (Verses 13–23)

[13] So let’s not judge one another any more.
Rather, decide this:
No one should put a stumbling block in his brother’s way,
or something that makes him fall.
[14] I know
and am persuaded in the Lord Jesus
that nothing is unclean of itself.
But to anyone
who considers something to be unclean,
to him it is unclean.
[15] Yet if your brother is hurt because of your food,
you are no longer walking in love.
Don’t destroy with your food
the one for whom Christ died.
[16] So don’t let what is good for you
be spoken of as evil.
[17] For God’s Kingdom is not eating and drinking,
but righteousness,
peace,
and joy in the Holy Spirit.
[18] Whoever serves Christ in these things
is acceptable to God
and approved by people.
[19] So then, let’s follow after things
which make for peace,
and things by which we may build one another up.
[20] Don’t tear down God’s work
for the sake of food.
All things indeed are clean.
But it is wrong for a person
to make others stumble by what he eats.
[21] It is good
not to eat meat,
not to drink wine,
and not to do anything
by which your brother stumbles,
is hurt or is made weak.
[22] Do you have faith?
Keep it to yourself before God.
Happy is the one
who doesn’t judge himself
in what he approves.
[23] But whoever doubts and still eats
is condemned,
because it isn’t done in faith.
And whatever
is not done in faith
is sin.

*stumbling block* = something that makes someone trip and fall

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Nothing is unclean of itself”: For Christians from the nations, the Jewish food laws are not an obligation. But Paul respects those who keep them. For Jews, the food laws are still an important part of their life with God today.
> Verses 15 and 21: Love is more important than your own rights. Whoever is free should be considerate of others.
> Verse 17: God’s Kingdom is not about food rules, but about righteousness, peace and joy.
> Verse 23: You should not act against your own conscience.
> For today: For example, if you have a guest who struggles with alcohol, out of love you go without alcohol. That is what is meant.

## Praise of God (Verses 24–26)

[24] Now to him
who is able to make you strong,
according to my Good News
and the preaching of Jesus Christ,
according to the revelation of the mystery
which has been kept secret through long ages,
[25] but now is revealed.
Through the Scriptures of the prophets,
by the command of the eternal God,
it has been made known to all the nations,
so that they may believe and obey.
[26] To the only wise God,
through Jesus Christ,
be the glory forever!
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> About the numbering: In the WEB this chapter has 26 verses. Many other Bibles end this chapter with verse 23.

> Verses 24–26: This praise of God appears in different places in the old manuscripts: in some after chapter 14, in others at the end of the letter after chapter 16 or after chapter 15. The WEB puts it here, as verses 24–26 of chapter 14. Many other Bibles have it as Romans 16:25–27.
> Its content: God’s plan was hidden for a long time. Now it has been revealed: Through Jesus Christ all nations are to come to faith.
