# Romans – Chapter 4
### Abraham, the father of all who believe

---

## Abraham believed God (Verses 1–12)

[1] What then will we say?
What has Abraham,
our forefather according to the flesh,
found?
[2] For if Abraham was justified by works,
he would have something
to boast about,
but not before God.
[3] For what does the Scripture say?
“Abraham believed God,
and it was accounted to him for righteousness.”
[4] To someone who works,
the wage is not counted as a gift,
but as something
that is owed.
[5] But to someone who doesn’t work,
but believes in him
who justifies the ungodly,
his faith is accounted for righteousness.
[6] So David also calls the person blessed
to whom God counts righteousness
apart from works:
[7] “Blessed are they
whose iniquities are forgiven,
whose sins are covered.
[8] Blessed is the man
whom the Lord will by no means charge with sin.”
[9] Is this blessing then only for the circumcised,
or for the uncircumcised also?
For we say
that faith was accounted to Abraham for righteousness.
[10] When was it counted?
When he was already circumcised,
or when he was still uncircumcised?
Not when he was circumcised,
but when he was still uncircumcised.
[11] He received the sign of circumcision
as a seal of the righteousness of the faith
which he already had
while he was uncircumcised.
So he was to be the father of all
who believe,
even if they are uncircumcised,
so that righteousness might also be counted to them.
[12] And he is also the father of the circumcised
who are not only circumcised,
but also walk in the steps of the faith
that our father Abraham had
while he was still uncircumcised.

*accounted* = counted, credited
*iniquities* = wrong deeds
*seal* = a mark that confirms something

> **What does this mean?**
> Abraham is the forefather of Israel. Paul uses him to show: At the very beginning there was faith, trust in God.
> Verse 3 quotes Genesis 15:6. Paul quotes this verse several times.
> Verse 5: “Him who justifies the ungodly”: God accepts people before they are good. That is grace.
> Verses 7–8 quote Psalm 32:1–2.
> Verses 10–11: Abraham believed God (Genesis 15) before he was circumcised (Genesis 17). So he can be the father of all who believe, from Israel and from all nations.
> Important: Abraham is the common father of Jews and Christians. Islam also refers to Abraham. Abraham connects people; he doesn’t divide them.

---

## The promise is for all (Verses 13–17)

[13] For the promise to Abraham and to his offspring,
that he would be heir of the world,
did not come through the law,
but through the righteousness of faith.
[14] For if those
who are of the law
are the heirs,
then faith is made empty
and the promise is made of no effect.
[15] For the law brings wrath.
For where there is no law,
there is no disobedience either.
[16] For this reason it is of faith,
so that it may be by grace.
So the promise is to be sure for all the offspring:
not only for those
who are of the law,
but also for those
who are of the faith of Abraham.
He is the father of us all.
[17] As it is written,
“I have made you a father of many nations.”
He is this in the presence of God,
in whom he believed:
the God
who gives life to the dead
and calls the things
that are not,
as though they were.

*heir* = someone who receives an inheritance

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: “Heir of the world”: God had promised Abraham that all nations would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3).
> Verse 15: The law shows what is wrong. That is why it makes disobedience visible.
> Verse 16: “The father of us all”: Abraham is the father of the Jews and of those who believe from all nations. Both belong to his offspring.
> Verse 17 quotes Genesis 17:5. God calls into being what does not yet exist. He can create something new out of nothing.

## Abraham trusted against all hope (Verses 18–25)

[18] Against hope,
Abraham in hope believed
that he would become a father of many nations,
as it had been told to him:
“So will your offspring be.”
[19] He was not weakened in faith.
He didn’t look at his own body,
which was already worn out,
for he was about a hundred years old.
And he didn’t look at the fact
that Sarah’s womb was as good as dead.
[20] He looked to the promise of God.
He didn’t waver through unbelief,
but grew strong through faith.
He gave glory to God
[21] and was fully sure:
What God has promised,
he is also able to do.
[22] So it also was
“credited to him for righteousness.”
[23] But it was not written only for his sake
that it was counted to him,
[24] but for our sake also.
It will be counted to us too,
when we believe in him
who raised Jesus our Lord
from the dead.
[25] He was handed over for our trespasses
and was raised
for our justification.

*trespasses* = wrongs, sins

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: “Against hope, Abraham in hope believed”: He trusts God, although it seems humanly impossible.
> Verse 18 quotes Genesis 15:5. God had shown Abraham the stars in the sky: You will have that many descendants.
> Verse 19: Abraham was almost a hundred, and Sarah was old. Still they had a son, Isaac (Genesis 21).
> Verses 24–25: What was true for Abraham is also true for us: God accepts us when we trust him. The center of faith is Jesus, who died for us and was raised.
