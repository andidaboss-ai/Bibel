# Romans – Chapter 12
### The new life in everyday life

---

> **An important note first**
> With chapter 12 a new part of the letter to the Romans begins. In chapters 1 to 11 Paul explained what God has done for people. Now he shows how Christians can respond: in everyday life, in the church, and in how they treat all people.

---

## A living sacrifice (Verses 1–2)

[1] So I urge you, brothers,
by the mercies of God:
Present your bodies to God
as a living sacrifice,
holy, acceptable to God.
This is your spiritual service.
[2] Don’t be conformed to this world,
but be transformed
by the renewing of your mind.
Then you can test
what the will of God is:
what is good,
well-pleasing,
and perfect.

*conformed* = made the same as everyone else

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: In the past people brought animals to God as sacrifices. Paul says: Give yourselves to God, with your whole life. “Living” means: not dying, but living in a way that pleases God. It is not about harming yourself.
> Verse 1: “Bodies” here means the whole person, with everything they do in everyday life.
> Verse 1: “Spiritual service”: The Greek word can also mean *reasonable* or *fitting*. It means: A life for God is the true worship.
> Verse 2: Christians should not simply go along with everything that everyone does. They should think for themselves and test what is good.

---

## One body, many gifts (Verses 3–8)

[3] For through the grace
that was given to me,
I say to each one of you:
Don’t think of yourself more highly
than you ought to think.
But think reasonably about yourself,
as God has given each person a measure of faith.
[4] For as we have many members in one body,
and all the members don’t have the same function,
[5] so we,
who are many,
are one body in Christ.
And each one is a member of the others.
[6] We have different gifts,
according to the grace God has given us.
If someone has the gift of prophecy,
let them prophesy
according to the measure of their faith.
[7] If someone has the gift of service,
let them give themselves to serving.
Whoever teaches,
to their teaching.
[8] Whoever encourages,
to encouraging.
Whoever gives,
let them do it with generosity.
Whoever leads,
let them do it with diligence.
Whoever shows mercy,
let them do it with cheerfulness.

*members* = here: parts of the body
*diligence* = careful, eager effort

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 4–5: Paul compares the church to a body. Every part is important, even if it has a different task. A similar picture is found in 1 Corinthians 12.
> Verses 6–8: Every person has gifts from God. No one is unimportant. The gifts are different, but all serve the community.
> Verse 6: “Prophecy” here doesn’t mean fortune-telling, but: encouraging, warning and comforting on God’s behalf.
> For today: People with disabilities, old people and children also have their gifts. A church needs everyone.

## This is what real love looks like (Verses 9–21)

[9] Let love be without hypocrisy.
Hate what is evil.
Cling to what is good.
[10] Love one another warmly
like brothers and sisters.
Be the first
to show honor
to one another.
[11] Don’t lag behind in eagerness.
Be on fire in spirit.
Serve the Lord.
[12] Rejoice in hope.
Endure in troubles.
Keep on praying faithfully.
[13] Help the saints
when they are in need.
Be eager to welcome guests.
[14] Bless those
who persecute you.
Bless them,
and don’t curse them.
[15] Rejoice with those
who rejoice.
Weep with those
who weep.
[16] Be of one mind with each other.
Don’t set your mind on high things,
but associate with the humble.
Don’t be wise in your own eyes.
[17] Repay no one evil for evil.
Respect what is honorable
in the sight of all people.
[18] If it is possible,
as much as it is up to you,
be at peace with all people.
[19] Don’t seek revenge yourselves,
beloved,
but leave room for God’s wrath.
For it is written,
“Vengeance belongs to me;
I will repay,
says the Lord.”
[20] Therefore
“If your enemy is hungry,
feed him.
If he is thirsty,
give him a drink;
for in doing so,
you will heap coals of fire on his head.”
[21] Don’t be overcome by evil,
but overcome evil with good.

*hypocrisy* = pretending to be better than you are
*hospitality* = welcoming guests kindly

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “Without hypocrisy” means: real, not just acted.
> Verse 13: “The saints” are the Christians, for example poor churches. Hospitality was very important for the first Christians.
> Verse 14: Christians should wish good even for their persecutors. Jesus also taught this (Matthew 5:44).
> Verse 15: Feeling with others is a form of love: rejoicing with them and grieving with them.
> Verse 18: “As much as it is up to you”: You can’t always force peace. But you should do what you can.
> Verse 19 quotes Deuteronomy 32:35. Revenge is not our business. But we may bring injustice before God in lament and report it to the police or to a court. That is not revenge.
> Verse 20: “Coals of fire on his head” is an old picture. Many scholars understand it like this: Kindness makes the enemy ashamed and can lead him to change his mind. The verse quotes Proverbs 25:21–22.
> Verse 21: Violence and hatred are not overcome with more violence and hatred, but with good.
> Important: These verses don’t mean that you simply have to put up with violence. Whoever experiences violence may protect themselves and get help. In the US, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call the police: 911 in the US, 999 in the UK.
