# Romans – Chapter 13
### The state, love and the new day

---

## Christians and the state (Verses 1–7)

[1] Let every person be subject to the governing authorities.
For there is no authority
except from God.
And the authorities
that exist
are appointed by God.
[2] So whoever resists the authority
resists what God has ordained.
And those who resist
will receive judgment.
[3] For rulers are not a terror
to good work,
but to evil.
Do you want to have no fear of the authority?
Then do what is good,
and you will have praise from it.
[4] For it is a servant of God
to you for good.
But if you do what is evil,
be afraid.
For it doesn’t bear the sword in vain.
It is a servant of God,
who punishes in anger
the one who does evil.
[5] So you need to be subject,
not only because of the anger,
but also for conscience’ sake.
[6] For this reason you also pay taxes.
For the officials are servants of God,
who continually do this very thing.
[7] So give everyone
what you owe:
taxes
to whom taxes are owed,
customs
to whom customs are owed,
respect
to whom respect is owed,
honor
to whom honor is owed.

*authorities* = people who govern, such as rulers and officials
*customs* = taxes on goods

> **Important note**
> These verses are among the most misused passages in the Bible. Rulers and also churches have often used them to demand that people obey every government, even an unjust one. During the time of National Socialism in Germany, many Christians behaved this way. That was a grave mistake.

> **What does this mean?**
> Paul writes to Christians in Rome, under the emperor. He wants to say: Christians are not troublemakers. Order, laws and taxes are good for living together.
> Verses 3–4: Paul describes what a state is for: It should protect the good and punish evil. A state is “a servant of God to you for good.” Every state must be measured by this.
> When a state itself does grave injustice, another word of the Bible applies too. The apostles said before the council: “We must obey God rather than men” (Acts 5:29). In Revelation 13 a state that sets itself up as God is described as a monster.
> The *Barmen Theological Declaration* of 1934 says: The state has the task of providing for justice and peace. But it must not try to control the whole of people’s lives.
> For today: In a democracy it is also part of our responsibility to vote, to take part in public life, and to stand up peacefully against injustice.

## Love is the fulfillment of the law (Verses 8–10)

[8] Owe no one anything,
except to love one another.
For whoever loves his neighbor
has fulfilled the law.
[9] For the commandments,
“You shall not commit adultery,”
“You shall not murder,”
“You shall not steal,”
“You shall not covet,”
and whatever other commandments there are,
are all summed up in this one saying:
“You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
[10] Love doesn’t harm a neighbor.
So love is the fulfillment of the law.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Love is a debt you never fully pay off. You always owe it to one another.
> Verse 9 quotes commandments from the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:13–17; Deuteronomy 5:17–21) and the command to love your neighbor from Leviticus 19:18. Jesus also called this command one of the most important (Mark 12:31).
> Verse 10: Whoever really loves harms no one. In this way they fulfill what God wants in the law.

## The day is near (Verses 11–14)

[11] Do this,
because you know
what time it is:
It is already time
to wake up out of sleep.
For salvation is now nearer to us
than when we first believed.
[12] The night is far gone,
and the day is near.
So let’s throw off the deeds of darkness
and put on the armor of light.
[13] Let’s live properly,
as in the day:
not in wild partying and drunkenness,
not in sexual promiscuity and lustful acts,
and not in strife and jealousy.
[14] But put on the Lord Jesus Christ.
And make no provision for the flesh,
to satisfy its lusts.

*provision* = making plans for something

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 11–12: Paul compares the time to the early morning. The night is coming to an end; God’s day is coming. That is why Christians should be awake and live with care.
> Verse 12: “The armor of light” is a picture. It means good deeds, truth and love, not real weapons.
> Verse 14: “Put on the Lord Jesus Christ” is also a picture: to live so closely connected with Christ that you become like him, as you put on new clothes.
> Verses 13–14: In the year 386 these verses moved the church father Augustine so much that he changed his life.
