# Romans – Chapter 8
### Nothing can separate us from God’s love

---

## Life in the Spirit (Verses 1–11)

[1] There is therefore now no condemnation
for those who are in Christ Jesus,
who don’t walk according to the flesh,
but according to the Spirit.
[2] For the law of the Spirit,
who gives life in Christ Jesus,
made me free
from the law of sin and of death.
[3] For there was something the law couldn’t do,
because it was weak through the flesh.
God did it:
He sent his own Son
in the likeness of sinful flesh
and for sin.
So he condemned sin in the flesh,
[4] so that the requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us
who don’t walk according to the flesh,
but according to the Spirit.
[5] For those who live according to the flesh
set their minds on the things of the flesh.
But those who live according to the Spirit
set their minds on the things of the Spirit.
[6] For the mind of the flesh is death.
But the mind of the Spirit
is life and peace.
[7] For the mind of the flesh is hostile toward God.
It does not submit to God’s law,
and it cannot do so.
[8] Those who are in the flesh
can’t please God.
[9] But you are not in the flesh
but in the Spirit,
if the Spirit of God really lives in you.
But whoever doesn’t have the Spirit of Christ
doesn’t belong to him.
[10] If Christ is in you,
the body is dead because of sin,
but the spirit is alive because of righteousness.
[11] But if the Spirit of him
who raised up Jesus from the dead
lives in you,
then he
who raised up Christ Jesus from the dead
will also give life to your mortal bodies
through his Spirit
who lives in you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “No condemnation”: Whoever belongs to Christ need not fear condemnation before God. A great comfort.
> Verse 1: The words “who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit” are missing from verse 1 in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them. In verse 4 they are in all manuscripts.
> “Flesh” and “Spirit”: “Flesh” doesn’t mean the body as something bad. It means a person who looks only at themselves and lives without God. “Spirit” means a life that is guided by the Holy Spirit.
> Verse 3: God sent his Son as a real human being. In this way he broke the power of sin.
> Verse 11: The Spirit who raised Jesus will also raise us. That is the Christian hope beyond death.

---

## Children of God (Verses 12–17)

[12] So then, brothers,
we don’t owe anything to the flesh,
so that we would have to live by the flesh.
[13] For if you live by the flesh,
you must die.
But if by the Spirit
you put to death the deeds of the body,
you will live.
[14] For all
who are led by the Spirit of God
are children of God.
[15] For you didn’t receive the spirit of slavery,
so that you would be afraid again.
But you received the Spirit of adoption.
By him we cry,
“Abba! Father!”
[16] The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit
that we are children of God.
[17] And if we are children,
then we are also heirs:
heirs of God
and joint heirs with Christ,
if indeed we suffer with him,
so that we may also be glorified with him.

*adoption* = taking someone as your own child

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: “If by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.” This is a picture. It means: not letting evil rule within you. It does not mean that you should harm your body. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, please get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 15: “Abba” is Aramaic and means *father*, the loving way a child speaks to a father. Jesus himself prayed to God like this (Mark 14:36).
> Verse 15: “Spirit of adoption”: God takes us as his children.
> Verse 17: Children are heirs. We receive a share in everything that belongs to God.
> For today: We don’t need to be afraid of God. We may call him Father.

## All creation is waiting (Verses 18–30)

[18] For I am sure:
The sufferings of this present time
are nothing compared with the glory
which will be revealed toward us.
[19] For the creation waits with eager expectation
for the children of God to be revealed.
[20] For the creation was subjected to emptiness,
not of its own will,
but because of him
who subjected it.
But there is hope
[21] that the creation itself also will be set free
from the slavery of decay
into the freedom of the glory of the children of God.
[22] For we know:
The whole creation groans
and has been in the pains of childbirth until now.
[23] Not only so,
but we ourselves also,
who have the first fruits of the Spirit,
groan within ourselves.
We are waiting for adoption,
the redemption of our body.
[24] For we were saved in hope.
But hope
that is seen
is not hope.
For who hopes for something
he already sees?
[25] But if we hope for something
we don’t see,
we wait for it with patience.
[26] In the same way the Spirit also helps our weaknesses.
For we don’t know
how to pray as we ought.
But the Spirit himself prays for us
with groanings
which can’t be put into words.
[27] He who searches the hearts
knows
what is on the Spirit’s mind.
For the Spirit prays for the saints
according to God’s will.
[28] We know
that all things work together for good
for those who love God,
for those
who are called according to his purpose.
[29] For those he knew beforehand,
he also chose beforehand
to become like the image of his Son.
So his Son was to be the firstborn
among many brothers.
[30] Those he chose beforehand,
he also called.
Those he called,
he also justified.
Those he justified,
he also glorified.

*first fruits* = the first part of a harvest; a first gift that promises more to come
*predestined* = chosen beforehand

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–22: Not only people, but the whole creation, animals, plants and the earth, are waiting for redemption. Many Christians see this as a task to care for creation.
> Verse 22: “The pains of childbirth”: The suffering of the world is like pain during a birth. After it comes something new.
> Verse 26: When we don’t know what to pray, the Spirit prays for us. Even a quiet sigh is a prayer.
> Verse 28: “All things work together for good for those who love God.” This doesn’t mean that everything terrible is good. It means: God can let something good grow even out of hard things. Whoever grieves or suffers may still lament.
> Verses 29–30: “Chose beforehand”: Christians have thought a lot about these verses. The churches understand them differently. Here Paul mainly wants to comfort: God has a plan and does not let go of his own.

## God’s love holds on (Verses 31–39)

[31] What then shall we say about these things?
If God is for us,
who can be against us?
[32] He didn’t spare his own Son,
but handed him over for us all.
How would he not also with him
freely give us all things?
[33] Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones?
It is God
who justifies.
[34] Who is he who condemns?
It is Christ
who died,
yes rather,
who was raised from the dead.
He is at the right hand of God
and also prays for us.
[35] Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?
Could oppression, or anguish,
or persecution,
or famine,
or nakedness,
or danger,
or sword?
[36] As it is written,
“For your sake we are killed all day long.
We were accounted as sheep for the slaughter.”
[37] No,
in all these things we are more than conquerors
through him
who loved us.
[38] For I am persuaded
that neither death, nor life,
nor angels, nor rulers,
nor things present, nor things to come,
nor powers,
[39] nor height, nor depth,
nor any other created thing
will be able to separate us from God’s love
which is in Christ Jesus
our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: “If God is for us, who can be against us?” A strong word of comfort.
> Verse 35: Paul knew distress, persecution and danger from his own experience.
> Verse 36 quotes Psalm 44:22 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 44:23).
> Verses 38–39 are among the best-known texts in the Bible. They are often read at funerals. Nothing, not even death, can separate us from God’s love.
> For today: If you feel abandoned, you may know: God’s love holds on to you. In hard times you can also get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
