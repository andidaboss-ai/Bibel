# Romans – Chapter 8
### Nothing can separate us from God’s love

---

## Life in the Spirit (Verses 1–11)

[1] So now there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.
They are the people who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
[2] The Spirit gives life in Christ Jesus.
And the law of the Spirit has set me free from the law of sin and death.
[3] The law could not do it, because the flesh made the law weak.
But God did it.
He sent his own Son as a human being, in a body like our sinful bodies.
He sent him to deal with sin.
So God condemned sin in the flesh.
[4] He did this so that what the law requires would be fulfilled in us.
We don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
[5] Some people live according to the flesh.
They think about what the flesh wants.
Others live according to the Spirit.
They think about what the Spirit wants.
[6] A mind set on the flesh leads to death.
But a mind set on the Spirit leads to life and peace.
[7] A mind set on the flesh is against God.
It does not obey God’s law.
And it cannot obey it.
[8] Those who live in the flesh can’t please God.
[9] But you do not live in the flesh.
You live in the Spirit, if the Spirit of God really lives in you.
If anyone doesn’t have the Spirit of Christ, he doesn’t belong to Christ.
[10] If Christ is in you, your body is dead because of sin.
But your spirit is alive because God has made you righteous.
[11] God raised Jesus from the dead.
His Spirit lives in you.
So God, who raised Christ Jesus from the dead, will also give life to your bodies that must die.
He will do it through his Spirit, who lives in you.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “No condemnation”: Whoever belongs to Christ need not fear condemnation before God. A great comfort.
> Verse 1: The words “who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit” are missing from verse 1 in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them. In verse 4 they are in all manuscripts.
> “Flesh” and “Spirit”: “Flesh” doesn’t mean the body as something bad. It means a person who looks only at themselves and lives without God. “Spirit” means a life that is guided by the Holy Spirit.
> Verse 3: God sent his Son as a real human being. In this way he broke the power of sin.
> Verse 11: The Spirit who raised Jesus will also raise us. That is the Christian hope beyond death.

---

## Children of God (Verses 12–17)

[12] So, brothers, we owe nothing to the flesh.
We don’t have to live the way the flesh wants.
[13] If you live by the flesh, you will die.
But if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
[14] All who are led by the Spirit of God are children of God.
[15] You did not receive a spirit that makes you slaves again, so that you would be afraid.
No, you received the Spirit of adoption.
Through him we call out, “Abba! Father!”
[16] The Spirit himself tells our spirit that we are children of God.
[17] And if we are children, then we will also inherit.
We will inherit from God, and we will inherit together with Christ.
This is true if we suffer with him, so that we will also share in his glory.

*adoption* = taking someone as your own child

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: “If by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.” This is a picture. It means: not letting evil rule within you. It does not mean that you should harm your body. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, please get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verse 15: “Abba” is Aramaic and means *father*, the loving way a child speaks to a father. Jesus himself prayed to God like this (Mark 14:36).
> Verse 15: “Spirit of adoption”: God takes us as his children.
> Verse 17: Children are heirs. We receive a share in everything that belongs to God.
> For today: We don’t need to be afraid of God. We may call him Father.

## All creation is waiting (Verses 18–30)

[18] I am sure of this:
What we suffer now is nothing compared with the glory that will be shown to us.
[19] The creation is waiting and longing for the children of God to be shown.
[20] The creation was made subject to emptiness.
It was not its own choice.
It happened because of the one who made it subject.
But there is hope:
[21] The creation itself will also be set free.
It will be set free from being a slave to decay.
It will share in the freedom and glory of the children of God.
[22] We know that the whole creation groans until now.
It suffers, as in the pains of childbirth.
[23] And not only the creation.
We ourselves groan inside too.
We already have the Spirit as the first fruits.
But we are waiting for God to adopt us fully as his children.
We are waiting for our bodies to be set free.
[24] We were saved in this hope.
But hope that is seen is not hope.
Who hopes for something he already sees?
[25] But we hope for what we don’t see.
So we wait for it patiently.
[26] In the same way, the Spirit helps us in our weakness.
We don’t know how we should pray.
But the Spirit himself prays for us.
He prays with groans that words cannot express.
[27] God searches our hearts.
He knows what the Spirit is thinking.
For the Spirit prays for the saints the way God wants.
[28] We know that all things work together for good for those who love God.
They are the people he has called according to his purpose.
[29] God knew them beforehand.
And he chose beforehand that they would become like his Son.
So his Son would be the firstborn among many brothers.
[30] Those he chose beforehand, he also called.
Those he called, he also made righteous.
Those he made righteous, he also gave glory.

*first fruits* = the first part of a harvest; a first gift that promises more to come

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 19–22: Not only people, but the whole creation, animals, plants and the earth, are waiting for redemption. Many Christians see this as a task to care for creation.
> Verse 22: “The pains of childbirth”: The suffering of the world is like pain during a birth. After it comes something new.
> Verse 26: When we don’t know what to pray, the Spirit prays for us. Even a quiet sigh is a prayer.
> Verse 28: “All things work together for good for those who love God.” This doesn’t mean that everything terrible is good. It means: God can let something good grow even out of hard things. Whoever grieves or suffers may still lament.
> Verses 29–30: “Chose beforehand”: Christians have thought a lot about these verses. The churches understand them differently. Here Paul mainly wants to comfort: God has a plan and does not let go of his own.

## God’s love holds on (Verses 31–39)

[31] So what can we say about all this?
If God is for us, who can be against us?
[32] God did not spare his own Son.
He gave him up for all of us.
So he will surely give us everything else with him, as a gift.
[33] Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones?
It is God who justifies.
[34] Who can condemn them?
It is Christ who died.
More than that, he was raised from the dead.
He is at the right hand of God.
And he speaks up for us.
[35] Who can separate us from the love of Christ?
Can trouble or distress?

Can persecution or hunger?
Can having no clothes, or danger, or the sword?
[36] As it is written:
“For your sake we are killed all day long.
We are treated like sheep to be slaughtered.”
[37] No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us.
[38] I am sure of this:
Neither death nor life, neither angels nor rulers,
nothing now and nothing in the future, no powers,
[39] nothing high and nothing deep, and nothing else in all creation
will be able to separate us from God’s love which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 31: “If God is for us, who can be against us?” A strong word of comfort.
> Verse 35: Paul knew distress, persecution and danger from his own experience.
> Verse 36 quotes Psalm 44:22 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 44:23).
> Verses 38–39 are among the best-known texts in the Bible. They are often read at funerals. Nothing, not even death, can separate us from God’s love.
> For today: If you feel abandoned, you may know: God’s love holds on to you. In hard times you can also get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
