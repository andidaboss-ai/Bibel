# 2 Thessalonians – Chapter 2
### Don’t let yourselves be confused

---

## The day of the Lord is not here yet (Verses 1–12)

[1] Brothers,
this is about the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ
and about
our gathering together to him.
We ask you:
[2] Don’t be quickly shaken in your mind
and don’t be troubled,
either by a spirit
or by a word
or by a letter
that supposedly comes from us
and claims
that the day of Christ has already come.
[3] Let no one deceive you in any way.
For first the rebellion must come.
And the man of sin must be revealed,
the son of destruction.
[4] He opposes everything
that is called God
or that is worshiped,
and exalts himself above it.
He even sits in the temple of God
and sets himself up as God.
[5] Don’t you remember?
I told you these things
when I was still with you.
[6] And you know
what is restraining him now,
so that he will be revealed only in his own time.
[7] For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work.
Only there is one now
who restrains it,
until he is taken out of the way.
[8] Then the lawless one will be revealed.
The Lord will kill him with the breath of his mouth
and destroy him
when he appears and comes.
[9] The coming of the lawless one happens through the working of Satan,
with all power,
with signs
and with lying wonders,
[10] and with all the deception of wickedness
for those
who are being lost.
For they didn’t receive the love of the truth,
by which they would be saved.
[11] That is why God sends them a powerful delusion,
so that they believe the lie.
[12] So all are to be judged
who didn’t believe the truth,
but took pleasure in unrighteousness.

> **What does this mean?**
> Rebellion means: turning away from God.
> Delusion means: a false belief that someone holds on to.
> Verse 2: Some in the church claimed that *the day of the Lord* had already come. Perhaps there was even a forged letter in Paul’s name. That unsettled people and made them afraid.
> Verse 3: The letter says: Stay calm. Some things still have to happen first. The WEB says “man of sin.” Some old manuscripts say *man of lawlessness*.
> Verse 4: The “man of sin” is a figure who puts himself in God’s place. This recalls rulers like the Syrian king Antiochus IV. In 167 BC he had defiled the temple in Jerusalem (compare Daniel 11:36). The Roman emperor Caligula also wanted to have his statue set up in the temple.
> Verses 6–7: We don’t know what or who is “restraining” the lawless one. Paul had explained it to the church in person. Interpreters have many guesses: the Roman Empire, the order of the state, the preaching of the Good News or God himself.
> Verse 8: The lawless one is not all-powerful. Christ defeats him with a single breath. This is meant to give courage, not fear.
> Very important: In history Christians again and again called certain people or groups *the lawless one* or *the Antichrist*: popes, reformers, rulers or even Jews. That caused much hatred and suffering. No one should put such a label on any human being.
> For today: The text warns against people and powers who let themselves be worshiped like God and lead people astray with lies and false wonders. It calls us to be alert: Love the truth.
> Verses 11–12: Whoever rejects the truth again and again eventually loses the ability to recognize it. The letter says this as a warning, not as a threat against particular people.

## God has chosen you (Verses 13–17)

[13] But we must always give thanks to God for you,
brothers
loved by the Lord.
For God chose you from the beginning for salvation,
through the sanctification of the Spirit
and belief in the truth.
[14] To this he called you
through our Good News,
so that you may share in the glory
of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[15] So then,
brothers:
Stand firm and hold to the traditions
that you were taught by us,
whether by word
or by letter.
[16] May our Lord Jesus Christ himself
and God
our Father,
who loved us
and gave us eternal comfort
and good hope through grace,
[17] comfort your hearts
and strengthen you
in every good work and word.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sanctification means: being made holy.
> Verse 13: After the serious words comes comfort: God has loved and chosen you. You don’t need to be afraid.
> Verse 15: “Traditions” are the teachings that Paul passed on to the church. Some were written down, others told by word of mouth.
> Different churches understand this verse differently: The Catholic and Orthodox churches also see in it a basis for church tradition alongside the Bible. Protestant churches stress that the Bible is the standard for all tradition.
> Verses 16–17: A prayer for comfort and strength. God’s comfort is “eternal comfort”: it never stops.
