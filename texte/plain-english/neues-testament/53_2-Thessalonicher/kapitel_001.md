# 2 Thessalonians – Chapter 1
### God is just

---

## Before we begin: The second letter to the Thessalonians

This letter is addressed to the same church as the first letter to the Thessalonians: to the Christians in Thessalonica, today’s Thessaloniki in Greece.
The church continued to face hostility and persecution. In addition, some claimed that *the day of the Lord* had already come. That made many afraid. Some even stopped working.
The letter calls itself a letter from Paul, Silvanus and Timothy. Some scholars think that a student of Paul wrote it later. Others consider Paul himself to be the author, shortly after the first letter.
The letter wants to comfort and calm: God is just. Don’t let yourselves be confused. Go on living calmly and do good.
**Important note:** In chapters 1 and 2 there are pictures of God’s judgment. They are meant to give courage to persecuted people. They are no invitation to wish evil on other people or to do evil to them.
The second letter to the Thessalonians has 3 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
Silvanus
and Timothy
write to the assembly of the Thessalonians
in God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.
[2] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> The greeting is almost exactly as in the first letter. Silvanus is the same man as Silas in Acts.

---

## God is just (Verses 3–12)

[3] We must always give thanks to God for you,
brothers.
That is fitting.
For your faith is growing greatly,
and the love of each one of you for one another
keeps increasing.
[4] So we ourselves boast about you in the assemblies of God,
because of your perseverance and faith
in all the persecutions and afflictions
that you endure.
[5] This is a clear sign of the righteous judgment of God.
For you are to be counted worthy
of God’s Kingdom,
for which you are also suffering.
[6] For it is a righteous thing with God
to repay with affliction
those who afflict you,
[7] and to give relief to you
who are afflicted,
together with us.
That will happen
when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven
with his mighty angels
in flaming fire.
[8] Then he will punish those
who don’t know God,
and those
who don’t obey the Good News of our Lord Jesus.
[9] They will pay the penalty:
eternal destruction,
far from the face of the Lord
and from the glory of his might.
[10] This will happen on that day,
when he comes
to be glorified in his saints
and to be admired among all
who have believed.
And you too believed our testimony.
[11] That is why we always pray for you:
May our God count you worthy of your calling.
May he fulfill with his power every desire for goodness
and every work of faith,
[12] so that the name of our Lord Jesus
may be glorified in you,
and you in him,
by the grace of our God
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 3–4: The church is being persecuted. Still its faith and love are growing. Paul is proud of this.
> Verses 5–7: The Christians ask: Why does God allow us to suffer? The letter answers: God is just. He sees the injustice. In the end he will comfort the victims and hold the wrongdoers accountable.
> Important: The letter leaves revenge to God. Christians are not to return evil for evil themselves (compare 1 Thessalonians 5:15 and Romans 12:19).
> Verses 7–9: Fire and angels are pictures from the prophets for God’s coming. They describe how seriously God takes injustice.
> Verses 8–9: Christians understand in different ways who exactly is meant and what “eternal destruction” means. Many stress: Judgment belongs to God alone. No human being may judge the eternal fate of another. And God wants all people to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4).
> Verse 9: “From the face of the Lord”: The worst thing is to be separated from God.
> Verses 11–12: Paul prays that God himself will complete the good that the Christians want and do.
