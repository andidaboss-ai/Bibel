# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 6
### Your body belongs to God

---

## Christians quarrel in court (Verses 1–8)

[1] If any of you has a quarrel with another,
does he dare to go to court
before the unrighteous,
and not before the saints?
[2] Don’t you know
that the saints will judge the world?
And if the world is judged by you,
are you not able
to decide the smallest matters?
[3] Don’t you know
that we will judge angels?
How much more things of everyday life!
[4] So if you have to judge things of everyday life,
do you appoint as judges people
who count for nothing in the assembly?
[5] I say this
to make you ashamed.
Isn’t there even one wise person among you
who would be able to decide between his brothers?
[6] Instead, brother goes to court against brother,
and that before unbelievers!
[7] It is already a complete failure for you
that you have lawsuits against one another.
Why not rather be wronged?
Why not rather be cheated?
[8] No,
you yourselves do wrong and cheat others,
and you even do it to your brothers.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: The Christians in Corinth took small quarrels, for example about money, to the Roman courts. Paul thinks: Christians should settle their quarrels among themselves peacefully.
> Verses 2–3: Paul writes “that the saints will judge the world.” He means the future, when God’s Kingdom comes.
> Verse 7: Paul says: It is better to suffer wrong than to do wrong.
> Important: This text is about small quarrels. It does not mean that crimes like violence or abuse should be handled inside the church. Such acts belong before the police and a court of the state. That protects the victims. Police: 911 (US), 999 (UK).
> For today: Many churches offer conflict resolution and mediation.

## You were washed (Verses 9–11)

[9] Or don’t you know
that the unrighteous will not inherit God’s Kingdom?
Don’t be deceived!
Neither the sexually immoral,
nor idolaters,
nor adulterers,
nor male prostitutes,
nor homosexuals,
[10] nor thieves,
nor the greedy,
nor drunkards,
nor slanderers,
nor those who rob others
will inherit God’s Kingdom.
[11] Some of you were such people.
But you were washed.
You were made holy.
You were made righteous
in the name of the Lord Jesus
and in the Spirit of our God.

*adulterer* = someone who is unfaithful in marriage

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: Paul lists ways of behaving that don’t fit with God’s Kingdom. Many of them are about how we treat other people: cheating, greed, slander, robbery.
> Verse 9: Two Greek words in this verse are hard to translate. The WEB translates them as “male prostitutes” and “homosexuals.” The first word (*malakoi*) literally means *the soft ones*. The second word (*arsenokoitai*) means something like *men who sleep with men*. It does not appear in any known text before Paul. The word “homosexual” has only existed since the 19th century. That is why other Bibles translate these words very differently.
> The churches understand this passage differently today. Some see it as a lasting command against same-sex sexuality. Others say: Paul meant exploitation, prostitution and the abuse of boys as it existed in his time, not loving, faithful partnerships as we know them today. Many churches are seriously wrestling with this question.
> Very important: This text gives no one the right to despise, insult or use violence against people. Every person has the same dignity before God. If you experience exclusion or violence because of your sexual orientation, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988; young LGBTQ people can also contact The Trevor Project. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verse 11 is the heart: “But you were washed. You were made holy. You were made righteous.” God changes people. No one is shut out from his grace.

## Your body is a temple (Verses 12–20)

[12] “All things are lawful for me,”
but not all things are helpful.
“All things are lawful for me,”
but I will not be ruled by anything.
[13] “Foods for the belly,
and the belly for foods,”
but God will bring both to nothing.
But the body is not for sexual immorality,
but for the Lord,
and the Lord for the body.
[14] God raised up the Lord,
and he will also raise us up by his power.
[15] Don’t you know
that your bodies are members of Christ?
Shall I then take the members of Christ
and make them members of a prostitute?
May it never be!
[16] Or don’t you know
that he who is joined to a prostitute
is one body with her?
For it says:
“The two will become one flesh.”
[17] But he who is joined to the Lord
is one spirit with him.
[18] Flee sexual immorality!
“Every sin
that a person does
is outside the body.”
But he who commits sexual immorality
sins against his own body.
[19] Or don’t you know
that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit
who is in you
and whom you have from God?
You are not your own.
[20] For you were bought with a price.
So glorify God in your body
and in your spirit,
which belong to God.

*members* = here: parts of the body

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–13 and 18: The sentences in quotation marks were probably sayings that people used in Corinth. Some thought: The body is unimportant, you can do anything with it. Paul disagrees.
> Verses 13–14: The body is not worthless. God will raise us up with our bodies too.
> Verses 15–16: There was a lot of prostitution in Corinth. Verse 16 quotes Genesis 2:24. Sexuality joins people deeply together. It is not something minor.
> Verse 19: Paul writes “that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit.” Every person may value their body and take good care of it.
> Verse 20: “Bought with a price”: In those days slaves were bought free. Jesus bought us free with his life.
> Important: Many people in prostitution are exploited or are victims of human trafficking. They deserve protection and help, not contempt. In the US, call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888. In the UK, call the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.
