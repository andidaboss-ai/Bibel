# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 8
### Knowledge and love

---

## Knowledge puffs up, love builds up (Verses 1–6)

[1] Now about things sacrificed to idols:
We know
that we all have knowledge.
Knowledge puffs up,
but love builds up.
[2] If anyone thinks
that he knows anything,
he doesn’t yet know
as he ought to know.
[3] But anyone who loves God
is known by him.
[4] So about eating things sacrificed to idols,
we know:
No idol in the world
is anything.
And there is no other God
but one.
[5] For even though there are things
that are called “gods,”
whether in the heavens or on earth,
as there are many “gods” and many “lords,”
[6] yet for us there is one God,
the Father.
All things come from him,
and we live for him.
And there is one Lord,
Jesus Christ.
Through him are all things,
and we live through him.

*sacrificed* = offered as a gift to a god

> **What does this mean?**
> In Corinth there were many temples for Greek and Roman gods. Animals were sacrificed there. Afterwards the meat was eaten or sold at the market. The Christians asked: May we eat such meat?
> Verse 1: That “we all have knowledge” was probably a saying from Corinth. Some Christians were proud of their knowledge. Paul answers: Knowledge alone makes people proud, but love builds up.
> Verse 3: More important than our knowledge about God is that God knows and loves us.
> Verses 4–6: There is only one God. Here Paul takes up the basic Jewish confession of faith, the *Shema*: *Hear, Israel* (Deuteronomy 6:4). He joins it with the confession of Jesus Christ as the one Lord.

---

## Caring for the weak (Verses 7–13)

[7] But not everyone has this knowledge.
Some are still used to idols until now.
So they eat the meat
as something
sacrificed to an idol.
And their conscience,
being weak,
is made unclean.
[8] Food will not bring us closer to God.
If we don’t eat,
we are not worse off.
And if we eat,
we are not better off.
[9] But be careful
that this freedom of yours
doesn’t become a stumbling block to the weak.
[10] For if someone sees you,
who have knowledge,
sitting at a meal in an idol’s temple,
won’t his conscience,
if he is weak,
be encouraged
to eat things sacrificed to idols?
[11] And through your knowledge
the weak one is ruined,
the brother
for whom Christ died.
[12] When you sin against the brothers in this way
and wound their weak conscience,
you sin against Christ.
[13] So,
if food causes my brother to stumble,
I will never eat meat again,
so that I don’t cause my brother to stumble.

*conscience* = the inner voice that tells us what is right and wrong
*stumbling block* = something that makes someone fall, here: fall away from faith

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Some Christians had worshiped idols themselves before. When they ate sacrificed meat, they felt pulled back into their old life. Their conscience troubled them.
> Verses 9–12: Paul says: Even if you know that idols are nothing, you should care for others. Your freedom must not harm others.
> Verse 13: Paul is ready to give up something that he is allowed to do, for the sake of others. Love is more important than your own rights.
> For today: If you know, for example, that a friend is struggling with alcohol or gambling, out of love you give it up when you are with them.
