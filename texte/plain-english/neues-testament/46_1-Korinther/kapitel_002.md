# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 2
### The wisdom that comes from the Spirit

---

## Paul preaches Christ crucified (Verses 1–5)

[1] Brothers, when I came to you,
I told you the testimony of God.
That means: what God has shown and said.
But I did not come with fine speeches or with great wisdom.
[2] I made up my mind not to know anything while I was with you,
except Jesus Christ, and him crucified.
[3] I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling.
[4] My speaking and my preaching did not use clever words of human wisdom.
Instead, they showed the Spirit and God’s power.
[5] So your faith would not rest on human wisdom.
It would rest on the power of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Paul was not a brilliant speaker. He came to Corinth “in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling.” He says this honestly.
> Verse 2: At the center of his preaching was Jesus, who died on the cross.
> Verse 5: Faith should not rest on an impressive preacher, but on God.
> For today: If you are afraid of speaking in front of others, you can know: Paul felt the same way. God doesn’t need perfect people.

---

## God’s hidden wisdom (Verses 6–16)

[6] But we do speak wisdom among those who are full grown.
But it is not the wisdom of this world.
It is not the wisdom of the rulers of this world.
They are coming to nothing.
[7] We speak God’s wisdom.
It is a mystery.
This wisdom was hidden.
Before the worlds began, God planned it for our glory.
[8] None of the rulers of this world has known it.
If they had known it,
they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.
[9] But it is as it is written:
“No eye has seen these things.
No ear has heard them.
No human heart has thought of them.
These things God has prepared for those who love him.”
[10] But God has shown them to us through the Spirit.
The Spirit searches everything,
even the deep things of God.
[11] Who knows what is inside a person?
Only the person’s own spirit inside them knows.
In the same way, no one knows the things of God,
except God’s Spirit.
[12] We did not receive the spirit of the world.
We received the Spirit who comes from God.
So we can know the things that God has freely given us.
[13] We also speak about these things.
We do not use words that human wisdom teaches.
We use words that the Holy Spirit teaches.
So we compare spiritual things with spiritual things.
[14] The natural man does not accept the things of God’s Spirit.
They seem foolish to him.
He cannot understand them,
because they can only be judged with the help of the Spirit.
[15] But a spiritual person can judge all things.
And no one can judge him.
[16] “Who has known the mind of the Lord,
so that he could teach him?”
But we have the mind of Christ.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: “Full grown” means: grown up. It means people who have grown in faith.
> Verse 8: “None of the rulers of this world has known it.” The rulers probably means the powerful people who had Jesus crucified, above all the Roman power. Some interpreters also think of invisible powers.
> Verse 9 recalls Isaiah 64:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 64:3) and Isaiah 65:17. Paul quotes freely here. These words are often read at funerals: God has prepared more for those who love him than we can imagine.
> Verses 10–12: God’s Spirit helps us to understand God. By our own strength we cannot fully grasp God.
> Verse 14: “The natural man” means a person who judges only with his own reason, without God’s Spirit.
> Verse 16 quotes Isaiah 40:13.
> Verses 15–16 don’t mean that Christians are better than others or are above everything. In the next chapter Paul scolds the Corinthians precisely because they think they are especially spiritual.
