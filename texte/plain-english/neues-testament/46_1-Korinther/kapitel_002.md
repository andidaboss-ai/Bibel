# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 2
### The wisdom that comes from the Spirit

---

## Paul preaches Christ crucified (Verses 1–5)

[1] When I came to you, brothers,
I didn’t come with excellent speech
or with great wisdom,
when I proclaimed to you the testimony of God.
[2] For I decided
not to know anything among you
except Jesus Christ
and him crucified.
[3] I was with you in weakness,
in fear,
and in much trembling.
[4] My speech and my preaching
were not in persuasive words of human wisdom,
but in the showing
of the Spirit and of power,
[5] so that your faith wouldn’t stand in the wisdom of people,
but in the power of God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Paul was not a brilliant speaker. He came to Corinth “in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling.” He says this honestly.
> Verse 2: At the center of his preaching was Jesus, who died on the cross.
> Verse 5: Faith should not rest on an impressive preacher, but on God.
> For today: If you are afraid of speaking in front of others, you can know: Paul felt the same way. God doesn’t need perfect people.

---

## God’s hidden wisdom (Verses 6–16)

[6] Yet we do speak wisdom
among those who are full grown.
But it is not a wisdom of this world,
nor of the rulers of this world,
who are coming to nothing.
[7] But we speak God’s wisdom,
which is a mystery
and has been hidden.
God decided on it before the worlds,
for our glory.
[8] None of the rulers of this world has known it.
For if they had known it,
they wouldn’t have crucified the Lord of glory.
[9] But it is
as it is written:
“Things which an eye didn’t see,
and an ear didn’t hear,
which didn’t enter into the heart of man,
these God has prepared for those who love him.”
[10] But to us God revealed them
through the Spirit.
For the Spirit searches all things,
even the deep things of God.
[11] For who among people knows
the things of a person
except the spirit of the person
which is in him?
In the same way, no one knows
the things of God
except God’s Spirit.
[12] But we didn’t receive the spirit of the world,
but the Spirit
which is from God.
So we can know
the things that God has freely given us.
[13] We also speak about these things,
not in words
which human wisdom teaches,
but in words
which the Holy Spirit teaches.
So we explain spiritual things with spiritual things.
[14] But the natural man doesn’t receive
the things of God’s Spirit.
For they are foolishness to him.
He can’t know them,
because they can only be judged spiritually.
[15] But he who is spiritual
judges all things.
He himself is judged by no one.
[16] “For who has known the mind of the Lord
that he should instruct him?”
But we have Christ’s mind.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: “Full grown” means: grown up. It means people who have grown in faith.
> Verse 8: “None of the rulers of this world has known it.” The rulers probably means the powerful people who had Jesus crucified, above all the Roman power. Some interpreters also think of invisible powers.
> Verse 9 recalls Isaiah 64:4 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 64:3) and Isaiah 65:17. Paul quotes freely here. These words are often read at funerals: God has prepared more for those who love him than we can imagine.
> Verses 10–12: God’s Spirit helps us to understand God. By our own strength we cannot fully grasp God.
> Verse 14: “The natural man” means a person who judges only with his own reason, without God’s Spirit.
> Verse 16 quotes Isaiah 40:13.
> Verses 15–16 don’t mean that Christians are better than others or are above everything. In the next chapter Paul scolds the Corinthians precisely because they think they are especially spiritual.
