# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 9
### Becoming all things to all people

---

## The rights of an apostle (Verses 1–14)

[1] Am I not free?
Am I not an apostle?
Haven’t I seen Jesus Christ,
our Lord?
Aren’t you my work in the Lord?
[2] If I am not an apostle to others,
at least I am one to you.
For you are the seal
that I really am an apostle in the Lord.
[3] To those
who want to examine me,
I answer like this:
[4] Don’t we have the right
to eat and to drink?
[5] Don’t we have the right
to take along a wife who is a believer,
as the rest of the apostles do,
and the brothers of the Lord,
and Cephas?
[6] Or do only Barnabas and I
have no right
not to work?
[7] What soldier ever serves at his own expense?
Who plants a vineyard
and doesn’t eat of its fruit?
Or who tends a flock
and doesn’t drink from the flock’s milk?
[8] Do I say these things only in a human way?
Or doesn’t the law also say the same thing?
[9] For it is written in the law of Moses:
“You shall not muzzle an ox
while it treads out the grain.”
Is it for the oxen that God cares?
[10] Or doesn’t he surely say it for our sake?
Yes, it was written for our sake.
For he who plows
should plow in hope.
And he who threshes
should thresh in the hope
of getting his share.
[11] If we sowed spiritual things among you,
is it too much
if we reap material things from you?
[12] If others have this right over you,
don’t we have it even more?
But we didn’t use this right.
Instead we put up with everything,
so that we cause no hindrance
to the Good News of Christ.
[13] Don’t you know
that those
who serve in the temple
eat from the things
of the temple?
And that those
who serve at the altar
get their share from the altar?
[14] In the same way the Lord commanded
that those
who proclaim the Good News
should live from the Good News.

*seal* = a stamp that proves something is real
*muzzle* = to tie an animal’s mouth shut

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Some in Corinth doubted whether Paul was a real apostle. Paul says: I have seen the risen Jesus (Acts 9).
> Verse 5: The other apostles, the brothers of Jesus and Peter (“Cephas”) were married. Their wives traveled with them.
> Verse 5: “The brothers of the Lord”: Christians understand this in different ways: as Jesus’ own brothers, as half-brothers or as close relatives.
> Verse 9 quotes Deuteronomy 25:4. An ox that works may also eat. Paul applies this to people.
> Verse 14 recalls a word of Jesus (Luke 10:7).
> Verse 12: Paul had the right to be supported by the church. But he gave it up. No one should think that he preached only for money.

## Paul gives up his rights freely (Verses 15–23)

[15] But I have used none of these things.
And I don’t write this
so that it will be done for me now.
For I would rather die
than let anyone take away my reason to boast.
[16] For if I preach the Good News,
I have nothing
to boast about.
For I must do it.
Woe to me
if I don’t preach the Good News!
[17] For if I do this of my own will,
I have a reward.
But if not of my own will,
I have a task as a steward entrusted to me.
[18] What then is my reward?
That I present the Good News of Christ
without charge,
so that I don’t misuse my right
in the Good News.
[19] For though I was free from all,
I made myself a slave to all,
so that I might win as many as possible.
[20] To the Jews I became as a Jew,
so that I might win Jews.
To those
who are under the law,
I became as one under the law,
so that I might win those
who are under the law.
[21] To those
who are without law,
I became as one without law,
though I am not without law toward God,
but under the law of Christ,
so that I might win those
who are without law.
[22] To the weak I became as weak,
so that I might win the weak.
I have become all things to all people,
so that by all means I may save some.
[23] I do all this for the sake of the Good News,
so that I may have a share in it myself.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Paul feels sent by God. He simply can’t help telling about Jesus.
> Verse 18: Paul worked as a tentmaker and preached without charge.
> Verse 20: Paul was a Jew himself. He means: When he was with Jews, he lived according to the Torah and its customs. When he was with non-Jews, he respected their way of life.
> Verse 22: “All things to all people” doesn’t mean giving up your convictions. It means: putting yourself in other people’s shoes, going toward them and speaking their language.
> For today: If you want to tell others about God, first listen and try to understand them.

## Like a runner in the stadium (Verses 24–27)

[24] Don’t you know
that in a race all the runners run,
but only one receives the prize?
Run like that,
so that you may win.
[25] Everyone
who competes in the games
exercises self-control in all things.
They do it
to receive a crown that withers,
but we to receive one that never withers.
[26] So I don’t run aimlessly.
I fight like a boxer
who doesn’t beat the air.
[27] But I beat my body
and bring it under control,
so that after I have preached to others,
I myself won’t be disqualified.

> **What does this mean?**
> The famous Isthmian Games took place near Corinth, similar to the Olympic Games. The winners received a crown made of plants, which soon withered.
> Verses 24–25: Paul compares life as a Christian with a race. It takes effort and endurance. But unlike in sports, everyone can reach the goal.
> Verse 27: “I beat my body” is a picture from boxing. It means self-discipline. It does not mean that you should hurt yourself. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, you can get help.
