# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 11
### Worship and the Lord’s Supper

---

## Head coverings in worship (Verses 1–16)

[1] Follow my example,
as I also follow the example of Christ.
[2] Now I praise you, brothers,
that you remember me in all things
and hold firmly to the traditions,
as I passed them on to you.
[3] But I want
you to know:
The head of every man is Christ.
The head of the woman is the man.
And the head of Christ is God.
[4] Every man
who prays or prophesies with his head covered
dishonors his head.
[5] But every woman
who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered
dishonors her head.
For it is the same
as if she were shaved.
[6] For if a woman is not covered,
let her hair also be cut off.
But if it is shameful for a woman
to have her hair cut off or be shaved,
let her be covered.
[7] For a man ought not to have his head covered,
because he is the image and glory of God.
But the woman is the glory of the man.
[8] For man is not from woman,
but woman from man.
[9] And man wasn’t created for the woman,
but woman for the man.
[10] For this reason the woman ought to have authority over her own head,
because of the angels.
[11] Yet in the Lord, neither is the woman independent of the man,
nor the man independent of the woman.
[12] For as woman came from man,
so a man also comes through a woman.
But all things are from God.
[13] Judge for yourselves:
Is it fitting
that a woman pray to God unveiled?
[14] Doesn’t even nature itself teach you
that it is a dishonor for a man
to have long hair?
[15] But if a woman has long hair,
it is a glory to her.
For her hair is given to her as a covering.
[16] But if anyone wants to argue about this:
We have no such custom,
nor do God’s assemblies.

*prophesy* = to speak a message from God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: First of all, it is important: Women in Corinth prayed and prophesied out loud in worship. Paul takes this for granted and finds it right.
> Verses 3–15: In those days respectable women in public often wore a head covering or pinned up their hair. Paul wants the church not to give offense. He gives reasons from the creation story (Genesis 2) and from the customs of his time.
> Verse 3: “Head” can mean ruler over someone, but also origin or source. Interpreters understand it differently.
> Verse 10: “Because of the angels”: What Paul means by this is unclear. The WEB says: The woman ought to have “authority over her own head.”
> Verses 11–12: Paul stresses: In the Lord, man and woman depend on each other. Both come from God.
> Today: Most churches see the head covering as a custom of that time. Some Christian women still wear one in worship today. Other churches stress the equal dignity of man and woman (Galatians 3:28). What is important: Man and woman have the same dignity before God.

## Wrongs at the Lord’s Supper (Verses 17–22)

[17] But in giving you this instruction
I can’t praise you.
For your meetings don’t make things better,
but worse.
[18] For first of all I hear
that there are divisions among you
when you come together as a church.
And I partly believe it.
[19] For there must also be factions among you,
so that it becomes clear
which of you are approved.
[20] So when you come together,
it is not the Lord’s Supper that you eat.
[21] For when you eat, each one takes his own supper first.
One is hungry,
and another is drunk.
[22] What, don’t you have houses
to eat and drink in?
Or do you despise God’s assembly
and put to shame those
who don’t have enough?
What shall I tell you?
Shall I praise you?
In this I don’t praise you.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days Christians celebrated the Lord’s Supper as a shared meal in a home. The rich came earlier because they didn’t have to work. They were already eating before the poor and the slaves came after their work. So nothing was left for the poor.
> Paul says: That is not the Lord’s Supper. Whoever shames the poor despises God’s assembly.
> For today: With God there are no first-class and second-class people. A church should make sure that everyone belongs, including the poor.

## The Lord’s Supper (Verses 23–34)

[23] For I received from the Lord
what I also passed on to you:
On the night in which he was betrayed,
the Lord Jesus took bread.
[24] When he had given thanks,
he broke it
and said:
“Take, eat.
This is my body,
which is broken for you.
Do this in memory of me.”
[25] In the same way he also took the cup after supper
and said:
“This cup is the new covenant in my blood.
Do this,
as often as you drink,
in memory of me.”
[26] For as often as you eat this bread
and drink this cup,
you proclaim the Lord’s death
until he comes.
[27] So whoever eats this bread
or drinks the Lord’s cup
in a way unworthy of the Lord
will be guilty
of the body and the blood of the Lord.
[28] But let each one examine himself,
and so let him eat of the bread
and drink of the cup.
[29] For he who eats and drinks in an unworthy way
eats and drinks judgment on himself
if he doesn’t discern the Lord’s body.
[30] For this reason many among you are weak and sick,
and quite a few have died.
[31] If we examined ourselves,
we wouldn’t be judged.
[32] But when we are judged by the Lord,
we are disciplined,
so that we will not be condemned with the world.
[33] So, my brothers,
when you come together to eat,
wait for one another.
[34] But if anyone is hungry,
let him eat at home,
so that your coming together doesn’t lead to judgment.
The rest I will put in order
when I come.

*covenant* = a binding promise between God and people
*discern* = to recognize, to see clearly

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–25: This is the oldest written report about the Lord’s Supper, even older than the Gospels. These words are still spoken at every Lord’s Supper today, in all churches. (Compare Mark 14:22–25; Matthew 26:26–29; Luke 22:19–20.)
> Verse 24: The WEB says “broken for you.” Some old manuscripts only say *for you*.
> Verse 26: At the Lord’s Supper, Christians remember the death of Jesus and wait for him to come again.
> Verses 27–29: “In an unworthy way” here mainly means: the way it was happening in Corinth, without care for the poor. To “discern the Lord’s body” probably also means: to respect the church as the body of Christ (compare 10:17).
> Important: No one has to be perfect to come to the Lord’s Supper. Precisely those who know their weakness are invited.
> Verse 30: Paul sees illnesses in Corinth as a result of these wrongs. That doesn’t mean that illness in general is a punishment from God. Jesus himself rejected that idea (John 9:2–3).
