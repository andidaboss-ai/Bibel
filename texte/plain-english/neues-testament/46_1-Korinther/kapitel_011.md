# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 11
### Worship and the Lord’s Supper

---

## Head coverings in worship (Verses 1–16)

[1] Follow my example,
just as I follow the example of Christ.
[2] Brothers, I praise you,
because you remember me in everything.
And you hold on to the traditions that I passed on to you.
[3] But I want you to know this:
Christ is the head of every man.
The man is the head of the woman.
And God is the head of Christ.
[4] Any man who prays or prophesies with his head covered
dishonors his head.
To prophesy means: to speak a message from God.
[5] But any woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered
dishonors her head.
It is the same as if her head were shaved.
[6] If a woman does not cover her head,
she should also cut her hair off.
But it is shameful for a woman to cut her hair off or to shave her head.
So she should cover her head.
[7] A man should not cover his head,
because he is the image and glory of God.
But the woman is the glory of the man.
[8] Man did not come from woman.
Woman came from man.
[9] And man was not created for the woman.
Woman was created for the man.
[10] That is why the woman should have authority over her own head,
because of the angels.
[11] But in the Lord, the woman does not exist without the man,
and the man does not exist without the woman.
[12] Woman came from man.
In the same way, a man is born from a woman.
But everything comes from God.
[13] Judge for yourselves:
Is it fitting for a woman to pray to God without a veil?
[14] Doesn’t nature itself teach you
that long hair is a dishonor for a man?
[15] But if a woman has long hair, it is a glory to her.
Her hair is given to her as a covering.
[16] But someone may want to argue about this.
Then I say: We have no such custom,
and neither do God’s churches.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 5: First of all, it is important: Women in Corinth prayed and prophesied out loud in worship. Paul takes this for granted and finds it right.
> Verses 3–15: In those days respectable women in public often wore a head covering or pinned up their hair. Paul wants the church not to give offense. He gives reasons from the creation story (Genesis 2) and from the customs of his time.
> Verse 3: “Head” can mean ruler over someone, but also origin or source. Interpreters understand it differently.
> Verse 10: “Because of the angels”: What Paul means by this is unclear. The WEB says: The woman ought to have “authority over her own head.”
> Verses 11–12: Paul stresses: In the Lord, man and woman depend on each other. Both come from God.
> Today: Most churches see the head covering as a custom of that time. Some Christian women still wear one in worship today. Other churches stress the equal dignity of man and woman (Galatians 3:28). What is important: Man and woman have the same dignity before God.

## Wrongs at the Lord’s Supper (Verses 17–22)

[17] Now I give you this instruction.
And here I do not praise you.
When you meet, it does more harm than good.
[18] First of all, I hear that there are divisions among you
when you come together as a church.
And I partly believe it.
[19] There must be groups among you,
so that it becomes clear which of you are faithful.
[20] So when you meet,
it is not the Lord’s Supper that you eat.
[21] When you eat, each person eats his own supper first.
One person stays hungry,
and another gets drunk.
[22] Don’t you have houses where you can eat and drink?
Or do you despise God’s church?
Do you want to shame the people who do not have enough?
What should I say to you?
Should I praise you?
No, in this I do not praise you.

> **What does this mean?**
> In those days Christians celebrated the Lord’s Supper as a shared meal in a home. The rich came earlier because they didn’t have to work. They were already eating before the poor and the slaves came after their work. So nothing was left for the poor.
> Paul says: That is not the Lord’s Supper. Whoever shames the poor despises God’s assembly.
> For today: With God there are no first-class and second-class people. A church should make sure that everyone belongs, including the poor.

## The Lord’s Supper (Verses 23–34)

[23] I received from the Lord what I also passed on to you:
On the night Jesus was betrayed,
the Lord Jesus took bread.
[24] He gave thanks.
Then he broke it and said,
“Take, eat.
This is my body,
which is broken for you.
Do this in memory of me.”
[25] In the same way, he also took the cup after supper and said,
“This cup is the new covenant in my blood.
Do this, as often as you drink, in memory of me.”
A covenant is a binding promise between God and people.
[26] Every time you eat this bread and drink this cup,
you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes.
That means: you tell everyone about it.
[27] So anyone who eats this bread or drinks the Lord’s cup
in a way unworthy of the Lord
will be guilty of the body and the blood of the Lord.
[28] Each person should test himself first.
Then he may eat the bread and drink from the cup.
[29] Someone may eat and drink in an unworthy way,
if he doesn’t discern the Lord’s body.
To discern means: to recognize.
Then he eats and drinks judgment on himself.
[30] That is why many of you are weak and sick,
and quite a few have died.
[31] If we tested ourselves,
we would not be judged.
[32] But when the Lord judges us,
he corrects us.
So we will not be condemned with the world.
[33] So, my brothers,
when you come together to eat,
wait for one another.
[34] If anyone is hungry,
he should eat at home.
Then your meeting will not bring judgment on you.
I will put the other things in order when I come.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 23–25: This is the oldest written report about the Lord’s Supper, even older than the Gospels. These words are still spoken at every Lord’s Supper today, in all churches. (Compare Mark 14:22–25; Matthew 26:26–29; Luke 22:19–20.)
> Verse 24: The WEB says “broken for you.” Some old manuscripts only say *for you*.
> Verse 26: At the Lord’s Supper, Christians remember the death of Jesus and wait for him to come again.
> Verses 27–29: “In an unworthy way” here mainly means: the way it was happening in Corinth, without care for the poor. To “discern the Lord’s body” probably also means: to respect the church as the body of Christ (compare 10:17).
> Important: No one has to be perfect to come to the Lord’s Supper. Precisely those who know their weakness are invited.
> Verse 30: Paul sees illnesses in Corinth as a result of these wrongs. That doesn’t mean that illness in general is a punishment from God. Jesus himself rejected that idea (John 9:2–3).
