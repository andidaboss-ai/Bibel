# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 4
### Servants of Christ

---

## God alone judges (Verses 1–5)

[1] So people should think of us
as Christ’s servants
and stewards of God’s mysteries.
[2] Above all, stewards are required
to be faithful.
[3] But to me it is a very small thing
whether I am judged by you
or by a human court.
Yes, I don’t even judge myself.
[4] For I know nothing against myself.
Yet that doesn’t make me righteous.
He who judges me
is the Lord.
[5] So judge nothing before the time,
until the Lord comes.
He will bring to light
the things hidden in darkness,
and he will reveal
the intentions of the hearts.
Then each one will get his praise from God.

> **What does this mean?**
> Steward means: a manager who takes care of someone else’s house or property.
> Verses 1–2: In those days a steward managed someone else’s house or property. He had to be faithful. In the same way, the leaders in the church should faithfully pass on God’s gifts.
> Verses 3–4: Paul doesn’t depend on what others think of him. He doesn’t even judge himself. Only God can truly judge a person.
> Verse 5: “Judge nothing before the time”: We don’t know another person’s heart. So we shouldn’t judge others too quickly.
> For today: If you constantly worry about what others think of you, you can know: God’s judgment is what counts. And God wants to praise us (verse 5).

## What do you have that you didn’t receive? (Verses 6–13)

[6] Brothers,
I have applied these things
to myself and Apollos as an example,
for your sake.
From us you should learn:
“Not beyond what is written.”
Then none of you will be puffed up
against one another.
[7] For who makes you different?
What do you have
that you didn’t receive?
But if you did receive it,
why do you boast
as if you had not received it?
[8] You are already full.
You have already become rich.
You have come to reign like kings
without us.
Yes, I wish
that you really did reign,
so that we also might reign with you!
[9] For I think
God has put us apostles last of all,
like men
sentenced to death.
For we have become a spectacle
to the world,
both to angels and to people.
[10] We are fools for Christ’s sake,
but you are wise in Christ.
We are weak,
but you are strong.
You have honor,
but we are despised.
[11] Even to this present hour
we are hungry and thirsty,
we don’t have enough clothes,
we are beaten,
and we have no settled home.
[12] We work hard
with our own hands.
When people curse us,
we bless.
When we are persecuted,
we endure.
[13] When people speak badly of us,
we answer kindly.
We have become like the filth of the world,
like the dirt
wiped off by everyone,
even until now.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: “Not beyond what is written” was probably a well-known saying. It means: Stay with what Holy Scripture says, and don’t make yourselves more important.
> Verse 7 is an important question: “What do you have that you didn’t receive?” Everything we have and can do is a gift.
> Verses 8–10: Here Paul speaks with irony. The Corinthians think they are rich, wise and strong. Paul shows: The apostles live very differently, poor and despised.
> Verse 9: “A spectacle”: In the arena, people sentenced to death were brought out at the end. That is how the apostles feel.
> Verse 12: Paul earned his own money as a tentmaker (Acts 18:3).
> Verses 12–13: The apostles answer evil with good, as Jesus taught.

## Paul as father of the church (Verses 14–21)

[14] I don’t write these things
to shame you,
but to warn you
as my beloved children.
[15] For even if you have 10,000 tutors in Christ,
you don’t have many fathers.
For in Christ Jesus
I became your father through the Good News.
[16] So I beg you:
Follow my example.
[17] That is why I have sent Timothy to you.
He is my beloved and faithful child in the Lord.
He will remind you of my ways in Christ,
as I teach everywhere in every church.
[18] But some of you are puffed up,
as though I were not coming to you.
[19] But I will come to you soon,
if the Lord is willing.
Then I will pay attention not to the words of those who are puffed up,
but to their power.
[20] For God’s Kingdom is not in words,
but in power.
[21] What do you want?
Shall I come to you with a rod,
or in love and a spirit of gentleness?

> **What does this mean?**
> Tutor means: here: a guardian who looked after children.
> Verse 15: In those days a “tutor” was often a slave who took the children to school and watched over them. Paul says: You have many teachers, but I founded the church. So I am like a father to you.
> Verse 16: “Follow my example”: Paul means his life of simplicity, patience and love (verses 11–13).
> Verse 17: Timothy was a close co-worker of Paul (Acts 16:1–3).
> Verse 21: “With a rod” is a picture for a strict warning. But Paul wishes to come with love and gentleness.
> Important: This picture from the way children were raised back then is no permission to hit children. Children have a right to grow up without violence.
