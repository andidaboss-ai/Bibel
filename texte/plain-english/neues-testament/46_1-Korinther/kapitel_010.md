# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 10
### Learning from history

---

## An example from the wilderness (Verses 1–13)

[1] Brothers, I want you to know this:
Our ancestors were all under the cloud,
and all passed through the sea.
[2] All were baptized into Moses
in the cloud and in the sea.
[3] All ate the same spiritual food,
[4] and all drank the same spiritual drink.
For they drank from a spiritual rock
that followed them.
And the rock was Christ.
[5] But with most of them
God was not pleased.
For they died in the wilderness.
[6] These things became examples for us,
so that we don’t long for evil things
as they longed for them.
[7] Don’t be idolaters,
as some of them were.
As it is written:
“The people sat down
to eat and drink,
and rose up
to play.”
[8] Let’s not commit sexual immorality,
as some of them did,
and in one day 23,000 fell.
[9] Let’s not test Christ,
as some of them tested him
and died by the snakes.
[10] Don’t grumble,
as some of them grumbled
and died by the destroyer.
[11] All these things happened to them
as an example.
They were written down
to warn us.
The end of the ages has come to us.
[12] So whoever thinks
that he stands firm
should be careful
that he doesn’t fall.
[13] No temptation has come to you
except what is common to all people.
God is faithful.
He will not allow
you to be tempted beyond what you are able.
But with the temptation he will also make the way of escape,
so that you may be able to endure it.

*idolater* = someone who worships idols
*temptation* = something that pulls you toward doing wrong

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–4: Paul recalls Israel’s exodus from Egypt (Exodus 13–17). The cloud led the people. They passed through the sea. God gave them manna to eat and water from the rock. Paul sees in this pictures of baptism and the Lord’s Supper.
> Verse 4: “The rock was Christ”: Paul says: Christ was already with his people back then. Jewish interpreters told a story that the rock went along with the people.
> Verse 7 quotes Exodus 32:6 (the golden calf). Verse 8 recalls Numbers 25:1–9. There 24,000 dead are mentioned. Paul says 23,000. Verse 9 recalls Numbers 21:4–9. Verse 10 recalls Numbers 14 and 16.
> Important: Paul doesn’t tell these stories to judge Israel. He speaks of “our ancestors” and says: This is a warning for us, for Christians.
> Verse 13 is a great comfort: God is faithful. He makes a way of escape. No one has to go through hard times alone.
> For today: If you feel overwhelmed, you may look for help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## The Lord’s Supper and idol sacrifices (Verses 14–22)

[14] So, my beloved,
flee from idolatry.
[15] I speak to you as to sensible people.
Judge for yourselves
what I say.
[16] The cup of blessing
which we bless,
isn’t it a sharing in the blood of Christ?
The bread
which we break,
isn’t it a sharing in the body of Christ?
[17] Because there is one loaf of bread,
we,
who are many,
are one body.
For we all share in the one loaf of bread.
[18] Look at the people of Israel:
Don’t those
who eat the sacrifices
share in the altar?
[19] What am I saying then?
That a thing sacrificed to idols is anything?
Or that an idol is anything?
[20] But I say:
What the Gentiles sacrifice,
they sacrifice to demons
and not to God.
And I don’t want
you to have fellowship with demons.
[21] You can’t drink the cup of the Lord
and the cup of demons at the same time.
You can’t share in the table of the Lord
and in the table of demons at the same time.
[22] Or do we want to make the Lord jealous?
Are we stronger than he is?

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 16–17: This is one of the oldest texts about the Lord’s Supper. At the Lord’s Supper, Christians share in Christ and with one another. One loaf of bread, one body: All belong together.
> The churches understand the Lord’s Supper (the Eucharist) in different ways: Catholic and Orthodox Christians believe that bread and wine become the body and blood of Christ. Lutheran Christians believe that Christ is truly present in, with and under bread and wine. Reformed Christians stress the spiritual presence of Christ. All churches say: Christ himself invites.
> Verses 20–21: Whoever belongs to Christ should not at the same time take part in pagan sacrifice feasts.
> Important: This text is not a judgment on people of other religions today. Paul is speaking about the sacrifice cults of his time in Corinth.

## Everything for the glory of God (Verses 23–33)

[23] “All things are lawful for me,”
but not all things are helpful.
“All things are lawful for me,”
but not all things build up.
[24] No one should seek only his own good,
but each one his neighbor’s good.
[25] Whatever is sold in the meat market,
eat it,
without asking questions for the sake of conscience.
[26] For “the earth is the Lord’s,
and its fullness.”
[27] If one of those who don’t believe
invites you to a meal,
and you want to go,
eat whatever is set before you,
without asking questions for the sake of conscience.
[28] But if anyone says to you,
“This was offered to idols,”
don’t eat it,
for the sake of the one
who told you,
and for the sake of conscience.
For “the earth is the Lord’s,
with all its fullness.”
[29] I mean not your own conscience,
but the other person’s conscience.
For why should my freedom
be judged by someone else’s conscience?
[30] If I eat with thankfulness,
why am I blamed
for something
I give thanks for?
[31] So whether you eat or drink,
or whatever you do:
Do all to the glory of God.
[32] Give no cause for stumbling,
whether to Jews,
or to Greeks,
or to the church of God,
[33] just as I also try to please everyone in all things.
I don’t seek my own advantage,
but the advantage of the many,
so that they may be saved.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 25–27: Christians may freely eat what is sold at the market or what hosts offer them. Everything comes from God.
> Verses 26 and 28 quote Psalm 24:1. The words at the end of verse 28 are missing in many old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verses 28–29: But if someone points it out on purpose, you should give it up out of respect for that person’s conscience.
> Verse 31: “Do all to the glory of God”: Everyday life too, eating and drinking, can be a way of worshiping God.
> Verse 32: Christians should not make anyone stumble, neither Jews nor people of other backgrounds nor their own church.
