# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 14
### Speaking clearly in worship

---

## Prophecy and other languages (Verses 1–5)

[1] Follow after love!
Eagerly desire spiritual gifts,
but most of all
that you may prophesy.
[2] For he who speaks in another language
speaks not to people,
but to God.
For no one understands him.
In the Spirit he speaks mysteries.
[3] But he who prophesies
speaks to people.
He builds them up,
encourages them
and comforts them.
[4] He who speaks in another language
builds up himself.
But he who prophesies
builds up the assembly.
[5] I would like
all of you to speak in other languages,
but even more
that you would prophesy.
For he who prophesies
is greater than he
who speaks in other languages,
unless he also interprets,
so that the assembly may be built up.

> **What does this mean?**
> “Speaking in another language” (often called *speaking in tongues*) was very popular in Corinth. People prayed in sounds that others didn’t understand.
> “Prophesy” here means: speaking clearly on God’s behalf, so that others are built up, encouraged and comforted (verse 3).
> Paul doesn’t forbid speaking in tongues. But in worship it is more important to him that everyone understands what is said.

---

## What you don’t understand doesn’t help (Verses 6–19)

[6] Brothers,
if I came to you
speaking in other languages,
what good would I do you
unless I also spoke to you
with a revelation,
or with knowledge,
or with prophecy,
or with teaching?
[7] Even lifeless things
that make a sound,
like a flute or a harp:
If they don’t give clear notes,
how would anyone know
what is played on the flute or harp?
[8] And if the trumpet gives an uncertain sound,
who will prepare himself for battle?
[9] It is the same with you:
Unless you speak words with your tongue that are easy to understand,
how will anyone know
what is said?
You would be speaking into the air.
[10] There may be very many languages in the world,
and none of them is without meaning.
[11] But if I don’t know the meaning of a language,
I will be a foreigner
to the one
who speaks it.
And the one who speaks it
will be a foreigner to me.
[12] It is the same with you.
Since you are so eager for spiritual gifts,
seek to be rich
in what builds up the assembly.
[13] So let him
who speaks in another language
pray
that he may interpret.
[14] For if I pray in another language,
my spirit prays,
but my understanding bears no fruit.
[15] What should I do then?
I will pray with the spirit,
and I will pray with the understanding also.
I will sing with the spirit,
and I will sing with the understanding also.
[16] Otherwise, if you only praise with the spirit,
how can someone
who doesn’t understand
say the “Amen” to your giving of thanks?
He doesn’t know
what you are saying.
[17] You certainly give thanks well,
but the other person is not built up.
[18] I thank my God:
I speak in other languages more than all of you.
[19] But in the assembly I would rather speak five words
with my understanding,
so that I teach others too,
than ten thousand words in another language.

*interpret* = to explain what something means

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–11: Paul uses pictures: An instrument that doesn’t give clear notes is no use. A trumpet must sound clearly, or no one knows what to do.
> Verse 15: Faith needs heart and mind.
> Verse 16: “Amen” means *So it is* or *So be it*. You can only say Amen if you understand what was prayed.
> For today: Worship should be understandable for everyone, including guests and people who can’t read or hear well. That is why plain language is important too.

## A sign for outsiders (Verses 20–25)

[20] Brothers,
don’t be children in your thinking.
Be like babies
when it comes to evil.
But in your thinking be mature.
[21] In the law it is written:
“By people of strange languages
and by the lips of strangers
I will speak to this people.
They won’t even listen to me that way,
says the Lord.”
[22] So other languages are a sign,
not for those
who believe,
but for unbelievers.
But prophecy is a sign,
not for unbelievers,
but for those
who believe.
[23] So if the whole assembly comes together
and all speak in other languages,
and outsiders or unbelievers come in,
won’t they say
that you are crazy?
[24] But if all prophesy,
and an unbeliever or outsider comes in,
he is convinced of his sin by all
and examined by all.
[25] The secrets of his heart
are revealed.
Then he will fall down on his face,
worship God
and declare:
“God is truly among you!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21 takes up Isaiah 28:11–12. “The law” here means the whole Holy Scripture of Israel.
> Verses 23–25: Paul is thinking of guests in worship. If everyone talks at once in sounds that can’t be understood, it seems strange to outsiders. But if they hear words they can understand, God can touch their heart.

## Order in worship (Verses 26–40)

[26] What does this mean then, brothers?
When you come together,
each one has something to give:
a psalm,
a teaching,
a revelation,
another language
or an interpretation.
Let everything be done
to build each other up.
[27] If anyone speaks in another language,
let there be two,
or at the most three,
one after the other.
And let one interpret.
[28] But if there is no interpreter,
let him keep silent in the assembly.
Let him speak to himself and to God.
[29] Let two or three of the prophets speak,
and let the others weigh what is said.
[30] But if a revelation comes to another
who is sitting there,
let the first one keep silent.
[31] For you can all prophesy one by one,
so that all may learn
and all may be encouraged.
[32] The spirits of the prophets
are under the control of the prophets.
[33] For God is not a God of confusion
but of peace,
as in all the assemblies of the saints.
[34] Let the wives be quiet in the assemblies.
For they are not permitted to talk,
except in submission,
as the law also says,
[35] if they want to learn anything.
“Let them ask their own husbands at home,
for it is shameful for a wife
to be talking in the assembly.”
[36] What!?
Was it from you that the word of God went out?
Or did it come only to you?
[37] If anyone thinks
he is a prophet or spiritual,
let him recognize
that the things
I write to you
are a command of the Lord.
[38] But if anyone doesn’t recognize this,
let him not recognize it.
[39] So, brothers:
Eagerly desire
to prophesy,
and don’t forbid
speaking in other languages.
[40] Let all things be done decently and in order.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 26: In worship back then, everyone could contribute something. The goal was always: building one another up.
> Verse 33: “God is not a God of confusion but of peace.” That is why worship should be orderly.
> Verses 34–35 are among the most debated verses of the Bible. It is important to know:
> In chapter 11:5 Paul takes it for granted that women pray and prophesy in worship. That doesn’t fit with a complete command to be silent.
> The WEB puts part of verse 35 in quotation marks. Some interpreters think: Paul is quoting an opinion from Corinth here and disagrees with it in verse 36 (“What!?”).
> In some old manuscripts, verses 34–35 are found in another place, after verse 40. That is why some scholars think they were added later.
> Other interpreters think: Paul only wanted to prevent disturbances in worship, such as loud questions in between.
> Some churches still understand these verses as a rule that women should not preach or hold an office. Many other churches have women pastors and preachers.
> Important: These verses give no one the right to look down on women or to deny them their dignity. Man and woman are of equal worth before God.
> Verses 39–40: Paul ends in a reconciling way: Prophecy is welcome, speaking in tongues is not forbidden, but everything should be done in order.
