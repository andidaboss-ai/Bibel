# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 1
### The message of the cross

---

## Before we begin: The first letter to the Corinthians

Corinth was a large, rich port city in Greece. People from many countries lived there. There was a lot of trade, there were many religions, and there were big differences between rich and poor.
Paul had founded the church in Corinth himself. He lived there for about a year and a half (Acts 18). Later he wrote several letters to the church. He probably wrote this letter around AD 54 or 55 in Ephesus.
The church had many problems: quarrels and groups, questions about marriage and sexuality, about food, about worship, about the gifts of the Spirit and about the resurrection. Paul answers all these questions.
The first letter to the Corinthians contains some of the best-known texts of the Bible: the “Song of Love” (chapter 13), the words about the Lord’s Supper (chapter 11) and the great chapter about the resurrection (chapter 15).
**An important note:** Some passages in this letter are about women in worship and about other questions that were much debated at that time. We translate everything exactly. In the boxes we explain how Christians understand these passages differently today.
The first letter to the Corinthians has 16 chapters.

## Greeting (Verses 1–3)

[1] Paul,
called to be an apostle of Jesus Christ
through the will of God,
and our brother Sosthenes,
[2] write to the assembly of God
which is at Corinth:
to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus,
called saints,
with all who call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ
in every place,
both theirs and ours.
[3] Grace to you and peace
from God our Father
and the Lord Jesus Christ.

*assembly* = church, the community of Christians
*sanctified* = made holy, belonging to God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Sosthenes was perhaps the leader of the synagogue in Corinth that Acts 18:17 tells about. That is not certain.
> Verse 2: “Saints” are not especially pious people, but all Christians. They belong to God.
> Verse 2: Paul writes not only to Corinth, but to all Christians in every place. The letter is meant for us too.

---

## Paul thanks God (Verses 4–9)

[4] I always thank my God for you,
for the grace of God
which was given you in Christ Jesus.
[5] For in him you were made rich in everything,
in all speech and all knowledge.
[6] So the testimony of Christ
was made firm in you.
[7] So you lack no gift,
while you wait
for the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[8] He will also make you firm until the end,
so that you are blameless
in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[9] God is faithful.
Through him you were called
into the fellowship of his Son,
Jesus Christ our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Even though there were many problems in Corinth, Paul first gives thanks for the good. He sees the gifts that God has given the church.
> Verses 7–8: The first Christians were waiting for Jesus to come again.
> Verse 9: “God is faithful.” That is the reason why Christians can hope, even when they themselves are weak.
> For today: Even in a quarrel, it is worth seeing the good in the other person first.

## Quarrels in the church (Verses 10–17)

[10] Now I beg you, brothers,
through the name of our Lord Jesus Christ:
All of you, speak the same thing.
There should be no divisions among you.
Be joined together
in the same mind
and in the same judgment.
[11] For it has been reported to me about you,
my brothers,
by those from Chloe’s household,
that there are quarrels among you.
[12] I mean this:
Each one of you says,
“I follow Paul,”
“I follow Apollos,”
“I follow Cephas,”
and, “I follow Christ.”
[13] Is Christ divided?
Was Paul crucified for you?
Or were you baptized into the name of Paul?
[14] I thank God
that I baptized none of you
except Crispus and Gaius,
[15] so that no one can say
that I baptized you into my own name.
[16] (I also baptized the household of Stephanas.
Besides them, I don’t know
whether I baptized anyone else.)
[17] For Christ didn’t send me
to baptize,
but to preach the Good News,
and not with clever words,
so that the cross of Christ doesn’t lose its power.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: Chloe was probably a woman who ran a household with workers or slaves. Her people told Paul about the quarrels.
> Verse 12: In Corinth, groups formed around well-known teachers. Apollos was a gifted preacher (Acts 18:24–28). “Cephas” is the Aramaic name of Peter.
> Verse 13: Paul asks: “Is Christ divided?” All Christians belong to the one Christ, not to a human teacher.
> Verse 14: Crispus was the leader of the synagogue in Corinth (Acts 18:8).
> Verse 17: Paul doesn’t say that baptism is unimportant. But his special task was preaching.
> For today: Today too there are many churches and groups. This text reminds us: We all belong to Christ. That is why ecumenism, the churches living and working together, is important.

## God’s wisdom and human wisdom (Verses 18–31)

[18] For the message of the cross
is foolishness
to those who are dying.
But to us
who are being saved
it is the power of God.
[19] For it is written:
“I will destroy the wisdom of the wise.
I will bring the discernment of the discerning to nothing.”
[20] Where is the wise?
Where is the scribe?
Where is the debater of this age?
Hasn’t God made foolish
the wisdom of this world?
[21] For in the wisdom of God
the world through its wisdom
didn’t know God.
So it pleased God
through the foolishness of the preaching
to save those
who believe.
[22] For Jews ask for signs,
and Greeks seek after wisdom.
[23] But we preach Christ crucified.
To Jews this is a stumbling block,
and to Greeks it is foolishness.
[24] But to those who are called,
both Jews and Greeks,
Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God.
[25] For the foolishness of God
is wiser than people.
And the weakness of God
is stronger than people.
[26] Look at your calling, brothers:
Not many of you are wise by human standards,
not many are mighty,
and not many are noble.
[27] But God chose the foolish things of the world
to put to shame those who are wise.
God chose the weak things of the world
to put to shame the things that are strong.
[28] God chose the lowly things of the world,
and the things that are despised,
and the things that don’t exist,
to bring to nothing
the things that exist,
[29] so that no one can boast before God.
[30] Because of him, you are in Christ Jesus.
He was made to us wisdom from God,
and righteousness,
and holiness,
and redemption.
[31] So it is
as it is written:
“He who boasts,
let him boast in the Lord.”

*scribe* = a teacher of the Scriptures
*redemption* = being set free

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: A crucified savior sounded like nonsense to many people at that time. Crucifixion was the worst and most shameful death penalty. Paul says: It is precisely in the cross that God’s power shows itself.
> Verse 19 quotes Isaiah 29:14. Verse 31 takes up Jeremiah 9:24 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 9:23).
> Verse 21: “In the wisdom of God”: God in his wisdom arranged it this way. People cannot find God by their own reason alone. God shows himself, in the message about Jesus.
> Verses 22–24: Paul describes why the message of the cross was hard to accept for many people of his time, for Jews as well as for Greeks. He himself is a Jew. He is speaking about a question of faith, not speaking badly about a people. And he says clearly: Many Jews also belong to those who are called (verse 24).
> Verses 26–28: The church in Corinth was made up mainly of ordinary people, of the poor and of slaves. God chooses precisely the weak and the despised.
> For today: With God, education, money and reputation don’t count. Every person is valuable.
