# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 12
### Many gifts, one body

---

## One Spirit, many gifts (Verses 1–11)

[1] Brothers, now I will write about spiritual gifts.
I want you to understand them.
[2] You know that you were once heathen.
Heathen are people who worship many gods.
Back then, you were led away to idols that cannot speak.
You let yourselves be led in any direction.
[3] So I want you to know this:
No one who speaks by God’s Spirit says, “Jesus is cursed.”
And no one can say, “Jesus is Lord,”
unless the Holy Spirit helps him.
[4] There are different kinds of gifts,
but there is one and the same Spirit.
[5] There are different kinds of service,
and there is one and the same Lord.
[6] There are different ways in which God works,
but it is the same God who does all things in all people.
[7] Each person is given a way to show the Spirit,
for the good of all.
[8] Through the Spirit, one person is given words of wisdom.
The same Spirit gives another person words of knowledge.
[9] The same Spirit gives another person faith.
The same Spirit gives another person gifts of healing.
[10] Another person can work miracles.
Another can prophesy.
Another can tell which spirits are from God and which are not.
Another can speak different kinds of languages.
Another can explain what those languages mean.
[11] The one and the same Spirit does all these things.
He gives gifts to each person,
just as he wants.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: The Holy Spirit leads people to confess Jesus as Lord. Whoever does that has the Spirit, even without special miraculous gifts.
> Verses 4–6: Here Spirit, Lord (Jesus) and God are named together. Christians see in this an early hint of the triune God.
> Verse 7: Every person in the church has a gift. The gifts are not there for yourself, but “for the good of all.”
> Verse 10: “Different kinds of languages”: This means *speaking in tongues*, praying in sounds that others don’t understand. This gift was very popular in Corinth. Paul writes about it in detail in chapter 14.
> Verse 9: “Gifts of healing”: God can heal. But whoever stays ill is not less faithful. Medical help is also a gift from God.

## One body, many parts (Verses 12–31)

[12] The body is one,
but it has many parts.
All the parts of the body are many,
but together they are one body.
It is the same with Christ.
[13] We were all baptized by one Spirit into one body,
whether we are Jews or Greeks,
whether we are slaves or free.
And we were all given the one Spirit to drink.
[14] The body is not made of one part,
but of many parts.
[15] The foot might say,
“I am not a hand, so I am not part of the body.”
But it is still part of the body.
[16] The ear might say,
“I am not an eye, so I am not part of the body.”
But it is still part of the body.
[17] If the whole body were an eye,
how could it hear?
If the whole body were an ear,
how could it smell?
[18] But God has put each part in the body,
just as he wanted.
[19] If all were only one part,
where would the body be?
[20] But there are many parts,
and only one body.
[21] The eye cannot say to the hand,
“I have no need for you.”
And the head cannot say to the feet,
“I have no need for you.”
[22] No, it is just the other way around.
The parts of the body that seem weaker are necessary.
[23] We give more honor to the parts of the body
that we think are less honorable.
And we cover the parts that we do not show
with special care.
[24] The parts that we show do not need this.
But God put the body together.
He gave more honor to the parts that lacked it.
[25] So there should be no division in the body.
All the parts should care for one another in the same way.
[26] When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it.
When one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it.
A member here means: a part of the body.
[27] You are the body of Christ.
And each one of you is a part of it.
[28] God has put some people in the church:
first apostles,
second prophets,
third teachers,
then people who work miracles,
then gifts of healing,
helping others,
leading,
and different kinds of languages.
[29] Are all apostles?
Are all prophets?
Are all teachers?
Do all work miracles?
[30] Do all have gifts of healing?
Do all speak in different languages?
Do all explain what they mean?
[31] But eagerly desire the best gifts.
And now I will show you a most excellent way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–27: The picture of the body was well known at that time. Paul uses it like this: The church is like a body. Every part is important, and all need one another.
> Verse 13: In the church, differences of background and social position no longer count: Jews and Greeks, slaves and free people belong to the one body.
> Verses 15–16: No one should think that they are not important.
> Verses 21–22: And no one should say to another: “I have no need for you.” Especially those who seem weak are needed.
> For today: People with disabilities, old, sick and poor people belong as a matter of course. A church in which they are missing is not complete.
> Verse 26: “When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it.” Compassion is part of what the church is.
> Verse 31: The “most excellent way” is love. The next chapter is about that.
