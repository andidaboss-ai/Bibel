# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 12
### Many gifts, one body

---

## One Spirit, many gifts (Verses 1–11)

[1] Now about spiritual gifts, brothers,
I don’t want you to be ignorant.
[2] You know:
When you were still heathen,
you were led away to those mute idols,
however you were led.
[3] So I make known to you:
No one
who speaks by God’s Spirit
says, “Jesus is accursed.”
And no one can say,
“Jesus is Lord,”
except by the Holy Spirit.
[4] There are different kinds of gifts,
but the same Spirit.
[5] There are different kinds of service,
and the same Lord.
[6] There are different kinds of workings,
but the same God
who works all things in all.
[7] But to each one the Spirit is shown
for the good of all.
[8] To one is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom,
to another the word of knowledge
by the same Spirit,
[9] to another faith
by the same Spirit,
to another gifts of healing
by the same Spirit,
[10] to another the power
to work miracles,
to another prophecy,
to another the ability
to tell spirits apart,
to another different kinds of languages,
to another the interpretation of languages.
[11] But the one and the same Spirit does all of these.
He gives to each one separately
as he wants.

*heathen* = people who worship many gods
*accursed* = cursed, under a curse

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: The Holy Spirit leads people to confess Jesus as Lord. Whoever does that has the Spirit, even without special miraculous gifts.
> Verses 4–6: Here Spirit, Lord (Jesus) and God are named together. Christians see in this an early hint of the triune God.
> Verse 7: Every person in the church has a gift. The gifts are not there for yourself, but “for the good of all.”
> Verse 10: “Different kinds of languages”: This means *speaking in tongues*, praying in sounds that others don’t understand. This gift was very popular in Corinth. Paul writes about it in detail in chapter 14.
> Verse 9: “Gifts of healing”: God can heal. But whoever stays ill is not less faithful. Medical help is also a gift from God.

## One body, many parts (Verses 12–31)

[12] For as the body is one
and has many members,
and all the members of the body,
though they are many,
are one body,
so also is Christ.
[13] For in one Spirit
we were all baptized into one body,
whether Jews or Greeks,
whether slaves or free.
And we were all given one Spirit to drink.
[14] For the body is not one member,
but many.
[15] If the foot would say,
“Because I’m not the hand,
I’m not part of the body,”
it is still part of the body.
[16] If the ear would say,
“Because I’m not the eye,
I’m not part of the body,”
it is still part of the body.
[17] If the whole body were an eye,
where would the hearing be?
If the whole body were hearing,
where would the smelling be?
[18] But now God has set the members in the body,
each one of them,
just as he wanted.
[19] If they were all one member,
where would the body be?
[20] But now they are many members,
but one body.
[21] The eye can’t say to the hand,
“I have no need for you.”
And the head can’t say to the feet,
“I have no need for you.”
[22] No,
the members of the body
which seem to be weaker
are necessary.
[23] And the parts of the body
which we think are less honorable,
we treat with greater honor.
And our unpresentable parts
we treat with greater modesty.
[24] Our presentable parts have no such need.
But God put the body together
and gave greater honor to the part that lacked it,
[25] so that there should be no division in the body,
but that the members should have the same care for one another.
[26] When one member suffers,
all the members suffer with it.
When one member is honored,
all the members rejoice with it.
[27] Now you are the body of Christ,
and each one of you is a member of it.
[28] God has set some in the assembly:
first apostles,
second prophets,
third teachers,
then miracle workers,
then gifts of healing,
helping,
leading,
and different kinds of languages.
[29] Are all apostles?
Are all prophets?
Are all teachers?
Are all miracle workers?
[30] Do all have gifts of healing?
Do all speak in different languages?
Do all interpret?
[31] But eagerly desire the greater gifts.
And I will show you a most excellent way.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–27: The picture of the body was well known at that time. Paul uses it like this: The church is like a body. Every part is important, and all need one another.
> Verse 13: In the church, differences of background and social position no longer count: Jews and Greeks, slaves and free people belong to the one body.
> Verses 15–16: No one should think that they are not important.
> Verses 21–22: And no one should say to another: “I have no need for you.” Precisely those who seem weak cannot be done without.
> For today: People with disabilities, old, sick and poor people belong as a matter of course. A church in which they are missing is not complete.
> Verse 26: “When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it.” Compassion is part of what the church is.
> Verse 31: The “most excellent way” is love. The next chapter is about that.
