# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 16
### Plans and greetings

---

## The collection for Jerusalem (Verses 1–4)

[1] Now about the collection for God’s holy people.
A collection is money that is gathered for a good purpose.
Do what I told the churches in Galatia to do.
[2] On the first day of every week,
each of you should put some money aside,
as much as he is able.
Then no collections will have to be made when I come.
[3] When I arrive, I will send the people you choose.
I will give them letters.
They will take your gift to Jerusalem.
[4] If it seems right for me to go too,
they will go with me.

> **What does this mean?**
> The church in Jerusalem was poor. Paul collected money for it in many churches (compare Romans 15:25–27).
> Verse 2: “On the first day of every week”: This means Sunday, the day of Jesus’ resurrection. Even then Christians met on this day.
> Everyone should give “as much as he is able.” No one should be overburdened.
> Verse 3: Paul is honest with money. Representatives of the church are to bring it to Jerusalem themselves.

---

## Travel plans (Verses 5–12)

[5] I will come to you after I have traveled through Macedonia.
I am going to travel through Macedonia.
[6] Perhaps I will stay with you for a while,
or even spend the winter with you.
Then you can help me on my journey,
wherever I go next.
[7] This time I do not want to see you only in passing.
I hope to stay with you for a while,
if the Lord allows it.
[8] But I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost.
[9] A great and effective door has opened to me.
And there are many people against me.
[10] Timothy may come to you.
If he does, make sure that he has nothing to fear while he is with you.
He is doing the Lord’s work, just as I am.
[11] So no one should look down on him.
Help him on his way in peace,
so that he can come back to me.
I am waiting for him and the brothers.
[12] Now about our brother Apollos:
I strongly urged him to visit you with the brothers.
But he did not want to come now at all.
He will come when he has the chance.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: Paul writes this letter in Ephesus, a large city in what is now Turkey. At that time “Pentecost” was the Jewish Feast of Weeks, 50 days after Passover.
> Verse 9: “A great and effective door has opened to me.” That means: There is a good opportunity to proclaim the Good News.
> Verses 10–11: Timothy was still young. Paul asks the church to welcome him kindly.
> Verse 12: Apollos was very popular in Corinth (1 Corinthians 1:12). Paul and Apollos were not opponents, but co-workers.

---

## Last words of advice (Verses 13–18)

[13] Stay alert!
Stand firm in the faith!
Be brave!
Be strong!
[14] Let all that you do be done in love.
[15] Brothers, I ask you something.
You know the family of Stephanas.
They were the first people in Achaia to believe.
And they have given themselves to serving God’s holy people.
[16] Follow the lead of such people,
and of everyone who works and labors hard with them.
[17] I am happy that Stephanas, Fortunatus and Achaicus have come.
They have made up for what I was missing from you.
[18] They have refreshed my spirit and yours.
So value people like that.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Let all that you do be done in love.” This sums up the whole letter.
> Verses 15–16: Whoever serves faithfully in the church deserves respect.
> Verse 17: Stephanas, Fortunatus and Achaicus probably brought Paul the Corinthians’ letter (compare 7:1).

---

## Greetings and blessing (Verses 19–24)

[19] The churches of Asia send you greetings.
Aquila and Priscilla and the church that meets in their house
send you warm greetings in the Lord.
[20] All the brothers send you greetings.
Greet one another with a holy kiss.
[21] I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand.
[22] If anyone does not love the Lord Jesus Christ,
let him be cursed.
Come, Lord!
[23] May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with you.
[24] My love to all of you in Christ Jesus.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Aquila and Priscilla knew the Corinthians well. They had lived there with Paul (Acts 18:2–3).
> Verse 21: Paul dictated the letter. He writes the last greeting himself, as a sign that the letter is genuine.
> Verse 22: “Come, Lord!” is *Maranatha* in Aramaic. It was a prayer of the first Christians: They asked Jesus to come again soon.
> Verse 22: “Let him be cursed” is a fixed formula. It leaves the judgment to God. It is no permission to curse people, shut them out or harm them.
> Verse 24: After all the serious words, Paul ends with love.

## Looking back: What have we read in the first letter to the Corinthians?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A church with many problems:** In Corinth there were quarrels, groups, pride and questions about the right way to live as a Christian.
> **The message of the cross:** God shows his power precisely in weakness, in the crucified Christ (chapters 1 to 4).
> **Living with the body:** The body is a temple of the Holy Spirit. Paul speaks about sexuality, marriage and staying single (chapters 5 to 7).
> **Freedom and care for others:** Christians are free. But they should not use their freedom against those who are weaker (chapters 8 to 10).
> **Worship and the Lord’s Supper:** At the Lord’s Supper everyone should belong, especially the poor (chapter 11).
> **Many gifts, one body:** Everyone in the church is important (chapter 12). Love is greater than all gifts (chapter 13). Worship should be understandable (chapter 14).
> **The resurrection:** Christ is risen. Death is defeated (chapter 15).
> **For today:** “Let all that you do be done in love” (16:14).
> **What comes next?** Next comes Paul’s second letter to the Corinthians. In it Paul shares many personal things: about suffering and comfort, about weakness and God’s power. A famous word from it says, in short: God’s power shows itself precisely in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9).
