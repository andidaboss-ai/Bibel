# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 5
### A serious case in the church

---

## A man lives with his father’s wife (Verses 1–5)

[1] It is actually reported
that there is sexual immorality among you,
and such sexual immorality
as is not even found among the Gentiles:
one has his father’s wife.
[2] And you are arrogant!
You should rather have mourned,
so that he
who had done this deed
might be removed from among you.
[3] For I am absent in body,
but present in spirit.
And I have already judged him
who has done this thing,
as though I were present.
[4] In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,
when you are gathered together
and my spirit is with you,
with the power of our Lord Jesus Christ,
[5] you are to hand such a one over to Satan
for the destruction of the flesh,
so that the spirit may be saved
in the day of the Lord Jesus.

*Gentiles* = people who are not Jews

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “One has his father’s wife.” This probably means his stepmother. Such a relationship was forbidden in the law of Israel (Leviticus 18:8) and also under Roman law.
> Verse 2: Paul scolds not only the man, but above all the church. It was proud of its “freedom” and looked away.
> Verse 5: “Hand such a one over to Satan” is a difficult phrase. It probably means: The man is to be excluded from the fellowship of the church. Outside, he should notice what he has lost. The goal is not his destruction, but his rescue: “so that the spirit may be saved.”
> Important: This verse is no permission to use violence against people, to curse them or to harm them. The goal is turning back and rescue.

## A little yeast (Verses 6–8)

[6] Your boasting is not good.
Don’t you know
that a little yeast
makes the whole lump of dough rise?
[7] Clean out the old yeast,
so that you may be a new lump,
as you really are unleavened.
For Christ, our Passover lamb,
has been sacrificed in our place.
[8] So let’s keep the feast,
not with old yeast,
nor with the yeast of malice and wickedness,
but with the unleavened bread
of sincerity and truth.

*unleavened* = without yeast

> **What does this mean?**
> Before the Passover feast, Jewish families still remove all yeast from the house today. Then they eat unleavened bread (Exodus 12:15–20).
> Paul uses this picture: Just as a little yeast goes through the whole dough, a bad example can change the whole church.
> Verse 7: Paul compares Jesus with the Passover lamb. Jesus died at the time of the Passover feast.
> Verse 7: The WEB says “in our place.” These words are missing in many old manuscripts.

## Who belongs to the church? (Verses 9–13)

[9] I wrote to you in my letter
that you should have no company
with people who live in sexual immorality.
[10] But I didn’t mean
the sexually immoral people of this world in general,
or the greedy and those who rob others,
or those who worship idols.
For then you would have to leave the world.
[11] But I wrote to you:
Don’t associate with anyone
who is called a brother
and lives in sexual immorality,
or is greedy,
or worships idols,
or slanders others,
or is a drunkard,
or robs others.
Don’t even eat with such a person.
[12] For what do I have to do
with judging those who are outside?
Don’t you judge those
who are inside?
[13] But those who are outside, God judges.
“Put away the wicked man from among yourselves.”

*idol* = a statue or image that people worship as a god
*slander* = to tell lies about others

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Paul had already written an earlier letter to the Corinthians. This letter has not survived.
> Verses 10–12: Christians should not cut themselves off from the world. They should not judge people outside the church. But inside the church they should look out for one another.
> Verse 13 takes up a saying that comes often in Deuteronomy (for example Deuteronomy 17:7 and 22:24).
> Important: How churches deal with serious wrong is a serious question today too. Sadly, churches have often protected offenders and left victims alone, for example in cases of sexual abuse. This text shows: Looking away is wrong. If you have experienced abuse, you can get help. In the US, call RAINN at 1-800-656-4673. In the UK, call NAPAC at 0808 801 0331.
> In the second letter to the Corinthians, Paul writes that a person who is sorry should be forgiven and comforted again (2 Corinthians 2:5–11). Some interpreters think it is about the same case there.
