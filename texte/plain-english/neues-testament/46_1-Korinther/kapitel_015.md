# 1 Corinthians – Chapter 15
### Christ is risen

---

## The Good News of the resurrection (Verses 1–11)

[1] Brothers,
I remind you of the Good News
which I preached to you.
You received it,
and you stand firm in it.
[2] Through it you are also saved,
if you hold firmly to the word
which I preached to you.
Otherwise you believed in vain.
[3] For first of all I passed on to you
what I also received:
Christ died for our sins
according to the Scriptures.
[4] He was buried.
He was raised on the third day
according to the Scriptures.
[5] He appeared to Cephas,
then to the twelve.
[6] Then he appeared to over five hundred brothers at once.
Most of them are still alive,
but some have fallen asleep.
[7] Then he appeared to James,
then to all the apostles.
[8] Last of all
he appeared to me also,
as to one born at the wrong time.
[9] For I am the least of the apostles.
I am not worthy
to be called an apostle,
because I persecuted the assembly of God.
[10] But by the grace of God I am
what I am.
And his grace,
which was given to me,
was not in vain.
I worked harder than all of them,
yet not I,
but the grace of God
which was with me.
[11] Whether it is I or they:
This is what we preach,
and this is what you believed.

*resurrection* = rising from the dead to new life

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 3–5 are one of the oldest confessions of faith of the Christians. Paul himself had received it, probably only a few years after Jesus’ death.
> The core sentences are: Christ died, he was buried, he was raised, he appeared.
> Verse 5: “Cephas” is Peter.
> Verse 6: Paul says: Many witnesses are still alive. You can ask them.
> Verse 7: James was a brother of Jesus and later a leader of the church in Jerusalem.
> Verse 8: “As to one born at the wrong time”: Paul feels like someone who became an apostle late and unexpectedly.
> Verse 10: “By the grace of God I am what I am.” Paul once persecuted Christians. God called him anyway.

---

## If Christ had not been raised (Verses 12–19)

[12] But if it is preached
that Christ has been raised from the dead,
how can some of you say:
“There is no resurrection of the dead”?
[13] If there is no resurrection of the dead,
then Christ has not been raised either.
[14] But if Christ has not been raised,
then our preaching is empty,
and your faith is empty too.
[15] Then we are even found to be false witnesses of God.
For we testified about God
that he raised Christ.
But he did not raise him
if it is true
that the dead are not raised.
[16] For if the dead are not raised,
then Christ has not been raised either.
[17] But if Christ has not been raised,
then your faith is useless.
Then you are still in your sins.
[18] Then those also are lost
who have fallen asleep in Christ.
[19] If we have hoped in Christ only for this life,
then we are the most pitiable of all people.

> **What does this mean?**
> In Corinth there were Christians who said: “There is no resurrection of the dead.” Perhaps they thought that only the soul lives on, or that they had already received everything now.
> Paul answers very clearly: The resurrection of Jesus is the center of the Christian faith. Without it, faith would be empty.
> Verse 18: “Fallen asleep” is a word for having died. It sounds like hope: Whoever sleeps will wake up again.

---

## Christ is the first (Verses 20–28)

[20] But now Christ has been raised from the dead.
He became the first fruit of those
who are asleep.
[21] For since death came through a man,
the resurrection of the dead also came through a man.
[22] For as in Adam all die,
so also in Christ all will be made alive.
[23] But each in his own order:
Christ the first fruits,
then, at his coming, those
who belong to Christ.
[24] Then the end comes,
when he hands over the Kingdom to God the Father,
when he has done away with all rule
and all authority and power.
[25] For he must reign
until he has put all his enemies under his feet.
[26] The last enemy
that will be done away with
is death.
[27] For: “He put all things in subjection under his feet.”
But when it says,
“All things are put in subjection,”
it is clear
that he is excepted
who put all things in subjection to him.
[28] When all things have been put in subjection to him,
then the Son himself will also be subject to him
who put all things in subjection to him,
so that God may be all in all.

*first fruit* = the first part of the harvest

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: The WEB says “first fruit,” the first fruit of the harvest. The first fruit is the sign that the whole harvest is coming. In the same way, the resurrection of Jesus is the sign that we will also rise.
> Verses 21–22: Adam stands for the human being who dies. Christ stands for the new human being who lives.
> Verse 26: “The last enemy that will be done away with is death.” In the end God will defeat death too.
> Verse 25 recalls Psalm 110:1. Verse 27 quotes Psalm 8:6 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 8:7).
> Verse 28: “So that God may be all in all”: In the end God will fill everything completely.

## Why we keep going anyway (Verses 29–34)

[29] Otherwise, what will they do
who are baptized for the dead?
If the dead aren’t raised at all,
why then are they baptized for them?
[30] And why are we in danger every hour?
[31] I assure you,
as surely as I can boast about you in Christ Jesus
our Lord:
I die daily.
[32] If I fought with wild animals at Ephesus
only for human reasons,
what does it profit me?
If the dead are not raised,
then:
“Let’s eat and drink,
for tomorrow we die.”
[33] Don’t be deceived!
“Bad company corrupts good morals.”
[34] Wake up and live rightly, and don’t sin.
For some have no knowledge of God.
I say this
to make you ashamed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 29: “Baptized for the dead”: What this means is no longer known for certain today. There are more than 40 different explanations. Paul only mentions the custom, without praising it or blaming it.
> Verse 31: “I die daily”: Paul is constantly in danger of his life.
> Verse 32: “Fought with wild animals” is perhaps a picture for fierce enemies in Ephesus. The quotation comes from Isaiah 22:13.
> Verse 33 is a well-known saying of a Greek poet (Menander).

## How will the dead be raised? (Verses 35–49)

[35] But someone will ask:
“How are the dead raised?
And with what kind of body do they come?”
[36] You foolish one!
What you sow
is not made alive
unless it dies first.
[37] And what you sow
is not the body
that will grow later,
but a bare grain,
maybe of wheat or of some other kind.
[38] But God gives it a body
as he wants,
and to each seed a body of its own.
[39] Not all flesh is the same.
There is one flesh of people,
another flesh of animals,
another of fish,
and another of birds.
[40] There are heavenly bodies and earthly bodies.
But the glory of the heavenly is different
from that of the earthly.
[41] The sun has its own glory,
the moon has another glory,
and the stars have yet another glory.
For one star differs from another star in glory.
[42] So also is the resurrection of the dead.
What is sown
is perishable.
What is raised
is imperishable.
[43] What is sown
is without honor.
What is raised
is full of glory.
What is sown
is weak.
What is raised
is full of power.
[44] A natural body is sown.
A spiritual body is raised.
If there is a natural body,
there is also a spiritual body.
[45] So also it is written:
“The first man Adam
became a living soul.”
The last Adam became a spirit
who gives life.
[46] But the spiritual is not first,
but the natural,
then the spiritual.
[47] The first man is of the earth,
made of dust.
The second man is the Lord from heaven.
[48] As the one made of dust is,
so are those
who are made of dust.
And as the heavenly one is,
so are those who are heavenly.
[49] As we have carried the image of the one
made of dust,
so let’s also bear the image of the heavenly one.

*perishable* = something that decays and comes to an end
*imperishable* = something that never decays

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 36–38: Paul compares the resurrection with a seed. You put a small grain into the ground. A completely new plant grows from it. In the same way our body will be changed.
> Verses 42–44: The new body is imperishable, full of power and glory. We cannot know exactly what it will look like.
> Verse 45 quotes Genesis 2:7. The WEB says “living soul” there, a living being. “The last Adam” means Christ.
> Verse 49: The WEB says “let’s also bear.” Many old manuscripts say: *we will also bear*.
> For today: People with illness or disability may hope too: God will make everything new.

## Death is defeated (Verses 50–58)

[50] But I say this, brothers:
Flesh and blood can’t inherit God’s Kingdom.
And the perishable doesn’t inherit the imperishable.
[51] Listen,
I tell you a mystery:
We will not all sleep,
but we will all be changed,
[52] suddenly,
in the twinkling of an eye,
at the last trumpet.
For the trumpet will sound,
and the dead will be raised imperishable,
and we will be changed.
[53] For this perishable body
must become imperishable,
and this mortal body
must put on immortality.
[54] But when this perishable body
has become imperishable,
and this mortal body
has put on immortality,
then what is written will happen:
“Death is swallowed up in victory.”
[55] “Death, where is your sting?
Hades, where is your victory?”
[56] The sting of death is sin.
And the power of sin is the law.
[57] But thanks be to God,
who gives us the victory
through our Lord Jesus Christ.
[58] So, my beloved brothers:
Be steadfast and don’t let anything move you.
Always do more and more in the Lord’s work.
For you know:
Your work is not in vain in the Lord.

*mortal* = something that must die
*immortality* = never dying

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 51–52: Paul expects that Jesus will come again soon. Then the dead will rise, and the living will be changed.
> Verse 54 takes up Isaiah 25:8. Verse 55 takes up Hosea 13:14.
> Verse 55: The WEB says “Hades.” That is the Greek word for the realm of the dead.
> Verse 56: Sin gives death its power. Paul explains this in more detail in the letter to the Romans (Romans 7).
> Verses 55–57: These verses are often read at funerals. They say: Death does not have the last word. Through Jesus it has been defeated.
> Verse 58: Nothing we do in faith and love is in vain.
> For today: If you are grieving for someone, you may hope. Help in grief is available from grief support groups and pastors. In a crisis: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
