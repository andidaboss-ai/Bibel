# Revelation – Chapter 21
### The new heaven and the new earth

---

## God makes all things new (Verses 1–8)

[1] I saw a new heaven
and a new earth.
For the first heaven and the first earth
have passed away.
And the sea is no more.
[2] I saw the holy city,
New Jerusalem,
coming down out of heaven from God.
It was prepared like a bride
adorned for her husband.
[3] I heard a loud voice out of heaven
saying,
“Look,
God’s dwelling is with people!
He will dwell with them,
and they will be his people.
And God himself will be with them
as their God.
[4] He will wipe away every tear from their eyes.
Death will be no more.
There will be no more mourning, nor crying, nor pain
any more.
The first things have passed away.”
[5] He who sits on the throne
said,
“Look,
I am making all things new.”
And he said,
“Write,
for these words of God are faithful and true.”
[6] He said to me,
“I am the Alpha and the Omega,
the Beginning and the End.
To him who is thirsty
I will give freely
from the spring of the water of life.
[7] He who overcomes,
I will give him these things.
I will be his God,
and he will be my son.
[8] But the cowardly and unbelieving,
the sinners and abominable,
the murderers and sexually immoral,
the sorcerers and idolaters
and all liars:
Their part is in the lake
that burns with fire and sulfur.
That is the second death.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This is the great goal of the whole Bible. The Bible begins with the creation of heaven and earth (Genesis 1). It ends with a new heaven and a new earth. The promise is already in Isaiah (Isaiah 65:17).
> “The sea is no more”: The sea was a picture for chaos and danger. The beast came out of the sea (13:1). In God’s new world there is nothing threatening any more.
> Verse 2: The New Jerusalem comes down from heaven to earth. God doesn’t take people away from the earth. He comes to them.
> Verse 3: This is the goal of God’s whole story with people: God lives with them. He already promised this to Israel (for example Leviticus 26:11–12 and Ezekiel 37:27).
> Verse 4 is one of the most comforting sentences in the Bible: God wipes away every tear. No more death, no mourning, no pain. Whoever is grieving may hold on to this hope.
> Verse 5: “I am making all things new”: God doesn’t throw the world away. He makes it new.
> Verse 6: “Freely”: You can’t buy the water of life. God gives it as a gift.

> **Important note**
> Verse 8 is a warning, not a verdict on individual people. It is interesting: “The cowardly” come first. This means those who gave up their faith out of fear and took part in emperor worship. It is a call to courage.
> None of us is without guilt. Whoever looks at this list and finds himself in it may still come to God. Verse 6 says: Whoever is thirsty receives freely. God forgives (compare 1 John 1:9).

## The holy city (Verses 9–21)

[9] One of the seven angels
who had the seven bowls with the seven last plagues
came and spoke with me.
He said,
“Come here!
I will show you the bride,
the Lamb’s wife.”
[10] He carried me away in the Spirit
to a great and high mountain.
And he showed me the holy city,
Jerusalem,
coming down out of heaven from God.
[11] It had the glory of God.
Its light was like a most precious stone,
like a jasper stone,
clear as crystal.
[12] It had a great and high wall
with twelve gates.
At the gates were twelve angels.
And names were written on the gates:
the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel.
[13] On the east were three gates,
on the north three gates,
on the south three gates
and on the west three gates.
[14] The wall of the city had twelve foundations.
And on them were the twelve names
of the twelve Apostles of the Lamb.
[15] He who spoke with me
had a golden reed as a measuring rod,
to measure the city, its gates and its walls.
[16] The city is square.
Its length is as great as its width.
He measured the city with the reed:
twelve thousand twelve stadia.
Its length, width and height are equal.
[17] Its wall is one hundred forty-four cubits,
by the measure of a man,
which is also the measure of the angel.
[18] Its wall was built of jasper.
The city was pure gold,
like pure glass.
[19] The foundations of the city’s wall
were adorned with all kinds of precious stones.
The first foundation was jasper,
the second sapphire,
the third chalcedony,
the fourth emerald,
[20] the fifth sardonyx,
the sixth sardius,
the seventh chrysolite,
the eighth beryl,
the ninth topaz,
the tenth chrysoprase,
the eleventh jacinth
and the twelfth amethyst.
[21] The twelve gates were twelve pearls.
Each one of the gates was made of one pearl.
And the street of the city was pure gold,
like transparent glass.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–14: On the gates are the names of the twelve tribes of Israel. On the foundations are the names of the twelve apostles. Israel and the church both belong to God’s city. Israel has not been replaced. God’s people are Israel and the church together.
> Verse 13: There are gates in every direction. People from the whole world are welcome.
> Verse 16: The city is a cube. The Most Holy Place in the temple was also a cube (1 Kings 6:20). The whole city is now like the Most Holy Place: God is very near everywhere.
> 12,000 stadia are about 1,400 miles (2,200 kilometers). The WEB has “twelve thousand twelve” here. Most manuscripts have “twelve thousand.” The number means: unimaginably large, room for everyone.
> Verse 17: A cubit is about 18 inches. 144 cubits are about 216 feet. 144 is 12 times 12, again the number of the whole people of God.
> Verses 19–20: The precious stones recall the twelve stones on the breastpiece of the high priest (Exodus 28:17–21). The names of some stones are no longer completely certain today.
> Verse 21: Each gate is made of a single huge pearl. Here gold is as common as paving stones. What counts most on earth is completely ordinary in God’s world.

## God and the Lamb are its light (Verses 22–27)

[22] I saw no temple in the city.
For the Lord God,
the Almighty,
and the Lamb are its temple.
[23] The city has no need for the sun or moon
to shine on it.
For the very glory of God lights it up,
and its lamp is the Lamb.
[24] The nations will walk in its light.
And the kings of the earth
bring the glory and honor of the nations into it.
[25] Its gates will in no way be shut by day,
for there will be no night there.
[26] And they will bring the glory and the honor of the nations
into it,
so that they may enter.
[27] But nothing profane will in any way enter into it,
nor anyone who causes an abomination or a lie,
but only those
who are written in the Lamb’s book of life.
Profane means: unholy.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: There is no more temple. You don’t need a special place any more to meet God. God is directly present everywhere.
> Verse 23 recalls Isaiah 60:19–20.
> Verses 24–26: The nations and kings come into the city and bring their treasures with them. The good from all cultures finds a place in God’s new world (compare Isaiah 60:3–11).
> Verse 25: The gates are always open. There is no more danger. God’s city is open and welcoming.
