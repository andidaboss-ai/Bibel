# Revelation – Chapter 21
### The new heaven and the new earth

---

## God makes all things new (Verses 1–8)

[1] I saw a new heaven and a new earth.
For the first heaven and the first earth had passed away.
And the sea is no more.
[2] I saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God.
It was made ready like a bride who is dressed beautifully for her husband.
[3] I heard a loud voice from heaven say,
“Look! God’s home is with people.
He will live with them.
They will be his people.
God himself will be with them as their God.
[4] He will wipe away every tear from their eyes.
There will be no more death.
There will be no more grief, no more crying, and no more pain.
The first things have passed away.”
[5] The one who sits on the throne said,
“Look, I am making all things new.”
He said,
“Write this down,
for these words of God are trustworthy and true.”
[6] He said to me,
“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End.
I will give water from the spring of the water of life
as a free gift to anyone who is thirsty.
[7] Whoever wins the victory will receive these things.
I will be his God, and he will be my son.
[8] But there is a place for the cowardly, the unbelieving, the sinners,
and those who do disgusting things.
It is also for murderers, the sexually immoral, those who practice magic,
those who worship idols, and all liars.
Their place is in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur.
That is the second death.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This is the great goal of the whole Bible. The Bible begins with the creation of heaven and earth (Genesis 1). It ends with a new heaven and a new earth. The promise is already in Isaiah (Isaiah 65:17).
> “The sea is no more”: The sea was a picture for chaos and danger. The beast came out of the sea (13:1). In God’s new world there is nothing threatening any more.
> Verse 2: The New Jerusalem comes down from heaven to earth. God doesn’t take people away from the earth. He comes to them.
> Verse 3: This is the goal of God’s whole story with people: God lives with them. He already promised this to Israel (for example Leviticus 26:11–12 and Ezekiel 37:27).
> Verse 4 is one of the most comforting sentences in the Bible: God wipes away every tear. No more death, no mourning, no pain. Whoever is grieving may hold on to this hope.
> Verse 5: “I am making all things new”: God doesn’t throw the world away. He makes it new.
> Verse 6: Alpha is the first letter of the Greek alphabet, and Omega is the last. God is the beginning and the end of everything.
> “As a free gift”: You can’t buy the water of life. God gives it as a gift.

> **Important note**
> Verse 8 is a warning, not a verdict on individual people. It is interesting: “The cowardly” come first. This means those who gave up their faith out of fear and took part in emperor worship. It is a call to courage.
> None of us is without guilt. Whoever looks at this list and finds himself in it may still come to God. Verse 6 says: Whoever is thirsty receives it as a free gift. God forgives (compare 1 John 1:9).

## The holy city (Verses 9–21)

[9] One of the seven angels came and spoke with me.
He was one of the angels who had the seven bowls full of the seven last plagues.
He said,
“Come here!
I will show you the bride, the Lamb’s wife.”
[10] Then the Spirit came over me,
and the angel carried me away to a great, high mountain.
He showed me the holy city, Jerusalem,
coming down out of heaven from God.
[11] It had the glory of God.
Its light was like a very precious stone,
like a jasper stone, clear as crystal.
[12] It had a great, high wall with 12 gates.
At the gates were 12 angels.
Names were written on the gates.
They are the names of the 12 tribes of the people of Israel.
[13] There were three gates on the east,
three gates on the north,
three gates on the south,
and three gates on the west.
[14] The wall of the city had 12 foundation stones.
On them were the 12 names of the 12 Apostles of the Lamb.
[15] The angel who spoke with me had a golden measuring stick.
He was to measure the city, its gates, and its walls.
[16] The city is square.
It is as long as it is wide.
He measured the city with the measuring stick:
It was about 1,400 miles.
Its length, its width, and its height are the same.
[17] Its wall is about 216 feet.
That is by a human measure, which the angel was using too.
[18] The wall was built of jasper.
The city was pure gold, like clear glass.
[19] The foundation stones of the city wall were decorated with all kinds of precious stones.
The first foundation stone was jasper,
the second sapphire,
the third chalcedony,
the fourth emerald,
[20] the fifth sardonyx,
the sixth sardius,
the seventh chrysolite,
the eighth beryl,
the ninth topaz,
the tenth chrysoprase,
the eleventh jacinth,
and the twelfth amethyst.
[21] The 12 gates were 12 pearls.
Each gate was made of a single pearl.
The street of the city was pure gold, like glass you can see through.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 12–14: On the gates are the names of the 12 tribes of Israel. On the foundations are the names of the 12 apostles. Israel and the church both belong to God’s city. Israel has not been replaced. God’s people are Israel and the church together.
> Verse 13: There are gates in every direction. People from the whole world are welcome.
> Verse 16: The city is a cube. The Most Holy Place in the temple was also a cube (1 Kings 6:20). The whole city is now like the Most Holy Place: God is very near everywhere.
> The old measure is given in stadia. 12,000 stadia are about 1,400 miles. The WEB, our English source text, has 12,012 stadia here. Most manuscripts have 12,000. The number means: unimaginably large, room for everyone.
> Verse 17: The wall measures 144 cubits. A cubit is about 18 inches. 144 cubits are about 216 feet. 144 is 12 times 12, again the number of the whole people of God.
> Verses 19–20: The precious stones recall the 12 stones on the breastpiece of the high priest (Exodus 28:17–21). The names of some stones are no longer completely certain today.
> Verse 21: Each gate is made of a single huge pearl. Here gold is as common as paving stones. What counts most on earth is completely ordinary in God’s world.

## God and the Lamb are its light (Verses 22–27)

[22] I saw no temple in the city.
For the Lord God, the Almighty, and the Lamb are its temple.
[23] The city does not need the sun or the moon to shine on it.
For the glory of God gives it light,
and the Lamb is its lamp.
[24] The nations will walk in its light.
The kings of the earth bring the glory and honor of the nations into it.
[25] Its gates will never be shut during the day,
and there will be no night there.
[26] People will bring the glory and the honor of the nations into it,
so that they may enter.
[27] Nothing unholy will ever enter it.
No one who does disgusting things or tells lies will enter.
Only those whose names are written in the Lamb’s book of life will enter.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: There is no more temple. You don’t need a special place any more to meet God. God is directly present everywhere.
> Verse 23 recalls Isaiah 60:19–20.
> Verses 24–26: The nations and kings come into the city and bring their treasures with them. The good from all cultures finds a place in God’s new world (compare Isaiah 60:3–11).
> Verse 25: The gates are always open. There is no more danger. God’s city is open and welcoming.
