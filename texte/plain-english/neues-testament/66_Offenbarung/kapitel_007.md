# Revelation – Chapter 7
### God keeps his people safe

---

## The servants of God receive a seal (Verses 1–8)

[1] After this, I saw four angels
standing at the four corners of the earth.
They were holding the four winds of the earth,
so that no wind would blow on the earth,
or on the sea,
or on any tree.
[2] I saw another angel
ascend from the sunrise.
He had the seal of the living God.
He cried with a loud voice to the four angels
to whom it was given
to harm the earth and the sea.
[3] He said,
“Don’t harm the earth,
the sea
or the trees,
until we have sealed the servants of our God
on their foreheads!”
[4] And I heard the number of those
who were sealed:
one hundred forty-four thousand.
They were sealed
out of every tribe of the children of Israel:
[5] Of the tribe of Judah
twelve thousand were sealed,
of the tribe of Reuben twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Gad twelve thousand,
[6] of the tribe of Asher twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Naphtali twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Manasseh twelve thousand,
[7] of the tribe of Simeon twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Levi twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Issachar twelve thousand,
[8] of the tribe of Zebulun twelve thousand,
of the tribe of Joseph twelve thousand,
and of the tribe of Benjamin
twelve thousand were sealed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Before the hard things come, God takes care of his own. A seal shows: This belongs to me, this is protected. This recalls Ezekiel 9:4, where the faithful receive a mark on their foreheads.
> Verse 4: 144,000 is 12 times 12 times 1,000. It is a number full of meaning: the 12 tribes of Israel, complete and very large. It probably means: God’s whole people, no one is missing.
> Verses 5–8: Judah comes first, because the Messiah comes from Judah. The tribe of Dan is missing from this list; Manasseh is there instead. We don’t know for sure why.
> Christians understand this passage in different ways: Some think it means the Jewish people. Others think it means the whole people of God, Jews and non-Jews. What is important: God doesn’t forget Israel.
> Some groups teach that only exactly 144,000 people will go to heaven. That is not what it says here. Right after this (verse 9), John sees a multitude that no one can count.

---

## A huge multitude from all peoples (Verses 9–12)

[9] After these things I looked,
and look:
a great multitude
which no one could count,
out of every nation
and of all tribes,
peoples
and languages.
They were standing before the throne
and before the Lamb.
They were dressed in white robes
and had palm branches in their hands.
[10] They cried with a loud voice,
“Salvation be to our God,
who sits on the throne,
and to the Lamb!”
[11] And all the angels were standing around the throne,
the elders
and the four living creatures.
They fell on their faces
before the throne
and worshiped God.
[12] They said,
“Amen!
Blessing and glory,
wisdom and thanksgiving,
honor and power and might
be to our God
forever and ever!
Amen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: John hears a number (144,000), but he sees a multitude that no one can count. This recalls Chapter 5: There he hears “Lion” and sees a Lamb. God’s people are much larger than people think.
> The people come from all peoples and languages. With God there are no borders of origin, skin color or language.
> Palm branches are a sign of victory and joy. They recall the Jewish Feast of Tabernacles and Jesus’ entry into Jerusalem (John 12:13).
> Verse 10: Salvation comes from God. No one saves himself.

## Who are these people? (Verses 13–17)

[13] One of the elders asked me,
“These who are dressed in the white robes,
who are they,
and where did they come from?”
[14] I told him,
“My lord,
you know.”
He said to me,
“These are those
who came out of the great suffering.
They washed their robes
and made them white in the Lamb’s blood.
[15] Therefore they are before the throne of God.
And they serve him day and night in his temple.
He who sits on the throne
will spread his tabernacle over them.
A tabernacle is a tent where God lives.
[16] They will never be hungry
or thirsty any more.
The sun won’t beat on them,
nor any heat.
[17] For the Lamb
who is in the middle of the throne
shepherds them
and leads them to springs
of life-giving waters.
And God will wipe away every tear
from their eyes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Made them white in the Lamb’s blood” is a surprising picture. Blood doesn’t really make things white. What is meant: Through the death of Jesus, people become clean and free from guilt.
> Verse 15: “Spread his tabernacle” recalls the holy tent in the wilderness. God lives among his people and protects them.
> Verses 16–17 recall Isaiah 49:10 and Isaiah 25:8.
> Verse 17: The Lamb is at the same time the shepherd. And God himself wipes away every tear. That is one of the most beautiful sentences of comfort in the Bible. It comes once more at the end of the book (21:4).
> Whoever is grieving may know: God sees every tear.
