# Revelation – Chapter 7
### God keeps his people safe

---

## The servants of God receive a seal (Verses 1–8)

[1] After this, I saw four angels.
They were standing at the four corners of the earth.
They were holding back the four winds of the earth,
so that no wind would blow on the earth, or on the sea, or on any tree.
[2] I saw another angel come up from where the sun rises.
He had the seal of the living God.
The four angels had been given power to harm the earth and the sea.
He called out to them with a loud voice.
[3] He said,
“Don’t harm the earth, the sea, or the trees
until we have put a seal on the foreheads of the servants of our God!”
[4] I heard the number of those who were sealed.
It was 144,000.
They were sealed out of every tribe of the people of Israel:
[5] From the tribe of Judah 12,000 were sealed.
From the tribe of Reuben 12,000.
From the tribe of Gad 12,000.
[6] From the tribe of Asher 12,000.
From the tribe of Naphtali 12,000.
From the tribe of Manasseh 12,000.
[7] From the tribe of Simeon 12,000.
From the tribe of Levi 12,000.
From the tribe of Issachar 12,000.
[8] From the tribe of Zebulun 12,000.
From the tribe of Joseph 12,000.
And from the tribe of Benjamin 12,000 were sealed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 1–3: Before the hard things come, God takes care of his own. A seal shows: This belongs to me, this is protected. This recalls Ezekiel 9:4, where the faithful receive a mark on their foreheads.
> Verse 4: 144,000 is 12 times 12 times 1,000. It is a number full of meaning: the 12 tribes of Israel, complete and very large. It probably means: God’s whole people, no one is missing.
> Verses 5–8: Judah comes first, because the Messiah comes from Judah. The tribe of Dan is missing from this list; Manasseh is there instead. We don’t know for sure why.
> Christians understand this passage in different ways: Some think it means the Jewish people. Others think it means the whole people of God, Jews and non-Jews. What is important: God doesn’t forget Israel.
> Some groups teach that only exactly 144,000 people will go to heaven. That is not what it says here. Right after this (verse 9), John sees a crowd that no one can count.

---

## A huge multitude from all peoples (Verses 9–12)

[9] After these things I looked.
I saw a great crowd.
No one could count them.
They came from every nation and from all tribes, peoples, and languages.
They were standing before the throne and before the Lamb.
They were dressed in white robes.
They had palm branches in their hands.
[10] They called out with a loud voice,
“Salvation comes from our God, who sits on the throne, and from the Lamb!”
[11] All the angels were standing around the throne, the elders, and the four living creatures.
They fell on their faces before his throne and worshiped God.
[12] They said,
“Amen!
Blessing, glory, wisdom, thanks, honor, power, and strength
belong to our God forever and ever!
Amen.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: John hears a number (144,000), but he sees a crowd that no one can count. This recalls Chapter 5: There he hears “Lion” and sees a Lamb. God’s people are much larger than people think.
> The people come from all peoples and languages. With God there are no borders of origin, skin color or language.
> Palm branches are a sign of victory and joy. They recall the Jewish Feast of Booths and Jesus’ entry into Jerusalem (John 12:13).
> Verse 10: Salvation comes from God. No one saves himself.

---

## Who are these people? (Verses 13–17)

[13] Then one of the elders spoke to me.
He asked,
“These people dressed in white robes, who are they?
And where did they come from?”
[14] I said to him, “My lord, you know.”
He said to me,
“These are the people who came out of the great suffering.
They washed their robes and made them white in the Lamb’s blood.
[15] That is why they are before the throne of God.
They serve him day and night in his temple.
The one who sits on the throne will spread his tent over them.
[16] They will never be hungry or thirsty again.
The sun will not beat down on them.
No heat will burn them.
[17] For the Lamb in the middle of the throne is their shepherd.
He leads them to springs of water that give life.
And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Made them white in the Lamb’s blood” is a surprising picture. Blood doesn’t really make things white. What is meant: Through the death of Jesus, people become clean and free from guilt.
> Verse 15: “Spread his tent” recalls the holy tent in the wilderness. God lives among his people and protects them.
> Verses 16–17 recall Isaiah 49:10 and Isaiah 25:8.
> Verse 17: The Lamb is at the same time the shepherd. And God himself wipes away every tear. That is one of the most beautiful sentences of comfort in the Bible. It comes once more at the end of the book (21:4).
> Whoever is grieving may know: God sees every tear.
