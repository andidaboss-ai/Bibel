# Revelation – Chapter 19
### Hallelujah! The wedding of the Lamb

---

## Hallelujah in heaven (Verses 1–5)

[1] After these things I heard something like a loud voice
of a great multitude in heaven.
They said,
“Hallelujah!
Salvation, power and glory
belong to our God.
[2] For his judgments are true and righteous.
He has judged the great prostitute
who corrupted the earth with her sexual immorality.
And he has avenged the blood of his servants
that she shed.”
[3] A second time they said,
“Hallelujah!
Her smoke goes up forever and ever.”
[4] The twenty-four elders
and the four living creatures fell down
and worshiped God
who sits on the throne.
They said,
“Amen!
Hallelujah!”
[5] A voice came from the throne,
saying,
“Give praise to our God,
all you his servants,
you who fear him,
the small and the great!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hallelujah” is Hebrew and means: Praise God! (Hallelu = praise, Jah = short form of the name of God). The word often appears in the Psalms (for example Psalms 146–150). In the New Testament it appears only here in this chapter.
> Verse 2: The people in heaven don’t rejoice over the suffering, but that injustice has an end. The victims receive justice.
> Verse 5: All are to praise God, “the small and the great.” With God everyone counts.

---

## The wedding of the Lamb (Verses 6–10)

[6] I heard something like the voice of a great multitude,
and like the voice of many waters,
and like the voice of mighty thunders.
They said,
“Hallelujah!
For the Lord our God,
the Almighty,
reigns!
[7] Let’s rejoice
and be exceedingly glad,
and let’s give the glory to him!
For the wedding of the Lamb has come,
and his wife has made herself ready.
[8] It was given to her
to dress herself in bright, pure, fine linen.
For the fine linen
is the righteous acts of the saints.”
[9] He said to me,
“Write:
‘Blessed are those
who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb.’”
And he said to me,
“These are true words of God.”
[10] Then I fell down before his feet
to worship him.
But he said to me,
“Look!
Don’t do it!
I am a fellow servant with you
and with your brothers
who hold the testimony of Jesus.
Worship God!
For the testimony of Jesus
is the Spirit of Prophecy.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The Bible often uses the picture of a wedding for the love between God and his people (for example Isaiah 62:5 and Hosea 2). Jesus compared God’s Kingdom to a wedding feast (Matthew 22). The “wife” of the Lamb is the community of all who belong to Jesus.
> Verse 8: The bride’s dress is a gift (“It was given to her”). It is made of the good deeds of those who believe. That is the contrasting picture to the splendidly decked prostitute in Chapter 17.
> Verse 9: This is the fourth “Blessed” in the Revelation. God invites people to his feast.
> Verse 10: Not even an angel may be worshiped. Worship belongs to God alone. That is also true for saints, preachers or other people.

---

## The rider on the white horse (Verses 11–16)

[11] I saw heaven opened.
And look:
a white horse.
He who sits on it
is called Faithful and True.
In righteousness he judges and makes war.
[12] His eyes are a flame of fire.
On his head are many crowns.
He has names written,
and a name written
which no one knows
but he himself.
[13] He is clothed in a garment
sprinkled with blood.
And his name is called
“The Word of God.”
[14] The armies which are in heaven followed him on white horses.
They were clothed in white, pure, fine linen.
[15] Out of his mouth comes a sharp, double-edged sword,
so that he may strike the nations with it.
He will rule them with an iron rod.
And he treads the wine press
of the fierceness of the wrath of God,
the Almighty.
[16] On his garment and on his thigh
a name is written:
“KING OF KINGS
AND LORD OF LORDS.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The rider is Jesus. He is called “Faithful and True” (compare 3:14). His name is “The Word of God” (verse 13). Compare John 1:1.
> Verse 13: His garment is already sprinkled with blood before the battle begins. Many understand it like this: It is his own blood that he shed on the cross. He wins by giving himself.
> Verse 14: The armies carry no weapons and wear no armor, but white clothes. They don’t fight themselves.
> Verse 15: The sword comes out of his mouth. It is his word, not a real weapon. Jesus wins through the truth.

> **Important note**
> In the course of history people have used pictures like these to justify wars and violence in the name of Christ, for example in the Crusades. That is a terrible misuse. In the Revelation, Christ alone fights, and he does so with his word. His people carry no weapons. Jesus himself said: “Put your sword back into its place!” (Matthew 26:52).

## The beast is defeated (Verses 17–21)

[17] I saw an angel standing in the sun.
He cried with a loud voice,
saying to all the birds
that fly in the sky,
“Come!
Be gathered together to the great supper of God!
[18] You are to eat the flesh of kings,
the flesh of captains,
the flesh of mighty men,
the flesh of horses and of those who sit on them,
and the flesh of all people,
both free and slave,
small and great.”
[19] I saw the beast
and the kings of the earth
and their armies.
They had gathered together
to make war against him
who sat on the horse,
and against his army.
[20] The beast was taken,
and with him the false prophet
who did the signs in his sight.
With these signs he had deceived those
who had received the mark of the beast
and who worshiped his image.
These two were thrown alive
into the lake of fire
that burns with sulfur.
[21] The rest were killed
with the sword of him
who sat on the horse,
the sword
which came out of his mouth.
And all the birds were filled with their flesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: A terrible contrasting picture to the wedding supper in verse 9. There are two “meals”: the feast for those who are invited, and the end of the powers that set themselves against God. The picture comes from Ezekiel 39:17–20.
> Verses 19–21: There is no real battle at all. The beast and the false prophet are simply captured. God wins without effort. And he wins with the word from his mouth.
> Verse 20: The “lake of fire” is a picture for the final end of evil. The powers that deceived and oppressed people are defeated forever.
