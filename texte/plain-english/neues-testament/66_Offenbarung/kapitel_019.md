# Revelation – Chapter 19
### Hallelujah! The wedding of the Lamb

---

## Hallelujah in heaven (Verses 1–5)

[1] After these things I heard something like the loud voice of a great crowd in heaven.
They said,
“Hallelujah!
Salvation, power, and glory belong to our God.
[2] For his judgments are true and just.
He has judged the great prostitute.
She ruined the earth with her sexual immorality.
He has made her pay for the blood of his servants that she shed.”
[3] A second time they said,
“Hallelujah!
Her smoke goes up forever and ever.”
[4] The twenty-four elders and the four living creatures bowed down.
They worshiped God, who sits on the throne.
They said, “Amen! Hallelujah!”
[5] A voice came from the throne.
It said,
“Praise our God, all you his servants,
you who fear him, the small and the great!”

> **What does this mean?**
> “Hallelujah” is Hebrew and means: Praise God! (Hallelu = praise, Jah = short form of the name of God). The word often appears in the Psalms (for example Psalms 146–150). In the New Testament it appears only here in this chapter.
> Verse 2: The people in heaven don’t rejoice over the suffering, but that injustice has an end. The victims receive justice.
> Verse 5: All are to praise God, “the small and the great.” With God everyone counts.

---

## The wedding of the Lamb (Verses 6–10)

[6] I heard something like the voice of a great crowd.
It was like the sound of many waters
and like the sound of loud thunder.
They said,
“Hallelujah!
For the Lord our God, the Almighty, rules as king!
[7] Let’s be glad and very happy!
Let’s give him the glory!
For the time has come for the wedding of the Lamb.
His wife has made herself ready.”
[8] She was given bright, pure, fine linen to wear.
The fine linen is the good and right deeds of the saints.
[9] The angel said to me,
“Write this:
‘Blessed are those who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb.’”
He also said to me,
“These are true words of God.”
[10] I fell down at his feet to worship him.
But he said to me,
“Look! Don’t do that!
I am a servant, just like you
and like your brothers who hold on to the testimony of Jesus.
Worship God!
For the testimony of Jesus is the Spirit of Prophecy.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The Bible often uses the picture of a wedding for the love between God and his people (for example Isaiah 62:5 and Hosea 2). Jesus compared God’s Kingdom to a wedding feast (Matthew 22). The “wife” of the Lamb is the community of all who belong to Jesus.
> Verse 8: The bride’s dress is a gift (“She was given”). It is made of the good deeds of those who believe. That is the contrasting picture to the splendidly decked prostitute in Chapter 17.
> Verse 9: This is the fourth “Blessed” in the Revelation. God invites people to his feast.
> Verse 10: Not even an angel may be worshiped. Worship belongs to God alone. That is also true for saints, preachers or other people.

---

## The rider on the white horse (Verses 11–16)

[11] I saw heaven standing open.
There was a white horse.
The one who sits on it is called Faithful and True.
He judges and makes war in a just way.
[12] His eyes are like a flame of fire.
On his head are many crowns.
He has names written on him.
And he has a name written that no one knows except he himself.
[13] He wears a robe that is sprinkled with blood.
His name is “The Word of God.”
[14] The armies of heaven followed him on white horses.
They were dressed in white, pure, fine linen.
[15] A sharp sword with two edges comes out of his mouth.
With it he will strike the nations.
He will rule them with a rod of iron.
He crushes the grapes in the winepress of the fierce anger of God, the Almighty.
[16] A name is written on his robe and on his thigh:
“KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The rider is Jesus. He is called “Faithful and True” (compare 3:14). His name is “The Word of God” (verse 13). Compare John 1:1.
> Verse 13: His robe is already sprinkled with blood before the battle begins. Many understand it like this: It is his own blood that he shed on the cross. He wins by giving himself.
> Verse 14: The armies carry no weapons and wear no armor, but white clothes. They don’t fight themselves.
> Verse 15: The sword comes out of his mouth. It is his word, not a real weapon. Jesus wins through the truth.

> **Important note**
> In the course of history people have used pictures like these to justify wars and violence in the name of Christ, for example in the Crusades. That is a terrible misuse. In the Revelation, Christ alone fights, and he does so with his word. His people carry no weapons. Jesus himself said: “Put your sword back into its place!” (Matthew 26:52).

## The beast is defeated (Verses 17–21)

[17] I saw an angel standing in the sun.
He called out with a loud voice to all the birds that fly in the sky,
“Come!
Gather together for the great supper of God!
[18] Come and eat the flesh of kings,
the flesh of captains, and the flesh of mighty men.
Eat the flesh of horses and of their riders.
Eat the flesh of all people, both free and slave, small and great.”
[19] I saw the beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies.
They had gathered together to make war against the one who sat on the horse,
and against his army.
[20] The beast was captured.
The false prophet was captured with him.
The false prophet had done signs in front of the beast.
With these signs he had deceived the people who had received the mark of the beast
and who worshiped his image.
These two were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulfur.
[21] The rest were killed with the sword of the one who sat on the horse.
It was the sword that came out of his mouth.
All the birds ate their fill of their flesh.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–18: A terrible contrasting picture to the wedding supper in verse 9. There are two “meals”: the feast for those who are invited, and the end of the powers that set themselves against God. The picture comes from Ezekiel 39:17–20.
> Verses 19–21: There is no real battle at all. The beast and the false prophet are simply captured. God wins without effort. And he wins with the word from his mouth.
> Verse 20: The “lake of fire” is a picture for the final end of evil. The powers that deceived and oppressed people are defeated forever.
