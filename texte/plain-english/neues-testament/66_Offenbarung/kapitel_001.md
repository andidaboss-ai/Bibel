# Revelation – Chapter 1
### John sees Jesus in his glory

---

## Before we begin: The Revelation to John

The Revelation is the last book of the Bible.
A man named John wrote it down. He was on the island of Patmos, probably as an exile. According to old tradition, he is the apostle John. Many scholars think it was another Christian prophet with the name John.
The book was probably written around AD 95. At that time the Roman Empire ruled. Christians were put under pressure to worship the emperor like a god. Some were persecuted.
The Revelation is full of pictures and signs: animals, angels, numbers, colors, dragons, trumpets. Books like this are called “apocalypse.” The Greek word means: unveiling. God shows what is behind things.
The pictures are not a map and not a timetable for the end of the world. They are like a coded message to people in need. The message is: God has the last word. Evil will not win. Jesus, the Lamb, has already won.
Many pictures come from the Hebrew Bible, especially from the books of Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah and Zechariah.
The Revelation has 22 chapters.

> **Important note first**
> Some people have used the Revelation to make others afraid, or have calculated the date of the end of the world. All these calculations were wrong. Jesus himself says: No one knows the day and the hour (compare Matthew 24:36).
> The Revelation wants to comfort, not to frighten. Whoever becomes very afraid because of this book can talk with someone about it, for example with a pastor or a counselor. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.
> Be careful also of groups that use the Revelation to put pressure on people and say: Only with us are you safe. That is a warning sign.

## The title of the book (Verses 1–3)

[1] This is the Revelation of Jesus Christ.
God gave it to him,
so that he would show his servants
the things which must happen soon.
He sent his angel
and made it known to his servant John.
[2] John testified to God’s word
and to the testimony of Jesus Christ,
everything that he saw.
[3] Blessed is he
who reads aloud the words of this prophecy,
and blessed are those
who hear them
and keep
what is written in it.
For the time is near.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Revelation of Jesus Christ”: Jesus is the one who shows something, and he is also the one it is about.
> Verse 3: The book was read aloud in worship. That is why the reader and the listeners are meant. It is the first of seven “Blessed is …” in the Revelation.
> “The time is near”: God will soon step in. For the persecuted Christians at that time, this was a comfort.

## Greeting to the seven churches (Verses 4–8)

[4] John writes to the seven churches in the province of Asia:
Grace to you and peace
from God,
who is and who was and who is to come,
and from the seven Spirits
who are before his throne,
[5] and from Jesus Christ.
He is the faithful witness,
the firstborn of the dead
and the ruler of the kings of the earth.
To him who loves us
and washed us from our sins by his blood
[6] and who made us to be a Kingdom,
priests to his God and Father,
to him belong the glory and the dominion
forever and ever.
Amen.
[7] Look,
he is coming with the clouds.
Every eye will see him,
including those
who pierced him.
And all the tribes of the earth will mourn over him.
Even so.
Amen.
[8] “I am the Alpha and the Omega,”
says the Lord God,
“who is and who was and who is to come,
the Almighty.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “Asia” here is a Roman province in the west of today’s Turkey. The number seven in the Bible means: complete. The seven churches stand for the whole church.
> “Who is and who was and who is to come” recalls the name of God that God told Moses (Exodus 3:14).
> “The seven Spirits” probably means the Holy Spirit in all his fullness.
> Verse 5: “The ruler of the kings of the earth”: Not the Roman emperor has the highest power, but Jesus.
> Verse 6: All Christians are like priests: They may come directly to God. God said this first to Israel (Exodus 19:6).
> Verse 7 recalls Daniel 7:13 and Zechariah 12:10.
> Verse 8: Alpha is the first letter of the Greek alphabet, Omega the last. That means: God is the beginning and the end of everything.

> **Important note**
> Verse 7: “Those who pierced him” is not an accusation against the Jewish people. Roman soldiers crucified Jesus. According to Christian faith, Jesus died for the guilt of all people. The mourning concerns “all the tribes of the earth.”

## John on Patmos (Verses 9–11)

[9] I, John,
am your brother.
I share with you the oppression,
the Kingdom and the perseverance in Christ Jesus.
I was on the island
that is called Patmos,
because of God’s Word
and because of the testimony of Jesus Christ.
[10] On the Lord’s day
I was in the Spirit.
Then I heard behind me a loud voice,
like a trumpet.
[11] It said,
“Write in a book
what you see,
and send it to the seven churches:
to Ephesus, to Smyrna, to Pergamum,
to Thyatira, to Sardis, to Philadelphia
and to Laodicea.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: Patmos is a small island in the Mediterranean Sea. John had probably been banished there because he told people about Jesus.
> Verse 10: “On the Lord’s day”: This means Sunday, the day on which Jesus rose from the dead. The Christians held worship on this day.
> Verse 11: The seven cities lie in the west of today’s Turkey. A messenger could visit them one after another on a round trip.

## John sees Jesus (Verses 12–20)

[12] I turned around
to see
who was speaking with me.
And when I had turned around,
I saw seven golden lampstands.
[13] And among the lampstands
was one
who looked like a son of man.
He wore a long robe reaching down to his feet
and a golden sash around his chest.
[14] His head and his hair were white
as white wool,
like snow.
His eyes were like a flame of fire.
[15] His feet were like burnished brass,
refined until it glows in a furnace.
His voice was like the voice of many waters.
[16] He had seven stars in his right hand.
Out of his mouth came a sharp two-edged sword.
And his face was like the sun
shining at its brightest.
[17] When I saw him,
I fell at his feet like a dead man.
He laid his right hand on me
and said,
“Don’t be afraid.
I am the first and the last,
[18] and the Living one.
I was dead,
and look:
I am alive forever and ever.
Amen.
I have the keys of Death
and of Hades.
Hades is the world of the dead.
[19] So write
the things which you have seen,
and the things which are,
and the things which will happen after this.
[20] This is the mystery of the seven stars
which you saw in my right hand,
and of the seven golden lampstands:
The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches.
And the seven lampstands are the seven churches.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: “One who looked like a son of man” recalls Daniel 7:13. Jesus is meant.
> Verse 14: The white hair recalls God, the “Ancient of Days,” in Daniel 7:9. Jesus is described here with the glory of God.
> Verse 16: The sword out of his mouth is not a real sword. It is a picture for his word. Jesus doesn’t fight with weapons, but with the truth.
> Verse 17: “Don’t be afraid”: That is the first thing Jesus says. His hand on John is a gesture of care.
> Verse 18: Jesus has the keys of Death. That means: Death doesn’t have the last word. Jesus is stronger than death.
> Verse 20: Jesus is among his churches. He holds them in his hand. The “angels of the seven churches” are perhaps heavenly protectors, or perhaps the leaders of the churches.
