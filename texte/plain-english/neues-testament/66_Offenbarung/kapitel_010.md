# Revelation – Chapter 10
### The angel with the little scroll

---

## A mighty angel and seven thunders (Verses 1–7)

[1] I saw another mighty angel
coming down out of the sky.
He was clothed with a cloud.
A rainbow was on his head.
His face was like the sun,
and his feet were like pillars of fire.
[2] In his hand he had a little book
that was open.
He set his right foot on the sea
and his left on the land.
[3] He cried with a loud voice,
as a lion roars.
And when he cried,
the seven thunders uttered their voices.
[4] When the seven thunders had sounded,
I was about to write.
But I heard a voice from the sky
saying,
“Seal up
the things which the seven thunders said,
and don’t write them.”
[5] The angel
whom I saw standing on the sea and on the land
lifted up his right hand to the sky.
[6] And he swore by him
who lives forever and ever,
who created heaven
and the things that are in it,
the earth
and the things that are in it,
and the sea
and the things that are in it:
“There will no longer be delay.
[7] But in the days
of the voice of the seventh angel,
when he is about to sound,
then the mystery of God is finished,
as he declared to his servants,
the prophets.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: The angel stands on the sea and on the land. That means: His message is for the whole world.
> Verse 4: John may not write everything down. There are things that only God knows. We don’t have to know everything about the future, and we can’t.
> Verses 6–7: “There will no longer be delay”: For the persecuted Christians who asked “How long?” (6:10), this is an answer. God will finish his work.
> “The mystery of God” is his plan to save the world and to make everything good. The prophets already announced it.

## John eats the little scroll (Verses 8–11)

[8] The voice
which I had heard from heaven
spoke with me again
and said,
“Go, take the book
which is open in the hand of the angel
who stands on the sea and on the land.”
[9] I went to the angel
and told him
to give me the little book.
He said to me,
“Take it and eat it.
It will make your stomach bitter.
But in your mouth it will be as sweet as honey.”
[10] I took the little book out of the angel’s hand
and ate it.
It was as sweet as honey in my mouth.
But when I had eaten it,
my stomach was made bitter.
[11] And they told me,
“You must prophesy again
over many peoples, nations,
languages and kings.”

> **What does this mean?**
> This recalls the prophet Ezekiel. He too had to eat a scroll, and it was as sweet as honey (Ezekiel 2:8–3:3).
> “Eating the book” means: taking God’s word completely into yourself before you pass it on.
> Sweet and bitter at the same time: God’s word is beautiful and good. But it can also be hard to pass it on, especially when it is about suffering and judgment.
> Verse 11: John receives a new task. His message is for all peoples.
