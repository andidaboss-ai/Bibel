# Revelation – Chapter 5
### The Lamb and the scroll

---

## Who can open the scroll? (Verses 1–5)

[1] I saw in the right hand of him
who sat on the throne
a book.
It was written inside and outside
and sealed shut with seven seals.
[2] And I saw a mighty angel.
He proclaimed with a loud voice,
“Who is worthy
to open the book
and to break its seals?”
[3] But no one in heaven above,
on the earth
or under the earth
was able to open the book
or to look in it.
[4] Then I wept much,
because no one was found
who was worthy
to open the book
or to look in it.
[5] One of the elders said to me,
“Don’t weep.
Look:
The Lion who is of the tribe of Judah,
the Root of David,
has overcome.
He can open the book and its seven seals.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: At that time the “book” was a scroll. The seals were made of wax. Only when all seven seals are broken can you read the whole scroll. The scroll probably contains God’s plan for the world.
> Verse 4: John weeps because it looks as if God’s plan can’t go on. That is a very human reaction.
> Verse 5: “The Lion who is of the tribe of Judah” and “the Root of David” recall Jacob’s blessing for Judah (Genesis 49:9) and Isaiah 11:1–10. Both are pictures for the Messiah from the Jewish people. Jesus came from the tribe of Judah and from the family of David.

---

## The Lamb (Verses 6–10)

[6] And I saw in the middle of the throne
and of the four living creatures,
and in the middle of the elders,
a Lamb standing.
It looked
as though it had been slain.
It had seven horns and seven eyes.
These are the seven Spirits of God,
sent out into all the earth.
[7] Then he came
and took the book out of the right hand of him
who sat on the throne.
[8] When he had taken the book,
the four living creatures
and the twenty-four elders
fell down before the Lamb.
Each one had a harp
and golden bowls full of incense.
These are the prayers of the saints.
[9] They sang a new song:
“You are worthy
to take the book
and to open its seals.
For you were killed.
And with your blood you bought us for God
out of every tribe and language,
out of every people and nation.
[10] And you made us kings and priests
to our God.
And we will reign on the earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6 are a great surprise: John hears “Lion.” But when he looks, he sees a Lamb that had been slain. God doesn’t win with violence like a beast of prey, but through Jesus giving himself on the cross. That is the most important message of the whole Revelation.
> The Lamb recalls the Passover lamb at the exodus from Egypt (Exodus 12) and the suffering servant of God, who was like a lamb (Isaiah 53:7).
> “Seven horns and seven eyes”: Horns are a picture for strength, eyes for knowledge. Seven means: perfect.
> Verse 8: People’s prayers reach God. They are precious like incense in golden bowls.
> Verse 9: God’s people come from all peoples, languages and cultures. No one is shut out.
> Verse 10: Some manuscripts have “we reign” instead of “we will reign.”

---

## The whole creation praises God and the Lamb (Verses 11–14)

[11] I looked,
and I heard something like a voice of many angels
around the throne,
the living creatures and the elders.
Their number was ten thousands of ten thousands,
and thousands of thousands.
[12] They said with a loud voice,
“Worthy is the Lamb
who has been killed
to receive the power,
wealth,
wisdom,
strength,
honor,
glory
and blessing!”
[13] And I heard every created thing
which is in heaven,
on the earth,
under the earth,
on the sea,
and everything in them,
saying,
“To him who sits on the throne
and to the Lamb
be the blessing and the honor,
the glory and the dominion,
forever and ever!
Amen!”
[14] The four living creatures said,
“Amen!”
Then the elders fell down
and worshiped.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: The number is unimaginably large. It means: more than anyone can count.
> Verse 12: Seven things are given to the Lamb: power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory and blessing. Seven again means: perfect.
> Verse 13: At the end the whole creation joins in. God and the Lamb are praised together. That shows: Jesus belongs completely on God’s side.
> Many songs and pieces of music have taken up these words. They are still sung in worship today.
