# Revelation – Chapter 5
### The Lamb and the scroll

---

## Who can open the scroll? (Verses 1–5)

[1] I saw a book in the right hand of the one who sat on the throne.
It had writing on the inside and on the outside.
It was sealed shut with seven seals.
[2] I saw a mighty angel.
He called out with a loud voice,
“Who is worthy to open the book and to break its seals?”
[3] But no one was able to open the book or to look into it.
No one in heaven above could do it,
no one on the earth,
and no one under the earth.
[4] I cried and cried,
because no one was found who was worthy to open the book or to look into it.
[5] Then one of the elders said to me,
“Don’t cry.
Look! The Lion from the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has won the victory.
He is the one who opens the book and its seven seals.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: At that time the “book” was a scroll. The seals were made of wax. Only when all seven seals are broken can you read the whole scroll. The scroll probably contains God’s plan for the world.
> Verse 4: John cries because it looks as if God’s plan can’t go on. That is a very human reaction.
> Verse 5: “The Lion from the tribe of Judah” and “the Root of David” recall Jacob’s blessing for Judah (Genesis 49:9) and Isaiah 11:1–10. Both are pictures for the Messiah from the Jewish people. Jesus came from the tribe of Judah and from the family of David.

---

## The Lamb (Verses 6–10)

[6] Then I saw a Lamb standing in the middle of the throne and of the four living creatures,
in the middle of the elders.
The Lamb looked as if it had been killed.
It had seven horns and seven eyes.
These are the seven Spirits of God.
They are sent out into all the earth.
[7] The Lamb came.
He took the book out of the right hand of the one who sat on the throne.
[8] When he had taken the book,
the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders bowed down before the Lamb.
Each of them had a harp.
And they had golden bowls full of incense.
The incense is the prayers of the saints.
The saints are the people who belong to God.
[9] They sang a new song:
“You are worthy to take the book and to open its seals.
For you were killed.
With your blood you bought us for God.
You bought us from every tribe, language, people, and nation.
[10] You made us kings and priests for our God.
And we will rule on the earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6 are a great surprise: John hears “Lion.” But when he looks, he sees a Lamb that had been killed. God doesn’t win with violence like a beast of prey, but through Jesus giving himself on the cross. That is the most important message of the whole Revelation.
> The Lamb recalls the Passover lamb at the exodus from Egypt (Exodus 12) and the suffering servant of God, who was like a lamb (Isaiah 53:7).
> “Seven horns and seven eyes”: Horns are a picture for strength, eyes for knowledge. Seven means: perfect.
> Verse 8: People’s prayers reach God. They are precious like incense in golden bowls.
> Verse 9: God’s people come from all peoples, languages and cultures. No one is shut out.
> Verse 10: Some manuscripts have “we rule” instead of “we will rule.”

---

## The whole creation praises God and the Lamb (Verses 11–14)

[11] I looked, and I heard something like the voice of many angels.
They were around the throne, the living creatures, and the elders.
There were ten thousands times ten thousands of them,
and thousands times thousands.
[12] They said with a loud voice,
“The Lamb who was killed is worthy!
He is worthy to receive the power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing!”
[13] I heard every created thing in heaven, on the earth, under the earth, and on the sea.
I heard everything in them.
They said,
“To the one who sits on the throne and to the Lamb
be the blessing, the honor, the glory, and the power to rule, forever and ever!
Amen!”
[14] The four living creatures said, “Amen!”
Then the elders bowed down and worshiped.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: The number is unimaginably large. It means: more than anyone can count.
> Verse 12: Seven things are given to the Lamb: power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory and blessing. Seven again means: perfect.
> Verse 13: At the end the whole creation joins in. God and the Lamb are praised together. That shows: Jesus belongs completely on God’s side.
> Many songs and pieces of music have taken up these words. They are still sung in worship today.
