# Revelation – Chapter 4
### The throne in heaven

---

## A door opened in heaven (Verses 1–6a)

[1] After these things I looked,
and I saw:
A door was opened in heaven.
And the first voice
that I had heard,
which spoke with me like a trumpet,
said,
“Come up here.
I will show you
the things which must happen after this.”
[2] Immediately I was in the Spirit.
And look:
There was a throne set in heaven,
and one was sitting on the throne.
[3] He looked like a jasper stone
and a sardius.
And there was a rainbow around the throne.
It looked like an emerald.
Jasper, sardius and emerald are precious stones.
[4] Around the throne were twenty-four thrones.
On the thrones twenty-four elders were sitting.
They were dressed in white garments
and had crowns of gold on their heads.
[5] Out of the throne came lightnings,
sounds and thunders.
Seven lamps of fire were burning before the throne.
These are the seven Spirits of God.
[6] Before the throne was something like a sea of glass,
similar to crystal.

> **What does this mean?**
> After the letters to the churches on earth, John now looks into heaven. Before hard things are told, God shows: He sits on the throne. He rules, not the emperor in Rome.
> Verse 3: John doesn’t describe God with a face, but only with colors and light. God is greater than any picture. The rainbow recalls God’s promise after the flood (Genesis 9:13): God stands by his creation.
> Verse 4: The 24 elders perhaps stand for the people of God: the 12 tribes of Israel and the 12 apostles.
> Verse 5: Lightning and thunder recall how God appeared at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:16).
> Verse 6: For people at that time, the sea was a picture of chaos and danger. Here it is completely calm, like glass. Before God, chaos has no power.

## The four living creatures (Verses 6b–8)

In the middle of the throne and around the throne
were four living creatures.
They were full of eyes in front and behind.
[7] The first creature was like a lion.
The second creature was like a calf.
The third creature had a face like a man.
And the fourth was like a flying eagle.
[8] Each of the four living creatures had six wings.
They are full of eyes around and within.
Day and night they have no rest.
They say,
“Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God,
the Almighty,
who was and who is and who is to come!”

> **What does this mean?**
> The four living creatures recall the beings that the prophet Ezekiel saw (Ezekiel 1), and the seraphim in Isaiah (Isaiah 6).
> Lion, calf, man and eagle perhaps stand for the whole living creation: the strongest wild animal, the strongest farm animal, the human being and the strongest bird. The whole creation praises God.
> Later, Christians used these four creatures as signs for the four Gospels: Matthew (man), Mark (lion), Luke (calf) and John (eagle).
> The many eyes mean: They see everything. God keeps everything in view.
> Verse 8: “Holy, holy, holy” is the same song of praise as in Isaiah 6:3. It is still sung today in Jewish and Christian worship.

---

## All worship God (Verses 9–11)

[9] Whenever the living creatures
give glory, honor and thanks
to him who sits on the throne,
to him who lives forever and ever,
[10] then the twenty-four elders fall down
before him who sits on the throne.
They worship him
who lives forever and ever.
They throw their crowns before the throne
and say,
[11] “Worthy are you,
our Lord and God,
the Holy One,
to receive the glory, the honor and the power.
For you created all things.
Because of your desire
they existed
and were created!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 10: The elders throw their crowns down before God. That means: All honor belongs to God, not to us.
> Verse 11: “Worthy are you”: At that time people cheered the emperor with similar words. The Revelation says: Only God is worthy, not the emperor.
> “Because of your desire”: The world is not an accident. God wanted it. That is why every creature is wanted and precious.
