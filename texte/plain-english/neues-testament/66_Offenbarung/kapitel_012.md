# Revelation – Chapter 12
### The woman and the dragon

---

## The woman, the child and the dragon (Verses 1–6)

[1] A great sign appeared in heaven:
a woman dressed with the sun.
The moon was under her feet.
On her head was a crown of 12 stars.
[2] She was pregnant.
She cried out in pain, because she was in labor and about to give birth.
[3] Another sign appeared in heaven.
Look! There was a great red dragon.
He had seven heads and ten horns.
On his heads were seven crowns.
[4] His tail swept away one third of the stars of the sky and threw them down to the earth.
The dragon stood in front of the woman who was about to give birth.
He wanted to eat up her child as soon as it was born.
[5] She gave birth to a son, a boy.
He will rule all the nations with a rod of iron.
Her child was caught up to God and to his throne.
[6] The woman fled into the wilderness.
God has prepared a place for her there.
There they will feed her and take care of her for 1,260 days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a new part begins. John now shows what is behind everything: the battle between God and evil.
> Verse 1: The woman with 12 stars recalls the people of Israel with its 12 tribes (compare Joseph’s dream, Genesis 37:9). The Messiah comes from Israel.
> Christians understand the woman in different ways: as a picture for Israel, for the church or for Mary, the mother of Jesus. Especially in the Catholic and Orthodox churches, the woman is often connected with Mary. All these interpretations have something to them.
> Verse 3: The dragon is the devil (verse 9). Seven heads and ten horns show his great power. Red is the color of blood and violence.
> Verse 5: The child is Jesus, the Messiah (compare Psalm 2:9). “Caught up” means his resurrection and ascension into heaven. The dragon can’t harm him.
> Verse 6: In the Bible, the wilderness is a place where God protects and provides for his people, as for Israel after the exodus from Egypt.

## War in heaven (Verses 7–12)

[7] There was war in the sky.
Michael and his angels fought against the dragon.
The dragon and his angels fought back.
[8] But they did not win.
There was no longer any place for them in heaven.
[9] The great dragon was thrown down.
He is the old serpent, who is called the devil and Satan.
He leads the whole world astray.
He was thrown down to the earth,
and his angels were thrown down with him.
[10] I heard a loud voice in heaven say,
“Now the salvation, the power, and the Kingdom of our God have come,
and the authority of his Christ.
For the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down.
He accused them before our God day and night.
[11] They defeated him because of the Lamb’s blood
and because of the word of their testimony.
They did not love their own life so much that they were afraid to die.
[12] So be glad, you heavens, and you who live in them!
But how terrible it will be for the earth and for the sea!
For the devil has come down to you.
He is very angry,
because he knows that he has only a short time.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Michael is an archangel. He also appears in the book of Daniel as the protector of the people of Israel (Daniel 12:1).
> Verse 9: “The old serpent” recalls the serpent in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3). Here the devil is also called the one who leads the whole world astray.
> Verse 10: “Satan” means in Hebrew: accuser. He accuses people. But he has nothing more to say before God. Whoever belongs to Jesus is no longer condemned.
> Verse 11: The victory doesn’t come through weapons, but through the Lamb and through the brave testimony of those who believe.
> Verse 12: An important comfort: The devil is already defeated. He rages because he knows that his time will soon be over.

---

## The dragon persecutes the woman (Verses 13–17)

[13] The dragon saw that he had been thrown down to the earth.
So he hunted down the woman who had given birth to the boy.
[14] But the woman was given the two wings of the great eagle.
So she could fly into the wilderness to her place.
There she is fed and taken care of for a time, times, and half a time,
far away from the serpent.
[15] The serpent spat water out of his mouth after the woman, like a river.
He wanted the flood to sweep her away.
[16] But the earth helped the woman.
The earth opened its mouth
and swallowed the river that the dragon had spat out of his mouth.
[17] Then the dragon became angry with the woman.
He went away to make war against the rest of her children.
They keep God’s commandments and hold on to Jesus’ testimony.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “A time, times, and half a time” are three and a half years, like the 1,260 days in verse 6. It is a limited time. The “wings of the great eagle” recall Exodus 19:4: God carried Israel as on eagles’ wings.
> Verse 16: Even the creation helps the people of God.
> Verse 17: “The rest of her children” are all who believe in Jesus. The dragon attacks them. That is why life as a Christian is sometimes hard. But God protects his people.
> In many manuscripts and Bibles there is one more sentence here about the dragon: He stood on the sand of the sea. Sometimes that is verse 18. Other manuscripts have the sentence with “I.” That is how the WEB has it. There the sentence stands at the beginning of Chapter 13: “Then I stood on the sand of the sea” (13:1).
