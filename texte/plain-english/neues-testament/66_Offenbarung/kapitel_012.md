# Revelation – Chapter 12
### The woman and the dragon

---

## The woman, the child and the dragon (Verses 1–6)

[1] A great sign was seen in heaven:
a woman
clothed with the sun.
The moon was under her feet,
and on her head was a crown of twelve stars.
[2] She was with child.
She cried out in pain,
because she was in labor
to give birth.
[3] And another sign was seen in heaven.
Look:
a great red dragon.
He had seven heads and ten horns,
and on his heads seven crowns.
[4] His tail drew one third of the stars of the sky
and threw them to the earth.
The dragon stood before the woman
who was about to give birth,
so that he might devour her child
as soon as she gave birth.
[5] She gave birth to a son,
a male child.
He is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron.
And her child was caught up to God
and to his throne.
[6] The woman fled into the wilderness.
There she has a place
prepared by God,
so that they may nourish her there
for one thousand two hundred sixty days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here a new part begins. John now shows what is behind everything: the battle between God and evil.
> Verse 1: The woman with twelve stars recalls the people of Israel with its twelve tribes (compare Joseph’s dream, Genesis 37:9). The Messiah comes from Israel.
> Christians understand the woman in different ways: as a picture for Israel, for the church or for Mary, the mother of Jesus. Especially in the Catholic and Orthodox churches, the woman is often connected with Mary. All these interpretations have something to them.
> Verse 3: The dragon is the devil (verse 9). Seven heads and ten horns show his great power. Red is the color of blood and violence.
> Verse 5: The child is Jesus, the Messiah (compare Psalm 2:9). “Caught up” means his resurrection and ascension into heaven. The dragon can’t harm him.
> Verse 6: In the Bible, the wilderness is a place where God protects and provides for his people, as for Israel after the exodus from Egypt.

## War in heaven (Verses 7–12)

[7] There was war in the sky.
Michael and his angels
made war on the dragon.
And the dragon and his angels made war.
[8] But they didn’t prevail.
And no place was found for them any more in heaven.
[9] The great dragon was thrown down,
the old serpent,
who is called the devil and Satan,
the deceiver of the whole world.
He was thrown down to the earth,
and his angels were thrown down with him.
[10] I heard a loud voice in heaven,
saying,
“Now the salvation has come,
and the power and the Kingdom of our God,
and the authority of his Christ.
For the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down,
who accuses them before our God day and night.
[11] They overcame him
because of the Lamb’s blood
and because of the word of their testimony.
They didn’t love their life,
even to death.
[12] Therefore rejoice,
heavens,
and you who dwell in them!
But woe to the earth and to the sea!
For the devil has gone down to you.
He has great wrath,
because he knows
that he has but a short time.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: Michael is an archangel. He also appears in the book of Daniel as the protector of the people of Israel (Daniel 12:1).
> Verse 9: “The old serpent” recalls the serpent in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3). Here the devil is called “deceiver.”
> Verse 10: “Satan” means in Hebrew: accuser. He accuses people. But he has nothing more to say before God. Whoever belongs to Jesus is no longer condemned.
> Verse 11: The victory doesn’t come through weapons, but through the Lamb and through the brave testimony of those who believe.
> Verse 12: An important comfort: The devil is already defeated. He rages because he knows that his time will soon be over.

## The dragon persecutes the woman (Verses 13–17)

[13] When the dragon saw
that he was thrown down to the earth,
he persecuted the woman
who had given birth to the male child.
[14] Two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman,
so that she might fly into the wilderness to her place.
There she will be nourished for a time, times, and half a time,
far from the face of the serpent.
[15] The serpent spewed water out of his mouth
after the woman,
like a river,
so that he might carry her away with the stream.
[16] But the earth helped the woman.
The earth opened its mouth
and swallowed up the river
which the dragon spewed out of his mouth.
[17] Then the dragon grew angry with the woman.
And he went away
to make war with the rest of her offspring,
who keep God’s commandments
and hold to Jesus’ testimony.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “A time, times, and half a time” are three and a half years, like the 1,260 days in verse 6. It is a limited time. The “wings of the great eagle” recall Exodus 19:4: God carried Israel as on eagles’ wings.
> Verse 16: Even the creation helps the people of God.
> Verse 17: “The rest of her offspring” are all who believe in Jesus. The dragon attacks them. That is why life as a Christian is sometimes hard. But God protects his people.
> In many manuscripts and Bibles there is one more sentence here about the dragon: He stood on the sand of the sea. Sometimes that is verse 18. Other manuscripts have the sentence with “I.” That is how the WEB has it. There the sentence stands at the beginning of Chapter 13: “Then I stood on the sand of the sea” (13:1).
