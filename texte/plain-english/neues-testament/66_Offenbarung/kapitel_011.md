# Revelation – Chapter 11
### The two witnesses and the seventh trumpet

---

## The temple is measured (Verses 1–2)

[1] I was given a reed like a measuring stick.
Someone said,
“Get up and measure God’s temple and the altar.
And count the people who worship there.
[2] But leave out the courtyard outside the temple.
Don’t measure it,
because it has been given to the nations.
They will trample the holy city for 42 months.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Measuring here means: God knows and protects what belongs to him. This recalls Ezekiel 40–42 and Zechariah 2, where the temple and Jerusalem are measured.
> Verse 2: “Forty-two months” are three and a half years. This period of time comes from the book of Daniel (Daniel 7:25 and 12:7). It is a picture for a limited time of distress. It comes up several times in the Revelation: as 42 months, as 1,260 days, or as “a time, times, and half a time.”
> Many understand the temple here as a picture for the church of God: It is oppressed, but kept safe at its very heart.

---

## The two witnesses (Verses 3–6)

[3] I will give power to my two witnesses.
They will be dressed in sackcloth,
and they will prophesy for 1,260 days.”
[4] These two witnesses are the two olive trees and the two lampstands.
They stand before the Lord of the earth.
[5] If anyone wants to harm them,
fire comes out of their mouth and burns up their enemies.
Anyone who wants to harm them must be killed in this way.
[6] They have the power to shut the sky,
so that no rain falls during the days when they prophesy.
They have power over the waters, to turn them into blood.
And they have power to strike the earth with every kind of plague,
as often as they want.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Sackcloth was a rough garment. People wore it when they were grieving or repenting.
> Verse 4: The two olive trees recall Zechariah 4.
> Verse 6: The two witnesses recall Elijah, who made the rain stop (1 Kings 17), and Moses, who turned the water into blood (Exodus 7).
> It is not certain who the two witnesses are. Many think: They stand for the whole church that bravely tells about God, as Moses and Elijah did.
> Verse 5: “Fire comes out of their mouth” is a picture for the power of their words, as in Jeremiah 5:14. It is not permission for violence.

---

## The witnesses are killed and raised (Verses 7–14)

[7] When they have finished their testimony,
the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war against them.
It will defeat them and kill them.
[8] Their dead bodies will lie in the street of the great city.
In a spiritual sense this city is called Sodom and Egypt.
Their Lord was also crucified there.
[9] People from the peoples, tribes, languages, and nations
will look at their dead bodies for three and a half days.
They will not allow anyone to bury the bodies in a tomb.
[10] Those who live on the earth will be happy about their death and celebrate.
They will send gifts to one another,
because these two prophets tormented those who live on the earth.
[11] After the three and a half days, the breath of life from God entered them.
They stood up on their feet.
Great fear came over the people who saw them.
[12] I heard a loud voice from heaven say to them, “Come up here!”
They went up into heaven in a cloud,
and their enemies watched them.
[13] At that time there was a great earthquake.
A tenth of the city fell.
7,000 people were killed in the earthquake.
The rest were terrified, and they gave glory to the God of heaven.
[14] The second disaster is over.
Look, the third disaster is coming soon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The “beast” is a picture for an evil, violent power. In Chapter 13 it is described more closely.
> Verse 8: “Sodom and Egypt” are names in a spiritual sense. They stand for wickedness and oppression. What is meant is every city and every power that sets itself against God and kills innocent people.

> **Important note**
> Verse 8 says: “Their Lord was also crucified there.” That is not an accusation against Jerusalem or against the Jewish people. Jesus was crucified by the Romans. The “great city” is a picture for the world that sets itself against God, everywhere and at every time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: “The breath of life” recalls Ezekiel 37: God makes dead bones alive again. The witnesses are raised like Jesus.
> Verse 13: Here there is something new: The people give glory to God. Only a tenth of the city falls; most people stay alive and turn to God. The brave testimony has had an effect.

---

## The seventh trumpet: God reigns (Verses 15–19)

[15] The seventh angel blew his trumpet.
Then loud voices in heaven said,
“The kingdom of the world has become the Kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ.
He will rule forever and ever!”
[16] The twenty-four elders sit on their thrones before God’s throne.
They fell on their faces and worshiped God.
[17] They said,
“We thank you, Lord God, the Almighty,
the one who is and who was.
For you have taken your great power and begun to rule.
[18] The nations were angry.
But now your anger has come.
The time has come for the dead to be judged.
It is time to give the reward to your servants, the prophets,
and to the saints and those who fear your name,
to the small and the great.
And it is time to destroy those who destroy the earth.”
[19] Then God’s temple in heaven was opened.
The ark of the Lord’s covenant could be seen in his temple.
Lightning, loud sounds, thunder, an earthquake, and great hail followed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: This is the high point: God rules. In the end all the power of the world belongs to God and his Christ. These words have become well known through famous music.
> Verse 17: God is now only called “the one who is and who was.” The “who is to come” is missing. For now he has come.
> Verse 18: God gives the reward “to the small and the great.” Before God every person counts, no matter how important he was in the world.
> “To destroy those who destroy the earth”: God does care when people destroy his creation. This sentence is important today, too.
> Verse 19: The ark of the covenant was the holiest sign of God’s presence in Israel. It had been lost since the destruction of the first temple. Here it is shown: God’s covenant stands; it is not lost.
