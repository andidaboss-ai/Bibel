# Revelation – Chapter 11
### The two witnesses and the seventh trumpet

---

## The temple is measured (Verses 1–2)

[1] A reed was given to me,
like a measuring rod.
Someone said,
“Rise
and measure God’s temple
and the altar
and those
who worship in it.
[2] But leave out the court
which is outside of the temple,
and don’t measure it.
For it has been given to the nations.
They will tread the holy city under foot
for forty-two months.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Measuring here means: God knows and protects what belongs to him. This recalls Ezekiel 40–42 and Zechariah 2, where the temple and Jerusalem are measured.
> Verse 2: “Forty-two months” are three and a half years. This period of time comes from the book of Daniel (Daniel 7:25 and 12:7). It is a picture for a limited time of distress. It comes up several times in the Revelation: as 42 months, as 1,260 days, or as “a time, times, and half a time.”
> Many understand the temple here as a picture for the church of God: It is oppressed, but kept safe at its very heart.

---

## The two witnesses (Verses 3–6)

[3] And I will give power to my two witnesses.
They will prophesy for one thousand two hundred sixty days,
clothed in sackcloth.”
[4] These are the two olive trees
and the two lampstands,
standing before the Lord of the earth.
[5] If anyone desires to harm them,
fire comes out of their mouth
and devours their enemies.
If anyone desires to harm them,
he must be killed in this way.
[6] They have the power
to shut up the sky,
so that it doesn’t rain during the days of their prophecy.
They have power over the waters,
to turn them into blood,
and to strike the earth with every plague,
as often as they desire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: Sackcloth was a rough garment. People wore it when they were grieving or repenting.
> Verse 4: The two olive trees recall Zechariah 4.
> Verse 6: The two witnesses recall Elijah, who made the rain stop (1 Kings 17), and Moses, who turned the water into blood (Exodus 7).
> It is not certain who the two witnesses are. Many think: They stand for the whole church that bravely tells about God, as Moses and Elijah did.
> Verse 5: “Fire comes out of their mouth” is a picture for the power of their words, as in Jeremiah 5:14. It is not permission for violence.

---

## The witnesses are killed and raised (Verses 7–14)

[7] When they have finished their testimony,
the beast
that comes up out of the abyss
will make war with them.
It will overcome them
and kill them.
[8] Their dead bodies will lie in the street
of the great city.
Spiritually it is called Sodom and Egypt.
There their Lord was also crucified.
[9] People from among the peoples,
tribes, languages and nations
will look at their dead bodies
for three and a half days.
And they will not allow
their dead bodies to be laid in a tomb.
[10] Those who dwell on the earth
will rejoice over them and be glad.
They will give gifts to one another,
because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth.
[11] After the three and a half days,
the breath of life from God entered into them.
And they stood on their feet.
Great fear fell on those
who saw them.
[12] I heard a loud voice from heaven
saying to them,
“Come up here!”
And they went up into heaven in a cloud,
and their enemies saw them.
[13] In that day there was a great earthquake.
A tenth of the city fell.
Seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake.
And the rest were terrified
and gave glory to the God of heaven.
[14] The second woe is past.
Look,
the third woe comes quickly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: The “beast” is a picture for an evil, violent power. In Chapter 13 it is described more closely.
> Verse 8: “Sodom and Egypt” are names in a spiritual sense. They stand for wickedness and oppression. What is meant is every city and every power that sets itself against God and kills innocent people.

> **Important note**
> Verse 8 says: “There their Lord was also crucified.” That is not an accusation against Jerusalem or against the Jewish people. Jesus was crucified by the Romans. The “great city” is a picture for the world that sets itself against God, everywhere and at every time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: “The breath of life” recalls Ezekiel 37: God makes dead bones alive again. The witnesses are raised like Jesus.
> Verse 13: Here there is something new: The people give glory to God. Only a tenth of the city falls; most people stay alive and turn to God. The brave testimony has had an effect.

## The seventh trumpet: God reigns (Verses 15–19)

[15] The seventh angel sounded.
Then great voices in heaven followed,
saying,
“The kingdom of the world
has become the Kingdom of our Lord
and of his Christ.
He will reign
forever and ever!”
[16] The twenty-four elders,
who sit on their thrones before God’s throne,
fell on their faces
and worshiped God.
[17] They said,
“We give you thanks,
Lord God,
the Almighty,
the one who is and who was,
because you have taken your great power
and reigned.
[18] The nations were angry.
And your wrath came,
and the time
for the dead to be judged,
and to give your servants,
the prophets,
their reward,
as well as to the saints
and those who fear your name,
to the small and the great,
and to destroy those
who destroy the earth.”
[19] And God’s temple that is in heaven was opened.
And the ark of the Lord’s covenant was seen in his temple.
And lightnings, sounds, thunders,
an earthquake
and great hail followed.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: This is the high point: God reigns. In the end all the power of the world belongs to God and his Christ. These words have become well known through famous music.
> Verse 17: God is now only called “the one who is and who was.” The “who is to come” is missing. For now he has come.
> Verse 18: God gives the reward “to the small and the great.” Before God every person counts, no matter how important he was in the world.
> “To destroy those who destroy the earth”: God does care when people destroy his creation. This sentence is important today, too.
> Verse 19: The ark of the covenant was the holiest sign of God’s presence in Israel. It had been lost since the destruction of the first temple. Here it is shown: God’s covenant stands; it is not lost.
