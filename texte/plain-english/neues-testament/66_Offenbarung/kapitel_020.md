# Revelation – Chapter 20
### The thousand years and the last judgment

---

## Satan is bound (Verses 1–3)

[1] I saw an angel coming down out of heaven.
He had the key of the abyss
and a great chain in his hand.
[2] He seized the dragon,
the old serpent,
who is the devil and Satan,
who deceives the whole inhabited earth.
And he bound him for a thousand years.
[3] He threw him into the abyss,
shut it
and sealed it over him,
so that he would deceive the nations no more
until the thousand years were finished.
After this, he must be freed for a short time.

> **What does this mean?**
> The devil is bound. He is not as strong as he pretends. A single angel is enough to tie him up.
> Verse 3: He may no longer deceive the nations. His power is limited.

---

## The thousand years (Verses 4–6)

[4] I saw thrones.
And people sat on them,
and judgment was given to them.
I saw the souls of those
who had been beheaded
for the testimony of Jesus
and for the word of God.
They didn’t worship the beast nor his image,
and didn’t receive the mark on their forehead and on their hand.
They lived
and reigned with Christ for a thousand years.
[5] The rest of the dead didn’t live
until the thousand years were finished.
This is the first resurrection.
[6] Blessed and holy is he
who has part in the first resurrection.
Over these, the second death has no power.
But they will be priests of God and of Christ
and will reign with him one thousand years.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: The martyrs, who were killed because they stayed faithful, are honored. The world condemned them. But God declares them right.
> Christians have thought very differently about the “thousand years”:
> – Some think: The thousand years are a picture for the time of the church now, between the resurrection of Jesus and his coming again. Many large churches see it this way.
> – Others think: Jesus comes again and then reigns on earth for a thousand years.
> – Still others think: The world gets better little by little, and after that Jesus comes.
> All agree: Christ wins, and the faithful are with him. The number thousand means a very long, complete time.

> **Important note**
> The National Socialists (Nazis) called their state the “Thousand-Year Reich.” In doing so they misused this Bible text. Their empire was the opposite of what is meant here: It murdered millions of people, especially six million Jews. It has nothing to do with God’s Kingdom.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: This is the fifth “Blessed” in the Revelation. Whoever belongs to Christ doesn’t need to fear the “second death.”

## The last battle (Verses 7–10)

[7] And after the thousand years,
Satan will be released from his prison.
[8] He will come out
to deceive the nations which are in the four corners of the earth,
Gog and Magog,
and to gather them together to the war.
Their number is like the sand of the sea.
[9] They went up over the width of the earth
and surrounded the camp of the saints
and the beloved city.
But fire came down out of heaven from God
and devoured them.
[10] And the devil
who deceived them
was thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur,
where the beast and the false prophet are also.
They will be tormented day and night
forever and ever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 8: “Gog and Magog” appear in the prophet Ezekiel (Ezekiel 38–39). There they are pictures for enemy powers from faraway lands. Here they stand for all the powers that set themselves against God. They are not particular countries of today.
> Verse 9: Again there is no battle. God ends the attack immediately. The saints don’t have to fight themselves.
> Verse 10: The devil is defeated for good. He can never deceive again.

## The great judgment (Verses 11–15)

[11] I saw a great white throne
and him who sat on it.
From his face the earth and the sky fled away.
And no place was found for them.
[12] I saw the dead,
the great and the small,
standing before the throne.
And books were opened.
And another book was opened:
that is the book of life.
The dead were judged out of the things
which were written in the books,
according to their works.
[13] The sea gave up the dead
who were in it.
Death and Hades gave up the dead
who were in them.
And they were judged,
each one according to his works.
[14] Death and Hades
were thrown into the lake of fire.
This is the second death,
the lake of fire.
[15] If anyone was not found written in the book of life,
he was thrown into the lake of fire.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Before God all stand as equals, “the great and the small.” No one can buy his way out with money or power.
> “According to their works”: It is not unimportant how people live. God takes seriously what people do, especially what they have done to others. For the victims of injustice that is a comfort.
> But there is also the “book of life.” In it are the names of the people who belong to the Lamb (13:8 and 21:27). Salvation doesn’t come from your own works, but from God’s grace.
> Verse 14: A wonderful sentence: Death itself is destroyed. There will never be death again (compare 21:4 and 1 Corinthians 15:26).

> **Important note**
> These verses are serious. But they are not meant to make anyone afraid who is seeking God. Whoever wants to know whether he is written in the book of life may hold on to Jesus: He promised not to throw out anyone who comes to him (compare John 6:37).
> We don’t know exactly how God will deal with each person in the end. But we do know: God is just, and God is love.
