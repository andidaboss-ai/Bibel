# Revelation – Chapter 22
### The water of life – Jesus is coming

---

## The river and the tree of life (Verses 1–5)

[1] He showed me a river of water of life,
clear as crystal.
It came out of the throne of God and of the Lamb
[2] and flowed through the middle of the city’s street.
On both sides of the river
was the tree of life.
It bears twelve kinds of fruits
and yields its fruit every month.
And the leaves of the tree
were for the healing of the nations.
[3] There will be no curse any more.
The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in the city.
And his servants will serve him.
[4] They will see his face.
And his name will be on their foreheads.
[5] There will be no night.
They need no light from a lamp
and no light from the sun.
For the Lord God will give them light.
And they will reign forever and ever.

> **What does this mean?**
> Here the circle of the whole Bible closes. At the beginning the tree of life stood in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9). After the curse, people no longer had access to it (Genesis 3:22–24). Now it stands there again, for everyone.
> Verses 1–2: The river recalls the river flowing out of the temple in the prophet Ezekiel (Ezekiel 47:1–12). Wherever it flows, everything comes to life.
> Verse 2: “For the healing of the nations”: God’s new world heals all the wounds between the nations.
> Verse 3: “No curse any more”: Everything that has gone wrong since the beginning is healed.
> Verse 4: “They will see his face”: That is the greatest longing in the Bible. Moses was not allowed to see God’s face (Exodus 33:20). Now it becomes possible. There is no more separation between God and people.

---

## Jesus is coming soon (Verses 6–15)

[6] He said to me,
“These words are faithful and true.
The Lord God of the spirits of the prophets
sent his angel
to show to his servants
the things which must happen soon.”
[7] “Look,
I am coming soon!
Blessed is he
who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book.”
[8] I, John,
am the one
who heard and saw these things.
And when I had heard and seen,
I fell down before the feet of the angel
who had shown me these things,
to worship him.
[9] He said to me,
“You must not do that!
I am a fellow servant with you
and with your brothers,
the prophets,
and with those
who keep the words of this book.
Worship God!”
[10] He said to me,
“Don’t seal up the words of the prophecy of this book,
for the time is at hand.
[11] He who acts unjustly,
let him act unjustly still.
He who is filthy,
let him be filthy still.
He who is righteous,
let him do righteousness still.
And he who is holy,
let him be holy still.”
[12] “Look,
I am coming soon!
And my reward is with me,
to repay to each one
according to his work.
[13] I am the Alpha and the Omega,
the First and the Last,
the Beginning and the End.
[14] Blessed are those
who do his commandments.
They have the right to the tree of life
and may enter in by the gates into the city.
[15] Outside are the dogs,
the sorcerers,
the sexually immoral,
the murderers,
the idolaters
and everyone who loves and practices falsehood.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: This is the sixth “Blessed” in the Revelation. Verse 14 is the seventh and last. Seven is the number of perfection.
> Verse 10: In the book of Daniel the words were to be sealed (Daniel 12:4). Not here: The message is to be read openly, now.
> Verse 11: This sentence sounds harsh. It probably means: Time is pressing. Every person shows by his life what he decides for. But the door is still open (verse 17).
> Verse 14: Here it says: “Blessed are those who do his commandments.” Some manuscripts have instead: Blessed are those who wash their robes (compare 7:14).
> Verse 15: At that time “dogs” was an insult for people who behaved shamelessly. It is a picture. It is not permission to insult other people. And here too it is true: The gates of the city are never shut (21:25). Everyone may turn back.

## Come! (Verses 16–21)

[16] I, Jesus,
have sent my angel
to testify these things to you for the churches.
I am the root and the offspring of David,
the Bright and Morning Star.”
[17] The Spirit and the bride say,
“Come!”
And he who hears,
let him say,
“Come!”
He who is thirsty,
let him come.
He who desires,
let him take the water of life freely.
[18] I testify to everyone
who hears the words of the prophecy of this book:
If anyone adds to them,
God will add to him the plagues
which are written in this book.
[19] And if anyone takes away from the words
of the book of this prophecy,
God will take away his part
from the tree of life
and out of the holy city,
which are written in this book.
[20] He who testifies these things
says,
“Yes,
I am coming soon.”
Amen!
Yes, come, Lord Jesus!
[21] The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ
be with all the saints.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 16: Jesus himself speaks. He is “the root and the offspring of David”: He comes from the Jewish people and is the promised Messiah (compare Isaiah 11:1). He is the “Morning Star,” the star that announces the new day.
> Verse 17: This is the great invitation at the end of the Bible. Everyone who is thirsty may come. The water of life is free. No one who wants to come is shut out.
> Verses 18–19: This warning is for the book of Revelation. At that time books were copied by hand. No one was to change the message. The warning recalls Deuteronomy 4:2. For this translation it was an important rule: No verse and no word is left out.
> Verse 20: “Come, Lord Jesus!” is one of the oldest prayers of Christians. In Aramaic, the language of Jesus, it is “Maranatha” (compare 1 Corinthians 16:22).
> Verse 21: The Bible ends with a blessing: grace. God’s kindness has the last word.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Revelation?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Jesus lives:** John sees Jesus in his glory. “Don’t be afraid” (Chapter 1).
> **Seven letters:** Jesus knows his churches, praises them, warns them and gives them courage (Chapters 2–3).
> **God sits on the throne:** Not the emperor, but God rules the world (Chapter 4).
> **The Lamb wins:** God doesn’t win through violence, but through Jesus giving himself on the cross (Chapter 5).
> **Seals, trumpets and bowls:** Pictures for war, need and judgment. Evil has limits, and God calls people to repent again and again (Chapters 6–16).
> **Dragon and beasts:** Behind violence and oppression is evil. But it is already defeated (Chapters 12–13).
> **Babylon falls:** Every power built on greed, exploitation and violence goes down (Chapters 17–18).
> **The wedding of the Lamb:** God invites people to his feast (Chapter 19).
> **The judgment:** God takes injustice seriously. And death itself is destroyed (Chapter 20).
> **All things new:** God lives with people. No more tears, no death, no pain (Chapters 21–22).
> **For today:** The Revelation doesn’t want to make people afraid, but to give hope. No matter how dark it is: God has the last word, and his last word is grace.

## Looking back: What have we read in the whole Bible?

> **What does this mean?**
> The Bible is not a single story, but a whole library. It has 66 books and 1,189 chapters. Many people wrote it over more than a thousand years.
> **In the beginning:** God creates heaven and earth. Everything is good. People are created in God’s image (Genesis 1–2).
> **The break:** People turn away from God. Suffering, violence and death come into the world (Genesis 3–11).
> **God’s way with Israel:** God calls Abraham and promises: Through you all peoples will be blessed. He frees Israel from Egypt, gives the Torah at Sinai and goes with his people through good and hard times. Prophets call people to repent and announce hope. Psalms bring praise and lament before God.
> **Jesus:** God becomes human. Jesus, a Jew from Nazareth, tells about God’s Kingdom, heals, forgives, dies on the cross and is raised by God.
> **The first church:** The Holy Spirit comes. The Good News goes from Jerusalem into all the world. Letters encourage the churches.
> **In the end:** God makes all things new. The tree of life, which was lost at the beginning, is there again. God lives with people.
> A red thread runs through the whole Bible: God doesn’t give up on his people. He seeks them, he saves them, and he stays faithful to his promises, to Israel and to all peoples.

> **What comes next?**
> With this, the whole Bible has been put into plain language, all 66 books and 1,189 chapters, verse by verse, without leaving anything out.
> The Bible is not something you read once and then put away. Many people read it again and again all their lives. It is good to read it together with others and to talk about it.
> Whoever has questions can turn, for example, to a local church, a pastor, a priest or a counselor.
