# 2 Timothy – Chapter 4
### I have finished the race

---

## Preach the word (Verses 1–5)

[1] I solemnly command you before God
and before the Lord Jesus Christ,
who will judge the living and the dead
at his appearing
and his Kingdom:
[2] Preach the word.
Be ready
in season and out of season.
Correct,
rebuke
and encourage,
with all patience and teaching.
[3] For the time will come
when people will not put up with sound teaching.
But having itching ears,
they will gather around themselves teachers
who suit their own desires.
[4] They will turn their ears away from the truth
and turn to fables.
[5] But you, be sober in all things.
Endure hardship.
Do the work of an evangelist.
Fulfill your ministry completely.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “In season and out of season”: You should not pass on the Good News only when it is convenient. But always “with all patience,” not pushy or rude.
> Verses 3–4: “Itching ears” means: wanting to hear only what you like. That happens in every age.
> Verse 5: An “evangelist” is someone who passes on the Good News (in Greek *euangelion*).

---

## Paul says farewell (Verses 6–8)

[6] For I am already being poured out as an offering.
The time of my departure has come.
[7] I have fought the good fight.
I have finished the race.
I have kept the faith.
[8] From now on the crown of righteousness is stored up for me.
The Lord,
the righteous judge,
will give it to me on that day.
And not to me only,
but also to all
who have loved his appearing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: “Poured out as an offering”: A picture of the drink offering, in which wine was poured out (compare Philippians 2:17). Paul expects to be executed. According to old tradition, Paul was executed in Rome, probably under Emperor Nero.
> “Departure”: Paul speaks of death as of a journey. He is going to Christ.
> Verse 7: Three pictures: the fight, the race in the stadium and faithfulness. Paul looks back on his life with contentment.
> Verse 8: The “crown” was a wreath of leaves that winners received at a competition. God gives this crown not only to Paul, but to all who hope in Jesus.

---

## Personal requests (Verses 9–18)

[9] Do your best
to come to me soon.
[10] For Demas has left me,
because he loved this present world,
and has gone to Thessalonica.
Crescens has gone to Galatia,
Titus to Dalmatia.
[11] Only Luke is with me.
Get Mark and bring him with you.
For he is useful to me for service.
[12] But I sent Tychicus to Ephesus.
[13] When you come,
bring the cloak
that I left at Troas with Carpus,
and the books,
especially the parchments.
[14] Alexander,
the coppersmith,
did me much harm.
The Lord will repay him according to his deeds.
[15] You too, beware of him.
For he strongly opposed our words.
[16] At my first defense in court
no one stood by me.
All left me.
May it not be held against them.
[17] But the Lord stood by me
and strengthened me,
so that through me the message might be fully proclaimed
and all the nations might hear it.
So I was rescued out of the mouth of the lion.
[18] And the Lord will rescue me from every evil work
and keep me safe for his heavenly Kingdom.
To him be the glory forever and ever.
Amen.

*parchments* = writing material made from animal skin

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 10–11: Paul is lonely. Some have left him. Only Luke, the doctor, is with him.
> Verse 11: Mark had once parted from Paul (Acts 15:37–39). Now Paul specifically asks for him. Reconciliation is possible.
> Verse 13: A very human wish: Paul is cold in prison and needs his cloak. And he wants to read his books.
> Verse 16: “May it not be held against them”: Paul forgives those who let him down, just as Jesus forgave on the cross (Luke 23:34).
> Verse 17: “The mouth of the lion”: a picture of great danger to life (compare Psalm 22:21, in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 22:22).
> Verse 18: Paul trusts: Even if he dies, he is safe with God.

---

## Last greetings (Verses 19–22)

[19] Greet Prisca and Aquila
and the house of Onesiphorus.
[20] Erastus stayed in Corinth.
But I left Trophimus sick at Miletus.
[21] Do your best
to come before winter.
Eubulus greets you,
as do Pudens,
Linus,
Claudia
and all the brothers.
[22] The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
Grace be with you.
Amen.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Prisca (also called Priscilla) and Aquila were a married couple and close coworkers of Paul. Prisca is often named first. She was probably especially important in the church.
> Verse 20: Paul did not heal Trophimus, but left him behind sick. Apostles too could not heal every illness. Illness is not a punishment and not a sign of weak faith.
> Verse 21: “Before winter”: In winter no ships sailed. Paul hopes to see Timothy one more time.
> Among those sending greetings is also a woman: Claudia. According to old tradition, Linus later became a leader of the church in Rome.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the second letter to Timothy?

> **What does this mean?**
> **A farewell letter:** Paul is in prison in Rome and expects to die. He writes to Timothy, his *beloved child* in faith.
> **No spirit of fear:** God gives power, love and self-control (chapter 1).
> **Faith is passed on:** from grandmother to mother to son, from Paul to Timothy, from Timothy to others (chapters 1 and 2).
> **God remains faithful:** even when we are faithless (chapter 2).
> **Staying gentle:** God’s servants are not to quarrel, but to be patient and gentle (chapter 2).
> **The holy Scriptures:** They are God-breathed and help toward faith and a good life (chapter 3).
> **Finishing the race:** Paul looks back on his life. He has kept the faith (chapter 4).
> **Loneliness and forgiveness:** Many have left Paul. He forgives them and trusts in God (chapter 4).
> **For today:** Even at the end of life we may know that we are safe in God’s hands.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter to Titus, another coworker of Paul, on the island of Crete.
