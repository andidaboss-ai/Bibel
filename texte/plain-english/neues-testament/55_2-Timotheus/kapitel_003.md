# 2 Timothy – Chapter 3
### Stay with what you have learned

---

## Hard times (Verses 1–9)

[1] But know this:
In the last days hard times will come.
[2] For people will love themselves
and love money.
They will be boastful,
arrogant
and blasphemers.
They will be disobedient to parents,
unthankful
and unholy,
[3] without love for their own families,
unforgiving,
slanderers,
without self-control,
brutal
and not lovers of good.
[4] They will be traitors,
headstrong
and conceited.
They love pleasure more than God.
[5] They hold a form of godliness,
but they have denied its power.
Turn away from such people.
[6] For among them are those
who creep into houses
and take captive gullible women
who are loaded down with sins
and led away by all kinds of desires.
[7] They are always learning
and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.
[8] Just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses,
so these also oppose the truth.
Their minds are corrupt,
and they have failed in the faith.
[9] But they will not get far.
For their foolishness will be obvious to all,
as it was with those two.

> **What does this mean?**
> Blasphemers means: people who mock or insult God.
> Gullible means: easily fooled.
> Verse 1: For the New Testament, “the last days” means the whole time since Jesus. It is not about a particular date.
> Verses 2–5: The list describes people who think only of themselves. The worst thing: They act pious, but their lives are not changed by God (verse 5).
> Verses 6–7: These teachers deliberately looked for people who were unsure or burdened, in order to influence them. At that time women usually had no chance of a good education. That is why they were easier to deceive. This is no judgment about women in themselves.
> For today: Today too there are groups and people who deliberately approach people in crisis and make them dependent. Counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse can help.
> Verse 8: According to Jewish tradition, Jannes and Jambres are the names of the Egyptian magicians who opposed Moses (compare Exodus 7:11). In the Bible itself their names are mentioned only here.

## Stay with what you have learned (Verses 10–17)

[10] But you have followed my teaching,
my conduct,
my purpose,
my faith,
my patience,
my love
and my steadfastness,
[11] also my persecutions and sufferings,
the things that happened to me at Antioch,
at Iconium
and at Lystra.
I endured those persecutions.
And the Lord rescued me out of them all.
[12] Yes,
all
who want to live a godly life in Christ Jesus
will be persecuted.
[13] But evil people and impostors
will grow worse and worse.
They deceive others
and are deceived themselves.
[14] But you must remain in the things
that you have learned
and are sure of.
For you know
from whom you learned them.
[15] And from infancy you have known the holy Scriptures.
They are able to make you wise for salvation
through faith in Christ Jesus.
[16] Every Scripture is God-breathed
and useful for teaching,
for showing what is wrong,
for correcting
and for training in righteousness,
[17] so that each person
who belongs to God
may be complete
and well equipped for every good work.

> **What does this mean?**
> Impostors means: people who pretend to be someone they are not.
> Verse 11: Paul was persecuted in these cities. In Lystra he was even stoned (Acts 14:19). Timothy came from this region and probably saw it happen.
> Verse 12: Whoever takes their faith seriously may face opposition because of it. But that doesn’t mean you should seek suffering.
> Verses 14–15: Timothy came to know the holy Scriptures as a child, from his mother and grandmother (compare 1:5). This means the Scriptures of Israel, our Old Testament.
> Verse 16: The WEB says “God-breathed,” that is: *breathed out by God*. The Bible came into being through God’s Spirit. But it was written by human beings, in their language and in their time.
> Christians understand this verse in different ways: Some stress that every word of the Bible comes exactly from God. Others stress that God speaks through human words that must be understood in their time. Everyone agrees: The Bible is important for faith and life.
> Verse 17: The goal of Scripture is that people do good.
