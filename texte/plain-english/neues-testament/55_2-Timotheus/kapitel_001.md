# 2 Timothy – Chapter 1
### Don’t be ashamed

---

## Before we begin: The second letter to Timothy

This letter is like a farewell letter. Paul is in prison in Rome (1:17). He expects to be executed soon (4:6). Many have left him. Only Luke is still with him (4:11).
Paul writes to Timothy, his *beloved child* in faith. He encourages him to stay faithful, even when it gets hard. And he asks him to come to him soon.
As with the first letter to Timothy, many scholars think that a student of Paul wrote the letter, in Paul’s spirit. Others consider Paul himself to be the author. This letter in particular contains many personal details.
The second letter to Timothy has 4 chapters.

---

## Greeting (Verses 1–2)

[1] Paul,
an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God,
according to the promise of the life
which is in Christ Jesus,
[2] writes to Timothy,
his beloved child:
Grace,
mercy
and peace
from God the Father
and from Christ Jesus our Lord.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “The promise of the life which is in Christ Jesus”: Paul expects death. But he knows: In Christ there is life that does not end.

---

## Thanks and encouragement (Verses 3–7)

[3] I thank God,
whom I serve as my forefathers did,
with a pure conscience.
Without ceasing I remember you in my prayers,
night and day.
[4] I long
to see you.
I remember your tears.
When I see you,
I will be filled with joy.
[5] I remember your sincere faith.
It lived first in your grandmother Lois
and in your mother Eunice.
And I am convinced:
It lives in you also.
[6] That is why I remind you:
Stir up the gift of God
that is in you
since I laid my hands on you.
[7] For God didn’t give us a spirit of fear,
but of power,
love
and self-control.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “As my forefathers did”: Paul sees himself in the tradition of his Jewish ancestors. He serves the same God as they did.
> Verse 4: “Your tears”: Probably at their last farewell. Friendship and tears are part of faith.
> Verse 5: Faith was passed on in the family, from grandmother Lois to mother Eunice and to Timothy. His mother was Jewish (Acts 16:1). Women passed on the faith.
> Verse 6: “Stir up”: like a fire that is fanned again. Gifts can fall asleep if they are not used.
> Verse 7 is a great verse of comfort: God’s Spirit doesn’t make us fearful, but brave, loving and self-controlled.
> Important: This doesn’t mean that Christians are never afraid. Fear is a normal feeling. Whoever suffers from strong fears may seek help. That is no sign of weak faith. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## Don’t be ashamed of the Good News (Verses 8–14)

[8] So don’t be ashamed of the testimony of our Lord,
nor of me,
his prisoner.
But suffer hardship with me for the Good News,
by the power of God.
[9] He saved us
and called us with a holy calling,
not because of our works,
but according to his own purpose and grace.
This grace was given to us in Christ Jesus
before times eternal.
[10] But now it has been revealed
by the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus.
He abolished death
and brought life and immortality to light
through the Good News.
[11] For this I was appointed
as a preacher,
an apostle
and a teacher of the nations.
[12] That is why I also suffer these things.
Yet I am not ashamed.
For I know him
whom I have believed.
And I am convinced
that he is able to guard
what I have entrusted to him
until that day.
[13] Hold to the pattern of sound words
that you heard from me,
in faith and love
which are in Christ Jesus.
[14] Guard the good treasure
that was entrusted to you,
through the Holy Spirit
who lives in us.

> **What does this mean?**
> Immortality means: life that never ends.
> Verse 8: At that time being a prisoner was a disgrace. Many were ashamed to be connected with Paul. Paul asks Timothy: Stand by me and by the Good News.
> Verse 9: Salvation is God’s gift, not something we earn.
> Verse 10: “He abolished death”: Death no longer has the last word.
> Verse 12: “I know him whom I have believed”: Paul doesn’t trust in an idea, but in a person, in Jesus. He puts his life into God’s hands.

---

## Faithfulness and unfaithfulness (Verses 15–18)

[15] You know
that all who are in Asia have turned away from me,
among them Phygelus and Hermogenes.
[16] May the Lord grant mercy to the house of Onesiphorus.
For he often refreshed me
and was not ashamed of my chain.
[17] But when he was in Rome,
he searched for me eagerly
and found me.
[18] May the Lord grant him
to find the Lord’s mercy on that day.
And how much he helped in Ephesus,
you know very well.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: “Asia” was the Roman province in the west of what is now Turkey, with Ephesus as its capital. Many had let Paul down. That hurt him.
> Verses 16–17: Onesiphorus was different. He searched for Paul in Rome, even though it was dangerous, and visited him in prison.
> For today: Visiting prisoners, the sick and the lonely is a work of love (compare Matthew 25:36). A visit can mean a lot.
> Verse 18: Paul speaks of the “house of Onesiphorus” and prays for him. Some interpreters think that Onesiphorus had already died.
