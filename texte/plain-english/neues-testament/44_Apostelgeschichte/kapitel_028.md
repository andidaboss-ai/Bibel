# Acts – Chapter 28
### Paul comes to Rome

---

## On the island of Malta (Verses 1–10)

[1] When we had escaped,
we learned
that the island was called Malta.
[2] The local people showed us unusual kindness.
They lit a fire
and welcomed us all,
because it was raining
and it was cold.
[3] Paul gathered a bundle of sticks
and laid them on the fire.
Then a viper came out because of the heat
and fastened itself on his hand.
[4] When the local people saw the creature hanging from his hand,
they said to one another,
“No doubt this man is a murderer.
Though he has escaped from the sea,
Justice has not allowed him to live.”
[5] But Paul shook off the creature into the fire.
He wasn’t harmed.
[6] They expected
that he would swell up
or suddenly fall down dead.
They watched for a long time.
But when they saw
that nothing bad happened to him,
they changed their minds
and said
that he was a god.
[7] Near that place
the chief man of the island had lands.
His name was Publius.
He welcomed us
and kindly took care of us for three days.
[8] The father of Publius lay sick in bed
with fever and dysentery.
Paul went in to him,
prayed,
laid his hands on him
and healed him.
[9] After this the other sick people on the island
also came
and were cured.
[10] They honored us with many honors.
And when we sailed,
they put on board the things
we needed.

*viper* = a poisonous snake
*dysentery* = a serious illness of the bowels

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: The people of Malta help strangers who have been shipwrecked. Hospitality toward people in need is a sign of humanity.
> Verse 4: “Justice” (in Greek *Dike*) was a goddess for the people there. They thought: Whoever goes through something terrible must be guilty. That is not true. Misfortune is not a punishment.
> Verse 6: First they think Paul is a murderer, then a god. Both are wrong. He is a human being who trusts God.
> Verse 8: Paul prays and lays his hands on him. The healing comes from God.
> For today: Whoever takes in people in need does something good, no matter where they come from.

## From Malta to Rome (Verses 11–16)

[11] After three months we set sail in a ship of Alexandria.
It had spent the winter at the island.
Its figurehead was “The Twin Brothers.”
[12] We landed at Syracuse
and stayed there three days.
[13] From there we sailed around
and arrived at Rhegium.
After one day a south wind came up.
On the second day we came to Puteoli.
[14] There we found brothers.
They begged us
to stay with them for seven days.
And so we came to Rome.
[15] The brothers there had heard about us.
They came to meet us as far as The Market of Appius
and The Three Taverns.
When Paul saw them,
he thanked God
and took courage.
[16] When we came into Rome,
the centurion handed the prisoners over to the captain of the guard.
But Paul was allowed to live by himself,
with the soldier
who guarded him.

*figurehead* = a carved figure on the front of a ship

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: “The Twin Brothers” were Castor and Pollux, two gods who, as sailors believed, protected ships.
> Verse 14: There are already Christians in Italy. Paul is not the first to bring the good news to Italy.
> Verse 15: The Christians from Rome walk a long way to meet Paul. The Market of Appius was about 40 miles (65 kilometers) from Rome. Paul takes courage: He is not alone.
> Verse 16: Paul is a prisoner, but he may live in his own lodging, guarded by a soldier.

## Paul speaks with the Jews in Rome (Verses 17–29)

[17] After three days
Paul called together the leaders of the Jews.
When they had come together,
he said to them,
“Brothers,
I have done nothing against our people
or the customs of our ancestors.
Still I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem
into the hands of the Romans.
[18] They questioned me
and wanted to set me free,
because I had done nothing
that deserves death.
[19] But the Jews spoke against it.
So I was forced
to appeal to Caesar.
But I have nothing to accuse my nation of.
[20] For this reason I asked
to see you
and to speak with you.
For because of the hope of Israel
I am bound with this chain.”
[21] They said to him,
“We have received no letters about you from Judea.
And none of the brothers has come here
and reported or said anything bad about you.
[22] But we want to hear from you
what you think.
For about this sect we know
that everywhere people speak against it.”
[23] They set a day with him.
Then many people came to him at his lodging.
He explained everything to them
and testified about God’s Kingdom.
From morning until evening
he tried
to persuade them about Jesus,
from the law of Moses
and from the prophets.
[24] Some believed
the things
that were spoken.
Others did not believe.
[25] They didn’t agree among themselves
and left.
Before that, Paul spoke one more message:
“The Holy Spirit spoke rightly
through Isaiah the prophet to our ancestors.
[26] He said,
‘Go to this people and say:
You will hear and hear,
but will in no way understand.
You will see and see,
but will in no way really see.
[27] For this people’s heart has grown hard.
Their ears hear with difficulty.
They have closed their eyes.
Otherwise they would see with their eyes,
hear with their ears,
understand with their heart,
and turn again,
and I would heal them.’
[28] So know this:
The salvation of God is sent to the nations,
and they will listen.”
[29] When he had said these words,
the Jews left
and argued strongly among themselves.

*lodging* = a place where someone lives for a while

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 17–20: Paul calls the Jews in Rome “Brothers.” He says: I have nothing against my people. He is bound with this chain “because of the hope of Israel.” Paul stays connected with his people to the very end.
> Verse 24: Some Jews come to faith, others don’t. The people are not against Paul; they are divided.
> Verses 26–27 quote Isaiah 6:9–10. The prophet Isaiah said these words to his own people. They are a lament from within Israel, not a judgment from outside.
> Verse 28: This does not mean that God has rejected his people Israel. In his letter to the Romans, Paul himself writes that God has not rejected his people (Romans 11:1–2). God’s gifts and his calling cannot be taken back (Romans 11:29).
> Important: In history Christians often used these verses to claim that God had rejected the Jews. That was wrong and caused much suffering. Today the churches teach: God’s covenant with Israel remains valid (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).
> Verse 29: This verse is missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has it.

## Paul preaches in Rome (Verses 30–31)

[30] Paul stayed two whole years
in his own rented house.
He welcomed all
who came to him.
[31] He preached God’s Kingdom
and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ
with all boldness,
without hindrance.

> **What does this mean?**
> The book ends open. We don’t learn how Paul’s trial ended. According to old tradition, Paul later died in Rome as a martyr, probably under the emperor Nero.
> Verse 31: The last word of the book in Greek means “without hindrance.” Paul is a prisoner, but the word of God is not a prisoner.
> At the beginning Jesus had said that the apostles would be his witnesses to the ends of the earth (1:8). Now the good news has arrived in Rome, at the center of the world of that time.

## Looking back: What have we read in the Acts of the Apostles?

> **What does this mean?**
> **The continuation of Luke’s Gospel:** Luke tells what happens after Easter. Jesus goes to the Father, but he sends the Holy Spirit.
> **Pentecost:** The Holy Spirit comes. People from many countries hear the good news in their own language. The church begins.
> **The first church:** The first Christians share, pray, break bread together and care for the poor. They live as Jews and pray in the temple.
> **From Jerusalem to Rome:** The good news goes to Samaria, to the man from Ethiopia, to the Roman centurion Cornelius, and finally into the whole world.
> **God shows no favoritism:** At the meeting of the apostles in Jerusalem (chapter 15) it is decided: People from all nations belong, without first having to become Jews.
> **Peter and Paul:** Peter leads the church at the beginning. Paul, the former persecutor, becomes the great missionary to the nations. He travels through Asia Minor and Greece, and in the end he comes to Rome as a prisoner.
> **Women in the church:** Tabitha, Lydia, Priscilla, the daughters of Philip and many others play an important role.
> **An important note:** In Acts there is conflict between the apostles and some Jewish groups. But the apostles were Jews themselves. Many thousands of Jews came to faith. To the very end Paul calls the Jews “Brothers.” No text of this book may be used against the Jewish people.
> **For today:** The Holy Spirit makes people brave. The good news is there for all people. “Without hindrance”: God’s word cannot be stopped.
> **What comes next?** Next comes Paul’s letter to the Romans. It is Paul’s longest and perhaps most important letter. He wrote it to the church in Rome before he came there himself. In it he explains how God makes people righteous: through faith, by grace.
