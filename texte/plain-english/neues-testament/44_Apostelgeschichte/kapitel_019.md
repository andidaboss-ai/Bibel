# Acts – Chapter 19
### Paul in Ephesus

---

## Disciples of John the Baptizer (Verses 1–7)

[1] While Apollos was in Corinth,
Paul passed through the upper country
and came to Ephesus.
There he found some disciples.
[2] He said to them,
“Did you receive the Holy Spirit
when you believed?”
They said to him,
“No,
we haven’t even heard
that there is a Holy Spirit.”
[3] He said,
“Into what then were you baptized?”
They said,
“Into John’s baptism.”
[4] Paul said,
“John baptized with the baptism of repentance.
He told the people
that they should believe in the one
who would come after him,
that is, in Christ Jesus.”
[5] When they heard this,
they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.
[6] Paul laid his hands on them.
Then the Holy Spirit came on them.
They spoke in other languages
and prophesied.
[7] They were about 12 men in all.

> **What does this mean?**
> Ephesus was one of the largest cities in the Roman Empire, on the west coast of today’s Turkey. Paul stayed there for more than two years.
> Verses 2–4: These disciples only knew the baptism of John the Baptizer. Paul explains to them: John pointed to Jesus.
> Verse 6: The Holy Spirit comes, as at Pentecost.

## Paul teaches in Ephesus (Verses 8–20)

[8] Paul went into the synagogue
and spoke boldly there for three months.
He reasoned with the people
and tried to persuade them about God’s Kingdom.
[9] But some were hardened
and disobedient.
Before the crowd they spoke evil of the Way.
So Paul left them
and took the disciples with him.
Every day he reasoned in the school of Tyrannus.
[10] This continued for two years.
So all those who lived in Asia
heard the word of the Lord Jesus,
both Jews and Greeks.
[11] God did special miracles
through the hands of Paul.
[12] People even carried handkerchiefs or aprons
that had touched his body
to the sick.
Then the diseases left them,
and the evil spirits went out.
[13] But some Jewish exorcists
who traveled from place to place
also tried
to call the name of the Lord Jesus
over those who had evil spirits.
They said,
“We command you by Jesus
whom Paul preaches!”
[14] Seven sons of a man named Sceva,
a Jewish chief priest,
did this.
[15] But the evil spirit answered,
“Jesus I know,
and Paul I know,
but who are you?”
[16] The man
in whom the evil spirit was
leaped on them,
overpowered them
and was stronger than they were.
They fled out of that house naked and wounded.
[17] This became known to all,
both Jews and Greeks,
who lived at Ephesus.
Fear fell on them all.
And the name of the Lord Jesus was highly honored.
[18] Many
of those who had believed
came
and openly confessed
what they had done.
[19] Many
of those who had practiced magic
brought their books together
and burned them in the sight of all.
They counted the price of the books:
fifty thousand pieces of silver.
[20] So the word of the Lord grew
and became mighty.

*exorcist* = someone who tries to drive out evil spirits

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: “The Way” is an early name for the Christian faith. Some reject the message, not all.
> Verse 10: Jews and Greeks in the province of Asia hear the good news. Perhaps the churches in Colossae and Laodicea also began at this time.
> Verse 12: People hope for healing, even through cloths from Paul. But the healing comes from God (verse 11).
> Verses 13–16: You can’t use the name of Jesus like a magic formula. What matters is real faith.
> Verse 14: A chief priest named Sceva is not known from anywhere else. Perhaps he only called himself that.
> Verse 19: Ephesus was known for books of magic. The people burn their own books freely. This is a sign that they turn away from magic completely.
> Important: This story gives no one the right to burn other people’s books. Burning books against the will of others, as in Germany in 1933, is wrong.

## The riot of the silversmiths (Verses 21–41)

[21] After these things had ended,
Paul decided in the Spirit
to pass through Macedonia and Achaia
and then go to Jerusalem.
He said,
“After I have been there,
I must also see Rome.”
[22] He sent two of his helpers,
Timothy and Erastus,
into Macedonia.
He himself stayed in Asia for a while.
[23] About that time there was a great disturbance
about the Way.
[24] A silversmith named Demetrius
made silver shrines of Artemis.
In this way he brought a lot of business
to the craftsmen.
[25] He gathered them together
with the workers of similar trades.
He said,
“Sirs,
you know
that we have our wealth from this business.
[26] You see and hear
that not only at Ephesus,
but almost throughout all Asia,
this Paul has persuaded and turned away many people.
He says
that gods
that are made with hands
are no gods.
[27] There is not only the danger
that this trade of ours will get a bad name.
But also the temple of the great goddess Artemis
will be counted as nothing.
And her majesty will be destroyed,
though all Asia and the world worship her.”
[28] When they heard this,
they were filled with anger
and cried out,
“Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
[29] The whole city was filled with confusion.
They all rushed together into the theater.
They seized Gaius and Aristarchus,
men from Macedonia,
Paul’s travel companions.
[30] Paul wanted to go in to the people,
but the disciples didn’t allow him.
[31] Some of the Asiarchs,
who were his friends,
also sent to him
and begged him
not to go into the theater.
[32] Some cried one thing,
and some another.
For the assembly was in confusion.
Most of them didn’t even know
why they had come together.
[33] They brought Alexander out of the crowd.
The Jews pushed him forward.
Alexander waved with his hand
and wanted to make a defense before the people.
[34] But when they realized
that he was a Jew,
all of them cried out with one voice
for about two hours,
“Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
[35] The town clerk quieted the crowd
and said,
“You men of Ephesus,
who doesn’t know
that the city of the Ephesians is temple keeper
of the great goddess Artemis,
and of the image
which fell down from Zeus?
[36] Since these things can’t be denied,
you ought to be quiet
and do nothing rash.
[37] For you have brought these men here,
who are neither robbers of temples
nor blasphemers of your goddess.
[38] If Demetrius and the craftsmen
who are with him
have a complaint against anyone,
the courts are open
and there are proconsuls.
Let them bring charges against one another there.
[39] But if you want anything else,
it will be settled in the regular assembly.
[40] For we are in danger
of being accused because of today’s riot.
There is no reason for it.
We wouldn’t be able to give an account of this commotion.”
[41] When he had said this,
he dismissed the assembly.

*shrine* = here: a small model of a temple
*Asiarchs* = respected leading men of the province of Asia

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Paul plans to go to Rome. This becomes the goal of the whole book.
> Verse 24: The temple of Artemis in Ephesus was one of the seven wonders of the ancient world. Demetrius sold small souvenirs of the temple.
> Verses 25–27: Demetrius is worried most of all about his money. For him religion is also a business.
> Verse 29: The theater of Ephesus had room for about 25,000 people. You can still visit it today.
> Verse 31: Some of these respected men were friends of Paul.
> Verses 33–34: The Jews in Ephesus probably wanted to show that they had nothing to do with Paul. But when the crowd realizes that Alexander is a Jew, they don’t let him speak. Here too hostility toward Jews shows itself.
> Verses 35–40: The town clerk acts wisely. He says: There are courts. Problems should be solved by law, not by violence.
> For today: When a crowd gets angry, calm and lawful action is important.
