# Acts – Chapter 24
### Paul before the governor Felix

---

## The accusation (Verses 1–8)

[1] After five days the high priest Ananias came down
with some elders
and a speaker named Tertullus.
They brought charges against Paul before the governor.
[2] Paul was called.
Then Tertullus began to accuse him,
saying,
“Through you we enjoy much peace.
And through your foresight
things are getting better and better for this nation.
[3] We accept this in all ways and in all places,
most excellent Felix,
with all thankfulness.
[4] But I don’t want to delay you for long.
I ask you
kindly to hear a few words from us.
[5] For we have found
this man to be a plague.
He stirs up riots among all the Jews throughout the world.
He is a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes.
[6] He even tried
to profane the temple.
So we arrested him.
[7] By questioning him yourself
you can find out all these things
of which we accuse him.”
[8] The Jews also joined in the attack
and claimed
that these things were so.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sect means: a group with its own teaching.
> Profane means: make something holy unclean.
> About the numbering: In the WEB this chapter has 26 verses. Some Bibles have 27. There, verses 6–8 have a few more words: The accusers say they wanted to judge Paul by their law, but Lysias took him away from them. These words are missing in the oldest manuscripts. That is why the WEB leaves them out. Our verses 7–26 are verses 8–27 in some Bibles.
> Verses 2–4: Tertullus flatters the governor. In reality Felix was known for his harshness.
> Verse 5: “Nazarenes” was a name for the Christians, after Jesus of Nazareth.
> Verse 8: “The Jews” here means the accusers from Jerusalem: the high priest and some elders.

---

## Paul defends himself (Verses 9–20)

[9] The governor signaled to Paul
that he could speak.
Then Paul answered,
“I know
that you have been a judge of this nation for many years.
So I gladly make my defense.
[10] You can check:
It is not more than 12 days
since I went up to Jerusalem
to worship.
[11] They didn’t find me in the temple
arguing with anyone
or stirring up a crowd,
either in the synagogues
or in the city.
[12] Nor can they prove to you
the things of which they now accuse me.
[13] But this I confess to you:
I serve the God of our ancestors
according to the Way,
which they call a sect.
I believe all things
which are according to the law
and which are written in the prophets.
[14] I have hope in God,
which these men themselves also have:
that there will be a resurrection of the dead,
both of the just and the unjust.
[15] So I also always try
to have a clear conscience
before God and people.
[16] After some years I came
to bring gifts for the needy to my nation
and to offer sacrifices.
[17] While I was doing this,
some Jews from Asia found me in the temple.
I had been purified.
I was not with a crowd
and made no noise.
[18] They should have been here before you
to accuse me,
if they had anything against me.
[19] Or else let these men themselves say
what wrong they found in me
when I stood before the council.
[20] Unless it is about this one thing
that I cried out
as I stood among them:
‘Concerning the resurrection of the dead
I am being judged before you today!’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: Paul says: I serve the God of our ancestors. I believe everything that is written in the law and in the prophets. He does not see his faith in Jesus as falling away from Judaism, but as part of it.
> Verse 14: Paul shares the hope of the resurrection with many Jews, especially with the Pharisees.
> Verse 16: Paul brought a collection of money for the poor in Jerusalem. He wanted to help his people.
> Verse 17: “Some Jews from Asia”: This is about a few particular people. They didn’t even come as witnesses.

---

## Felix hesitates (Verses 21–26)

[21] But Felix knew the Way well.
He put off the case and said,
“When Lysias, the commanding officer, comes down,
I will decide your case.”
[22] He ordered the centurion
to keep Paul in custody,
but to give him some freedoms.
None of his friends should be stopped
from serving him
or visiting him.
[23] After some days Felix came
with his wife Drusilla,
who was Jewish.
He sent for Paul
and listened to him
speak about faith in Christ Jesus.
[24] Paul spoke about righteousness,
self-control,
and the judgment to come.
Then Felix was terrified
and answered,
“Go away for now.
When it is convenient for me,
I will send for you again.”
[25] At the same time he hoped
that Paul would give him money,
so that he would release him.
So he sent for him often
and talked with him.
[26] When two years had passed,
Felix was followed in office by Porcius Festus.
Felix wanted to do the Jews a favor
and left Paul in chains.

> **What does this mean?**
> Custody means: being held as a prisoner.
> Verse 22: Paul is allowed to have visitors. Friends take care of him. Prisoners depend on help from outside.
> Verse 23: Drusilla was a daughter of King Herod Agrippa I (chapter 12).
> Verse 24: Paul speaks about righteousness and self-control. This hits Felix, who was known for injustice.
> Verse 25: Felix is waiting for a bribe. That is corruption and injustice.
> Verse 26: Paul remains a prisoner for two years, although he is innocent, only for political reasons. “The Jews” here means the influential accusers in Jerusalem.
> For today: Anyone who is in prison while innocent needs people who visit them and stand up for them. Human rights organizations still work for political prisoners today.
