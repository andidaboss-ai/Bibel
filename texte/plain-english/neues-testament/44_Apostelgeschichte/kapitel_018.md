# Acts – Chapter 18
### Paul in Corinth, Priscilla and Aquila

---

## Paul in Corinth (Verses 1–11)

[1] After these things Paul left Athens
and came to Corinth.
[2] There he found a Jew named Aquila.
He came from Pontus
and had recently come from Italy
with his wife Priscilla,
because Claudius had commanded
all the Jews to leave Rome.
Paul went to them.
[3] Because he had the same trade,
he lived with them
and worked with them.
By trade they were tent makers.
[4] Every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue
and persuaded Jews and Greeks.
[5] When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia,
Paul was pressed by the Spirit.
He testified to the Jews
that Jesus was the Christ.
[6] When they opposed him
and blasphemed,
he shook out his clothing
and said to them,
“Your blood be on your own heads!
I am clean.
From now on, I will go to the Gentiles!”
[7] He left there
and went into the house of a man named Justus.
Justus worshiped God.
His house was next door to the synagogue.
[8] Crispus,
the ruler of the synagogue,
believed in the Lord with all his household.
And many of the Corinthians
who heard
believed
and were baptized.
[9] In the night the Lord said to Paul in a vision,
“Don’t be afraid,
but speak
and don’t be silent.
[10] For I am with you.
No one will attack you
to harm you.
For I have many people in this city.”
[11] Paul lived there a year and six months,
teaching the word of God among them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Corinth was a large port city in Greece. Later Paul wrote two long letters to the church there.
> Verse 2: Around AD 49 the emperor Claudius drove Jews out of Rome. That too was hostility toward Jews.
> Priscilla and Aquila are a married couple. They become important co-workers of Paul. Priscilla is often named first. This probably shows how important she was in the church.
> Verse 3: Paul earned his own money with his trade. He didn’t want to be a burden to anyone.
> Verse 6: “Your blood be on your own heads” means: You are responsible yourselves. I have done my duty. Shaking out his clothes is a sign of this. It is meant for certain people in Corinth, not for all Jews. Paul keeps going to synagogues later (verse 19).
> Verse 8: Even the ruler of the synagogue, Crispus, comes to faith.
> Verses 9–10: Paul was probably afraid. God encourages him: “Don’t be afraid.” And: “For I am with you.”

## Paul before Gallio (Verses 12–17)

[12] When Gallio was proconsul of Achaia,
the Jews rose up together against Paul.
They brought him before the judgment seat
[13] and said,
“This man persuades people
to worship God
against the law.”
[14] Paul was about to speak.
But Gallio said to the Jews,
“You Jews,
if it were a matter of wrongdoing
or of a wicked crime,
it would be reasonable for me to listen to you.
[15] But if these are questions
about words and names
and your own law,
see to it yourselves.
I don’t want to be a judge of these matters.”
[16] So he drove them away from the judgment seat.
[17] Then all the Greeks seized Sosthenes,
the ruler of the synagogue,
and beat him in front of the judgment seat.
But Gallio didn’t care about any of these things.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: Gallio was the brother of the famous philosopher Seneca. An inscription shows that he was governor in Corinth around AD 51/52. This helps to date Paul’s journeys.
> Verses 14–15: For Gallio this is a dispute within Judaism. He doesn’t want to get involved.
> Verse 17: A Jewish man, Sosthenes, is beaten up by the crowd, and the governor looks away. That too is injustice and hostility toward Jews. Whoever looks away from violence shares the guilt.
> Perhaps this Sosthenes is the same one who is later called a brother in 1 Corinthians (1 Corinthians 1:1). But that is not certain.

## Paul travels back to Antioch (Verses 18–23)

[18] After this Paul stayed there many more days.
Then he said goodbye to the brothers
and sailed from there for Syria,
together with Priscilla and Aquila.
In Cenchreae he had his head shaved,
for he had taken a vow.
[19] He came to Ephesus
and left the two of them there.
But he himself went into the synagogue
and reasoned with the Jews.
[20] They asked him
to stay with them longer.
But he declined.
[21] He said goodbye to them
and said,
“I must by all means keep this coming feast in Jerusalem.
But I will return to you again
if God wills.”
Then he set sail from Ephesus.
[22] When he had landed at Caesarea,
he went up
and greeted the church.
Then he went down to Antioch.
[23] He spent some time there.
Then he left
and went through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, place by place.
He strengthened all the disciples.

*vow* = a solemn promise made to God

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: Paul takes a vow, as devout Jews did (compare Numbers 6). He continues to live as a devout Jew.
> Verses 19–20: The Jews in Ephesus ask Paul to stay longer. They are open to his message.
> Verse 21: Paul wants to go to Jerusalem for the feast. Some Bibles don’t have the sentence about the feast. It is missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has it.
> Verse 22: The second great journey ends with the return to Antioch. The third begins with verse 23.

## Apollos (Verses 24–28)

[24] A Jew named Apollos,
who came from Alexandria,
came to Ephesus.
He was an eloquent man
and knew the Scriptures well.
[25] He had been taught the way of the Lord.
He was on fire in spirit
and spoke and taught accurately about Jesus.
But he knew only the baptism of John.
[26] He began
to speak boldly in the synagogue.
When Priscilla and Aquila heard him,
they took him aside
and explained the way of God to him more accurately.
[27] Apollos wanted to travel to Achaia.
The brothers encouraged him
and wrote to the disciples there
to welcome him.
When he arrived,
he greatly helped those
who had believed through grace.
[28] For he powerfully refuted the Jews
in public.
He showed from the Scriptures
that Jesus was the Christ.

*eloquent* = good at speaking
*refute* = prove that something is wrong

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: Alexandria in Egypt was a city of great learning with a large Jewish community.
> Verse 25: Apollos knew a lot about Jesus, but not yet everything.
> Verse 26: Priscilla and Aquila teach the educated Apollos kindly and in private, not in public. Again Priscilla is named first. A woman teaches a learned man.
> Verse 27: Achaia is the province in the south of Greece that included Corinth. Later Apollos becomes important in Corinth (1 Corinthians 3:6).
> Verse 28: This is about a debate among Jews about Scripture. Apollos is a Jew himself.
> For today: Whoever knows something better should help others kindly, without embarrassing them.
