# Acts – Chapter 17
### Paul in Athens: “To an unknown god”

---

## In Thessalonica (Verses 1–9)

[1] They passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia
and came to Thessalonica.
There was a Jewish synagogue there.
[2] Paul went in to them,
as was his custom.
For three Sabbath days he reasoned with them
from the Scriptures.
[3] He explained
and showed them
that the Christ had to suffer
and rise again from the dead.
He said,
“This Jesus,
whom I proclaim to you,
is the Christ.”
[4] Some of them were persuaded
and joined Paul and Silas,
and also a great crowd of the devout Greeks,
and not a few of the leading women.
[5] But the Jews
who were not persuaded
took along some wicked men from the marketplace.
They gathered a crowd
and set the city in an uproar.
They attacked the house of Jason.
They tried to bring Paul and Silas
out to the people.
[6] When they didn’t find them,
they dragged Jason and some brothers
before the rulers of the city.
They shouted,
“These who have turned the world upside down
have come here also!
[7] Jason has welcomed them.
These all act against the decrees of Caesar.
They say
that there is another king:
Jesus!”
[8] The crowd and the rulers of the city
were troubled
when they heard these things.
[9] They took security from Jason and the rest
and then let them go.

> **What does this mean?**
> Security means: here: money paid as a guarantee.
> Thessalonica is today’s Thessaloniki in Greece. Later Paul wrote two letters to the church there.
> Verse 2: Paul goes to the synagogue first, “as was his custom.” He remains connected with Judaism.
> Verse 4: Some Jews, many devout Greeks and leading women come to faith.
> Verse 5: “The Jews who were not persuaded”: This is about a certain group in Thessalonica, not about all Jews.
> Verse 7: The accusation is political: They are supposed to be against the emperor. That was dangerous.
> Verse 9: Jason had to pay a kind of bail.

## In Beroea (Verses 10–15)

[10] The brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away
by night to Beroea.
When they arrived,
they went into the Jewish synagogue.
[11] These people were more noble-minded
than those in Thessalonica.
They received the word with great eagerness.
Every day they examined the Scriptures
to see whether these things were so.
[12] So many of them believed,
and also many of the prominent Greek women,
and not a few men.
[13] But when the Jews of Thessalonica learned
that Paul was proclaiming the word of God in Beroea also,
they came there too
and stirred up the crowds.
[14] Then the brothers immediately sent Paul away
to go as far as the sea.
But Silas and Timothy stayed there.
[15] The men
who escorted Paul
brought him as far as Athens.
They received a command
that Silas and Timothy should come to him
as quickly as possible.
Then they left.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 11: The Jews in Beroea are praised: Every day they examine the Holy Scriptures themselves to see whether what Paul says is true. That is a good example for all who believe: read it yourself and check it.
> Verse 12: Many Jews in Beroea come to faith.
> Verse 13: Again it is about a certain group from Thessalonica.

## Paul on the Areopagus in Athens (Verses 16–34)

[16] While Paul waited for them in Athens,
his spirit was stirred up within him,
because he saw
that the city was full of idols.
[17] So he reasoned in the synagogue
with the Jews and the devout people.
And every day he spoke in the marketplace
with those he met there.
[18] Some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers
also talked with him.
Some said,
“What does this babbler want to say?”
Others said,
“He seems to be promoting foreign gods.”
For he preached Jesus
and the resurrection.
[19] They took hold of him
and brought him to the Areopagus.
They said,
“May we know
what this new teaching is
which you are speaking about?
[20] For you bring some strange things to our ears.
So we want to know
what these things mean.”
[21] Now all the Athenians
and the foreigners
living there
spent their time on nothing else
but telling or hearing some new thing.
[22] Paul stood in the middle of the Areopagus
and said,
“You men of Athens,
I see
that you are very religious in all things.
[23] For as I walked around
and looked at the objects of your worship,
I also found an altar
with this inscription:
‘TO AN UNKNOWN GOD.’
So what you worship
without knowing it,
I announce to you.
[24] God made the world
and all things in it.
He is Lord of heaven and earth.
He doesn’t live in temples
made with hands.
[25] He isn’t served by human hands,
as though he needed anything.
For he himself gives to all
life and breath and all things.
[26] He made from one blood
every nation of people
to live on the whole surface of the earth.
He set appointed times for them
and the boundaries of the places where they live.
[27] They were to seek the Lord,
if perhaps they might reach out for him and find him.
For he is not far from each one of us.
[28] ‘For in him we live,
move,
and have our being.’
As some of your own poets have said,
‘For we are also his offspring.’
[29] So since we are the offspring of God,
we ought not to think
that the divine nature is like gold, or silver, or stone,
shaped by the art and design of human beings.
[30] God overlooked the times of ignorance.
But now he commands all people everywhere
to repent.
[31] For he has appointed a day
on which he will judge the world in righteousness
through the man
whom he has chosen for this.
He has given proof of this to all people
by raising him from the dead.”
[32] When they heard of the resurrection of the dead,
some mocked.
But others said,
“We want to hear you again about this.”
[33] So Paul went out from among them.
[34] But some men joined him
and believed,
among them Dionysius,
a member of the Areopagus,
and a woman named Damaris,
and others with them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Babbler means: someone who talks a lot of nonsense.
> Offspring means: children, descendants.
> Verse 16: Athens was the most famous city of philosophy. Statues of gods stood everywhere.
> Verse 18: Epicureans and Stoics were two well-known schools of philosophy. The Epicureans sought a calm, happy life. The Stoics taught that a divine reason fills everything.
> Verse 18: “Jesus and the resurrection”: Some probably thought these were two new gods.
> Verse 19: The Areopagus was a hill in Athens and also the name of the council that met there.
> Verses 22–31: Paul speaks kindly and wisely. He builds on what the Athenians know: their altar and their poets. He doesn’t insult them.
> Verse 26: “From one blood every nation”: All people are one family. This stands against every kind of racism.
> Verse 28: Here Paul quotes Greek poets. The sentence “For we are also his offspring” is found in the poet Aratus.
> Verses 30–31: Paul speaks about the judgment and the resurrection of Jesus without naming him.
> Verse 34: Damaris is a woman who comes to faith in the middle of philosophers.
> For today: You can speak respectfully with people of other worldviews and still say clearly what you believe.
