# Acts – Chapter 20
### Farewell to Ephesus

---

## Journey through Macedonia and Greece (Verses 1–6)

[1] After the uproar had ended,
Paul sent for the disciples.
He said goodbye to them
and left to go into Macedonia.
[2] He went through those regions
and encouraged the people with many words.
Then he came into Greece.
[3] He stayed there three months.
He was about to set sail for Syria.
Then a plot was made against him by Jews.
So he decided
to return through Macedonia.
[4] These went with him as far as Asia:
Sopater of Beroea,
Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica,
Gaius of Derbe,
Timothy,
and Tychicus and Trophimus of Asia.
[5] These had gone ahead
and were waiting for us at Troas.
[6] After the days of Unleavened Bread
we sailed away from Philippi.
In five days we came to them at Troas.
There we stayed seven days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “By Jews” here means certain opponents, not the Jewish people.
> Verse 4: Paul travels with a group from many churches. They were probably bringing a collection of money for the poor Christians in Jerusalem (compare Romans 15:25–26).
> Verse 5: Here the report in the “we” form begins again.
> Verse 6: Paul continues to live by the Jewish feasts.

---

## Eutychus falls from the window (Verses 7–12)

[7] On the first day of the week
the disciples gathered together
to break bread.
Paul talked with them.
He wanted to leave the next day.
So he kept on speaking until midnight.
[8] There were many lamps
in the upper room
where we were gathered.
[9] A young man named Eutychus
sat in the window.
He became very sleepy.
Paul kept speaking even longer.
Then sleep overcame him,
and he fell down from the third floor.
He was picked up dead.
[10] Paul went down,
threw himself on him,
put his arms around him
and said,
“Don’t be troubled,
for his life is in him.”
[11] Then Paul went up again,
broke bread
and ate.
He talked with them a long while,
until daybreak.
Then he left.
[12] They brought the boy in alive
and were greatly comforted.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 7: “On the first day of the week”: That is Sunday, the day of the resurrection. Already the first Christians met on this day to break bread, that is, for the Lord’s Supper or Eucharist.
> Verse 9: A very human, even somewhat funny scene: A sermon lasts so long that a young man falls asleep. But then it becomes serious.
> Verse 10: Paul embraces the boy, as the prophets Elijah and Elisha did (1 Kings 17:21; 2 Kings 4:34).
> For today: God gives life, even when everything seems lost.

## From Troas to Miletus (Verses 13–16)

[13] But we went ahead to the ship
and sailed for Assos.
There we wanted to take Paul on board.
He had arranged it this way,
for he himself wanted to go by land.
[14] When he met us at Assos,
we took him on board
and came to Mitylene.
[15] We sailed on from there
and the next day came opposite Chios.
The day after, we touched at Samos
and stayed at Trogyllium.
The following day we came to Miletus.
[16] For Paul had decided
to sail past Ephesus,
so that he would not have to spend time in Asia.
For he was hurrying
to be in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost,
if possible.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 13–15: Luke describes the journey exactly, port by port. He was probably there himself.
> Verse 15: “And stayed at Trogyllium”: These words are missing in some old manuscripts. The WEB has them.
> Verse 16: Paul wants to be in Jerusalem for Pentecost, a Jewish feast.

## Paul says goodbye (Verses 17–38)

[17] From Miletus Paul sent to Ephesus
and called the elders of the assembly to himself.
[18] When they had come to him,
he said to them,
“You yourselves know
how I was with you all the time,
from the first day
that I set foot in Asia.
[19] I served the Lord
with all humility,
with many tears,
and with trials
that came to me through the plots of the Jews.
[20] You know
that I didn’t hold back from telling you anything
that was useful to you.
I taught you publicly
and from house to house.
[21] I testified to both Jews and Greeks:
Turn to God in repentance
and have faith in our Lord Jesus.
[22] And now
I am going to Jerusalem,
bound by the Spirit.
I don’t know
what will happen to me there.
[23] I only know
that the Holy Spirit testifies to me in every city:
Chains and troubles are waiting for me.
[24] But these things don’t count.
I don’t hold my life dear to myself.
What matters
is that I finish my race with joy,
and the ministry
which I received from the Lord Jesus:
to testify to the Good News of the grace of God.
[25] And now
I know
that you all,
among whom I went about
preaching God’s Kingdom,
will see my face no more.
[26] So I testify to you today
that I am clean from the blood of all people.
[27] For I didn’t hold back.
I declared to you the whole plan of God.
[28] So take care of yourselves
and of all the flock.
The Holy Spirit has made you overseers in it.
You are to shepherd the assembly of the Lord and God
which he bought with his own blood.
[29] For I know
that after I have left,
vicious wolves will come in among you.
They will not spare the flock.
[30] Men will also rise up
from among your own selves.
They will speak twisted things
to draw the disciples away after them.
[31] So be watchful!
Remember
that for three years
I didn’t stop
warning everyone night and day
with tears.
[32] And now, brothers,
I entrust you to God
and to the word of his grace.
It is able
to build you up
and to give you the inheritance
among all those who are made holy.
[33] I wanted no one’s silver,
gold,
or clothing.
[34] You yourselves know
that these hands
earned what I needed
and what those who were with me needed.
[35] In all things I gave you an example:
By working like this
you ought to help the weak.
And remember the words of the Lord Jesus.
He himself said,
‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’ ”
[36] When he had said these things,
he knelt down
and prayed with them all.
[37] They all wept very much.
They hugged Paul
and kissed him.
[38] They were sad most of all because of the word
that they would see his face no more.
Then they went with him to the ship.

*overseer* = someone who watches over and cares for others
*flock* = a group of sheep; here: the church

> **What does this mean?**
> This is the only speech of Paul in Acts that he gives to Christians. It is a farewell speech.
> Verse 19: “The plots of the Jews” means certain opponents. Paul himself is a Jew.
> Verses 22–24: Paul knows that danger is waiting for him. Still he goes. He trusts God.
> Verse 26: “I am clean from the blood of all people” means: I have done my task. No one can say that I kept something from them.
> Verse 28: “Overseer” in Greek is *episkopos*. The word “bishop” comes from it. The church leaders are to care for the church like shepherds.
> Verse 28: The WEB writes “the assembly of the Lord and God.” The old manuscripts have different forms here: *the church of God* or *the church of the Lord*.
> Verses 29–30: Paul warns against false teachers who want to tie people to themselves. Anyone who is put under pressure in a group can find advice at counseling services for cults and spiritual abuse.
> Verse 35: “It is more blessed to give than to receive”: This saying of Jesus is not in the Gospels. Paul knew it from what was passed on by word of mouth.
> Verses 37–38: A tearful farewell. The church loves Paul. Christians may show their feelings.
