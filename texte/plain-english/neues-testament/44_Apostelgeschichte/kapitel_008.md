# Acts – Chapter 8
### The good news comes to Samaria and Africa

---

## The church is persecuted (Verses 1–3)

[1] Saul agreed to his death.
On that day a great persecution began
against the church in Jerusalem.
They were all scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria,
except for the apostles.
[2] Devout men buried Stephen
and mourned greatly over him.
[3] But Saul was destroying the church.
He went into every house,
dragged both men and women off,
and put them in prison.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: After Stephen’s death many Christians have to flee from Jerusalem. The apostles stay.
> Verse 2: The “devout men” were probably faithful Jews. They mourn for Stephen and bury him with dignity. Not everyone was against him.
> Verse 3: Saul, who later becomes Paul, persecutes the Christians. Later he himself will say that he regrets it (1 Corinthians 15:9).
> For today: People can change. Saul the persecutor becomes Paul the apostle.

---

## Philip in Samaria (Verses 4–25)

[4] So those who were scattered
went around
preaching the word.
[5] Philip went down to the city of Samaria
and proclaimed the Christ to them.
[6] The crowds all listened
to the things that Philip said.
They heard him
and saw the signs
which he did.
[7] For unclean spirits came out of many
who had them,
crying with a loud voice.
Many who had been paralyzed or lame were healed.
[8] And there was great joy in that city.
[9] But in the city there was a man named Simon.
For a long time he had practiced sorcery
and amazed the people of Samaria.
He made himself out to be some great one.
[10] They all listened to him,
from the least to the greatest.
They said,
“This man is that great power of God.”
[11] They listened to him
because for a long time
he had amazed them with his sorceries.
[12] But then they believed Philip.
He preached the good news about God’s Kingdom
and the name of Jesus Christ.
So both men and women were baptized.
[13] Simon himself also believed.
He was baptized
and stayed with Philip.
When he saw the signs and great miracles
that were happening,
he was amazed.
[14] The apostles in Jerusalem heard
that Samaria had received the word of God.
So they sent Peter and John to them.
[15] The two came down
and prayed for them,
that they might receive the Holy Spirit.
[16] For as yet he had not come on any of them.
They had only been baptized in the name of Christ Jesus.
[17] Then they laid their hands on them,
and they received the Holy Spirit.
[18] Simon saw
that the Holy Spirit was given
through the laying on of the apostles’ hands.
So he offered them money
[19] and said,
“Give me also this power,
so that whomever I lay my hands on
may receive the Holy Spirit.”
[20] But Peter said to him,
“May your silver perish with you,
because you thought
you could get the gift of God with money!
[21] You have no part or share in this matter,
for your heart isn’t right before God.
[22] So repent of this wickedness of yours,
and ask God
whether perhaps the thought of your heart
may be forgiven you.
[23] For I see
that you are full of bitter poison
and bound by wrongdoing.”
[24] Simon answered,
“Pray for me to the Lord,
so that none of the things
which you have spoken
happen to me.”
[25] When Peter and John had testified
and spoken the word of the Lord,
they returned to Jerusalem.
On the way they preached the Good News
in many villages of the Samaritans.

> **What does this mean?**
> Sorcery means: magic.
> Verse 5: The Samaritans were related to the Jews, but there had been hostility between them for a long time (compare John 4:9). Now the good news comes to them too. In this way what Jesus said comes true (1:8).
> This Philip is one of the seven helpers from chapter 6, not the apostle Philip.
> Verse 7: “Unclean spirits”: At that time many illnesses were understood this way. Important: Mental illnesses are not possession. They can be treated.
> Verses 9–11: Simon was a magician who made himself great. In later tradition he is called *Simon Magus*.
> Verses 14–17: The apostles come from Jerusalem so that the new church is connected with the whole church. The churches understand differently how baptism and receiving the Spirit belong together. Catholic and Orthodox churches see here a link to confirmation.
> Verses 18–20: Simon wants to buy God’s gift. From his name comes the word simony: buying or selling church offices. God’s gifts can’t be bought.
> Verse 22: Simon too gets a chance to turn back.

## Philip and the man from Ethiopia (Verses 26–39)

[26] An angel of the Lord said to Philip,
“Get up,
and go toward the south
to the road
that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza.
This is a desert.”
[27] Philip got up
and went.
There was a man from Ethiopia,
a eunuch.
He was a powerful official under Candace,
queen of the Ethiopians.
He was in charge of all her treasure.
He had come to Jerusalem
to worship.
[28] Now he was on his way home.
He was sitting in his chariot
and reading the prophet Isaiah.
[29] The Spirit said to Philip,
“Go near,
and join this chariot.”
[30] Philip ran to him.
He heard him
reading Isaiah the prophet out loud.
He asked,
“Do you understand
what you are reading?”
[31] The man said,
“How can I,
unless someone explains it to me?”
And he begged Philip
to come up and sit with him.
[32] Now the passage of the Scripture
which he was reading
was this:
“He was led as a sheep to the slaughter.
As a lamb before his shearer is silent,
so he doesn’t open his mouth.
[33] In his humiliation,
his judgment was taken away.
Who will tell about his generation?
For his life is taken from the earth.”
[34] The eunuch asked Philip,
“Who is the prophet talking about?
About himself,
or about someone else?”
[35] Then Philip began to speak.
Beginning from this Scripture,
he preached to him about Jesus.
[36] As they went on the way,
they came to some water.
The eunuch said,
“Here is water.
What is keeping me from being baptized?”
[37] He commanded the chariot to stop.
They both went down into the water,
both Philip and the eunuch,
and Philip baptized him.
[38] When they came up out of the water,
the Spirit of the Lord took Philip away.
The eunuch didn’t see him any more,
for he went on his way rejoicing.
[39] But Philip was found at Azotus.
He passed through the land
and preached the Good News to all the cities,
until he came to Caesarea.

> **What does this mean?**
> Eunuch means: a man who had been castrated; such men often served at royal courts.
> Chariot means: a carriage pulled by horses.
> Important about the numbering: The WEB has 39 verses in this chapter. Some Bibles have 40. There is an additional verse 37: Philip asks about the man’s faith, and the man confesses Jesus Christ as the Son of God. This verse is missing in the oldest manuscripts. That is why the WEB leaves it out. Our verses 37–39 are verses 38–40 in those Bibles.
> Verse 27: At that time Ethiopia meant the kingdom south of Egypt, in today’s Sudan. Candace was a title of the queens there. Eunuchs often served at royal courts. According to Deuteronomy 23:1 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 23:2), they were not allowed to enter the assembly of the Lord. But the prophet Isaiah had promised: God will accept them too (Isaiah 56:3–5).
> Verses 32–33 quote Isaiah 53:7–8 in the Greek translation. Christians understand this passage about the suffering servant of God as pointing to Jesus. In Judaism it is often related to the people of Israel.
> Verse 36: “What is keeping me from being baptized?” The answer is: nothing. A man from Africa, a foreigner, a eunuch, is baptized without hesitation. No one is excluded.
> Verse 39: Azotus is the old city of Ashdod. It was on the coast. Caesarea was a port city and the seat of the Roman governor.
> For today: If you don’t understand the Bible, you may ask. And whoever understands it should help others to understand it.
