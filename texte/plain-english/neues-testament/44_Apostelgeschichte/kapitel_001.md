# Acts – Chapter 1
### Jesus goes to the Father

---

## Before we begin: The Acts of the Apostles

The Acts of the Apostles continues the Gospel of Luke. Both books are written to a man named Theophilus. According to old church tradition, Luke wrote both books. He was a companion of the apostle Paul. Acts was probably written between AD 70 and 90. No one knows exactly.
The book tells how the first Christian church came into being: Jesus goes to the Father, and at Pentecost the Holy Spirit comes. Then the good news spreads: from Jerusalem to Judea and Samaria and all the way to Rome, the capital of the world at that time. In the first part Peter is mostly at the center, in the second part Paul.
An important theme is: The good news is for all people, for Jews and for people from all other nations.
**An important note:** The first Christians were Jews. They prayed in the temple and kept the Jewish feasts. In Acts, too, there are conflicts between the apostles and Jewish leaders. Sometimes the text says “the Jews.” Then certain groups are meant, never the whole Jewish people. No text of this book may be used against Jews.
The Acts of the Apostles has 28 chapters.

## Jesus promises the Holy Spirit (Verses 1–8)

[1] In the first book, Theophilus,
I wrote about all
that Jesus began both to do and to teach,
[2] until the day
on which he was taken up.
Before that, he had given commands
through the Holy Spirit
to the apostles
whom he had chosen.
[3] After he suffered,
he showed himself alive to them
by many proofs.
He appeared to them over a period of forty days
and spoke about God’s Kingdom.
[4] While he was together with them,
he commanded them,
“Don’t leave Jerusalem,
but wait for the promise of the Father,
which you heard from me.
[5] For John baptized in water,
but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit
not many days from now.”
[6] When they had come together,
they asked him,
“Lord, are you now restoring the kingdom to Israel?”
[7] He said to them,
“It isn’t for you to know times or seasons
which the Father has set
by his own authority.
[8] But you will receive power
when the Holy Spirit has come upon you.
And you will be witnesses to me
in Jerusalem,
in all Judea and Samaria,
and to the farthest parts of the earth.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “The first book” is the Gospel of Luke. There too Luke speaks to Theophilus (Luke 1:3). The name means friend of God.
> Verse 3: Forty is a special number in the Bible: Israel was in the wilderness for 40 years, Jesus fasted for 40 days. That is why Christians celebrate Ascension Day 40 days after Easter.
> Verse 5: John here is John the Baptizer.
> Verse 6: The disciples are still hoping for a political kingdom for Israel. Jesus does not reject the question, but he says: God decides the time.
> Verse 8 is like a table of contents for Acts: Jerusalem (chapters 1–7), Judea and Samaria (chapters 8–12), to the farthest parts of the earth (chapters 13–28).
> For today: No one needs to know when the world will end. Whoever wants to calculate it exactly is mistaken. What matters is to be a witness for Jesus now.

## Jesus is taken up into heaven (Verses 9–14)

[9] When he had said these things,
he was taken up
while they were watching.
A cloud took him out of their sight.
[10] They were looking steadily into the sky
as he went.
Suddenly two men in white clothing stood by them.
[11] They said,
“You men of Galilee,
why do you stand
looking into the sky?
This Jesus,
who was taken up from you into the sky,
will come back in the same way
as you saw him going into the sky.”
[12] Then they returned to Jerusalem
from the mountain called Olivet.
It is near Jerusalem,
a Sabbath day’s journey away.
[13] When they had come into the city,
they went up into the upper room
where they were staying:
Peter, John, James, and Andrew,
Philip and Thomas,
Bartholomew and Matthew,
James the son of Alphaeus,
Simon the Zealot,
and Judas the son of James.
[14] All these were of one mind
and kept on praying steadily,
together with the women,
and with Mary the mother of Jesus,
and with his brothers.

*Olivet* = the Mount of Olives

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: In the Bible the cloud is a sign of God’s presence (for example Exodus 13:21). Jesus goes into God’s world. “Heaven” does not mean a place above the clouds, but being near God.
> Verse 11: The two men in white are probably angels. They say: Don’t just stand around! Jesus will come back.
> Verse 12: A Sabbath day’s journey was the distance a person was allowed to walk on the Sabbath. That was about half a mile (1 kilometer).
> Verse 13: This is the list of the eleven apostles. A Zealot was someone who stood up very eagerly for God and the law. Some Zealots fought against the Romans.
> Verse 14: Women naturally belong to the first church. Mary, the mother of Jesus, is named here for the last time in the Bible. The brothers of Jesus are also there. The churches understand differently who exactly they were: brothers, half brothers or relatives.
> For today: Praying together makes a community stronger.

## Matthias becomes an apostle (Verses 15–26)

[15] In these days,
Peter stood up in the middle of the disciples.
About one hundred twenty people were together.
He said,
[16] “Brothers,
this Scripture had to be fulfilled,
which the Holy Spirit spoke before by the mouth of David
about Judas.
Judas was the guide for those
who arrested Jesus.
[17] For he was counted with us
and received his share in this ministry.
[18] This man bought a field
with the reward for his wickedness.
He fell headlong,
his body burst open,
and all his intestines gushed out.
[19] This became known to everyone
who lived in Jerusalem.
So in their language that field was called
‘Akeldama,’
that is,
‘The field of blood.’
[20] For it is written in the book of Psalms,
‘Let his home be made desolate.
Let no one live in it.’
And,
‘Let another take his office.’
[21] So one of the men
who have been with us all the time
that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,
[22] beginning from the baptism of John
to the day
that he was taken up from us,
one of these must become a witness with us of his resurrection.”
[23] They put forward two men:
Joseph called Barsabbas,
who was also called Justus,
and Matthias.
[24] They prayed and said,
“You, Lord, know the hearts of all people.
Show us
which one of these two you have chosen
[25] to take the place in this ministry
and apostleship.
Judas turned away from it
to go to his own place.”
[26] They drew lots for them.
The lot fell on Matthias.
And he was counted with the eleven apostles.

*desolate* = empty and ruined
*draw lots* = decide something by chance, like throwing dice

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 15: 120 people: The first church was still small.
> Verses 16–20: Peter speaks about the terrible end of Judas. Matthew 27:3–10 tells it a little differently. Both reports agree: Judas died, and with the money a field was bought that is called the field of blood.
> Verse 19: The WEB writes Akeldama. Some Bibles have Hakeldamach. The word is Aramaic, the language people spoke in Jerusalem at that time.
> Verse 20 quotes Psalm 69:25 (in the Hebrew Bible and in some Bibles 69:26) and Psalm 109:8.
> Important: If, like Judas, you feel heavy guilt and see no way out, please get help: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).
> Verses 21–22: An apostle had to be a witness of Jesus’ whole way and of his resurrection.
> Verse 26: In Israel drawing lots was a way to ask for God’s will (for example Leviticus 16:8). They prayed first. After this, the New Testament never tells of drawing lots again.
> The Twelve are complete again. The number twelve reminds us of the twelve tribes of Israel.
