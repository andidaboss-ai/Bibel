# Acts – Chapter 27
### Storm and shipwreck

---

## The journey begins (Verses 1–12)

[1] It was decided
that we should sail for Italy.
So they handed Paul and some other prisoners over
to a centurion named Julius,
of the emperor’s regiment.
[2] We went on board a ship of Adramyttium,
which was about to sail to places on the coast of Asia,
and put to sea.
Aristarchus,
a Macedonian of Thessalonica,
was with us.
[3] The next day we landed at Sidon.
Julius treated Paul kindly.
He allowed him
to go to his friends
and let them take care of him.
[4] We put to sea from there.
Because the winds were against us,
we sailed in the shelter of Cyprus.
[5] We sailed across the sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia
and came to Myra, a city of Lycia.
[6] There the centurion found a ship of Alexandria
sailing for Italy.
He put us on board.
[7] For many days we sailed slowly.
With difficulty we came near Cnidus.
The wind didn’t let us go further.
So we sailed in the shelter of Crete,
past Salmone.
[8] With difficulty we sailed along the coast
and came to a place called Fair Havens,
near the city of Lasea.
[9] Much time had passed.
Sailing was now dangerous,
because the Fast had already gone by.
So Paul warned them
[10] and said to them,
“Sirs,
I see
that this voyage will bring damage and much loss,
not only of the cargo and the ship,
but also of our lives.”
[11] But the centurion listened more to the captain
and to the owner of the ship
than to the things
Paul said.
[12] The harbor was not suitable to spend the winter in.
So most of them advised
going to sea from there.
They wanted to try
to reach Phoenix
and spend the winter there.
That is a port of Crete,
facing southwest and northwest.

*shelter* = here: the side of an island protected from the wind

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: From here on Luke again tells the story in the “we” form. He was probably on the ship.
> Verse 3: The Roman centurion Julius is kind to Paul. People who believe differently can also act kindly.
> Verse 9: “The Fast” is the Jewish Day of Atonement, Yom Kippur, in the fall. After that, sailing on the Mediterranean was dangerous because of the storms.
> Verse 11: The experts want to sail on. Paul warns them. Sadly, no one listens to him.

## The storm (Verses 13–26)

[13] When a gentle south wind blew,
they thought
they had what they wanted.
They pulled up the anchor
and sailed along Crete, close to shore.
[14] But soon after,
a stormy wind rushed down from the land.
It is called Euroclydon.
[15] The ship was caught
and couldn’t face the wind.
We gave up
and let ourselves be driven along.
[16] We ran in the shelter of a small island called Clauda.
There, with difficulty, we were able to secure the lifeboat.
[17] They pulled it on board
and used ropes to tie the ship together
to make it stronger.
They were afraid
of running aground on the sandbars of Syrtis.
So they lowered the sea anchor
and so were driven along.
[18] The storm battered us badly.
The next day they began
to throw cargo overboard.
[19] On the third day they threw out the ship’s equipment
with their own hands.
[20] For many days
neither sun nor stars shone on us.
And the storm kept raging hard.
Then all hope
that we would be saved
was taken away.
[21] They had been without food for a long time.
Then Paul stood up in the middle of them
and said,
“Sirs,
you should have listened to me
and not set sail from Crete.
Then you would have been spared this damage and loss.
[22] But now I urge you:
Cheer up!
For there will be no loss of life among you.
Only the ship will be lost.
[23] For this night an angel stood by me.
He comes from the God
to whom I belong
and whom I serve.
[24] He said,
‘Don’t be afraid, Paul!
You must stand before Caesar.
And God has granted you all those
who sail with you.’
[25] So, sirs,
cheer up!
For I believe God
that it will be just
as it has been told to me.
[26] But we must run aground on an island.”

*sea anchor* = a device dragged behind a ship to slow it down

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 14: “Euroclydon” was a dangerous northeast wind.
> Verse 17: The Syrtis are dangerous sandbars off the coast of North Africa, in today’s Libya.
> Verse 20: Without sun and stars, the sailors couldn’t know where they were. Everyone loses hope.
> Verses 21–26: In the middle of despair Paul gives them courage. He trusts God. And God saves not only him, but everyone on the ship.
> For today: Even in great distress you may hope in God. If you are in despair, you can get help. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com.

## The shipwreck (Verses 27–44)

[27] It was the fourteenth night.
We were driven back and forth in the Adriatic Sea.
About midnight the sailors suspected
that they were coming near to land.
[28] They measured the depth
and found twenty fathoms.
After a little while they measured again
and found fifteen fathoms.
[29] They were afraid
of running onto rocks.
So they let down four anchors from the back of the ship
and wished for daylight.
[30] The sailors tried
to flee from the ship.
They lowered the lifeboat into the sea
and pretended
that they wanted to put out anchors from the front of the ship.
[31] Then Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers,
“Unless these stay in the ship,
you can’t be saved.”
[32] Then the soldiers cut the ropes of the lifeboat
and let it fall off.
[33] As day was coming,
Paul begged them all
to take some food.
He said,
“Today is the fourteenth day
that you have been waiting
and fasting
and have eaten nothing.
[34] So I beg you
to take some food.
This is for your safety.
For not a hair will perish from any of your heads.”
[35] When he had said this,
he took bread,
gave thanks to God in front of everyone,
broke it
and began to eat.
[36] Then they all cheered up
and also took food.
[37] In all, we were
two hundred seventy-six people on the ship.
[38] When they had eaten enough,
they made the ship lighter
by throwing the wheat into the sea.
[39] When it was day,
they didn’t recognize the land.
But they noticed a bay with a beach.
They decided to try
to run the ship onto it.
[40] They cut off the anchors
and left them in the sea.
At the same time they untied the ropes of the rudders.
They raised the front sail to the wind
and headed for the beach.
[41] But they came to a place
where two seas met.
There the ship ran aground.
The front stuck fast
and could not be moved.
But the back began to break up
from the force of the waves.
[42] The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners,
so that none of them would swim away and escape.
[43] But the centurion wanted to save Paul
and stopped them.
He commanded
that those who could swim
should jump overboard first
and go to land.
[44] The rest should follow,
some on planks
and some on other pieces of the ship.
So they all got safely to land.

*fathom* = a measure of depth, about 6 feet (1.8 meters)

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: At that time the “Adriatic Sea” meant the whole sea between Greece, Italy and Africa.
> Verse 28: Twenty fathoms are about 120 feet (36 meters).
> Verse 35: Paul takes bread, gives thanks to God and breaks it, as Jesus did. In the middle of the storm a meal becomes a sign of hope.
> Verse 37: The ship was large: 276 people were on board.
> Verses 42–43: The soldiers want to kill the prisoners, because they themselves would be punished harshly for every prisoner who escaped. The centurion prevents this.
> Verse 44: As Paul had said: Everyone is saved. No one dies.
> For today: In an emergency you help everyone, not only your own people.
