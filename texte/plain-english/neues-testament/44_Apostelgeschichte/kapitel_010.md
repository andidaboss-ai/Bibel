# Acts – Chapter 10
### God shows no favoritism

---

## Cornelius sees an angel (Verses 1–8)

[1] In Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius.
He was a centurion of what was called the Italian Regiment.
[2] He was a devout man
and feared God
with all his household.
He gave gifts generously to the needy among the people
and always prayed to God.
[3] At about the ninth hour of the day,
he clearly saw in a vision an angel of God.
The angel came to him
and said to him,
“Cornelius!”
[4] Cornelius looked at him
and was frightened.
He said,
“What is it, Lord?”
The angel said to him,
“Your prayers and your gifts to the needy
have gone up for a memorial before God.
[5] Now send men to Joppa,
and get Simon,
who is also called Peter.
[6] He is staying with a tanner named Simon,
whose house is by the seaside.”
[7] When the angel
who spoke to him
had left,
Cornelius called two of his household servants
and a devout soldier
from among those who served him personally.
[8] He explained everything to them
and sent them to Joppa.

*centurion* = a Roman officer in charge of about a hundred soldiers
*memorial* = something that keeps a person in memory

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Cornelius was a Roman officer, a centurion. He led about a hundred soldiers. He was not a Jew.
> Verse 2: Cornelius honored the God of Israel, prayed and helped the poor. Such people were called God-fearers. They often went to the synagogue, but had not become Jews.
> Verse 3: The ninth hour is about three in the afternoon, the time of the Jewish afternoon prayer. Cornelius prays at the same time as devout Jews.
> Verse 4: The prayers and gifts have gone up “for a memorial” before God. God has seen them and won’t forget them.

## Peter has a vision (Verses 9–23)

[9] On the next day, the men were on their journey
and came close to the city.
At about noon, Peter went up on the housetop
to pray.
[10] He became hungry
and wanted to eat.
While the food was being prepared,
he fell into a trance.
[11] He saw heaven opened.
Something came down to him
like a great sheet,
let down by four corners to the earth.
[12] In it were all kinds of four-footed animals of the earth,
wild animals,
reptiles,
and birds of the sky.
[13] A voice came to him,
“Get up, Peter,
kill and eat!”
[14] But Peter said,
“Not so, Lord!
For I have never eaten anything
that is common or unclean.”
[15] A voice came to him again the second time,
“What God has cleansed,
you must not call unclean.”
[16] This happened three times.
Then immediately the sheet was taken up into heaven.
[17] Peter was very puzzled
about what the vision might mean.
Just then the men
who had been sent by Cornelius
had asked their way to Simon’s house.
They were standing at the gate.
[18] They called out
and asked
whether Simon,
who was also called Peter,
was staying there.
[19] While Peter was still thinking about the vision,
the Spirit said to him,
“Three men are looking for you.
[20] Get up,
go down,
and go with them,
without doubting.
For I have sent them.”
[21] Peter went down to the men
and said,
“I am the one
you are looking for.
Why have you come?”
[22] They said,
“Cornelius, a centurion,
a righteous man
who fears God
and is well spoken of by all the nation of the Jews,
was told by a holy angel
to invite you to his house
and to listen
to what you say.”
[23] So Peter invited them in
and gave them a place to stay.
On the next day Peter got up
and went with them.
Some of the brothers from Joppa went along with him.

*trance* = a state like a dream while awake
*common* = here: not holy, not allowed by the food laws

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 9: At that time flat roofs were often used as a quiet place to pray.
> Verses 12–14: According to the food laws of the Torah (Leviticus 11), Jews were not allowed to eat certain animals. Peter keeps these laws, like a devout Jew.
> Verse 15: “What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean.” Peter soon understands: It is not only about food, but about people (verse 28).
> Verse 16: Three times, as with the question “Do you love me?” (John 21).
> Important: For Jews, the food laws are a valuable part of their faith up to today. This vision is not a judgment on them. It shows Peter that God is opening the door to the non-Jewish nations.
> Verse 22: Cornelius is respected by the Jewish people. Jews and Romans could live well together.

## Peter in the house of Cornelius (Verses 24–33)

[24] On the day after that, they came into Caesarea.
Cornelius was waiting for them.
He had called together his relatives
and his close friends.
[25] When Peter came in,
Cornelius met him.
He fell down at his feet
and worshiped him.
[26] But Peter raised him up
and said,
“Stand up!
I myself am also a man.”
[27] As he talked with him,
he went in.
He found many people gathered together.
[28] He said to them,
“You yourselves know
that it is against the law for a Jewish man
to associate with someone of another nation
or to visit him.
But God has shown me
that I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean.
[29] That is why I came without complaint
when I was sent for.
So I ask:
Why did you send for me?”
[30] Cornelius said,
“Four days ago,
I was fasting until this hour.
At the ninth hour, I was praying in my house.
Suddenly a man in bright clothing stood before me
[31] and said,
‘Cornelius,
your prayer is heard,
and your gifts to the needy are remembered in the sight of God.
[32] So send to Joppa
and call Simon,
who is also called Peter.
He is staying in the house of a tanner named Simon,
by the seaside.
When he comes,
he will speak to you.’
[33] So I sent for you at once.
It was good of you
to come.
Now we are all here in the sight of God
to hear all things
that God has commanded you.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 25–26: Peter refuses to be worshiped. No human being should be honored like God.
> Verse 28: At that time many devout Jews avoided close contact with non-Jews, especially eating together, because of the purity rules. Now Peter says: No human being is unclean.
> For today: “I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean.” This stands against every form of racism and exclusion.

## Peter preaches (Verses 34–43)

[34] Then Peter began to speak
and said,
“Truly I understand now
that God doesn’t show favoritism.
[35] But in every nation,
he who fears him
and does what is right
is acceptable to him.
[36] He sent the word to the children of Israel.
He preached the good news of peace
through Jesus Christ.
He is Lord of all.
[37] You yourselves know
what happened throughout all Judea,
beginning from Galilee,
after the baptism
which John preached:
[38] how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth
with the Holy Spirit and with power.
He went about doing good
and healing all
who were oppressed by the devil,
for God was with him.
[39] We are witnesses of everything
he did both in the country of the Jews
and in Jerusalem.
They killed him
by hanging him on a tree.
[40] God raised him up on the third day
and caused him to be seen,
[41] not by all the people,
but by witnesses
who were chosen before by God:
us.
We ate and drank with him
after he rose from the dead.
[42] He commanded us
to preach to the people
and to testify
that he is the one
whom God has appointed as Judge of the living and the dead.
[43] All the prophets testify about him
that through his name everyone
who believes in him
will receive forgiveness of sins.”

*favoritism* = treating some people better than others

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 34–35 contain one of the most important sentences in Acts: God doesn’t show favoritism. Origin, nation and skin color make no difference to God.
> Verse 36: “He is Lord of all”: Jesus doesn’t belong to only one people.
> Verse 38: A short summary of Jesus’ life: He did good and healed.
> Verse 39: “They” are those who had Jesus executed. Here Peter is not speaking to Jews, but to Romans.
> Verse 42: The words *the living and the dead* are also found in the Creeds of the churches.

## The Holy Spirit comes on non-Jews (Verses 44–48)

[44] While Peter was still speaking these words,
the Holy Spirit fell on all
who heard the word.
[45] The believers of the circumcision
who had come with Peter
were amazed,
because the gift of the Holy Spirit was also poured out
on the Gentiles.
[46] For they heard them
speaking in other languages
and praising God.
Then Peter said,
[47] “Can anyone forbid these people
from being baptized with water?
They have received the Holy Spirit
just like us.”
[48] He commanded
that they be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.
Then they asked him
to stay some days.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 45: “The believers of the circumcision” are Jewish Christians. “Gentiles” here simply means people from the other nations who are not Jewish. The word is not an insult.
> Verses 44–46: This is like a second Pentecost. God gives his Spirit also to people who are not Jews. God himself opens the door, even before they are baptized.
> Verse 47: Peter asks: “Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water?” No one may exclude people whom God has accepted.
> For today: The church is there for all people, from all nations and cultures.
