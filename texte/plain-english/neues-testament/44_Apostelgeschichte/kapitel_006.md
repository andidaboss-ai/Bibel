# Acts – Chapter 6
### Seven helpers for the church

---

## Seven men are chosen (Verses 1–7)

[1] In those days,
the number of the disciples kept growing.
Then the Hellenists complained
against the Hebrews,
because their widows were overlooked
in the daily serving of food.
[2] The twelve called the whole crowd of the disciples together
and said,
“It is not right
for us to neglect the word of God
and serve at tables.
[3] So, brothers,
choose from among you seven men
who have a good reputation,
full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom.
We will appoint them over this task.
[4] But we will keep on faithfully with prayer
and with serving the word.”
[5] These words pleased the whole crowd.
They chose Stephen,
a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit,
and Philip,
Prochorus,
Nicanor,
Timon,
Parmenas,
and Nicolaus,
a man from Antioch
who had become a Jew.
[6] They set these men before the apostles.
The apostles prayed
and laid their hands on them.
[7] The word of God spread.
The number of the disciples in Jerusalem grew very large.
A great number of the priests also
became obedient to the faith.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: “Hellenists” and “Hebrews”: Both groups were Jews who believed in Jesus. The Hellenists came from other countries and spoke Greek. The Hebrews probably came from the land of Israel and spoke Aramaic.
> At that time widows often had no one to take care of them. The church gave them food every day.
> Verses 2–4: The apostles settle the conflict wisely: They divide up the tasks. All seven men who are chosen have Greek names. So the group that had been treated unfairly gets its own representatives.
> Verse 6: Laying on hands is a sign of blessing and of giving someone a task. Many churches see here the beginning of the office of deacons, that is, helpers for the poor and needy.
> Verse 7: Even many priests from the temple come to faith.
> For today: If someone in the church is overlooked, it must be taken seriously and a fair solution must be found.

## Stephen is arrested (Verses 8–15)

[8] Stephen was full of faith and power.
He did great wonders and signs among the people.
[9] But some people stood up
from the synagogue called “The Libertines,”
and people from Cyrene and Alexandria,
and from Cilicia and Asia.
They argued with Stephen.
[10] But they weren’t able to stand against the wisdom and the Spirit
with which he spoke.
[11] Then they secretly got men
to say,
“We have heard him speak blasphemous words
against Moses and God.”
[12] They stirred up the people,
the elders,
and the scribes.
They came at Stephen,
seized him,
and brought him to the council.
[13] There they set up false witnesses
who said,
“This man never stops speaking blasphemous words
against this holy place
and the law.
[14] For we have heard him say
that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place
and will change the customs
which Moses handed down to us.”
[15] All who sat in the council
looked at him intently.
They saw his face
like the face of an angel.

> **What does this mean?**
> Blasphemous means: insulting God.
> Verse 9: “The Libertines” (that is, the freed people) were probably Jews who themselves or whose parents had been taken to Rome as slaves and later set free. These people had their own synagogue in Jerusalem.
> Verses 11–13: False witnesses are used against Stephen, as with Jesus (Mark 14:56–58). The Bible itself says: The accusation is false.
> Verse 14: The temple (“this holy place”) and the law of Moses were very important to the Jews. That is why the accusation was serious.
> Verse 15: Stephen’s face shines like that of an angel. This reminds us of Moses, whose face shone when he came from God (Exodus 34:29–30).
> Important: False accusations destroy people. If you are falsely accused, you may get help, for example from a lawyer or a counseling center.
