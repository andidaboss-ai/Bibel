# Acts – Chapter 9
### Saul becomes a disciple

---

## Jesus meets Saul (Verses 1–9)

[1] But Saul was still breathing threats and murder
against the disciples of the Lord.
He went to the high priest
[2] and asked him for letters to the synagogues of Damascus.
If he found any there
who belonged to the Way,
whether men or women,
he wanted to bring them tied up to Jerusalem.
[3] As he traveled,
he came close to Damascus.
Suddenly a light from the sky shone around him.
[4] He fell to the ground
and heard a voice
saying to him,
“Saul, Saul,
why do you persecute me?”
[5] He said,
“Who are you, Lord?”
The Lord said,
“I am Jesus,
whom you are persecuting.
[6] But get up
and go into the city.
There you will be told
what you must do.”
[7] The men
who traveled with him
stood speechless.
They heard the sound,
but saw no one.
[8] Saul got up from the ground.
When his eyes were opened,
he saw nothing.
They led him by the hand
and brought him into Damascus.
[9] For three days he couldn’t see.
He neither ate
nor drank.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Damascus was a large city in Syria with many Jewish communities. “The Way” was one of the first names for the Christians. They followed the way of Jesus.
> Verse 4: “Why do you persecute me?” Saul is persecuting the Christians. But Jesus says: You are persecuting me. Jesus is united with the people who suffer.
> Verses 5–6: Some Bibles, such as the King James Version, have more words here. They are not in the oldest manuscripts. The WEB doesn’t have them.
> Verse 9: Three days of darkness, without food or drink. Saul has to learn to see everything in a new way.
> People often speak of Paul’s “conversion.” But he doesn’t change his religion. He remains a Jew. He recognizes Jesus as the Christ and receives a new task. That is why many people today prefer to speak of his calling.
> For today: No one is too far away from God. God can change any life.

---

## Ananias visits Saul (Verses 10–19)

[10] There was a disciple in Damascus named Ananias.
The Lord said to him in a vision,
“Ananias!”
He said,
“Here I am, Lord.”
[11] The Lord said to him,
“Get up and go to the street
which is called Straight.
In the house of Judah, ask
for a man from Tarsus
named Saul.
For he is praying.
[12] And in a vision he has seen
a man named Ananias
coming in
and laying his hands on him,
so that he might see again.”
[13] But Ananias answered,
“Lord, I have heard from many about this man,
how much evil he did to your saints in Jerusalem.
[14] And here he has authority from the chief priests
to arrest all
who call on your name.”
[15] But the Lord said to him,
“Go your way!
For he is my chosen instrument
to carry my name
to the nations and kings,
and the people of Israel.
[16] For I will show him
how many things he must suffer for my name’s sake.”
[17] So Ananias went
and entered the house.
He laid his hands on him
and said,
“Brother Saul,
the Lord has sent me.
He appeared to you on the road
by which you came.
He sent me so that you may see again
and be filled with the Holy Spirit.”
[18] Immediately something like scales fell from his eyes,
and he could see again.
He got up
and was baptized.
[19] He took food
and became strong again.
Saul stayed several days with the disciples in Damascus.

> **What does this mean?**
> Instrument means: here: a tool, someone God uses.
> Verse 11: The street called Straight still exists in Damascus today. Tarsus was Saul’s hometown, in the south of today’s Turkey.
> Verses 13–14: Ananias is afraid. That is understandable. Saul was dangerous. But he obeys God anyway.
> Verse 17: “Brother Saul”: Ananias calls his former persecutor “brother.” That is forgiveness.
> Verse 18: The saying that the scales fell from someone’s eyes comes from this verse.
> For today: Sometimes God sends people to someone they are afraid of. Reconciliation takes courage.

## Saul preaches Jesus (Verses 20–31)

[20] Immediately he proclaimed the Christ in the synagogues.
He proclaimed that he is the Son of God.
[21] All who heard him
were amazed and said,
“Isn’t this the man
who in Jerusalem attacked those
who called on this name?
And didn’t he come here
to bring them tied up to the chief priests?”
[22] But Saul grew stronger and stronger.
He confused the Jews
who lived in Damascus.
For he proved to them
that this is the Christ.
[23] When many days had passed,
the Jews plotted together
to kill him.
[24] But Saul learned of their plot.
They watched the gates day and night
in order to kill him.
[25] But his disciples took him by night
and let him down through the wall,
lowering him in a basket.
[26] When Saul came to Jerusalem,
he tried
to join the disciples.
But they were all afraid of him.
They didn’t believe
that he was a disciple.
[27] But Barnabas took him
and brought him to the apostles.
He told them
how Saul had seen the Lord on the way,
and that the Lord had spoken to him.
And he told
how Saul had preached boldly in the name of Jesus in Damascus.
[28] From then on, Saul went in and out with them
in Jerusalem.
[29] He preached boldly in the name of the Lord Jesus.
He spoke and argued with the Hellenists.
But they were trying to kill him.
[30] When the brothers found out,
they brought him down to Caesarea
and sent him off to Tarsus.
[31] So the churches throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria
had peace.
They were built up
and lived in the fear of the Lord.
Through the comfort of the Holy Spirit,
they kept growing.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: Saul preaches in the synagogues. He speaks as a Jew to Jews.
> Verses 22–23: “The Jews” here means certain opponents in Damascus, not all Jews. Saul himself is a Jew, and the disciples in Damascus were Jews too.
> Verses 23–25: Paul himself tells about his escape in the basket (2 Corinthians 11:32–33).
> Verses 26–27: The disciples are afraid of Saul. Barnabas, the “Son of Encouragement” (4:36), gives him a chance. He builds a bridge.
> Verse 29: The Hellenists here are Greek-speaking Jews in Jerusalem.
> Verse 31: Here the WEB, our English source text, has the plural “assemblies,” that is, churches. Some Bibles have the singular, the church, following other manuscripts. All the churches in these regions are meant.
> For today: Whoever makes a new beginning needs people like Barnabas who trust him.

## Peter heals Aeneas and raises Tabitha (Verses 32–43)

[32] Peter went throughout all those regions.
He also came down to the saints
who lived at Lydda.
[33] There he found a man named Aeneas.
He had been in bed for eight years,
because he was paralyzed.
[34] Peter said to him,
“Aeneas,
Jesus Christ heals you.
Get up
and make your bed!”
Immediately he got up.
[35] All who lived at Lydda and in Sharon
saw him,
and they turned to the Lord.
[36] Now in Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha,
which when translated means Dorcas.
She was full of good works
and acts of mercy.
[37] In those days, she became sick
and died.
When they had washed her,
they laid her in an upper room.
[38] Lydda was near Joppa.
The disciples heard
that Peter was there.
So they sent two men to him.
They begged him
to come to them without delay.
[39] Peter got up
and went with them.
When he arrived,
they brought him into the upper room.
All the widows stood by him weeping.
They showed him the tunics and other garments
which Dorcas had made
while she was with them.
[40] Peter sent them all out.
He knelt down
and prayed.
Then he turned to the body
and said,
“Tabitha, get up!”
She opened her eyes.
When she saw Peter,
she sat up.
[41] He gave her his hand
and raised her up.
Then he called the saints and the widows
and showed them
that she was alive.
[42] This became known throughout all Joppa.
And many believed in the Lord.
[43] Peter stayed many days in Joppa
with a tanner named Simon.

> **What does this mean?**
> Tanner means: someone who makes leather from animal skins.
> Verse 32: “The saints” is a name for all Christians. Holy means: belonging to God.
> Verse 34: “Jesus Christ heals you”: Peter makes clear who heals.
> Verse 36: Tabitha is one of the few women in the New Testament who are explicitly called a disciple. Tabitha is Aramaic, Dorcas is Greek. Both mean gazelle.
> Verse 39: The widows show the clothes that Tabitha sewed for them. Her love can be seen.
> Verse 40: This reminds us of Jesus and the daughter of Jairus (Mark 5:40–41).
> Verse 43: A tanner worked with animal skins. According to the purity rules this was an unclean job. That Peter stays with him already prepares for chapter 10.
> For today: Tabitha shows: Even simple help like sewing clothes is a service to God.
