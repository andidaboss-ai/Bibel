# Acts – Chapter 23
### Paul before the council and the murder plot

---

## Paul before the council (Verses 1–11)

[1] Paul looked steadily at the council
and said,
“Brothers,
I have lived before God in all good conscience
until today.”
[2] Then the high priest Ananias commanded those
who stood by him
to strike Paul on the mouth.
[3] Then Paul said to him,
“God will strike you,
you whitewashed wall!
You sit here
to judge me according to the law,
and you command me to be struck
against the law?”
[4] Those who stood by said,
“Do you insult God’s high priest?”
[5] Paul said,
“I didn’t know, brothers,
that he was high priest.
For it is written,
‘You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’ ”
[6] Paul noticed
that one part of the council were Sadducees
and the other part Pharisees.
So he cried out in the council,
“Men and brothers,
I am a Pharisee,
a son of Pharisees.
Concerning the hope
and resurrection of the dead
I am being judged!”
[7] When he had said this,
an argument arose
between the Pharisees and Sadducees.
The crowd was divided.
[8] For the Sadducees say
that there is no resurrection,
nor angel, nor spirit.
But the Pharisees confess all of these.
[9] There was a great uproar.
Some of the scribes of the Pharisees stood up,
argued strongly
and said,
“We find no evil in this man.
But if a spirit or angel has spoken to him,
let’s not fight against God!”
[10] The argument became greater and greater.
The commanding officer was afraid
that they would tear Paul to pieces.
So he commanded the soldiers
to go down,
take Paul by force from among them
and bring him into the barracks.
[11] The following night
the Lord stood by him
and said,
“Cheer up, Paul!
For as you have testified about me at Jerusalem,
so you must testify also at Rome.”

*whitewashed* = painted white on the outside

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “Whitewashed wall” means: beautiful on the outside, bad on the inside. Paul accuses the high priest of acting against the law. For an accused person was not to be struck without a verdict.
> Verse 5: Paul quotes Exodus 22:28 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 22:27). He apologizes and respects the law.
> Verse 6: “I am a Pharisee”: Paul says this in the present tense. He still sees himself as a Pharisee.
> Verse 9: The Pharisees defend Paul. This shows: Many Pharisees were open and fair.
> Verse 11: Jesus encourages Paul: You will come to Rome. That is the goal of the book.

## The murder plot (Verses 12–22)

[12] When it was day,
some of the Jews banded together.
They bound themselves with a curse
that they would neither eat nor drink
until they had killed Paul.
[13] There were more than forty people
who had made this plot.
[14] They came to the chief priests and the elders
and said,
“We have bound ourselves under a great curse
to taste nothing
until we have killed Paul.
[15] So now you, together with the council,
must tell the commanding officer
to bring him down to you tomorrow,
as though you wanted to look into his case more exactly.
We are ready
to kill him
before he comes near.”
[16] But the son of Paul’s sister
heard about the ambush.
He went into the barracks
and told Paul.
[17] Paul called one of the centurions
and said,
“Bring this young man to the commanding officer.
He has something to tell him.”
[18] So he took him
and brought him to the commanding officer
and said,
“Paul, the prisoner, called me.
He asked me
to bring this young man to you.
He has something to tell you.”
[19] The commanding officer took him by the hand,
went aside with him
and asked him privately,
“What is it that you have to tell me?”
[20] He said,
“The Jews have agreed
to ask you
to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow,
as though they wanted to question him more exactly.
[21] So don’t give in to them!
For more than forty men
are lying in wait for him.
They have bound themselves under a curse
to neither eat nor drink
until they have killed him.
Now they are ready
and are only waiting for your promise.”
[22] So the commanding officer let the young man go
and ordered him,
“Tell no one
that you have told me these things.”

*ambush* = a hidden attack

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Some of the Jews”: This is about a group of about forty men. Not about the Jewish people. Their plan was a crime.
> Verses 14–15: Some leaders know about the murder plot and help with it. That is a serious injustice.
> Verse 16: Here we learn: Paul had a sister in Jerusalem. Her son saves his life. A brave young person.
> For today: Anyone who learns about a planned crime should report it. Call the police: 911 in the US, 999 in the UK.

## Paul is brought to Caesarea (Verses 23–35)

[23] He called two of the centurions to himself
and said,
“Prepare two hundred soldiers
to go as far as Caesarea,
with seventy horsemen
and two hundred men armed with spears.
They should leave at the third hour of the night.
[24] Also provide animals to ride,
so that Paul can ride on one.
Bring him safely to Felix the governor.”
[25] He wrote a letter
like this:
[26] “Claudius Lysias
to the most excellent governor Felix: Greetings.
[27] This man was seized by the Jews
and was about
to be killed by them.
Then I came with the soldiers
and rescued him,
for I had learned
that he was a Roman.
[28] I wanted to know
why they accused him.
So I brought him down to their council.
[29] I found
that he was accused about questions of their law.
But he has done nothing
that deserves death or prison.
[30] Then I was told
that the Jews were lying in wait for the man.
So I sent him to you immediately.
I also ordered his accusers
to bring their accusations against him before you.
Farewell.”
[31] So the soldiers did
what they were ordered.
They took Paul
and brought him by night to Antipatris.
[32] The next day they let the horsemen go on with him
and returned to the barracks.
[33] The horsemen came to Caesarea.
They delivered the letter to the governor
and also presented Paul to him.
[34] The governor read the letter
and asked
what province Paul was from.
When he learned
that he was from Cilicia,
[35] he said,
“I will hear you fully
when your accusers also arrive.”
And he commanded
that Paul be kept under guard in Herod’s palace.

*third hour of the night* = about 9 o’clock in the evening

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Almost 500 soldiers protect one prisoner. That is how seriously the Roman takes the danger.
> Verse 26: Now we learn the name of the commanding officer: Claudius Lysias. Felix was the Roman governor of Judea, from about AD 52 to 60.
> Verse 27: In the letter Lysias presents himself a little better than things really were: He only learned later that Paul was a Roman (22:25–29).
> Verse 29: The Roman also finds: Paul is innocent.
> Verse 35: Herod’s palace was the official residence of the governor in Caesarea.
