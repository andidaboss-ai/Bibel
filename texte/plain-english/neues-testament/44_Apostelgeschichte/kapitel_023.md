# Acts – Chapter 23
### Paul before the council and the murder plot

---

## Paul before the council (Verses 1–11)

[1] Paul looked straight at the council.
He said, “Brothers, I have lived my life before God with a completely clear conscience until this day.”
[2] Then the high priest Ananias gave an order to the men standing next to Paul.
They were to hit him on the mouth.
[3] Then Paul said to him, “God will strike you, you whitewashed wall!
You sit there to judge me by the law.
But you break the law yourself when you order them to hit me!”
[4] The men standing there said, “How dare you insult God’s high priest!”
[5] Paul said, “Brothers, I didn’t know that he was the high priest.
For it is written: ‘You must not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’”
[6] Paul knew that some of the council were Sadducees and the others were Pharisees.
So he called out in the council, “Brothers, I am a Pharisee, the son of Pharisees.
I am on trial because I hope that the dead will rise again!”
[7] When he said this, the Pharisees and the Sadducees began to argue.
The group was divided.
[8] The Sadducees say that there is no resurrection.
They say that there are no angels and no spirits.
But the Pharisees believe in all of these.
[9] There was a lot of shouting.
Some of the scribes who belonged to the Pharisees stood up.
They argued hard.
They said, “We find nothing wrong with this man.
Maybe a spirit or an angel has spoken to him.
Then let’s not fight against God!”
[10] The argument became more and more violent.
The commanding officer was afraid that they would tear Paul to pieces.
So he ordered the soldiers to go down and take Paul away from them by force.
They were to bring him into the barracks.
[11] The next night the Lord stood next to Paul.
He said, “Cheer up, Paul!
You have been my witness in Jerusalem.
You must also be my witness in Rome.”

*whitewashed* = painted white on the outside

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 3: “Whitewashed wall” means: beautiful on the outside, bad on the inside. Paul accuses the high priest of acting against the law. For an accused person was not to be struck without a verdict.
> Verse 5: Paul quotes Exodus 22:28 (in the Hebrew Bible and in many German Bibles 22:27). He apologizes and respects the law.
> Verse 6: “I am a Pharisee”: Paul says this in the present tense. He still sees himself as a Pharisee.
> Verse 9: The Pharisees defend Paul. This shows: Many Pharisees were open and fair.
> Verse 11: Jesus encourages Paul: You will come to Rome. That is the goal of the book.

## The murder plot (Verses 12–22)

[12] When it was day, some of the Jews got together and made a plan.
They swore an oath and put themselves under a curse.
They would not eat or drink anything until they had killed Paul.
[13] More than forty men were part of this plot.
[14] They went to the chief priests and the elders.
They said, “We have put ourselves under a strong curse.
We will not eat anything until we have killed Paul.
[15] So now you and the council must send a message to the commanding officer.
Ask him to bring Paul down to you tomorrow.
Act as if you want to look at his case more closely.
We are ready to kill him before he gets there.”
[16] But the son of Paul’s sister heard about the ambush.
He went into the barracks and told Paul.
[17] Paul called one of the centurions.
He said, “Take this young man to the commanding officer.
He has something to tell him.”
[18] So the centurion took him to the commanding officer.
He said, “Paul, the prisoner, called me.
He asked me to bring this young man to you.
He has something to tell you.”
[19] The commanding officer took the young man by the hand.
He went off to the side with him.
He asked him privately, “What do you have to tell me?”
[20] The young man said, “The Jews have agreed to ask you a favor.
They want you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow.
They will act as if they want to question him more closely.
[21] But don’t do what they ask!
More than forty men are hiding and waiting for him.
They have put themselves under a curse.
They will not eat or drink until they have killed him.
They are ready now.
They are only waiting for you to say yes.”
[22] The commanding officer let the young man go.
He told him, “Don’t tell anyone that you told me this.”

*ambush* = a hidden attack

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 12: “Some of the Jews”: This is about a group of about forty men. Not about the Jewish people. Their plan was a crime.
> Verses 14–15: Some leaders know about the murder plot and help with it. That is a serious injustice.
> Verse 16: Here we learn: Paul had a sister in Jerusalem. Her son saves his life. A brave young person.
> For today: Anyone who learns about a planned crime should report it. Call the police: 911 in the US, 999 in the UK.

## Paul is brought to Caesarea (Verses 23–35)

[23] Then the commanding officer called two of the centurions.
He said, “Get two hundred soldiers ready to go to Caesarea.
Also get seventy horsemen and two hundred men with spears.
Leave at the third hour of the night.
[24] Get some animals for Paul to ride on.
Bring him safely to Felix the governor.”
[25] He wrote a letter.
This is what it said:
[26] “From Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor Felix: Greetings!
[27] The Jews had taken hold of this man.
They were about to kill him.
But I came with my soldiers and rescued him.
I had learned that he was a Roman.
[28] I wanted to know why they were accusing him.
So I brought him to their council.
[29] I found out that they were accusing him about questions of their own law.
But he has done nothing that deserves death or prison.
[30] Then I was told that the Jews were lying in wait for him.
So I sent him to you at once.
I have also ordered his accusers to bring their charges against him to you.
Farewell.”
[31] So the soldiers did what they were ordered.
They took Paul and brought him to Antipatris during the night.
[32] The next day the horsemen went on with Paul.
The other soldiers went back to the barracks.
[33] The horsemen came to Caesarea.
They gave the letter to the governor.
They also brought Paul to him.
[34] The governor read the letter.
He asked Paul what province he was from.
He learned that Paul was from Cilicia.
[35] Then he said, “I will hear your case when your accusers arrive too.”
He ordered that Paul be kept under guard in Herod’s palace.

*third hour of the night* = about 9 o’clock in the evening

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 23: Almost 500 soldiers protect one prisoner. That is how seriously the Roman takes the danger.
> Verse 26: Now we learn the name of the commanding officer: Claudius Lysias. Felix was the Roman governor of Judea, from about AD 52 to 60.
> Verse 27: In the letter Lysias presents himself a little better than things really were: He only learned later that Paul was a Roman (22:25–29).
> Verse 29: The Roman also finds: Paul is innocent.
> Verse 35: Herod’s palace was the official residence of the governor in Caesarea.
