# Acts – Chapter 16
### The good news comes to Europe

---

## Timothy joins them (Verses 1–5)

[1] Paul came to Derbe and Lystra.
A disciple was there,
named Timothy.
His mother was a Jewish woman who believed,
but his father was a Greek.
[2] The brothers at Lystra and Iconium
spoke well of him.
[3] Paul wanted him to come along with him.
He took him
and circumcised him
because of the Jews
who lived in those parts.
For they all knew
that his father was a Greek.
[4] As they went on their way through the cities,
they passed on to the churches the decisions
which the apostles and elders in Jerusalem had made.
The churches were to keep them.
[5] So the churches were strengthened in the faith
and grew in number daily.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: Timothy had a Jewish mother and a Greek father. He becomes a close co-worker of Paul. Two letters in the Bible are addressed to him.
> Verse 3: Why does Paul circumcise Timothy, even though the Council of Jerusalem said that non-Jews don’t have to be circumcised? Because Timothy had a Jewish mother, many Jews considered him a Jew. Paul didn’t want that to become an obstacle. He respects the Jewish tradition.
> Verse 4: The “decisions” are the rules from the letter in chapter 15.

---

## Paul sees a man from Macedonia (Verses 6–10)

[6] They went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia.
The Holy Spirit kept them
from speaking the word in Asia.
[7] When they came near Mysia,
they tried
to go into Bithynia.
But the Spirit didn’t allow them.
[8] They passed by Mysia
and came down to Troas.
[9] In the night Paul had a vision:
A man of Macedonia was standing there,
begging him
and saying,
“Come over into Macedonia
and help us.”
[10] When he had seen the vision,
we immediately tried to set out for Macedonia.
For we were sure
that the Lord had called us
to preach the Good News to them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 6–7: Twice a way is blocked. Sometimes God also leads by closing a door.
> Verse 9: Macedonia is in the north of today’s Greece. With this journey the good news comes to Europe for the first time.
> Verse 10: From here on the narrator writes “we.” Many think: Luke, the author, was now there himself.

---

## Lydia in Philippi (Verses 11–15)

[11] So we set sail from Troas
and sailed straight to Samothrace,
and the next day to Neapolis,
[12] and from there to Philippi.
It is the leading city of that district of Macedonia,
a Roman colony.
We stayed some days in this city.
[13] On the Sabbath day we went outside the city
by a riverside.
We thought
that there was a place of prayer there.
We sat down
and spoke to the women
who had come together.
[14] A woman named Lydia was listening.
She was a seller of purple cloth
from the city of Thyatira.
She worshiped God.
The Lord opened her heart
to pay attention to the things
which Paul said.
[15] She was baptized,
together with her household.
Then she begged us,
“If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord,
come into my house
and stay.”
And she persuaded us.

> **What does this mean?**
> Colony means: here: a city where many Roman citizens lived, with Roman rights.
> Verse 12: Philippi was a Roman colony. Many former Roman soldiers lived there. Later Paul wrote a letter to the church there, the letter to the Philippians.
> Verse 13: There was probably no synagogue there. Jewish and God-fearing women met by the river to pray.
> Verse 14: Lydia is the first person in Europe of whom the Bible tells that she believed in Jesus. She was an independent businesswoman. Purple was an expensive dye for cloth.
> Verse 15: Lydia’s house becomes the first meeting place of the church in Philippi. She is hospitable.

## The slave girl with a spirit of fortune telling (Verses 16–24)

[16] As we were going to the place of prayer,
a young slave girl met us.
She had a spirit of fortune telling.
By her fortune telling she brought her masters much money.
[17] She followed Paul and us
and cried out,
“These men are servants of the Most High God!
They proclaim to us a way of salvation!”
[18] She did this for many days.
But Paul became greatly annoyed.
He turned
and said to the spirit,
“I command you in the name of Jesus Christ:
come out of her!”
And it came out that very hour.
[19] When her masters saw
that their hope of making money was gone,
they seized Paul and Silas.
They dragged them into the marketplace
before the rulers.
[20] They brought them to the magistrates
and said,
“These men, being Jews,
are stirring up our city.
[21] They promote customs
which it is not lawful for us to accept or to practice,
being Romans.”
[22] The crowd rose up together against them.
The magistrates tore their clothes off them
and commanded
that they be beaten with rods.
[23] When they had given them many blows,
they threw them into prison.
They ordered the jailer
to keep them safely.
[24] Having received such a command,
he threw them into the inner prison
and fastened their feet in the stocks.

> **What does this mean?**
> Magistrates means: the city judges.
> Stocks means: a wooden frame that holds a prisoner’s feet tight.
> Verse 16: The young woman was a slave. Other people made money from her. That is exploitation.
> Verse 18: Paul sets her free. After that she is “worthless” to her masters. Her masters only care about their money, not about the person.
> Verses 20–21: Here hostility toward Jews among the Romans shows itself: “Being Jews” is used as an accusation. The Bible tells this as an injustice.
> Verses 22–24: Paul and Silas are beaten and locked up without a trial.
> Important: Human trafficking and exploitation still exist today. If you are affected or notice something, you can call the police (911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or a hotline against human trafficking: in the US, the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888; in the UK, the Modern Slavery Helpline at 08000 121 700.

## An earthquake in the prison (Verses 25–40)

[25] About midnight Paul and Silas were praying
and singing hymns to God.
The other prisoners were listening to them.
[26] Suddenly there was a great earthquake,
so that the foundations of the prison were shaken.
Immediately all the doors were opened,
and everyone’s chains came loose.
[27] The jailer woke up.
He saw the prison doors open.
He drew his sword
and was about to kill himself,
because he thought
that the prisoners had escaped.
[28] But Paul cried with a loud voice,
“Don’t harm yourself,
for we are all here!”
[29] The jailer called for lights
and rushed in.
Trembling, he fell down before Paul and Silas.
[30] He brought them out
and said,
“Sirs,
what must I do
to be saved?”
[31] They said,
“Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ,
and you will be saved,
you and your household.”
[32] They spoke the word of the Lord to him,
and to all
who were in his house.
[33] He took them that same hour of the night
and washed their wounds.
Immediately he was baptized,
he and all his household.
[34] He brought them up into his house
and set food before them.
He rejoiced greatly with all his household,
because he had come to believe in God.
[35] When it was day,
the magistrates sent the officers,
saying,
“Let those men go.”
[36] The jailer reported these words to Paul,
saying,
“The magistrates have sent word to let you go.
So come out now
and go in peace.”
[37] But Paul said to them,
“They have beaten us publicly
without a trial,
men who are Romans,
and have thrown us into prison!
And now they want to release us secretly?
No, most certainly not!
Let them come themselves
and bring us out!”
[38] The officers reported these words to the magistrates.
They were afraid
when they heard
that they were Romans.
[39] They came
and begged them.
They brought them out
and asked them
to leave the city.
[40] They went out of the prison
and went into Lydia’s house.
When they had seen the brothers,
they encouraged them
and then left.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 25: In the middle of pain and imprisonment Paul and Silas pray and sing. Their faith gives them strength.
> Verses 27–28: The jailer wants to kill himself because he thinks he will be punished harshly for the escape. Paul saves him: “Don’t harm yourself!”
> Important: If you are thinking about ending your life, you are not alone. Help is available around the clock: In the US, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call Samaritans at 116 123. Elsewhere, find help at findahelpline.com. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
> Verses 30–34: The jailer who locked them up now washes their wounds. Enemies become brothers.
> Verse 37: Roman citizens were not allowed to be beaten without a trial. Paul insists on his rights. In this way he also protects the young church in Philippi.
> For today: Christians may also stand up for their rights. Human rights apply to everyone.
