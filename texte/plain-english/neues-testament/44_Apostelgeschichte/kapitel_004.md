# Acts – Chapter 4
### “We can’t help telling”

---

## Peter and John are arrested (Verses 1–4)

[1] While they were still speaking to the people,
the priests,
the captain of the temple,
and the Sadducees came to them.
[2] They were upset,
because the apostles were teaching the people
and proclaiming
that in Jesus there is the resurrection from the dead.
[3] They arrested them
and put them in custody until the next day,
for it was now evening.
[4] But many of those
who had heard the word
believed.
The number of the men came to about five thousand.

*custody* = prison, being held by guards

> **What does this mean?**
> The Sadducees were a group among the temple leaders. They didn’t believe in the resurrection of the dead (compare Luke 20:27). That is why it especially angers them that the apostles speak about the resurrection.
> The captain of the temple was responsible for order in the temple.
> Verse 4: The church grows in spite of the arrest. Only the men are counted, as was usual then. Women and children came in addition.

## Peter before the council (Verses 5–22)

[5] In the morning,
their rulers,
elders,
and scribes
gathered together in Jerusalem.
[6] Annas the high priest was there,
with Caiaphas, John, Alexander,
and all who were relatives of the high priest.
[7] They stood Peter and John in the middle of them
and asked,
“By what power,
or in what name,
have you done this?”
[8] Then Peter,
filled with the Holy Spirit,
said to them,
“You rulers of the people
and elders of Israel,
[9] today we are being questioned
about a good deed done to a crippled man.
You ask
by what means this man has been healed.
[10] Then may it be known to you all,
and to all the people of Israel:
In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth,
whom you crucified,
whom God raised from the dead,
by him this man stands here before you healthy.
[11] He is
‘the stone which was regarded as worthless by you, the builders,
which has become the head of the corner.’
[12] There is salvation in no one else.
For there is no other name under heaven
that is given among people,
by which we must be saved!”
[13] They saw the boldness of Peter and John.
And they noticed
that they were unlearned and ordinary men.
They were amazed.
They recognized
that they had been with Jesus.
[14] They also saw the man who was healed
standing with them.
So they could say nothing against it.
[15] They commanded them
to go out of the council.
Then they talked it over among themselves,
[16] saying,
“What shall we do to these men?
For a clear miracle has really been done through them.
Everyone who lives in Jerusalem
can plainly see it,
and we can’t deny it.
[17] But so that this spreads no further among the people,
let’s threaten them,
so that from now on they don’t speak to anyone
in this name.”
[18] They called them in
and commanded them
not to speak at all
or teach in the name of Jesus.
[19] But Peter and John answered them,
“Judge for yourselves
whether it is right in the sight of God
to listen to you rather than to God.
[20] For we can’t help telling
the things which we saw and heard.”
[21] They threatened them further
and then let them go.
They found no way
to punish them,
because of the people.
For everyone praised God
for what had happened.
[22] For the man
on whom this miracle of healing was done
was more than forty years old.

*the head of the corner* = the most important stone in a building
*unlearned* = without schooling in the Scriptures

> **What does this mean?**
> The council (in Greek “Sanhedrin”) was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem. We already know Annas and Caiaphas from the trial of Jesus (John 18).
> Verse 10: “Whom you crucified”: Here Peter is speaking to the leaders who had handed Jesus over to Pilate. He is not speaking to the Jewish people as a whole. The people praise God for the healing (verse 21).
> Verse 11 quotes Psalm 118:22.
> Verse 12: “There is salvation in no one else”: For Christians Jesus is the Savior. Christians think differently about how God deals with people of other religions. Many churches say today: God’s love is greater than our understanding. Christians are to respect other religions.
> Verse 13: “Unlearned” means: They had not studied with scribes. They were fishermen. But they speak boldly.
> Verses 19–20: In chapter 5 the apostles say something similar again: One must obey God more than people (5:29). Later this sentence gave many people courage to stand up against injustice, also in dictatorships.
> Verse 22: The man had been lame for over 40 years. That is why the miracle was so clear to everyone.

## The church prays (Verses 23–31)

[23] When they had been let go,
they went to their own people.
They reported everything
that the chief priests and the elders
had said to them.
[24] When the others heard it,
they all raised their voices to God together and said,
“O Lord,
you are God.
You made the sky, the earth, the sea,
and all that is in them.
[25] By the mouth of your servant David, you said,
‘Why do the nations rage,
and the peoples plot a vain thing?
[26] The kings of the earth take a stand,
and the rulers plot together,
against the Lord,
and against his Christ.’
[27] For truly, both Herod and Pontius Pilate,
with the Gentiles and the people of Israel,
were gathered together
against your holy servant Jesus,
whom you anointed.
[28] They did
whatever your hand and your plan
had decided beforehand
should happen.
[29] Now, Lord,
look at their threats.
Grant to your servants
to speak your word with all boldness,
[30] while you stretch out your hand to heal,
and so that signs and wonders may be done
through the name of your holy Servant Jesus.”
[31] When they had prayed,
the place was shaken
where they were gathered together.
They were all filled with the Holy Spirit,
and they spoke the word of God with boldness.

*vain* = useless, in vain
*Gentiles* = people who are not Jews

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 24: The church prays to the Creator. This reminds us of Psalm 146:6.
> Verses 25–26 quote Psalm 2:1–2.
> Verse 27: Many German Bibles also have the words in this city here. These words are in some old manuscripts; the WEB doesn’t have them.
> The church names everyone who stood against Jesus: Herod, Pilate, the Romans and people from Israel. No single group alone carries the guilt.
> Verse 29: The church doesn’t ask for revenge on its opponents. It asks for courage.
> For today: In fear and danger we may pray together and ask for courage.

## The believers share everything (Verses 32–37)

[32] The many people who believed
were of one heart and soul.
No one said of anything he owned
that it was his alone.
But they had all things in common.
[33] With great power,
the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus.
And great grace was on them all.
[34] For there was no one among them
who was in need.
All who owned lands or houses
sold them
and brought the money
[35] and laid it at the apostles’ feet.
Each one received from it
as much as he needed.
[36] Joses,
whom the apostles called Barnabas,
which means Son of Encouragement,
was a Levite
and came from Cyprus.
[37] He had a field.
He sold it,
brought the money,
and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 32: “One heart and soul”: Many languages have taken this saying from this verse.
> Verse 34: This reminds us of Deuteronomy 15:4: There God promises that there will be no poor among his people.
> The sharing was voluntary. It was not forced, but an expression of love.
> Verse 36: Joses is a form of Joseph; many German Bibles call him that. “Son of Encouragement” can also be translated son of comfort. Barnabas later becomes an important companion of Paul.
> A Levite belonged to the tribe of Levi. Levites helped with the service in the temple.
> For today: A church in which no one suffers need is a strong sign. If you are in money trouble, you can find help from a free debt advice service or from the social services of churches, such as Catholic Charities or the Salvation Army.
