# Acts – Chapter 4
### “We can’t help telling”

---

## Peter and John are arrested (Verses 1–4)

[1] Peter and John were still speaking to the people.
Then the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees came up to them.
[2] They were angry because the apostles were teaching the people.
The apostles were telling them that in Jesus the dead rise again.
[3] So they arrested them.
It was already evening.
So they put them in custody until the next day.
[4] But many of those who had heard the message believed.
The number of men grew to about 5,000.

> **What does this mean?**
> Custody means: prison, being held by guards.
> The Sadducees were a group among the temple leaders. They didn’t believe in the resurrection of the dead (compare Luke 20:27). That is why it especially angers them that the apostles speak about the resurrection.
> The captain of the temple was responsible for order in the temple.
> Verse 4: The church grows in spite of the arrest. Only the men are counted, as was usual then. Women and children came in addition.

---

## Peter before the council (Verses 5–22)

[5] The next morning the rulers, the elders, and the scribes met together in Jerusalem.
[6] Annas the high priest was there.
Caiaphas, John, and Alexander were there too, and all the relatives of the high priest.
[7] They had Peter and John stand in front of them.
They asked, “By what power did you do this?
Or in whose name did you do it?”
[8] Then Peter was filled with the Holy Spirit.
He said to them, “You rulers of the people and elders of Israel!
[9] Today we are being questioned about a good deed.
We helped a man who was crippled.
You want to know how this man was healed.
[10] So all of you, and all the people of Israel, should know this:
This man stands in front of you healthy and whole.
That happened in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.
You crucified him, but God raised him from the dead.
Through Jesus this man is healthy.
[11] Jesus is ‘the stone that you builders thought was worthless.
But it has become the head of the corner.’
[12] There is salvation in no one else.
There is no other name under heaven given to people by which we must be saved!”
[13] The leaders saw how bold Peter and John were.
They noticed that these men were unlearned and ordinary people.
They were amazed.
They realized that these men had been with Jesus.
[14] They also saw the healed man standing there with them.
So they could say nothing against it.
[15] They ordered them to leave the council room.
Then they talked it over among themselves.
[16] They said, “What should we do with these men?
They have clearly done an amazing miracle.
Everyone who lives in Jerusalem can see it.
We can’t deny it.
[17] But we must stop this from spreading any further among the people.
So let’s threaten them.
From now on they must not speak to anyone in this name.”
[18] They called them back in.
They ordered them not to speak or teach in the name of Jesus at all.
[19] But Peter and John answered them, “You decide for yourselves:
Is it right in God’s eyes to listen to you rather than to God?
[20] We can’t help telling what we have seen and heard.”
[21] The leaders threatened them even more.
Then they let them go.
They found no way to punish them, because of the people.
Everyone was praising God for what had happened.
[22] The man who had been healed by this miracle was more than 40 years old.


> **What does this mean?**
> The head of the corner means: the most important stone in a building.
> Unlearned means: without schooling in the Scriptures.
> The council (in Greek “Sanhedrin”) was the highest Jewish authority in Jerusalem. We already know Annas and Caiaphas from the trial of Jesus (John 18).
> Verse 10: “Whom you crucified”: Here Peter is speaking to the leaders who had handed Jesus over to Pilate. He is not speaking to the Jewish people as a whole. The people praise God for the healing (verse 21).
> Verse 11 quotes Psalm 118:22.
> Verse 12: “There is salvation in no one else”: For Christians Jesus is the Savior. Christians think differently about how God deals with people of other religions. Many churches say today: God’s love is greater than our understanding. Christians are to respect other religions.
> Verse 13: “Unlearned” means: They had not studied with scribes. They were fishermen. But they speak boldly.
> Verses 19–20: In chapter 5 the apostles say something similar again: One must obey God more than people (5:29). Later this sentence gave many people courage to stand up against injustice, also in dictatorships.
> Verse 22: The man had been lame for over 40 years. That is why the miracle was so clear to everyone.

## The church prays (Verses 23–31)

[23] After they were set free, Peter and John went back to their own people.
They told them everything the chief priests and the elders had said to them.
[24] When the believers heard it, they all prayed to God together.
They said, “Lord, you are God.
You made the sky, the earth, the sea, and everything in them.
[25] You spoke through your servant David.
He said: ‘Why are the nations so angry?
Why do the peoples make vain plans?
[26] The kings of the earth get ready to fight.
The rulers make plans together against the Lord and against his Christ.’
[27] And that is truly what happened.
Herod and Pontius Pilate came together with the Gentiles and the people of Israel.
They came together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed.
[28] They did what your power and your plan had decided long before.
[29] And now, Lord, look at their threats.
Help your servants to speak your word with great boldness.
[30] Stretch out your hand to heal.
Let signs and wonders happen through the name of your holy Servant Jesus.”
[31] When they had prayed, the place where they had gathered shook.
They were all filled with the Holy Spirit.
And they spoke the word of God boldly.

> **What does this mean?**
> Vain means: useless, in vain.
> Gentiles means: people who are not Jews.
> Verse 24: The church prays to the Creator. This reminds us of Psalm 146:6.
> Verses 25–26 quote Psalm 2:1–2.
> Verse 27: Some Bibles also have the words in this city here. These words are in some old manuscripts; the WEB doesn’t have them.
> The church names everyone who stood against Jesus: Herod, Pilate, the Romans and people from Israel. No single group alone carries the guilt.
> Verse 29: The church doesn’t ask for revenge on its opponents. It asks for courage.
> For today: In fear and danger we may pray together and ask for courage.

## The believers share everything (Verses 32–37)

[32] All the many believers were of one heart and soul.
No one said that anything he owned was his own.
They shared everything they had.
[33] The apostles told about the resurrection of the Lord Jesus with great power.
And great grace was on all of them.
[34] No one among them was in need.
Those who owned land or houses sold them.
They brought the money from the sale
[35] and put it at the apostles’ feet.
Then it was given out to each person as he had need.
[36] There was a man named Joses.
The apostles also called him Barnabas.
That name means “Son of Encouragement.”
He was a Levite, born in Cyprus.
[37] He owned a field.
He sold it, brought the money, and put it at the apostles’ feet.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 32: “One heart and soul”: Many languages have taken this saying from this verse.
> Verse 34: This reminds us of Deuteronomy 15:4: There God promises that there will be no poor among his people.
> The sharing was voluntary. It was not forced, but an expression of love.
> Verse 36: Joses is a form of Joseph; some Bibles call him that. “Son of Encouragement” can also be translated son of comfort. Barnabas later becomes an important companion of Paul.
> A Levite belonged to the tribe of Levi. Levites helped with the service in the temple.
> For today: A church in which no one suffers need is a strong sign. If you are in money trouble, you can find help from a free debt advice service or from the social services of churches, such as Catholic Charities or the Salvation Army.
