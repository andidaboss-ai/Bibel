# Acts – Chapter 15
### The meeting of the apostles in Jerusalem

---

## A dispute about circumcision (Verses 1–5)

[1] Some men came down from Judea
and taught the brothers,
“Unless you are circumcised
after the custom of Moses,
you can’t be saved.”
[2] So there was no small conflict
and a heated discussion
between them and Paul and Barnabas.
So it was decided:
Paul, Barnabas, and some others of them
were to go up to Jerusalem
to the apostles and elders
about this question.
[3] The assembly sent them on their way.
They passed through both Phoenicia and Samaria.
Everywhere they told
how the Gentiles were turning to God.
This caused great joy to all the brothers.
[4] When they came to Jerusalem,
they were welcomed by the assembly,
the apostles, and the elders.
They reported everything
that God had done with them.
[5] But some of the party of the Pharisees
who had come to believe
stood up and said,
“It is necessary to circumcise them,
and to command them
to keep the law of Moses.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The question was: Do people from other nations first have to become Jews, that is, be circumcised and keep the whole Torah, in order to be Christians?
> Circumcision is the sign of God’s covenant with Abraham (Genesis 17). It is still very important for Jews today.
> Verse 5: Pharisees also belonged to the church. Their concern was sincere: They wanted to stay faithful to God’s law.
> For today: There are conflicts in the church too. What matters is how they are settled: People come together, listen to one another and seek God’s will together.

## Peter and James speak (Verses 6–21)

[6] The apostles and the elders gathered together
to consider this matter.
[7] After much discussion,
Peter stood up
and said to them,
“Brothers,
you know
that a good while ago God made a choice among you:
By my mouth the nations were to hear
the word of the Good News
and believe.
[8] God, who knows the heart,
showed that he accepted them
by giving them the Holy Spirit,
just as he did to us.
[9] He made no distinction
between us and them.
He cleansed their hearts by faith.
[10] So why do you test God now?
Why do you want to put a yoke on the neck of the disciples
which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?
[11] But we believe
that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus,
just as they are.”
[12] The whole crowd kept silent.
They listened to Barnabas and Paul.
They reported
what signs and wonders God had done through them
among the nations.
[13] After they had finished,
James answered,
“Brothers, listen to me.
[14] Simeon has told
how God first visited the nations
to take out of them a people for his name.
[15] This agrees with the words of the prophets.
As it is written,
[16] ‘After these things I will return.
I will again build the tabernacle of David,
which has fallen.
I will again build its ruins.
I will set it up,
[17] so that the rest of men may seek after the Lord:
all the Gentiles who are called by my name,
says the Lord,
who does all these things.’
[18] All of God’s works are known to him from eternity.
[19] So my judgment is
that we don’t make it hard for those from among the Gentiles
who turn to God.
[20] But we should write to them
that they keep away from the pollution of idols,
from sexual immorality,
from what is strangled,
and from blood.
[21] For from generations of old,
Moses has in every city
those who preach him.
He is read in the synagogues every Sabbath.”

*yoke* = a wooden bar put on the necks of animals that pull a load
*strangled* = here: meat from animals killed without letting the blood drain out

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–9: Peter recalls Cornelius (chapter 10). God gave non-Jews the Spirit, without circumcision.
> Verse 10: A “yoke” is a piece of wood with which animals pull a cart. Peter means: You are putting a burden on the new Christians.
> Important: For devout Jews the Torah is not a burden, but a gift and a joy (Psalm 119). Here Peter does not say that the law is bad. He says: Salvation comes by grace, for Jews and non-Jews alike (verse 11).
> Verse 13: James is the brother of Jesus and a leader of the church in Jerusalem.
> Verse 14: “Simeon” is Simon Peter, with his Hebrew name.
> Verses 16–17 quote Amos 9:11–12 in the Greek translation.
> Verse 20: These four rules recall rules for foreigners who lived among Israel (Leviticus 17–18). In this way Jewish and non-Jewish Christians could eat and live together.
> Verse 21: James respects the Torah and the synagogue.

## The letter to the churches (Verses 22–33)

[22] Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders,
with the whole assembly,
to choose men from among them
and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas.
They chose Judas called Barsabbas,
and Silas.
Both were leading men among the brothers.
[23] They gave them a letter.
It said:
“The apostles, the elders, and the brothers,
to the brothers who are of the Gentiles
in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia:
greetings.
[24] We have heard
that some who went out from us
have troubled you with words
and unsettled your souls.
They said,
‘You must be circumcised
and keep the law.’
We gave them no such command.
[25] So it seemed good to us,
having come to one mind,
to choose men
and send them to you
with our beloved Barnabas and Paul.
[26] These are men who have risked their lives
for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[27] So we have sent Judas and Silas.
They will also tell you the same things
by word of mouth.
[28] For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us,
to put no greater burden on you
than these necessary things:
[29] that you keep away from things sacrificed to idols,
from blood,
from things strangled,
and from sexual immorality.
If you keep yourselves from these,
it will be well with you.
Farewell.”
[30] So when they were sent off,
they came to Antioch.
There they gathered the whole crowd together
and delivered the letter.
[31] When they had read it,
they rejoiced over the encouragement.
[32] Judas and Silas were also prophets themselves.
They encouraged the brothers with many words
and strengthened them.
[33] They spent some time there.
Then the brothers sent them back in peace
to the apostles.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The whole church takes part in the decision. They consult together.
> Verse 28: “It seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us”: The church trusts that God guided it in its decision.
> Verse 29: “Things strangled” and “blood” mean meat from animals whose blood has not been drained out. According to the Bible, blood, the sign of life, belongs to God (Leviticus 17:11).
> Verse 31: The letter brings joy. The new Christians don’t have to become Jews in order to belong to Jesus.
> This meeting is often called the Council of Jerusalem. It probably took place around AD 48 or 49.
> Important: For Christians of Jewish background the Torah remained important. The Council of Jerusalem does not declare Judaism outdated. It opens the door for the nations.

## Paul and Barnabas separate (Verses 34–40)

[34] But Paul and Barnabas stayed in Antioch.
They taught and preached the word of the Lord,
together with many others.
[35] After some days,
Paul said to Barnabas,
“Let’s go back now
and visit our brothers
in every city
in which we proclaimed the word of the Lord,
to see how they are doing.”
[36] Barnabas wanted to take John,
who was called Mark,
with them also.
[37] But Paul didn’t think it was a good idea
to take with them someone
who had left them in Pamphylia
and had not gone with them to do the work.
[38] Then the disagreement grew so sharp
that they separated from each other.
Barnabas took Mark with him
and sailed away to Cyprus.
[39] But Paul chose Silas
and went out.
The brothers entrusted him to the grace of God.
[40] He went through Syria and Cilicia,
strengthening the assemblies.

> **What does this mean?**
> Important about the numbering: The WEB has 40 verses in this chapter. Many other Bibles, and many German Bibles, have 41. There is an additional verse 34: Silas decides to stay in Antioch. This verse is missing in the oldest manuscripts. That is why our verses 34–40 are verses 35–41 in those Bibles.
> Verse 37: Mark had left the two on the first journey (13:13).
> Verse 38: Apostles argue too. Paul and Barnabas separate. The Bible doesn’t hide this.
> Later Paul is reconciled with Mark. In a letter he writes that Mark is useful to him for the ministry (2 Timothy 4:11).
> For today: Good people can also argue. Reconciliation is possible, sometimes only after a long time.
