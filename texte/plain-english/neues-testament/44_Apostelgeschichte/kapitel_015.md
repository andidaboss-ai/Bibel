# Acts – Chapter 15
### The meeting of the apostles in Jerusalem

---

## A dispute about circumcision (Verses 1–5)

[1] Some men came down from Judea.
They taught the brothers, “You must be circumcised, as Moses taught.
If you are not, you can’t be saved.”
[2] Paul and Barnabas disagreed strongly with them.
They argued a lot with them.
So the brothers chose Paul, Barnabas, and some others of them.
They were to go up to Jerusalem to the apostles and elders and talk about this question.
[3] The assembly sent them on their way.
They traveled through Phoenicia and Samaria.
There they told how the Gentiles had turned to God.
This made all the brothers very happy.
[4] When they arrived in Jerusalem, the assembly welcomed them.
So did the apostles and the elders.
Paul and Barnabas told them everything God had done through them.
[5] But some believers stood up.
They belonged to the party of the Pharisees.
They said, “The Gentiles must be circumcised.
They must be told to keep the law of Moses.”

> **What does this mean?**
> The question was: Do people from other nations first have to become Jews, that is, be circumcised and keep the whole Torah, in order to be Christians?
> Circumcision is the sign of God’s covenant with Abraham (Genesis 17). It is still very important for Jews today.
> Verse 5: Pharisees also belonged to the church. Their concern was sincere: They wanted to stay faithful to God’s law.
> For today: There are conflicts in the church too. What matters is how they are settled: People come together, listen to one another and seek God’s will together.

---

## Peter and James speak (Verses 6–21)

[6] The apostles and the elders met together to look into this matter.
[7] They talked about it for a long time.
Then Peter stood up and said to them, “Brothers, you know that some time ago God made a choice among you.
He chose me to tell the nations the word of the Good News, so that they would believe.
[8] God knows every heart.
He showed that he accepted them.
He gave them the Holy Spirit, just as he gave it to us.
[9] He made no difference between us and them.
He made their hearts clean through faith.
[10] So why are you testing God now?
Why do you want to put a yoke on the neck of the disciples?
Neither our fathers nor we were able to carry it.
[11] No, we believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they are.”
[12] The whole crowd became quiet.
They listened to Barnabas and Paul.
The two told them about the signs and wonders God had done through them among the nations.
[13] When they had finished speaking, James answered, “Brothers, listen to me.
[14] Simeon has told us how God first came to the nations.
God wanted to choose a people from them for his name.
[15] This agrees with the words of the prophets.
It is written:
[16] ‘After this I will return.
I will build up again the tabernacle of David, which has fallen down.
I will build up again its ruins.
I will set it up again,
[17] so that the rest of the people may seek the Lord.
All the Gentiles who are called by my name will seek him.
That is what the Lord says, who does all these things.’
[18] God has known all his works from eternity.
[19] So this is my judgment:
We should not make it hard for the Gentiles who turn to God.
[20] Instead we should write to them.
They should stay away from what is made unclean by idols.
They should stay away from sexual immorality.
They should not eat what is strangled, and they should not eat blood.
[21] For a very long time, in every city there have been people who preach Moses.
His books are read aloud in the synagogues every Sabbath.”

*yoke* = a wooden bar put on the necks of animals that pull a load
*strangled* = here: meat from animals killed without letting the blood drain out

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 7–9: Peter recalls Cornelius (chapter 10). God gave non-Jews the Spirit, without circumcision.
> Verse 10: A “yoke” is a piece of wood with which animals pull a cart. Peter means: You are putting a burden on the new Christians.
> Important: For devout Jews the Torah is not a burden, but a gift and a joy (Psalm 119). Here Peter does not say that the law is bad. He says: Salvation comes by grace, for Jews and non-Jews alike (verse 11).
> Verse 13: James is the brother of Jesus and a leader of the church in Jerusalem.
> Verse 14: “Simeon” is Simon Peter, with his Hebrew name.
> Verses 16–17 quote Amos 9:11–12 in the Greek translation.
> Verse 20: These four rules recall rules for foreigners who lived among Israel (Leviticus 17–18). In this way Jewish and non-Jewish Christians could eat and live together.
> Verse 21: James respects the Torah and the synagogue.

## The letter to the churches (Verses 22–33)

[22] Then the apostles and the elders and the whole assembly decided to choose some men from among them.
They would send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas.
They chose Judas, who was called Barsabbas, and Silas.
Both were leaders among the brothers.
[23] They gave them a letter.
It said:
“From the apostles, the elders, and the brothers.
To the brothers from the Gentiles in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia: Greetings!
[24] We have heard that some people from our group came to you.
They upset you with what they said.
They troubled your souls.
They said, ‘You must be circumcised and keep the law.’
But we did not give them any such orders.
[25] So we came together and all agreed.
We decided to choose some men and send them to you.
They come with our dear friends Barnabas and Paul.
[26] Barnabas and Paul have risked their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[27] So we are sending Judas and Silas.
They will tell you the same things in person.
[28] It seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us, not to put any heavier burden on you.
You only need to do these necessary things:
[29] Stay away from food sacrificed to idols.
Do not eat blood.
Do not eat things strangled.
And stay away from sexual immorality.
If you keep away from these things, you will do well.
Farewell.”
[30] So the men were sent off and went to Antioch.
There they gathered all the people together and gave them the letter.
[31] The people read it.
They were glad because it encouraged them.
[32] Judas and Silas were prophets too.
They said many things to encourage the brothers and make them strong.
[33] They stayed there for some time.
Then the brothers sent them back in peace to the apostles.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 22: The whole church takes part in the decision. They consult together.
> Verse 28: “It seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us”: The church trusts that God guided it in its decision.
> Verse 29: “Things strangled” and “blood” mean meat from animals whose blood has not been drained out. According to the Bible, blood, the sign of life, belongs to God (Leviticus 17:11).
> Verse 31: The letter brings joy. The new Christians don’t have to become Jews in order to belong to Jesus.
> This meeting is often called the Council of Jerusalem. It probably took place around AD 48 or 49.
> Important: For Christians of Jewish background the Torah remained important. The Council of Jerusalem does not declare Judaism outdated. It opens the door for the nations.

## Paul and Barnabas separate (Verses 34–40)

[34] But Paul and Barnabas stayed in Antioch.
They taught and preached the word of the Lord.
Many others did this with them.
[35] After some days Paul said to Barnabas, “Let’s go back and visit our brothers.
Let’s go to every city where we preached the word of the Lord.
Let’s see how they are doing.”
[36] Barnabas wanted to take John along too, who was called Mark.
[37] But Paul didn’t think it was a good idea to take him along.
Mark had left them in Pamphylia.
He had not gone on with them to do the work.
[38] Paul and Barnabas had such a sharp argument that they separated.
Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed to Cyprus.
[39] But Paul chose Silas and set out.
The brothers put him into the care of God’s grace.
[40] Paul traveled through Syria and Cilicia.
He made the assemblies strong.

> **What does this mean?**
> Important about the numbering: The WEB has 40 verses in this chapter. Many other Bibles, and many German Bibles, have 41. There is an additional verse 34: Silas decides to stay in Antioch. This verse is missing in the oldest manuscripts. That is why our verses 34–40 are verses 35–41 in those Bibles.
> Verse 37: Mark had left the two on the first journey (13:13).
> Verse 38: Apostles argue too. Paul and Barnabas separate. The Bible doesn’t hide this.
> Later Paul is reconciled with Mark. In a letter he writes that Mark is useful to him for the ministry (2 Timothy 4:11).
> For today: Good people can also argue. Reconciliation is possible, sometimes only after a long time.
