# Acts – Chapter 25
### “I appeal to Caesar!”

---

## Paul before Festus (Verses 1–12)

[1] Festus came into the province.
After three days he went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.
[2] Then the high priest
and the leading men of the Jews
brought charges against Paul before him.
They begged him
[3] for a favor against Paul:
He should have him brought to Jerusalem.
For they were planning
to kill him on the way.
[4] But Festus answered
that Paul should be kept in custody at Caesarea.
And he himself was going back there soon.
[5] He said,
“Those among you
who are in power
should go down with me.
If there is anything wrong in the man,
let them accuse him.”
[6] Festus stayed among them more than ten days.
Then he went down to Caesarea.
The next day he sat on the judgment seat
and commanded
Paul to be brought in.
[7] When Paul came,
the Jews
who had come down from Jerusalem
stood around him.
They brought many serious charges against him.
But they could not prove them.
[8] Paul defended himself
and said,
“I have not sinned at all,
neither against the law of the Jews,
nor against the temple,
nor against Caesar.”
[9] But Festus wanted to do the Jews a favor.
He answered Paul,
“Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem
and be judged by me there about these things?”
[10] But Paul said,
“I am standing before Caesar’s judgment seat.
This is where I ought to be tried.
I have done no wrong to the Jews,
as you also know very well.
[11] If I have done wrong
and have done anything
that deserves death,
I don’t refuse to die.
But if none of the things is true
that they accuse me of,
no one can hand me over to them.
I appeal to Caesar!”
[12] Then Festus talked with the council
and answered,
“You have appealed to Caesar.
To Caesar you shall go.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Appeal means: ask a higher court to decide.
> Festus was the Roman governor of Judea from about AD 59/60.
> Verses 2–3: Some leading men in Jerusalem are again planning a murder. “The Jews” here means this small group of powerful men, not the people.
> Verse 8: Paul says: I have done nothing against the Jewish law or the temple. He remains a faithful Jew.
> Verse 11: Every Roman citizen could demand that the emperor in Rome decide his case. Paul uses this right. In this way he comes to Rome, as Jesus had promised (23:11).
> Verse 12: “The council” here is the governor’s group of advisers, not the Jewish council.

## Festus tells King Agrippa about Paul (Verses 13–22)

[13] After some days King Agrippa and Bernice
arrived at Caesarea
and greeted Festus.
[14] They stayed there many days.
Then Festus told the king about Paul’s case
and said,
“There is a man here
whom Felix left as a prisoner.
[15] When I was at Jerusalem,
the chief priests and the elders of the Jews
brought charges against him.
They asked for a sentence against him.
[16] I answered them:
It is not the custom of the Romans
to hand anyone over to be put to death
before the accused has met the accusers face to face
and has had the opportunity
to defend himself against the charge.
[17] So when they had come together here,
I didn’t delay.
The next day I sat on the judgment seat
and commanded
the man to be brought in.
[18] When the accusers stood up,
they brought no charges about the crimes
I had expected.
[19] Instead they had some arguments with him
about their own religion
and about a certain Jesus,
who was dead.
But Paul claimed
that he is alive.
[20] I didn’t know
how to look into these questions.
So I asked him
whether he was willing to go to Jerusalem
and be judged there about these matters.
[21] But Paul asked
that his case be kept for the decision of the emperor.
So I commanded
him to be kept in custody
until I could send him to Caesar.”
[22] Agrippa said to Festus,
“I also would like to hear the man myself.”
Festus said,
“Tomorrow you shall hear him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 13: King Agrippa II was a son of Herod Agrippa I (chapter 12). Bernice was his sister. Agrippa knew the Jewish customs well.
> Verse 16: An important principle of law: Every accused person has the right to defend themselves. That still holds today.
> Verse 19: For the Roman Festus all this is hard to understand: a Jesus who was dead, and Paul says he is alive. That is exactly the point: the resurrection.

## Paul before Agrippa (Verses 23–27)

[23] The next day Agrippa and Bernice came
with great pomp.
They went into the hall of hearing,
together with the commanding officers
and the leading men of the city.
At the command of Festus, Paul was brought in.
[24] Festus said,
“King Agrippa,
and all of you who are here with us!
You see this man.
All the multitude of the Jews have appealed to me about him,
both at Jerusalem and here.
They cried
that he ought not to live any longer.
[25] But I found
that he had done nothing
that deserves death.
And because he himself appealed to the emperor,
I decided
to send him.
[26] But I have nothing certain about him
to write to my lord.
So I have brought him out before you,
and especially before you, King Agrippa.
After this hearing
I may have something
to write.
[27] For it seems unreasonable to me
to send a prisoner
without stating the charges against him.”

> **What does this mean?**
> Pomp means: a grand show of wealth and power.
> Verse 24: “All the multitude of the Jews”: Festus is exaggerating. He means the accusers and their followers.
> Verse 25: Again a Roman official says: Paul is innocent.
> Verse 26: “My lord” is the emperor in Rome, at that time Nero.
