# Acts – Chapter 14
### “We also are men”

---

## In Iconium (Verses 1–7)

[1] In Iconium, they went together
into the synagogue of the Jews.
They spoke in such a way
that a great crowd of both Jews and Greeks
believed.
[2] But the Jews
who did not believe
stirred up the Gentiles against the brothers
and made them angry.
[3] So they stayed there a long time.
They spoke boldly, trusting in the Lord.
He confirmed the word of his grace
by letting signs and wonders be done by their hands.
[4] But the people of the city were divided.
Some sided with the Jews,
and some with the apostles.
[5] Some of both the Gentiles and the Jews,
with their rulers,
made a violent attempt
to mistreat and stone them.
[6] The apostles became aware of it
and fled to the cities of Lycaonia,
Lystra and Derbe,
and the surrounding region.
[7] There they preached the Good News.

> **What does this mean?**
> Iconium is today’s Konya in Turkey.
> Verse 1: Many Jews and Greeks come to faith.
> Verse 2: The text speaks of the Jews “who did not believe.” It is about some of the Jews in Iconium, not all of them.
> Verse 3: “So”: Precisely because there was opposition, they stayed and encouraged the new believers.
> Verse 4: Paul and Barnabas are called “apostles” here. The word means “messengers.” Here it is used more widely than only for the Twelve.
> Verse 5: Gentiles and Jews stand together against the apostles. It is not a conflict of Jews against Christians.

---

## In Lystra (Verses 8–20)

[8] At Lystra a man was sitting
who couldn’t use his feet.
He had been lame from birth
and had never walked.
[9] He was listening to Paul speaking.
Paul looked straight at him.
He saw
that the man had faith
to be made well.
[10] So Paul said with a loud voice,
“Stand upright on your feet!”
The man leaped up
and walked.
[11] When the crowd saw
what Paul had done,
they shouted in the language of Lycaonia,
“The gods have come down to us
in the likeness of men!”
[12] They called Barnabas “Jupiter,”
and Paul “Mercury,”
because he was the chief speaker.
[13] The priest of Jupiter,
whose temple was in front of their city,
brought oxen and garlands to the gates.
Together with the crowds,
he wanted to make a sacrifice.
[14] But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it,
they tore their clothes.
They rushed into the crowd,
crying out,
[15] “Men, why are you doing these things?
We also are men of the same nature as you.
We bring you good news:
You should turn from these useless things
to the living God.
He made the sky, the earth, the sea,
and all that is in them.
[16] In the generations gone by,
he allowed all the nations
to walk in their own ways.
[17] Yet he didn’t leave himself without witness.
He did good.
He gave you rains from the sky
and fruitful seasons.
He filled our hearts with food and gladness.”
[18] Even though they said these things,
they could hardly stop the crowds
from making a sacrifice to them.
[19] But then some Jews came there
from Antioch and Iconium.
They persuaded the crowds.
They stoned Paul
and dragged him out of the city.
They thought
that he was dead.
[20] But as the disciples stood around him,
he got up
and went into the city.
On the next day he went with Barnabas to Derbe.

> **What does this mean?**
> Garlands means: wreaths of flowers.
> Verses 8–10: The healing reminds us of the healing of the lame man by Peter (3:1–10).
> Verse 12: The WEB uses the Roman names Jupiter and Mercury. In Greek they are called Zeus and Hermes. Hermes was considered the messenger of the gods; that is why they took Paul, the speaker, for him.
> Verse 14: Tearing one’s clothes was a sign of horror.
> Verses 15–17: Here Paul speaks to people who don’t know the Bible. He doesn’t speak about Moses, but about creation: Rain, harvest, food and joy show that there is a good God.
> Verse 15: “We also are men”: No preacher and no leader should let himself be worshiped like a god.
> Verse 19: Paul is stoned. Later he tells about it himself (2 Corinthians 11:25). “Some Jews” means a certain group, not the Jewish people.
> Important: How quickly a crowd can turn from admiration to violence! Violence against people is always wrong.

## Back to Antioch (Verses 21–28)

[21] They preached the Good News to that city too
and made many disciples.
Then they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch.
[22] They strengthened the disciples
and encouraged them
to continue in the faith.
They said
that through many hardships
we must enter into God’s Kingdom.
[23] They appointed elders for them in every church.
They prayed with fasting
and entrusted them to the Lord
in whom they had believed.
[24] They passed through Pisidia
and came to Pamphylia.
[25] When they had spoken the word in Perga,
they went down to Attalia.
[26] From there they sailed to Antioch.
There they had been entrusted to the grace of God
for the work
which they had now completed.
[27] When they had arrived,
they gathered the church together.
They reported all the things
that God had done with them.
And they told
that he had opened a door of faith to the nations.
[28] They stayed there with the disciples for a long time.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 21: Paul and Barnabas courageously return to the cities where they had been persecuted. They don’t leave the young churches alone.
> Verse 22: Being a Christian doesn’t protect you from difficulties. But God is there in hard times too.
> Verse 23: “Elders” were the leaders of the churches. Later the church offices developed from them.
> Verse 26: This ends Paul’s first great journey. It probably lasted one to two years.
> Verse 27: Paul and Barnabas tell that God “had opened a door of faith to the nations.” That is the heart of this journey.
> For today: A church rejoices together about what God does.
