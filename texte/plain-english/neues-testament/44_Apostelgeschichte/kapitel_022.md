# Acts – Chapter 22
### Paul tells his story

---

## “I am a Jew” (Verses 1–5)

[1] “Brothers and fathers,
listen to the defense
which I now make to you.”
[2] When they heard
that he spoke to them in the Hebrew language,
they became even more quiet.
He said,
[3] “I am a Jew.
I was born in Tarsus of Cilicia,
but brought up in this city.
At the feet of Gamaliel
I was taught exactly in the law of our ancestors.
I was zealous for God,
just as you all are today.
[4] I persecuted this Way to the death.
I bound both men and women
and put them in prison.
[5] The high priest
and all the council of the elders can testify to this.
From them I even received letters to the brothers in Damascus.
I traveled there
to bring those who were there to Jerusalem in chains,
to be punished.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: Paul speaks in the people’s mother tongue. That is why they listen to him. “The Hebrew language” probably means Aramaic.
> Verse 3: Paul stresses: I am a Jew, I learned from the famous teacher Gamaliel (5:34). I understand your zeal for God, because I had it too. Zealous means: full of eager devotion.
> “At the feet of” means: as a student.
> Verses 4–5: Paul speaks honestly about his guilt.

---

## The encounter near Damascus (Verses 6–16)

[6] “As I was on my way
and came close to Damascus,
about noon
a great light from the sky suddenly shone around me.
[7] I fell to the ground
and heard a voice
saying to me,
‘Saul, Saul,
why are you persecuting me?’
[8] I answered,
‘Who are you, Lord?’
He said to me,
‘I am Jesus of Nazareth,
whom you persecute.’
[9] Those
who were with me
saw the light
and were afraid.
But they didn’t understand the voice
of him who spoke to me.
[10] I said,
‘What shall I do, Lord?’
The Lord said to me,
‘Get up
and go into Damascus.
There you will be told everything
that you are meant to do.’
[11] I couldn’t see,
because that light had been so bright.
Those who were with me led me by the hand,
and so I came into Damascus.
[12] There was a man named Ananias.
He was devout and lived by the law.
All the Jews
who lived in Damascus
spoke well of him.
[13] He came to me,
stood by me
and said,
‘Brother Saul,
receive your sight!’
In that very hour I looked up at him.
[14] He said,
‘The God of our ancestors has chosen you
to know his will,
to see the Righteous One,
and to hear a voice from his mouth.
[15] For you will be a witness for him
to all people
of what you have seen and heard.
[16] Now why do you wait?
Get up,
be baptized,
and wash away your sins,
calling on the name of the Lord.’

> **What does this mean?**
> Here Paul tells the story from chapter 9 again in his own words. Some details are told a little differently. This is how people tell things when they report the same experience several times.
> Verse 9: In 9:7 it says that his companions heard the sound of a voice. Here it says: They didn’t understand the voice. So they heard something, but didn’t understand the words.
> Verse 12: Paul stresses that Ananias was a devout Jew who lived by the law.
> Verse 14: “The God of our ancestors”: It is the God of Israel who calls Paul.
> Verse 16: Baptism is a sign of the forgiveness of sins.

---

## God sends Paul to the nations (Verses 17–21)

[17] “After that I returned to Jerusalem.
While I was praying in the temple,
I fell into a trance.
[18] I saw him,
and he said to me,
‘Hurry
and get out of Jerusalem quickly!
For they will not accept your testimony about me.’
[19] I said,
‘Lord,
they themselves know
that in every synagogue I imprisoned and beat
those
who believed in you.
[20] And when the blood of Stephen,
your witness,
was shed,
I also was standing by.
I agreed with his death
and guarded the cloaks of those
who killed him.’
[21] He said to me,
‘Go!
For I will send you far away to the Gentiles.’”

> **What does this mean?**
> Trance means: a state like a dream, in which someone sees or hears things from God.
> Verse 17: Paul prays in the temple, like a devout Jew. There Jesus calls him.
> Verse 18: The WEB only says “him.” This means Jesus, the Lord.
> Verse 20: Paul remembers the death of Stephen (7:58). He doesn’t hide his guilt.
> Verse 21: God sends Paul to the nations. That is the task of his life.

---

## Paul is a Roman citizen (Verses 22–30)

[22] They listened to him until he said that.
But then they raised their voices
and shouted,
“Rid the earth of this fellow,
for he isn’t fit to live!”
[23] They shouted,
threw off their cloaks
and threw dust into the air.
[24] Then the commanding officer ordered
Paul to be brought into the barracks.
He ordered
him to be questioned under whipping.
In this way he wanted to find out
why they were shouting against him like that.
[25] When they had tied him up with straps,
Paul said to the centurion
who stood there,
“Is it lawful for you to whip a man
who is a Roman citizen
and has not been found guilty?”
[26] When the centurion heard it,
he went to the commanding officer,
told him
and said,
“Watch what you are about to do!
This man is a Roman!”
[27] The commanding officer came
and asked him,
“Tell me,
are you a Roman?”
Paul said,
“Yes.”
[28] The commanding officer answered,
“I bought my citizenship for a great price.”
Paul said,
“But I was born a Roman.”
[29] Immediately those who were about to question him
left him alone.
The commanding officer also was afraid
when he realized
that Paul was a Roman,
because he had bound him.
[30] The next day he wanted to know the truth
about why Paul was accused by the Jews.
He freed him from the chains
and ordered
the chief priests and all the council to come together.
Then he brought Paul down
and set him before them.

> **What does this mean?**
> Citizenship means: the rights of a citizen.
> Verse 22: When Paul says that God is sending him to the Gentiles, the crowd becomes angry. They were probably afraid that Paul was removing the boundaries of God’s people.
> Verse 24: Questioning under whipping was a form of torture. Torture is always a crime against human dignity.
> Verse 25: Paul claims his rights. Roman citizens could not be whipped without a verdict.
> Verse 28: The commanding officer had bought his citizenship. Paul was born with it, perhaps because his father was already a citizen.
> Verse 30: “Accused by the Jews”: This means the opponents in Jerusalem who accused Paul. Paul himself is a Jew.
> For today: Human rights protect all people from torture and arbitrary power.
