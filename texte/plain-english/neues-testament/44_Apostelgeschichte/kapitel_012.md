# Acts – Chapter 12
### Peter is set free from prison

---

## James is killed, Peter is arrested (Verses 1–5)

[1] About that time,
King Herod used violence against some of the assembly
to do them harm.
[2] He killed James,
the brother of John,
with the sword.
[3] When he saw
that it pleased the Jews,
he went on to arrest Peter also.
This was during the days of unleavened bread.
[4] When he had arrested him,
he put him in prison.
He handed him over to four squads
of four soldiers each
to guard him.
He intended to bring him out to the people
after the Passover.
[5] So Peter was kept in the prison.
But the assembly prayed constantly
to God for him.

*unleavened bread* = bread made without yeast, eaten at Passover

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 1: This Herod is Herod Agrippa I, a grandson of Herod the Great. He was king over Judea from AD 41 to 44. He worked closely with the Romans.
> Verse 2: James is one of the Twelve, a son of Zebedee. He is the first apostle to be killed for his faith.
> Verse 3: “The Jews” here means people in Jerusalem who were against the Christians, probably mainly influential circles. Not all Jews. After all, the Christians in Jerusalem were Jews themselves.
> Verses 3–4: The days of unleavened bread belong to the feast of Passover. This reminds us of the arrest of Jesus, which also happened at Passover.
> Verse 5: The church can’t free Peter. But it prays.
> Important: Killing people because of their faith is a crime.

---

## An angel sets Peter free (Verses 6–19)

[6] On the night
before Herod was going to bring him out,
Peter was sleeping between two soldiers.
He was bound with two chains.
Guards in front of the door were keeping the prison.
[7] Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood by him,
and a light shone in the cell.
He struck Peter on the side,
woke him up,
and said,
“Stand up quickly!”
His chains fell off his hands.
[8] The angel said to him,
“Get dressed
and put on your sandals.”
He did so.
The angel said to him,
“Put on your cloak
and follow me.”
[9] Peter went out
and followed him.
He didn’t know
that what the angel was doing
was real.
He thought
he was seeing a vision.
[10] They went past the first and the second guard.
Then they came to the iron gate
that leads into the city.
It opened for them by itself.
They went out
and went down one street.
And immediately the angel left him.
[11] When Peter came to himself,
he said,
“Now I truly know
that the Lord has sent his angel.
He has rescued me out of the hand of Herod,
and from everything
the Jewish people were expecting.”
[12] When he had thought about this,
he came to the house of Mary,
the mother of John
who was called Mark.
Many were gathered there
and were praying.
[13] Peter knocked at the door of the gate.
A servant girl named Rhoda
came to answer.
[14] She recognized Peter’s voice.
For joy she didn’t open the gate,
but ran in
and reported
that Peter was standing in front of the gate.
[15] They said to her,
“You are crazy!”
But she insisted
that it was so.
Then they said,
“It is his angel.”
[16] But Peter kept on knocking.
When they opened,
they saw him
and were amazed.
[17] He waved his hand to them
to be quiet.
He told them
how the Lord had brought him out of the prison.
He said,
“Tell these things to James and to the brothers.”
Then he left
and went to another place.
[18] As soon as it was day,
there was no small commotion among the soldiers
about what had become of Peter.
[19] Herod searched for him
but didn’t find him.
He questioned the guards
and commanded
that they be put to death.
Then he went down from Judea to Caesarea
and stayed there.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 6: Peter sleeps peacefully, even though he is to be executed the next day. He trusts God.
> Verse 11: “Everything the Jewish people were expecting”: This means the people who supported Herod and were waiting for the execution. It is not a judgment on all Jews.
> Verse 12: Mary is the mother of John Mark. Her house is a meeting place of the church. Women played an important role in the first church. Many think that John Mark is the author of the Gospel of Mark.
> Verses 13–16: An almost funny scene: The church prays for Peter, but when he is standing at the door, no one believes the servant girl Rhoda. She was right.
> Verse 15: “It is his angel”: At that time some people believed that every person has a guardian angel (compare Matthew 18:10).
> Verse 17: James here is a different James: the brother of Jesus. He becomes a leader of the church in Jerusalem.
> Verse 19: The guards are executed. That was cruel Roman law: Guards answered with their lives for their prisoners.

## The death of Herod (Verses 20–25)

[20] Herod was very angry with the people of Tyre and Sidon.
They came to him together.
They made Blastus,
the king’s personal aide,
their friend.
Then they asked for peace,
because their country depended on the king’s country
for food.
[21] On an appointed day,
Herod dressed himself in royal clothing,
sat on the throne,
and gave a speech to them.
[22] The people shouted,
“The voice of a god,
and not of a man!”
[23] Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him,
because he didn’t give God the glory.
He was eaten by worms
and died.
[24] But the word of God grew
and spread.
[25] Barnabas and Saul returned to Jerusalem
when they had finished their service.
They also took with them John
who was called Mark.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 20: Tyre and Sidon were trading cities by the sea, in today’s Lebanon. They needed grain from Herod’s land.
> Verses 22–23: Herod lets himself be celebrated like a god. He doesn’t object. This is very different from Peter, who said: “I myself am also a man” (10:26).
> Verse 23: The Jewish historian Josephus also reports that in AD 44, after a celebration, Herod Agrippa suddenly became very ill and died.
> Verse 24: The persecutor dies, but the word of God keeps on growing.
> Verse 25: Many old manuscripts have from Jerusalem instead of “to Jerusalem.” The WEB has “to Jerusalem.” Barnabas and Saul had brought the help to Jerusalem (11:30).
> For today: No human being is God. Whoever has power should stay humble.
