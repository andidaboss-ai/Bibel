# Acts – Chapter 21
### Paul is arrested in Jerusalem

---

> **An important note first**
> In this chapter and the next ones the text often says “the Jews.” This means certain people who oppose Paul, for example “the Jews from Asia” (verse 27). At the same time the text tells of “many thousands” of Jews who believe in Jesus and faithfully keep the law (verse 20). Paul himself says: “I am a Jew” (verse 39).
> These texts must never be used against the Jewish people (*Nostra Aetate*, 1965).

---

## Journey to Caesarea (Verses 1–14)

[1] We parted from them
and set sail.
We came on a straight course to Cos,
the next day to Rhodes,
and from there to Patara.
[2] There we found a ship
crossing over to Phoenicia.
We went on board
and set sail.
[3] We came in sight of Cyprus
and left it on our left.
We sailed to Syria
and landed at Tyre.
For the ship was to unload its cargo there.
[4] We found disciples
and stayed there seven days.
Through the Spirit they told Paul
that he should not go up to Jerusalem.
[5] When those days were over,
we left and went on our journey.
They all, with wives and children,
went with us
until we were out of the city.
We knelt down on the beach
and prayed.
[6] We said goodbye to each other.
Then we went on board the ship,
and they returned home.
[7] When we had finished the voyage from Tyre,
we arrived at Ptolemais.
We greeted the brothers
and stayed with them one day.
[8] The next day we,
Paul’s companions,
left
and came to Caesarea.
We went into the house of Philip
the evangelist.
He was one of the seven.
We stayed with him.
[9] He had four daughters
who were virgins
and prophesied.
[10] We stayed there some days.
Then a prophet named Agabus came down from Judea.
[11] He came to us,
took Paul’s belt,
tied his own feet and hands with it
and said,
“The Holy Spirit says:
‘So the Jews at Jerusalem will bind the man
who owns this belt,
and will hand him over to the Gentiles.’ ”
[12] When we heard these things,
both we and the people of that place
begged Paul
not to go up to Jerusalem.
[13] Then Paul answered,
“What are you doing?
Why are you weeping
and breaking my heart?
For I am ready
not only to be bound,
but also to die at Jerusalem
for the name of the Lord Jesus.”
[14] He would not be persuaded.
So we stopped and said,
“The Lord’s will be done.”

*evangelist* = someone who spreads the Good News

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: Through the Spirit the disciples sense that danger is waiting for Paul. They want to protect him. Paul goes anyway, because he knows that God has called him (20:22).
> Verse 5: Whole families, with wives and children, pray on the beach. A beautiful picture of fellowship.
> Verse 8: Philip is one of the seven helpers from chapter 6. He had baptized the man from Ethiopia (chapter 8).
> Verse 9: Philip’s four daughters were prophets. In the first church, women spoke in God’s name.
> Verse 11: Agabus does a symbolic action, like the prophets of Israel.
> Verses 13–14: Paul is ready to suffer for Jesus. His friends let him go: “The Lord’s will be done.”

## Paul with James in Jerusalem (Verses 15–26)

[15] After these days we packed our baggage
and went up to Jerusalem.
[16] Some of the disciples from Caesarea went with us.
They brought a man with them:
Mnason of Cyprus,
an early disciple.
We were to stay with him.
[17] When we came to Jerusalem,
the brothers welcomed us gladly.
[18] The next day Paul went with us to James.
All the elders were there.
[19] Paul greeted them
and told them one by one
the things God had done among the Gentiles
through his ministry.
[20] When they heard it,
they praised God.
They said to him,
“You see, brother,
how many thousands there are among the Jews
who have believed.
And they are all zealous for the law.
[21] They have been told about you
that you teach all the Jews
who live among the Gentiles
to turn away from Moses.
They say you tell them
not to circumcise their children
and not to live by the customs.
[22] What then?
The assembly will certainly meet,
for they will hear
that you have come.
[23] So do what we tell you.
We have four men
who have taken a vow.
[24] Take them
and purify yourself with them.
Pay their expenses,
so that they can shave their heads.
Then all will know
that there is no truth in the things
they have been told about you.
They will see
that you yourself also live by keeping the law.
[25] But about the Gentiles who believe,
we have written our decision:
They don’t need to keep any of this.
They should only keep themselves
from food offered to idols,
from blood,
from strangled things,
and from sexual immorality.”
[26] Then Paul took the men.
The next day he purified himself with them
and went into the temple.
There he announced
when the days of purification would be completed
and the offering would be made for each one of them.

*purify* = make clean according to the rules of the law

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 18: James is the brother of Jesus and the leader of the church in Jerusalem.
> Verse 20: “Many thousands” of Jews believe in Jesus and faithfully keep the law. For them both belong together.
> Verse 21: This is a false rumor. Paul didn’t tell Jews to leave Moses. He even circumcised Timothy (16:3).
> Verses 23–26: Paul goes into the temple and keeps the Jewish rules of purification (compare Numbers 6). He shows: I am a Jew who respects the law.
> Verse 25 recalls the decision from chapter 15.

## Paul is seized in the temple (Verses 27–40)

[27] The seven days were almost completed.
Then the Jews from Asia
saw Paul in the temple.
They stirred up the whole crowd
and seized him.
[28] They shouted,
“Men of Israel, help!
This is the man
who teaches everyone everywhere
against the people,
against the law,
and against this place!
And he has even brought Greeks into the temple
and has made this holy place unclean!”
[29] For they had seen Trophimus from Ephesus
with him in the city.
They thought
that Paul had brought him into the temple.
[30] The whole city was stirred up.
The people ran together.
They seized Paul
and dragged him out of the temple.
Immediately the doors were shut.
[31] They were trying to kill him.
Then news came to the commanding officer of the Roman troops
that all Jerusalem was in an uproar.
[32] Immediately he took soldiers and centurions
and ran down to them.
When they saw the commanding officer and the soldiers,
they stopped beating Paul.
[33] Then the commanding officer came near
and arrested Paul.
He ordered him to be bound with two chains
and asked
who he was
and what he had done.
[34] In the crowd some shouted one thing,
and some another.
Because of the noise he couldn’t find out the truth.
So he ordered
Paul to be brought into the barracks.
[35] When Paul came to the stairs,
the soldiers had to carry him,
because the crowd was so violent.
[36] For the crowd of people followed
and shouted,
“Away with him!”
[37] As Paul was about to be brought into the barracks,
he said to the commanding officer,
“May I speak to you?”
He answered,
“Do you know Greek?
[38] Aren’t you then the Egyptian
who some time ago started a revolt
and led the four thousand men of the Assassins
out into the wilderness?”
[39] But Paul said,
“I am a Jew from Tarsus in Cilicia,
a citizen of no unimportant city.
I beg you,
allow me
to speak to the people.”
[40] The commanding officer allowed it.
Paul stood on the stairs
and waved with his hand to the people.
There was a great silence.
Then he spoke to them in the Hebrew language,
saying:

*barracks* = a building where soldiers live

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 27: “The Jews from Asia”: These are people who knew Paul from Ephesus. Not all Jews in Jerusalem.
> Verses 28–29: Non-Jews were not allowed into the inner area of the temple. Warning signs at the temple threatened death. But the charge against Paul was false. It was a misunderstanding (verse 29).
> Verses 31–33: The Roman commanding officer saves Paul from the angry crowd. His name is Claudius Lysias (23:26).
> Verse 36: “Away with him!” The crowd had shouted something similar about Jesus too (Luke 23:18).
> Verse 38: The historian Josephus tells of an “Egyptian” who led a revolt. The “Assassins” (in Greek *sicarii*) were men who killed opponents with short daggers.
> Verse 40: “In the Hebrew language” probably means Aramaic, the language of the people in Jerusalem at that time. With the colon the speech continues in the next chapter.
> Important: Mob violence is always wrong, no matter against whom.
