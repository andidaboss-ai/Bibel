# Acts – Chapter 11
### For the first time they are called “Christians”

---

## Peter explains himself in Jerusalem (Verses 1–18)

[1] The apostles and the brothers in Judea heard
that the Gentiles had also received the word of God.
[2] When Peter came up to Jerusalem,
those who were of the circumcision argued with him.
[3] They said,
“You went in to uncircumcised men
and ate with them!”
[4] But Peter began
and explained everything to them in order.
He said,
[5] “I was in the city of Joppa
praying.
In a trance I saw a vision:
Something came down
like a great sheet,
let down from heaven by four corners.
It came right down to me.
[6] I looked at it closely
and studied it.
I saw the four-footed animals of the earth,
wild animals,
creeping things,
and birds of the sky.
[7] I also heard a voice
saying to me,
‘Get up, Peter,
kill and eat!’
[8] But I said,
‘Not so, Lord,
for nothing unholy or unclean
has ever entered into my mouth.’
[9] But a voice answered me the second time out of heaven,
‘What God has cleansed,
don’t you call unclean.’
[10] This happened three times.
Then everything was drawn up again into heaven.
[11] Immediately three men stood in front of the house
where I was.
They had been sent to me from Caesarea.
[12] The Spirit told me
to go with them
without discriminating.
These six brothers also came with me,
and we went into the man’s house.
[13] He told us
how he had seen the angel standing in his house.
The angel said to him,
‘Send to Joppa
and get Simon,
who is called Peter.
[14] He will speak words to you
by which you will be saved,
you and all your household.’
[15] As I began to speak,
the Holy Spirit fell on them,
just as on us at the beginning.
[16] I remembered the word of the Lord,
how he said,
‘John baptized in water,
but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit.’
[17] So if God gave them the same gift as us,
when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ,
who was I
that I could stand in God’s way?”
[18] When they heard these things,
they became quiet.
They praised God
and said,
“Then God has also granted to the Gentiles
repentance to life!”

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: “Those who were of the circumcision” are Jewish Christians for whom the Torah was very important.
> Verse 3: The accusation is: Peter ate with non-Jews. For devout Jews this was a big problem because of the purity rules.
> Verse 4: Peter doesn’t get angry. He calmly explains what happened. That is a good way to settle a conflict.
> Verse 12: “Without discriminating” means: without making a difference between people.
> Verse 16 recalls 1:5.
> Verse 18: The church in Jerusalem listens, changes its mind and praises God. That is a beautiful example: People can learn something new together.
> “Gentiles” means people from the non-Jewish nations. The word is not an insult here.

## The church in Antioch (Verses 19–26)

[19] Those who were scattered
by the persecution that arose after Stephen’s death
traveled as far as Phoenicia,
Cyprus, and Antioch.
But they spoke the word to no one except Jews.
[20] But some of them were men from Cyprus and Cyrene.
When they came to Antioch,
they also spoke to the Hellenists
and preached the good news about the Lord Jesus.
[21] The hand of the Lord was with them.
A great number believed
and turned to the Lord.
[22] The news about them came to the ears of the church in Jerusalem.
So they sent Barnabas to Antioch.
[23] When he arrived
and saw the grace of God,
he was glad.
He encouraged them all
to remain close to the Lord with a firm heart.
[24] For he was a good man,
full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.
And many people were added to the Lord.
[25] Barnabas went to Tarsus
to look for Saul.
[26] When he had found him,
he brought him to Antioch.
For a whole year they met with the church
and taught many people.
The disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 19: Phoenicia is today’s Lebanon. Antioch was a large city in Syria, today Antakya in Turkey. At that time it was one of the largest cities in the Roman Empire.
> Verse 20: The Hellenists here are Greek-speaking people. Non-Jews are probably meant here too. Some Bibles have Greeks, following other manuscripts.
> Verses 23–24: Barnabas sees God’s grace and is glad. He encourages people. He is “a good man.”
> Verses 25–26: Barnabas brings Saul to Antioch. Antioch becomes the starting point of Paul’s great journeys.
> Verse 26: “Christians” means: people who belong to Christ. Perhaps at first it was a nickname given by others.
> For today: A church in which people of different backgrounds live together is a gift.

## Help for the Christians in Judea (Verses 27–30)

[27] In those days,
prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.
[28] One of them was named Agabus.
He stood up
and showed through the Spirit
that there would be a great famine all over the world.
It came in the days of Claudius.
[29] So the disciples decided
that each of them,
as much as he was able,
should send relief to the brothers who lived in Judea.
[30] They did this.
They sent the help to the elders
by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

> **What does this mean?**
> Relief means: help for people in need.
> Verse 28: Claudius was Roman emperor from AD 41 to 54. In his time there were several severe famines in different regions. “All over the world” here means the Roman Empire.
> Verse 29: The young church in Antioch helps the church in Jerusalem. Each gives as much as he can.
> Verse 30: “The elders” were the leaders of the church in Jerusalem.
> For today: Christians help one another across borders. Church relief agencies all over the world still work this way today.
