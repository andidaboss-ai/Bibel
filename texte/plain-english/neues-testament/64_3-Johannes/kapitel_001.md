# 3 John – Chapter 1
### Hospitality for the messengers

---

## Before we begin: The third letter of John

This is the shortest book of the Bible. In the Greek text it has the fewest words.
Like the second letter it comes from “the elder.”
It is written to a man named Gaius. Gaius was a Christian who welcomed guests well.
The letter shows in a very human way what things were like in the first churches: Traveling messengers went from place to place and needed a place to stay. A church leader named Diotrephes didn’t want to take them in. There was a struggle for power.
The third letter of John has only 1 chapter.

---

## Greeting and joy (Verses 1–4)

[1] The elder writes
to my dear friend Gaius,
whom I love in truth.
[2] Dear friend,
I pray
that you may prosper in all things
and be healthy,
just as your soul prospers.
[3] For I rejoiced greatly
when brothers came
and told about your truth,
how you live in truth.
[4] I have no greater joy than this:
to hear
that my children live in the truth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 2: A completely normal, friendly wish, as people wrote it in letters at that time: I hope you are well and healthy.

> **Important note**
> Some preachers use verse 2 to say: Whoever believes rightly will always be rich and healthy. That is not what it says here. It is a greeting, not a promise. Poverty and sickness are not a punishment for weak faith. Prayer does not replace a doctor.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verse 4: “My children”: This is what the elder calls the people who came to faith through him.

---

## Praise for Gaius (Verses 5–8)

[5] Dear friend,
you act faithfully
in everything you do for the brothers,
even though they are strangers.
[6] They have told the church
about your love.
You do well
to send them on their journey
in a way that pleases God.
[7] For they went out for the sake of the Name
and took nothing from the Gentiles.
[8] That is why we should receive such people,
so that we may become fellow workers for the truth.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 5–6: Gaius takes traveling Christians into his home, even when he doesn’t know them at all. He gives them food and everything they need for the rest of their journey.
> Verse 7: “For the sake of the Name” means: for Jesus. The messengers didn’t want to take money from people who didn’t believe in Jesus. That way no one would think that they were making money from their faith.
> Verse 8: Whoever supports messengers works with them, even if they themselves stay at home.

---

## Trouble with Diotrephes (Verses 9–12)

[9] I wrote to the church.
But Diotrephes,
who loves to be first among them,
doesn’t accept what we say.
[10] So,
if I come,
I will call attention to his deeds.
He talks unjustly about us
with wicked words.
And not content with that:
He himself doesn’t receive the brothers.
And some people want to receive them.
But he forbids them to do it
and throws them out of the church.
[11] Dear friend,
don’t imitate what is evil,
but what is good.
Whoever does good
is of God.
Whoever does evil
hasn’t seen God.
[12] Everyone speaks well of Demetrius,
and the truth itself testifies to it.
We also testify to it,
and you know
that our testimony is true.

> **What does this mean?**
> Verses 9–10: Diotrephes wants power in the church. He speaks badly of others and throws people out. This shows: Even in the first churches power was misused.
> Whoever misuses power in a church acts against Jesus. Jesus said: Whoever wants to be first is to serve everyone (compare Mark 10:43–44).
> Whoever is put under pressure or shut out in a church may get help, for example from people they trust or from counseling services.
> Verse 12: Demetrius perhaps brought this letter to Gaius. The elder recommends him.

---

## Closing (Verses 13–14)

[13] I would have much more to write to you.
But I don’t want to write to you with ink and pen.
[14] But I hope
to see you soon.
Then we will speak face to face.
Peace be to you.
The friends greet you.
Greet the friends,
each one by name.

> **What does this mean?**
> In some Bibles the last sentence is counted as a separate verse 15.
> “The friends”: This is what the Christians called themselves here. Jesus called his disciples friends (John 15:15).
> “Each one by name”: Every person is important and is greeted personally.

---

## Looking back: What have we read in the third letter of John?

> **What does this mean?**
> **Living in the truth:** That is the elder’s greatest joy (verses 3–4).
> **Hospitality:** Gaius takes in messengers who are strangers and supports them (verses 5–8).
> **Misuse of power:** Diotrephes wants to be first and shuts others out. That is wrong (verses 9–10).
> **Imitating what is good:** Whoever does good is of God (verse 11).
> **For today:** Be hospitable and serve one another instead of ruling over others.
> **What comes next?** Next comes the letter of Jude. It is also very short and warns against false teachers.
