## The 30-Day Practice

Reading the *Tao Te Ching* is one thing. Living a little of it is another. This practice is a bridge between the two.

For thirty days, you will take one line from the text and one small task into your ordinary life. The tasks are modest on purpose. None of them asks you to change everything. Most take a few minutes, and some take no extra time at all, only a different kind of attention to what you are already doing.

The month has four movements. **Week one** is about stillness and emptiness: making a little room inside. **Week two** turns to softness, water, and yielding. **Week three** looks at simplicity, desire, and the quiet wealth of having enough. **Week four** brings the teaching into work and relationships: leading, serving, and acting without forcing. The last two days are for looking back and choosing what you want to keep.

Each morning, read the day's line slowly, twice, once aloud if you can. If you like, read the whole chapter it comes from. Then carry the task with you. In the evening, take a moment to write a few words on the page: what you noticed, what was hard, what surprised you.

If you miss a day, simply pick up where you left off. Forcing yourself through a practice about not forcing would miss the point. Go gently. That is the whole idea.

### Day 1 – Muddy Water

> Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear.

Source: Chapter 15

Sit for five minutes today and do nothing at all. When thoughts stir, do not fight them; simply stop stirring them further. Notice whether anything settles by itself.

### Day 2 – The Empty Vessel

> The Tao is (like) the emptiness of a vessel; and in our employment of it we must be on our guard against all fulness.

Source: Chapter 4

Look at your calendar for today and find one gap you could leave open. Protect it. When it comes, do not fill it with your phone; let it stay empty and see what it is like.

### Day 3 – The Use of Space

> Clay is fashioned into vessels; but it is on their empty hollowness, that their use depends.

Source: Chapter 11

Clear one surface in your home: a table, a shelf, a corner of your desk. Leave it bare for the rest of the day. Each time you pass it, notice how the empty space feels.

### Day 4 – Returning to the Root

> When things (in the vegetable world) have displayed their luxuriant growth, we see each of them return to its root.

Source: Chapter 16

Spend a few minutes outdoors with a tree or a plant. Think of what it draws back into its roots in winter. Then ask yourself what you could draw back from today, to rest and gather strength.

### Day 5 – Fewer Words

> Much speech to swift exhaustion lead we see

Source: Chapter 5

Speak a little less today. In one conversation, let a pause go by before you answer, and leave out the sentence you do not need. Notice how much energy your words usually take.

### Day 6 – Not Too Full

> It is better to leave a vessel unfilled, than to attempt to carry it when it is full.

Source: Chapter 9

Stop one thing a little before it is "full": eat until you are almost satisfied, end a work session while you still have energy, leave a task at good enough. See how it feels to carry less.

### Day 7 – Your Center

> Gravity is the root of lightness; stillness, the ruler of movement.

Source: Chapter 26

Before each busy part of your day, take three slow breaths and feel your feet on the floor. Return to that weight whenever you feel yourself being pulled in many directions.

### Day 8 – Like Water

> The highest excellence is like (that of) water.

Source: Chapter 8

Watch water closely today: in a glass, in the shower, in the rain. Notice how it takes the shape of what holds it without losing itself. Where in your life could you be a little more like that?

### Day 9 – Bending

> The partial becomes complete; the crooked, straight; the empty, full; the worn out, new.

Source: Chapter 22

Think of one thing in your life that feels unfinished, bent, or worn out. Instead of judging it, ask what it might be making room for. Write down one possibility.

### Day 10 – Soft and Hard

> The soft overcomes the hard; and the weak the strong.

Source: Chapter 36

The next time you meet resistance today, from a person or a task, try softening instead of pushing harder. Lower your voice, loosen your grip, ask a question. Watch what changes.

### Day 11 – Finding the Crevice

> The softest thing in the world dashes against and overcomes the hardest; that which has no (substantial) existence enters where there is no crevice.

Source: Chapter 43

Pick a problem you have been attacking head-on without success. Today, look for the quiet way in: a small step, a side door, a patient approach. Try it once.

### Day 12 – Staying Supple

> Man at his birth is supple and weak; at his death, firm and strong.

Source: Chapter 76

Notice one opinion you hold rigidly. Today, listen to someone who sees it differently, without trying to win. Also give your body a few minutes of gentle stretching, as a reminder of what suppleness feels like.

### Day 13 – Blunting the Edge

> We should blunt our sharp points, and unravel the complications of things

Source: Chapter 4

Before you send a message today, read it again and soften one sharp phrase. If something feels tangled, untie just one knot of it rather than all of them at once.

### Day 14 – From Knowing to Doing

> Every one in the world knows that the soft overcomes the hard, and the weak the strong, but no one is able to carry it out in practice.

Source: Chapter 78

Look back over this week. Where did you manage to yield, and where did you push? Choose one situation coming up tomorrow and decide in advance how you will meet it softly.

### Day 15 – Rich Enough

> He who is satisfied with his lot is rich

Source: Chapter 33

Write down ten things you already have that you are glad of, from the large to the very small. Read the list once in the evening before you sleep.

### Day 16 – The Wish to Be Getting

> There is no guilt greater than to sanction ambition; no calamity greater than to be discontented with one's lot; no fault greater than the wish to be getting.

Source: Chapter 46

Each time you feel the urge to buy, check, or acquire something today, pause and name the feeling underneath it. You do not have to resist every time. Just notice.

### Day 17 – Too Much Input

> Colour's five hues from th' eyes their sight will take

Source: Chapter 12

Reduce the noise for one part of the day: no music in the background, no scrolling, no second screen. Then eat one meal slowly and taste it fully.

### Day 18 – Less Each Day

> he who devotes himself to the Tao (seeks) from day to day to diminish (his doing).

Source: Chapter 48

Remove one thing from your to-do list without doing it, because it does not really matter. Then let go of one object you no longer need.

### Day 19 – The Uncarved Block

> The unwrought material, when divided and distributed, forms vessels.

Source: Chapter 28

Spend a quiet moment remembering yourself before your titles and roles. What did you love as a child, before anyone told you what to be? Do one small thing today in that spirit.

### Day 20 – Few Desires

> He whose (desires) are few gets them; he whose (desires) are many goes astray.

Source: Chapter 22

Write down what you want this week. Then cross out everything except the three wishes that matter most. Give your attention only to those.

### Day 21 – Simple Pleasures

> They should think their (coarse) food sweet; their (plain) clothes beautiful; their (poor) dwellings places of rest; and their common (simple) ways sources of enjoyment.

Source: Chapter 80

Enjoy something plain today on purpose: bread, tea, a walk around the block, your own bed. Give it the attention you would give a treat.

### Day 22 – Doing Without Forcing

> The Tao in its regular course does nothing (for the sake of doing it), and so there is nothing which it does not do.

Source: Chapter 37

Choose one task today and do it without strain: no rushing, no clenched jaw, no fighting it. Do only what the task needs, and then stop.

### Day 23 – A Single Step

> the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step.

Source: Chapter 64

Think of a large goal you keep postponing. Take one step toward it today, so small that it would be silly not to: one email, one page, five minutes.

### Day 24 – While It Is Small

> (The master of it) anticipates things that are difficult while they are easy, and does things that would become great while they are small.

Source: Chapter 63

Look for one small problem at work or at home that could grow if it is left alone. Deal with it today, while it is still easy.

### Day 25 – A Light Touch

> Governing a great state is like cooking small fish.

Source: Chapter 60

Small fish fall apart if you keep turning them. Today, resist the urge to check, correct, or stir something that is already going well. Trust it and leave it alone.

### Day 26 – Leading from Behind

> Their work was done and their undertakings were successful, while the people all said, 'We are as we are, of ourselves!'

Source: Chapter 17

Help someone today in a way that lets them feel they did it themselves. Offer a question instead of an answer, or quietly prepare the ground and step aside.

### Day 27 – Lower Than the Streams

> So it is that the sage (ruler), wishing to be above men, puts himself by his words below them, and, wishing to be before them, places his person behind them.

Source: Chapter 66

Serve someone today without being asked: let another speak first, take the smaller share, do a chore no one notices. Watch how it changes the mood around you.

### Day 28 – Good to All

> To those who are good (to me), I am good; and to those who are not good (to me), I am also good;—and thus (all) get to be good.

Source: Chapter 49

Think of one person you find difficult. Today, offer them a small kindness that does not depend on how they treat you. This does not mean accepting harm; it means not letting their mood set yours.

### Day 29 – Careful at the End

> If they were careful at the end, as (they should be) at the beginning, they would not so ruin them.

Source: Chapter 64

Look back over the past four weeks and your notes. Which day changed something for you? Which was hardest? Write down the three practices you most want to keep.

### Day 30 – Carrying It Out

> Scholars of the highest class, when they hear about the Tao, earnestly carry it into practice.

Source: Chapter 41

Choose one practice from this month to continue for the next thirty days. Write it down in a single sentence and put it where you will see it each morning. Then begin again, gently.
