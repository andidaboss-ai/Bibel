## The 30-Day Practice

Mulford believed that habits of mind are built the same way they are broken: a little at a time, through many small repetitions. This month-long practice takes him at his word.

Each day gives you one sentence from the essays, exactly as he wrote it, followed by a short task. The days move through the book roughly in order, two or three days per essay, so by the end you will have lived with every one of his main ideas. Days 29 and 30 are for looking back and deciding what to keep.

A few suggestions:

- **Read the day's sentence in the morning.** Copy it onto a card or into your phone and look at it a few times during the day.
- **Do the task, even imperfectly.** Most take a few minutes. Some ask you simply to notice something.
- **Write one or two lines each evening.** What did you notice? What was hard? What surprised you?
- **Miss a day? Just continue.** Mulford would be the first to say that scolding yourself does not help.

You do not need to believe every word of his philosophy to benefit. Treat each day as a small experiment and judge it by what you observe in your own life. And keep his practices in their proper place. They can support your well-being, but they go alongside, never instead of, the care of a doctor or therapist when you need one.

### Day 1 – Two Minds

> You have in a sense two minds—the mind of the body and the mind of the spirit.

Source: The Material Mind v. the Spiritual Mind

Today, notice two voices in yourself. One says "this is how things have always been." The other says "maybe there is more." Each time you catch the first voice, write down what the second might say instead.

### Day 2 – Speak Kindly to Yourself

> You should not call yourself “a vile sinner” any more than you would call any other person a “vile sinner.”

Source: The Material Mind v. the Spiritual Mind

Listen for the names you call yourself when you slip up. Each time you hear one, rephrase it as you would for a friend you respect. Notice whether the kinder version makes it easier to try again.

### Day 3 – Your Real Relations

> Every order of mind or quality of thought must have association with a corresponding order of mind and quality of thought, or it will suffer.

Source: Who Are Our Relations?

Make a short list of the people who leave you feeling more alive and more like yourself. They may or may not be family. Reach out to one of them today, even with a short message.

### Day 4 – Give from the Heart

> Real gifts are always surprises.

Source: Who Are Our Relations?

Give one small thing today that nobody expects or asked for: a note, a coffee, an hour of help. Give it only if you genuinely want to. Notice how it feels compared with something you give out of obligation.

### Day 5 – Notice the Current

> We need to be careful of what we think and talk.

Source: Thought Currents

Pay attention to the tone of your conversations today. When talk drifts into complaining or gloom, try gently steering it toward something useful or hopeful. Note which conversations leave you lighter and which leave you heavier.

### Day 6 – Choose Your Inputs

> Neither others nor yourself are one particle aided by your knowing of every fire, explosion, murder, theft or crime which the newspapers chronicle every twenty-four hours.

Source: Thought Currents

Set a limit on news and scrolling today, such as one short check at a fixed time. Use the time you win back for something that feeds you: a walk, a book, a real conversation. Stay informed about what you can act on, and let the rest go for one day.

### Day 7 – Make Room for Play

> Life should be full of playfulness.

Source: Thought Currents

Do one thing today purely because it is fun. Laugh at something, tell a silly story, play a game, put on music and move. Notice whether a lighter mood changes how the rest of your day goes.

### Day 8 – Slow Down

> There is always a bit of fear where there is a bit of hurry.

Source: One Way to Cultivate Courage

Choose three ordinary acts today, such as tying your shoes, pouring a drink or opening a door, and do them deliberately, with your full attention. When you feel yourself rushing, ask quietly: "What am I afraid of missing?"

### Day 9 – One Step at a Time

> In any transaction—in the doing of anything there is but one step to be taken at a time.

Source: One Way to Cultivate Courage

Pick a task you have been dreading. Write down only the very next step and do just that one. Let the rest of the task wait until the step in front of you is done.

### Day 10 – The Courage to Think

> No quality of mind is more needful to success in all undertakings than courage, and by courage I mean not only courage to act but courage to think.

Source: One Way to Cultivate Courage

Think of an idea you usually push away because it feels too big, such as a new job, a project or a move. Instead of rejecting it, let it sit for ten minutes and write down three questions you would need to answer to explore it. You are not committing to anything, only giving the idea room.

### Day 11 – Put Down the Shell

> If you have a sad remembrance fling it off.

Source: Look Forward!

When an old regret comes up today, notice it, name it, and then deliberately turn your attention to something you are looking forward to. If a memory is too heavy to set down on your own, consider sharing it with someone you trust or with a counselor. Putting it down is not the same as pretending it never happened.

### Day 12 – Keep the Bright Memories

> Banish only the sad part.

Source: Look Forward!

Write down three good memories from your past, such as a place, a person or a day outdoors. Spend a few minutes reliving one of them in detail. Mulford did not ask you to forget your life, only to stop living in its sad corners.

### Day 13 – Go Outside

> Communion with Nature is something far above a sentiment.

Source: God in the Trees; or, the Infinite Mind in Nature

Spend at least twenty minutes outdoors today, ideally among trees or near water. Leave your phone in your pocket. Notice what you see, hear and smell, and how you feel when you come back.

### Day 14 – Love What Lives

> What we really love we cannot abuse, kill or enslave.

Source: God in the Trees; or, the Infinite Mind in Nature

Choose one living thing near you, such as a tree on your street, a houseplant or a bird at the window, and give it real attention today. Water it, watch it, learn its name. Consider one small way to treat the natural world with more care.

### Day 15 – Your Face Remembers

> Your thoughts shape your face, and give it its peculiar expression.

Source: Some Laws of Health and Beauty

Glance at your face in a mirror a few times today, not to judge it, but to notice the expression you are wearing. Relax your jaw and brow. Let a small smile come if it wants to, and see whether your mood follows.

### Day 16 – A Cheerful Table

> Dyspepsia does not come so much of the food we eat, as of the thoughts we think while eating it.

Source: Some Laws of Health and Beauty

Eat at least one meal today without screens or news. Sit down, slow down and, if you share it, keep the talk pleasant. Mulford's digestion theory is dated, but a calm, sociable meal is still one of life's simple goods.

### Day 17 – Freedom of Range

> Bears, lions, tigers, deer, wolves and all other animals like liberty and freedom of range as well as man.

Source: Museum and Menagerie Horrors

Read about one wild animal living in its natural habitat, or about a wildlife sanctuary or conservation effort. Think about what freedom means for that creature. Mulford wrote this in an age that rarely asked such questions; you can ask them now.

### Day 18 – Watch, Don't Take

> Is the gratification of this curiosity worth the misery it costs?

Source: Museum and Menagerie Horrors

Spend ten quiet minutes watching birds, squirrels or insects going about their lives, without disturbing them. Notice how much you learn simply by being still. Ask yourself whether there is a habit of yours that costs another creature more than it gives you.

### Day 19 – Seeing a Fault Is Progress

> Every protest of your mind against your fault is a push of the spirit forward.

Source: The God in Yourself

Name one fault you have been frustrated with lately. Instead of scolding yourself, write: "I can see this now, which means I am already growing." Then choose one small way to act differently tomorrow.

### Day 20 – I Can

> Your “I can” is the power shoving back that bolt.

Source: The God in Yourself

Notice every "I can't" you say or think today. Each time, try rephrasing it as "I can't yet" or "How could I?" See whether any closed door looks a little more open by evening.

### Day 21 – Even Soil Must Rest

> Even soil must rest to bring the best crop.

Source: The Healing and Renewing Force of Spring

Build one real rest into today: a short nap, an early night or fifteen minutes lying down with your eyes closed. Treat it as part of your work, not a reward for finishing it. If tiredness is constant and unexplained, mention it to your doctor.

### Day 22 – Rhythm

> In the kingdom of nature, we find periods of rest constantly alternating with periods of activity.

Source: The Healing and Renewing Force of Spring

Work in focused blocks today, then step away completely for a few minutes between them. Stretch, look out of a window or step outside. Notice whether your energy holds up better than usual.

### Day 23 – A Mind That Stays Young

> A body thus ever renewing, beautifying, freshening and strengthening means a mind behind it ever renewing, with new ideas, plans, hope, purpose and aspiration.

Source: Immortality in the Flesh

Mulford's hope of endless physical youth was a vision of his time, not a promise. What you can do is keep your mind renewing. Learn one new thing today, try a new route or recipe, or start a hobby you set aside long ago.

### Day 24 – Use Every Help

> A skilled and sympathetic physician of any school may be of much assistance.

Source: Immortality in the Flesh

Even Mulford valued good medical care alongside his inner work. Take one practical step for your health today: book a checkup you have been putting off, refill a prescription, or ask a question you have been avoiding. Hold that step in a hopeful frame of mind.

### Day 25 – The Strength of Admiration

> You are always the stronger when in a condition of admiration.

Source: The Attraction of Aspiration

Look for something to admire in three people today, including at least one you find difficult. Write each one down. Notice how your own mood shifts when you go looking for the good.

### Day 26 – Goodness Is Catching

> Goodness is catching.

Source: The Attraction of Aspiration

Do one kind act today without announcing it. Watch for small ripples, such as a smile returned or a better tone in a conversation. Stand a little straighter as you go about your day; Mulford thought aspiration showed in posture.

### Day 27 – New Thought Is New Life

> New thought is new life.

Source: The Accession of New Thought

Feed your mind something genuinely new today: a subject you know nothing about, a different kind of music, a conversation with someone outside your usual circle. Write down one idea it gave you.

### Day 28 – The Evening Ledger

> “I,” may say another man at night, “am richer than I was this morning by so much more patience, by a bit more of skill or dexterity in my art, by certain knowledge of which I knew nothing twenty-four hours ago.”

Source: The Accession of New Thought

Before bed, write down three ways you are richer tonight than this morning: a bit of patience, a new skill, something learned. Keep this ledger for the rest of the month.

### Day 29 – Try Again

> If it does not come the first day of such trial, try the next, and the next after that.

Source: The Accession of New Thought

Look back through your notes from the past four weeks. Which practices helped? Which did you skip or struggle with? Pick the one you found hardest and give it another try today, without judging how it went the first time.

### Day 30 – Your Own Book

> You are a book for yourself.

Source: The God in Yourself

Choose three practices from this month that you want to keep. Write them down as a short personal commitment, in your own words, and put it somewhere you will see it. Mulford insisted that each person must find their own way; this is the start of yours.
