## The 30-Day Practice

Allen believed that the mind changes the way a garden does: slowly, through small daily care, not through one heroic afternoon. This month-long practice is built on that idea. Each day gives you one short passage from the two books, a reminder of where it comes from, and a simple task that takes only a few minutes. There is room to write below each day.

The month has a shape. In the first week you simply notice your thoughts, without trying to fix them. In the second you look at how thinking connects with your circumstances and choices, and you begin to work with purpose. The third week turns to habit, speech and gentle discipline. The fourth looks outward and upward, to vision, calm and kindness. The last two days are for looking back.

A few suggestions. Read the day's passage in the morning and carry it with you. Do the task in the most ordinary setting you can, at work, at home, in traffic. In the evening, write a line or two about what you noticed. If you miss a day, do not start over; just pick up where you left off. Allen's own advice applies here: if you fail, rise up and keep going.

None of these exercises is a test, and none is a replacement for medical or psychological care. They are invitations to pay closer attention to your own mind, with honesty and with kindness toward yourself.

### Day 1 – The Seed of Every Act

> every act of a man springs from the hidden seeds of thought, and could not have appeared without them.

Source: Thought and Character

Today, pick three ordinary actions, such as a text you send, a purchase, a sharp or kind word. For each, pause afterward and ask: what thought came just before this? Write the three thoughts down without judging them.

### Day 2 – Tending the Garden

> so may a man tend the garden of his mind, weeding out all the wrong, useless, and impure thoughts

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Set a reminder for three times today. Each time it goes off, notice what you were just thinking. Was it a flower, a weed or simply grass? Do not pull anything up yet; just get to know the garden.

### Day 3 – The Watcher

> Man is the keeper of his heart; the watcher of his mind; the solitary sentinel of his citadel of life.

Source: The Heart and the Life

When worry or irritation shows up today, try naming it quietly: "There is worry." Notice that the one who names the thought is not the same as the thought. Write down one moment when this gave you a little space.

### Day 4 – Tracing the Effects

> if he will watch, control, and alter his thoughts, tracing their effects upon himself, upon others, and upon his life and circumstances

Source: Thought and Character

Choose one thought that keeps coming back this week. Trace it: how does it make your body feel, how does it change the way you treat people, and what does it lead you to do or avoid? Write a short chain: thought, feeling, action.

### Day 5 – The Weaver

> Mind clothes itself in garments of its own making.

Source: The Nature and Power of Mind

Notice one story you tell yourself about who you are, such as "I'm not a morning person" or "I always mess this up." Write it down. Then ask: is this a fact, or a garment I keep putting on?

### Day 6 – Body and Mind

> If you would protect your body, guard your mind.

Source: Effect of Thought on Health and the Body

Today, notice where stress or worry lands in your body: jaw, shoulders, stomach, breath. When you catch it, take three slow breaths and relax that spot. This is care, not cure; for any health concern, talk to a doctor.

### Day 7 – Finding the Good

> to think well of all, to be cheerful with all, to patiently learn to find the good in all

Source: Effect of Thought on Health and the Body

Think of one person who tends to annoy you. Today, look for one genuinely good thing about them and write it down. Then look back over this first week: which thoughts did you notice most often?

### Day 8 – Choosing Thoughts

> he can choose his thoughts, and so indirectly, yet surely, shape his circumstances.

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Name one situation in your life that you cannot change directly. Then list two thoughts about it that you can choose, and one small action each thought would lead to. Pick one action and do it today.

### Day 9 – What Circumstance Reveals

> Circumstance does not make the man; it reveals him to himself

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Recall a recent difficult moment. Without blaming yourself for the difficulty itself, ask what your response showed you: a strength you did not expect, or a habit you want to change. Write one sentence about each.

### Day 10 – No One Can See the Whole

> a man's entire soul-condition (although it may be known to himself) cannot be judged by another from the external aspect of his life alone.

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Allen himself warns against judging people by their outward lives. Today, each time you catch yourself judging someone's situation, remind yourself that you are seeing only a small part of their story. Note how often it happens.

### Day 11 – A Change of Approach

> as he alters his thoughts towards things and other people, things and other people will alter towards him.

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Choose one relationship that feels stuck. Before your next contact with that person, decide on one fresh, generous thought about them. Carry it into the conversation and notice whether anything shifts, in them or in you.

### Day 12 – A Purpose of Your Own

> A man should conceive of a legitimate purpose in his heart, and set out to accomplish it.

Source: Thought and Purpose

Write down one purpose for the next three months. It can be small: finish a course, repair a friendship, walk every day. Then write the very first step, one you could take this week, and put it in your calendar.

### Day 13 – The Task in Front of You

> fix the thoughts upon the faultless performance of their duty, no matter how insignificant their task may appear.

Source: Thought and Purpose

Choose one dull task today: dishes, email, a form. Do it with full attention, as well as you can, without rushing toward the next thing. Afterward, note how it felt to give a small task your whole mind.

### Day 14 – Lifting Your Thoughts

> A man can only rise, conquer, and achieve by lifting up his thoughts.

Source: The Thought-Factor in Achievement

When you catch a thought that pulls you down today, such as "What's the point?" or "I can't," answer it with a truer, more hopeful one, and write both down. Then review the week: where did thinking and circumstance meet?

### Day 15 – How Habits Form

> A thought constantly repeated at last becomes a fixed habit of the mind, and from such habits proceeds the life.

Source: Formation of Habit

Write down one thought you repeat almost every day. Is it a habit you want to keep? If not, write a short replacement thought and repeat it on purpose five times today, at set moments.

### Day 16 – Calm Can Be Learned

> Calmness and patience can become habitual in the same way

Source: Formation of Habit

Pick one daily moment that usually makes you impatient, such as a queue, a slow computer or a child's dawdling. Today, meet it with one slow breath and a patient thought. Count how many times you managed it.

### Day 17 – The Lowest and Easiest Step

> it is necessary to commence at the lowest and easiest steps, and proceed by natural and progressive stages to the higher and more difficult.

Source: Doing and Knowing

Think of a change you have tried and given up. Make the first step so small it is almost silly: one push-up, one line in a journal, one minute of quiet. Do that small step today and tick it off.

### Day 18 – Knowing by Doing

> Virtue can only be known by doing

Source: Doing and Knowing

Choose one quality you admire, such as patience, generosity or honesty. Instead of thinking about it, do one specific act of it today. In the evening, write what you learned from doing it that you did not know from reading about it.

### Day 19 – Keeping What You Gain

> Victories attained by right thought can only be maintained by watchfulness.

Source: The Thought-Factor in Achievement

Recall one good change you have already made in your life. What keeps it alive? Write one small daily or weekly habit that protects it, and do that habit today.

### Day 20 – Speaking of the Absent

> He will not say of another that which he dare not say to him.

Source: First Steps in the Higher Life

For one whole day, say nothing about anyone who is not present that you would not say to their face. If you slip, simply notice and stop mid-sentence if you can. Write down how often the urge came.

### Day 21 – Softening Your Words

> The man who is ever on the watch to check his own words in order to soften and purify them will find the higher way and the truer life

Source: First Steps in the Higher Life

Before sending any message today, read it once more and soften one word or remove one unnecessary barb. In a tense conversation, pause for a breath before answering. Then look back over this week of habit and speech.

### Day 22 – Seedlings

> Dreams are the seedlings of realities.

Source: Visions and Ideals

Spend ten quiet minutes picturing a life you would be glad to grow into, not in terms of things you own, but of the person you would be. Write a short paragraph describing it. Keep it somewhere you will see it.

### Day 23 – Effort, Not Luck

> the strength of the effort is the measure of the result.

Source: Visions and Ideals

Think of someone whose life you admire and who seems "lucky." Find out, or imagine honestly, the work and setbacks behind it. Then name one effort, however modest, you will put toward your own vision today.

### Day 24 – The Helm

> Self-control is strength; Right Thought is mastery; Calmness is power.

Source: Serenity

Choose one moment today when you would normally react quickly: an email, a comment, a bit of bad news. Wait ten seconds before you respond. Note what you said or did differently because of the pause.

### Day 25 – A Sheltering Presence

> He is like a shade-giving tree in a thirsty land, or a sheltering rock in a storm.

Source: Serenity

Be a steady presence for one person today. Listen to them fully without fixing, advising or interrupting. Afterward, write a line about what it felt like to offer calm rather than reaction.

### Day 26 – Forgiveness

> exercising disinterested charity and large-heartedness towards all

Source: First Steps in the Higher Life

Think of a small grievance you are still carrying. Write it down, and then write one sentence of goodwill toward the person involved. Forgiving does not mean excusing harm or staying in danger; it means setting down a weight you no longer need to carry.

### Day 27 – Habits of Kindness

> beautiful thoughts of all kinds crystallize into habits of grace and kindliness

Source: Effect of Thought on Circumstances

Do three small kind things today that no one asked for: a thank-you note, a held door, a generous word. Notice how each one changes your own mood as well as the other person's.

### Day 28 – A Little Better Each Day

> every day less of evil and more of good shall issue therefrom

Source: Mental Conditions and their Effects

Tonight, write down one thing you did today that you are glad of and one thing you would do differently. Keep both short and honest, without harshness. Then look back over this week of vision, calm and kindness.

### Day 29 – Rising Again

> If you should fail ten times, do not be disheartened; if you should fail a hundred times, rise up and pursue your way

Source: Exhortation

Read back through your notes from the past four weeks. Mark the days that went well and the days that did not. Choose one practice that slipped and give it another try today, without self-reproach.

### Day 30 – The Sweetness of Discipline

> Discipline in itself is beautiful, and the end of discipline is sweet.

Source: Exhortation

Write a short letter to yourself: what you noticed this month, which practice helped most, and the one practice you want to keep. Choose that practice for the coming month, and decide when and where you will do it.
