## The 30-Day Practice

Epictetus did not think philosophy was something you finish reading. He thought of it as training, the way a runner or a wrestler trains: a little every day, with setbacks, until the new habit feels natural. This month-long plan is built on that idea.

Each day gives you one short passage from this book, the section it comes from, and a small task. The tasks take a few minutes of attention, not a change of lifestyle. Most of them happen in the middle of an ordinary day, at work, at home, in traffic or in a conversation. A notebook helps, and so does a pen kept next to this book.

The month follows a simple arc. Week one is about the first and most important line Epictetus draws: between what is up to you and what is not. Week two turns to impressions and judgments, the quick stories your mind tells about events, and how they drive anger, fear and hurt. Week three looks at desire, possessions and freedom. Week four is about your roles and the people in your life, and, gently, about loss and mortality. The last two days are for looking back and deciding what to keep.

If you miss a day, do not start over and do not scold yourself. Pick up where you left off. That, too, is part of the practice.

### Day 1 – Two Kinds of Things

> Of things some are in our power, and others are not.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 1

Take a sheet of paper and draw a line down the middle. On the left, write what is on your mind today that is truly up to you; on the right, what is not. Keep the sheet and look at it again tonight.

### Day 2 – The Part No One Can Chain

> You may fetter my leg, but my will not even Zeus himself can overpower.

Source: Of the Things Which Are in Our Power and Not in Our Power

Think of one pressure you are under right now: a deadline, a demand, a person with power over you. Ask what it can actually touch (your time, your money, your comfort) and what it cannot (your choices, your honesty, your effort). Write one sentence about the part that stays yours.

### Day 3 – Wanting What Happens

> Seek not that the things which happen should happen as you wish; but wish the things which happen to be as they are, and you will have a tranquil flow of life.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 8

Today, notice the small moments when you wish things were otherwise: a late bus, rain, a slow line. Each time, quietly say, "This is what is happening," and then ask what the best next step is. Count how many times you caught yourself.

### Day 4 – Obstacles to the Body, Not to You

> Disease is an impediment to the body, but not to the will, unless the will itself chooses.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 9

Pick one limit you live with: tiredness, an ache, a lack of time or money. Name what it blocks and what it leaves open. Then do one thing today that it leaves open.

### Day 5 – Difficulty as Training

> It is circumstances (difficulties) which show what men are.

Source: How We Should Struggle with Circumstances

Choose one difficult situation or person in your week and give it a new label: "my sparring partner." Before you face it, decide which quality you want to practice (patience, fairness, courage). Afterward, give yourself an honest score.

### Day 6 – Preparing All Your Life

> I have maintained that which was in my own power.

Source: Of Tranquillity (Freedom from Perturbation)

Socrates said his whole life was preparation for his trial. Think of a test you are facing soon, large or small. Write down what you can do well whatever the outcome, and let that list, not the result, be your goal.

### Day 7 – Contests You Can Win

> You can be invincible, if you enter into no contest in which it is not in your power to conquer.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 19

Look for one contest you have been fighting that cannot be won by effort alone: being liked by everyone, beating a colleague to praise, controlling an outcome. Decide to step out of it for today. Put that energy into something that depends on you.

### Day 8 – Opinions, Not Events

> Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 5

The next time you feel upset today, stop and separate two things: what actually happened, and what you told yourself about it. Write both down in two short lines. Notice which one is doing the hurting.

### Day 9 – When Someone Insults You

> Remember that it is not he who reviles you or strikes you, who insults you, but it is your opinion about these things as being insulting.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 20

If someone is rude to you today, give yourself a pause of three slow breaths before you answer. Use the time to ask, "Is this really harm, or is it only words?" You may still reply, but reply on purpose, not on reflex.

### Day 10 – Describe, Don't Judge

> do not say that he bathes badly, but that he bathes quickly.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 45

Spend one hour today describing people's actions without adding a verdict. Not "she was rude," but "she did not say hello." Notice how often your first thought comes with a judgment already attached.

### Day 11 – Starving Anger

> If then you wish not to be of an angry temper, do not feed the habit: throw nothing on it which will increase it: at first keep quiet, and count the days on which you have not been angry.

Source: How We Should Struggle Against Appearances

Start a simple tally in your notebook: one mark for each day without an angry outburst. Today, when irritation rises, keep quiet for a moment instead of adding fuel. You are not trying to be perfect, only to lengthen the streak.

### Day 12 – It Seemed So to Him

> It seemed so to him.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 42

Think of someone who treated you badly recently. Try to describe, in a few sentences, how the situation might have looked from their side. You do not have to agree with them; you only have to see that they acted on what seemed right to them.

### Day 13 – What Does This Person Want?

> When I see a man anxious, I say, What does this man want?

Source: On Anxiety (Solicitude)

When you feel anxious today, ask Epictetus's question of yourself: "What do I want right now that is not in my power?" Name it plainly. Then ask what you can still do, and do that.

### Day 14 – The Other Handle

> Everything has two handles, the one by which it may be borne, the other by which it may not.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 43

Choose one family member, friend or coworker who annoys you. Write down the "handle" you usually grab (what they do wrong) and then look for another one (what you share, what they have given you). Try holding the second handle the next time you see them.

### Day 15 – The Banquet

> Remember that in life you ought to behave as at a banquet.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 15

At one meal today, practice the banquet manners literally: take what comes to you with ease, do not reach for what has not arrived, and let things pass without grabbing. Afterward, think of one thing in your life you are reaching for too early.

### Day 16 – What Freedom Means

> He is free who lives as he wishes to live; who is neither subject to compulsion nor to hindrance, nor to force

Source: About Freedom

Write down one way in which you feel unfree. Then ask which desire or fear gives that thing its hold over you. You do not need to change it today; simply see the chain clearly.

### Day 17 – The Price of Calm

> at such price is sold tranquillity, but nothing is got for nothing.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 12

When a small loss or nuisance happens today (spilled coffee, a lost pen, a delay), say to yourself, "This is the price of my calm," and pay it without fuss. Notice how cheap most of these prices are.

### Day 18 – A Pause Before Pleasure

> let the thing wait for you, and allow yourself a certain delay on your own part.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 34

Pick one small craving you often give in to without thinking: a snack, a scroll, a purchase. Today, make it wait ten minutes. During that time, picture how you will feel afterward either way, and then choose.

### Day 19 – Enough

> for there is no limit to that which has once passed the true measure.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 39

Look at one thing you own or want to buy and ask what need it serves. Name what "enough" would be in that area of your life. If you find an easy excess, let go of one item or one planned purchase.

### Day 20 – Holding Things Lightly

> Do not admire your clothes, and then you will not be angry with the thief.

Source: That We Ought Not to Be Angry with the Errors (Faults) of Others

Think of one possession you would be very upset to lose. Keep caring for it, but say to yourself, "This is a thing, and things can go." Notice whether the thought makes you less tense rather than less grateful.

### Day 21 – The Opinions You Carry

> my opinions alone are mine and they cannot be taken from me

Source: On Freedom from Fear

Write down three convictions you would want to keep wherever you lived and whatever you lost. Read them aloud once. At the end of the day, note one moment when you acted on one of them.

### Day 22 – Your Part in the Play

> For this is your duty, to act well the part that is given to you; but to select the part, belongs to another.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 17

List the main roles you play right now: parent, child, colleague, friend, neighbor. Pick one and ask what playing it well would look like today. Do one small thing that fits that answer.

### Day 23 – Duties from Relations

> Duties are universally measured by relations.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 30

Think of a relationship where the other person is falling short. Instead of asking what they owe you, ask what your own part in the relationship asks of you. Write one sentence about it and act on it once.

### Day 24 – What Friendship Rests On

> For where else is friendship than where there is fidelity, and modesty, where there is a communion of honest things and of nothing else.

Source: On Friendship

Think of the friends you trust most and name what holds the friendship together. Send one of them a short message today. In one small way, be the kind of friend this passage describes.

### Day 25 – When You Hear Gossip About Yourself

> The man did not know the rest of my faults, for he would not have mentioned these only.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 33

If you hear that someone criticized you, resist the urge to defend yourself right away. Ask honestly whether any of it is true and worth fixing. Then try answering, at least in your head, with a little of the humor in this line.

### Day 26 – Borrowed, Not Owned

> take care of it as a thing which belongs to another, as travellers do with their inn.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 11

This passage is not a call to love less. It invites you to see what you love as a gift you hold for a while. Today, look at one person or thing you treasure and take a quiet moment to be grateful that it is with you now.

### Day 27 – Comforting Someone Who Grieves

> So far as words then do not be unwilling to show him sympathy, and even if it happens so, to lament with him.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 16

Epictetus does not tell you to hold back comfort. Think of someone who is going through a loss or a hard time, and reach out with a call, a note or a visit. Listen more than you advise.

### Day 28 – Looking at Mortality

> Let death and exile and every other thing which appears dreadful be daily before your eyes; but most of all death: and you will never think of anything mean nor will you desire anything extravagantly.

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 21

Spend a few calm minutes on a simple question: if your time were shorter than you expect, what would you stop doing, and what would you start? Write down one answer of each kind. If this exercise stirs up heavy feelings, set it aside and talk to someone you trust.

### Day 29 – Begin Again

> no man hinders us from renewing the combat again, and we are not compelled to wait for another four years that the games at Olympia may come again

Source: To Those Who Fall Off (Desist) from Their Purpose

Look back over the past four weeks. Note which days went well, which you skipped and which you found hardest. Choose one practice you let slip and do it again today, without waiting for a fresh start.

### Day 30 – Now Is the Contest

> remember that now is the contest, now are the Olympic games, and they cannot be deferred

Source: Enchiridion, Chapter 50

Pick the three practices from this month that helped you most and write them on a card you will see every day. Next to each, write when you will use it. Then begin, today, not tomorrow.
