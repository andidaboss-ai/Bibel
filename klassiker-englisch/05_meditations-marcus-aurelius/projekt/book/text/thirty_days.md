## The 30-Day Practice

Marcus did not read philosophy the way we read a novel. He came back to the same few ideas again and again, testing them against ordinary days: a bad night's sleep, a rude colleague, bad news from far away. This practice invites you to do the same.

Each day below gives you one sentence from Long's translation, the Book it comes from, and a small task. The tasks take a few minutes. Some ask you to write, some to notice, some simply to pause. Read the quotation in the morning, carry it with you, and check in with yourself in the evening. If you want to see the line in its setting, look it up in its Book; the surrounding passage often makes it clearer.

The month has a shape. Week one is about starting the day well and sorting what is in your power from what is not. Week two turns to judgments, anger and other people. Week three is about duty, work and the present moment. Week four looks at nature, change, death and gratitude. The last two days help you look back and set up an evening habit you can keep.

You do not need to be perfect at this. Marcus missed days too, and his notes are full of starting over. If you skip a day, pick up where you left off. The point is not a streak. The point is a slightly steadier mind.

### Day 1 – Preparing for the Day

> Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busybody, the ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial.

Source: The Second Book

Before you check your phone, take one minute to picture the day ahead, including the people who may test your patience. Name one or two likely frictions out loud. Then decide in advance how you want to respond: calmly, fairly, without being knocked off course. Marcus is not being gloomy here; he is getting ready.

### Day 2 – Getting Up

> I am rising to the work of a human being.

Source: The Fifth Book

When the alarm goes off today, say this line before you have time to argue with yourself. Notice the excuses your mind offers for staying put. Write down, in one sentence, the work that is yours to do today, whether that is a job, caring for someone, or simply getting through.

### Day 3 – Looking Within

> Within is the fountain of good, and it will ever bubble up, if thou wilt ever dig.

Source: The Seventh Book

Find five quiet minutes today and sit without a screen. Ask yourself what you actually value, beneath the noise of the day. Marcus believed the source of a good life is inside us, not out in the world, but that it takes regular digging to reach it. Write down one thing you found.

### Day 4 – What Is in Your Power

> Well, then, is it not better to use what is in thy power like a free man than to desire in a slavish and abject way what is not in thy power?

Source: The Ninth Book

Draw a line down the middle of a page. On the left, list what is worrying you today. On the right, next to each worry, write the part of it you can actually act on, if any. Spend your energy only on the right-hand column today.

### Day 5 – Fewer, Better Things

> Accordingly, on every occasion a man should ask himself, Is this one of the unnecessary things?

Source: The Fourth Book

Ask this question three times today: before you open an app, before you agree to something, and before you say something you are not sure needs saying. Drop one thing that fails the test. Notice how much room it leaves.

### Day 6 – Standing Firm

> Be like the promontory against which the waves continually break, but it stands firm and tames the fury of the water around it.

Source: The Fourth Book

Think of one situation today that usually throws you: a meeting, a commute, a hard conversation. Picture yourself as the rock, not the wave. When it comes, take one slow breath before you respond. Afterward, note what changed.

### Day 7 – Standing on Your Own Feet

> A man then must stand erect, not be kept erect by others.

Source: The Third Book

Notice today when you are waiting for someone else's approval before you feel all right. You do not have to stop caring about people; just catch the moment you hand them the controls. Choose one small thing to do today simply because you judge it right, without needing anyone to see it.

### Day 8 – The Event and the Judgment

> If thou art pained by any external thing, it is not this thing that disturbs thee, but thy own judgment about it.

Source: The Eighth Book

The next time something upsets you today, pause and split it in two. Write down what actually happened, as plainly as a camera would record it. Then write down the story you told yourself about it. See whether the story is the part that hurts.

### Day 9 – First Impressions Only

> Say nothing more to thyself than what the first appearances report.

Source: The Eighth Book

Today, practice not adding to the facts. If someone is late, they are late; that is all you know. If an email is short, it is short. Each time you catch yourself filling in the gaps with the worst version, say quietly, "That has not been reported."

### Day 10 – The Best Revenge

> The best way of avenging thyself is not to become like [the wrong-doer].

Source: The Sixth Book

Think of someone who has treated you badly, recently or long ago. Name the behavior that hurt you. Then make one plain promise to yourself: I will not do that to others. Marcus suggests this is the only kind of payback that leaves you better off.

### Day 11 – Understanding the Wrongdoer

> When a man has done thee any wrong, immediately consider with what opinion about good or evil he has done wrong.

Source: The Seventh Book

When someone annoys or wrongs you today, ask: what did they think they were gaining? What did they believe was good? You do not have to agree or excuse it. Understanding the motive usually turns anger into something cooler and more useful.

### Day 12 – Expecting Difficult People

> Do not, then, require what is impossible.

Source: The Ninth Book

A world without rude, careless or dishonest people is not on offer. Today, when you meet one, remind yourself that you knew such people existed, and that this one is simply showing up on schedule. See whether expecting them makes them easier to handle with patience.

### Day 13 – Made for One Another

> Men exist for the sake of one another.

Source: The Eighth Book

Do one helpful thing today for someone who did not ask for it, and do not mention it. It can be small: a clear explanation, a door held, a message checking in. At the end of the day, note how it felt to act as part of a team rather than alone.

### Day 14 – Open to Correction

> If any man is able to convince me and show me that I do not think or act right, I will gladly change; for I seek the truth, by which no man was ever injured.

Source: The Sixth Book

Today, when someone disagrees with you, listen to the end before you answer. Ask one honest question about their view. If they are right about something, say so plainly. Being corrected costs a little pride and gains a lot of truth.

### Day 15 – Stop Talking, Start Being

> No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but be such.

Source: The Tenth Book

Pick one quality you admire: patience, honesty, generosity. Do not read about it or plan it today. Act it out in one specific moment you can name tonight.

### Day 16 – The Task in Front of You

> Attend to the matter which is before thee, whether it is an opinion or an act or a word.

Source: The Eighth Book

Choose one task today and give it your full attention for twenty-five minutes, with notifications off. When your mind drifts to other worries, bring it back without scolding. Afterward, notice whether the work was better and whether you felt calmer.

### Day 17 – Good Without Applause

> When thou hast done a good act and another has received it, why dost thou still look for a third thing besides these, as fools do, either to have the reputation of having done a good act or to obtain a return?

Source: The Seventh Book

Do something kind or useful today and let it be finished the moment it is done. Notice any wish to be thanked or noticed. Let that wish pass without feeding it. The good act was the whole point.

### Day 18 – When It Seems Too Hard

> If a thing is difficult to be accomplished by thyself, do not think that it is impossible for man: but if anything is possible for man and conformable to his nature, think that this can be attained by thyself too.

Source: The Sixth Book

Think of something you have been avoiding because it feels beyond you. Name one person who has done something like it. Then take the smallest real step toward it today, even if it is only a phone call or a first paragraph.

### Day 19 – One Day at a Time

> Do not disturb thyself by thinking of the whole of thy life.

Source: The Eighth Book

When the future feels heavy today, shrink your view. Ask, as Marcus does, what about this present moment is actually unbearable. Usually the answer is: nothing, right now. Handle the next hour, then the next.

### Day 20 – Future Worries

> Let not future things disturb thee, for thou wilt come to them, if it shall be necessary, having with thee the same reason which now thou usest for present things.

Source: The Seventh Book

Write down one thing you are dreading. Under it, write the inner resources you will bring to it: your judgment, your experience, people you can ask for help. You will meet the future with the same mind you are using today. Put the note away and return to the present.

### Day 21 – While You Can

> While thou livest, while it is in thy power, be good.

Source: The Fourth Book

Is there something kind, honest or brave you have been putting off for "later"? An apology, a thank-you, a hard truth told gently? Do it today. Marcus wrote this line to stop himself from waiting for a better moment that might not come.

### Day 22 – Not Afraid of Change

> Is any man afraid of change?

Source: The Seventh Book

List three changes in your life that once scared you and that you now accept or even value. Then look at a change you are facing now. Ask whether it might one day join that list. Marcus points out that nothing useful happens without something changing.

### Day 23 – The River of Time

> Time is like a river made up of the events which happen, and a violent stream; for as soon as a thing has been seen, it is carried away, and another comes in its place, and this will be carried away too.

Source: The Fourth Book

Today, when a small irritation grabs you, picture it as something floating past on a fast river. It is already moving away. Let it go downstream instead of wading in after it.

### Day 24 – The View from Above

> Look down from above on the countless herds of men and their countless solemnities, and the infinitely varied voyagings in storms and calms, and the differences among those who are born, who live together, and die.

Source: The Ninth Book

Take two minutes today to imagine rising above your street, your city, the whole country, until you can see the earth turning. Look at today's problem from that height. It still matters, but it is now the right size. Come back down and deal with it.

### Day 25 – The Size of Fame

> Soon, very soon, thou wilt be ashes, or a skeleton, and either a name or not even a name; but name is sound and echo.

Source: The Fifth Book

Notice where you are chasing recognition today: likes, titles, being seen as right. Ask what you would do differently if no one would ever know your name. Do one thing today for its own sake.

### Day 26 – Death as Part of Nature

> Do not despise death, but be well content with it, since this too is one of those things which nature wills.

Source: The Ninth Book

Marcus thought about death often, not to frighten himself but to see life clearly. Today, think of death as part of the same natural pattern as growing up and growing old. Let that thought point you toward what matters while you are here. This is not a call to stop grieving or to stop caring about life; if thoughts about death feel heavy or dark for you, please talk to someone you trust or a professional (in an emergency in the US, call or text 988; in the UK, call Samaritans on 116 123).

### Day 27 – What You Already Have

> Think not so much of what thou hast not as of what thou hast: but of the things which thou hast select the best, and then reflect how eagerly they would have been sought, if thou hadst them not.

Source: The Seventh Book

Write down three things you have that you would badly miss if they were gone: a person, your health, a skill, a place. For each, picture life without it for a moment. Then return to it with fresh thanks. Marcus adds a warning too: enjoy these things without clinging so hard that losing them would break you.

### Day 28 – Gratitude for People

> To the gods I am indebted for having good grandfathers, good parents, a good sister, good teachers, good associates, good kinsmen and friends, nearly everything good.

Source: The First Book

Book I is Marcus's long thank-you note. Today, write your own short version: name five people and one thing each taught you. If one of them is still within reach, tell them.

### Day 29 – Looking Back on the Month

> When thou hast assumed these names, good, modest, true, rational, a man of equanimity, and magnanimous, take care that thou dost not change these names; and if thou shouldst lose them, quickly return to them.

Source: The Tenth Book

Look back over the past four weeks and note which day's practice helped most and where you slipped. Choose three words, like Marcus's "names" here, that describe who you want to be. When you lose them, as everyone does, the task is simply to return.

### Day 30 – The Evening Review

> To-day I have got out of all trouble, or rather I have cast out all trouble, for it was not outside, but within and in my opinions.

Source: The Ninth Book

Tonight, start a habit you can keep. Before sleep, ask yourself three things: what disturbed me today, and was it the event or my judgment of it; what I did well; and what I will do better tomorrow. Answer briefly and kindly, then let the day go.
