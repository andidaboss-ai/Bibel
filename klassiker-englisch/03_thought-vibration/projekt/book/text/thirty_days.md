## The 30-Day Practice

Reading about the mind is easy. Training it takes repetition. Atkinson knew this. Nearly every chapter of *Thought Vibration* ends with an order to practice, often "at least once an hour." This section turns his book into a month of small daily steps.

Each day gives you one sentence from Atkinson, exactly as he wrote it, and the place it comes from. Then comes a short task in plain words. Most tasks take five to fifteen minutes. A few ask you to watch yourself through the day.

The month follows the shape of the book. Week one is about noticing: what you think about, what you expect, and the mental climate you carry into a room. Week two moves to mind-building, the will, and fear. Week three deals with worry, focus, and desire. Week four takes up the later chapters on energy, habit, emotion and self-worth. It ends with a creed of your own and a commitment to keep going.

Keep a notebook for the month. Write the date, the theme and a line or two about what you noticed. If you miss a day, pick up where you left off. Skipping a day is not failing.

One reminder before you begin. These are exercises for everyday mental fitness. They work alongside medical and psychological care, not in place of it. If fear, worry or low mood stays heavy for weeks, talk with a doctor or therapist. That is part of taking charge of your mind.

### Day 1 – Thought as a Force

> When we come to see that Thought is a force—a manifestation of energy—having a magnet-like power of attraction, we will begin to understand the why and wherefore of many things that have heretofore seemed dark to us.

Source: The Law of Attraction in the Thought World

Set three alarms today. Each time one rings, write down the thought that was on your mind right then, without judging it. Tonight, read the three notes and ask one question: if these thoughts ran all day, what kind of day would they produce?

### Day 2 – Your Morning Weather

> The man who rises in the morning feeling “grumpy” usually manages to have the whole family in the same mood before the breakfast is over.

Source: The Law of Attraction in the Thought World

Before you speak to anyone this morning, name your mood in one word. Then watch how the first three conversations of the day go. Moods spread between people, and you are allowed to notice yours before you pass it on.

### Day 3 – Calm in the Storm

> One is not at the mercy of the fitful storms of Thought after he has learned the workings of the Law.

Source: The Law of Attraction in the Thought World

Today, when someone around you is tense or irritable, try not to catch it. Breathe out slowly, lower your shoulders and answer one tone calmer than you were spoken to. Afterward, note whether the exchange changed at all.

### Day 4 – You See What You Look For

> We generally see that for which we look.

Source: Thought-Waves and Their Process of Reproduction

Choose one person you find difficult. Today, look on purpose for three things they do well or kindly, and write them down. You are not excusing anything. You are testing how much your expectation has been editing what you see.

### Day 5 – Raising the Keynote

> But remember you possess the power to raise the keynote of your mind to a positive pitch by an effort of the will.

Source: Thought-Waves and Their Process of Reproduction

Pick one moment today when you feel flat: before a call, a chore or a meeting. Stand up, take three slow breaths and say one sentence of what you intend to do well. Notice whether the next ten minutes feel any different.

### Day 6 – Permission to Rest

> It is well to be able to relax and assume a certain degree of receptiveness, knowing that you are always able to spring back to the more positive state at will.

Source: Thought-Waves and Their Process of Reproduction

Atkinson did not want anyone "on" all day. Give yourself fifteen minutes with no task: a walk, music, a window. Spend that time listening and taking things in instead of pushing.

### Day 7 – The Momentum of Habit

> The Law applies to good habits as well as bad.

Source: A Talk About the Mind

List three things you do on autopilot every day, one helpful and two not. Choose one small helpful habit you would like to run on autopilot too, such as a glass of water on waking or a two-minute tidy. Do it today, and plan to repeat it at the same time tomorrow.

### Day 8 – Building on Purpose

> In fact, we are mind-building every hour of our lives, either consciously or unconsciously.

Source: Mind Building

Look at what went into your mind today: news, feeds, conversations, books. Roughly how much was chosen, and how much simply arrived? Swap one half-hour of drift for something you actually picked.

### Day 9 – Who Is in Charge?

> All things are good when we learn to master them, but no thing is good when it masters us.

Source: Mind Building

Name one appetite or impulse that tends to run you: snacking, scrolling, snapping, spending. Today, when it shows up, pause for ten seconds before acting. You may still choose it, but choose it rather than obey it.

### Day 10 – Want It Enough

> Strong Will follows strong Desire.

Source: The Secret of the Will

Write down one thing you keep saying you will do "some day." Under it, write honestly why it matters to you. If you cannot find a real reason, cross it off. If you can, keep that reason where you will see it.

### Day 11 – Find Out, Then Start

> Now, if you are really in earnest about this matter, get to work and first find out what you really want to do—then start to work and *do* it.

Source: The Secret of the Will

Take the goal from yesterday and break it into the smallest first step, one that takes under twenty minutes. Do that step today. Willpower tends to grow out of moving, not the other way around.

### Day 12 – The Disagreeable Task

> Anyone can do a pleasant thing cheerfully, but it takes Will to do the unpleasant thing cheerfully; and that is how you must do the work.

Source: The Secret of the Will

This is Atkinson's own exercise. Do one task today that you have been putting off, the one you most want to avoid. Try to do it without grumbling, even aloud. Afterward, write one line about how it felt to have it done.

### Day 13 – Let In the Light

> The best way to overcome the habit of Fear is to assume the mental attitude of Courage, just as the best way to get rid of darkness is to let in the light.

Source: How to Become Immune to Injurious Thought Attraction

Think of one situation that makes you nervous. Instead of telling yourself "don't be afraid," picture yourself handling it with courage, step by step, for one minute. Fear is a normal signal. If it is constant or overwhelming, this exercise is no substitute for talking with a professional.

### Day 14 – Courage in Action

> Take the word “Courage” with you as your watchword and manifest it in action.

Source: How to Become Immune to Injurious Thought Attraction

Do one small thing today that you would do if you were a little braver. You might ask a question in a meeting, make a call or send the message you have been drafting. Keep it safe and small. Courage grows through repeated small acts.

### Day 15 – Turning Worry Into Wanting

> Transmute Worry into keen Desire.

Source: The Transmutation of Negative Thought

Write down your biggest worry. Next to it, write what you actually want in that situation, stated positively. Then write one action that moves toward the want. Do the action, or schedule it, today.

### Day 16 – Most Worries Never Arrive

> The majority of things that we worry about don’t come to pass at all; a large proportion of the others come in a milder form than we had anticipated, and there are always other things which come at the same time which help us to overcome the trouble.

Source: The Transmutation of Negative Thought

Think back to something you worried about a year ago. What actually happened? Write it down. Then write a short note to your future self about one of today's worries.

### Day 17 – Don't Fight, Refocus

> There are better ways of overcoming objectionable thoughts than by fighting them.

Source: The Transmutation of Negative Thought

When an unwanted thought returns today, do not argue with it. Turn your full attention to something in front of you, such as your work, a conversation or the feel of your feet on the floor. Count how many times you managed the switch.

### Day 18 – Servants or Masters

> Your thoughts are either faithful servants or tyrannical masters—just as you allow them to be.

Source: The Law of Mental Control

Before a problem you need to solve, write it down as one clear question. Work on it with full attention for twenty minutes, then put it away on purpose. Notice whether a better answer turns up later, after you have stopped pushing.

### Day 19 – One Thing at a Time

> When you have some mental work to do concentrate upon it to the exclusion of everything else, and you will find that the mind will get right down to business—to the work at hand—and matters will be cleared up in no time.

Source: The Law of Mental Control

Choose one task and give it a single focused block of twenty-five minutes. Phone in another room, one tab open, no checking. When it ends, write down how much you got done compared with a usual scattered half-hour.

### Day 20 – Slowing the Engine

> It is just as easy to slow down the mind as it is to slow down an engine, and thousands of people are learning to do this in these days of New Thought.

Source: The Law of Mental Control

Tonight, set a clear end to your thinking day. Write tomorrow's top three tasks on paper, close the notebook and turn to something completely different, like a story, a puzzle or a bath. If sleeplessness is a regular problem, mention it to a doctor.

### Day 21 – Plug the Leaks

> Mental Force operates best when it is concentrated.

Source: The Attractive Power—Desire Force

Name the one aim that matters most to you this season. Then list three things that keep pulling your attention away from it. Pick one to reduce this week, and protect thirty minutes today for the main aim.

### Day 22 – Put Life Into It

> Put a little life into your work—into your pleasures—into yourself.

Source: Asserting the Life-Force

Choose one ordinary task, like washing up, a commute or a report. Do it with full interest, as if it were new to you. Notice three details you usually miss. Then take a few slow, deep breaths and ask how awake you feel.

### Day 23 – The Habit Question

> If you are confronted with the question: “Which of these two things should I do?” the best answer is: “I will do that which I would like to become a habit with me.”

Source: Training the Habit-Mind

Use this question at every small fork today: stairs or lift, snack or water, reply now or later. Keep a tally of how often you chose the habit you want. Expect some misses. The point is to ask.

### Day 24 – Emotions Deepen

> It is an axiom of psychology that “Emotions deepen by repetition.”

Source: The Psychology of Emotion

Notice one feeling today that you repeat often, such as irritation, envy or gloom. When it starts, name it quietly ("there is irritation") and let it pass without feeding it with more story. Feelings themselves are not wrong. This is about not rehearsing the ones that hurt you.

### Day 25 – Act It Out

> Thoughts take form in action; and in turn actions influence thought.

Source: Developing New Brain-Cells

Pick the quality you would like more of, such as cheerfulness, calm or confidence. For one hour, act as if you already had it: posture, voice, pace. Afterward, note whether your feeling moved even a little toward the act.

### Day 26 – Ordinary People, Real Results

> Don’t undervalue yourself, or overvalue others.

Source: The Great Dynamic Forces

Think of someone you admire who has done what you hope to do. Write down three ordinary things about them, then three ways they use their energy that you could borrow. Choose one and try it this week.

### Day 27 – Ask

> Many good things are lost for want of the asking.

Source: Claiming Your Own

Make one reasonable request today that you have been too shy to make. It might be for help, information, feedback or a chance to try something. Ask clearly and politely, and be ready to hear no, because the asking is the practice and the answer is not up to you.

### Day 28 – Clear the Atmosphere

> A state of discouragement is not the one in which bright ideas come to us.

Source: Law, Not Chance

When you are stuck today, do not force a solution while you feel low. First change your state: move, step outside, talk to a friend or rest. Then come back and write down three possible next steps, however small.

### Day 29 – Your Creed

> I believe in the I DO, as well as the I AM.

Source: My Working Creed

Reread Atkinson's creed. Then write your own, five to ten lines, each beginning "I believe." Include at least one line about what you will do, not only what you hold true. Keep the ones you could live by on an ordinary Tuesday.

### Day 30 – Your Commitment

> Life accepts no substitutes—you must step out yourself.

Source: Foreword

Look back through your notebook and circle the three practices that helped most. Write a short promise naming those three and when you will do them each week. Put it somewhere you will see it. Then do the first one today.
