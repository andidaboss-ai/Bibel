## Your Word Is Your Wand
### Reflection
This opening chapter gives the whole book its title and its central idea: the words you speak about your life carry weight. Shinn grounds this in two Bible passages she runs together, "By thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned" (Matthew 12:37) and "Death and life are in the power of the tongue" (Proverbs 18:21).

She tells of a woman who came to her with only two dollars (about $40 today). Instead of agreeing that things were hopeless, Shinn blessed the money and painted a playful picture of a purse "crammed, jammed" with bills and checks. Shinn reports that the woman later received a gift of six thousand dollars, roughly $110,000 today. In another story, a woman at sea speaks to a heavy fog and gives thanks for the sun.

Notice what Shinn is really teaching here: choose words that create a vivid, hopeful picture, and stay calm when "appearances are against" you. She also quotes "Ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free" (John 8:32) and echoes Isaiah 35:1, where the desert "shall blossom as the rose." For a reader today, the invitation is simple: notice the fog in your own life, and practice speaking about it with hope instead of dread.
### Practice
1. Name one "fog" in your life right now: a worry about money, love, work or health.
2. Write down the sentence you usually say about it ("I'll never…", "It's always…").
3. Cross it out and write a hopeful replacement in the present tense.
4. Say the new sentence aloud three times this morning and three times tonight.
5. Choose one small, practical step toward clearing that fog and do it today.
### Key Affirmation
> There are no fogs in Divine Mind, so let the fog be lifted! I give thanks for the sun!

## Success
### Reflection
Shinn opens this chapter by pointing out that certain words and images reach the deeper mind better than others. A man asked her to pray for his right work. She gave him a line drawn from Revelation 3:8, "I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it." It didn't seem to land, so she added that the door was "nailed back." That vivid detail, she says, electrified him, and soon he was offered a fine position in another city.

Her second story is about a woman earning a small salary who had a "hunch" to open a tearoom and candy shop. Scared but persistent, she went ahead, and Shinn reports that the shop became crowded with customers. On a slow holiday, the owner insisted every day was a good day, and an old friend paid a hundred dollars (roughly $1,900 today) for a box of candy.

The long list of affirmations that follows draws on many biblical images: "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5), the walls of Jericho falling (Joshua 6), not growing "weary in well doing" (Galatians 6:9), and fields "white already to harvest" (John 4:35). The thread through all of them is courage. Success, in Shinn's view, comes from fearless faith joined to bold action, not from competing with anyone else.
### Practice
Pick one affirmation from this chapter that gives you a jolt of energy when you read it. Write it on a card and keep it where you work. Then list three "hunches" you have had about your work or calling that you have not acted on. Choose the smallest one and take a first, concrete step toward it this week, such as a phone call, a sketch, a conversation or a bit of research.
### Key Affirmation
> I make friends with hindrances and every obstacle becomes a stepping-stone.

## Prosperity
### Reflection
Shinn begins with one of her boldest statements: every person "comes into the world financed by God," with what they need already on their path. In her teaching, this supply is released through faith and the spoken word. She quotes Jesus in Mark 9:23: "If thou canst believe, all things are possible."

Her example is a woman with no stage experience who wanted a good role in the theater. She repeated an affirmation from Shinn's earlier book "hundreds of times," and she later won an important part in a successful opera. The woman herself called it a miracle.

The affirmations that follow are full of abundance imagery: ships coming in over a calm sea, avalanches of plenty, an echo of Psalm 23:6 ("Goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life"). Some readers will love these images; others may find talk of "millions" uncomfortable. It helps to read them as Shinn's way of breaking the habit of thinking in terms of scarcity.

This chapter is not a promise of wealth. Notice, though, that two of its affirmations are about spending money "wisely and fearlessly." Shinn's ideal is a calm, generous relationship with money, free of panic and grasping. Today, that might mean pairing these words with a clear budget, honest work and good financial advice.
### Practice
1. Look at your bank balance or bills without flinching, and simply bless what is there.
2. Choose one affirmation from this chapter and say it aloud before you open your banking app each day this week.
3. Make one wise money decision: set up a small savings transfer, cancel an unused subscription, or ask for help with a budget.
4. Notice how your feelings about money shift when fear is not in the driver's seat.
### Key Affirmation
> I spend money under direct inspiration wisely and fearlessly, knowing my supply is endless and immediate.

## Happiness
### Reflection
Shinn borrows a line from the 1924 silent film *The Thief of Bagdad*: happiness must be earned. In her view, we earn it by learning to manage our emotions, especially fear, dread and resentment. When we truly trust that a loving power is protecting us and those we love, she says, nervous tension relaxes and contentment can take its place. She quotes God's promise in Isaiah 43:19: "I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert."

She plays on a line from Shakespeare's *Henry IV*, "Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown," turning it into "the head that wears a frown." Then she makes a striking claim: resentment has ruined more homes than drink.

Her story is about a woman whose husband died and left part of his estate to a relative. Consumed by resentment, the woman became very ill. A visitor told her that her bitterness was hurting her, and when she chose forgiveness, Shinn reports, her health returned. Whatever we make of the medical side of that story, many people know from experience how heavy resentment feels to carry. The chapter's lighter affirmations, like the lion on the path that turns out to be "a friendly airedale" (a breed of terrier), invite us to meet our fears with humor.
### Practice
Write down one resentment you are still carrying, however small. Next to it, write what holding on to it is costing you in sleep, mood, energy or relationships. Then say, "I release this and choose peace." Each time the old feeling returns this week, gently repeat the chapter's key affirmation and turn your attention to one thing you are glad about.
### Key Affirmation
> I am harmonious, happy, radiant; detached from the tyranny of fear.

## Love
### Reflection
This short chapter speaks to a fear Shinn says nearly every woman carries: the fear of losing love to "the other woman." Writing more than a decade after her own divorce, she speaks with obvious feeling. Her diagnosis is spiritual. In her view, the fear comes from believing in "duality," in separation and competition, when in truth there is only oneness with God. As long as we keep picturing someone coming between us and our love, she warns, we give that picture power.

Rather than telling stories here, Shinn offers affirmations meant to replace that fearful picture. They speak of an "undivided" love, of God's love flowing through us like a magnetic current, and of the apparent enemy becoming a friend.

Her language is shaped by the 1920s and focuses on women and husbands, but the heart of the chapter applies to anyone. Jealousy and insecurity usually grow when we rehearse the worst. Shinn invites us to rehearse love instead. Modern readers might pair these affirmations with honest conversations and healthy boundaries, since peace in a relationship also depends on how two people actually treat each other.
### Practice
For one week, notice whenever you imagine a rival, a rejection or a loss in a relationship. Each time, pause and quietly say the chapter's key affirmation. Then write one sentence describing the loving, secure relationship you want, as if it were already true. At the end of the week, reread your sentences and notice any change in your thoughts.
### Key Affirmation
> I am at peace with myself and with the whole world. I love everyone and everyone loves me.

## Marriage
### Reflection
Shinn believed that a marriage stands only when it is built on a shared inner life. She quotes a popular nineteenth-century stage line, "Two souls with but a single thought, two hearts that beat as one," and draws on Jesus' words about marriage: "they twain shall be one flesh" and "What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder" (Matthew 19:5–6).

Her example is a couple who drifted apart as the husband's success and tastes grew while the wife kept a narrow outlook. In Shinn's picture, he lived on Fifth Avenue in his thoughts, while she remained on Third Avenue and in the Five and Ten Cent Stores. She bends Proverbs 23:7 ("as he thinketh in his heart, so is he") to say that we live where our thoughts live.

Some of this reflects the 1920s. Shinn places the burden of keeping pace on the wife, which many readers today will question. The deeper point travels well, though: partners need to keep growing together and share a "thought world." She also speaks of a "divine selection," the right partner meant for each person. Today we might read this chapter as a call for both partners to grow, dream and talk together, and to hold their marriage in prayer.
### Practice
Set aside twenty quiet minutes with your partner, or by yourself if you are single. Each of you writes three hopes for the next five years. Share them and look for the places where they meet. If you are single, write a description of the shared life you hope for and the person you want to be within it. Close by saying the key affirmation aloud.
### Key Affirmation
> I give thanks that the marriage made in heaven is now made manifest upon earth.

## Forgiveness
### Reflection
This chapter contains no stories, only four short affirmations, but it carries one of Shinn's central themes. She believed that forgiveness opens the "gates" for our good. Holding a grudge, in her view, blocks the flow of blessing, and so does holding on to our own guilt.

Her line "I am under grace and not under karmic law" echoes Paul's words in Romans 6:14, "ye are not under the law, but under grace." She means that we do not have to stay trapped in the consequences of every mistake we have made. "Though my mistakes be as scarlet, I shall be washed whiter than snow" draws on Isaiah 1:18 and Psalm 51:7. Her final line is bold: what did not happen in God's perfect kingdom "never happened anywhere." It is a way of saying that our failures do not define who we truly are.

Forgiveness does not mean pretending harm was acceptable, or staying in an unsafe situation. It means releasing the hold that the past has on your heart.
### Practice
1. Write the name of one person you are ready to begin forgiving. It may be yourself.
2. Under it, write: "I release you, and I release myself."
3. Say the key affirmation aloud slowly, three times.
4. If anger rises, don't fight it. Breathe, and say the words again.
5. Repeat each evening for a week.
### Key Affirmation
> I forgive everyone and everyone forgives me. The gates swing open for my good.

## Words of Wisdom
### Reflection
This chapter is a collection of Shinn's sayings, many of them twists on familiar proverbs. "Look before you leap" becomes "Never look or you'd never leap." "There's many a slip 'twixt the cup and the lip" becomes a promise that the right cup reaches the right lip. "When in doubt play trumps" comes from card games and means: when unsure, take the bold, fearless step.

Several sayings deal with intuition, which Shinn calls a "hunch." She treats hunches as quiet divine guidance and warns against arguing with them, ignoring them or blocking someone else's. Christopher Columbus, in her telling, is the great example of someone who followed one.

Others draw on Scripture. "Faith without nerve is dead" plays on James 2:26 ("faith without works is dead"). "The way of the transgressor is hard" echoes Proverbs 13:15 ("the way of transgressors is hard"), and "No good thing will he withhold from them that walk uprightly" comes from Psalm 84:11. "Before you call you are answered" echoes Isaiah 65:24. Jehoshaphat's story of praise before a battle is told in 2 Chronicles 20; the cymbals are Shinn's own colorful touch.

Read slowly, this chapter works like a pocket book of encouragement. Pick one saying a day and let it keep you company.
### Practice
Read through the sayings and mark the three that make you smile or sit up. Write each one on a separate card. Carry one card per day for three days. Each evening, note one moment when the saying came to mind and how it changed what you did or felt.
### Key Affirmation
> It is dark before the dawn but the dawn never fails. Trust in the dawn.

## Faith
### Reflection
Shinn draws a sharp line between hope and faith. Hope looks forward; faith "knows it has already received and acts accordingly." Her favorite image for this is "digging ditches," taken from 2 Kings 3:16–17, where the prophet Elisha tells the people to dig ditches in a dry valley because water is coming. In her teaching, preparing for your good is a way of showing you believe in it.

She tells a funny story from one of her classes. A student asked why so many women who fill a Hope Chest never marry. "Because it is a Hope Chest and not a *Faith* Chest," she replied. She adds that it is wise not to talk about your hopes too soon, quoting Matthew 6:6, "pray to thy Father which is in secret."

The affirmations here are among her richest. They echo Psalm 37 (which she cites by name), Psalm 121:4 ("shall neither slumber nor sleep"), Isaiah 40:4 ("every valley shall be exalted… and the crooked shall be made straight"), Hebrews 11:34 ("turned to flight the armies of the aliens"), Psalm 78:41 (limiting "the Holy One of Israel") and Psalm 55:22 (casting your burden on the Lord). Faith, for Shinn, means thinking, speaking and acting as if your prayer is already heard.
### Practice
Choose one hope you are holding. Ask yourself: "If I truly believed this was coming, what would I do to prepare?" Write down three possible "ditches" and dig one of them this week. Clear a shelf, update a résumé, take a class or set aside a small fund. Keep it quiet for now, and let your action speak your faith.
### Key Affirmation
> I now exercise my fearless faith in three ways—by thinking, speaking and acting.

## Loss
### Reflection
Shinn teaches that losing something reflects a "belief of loss" in the subconscious mind, and that clearing that belief opens the way for the thing, or something equal to it, to return.

Her story is charming. A woman lost a silver pencil in a theater and could not find it. Instead of fretting, she affirmed that nothing that was rightfully hers could truly be lost. Weeks later a friend offered her a gold pencil that had cost fifty dollars (roughly $950 today) at Tiffany's. The delighted woman exclaimed that the silver pencil had not been good enough for her.

Shinn concludes that we can only lose what does not belong to us by divine right, or what is not good enough for us. That idea can be a real comfort when something slips away. It is best kept for everyday losses, like objects, chances and plans. Grief over people we love is a different and deeper matter, and it deserves time, care and support. Still, the habit of meeting small losses with calm trust instead of panic is a valuable one.
### Practice
Think of something you have lost recently, whether an object, an opportunity or a plan. Write it down, then write: "If this is mine, it will be restored or I will receive its equivalent." Do whatever is practical to recover it, then release the worry. Watch over the next weeks for surprising replacements, and write them down when they come.
### Key Affirmation
> There is no loss in Divine Mind, therefore, I cannot lose anything which belongs to me.

## Debt
### Reflection
Shinn approached debt the way she approached most problems: start with the inner belief. In her view, owing money or being owed money points to a "belief of debt" in the mind, which needs to be dissolved.

She tells of a woman who had been owed a thousand dollars (roughly $19,000 today) for years. Shinn told her to work on herself, not the man, and gave her a statement denying debt and sending him love and forgiveness. Shinn reports that the money arrived within about a month.

It is important to read this chapter wisely. Shinn's affirmations are about releasing fear, blame and resentment around money, which can be a real gift when debt feels shameful or overwhelming. They are not a way to make legal obligations disappear. Paired with practical steps, they can help you face your finances with a clearer head: listing what you owe, talking honestly with creditors, making a realistic plan and getting reputable advice. The emotional shift Shinn describes, from dread and grudges to calm and goodwill, often makes those practical steps easier to take.
### Practice
1. Make a simple list of every debt you owe or are owed.
2. Read the list aloud, then say the key affirmation, sending goodwill to everyone named.
3. Choose one practical step this week: a payment plan, a polite reminder, a call to a nonprofit credit counselor or a budget session.
4. Repeat the affirmation each time you make a payment.
### Key Affirmation
> I deny debt, there is no debt in Divine Mind, no man owes me anything, all is squared. I send forth love and forgiveness.

## Sales
### Reflection
This brief chapter tells a story about faith that does not look out the window. A woman in a country town wanted to sell her house and furniture in the middle of winter, with snow so deep that hardly anyone could reach her door. She had asked God to sell her things to the right person at the right price, so she ignored the weather. She polished the furniture, pushed it to the middle of the room and got ready to sell.

"I never looked out of the window at the blizzard," she said. Shinn reports that buyers came anyway, and that both the furniture and the house sold without an agent's commission.

The lesson is about preparation as an act of faith, the same "digging ditches" idea found in the Faith chapter. The affirmation she offers is notable for its fairness: it asks for the right buyer and the right price, "giving perfect satisfaction" to everyone. Whether you are selling a car, a home or your own handmade work, the chapter encourages you to prepare well and stop staring at the storm.
### Practice
If you have something to sell, prepare it as if the buyer is on the way. Clean it, photograph it well, write an honest description and set a fair price. Each time you check for responses, say the key affirmation instead of worrying. If nothing is for sale right now, apply the idea to a project you are waiting on.
### Key Affirmation
> I give thanks that this article (or property) is now sold to the right person or persons for the right price, giving perfect satisfaction.

## Interviews
### Reflection
Two short affirmations make up this chapter, written for the anxious moments before a meeting that matters. The first takes the pressure off: on the spiritual level there is no competition, and what is truly yours will come to you. The second asks you to feel connected "in love" with the person interviewing you, trusting that God protects your interests and that the best outcome will emerge. Together they shift your stance from fear and rivalry to calm goodwill, which is also a helpful frame of mind for any interview.
### Practice
On the morning of an interview, prepare thoroughly: research, practice answers, plan your outfit. Then sit quietly for two minutes. Picture the person you will meet and silently wish them well. Say both affirmations from this chapter aloud. Walk in remembering that you are there to meet someone, not to fight anyone.
### Key Affirmation
> There is no competition on the Spiritual plane. What is mine is given me, under grace.

## Guidance
### Reflection
Shinn believed that answers are always on our path, often in unexpected places. Her story is a lovely one. A worried woman wondered whether her troubles would ever clear up. Her maid, chatting nearby, told a story about a hotel gardener who, after three days of rain, replied: "My God, doesn't it always clear up?" The woman took it as a direct answer to her thoughts, and soon afterward, Shinn reports, her problem resolved in an unexpected way.

The affirmations ask for wisdom, intuition and humility. "My angel of destiny goes before me, keeping me in the Way" echoes Exodus 23:20, "I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way." Being "meek and lowly of heart" comes from Jesus in Matthew 11:29, and the idea that the last shall be first comes from Matthew 20:16. "Your will, not my will" recalls Jesus' prayer in Luke 22:42, and "in the twinkling of an eye" comes from 1 Corinthians 15:52.

For readers today, this chapter is an invitation to listen more closely: to conversations, chance remarks and quiet inner nudges.
### Practice
Write down a question you need guidance on. Say: "Infinite Spirit, give me wisdom," and then let it go. For the next three days, keep a small notebook. Jot down any remark, sign, song lyric or inner nudge that seems to speak to your question. At the end, read it over and choose one wise step.
### Key Affirmation
> I am always under direct inspiration. I know just what to do and give instant obedience to my intuitive leads.

## Protection
### Reflection
This chapter offers two brief affirmations for times when you feel exposed, anxious or surrounded by negativity. The first pictures you wrapped in the white light of Christ, where nothing negative can reach you. The second sees your "fear giants" shrinking to nothing as you walk in that light, a phrase that brings to mind the giants the Israelite spies feared in the promised land (Numbers 13:33). Many readers find that a calm image like this helps settle the nervous system before a hard day. It works best alongside sensible, practical safety.
### Practice
Before leaving home, close your eyes for one minute. Picture a soft white light surrounding you completely. Name one fear you are carrying today and imagine it growing smaller and smaller in that light. Say the key affirmation once aloud. Then go about your day, taking the usual sensible precautions.
### Key Affirmation
> I walk in the Light of the Christ and my fear giants dwindle into nothingness.

## Memory
### Reflection
This is the shortest chapter in the book: a single affirmation. Shinn applies her usual principle that there is no loss in Divine Mind to memory itself. The statement is wonderfully balanced. It asks not only to remember everything you should, but also to forget whatever is not for your good. Many of us need both: help recalling names, tasks and facts, and help letting go of old hurts that replay in our minds. If you are worried about real changes in your memory, talk with a doctor, and let this affirmation be a calm companion along the way.
### Practice
When you can't recall something, stop straining. Take a slow breath, say the affirmation, and move on to something else. Often the memory returns on its own. At night, use the second half of the statement to release one painful memory that keeps replaying.
### Key Affirmation
> There is no loss of memory in Divine Mind, therefore, I recollect everything I should remember and I forget all that is not for my good.

## Divine Design
### Reflection
Divine Design is one of Shinn's most beloved ideas. Just as the whole oak tree is present in the acorn, she says, a perfect pattern for each person's life exists in the "superconscious mind." That pattern holds health, abundance, love and "perfect self-expression," and it unfolds day by day as we follow our intuition.

Her story is about a woman furnishing a new apartment who suddenly pictured a Chinese cabinet in one corner. Soon she found a magnificent carved cabinet in an antique shop, said to be worth a thousand dollars (roughly $19,000 today). A price of two hundred dollars (about $3,800 today) came to her mind, so she offered it. Walking home through the snow, she kept repeating, "If it's mine I can't lose it and if it isn't mine, I don't want it." Days later, the owner accepted her offer.

Shinn quotes Psalm 127:1, "Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it," and echoes Isaiah 65:24, "before they call, I will answer." The point is not to grab anything you want. Even millions, she says, bring no happiness unless they belong to the plan meant for you. This idea can be freeing: you don't have to chase someone else's path.
### Practice
1. Sit quietly and ask: "What is the acorn in me? What am I uniquely made to do?"
2. Write freely for ten minutes without editing.
3. Underline anything that makes you feel lighter or more alive.
4. Choose one underlined item and take a small step toward it this week.
5. Use the key affirmation when you feel pulled toward someone else's path.
### Key Affirmation
> I let go of everything not divinely designed for me, and the perfect plan of my life now comes to pass.

## Health
### Reflection
In this chapter Shinn makes some of her strongest claims. She writes that "all sickness comes from sin or violation of Spiritual Law," that fear and resentment damage the body, and even that old age and death come from wrong mental pictures. She recalls Jesus' healings, where forgiveness and healing go together (as with the paralyzed man in Mark 2:5–11), and quotes Genesis 1:27 on being made in God's image. "The body electric" is borrowed from the poet Walt Whitman.

These views reflect New Thought teaching of the 1920s and should be read as her beliefs, not as medical fact. Illness is not a sign of personal failure or sin, and no one should feel blamed for being sick. At the same time, most of us know that stress, fear and bitterness affect how we feel, and that hope and peace can support well-being.

Please treat these affirmations as a spiritual and emotional practice that goes alongside medical care, never in place of it. If you have symptoms or a diagnosis, see a doctor and follow their advice. Many readers find that affirmations help them feel calmer and more hopeful during treatment.
### Practice
Each morning this week, place a hand on your heart and say the key affirmation slowly. Then name one thing you will do today to care for your body: drink water, take a walk, take your medication, rest, or book that overdue checkup. Affirmations work best as a companion to good medical care, never a replacement for it.
### Key Affirmation
> I live in the Kingdom of eternal joy and absorbing interests.

## Eyes
### Reflection
Shinn links poor eyesight with certain mental habits: fear, suspicion, always looking for obstacles, and living in the past or future instead of the present. Her affirmations are as much about how we see life as about physical sight: seeing God in every face, good in every situation, and an open road ahead. Read them as a spiritual practice for changing your outlook. They are not a treatment for eye conditions. Keep up regular eye exams, and see an eye doctor promptly about any change in your vision.
### Practice
Today, practice "seeing clearly" in Shinn's spiritual sense. Each time you notice yourself expecting the worst, look around and name one good thing you can actually see. At night, say the key affirmation. And if your eyes need attention, book an eye exam this week. The affirmation is a companion to professional care, not a substitute.
### Key Affirmation
> I see God in every face, I see good in every situation.

## Anaemia
### Reflection
Shinn connects anaemia (spelled the British way here) with "unfed desires" and a lack of happiness. Her single affirmation speaks of being nourished by the Spirit and filled with light, and she notes it may be used for any illness. Many readers find this a beautiful prayer for times of weariness or low spirits. It should never replace medical care. Anemia has real physical causes, such as low iron or other conditions, that a doctor can test for and treat. Use the affirmation as emotional and spiritual support alongside that care.
### Practice
1. Ask yourself: "What desire in me feels unfed?" Write it down.
2. Plan one small way to nourish it this week.
3. Say the key affirmation with each meal as a moment of gratitude.
4. If you feel persistently tired, weak or short of breath, see a doctor. Simple blood tests can find the cause.
### Key Affirmation
> I am nourished by the Spirit within.

## Ears
### Reflection
Shinn associates hearing problems with stubbornness, a strong personal will and not wanting to hear certain things. Her affirmation turns this into a prayer for open listening: hearing the voice of intuition clearly, and hearing "glad tidings of great joy," the angel's words to the shepherds in Luke 2:10. You can use it as a spiritual practice of becoming more open and receptive. It is not a treatment for hearing loss. Please see a doctor or hearing specialist about any change in your hearing, and use the affirmation alongside their care.
### Practice
For one day, practice listening fully. In each conversation, let the other person finish before you respond. In the evening, sit in silence for five minutes and listen inwardly, then say the key affirmation. If you have noticed changes in your hearing, make an appointment for a hearing test. Prayer and medical care can go together.
### Key Affirmation
> I hear clearly the voice of intuition and give instant obedience.

## Rheumatism
### Reflection
Shinn links rheumatism, a word once used for many kinds of joint and muscle pain, with faultfinding and criticism. Her affirmation asks for light to dissolve "acid thoughts" and affirms love for everyone. Letting go of a critical spirit is a worthy goal in itself, and it may bring more ease and peace. But joint pain has physical causes that need proper diagnosis and treatment. Treat this affirmation as a spiritual and emotional practice alongside, never instead of, the care of a doctor.
### Practice
For three days, keep a tally of critical thoughts about yourself and others. Don't judge the count, just notice it. Each time, gently replace the thought with "I love everyone and everyone loves me." If you live with joint pain, keep following your doctor's advice, or make an appointment if you haven't yet.
### Key Affirmation
> The Light of the Christ now floods my consciousness dissolving all acid thoughts.

## False Growths
### Reflection
Shinn ties abnormal growths in the body to jealousy, hatred, resentment and fear. Her affirmation draws on Matthew 15:13, "Every plant, which my heavenly Father hath not planted, shall be rooted up," and asks that every false idea be cleared from the mind. Releasing jealousy and resentment is spiritually healthy, but it is essential to be clear: any lump or growth needs prompt medical attention. Please see a doctor without delay. Use this prayer only as a spiritual and emotional practice alongside your treatment, never in place of it.
### Practice
Write down one jealous or resentful thought you would like to "uproot." Picture pulling it out like a weed and replacing it with a flower. Say the key affirmation as you do. Most important: if you have noticed any lump, swelling or unusual change in your body, call your doctor today. Your spiritual practice and your medical care belong together.
### Key Affirmation
> All false ideas in my consciousness are now obliterated.

## Heart Disease
### Reflection
Shinn connects heart trouble with fear and anger. Her affirmation describes the heart as a perfect idea in God's mind, "a happy heart, a fearless heart and a loving heart." There is real wisdom in tending the emotional heart, and it is well known that stress and anger affect how we feel. But heart disease is serious and needs medical care. Use this affirmation as a spiritual and emotional practice alongside your doctor's treatment, never instead of it. If you ever have chest pain or shortness of breath, call emergency services right away.
### Practice
Twice a day, sit quietly with one hand over your heart. Breathe slowly for one minute, then say the key affirmation. Let it soften fear and anger. Keep your medical appointments, take any prescribed medication and follow your doctor's advice. This affirmation is meant to support that care, not replace it.
### Key Affirmation
> It is a happy heart, a fearless heart and a loving heart.

## Animals
### Reflection
Shinn's love of animals shows in this little chapter of prayers, using a dog as her example. Her affirmations see the animal as a perfect idea in God's mind, guided by divine intelligence and always "in its right place." Many pet owners find comfort in speaking blessings over a sick, anxious or wandering animal. Pair this with good practical care. If your pet is unwell, take it to a veterinarian. If it is lost, search, call shelters and put up notices while you pray.
### Practice
Sit quietly with your pet, or picture an animal you love. Rest your hand on it, if you can, and slowly say the key affirmation. Notice any calm that settles over both of you. Then take one practical step for its well-being today, such as a walk, a vet appointment or a fresh look at its food and water.

> Infinite Intelligence illumines and directs this animal.

## Elements
### Reflection
Shinn believed that human beings, made in God's image (Genesis 1:26–27) and given "dominion" over creation, can influence even the weather through faith. She recalls Jesus calming the storm (Mark 4:39), when he "rebuked the wind" and "there was a great calm."

She tells of a Native American desert people who pray for rain through a ceremonial dance, and she says only those who show no fear may take part. A woman who saw it told her rain poured from a blue sky while the sun still shone. Shinn's description is brief and comes secondhand, and it reflects the outsider's view of her time.

Her short affirmations cover fire, drought and storms. Whatever your view of praying about the weather, they can steady the mind when nature feels frightening. In a storm or emergency, follow official safety advice first. Then let prayer bring calm.
### Practice
The next time a storm, heat wave or other weather worry unsettles you, take sensible precautions first. Then sit quietly and say: "The Christ within now rebukes the winds and the waves and there comes a great calm." Notice your breathing slow. Apply the same words to any "storm" in your inner life.
### Key Affirmation
> The Christ within now rebukes the winds and the waves and there comes a great calm.

## Journey
### Reflection
A single affirmation makes up this chapter. It asks for three things on any trip: the right journey, the right conditions and the right supply. It is a lovely prayer for travel of any kind, whether a commute, a vacation, a move to a new city or a big life transition. Saying it before setting out can turn nervousness into trust. Pair it with good planning, sensible budgeting and the usual travel precautions.
### Practice
Before your next trip, large or small, write down your plans and budget. Then sit for a moment, say the key affirmation, and picture yourself arriving safely and happily. Keep a copy of the affirmation in your wallet or phone and repeat it whenever delays or worries come up along the way.
### Key Affirmation
> I give thanks for the Divinely planned journey under Divinely planned conditions with the Divinely planned supply.

## Miscellaneous
### Reflection
This is one of the richest chapters. Shinn warns that what we hate or fear tends to come back to us, because resentment paints a vivid picture in the mind. Her cure is "non-resistance." She tells of a woman irritated by a suitor's endless talk of his charming female cousins. When a second man did the same, she laughed, blessed "all the cousins in the Universe," and never heard of them again.

Other stories show active faith. A woman praying for a lodger had a hunch to buy a four-dollar lamp (roughly $75 today) but felt she couldn't afford it. Shinn told her to buy it, and soon the ideal tenant appeared. Another woman, asking for five hundred dollars (about $9,500 today), began giving small gifts and generous tips, and Shinn reports her hostess handed her a check for exactly that amount.

Scripture runs throughout: "I will give to thee the land that thou seest" (Genesis 13:15), "faith without works is dead" (James 2:26), becoming "as little children" (Matthew 18:3), and "where there is no vision, the people perish" (Proverbs 29:18). "Great peace have they which love thy law" is Psalm 119:165.

The chapter's gentle "Magnolia Charlie," a shoemaker who loved the first magnolia and the afternoon train, reminds us that everyone carries a dream.
### Practice
1. Notice one complaint you keep repeating to others. For one week, stop voicing it.
2. Replace it with: "I cast every burden upon the Christ within and I go free."
3. Identify one small act of "active faith," like your own "lamp," and do it.
4. Give one unexpected small gift or kindness.
5. Write down what changes.
### Key Affirmation
> All things I seek are now seeking me.

## Conclusion
### Reflection
Shinn ends her book in just a few lines. Choose the affirmation that speaks to you most, she says, and "wave it over the situation" in front of you, because your word is "God in action." She closes with two Bible verses. The first is Isaiah 55:11, where God says his word "shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please." The second is Romans 10:18, "their words unto the ends of the world." (She quotes both from memory, with small differences from the King James wording.)

Her parting advice is wonderfully practical: you don't need to use every affirmation in the book. One phrase that rings true for you, spoken faithfully, is enough to begin. Many readers find that a single sentence becomes a lifelong companion.
### Practice
Look back through the book and choose the one affirmation that moved you most. Write it on a card and place it where you will see it every morning. Commit to saying it aloud daily for the next 30 days, and pair it with one practical action that supports it. Then turn to the 30-Day Practice that follows.
### Key Affirmation
> It shall not return unto me void but shall accomplish that whereunto it is sent.
