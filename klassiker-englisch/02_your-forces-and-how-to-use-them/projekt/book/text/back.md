## Afterword: Using All Your Forces

If you have read this far, you have walked through Larson's whole map of the human being: the ruling "I Am," the conscious and subconscious mind, thought and feeling, desire and will, concentration, speech, character, imagination and the higher forces of aspiration, gratitude, love and faith. It is a lot. You do not need to master it all at once.

What stands out, looking back, is how often Larson returns to one idea in different clothes. Your forces go where your attention goes. What you think about most, love most and say most often tends to shape who you become. Scatter your attention and your power scatters with it. Gather it around a clear ideal and the same power begins to build.

Not every claim in these pages has aged well. Larson wrote at a time when the science of the mind was young and its admirers were bold. He promised more than any honest teacher can promise today, and some of what he believed about the subconscious, heredity and the body has not held up. It is fair to read him with a friendly skepticism.

Yet the core of his teaching remains sturdy. People really can learn to notice their thoughts instead of obeying them. Habits really do change through steady, repeated practice. The words we use about ourselves really do color how we act. Character really is the thing that keeps talent and energy from going to waste. None of this is magic. All of it is work, and work that pays off over years rather than days.

So take what helps. Keep one or two practices that made a difference during the thirty days and let the rest wait for another season. Return to a chapter when life gives you a reason to. Read the creed on a hard morning.

Larson believed that each of us holds far more than we have yet used. You do not have to accept his every word to test that belief in your own life. The only way to find out is the way he always recommended: begin where you are, today, and use what you have.
