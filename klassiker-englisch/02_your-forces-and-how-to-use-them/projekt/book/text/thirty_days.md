## The 30-Day Practice

Reading about your forces is one thing. Using them is another. This month-long practice turns Larson's ideas into small daily actions you can actually do between breakfast and bedtime.

Each day gives you one sentence from the book, the chapter it comes from, and a short task. Read the sentence in the morning. Do the task sometime during the day. In the evening, take two minutes to write down what happened, even if the answer is "not much." Those notes will matter more than you expect by the end of the month.

The days follow the shape of the book. Week one is about awareness and what Larson calls the ruling principle: the part of you that decides. Week two turns to the subconscious, to thought and to change. Week three trains the will, attention, desire and speech. Week four moves to character, imagination and the higher forces. The last two days bring you back to the creed and to a commitment of your own.

A few ground rules. Do not try to be perfect. If you miss a day, simply pick up where you left off; there is no penalty and no need to start over. Keep the tasks small and honest. And if a task touches your health or your body, treat it as something that goes alongside good medical care, never as a replacement for it.

### Day 1 – Stepping Above

> You cannot control anything in your life, however, until you are above it.

Source: The Ruling Principle in Man

Three times today, pause for ten seconds and notice what you are feeling and thinking, as if you were watching it from a step above. Do not judge it or fix it. Simply notice that there is a "you" that can observe your moods instead of being swallowed by them.

### Day 2 – Knowing What You Want

> You must constantly know what you are and what you want, and you must constantly be determined to secure what you want.

Source: How We Govern the Forces We Possess

Write one sentence that begins "Right now, what I want most is…" Then write one sentence that begins "The kind of person I am choosing to be is…" Keep the card or note where you will see it tonight and read it once more before sleep.

### Day 3 – One Step Forward

> Man lives to move forward.

Source: How We Govern the Forces We Possess

Pick one task you have been putting off and move it forward by a single, visible step. It can be small: send the email, clear the desk, make the call. Notice how your energy feels afterward compared with how it felt while you were avoiding it.

### Day 4 – Making the Mind Tell

> The man who wins is the man who can apply in practical life every part of his mental ability, and who can make every action of his mind tell.

Source: The Use of Mind in Practical Action

Choose one hour of work today and give it your full mind: phone away, one task, no drifting. At the end of the hour, write down what you got done. Compare it honestly with a scattered hour.

### Day 5 – Believing in the Whole of You

> Believe in yourself but not simply in a part of yourself.

Source: The Use of Mind in Practical Action

List five strengths you have that are not related to your job, such as patience, humor, loyalty or a steady hand in a crisis. Read the list aloud. When a doubt comes up today, remind yourself that you are more than the one skill being tested.

### Day 6 – The Bigger Self

> Give the bigger man on the inside full right of way.

Source: The Use of Mind in Practical Action

Think of a moment in your past when you rose to an occasion and surprised yourself. Write it down in a few lines. Today, when you face something hard, ask: "How would that version of me handle this?" Then act on the answer.

### Day 7 – The Ideal and the Real

> Dream constantly of the ideal; work ceaselessly to perfect the real.

Source: The Forces of the Subconscious

Spend five minutes picturing one area of your life as you would truly like it to be. Then name one practical thing you can improve in that area today, and do it. End the first week by reading back over your notes from days one to six.

### Day 8 – Your Best in Every Circumstance

> Do your best under every circumstance, and believe that every circumstance will give its best to you.

Source: The Forces of the Subconscious

Choose the least pleasant task on today's list and decide in advance to do it well, not just to get it over with. Afterward, look for one small good thing that came out of it, whether a lesson, a finished job or a bit of goodwill.

### Day 9 – Regulating Your Thought

> There is nothing in your life that you cannot modify, change or improve when you learn to regulate your thought.

Source: Training the Subconscious for Special Results

Catch one recurring negative thought today, such as "I always mess this up." Write it down. Next to it, write a version that is honest but more useful, such as "I am learning this, and I am getting better." Each time the old thought returns, calmly swap in the new one.

### Day 10 – Be Your Best Now

> He who is his best develops the power to be better.

Source: Training the Subconscious for Special Results

Instead of planning a future self-improvement project, pick one way to be your best version today: on time, fully prepared, or kind under pressure. Live that one quality all day and note in the evening how it went.

### Day 11 – Thought with Feeling

> The more feeling there is in your thought the greater its power.

Source: The Art of Changing for the Better

Take the goal you wrote on Day 2. Spend three quiet minutes imagining it already true, and let yourself really feel what that would be like: the relief, the pride, the gladness. Words alone tend to fade; a goal you can feel tends to stay with you.

### Day 12 – Tending Your Own Garden

> Give less time trying to change the opinions of others, and more time trying to improve your own life.

Source: The Power of Subjective Thought

Today, skip one argument you would normally have, whether online, at work or at home. Put that time and energy into something that improves your own life: reading, a walk, a skill, a tidy room. Notice whether you miss the argument.

### Day 13 – The Mind's Eye

> The secret of right thinking is found in always keeping the mind’s eye stayed upon the greater and the better in all things.

Source: The Power of Subjective Thought

Before each meal today, name one thing that is going better than it was a year ago. It can be small. You are training your attention to look up and forward, which is a habit, not a mood.

### Day 14 – What You Love Most

> Personal man gradually but surely grows into the likeness of that which he thinks of the most, and man thinks the most of what he loves the best.

Source: The Art of Changing for the Better

Look at where your thoughts and spare minutes actually went this week. Write down the three things you dwelt on most. Ask honestly: are these what I want to grow into? If not, choose one thing to give more attention to next week.

### Day 15 – Wanting Clearly

> Know what you want and continue to want it.

Source: He Can Who Thinks He Can

Look back at your Day 2 card. Is your want still the same, or has it shifted? Rewrite it in the clearest, most specific words you can. Then commit to keeping that want steady for the rest of this month instead of changing it every day.

### Day 16 – Clear Sky or Fog

> The optimist lives under a clear sky; the pessimist lives in a fog.

Source: How We Secure What We Persistently Desire

Notice one moment today when you hesitate because you expect the worst. Ask: "What is the most useful thing I can do right now?" Then do it before the fog settles in. Optimism here is not a forecast; it is a decision to act.

### Day 17 – This One Thing I Do

> In its last analysis, the cause of all failure can be traced to the scattering of forces, and the cause of all achievement to the concentration of forces.

Source: Concentration and the Power Back of Suggestion

Choose the single most important task of the day. Work on it first, for at least thirty minutes, with nothing else open. When your mind wanders, gently bring it back with the words "this one thing." Count how many times you had to do it; the number usually drops with practice.

### Day 18 – Improve Yourself While You Wait

> While you are waiting for an opportunity to improve your time, improve yourself.

Source: The Development of the Will

Use one block of time that would usually be wasted, such as a commute, a queue or a waiting room, for deliberate self-improvement. Read ten pages, practice a language, or plan tomorrow. Waiting time is still your time.

### Day 19 – A Little More

> However much you may do, always remember you have the ability to do more.

Source: The Development of the Will

Today, when you would normally stop, do just a little more: five more minutes, one more page, one more call. Then stop on purpose. The will grows by being used in small, chosen doses, not by heroic strain.

### Day 20 – Building Your Mind

> A great mind does not come from ancestors, but from the life, the thought and the actions of the individual himself; and such a mind can be constructed by any one who understands the art of mind building, and who faithfully applies his art.

Source: The Building of a Great Mind

Pick one subject you would like to understand better and give it twenty focused minutes today. Write down one new idea you learned and one question it raised. A mind is built the same way a body is trained: a little, often.

### Day 21 – Words That Build

> Every word that is spoken exercises a power in personal life, and that power will work either for or against the person, depending upon the nature of the word.

Source: The Building Power of Constructive Speech

For one full day, listen to how you talk about yourself and your situation. Each time you hear a complaint or a put-down leave your mouth, restate it in words that are honest but constructive. End the third week by reading back over your notes.

### Day 22 – Character as Direction

> Character is the proper direction of all things, and the proper use of all things in the human system.

Source: The Art of Building Character

Choose one habit that pulls your energy in the wrong direction, such as late-night scrolling, snapping when tired or overspending. Do not attack it head-on. Instead, decide what that energy will go into today, and redirect it once when the habit calls.

### Day 23 – Kindness Returned

> Be good and kind to everybody and the world will be kind to you.

Source: How Character Determines Constructive Action

Do three quiet acts of kindness today without mentioning them to anyone: a helpful word, a held door, a thank-you note. Notice how people respond, and notice how you feel. Kindness is a character practice, not a transaction.

### Day 24 – Magnifying the Good

> Magnify the good; emphasize that which has worth; and talk only of those things that should live and grow.

Source: The Creative Forces in Man

In every conversation today, try to mention at least one thing that is working, whether in a project, in a person or in your day. You do not have to pretend problems away. You are simply making sure the good gets airtime too.

### Day 25 – Make It Better Today

> Take things as they are today and proceed at once to make them better.

Source: Imagination and the Master Mind

Pick one room, one routine or one relationship. Imagine it just ten percent better, and picture that clearly. Then take one action today that moves it in that direction. Imagination becomes useful the moment it is joined to a step.

### Day 26 – The Vision of the Soul

> Follow the vision of the soul.

Source: Imagination and the Master Mind

Write a short paragraph describing the life you would be proud to have lived ten years from now. Do not edit it for realism yet. Then underline one sentence in it and ask: "What would honor this today?" Do that.

### Day 27 – Gratitude

> Whenever you feel grateful for anything, you always feel nearer to the real quality of that particular thing.

Source: The Higher Forces in Man

Write down ten things you are grateful for, and make at least three of them people. Choose one of those people and tell them, by message, call or in person, what they mean to you. Gratitude spoken aloud tends to grow.

### Day 28 – Faith in Yourself

> The more faith you have in yourself, the more faith people will have in you.

Source: The Higher Forces in Man

Recall three things you did this month that you once might not have believed you could do, however small. Write them down. Then take on one task today with the calm assumption that you will find a way through it. End the fourth week by reading back over your notes.

### Day 29 – The Creed

> To forget the mistakes of the past and press on to the greater achievements of the future.

Source: Promise Yourself

Read the whole creed slowly, aloud if you can. Choose the one line that challenges you most and copy it onto a card. Carry it with you this week. If an old mistake comes to mind today, name what you learned from it and then set it down.

### Day 30 – Your Commitment

> It is right that we all should ascend to the higher, the greater and the better now.

Source: The Greatest Power in Man

Read back through all your notes from the past thirty days. Write down the three practices that helped you most and decide how you will keep them going. Finish by writing your own one-sentence promise to yourself, and date it.
